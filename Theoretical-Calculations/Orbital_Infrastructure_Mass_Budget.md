# Orbital Infrastructure — Mass Budget & Population Calculations

Derived from a research conversation with Grok (2026-07-01) exploring whether a rotating
O'Neill-cylinder-style habitat is physically plausible using only the mass already present
in Earth-orbit debris. These are AI-assisted back-of-envelope, order-of-magnitude figures —
useful for setting **plausibility bounds** on Inner Tepenia canon, not literal hard numbers.

Source transcript: `to-be-integrated/Conversation with Grok - theoretical logistics for
orbital infrastructure.rtf`

---

## 1. Total Mass of Earth-Orbiting Debris (Real-World Baseline)

- Total human-made mass in Earth orbit: **~9,000–16,200+ metric tonnes**
  - ESA (~2025–2026 estimate): >16,200 tonnes (all cataloged + modeled objects)
  - NASA (Jan 2022 figure): >9,000 tonnes
  - Other sources: 8,000–13,000+ tonnes in various years
  - LEO holds a large share: ~6,600+ tonnes in some LEO-focused reports
- Mass vs. object-count breakdown:
  - Large (>10 cm): ~34,000–54,000 tracked objects → **>99.9% of total mass**
  - Medium (1–10 cm): ~500,000–1.2 million objects → <0.05–0.1% of mass
  - Small (<1 cm): 100–140+ million objects >1mm → negligible mass
- Mass is dominated by rocket bodies, defunct satellites, and fragmentation debris
  (660+ known fragmentation events)

## 2. Mass Required for an Unconstrained O'Neill-Style Cylinder

Target: 5,000–10,000 humans + 5,000–10,000 robots, 1G via rotation, no material limit.

- Sizing: radius 250–500 m (comfortable low-RPM rotation), length 0.3–2 km,
  ~100–200 m²/person habitable floor area (roughly double for agriculture/recreation)
- Mass drivers in order of impact: **radiation shielding** (~5–10 tonnes/m² outside the
  magnetosphere) > structure > atmosphere > contents
- Reference design — **Kalpana One**: 250 m radius, 325 m length, ~3,000 people target,
  ~0.5 km² floor area → **~7 million tonnes total** (mostly shielding)
- Full-scale classic O'Neill cylinder (millions of inhabitants): billions–trillions of tonnes
- Optimistic minimum (advanced composites, lower cabin pressure, cislunar orbit with partial
  Earth shielding): hundreds of thousands of tonnes
- **Working estimate: ~1–10 million metric tons** for a functional 5,000–10,000
  human+robot cylinder at 1G (for scale, the ISS is ~450 tonnes)

## 3. Constrained Scenario: Building Using 100% of All Earth-Orbit Debris (~16,200 t)

- Verdict: feasible only at **proof-of-concept / small-village scale** — nowhere near
  Kalpana One's 7-million-tonne design
- A reduced-shielding small design (~100 m diameter, 3–4 rpm, low/no shielding) can run
  as low as **8.5–30 kilotonnes** for a basic habitable volume — within the debris budget
- Maximum population estimates:
  - Optimistic (heavy automation/robots, hydroponics, ~100–200+ m²/person): **500–2,000+ people**
  - Conservative/comfortable long-term: tens to ~200 people
- Shielding/structure mass scales with surface area and volume — it cannot be stretched
  arbitrarily. A full O'Neill-for-millions would need billions of tonnes regardless of
  efficiency gains.

## 4. Gravity Gradient / Radius Constraints (1G, Rotating Habitat)

Formula: **Δg/g ≈ h / r** (h = person height, r = radius to floor)

For a 6'5" (1.96 m) person:

| Radius | Head-to-foot Δg | Comfort |
|---|---|---|
| ~100 m | ~2% | acceptable / near-imperceptible |
| ~200 m | ~1% | very comfortable |
| ~400 m+ | <0.5% | truly imperceptible |

At r = 100–250 m and 1G, rotation rate lands around **2–3 rpm** — low enough Coriolis
effect for long-term habitation with some acclimation.

## 5. Population Capacity at 10 m²/Inhabitant Density

- 200 humans + 200 robots = 400 total → needs only ~4,000 m² floor area (trivial —
  fits in e.g. a 150 m-radius × 50–100 m cylinder)
- Scaling further within the ~16,200-tonne debris budget, low-orbit/reduced-shielding design:
  - Practical comfortable ceiling: several hundred to low thousands of total inhabitants
    (humans + robots)
  - Optimistic tight-packing ceiling: ~1,000–3,000+ total (approaching Kalpana One's
    population target, though not its generous per-person space allowance)

## 6. Best Single Estimate — 100 m-Radius Cylinder, 1G, Full Debris Budget (~16,200 t)

- Circumference ≈ 628 m; practical length 100–300 m → habitable floor area ≈
  60,000–190,000 m² (before multi-level or inner-cylinder multipliers)
- Mass per person in this low-shielding/higher-RPM regime: as low as **~10–20
  tonnes/person** (vs. millions of tonnes/person in fully-shielded deep-space designs)
- **Comfortable population: roughly 200–800 humans**, plus a similar-or-larger number
  of robots
- **Optimistic dense maximum: 1,000–2,000+ humans** (20–50 m²/person, minimal
  margin/redundancy)
- Key enabling assumptions: low-Earth-orbit placement (partial magnetosphere
  protection), debris-sourced aluminum/composite materials, closed-loop life support,
  hydroponics/vertical farming, heavy robot automation for construction and maintenance

## Design Sensitivity Notes

- **Shielding is the single largest mass driver.** Orbit choice — inside vs. outside
  the Van Allen belts/magnetosphere — has an outsized effect on feasible population.
- Scaling beyond ~1,000–2,000 people at this mass budget requires importing material
  from the Moon or asteroids; debris alone cannot support more.
- These are order-of-magnitude figures from AI-assisted research, not engineering-grade
  calculations. Treat them as plausibility bounds, not canon-locked numbers, unless a
  future pass deliberately canonizes specific values.
