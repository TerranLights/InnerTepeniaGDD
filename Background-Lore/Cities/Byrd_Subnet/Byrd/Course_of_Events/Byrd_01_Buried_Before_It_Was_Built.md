## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

An expedition sets out for Byrd Station's last recorded coordinates carrying nothing but old maps and
transport manifests — no confirmation the site is even still accessible after the long silence since
anyone last had reason to go there. Every other Tepenian exile community that has ever found a place to
settle has done it the same way: arrive, survey open ground, build outward from a chosen point.
Nothing about this expedition has any reason yet to expect otherwise.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

When the crew finally reaches the coordinates, there is no surface structure at all to find — no
station, no ruin, no obvious sign anyone was ever here. Only a wide, featureless field of compacted
snow, indistinguishable from any other stretch of Antarctic interior, sitting on top of whatever
infrastructure the old records insist is actually down there. If the expedition is going to become a
settlement at all, someone has to commit real effort to digging down into unknown, unverified ground
before a single visible structure exists to build up from — a complete inversion of how every other
Tepenian community has ever begun.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone who wants to
commit the expedition's limited remaining strength to excavating immediately, and someone who wants to
first secure a defensible surface foothold before anyone descends into a structure nobody has surveyed,
verified stable, or confirmed is even the right site. Neither position is unreasonable. A wants to dig
because open Antarctic ground offers no real shelter and the record insists there is something usable
below — every day spent not descending is a day spent exposed for nothing. B wants to hold the surface
first because descending blind into an unverified buried structure, this far from any help, risks
losing people to a collapse, bad air, or simply the wrong coordinates, with no way to call for rescue if
it goes wrong. This should read as two competent people making opposite bets under the same real time
pressure, not a caution-versus-recklessness morality tale.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly the expedition's
practical field leader — someone whose authority comes from having gotten the group this far and whose
instinct is that hesitation, this deep into an already-desperate journey, is its own kind of danger.
Character B is plausibly an engineer or surveyor attached to the expedition, someone whose training is
specifically in structural risk assessment and who is professionally unwilling to send people into a
space nobody has evaluated, whatever the cost of waiting. Either could plausibly be human or robot; the
conflict works the same either way, since it turns on method and risk tolerance rather than on anything
particular to one population.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The argument resolves in favor of descending — not recklessly, but with B's caution folded into how it
happens rather than whether it happens: a small, deliberately staged party goes down first, working
methodically, verifying passage before anyone else follows. The earliest arrivals dig down before they
can build up, discovering and clearing the buried station rather than settling onto open ground the way
every other Tepenian exile community does. Byrd's founding becomes genuinely unique in Tepenia — a
community that built downward first, and only afterward developed anything resembling a surface
presence at all.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the compromise that won the argument was "descend carefully, in verified stages" rather than
either side's original position outright, that staged, survey-before-committing method doesn't end with
the first descent — it becomes the working default for every subsequent expansion into unmapped buried
ground for as long as the earliest arrivals themselves are still directing the digging. What began as a
one-time compromise between two people hardens into an inherited procedure.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

As Byrd's underground economy eventually matures and diversifies into distinct sectors — fabrication,
dispatch, resource extraction, and the rest — that inherited survey-before-expansion procedure becomes
the specific inherited responsibility of whichever sector ends up tasked with keeping the buried city
structurally sound as it grows. The habit born from one early argument between two people quietly
becomes the founding discipline of an entire future line of work, long after neither of the two
originators is still the one making the call.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Byrd is now a city built downward before it ever existed on the surface — but one that inherited, from
the very first days of its founding, a deliberate culture of verifying ground before committing to it,
rather than either the pure urgency or the pure caution either of its founding arguers actually wanted.
The city's whole later reputation for methodical, unglamorous structural discipline traces back to a
compromise neither side fully got their way on.
