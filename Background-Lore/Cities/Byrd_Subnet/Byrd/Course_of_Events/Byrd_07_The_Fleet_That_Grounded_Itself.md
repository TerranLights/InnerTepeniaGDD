## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Byrd's aviation fleet serves for generations as the city's only real connection to the rest of Tepenia
for people, not just goods — the one link that lets Byrdians actually leave and actually arrive, rather
than simply shipping crates out along the overland routes.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The fleet develops a problem Byrd's own considerable engineering expertise can diagnose immediately but
cannot solve — not a mystery, but a dead end. Whatever specific material or component the repair
genuinely requires was never something Byrd itself could produce locally, and the outside supply chain
that once quietly provided it has stopped existing, without anyone in Byrd having tracked exactly when
or why. A city that manufactures the apparatus that creates robots finds itself stopped cold by a
problem that, on its face, should be far simpler than anything else it routinely builds.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a resource-allocation fight, not a mystery to be
solved. A wants to commit real fabrication capacity toward either reverse-engineering a substitute
material or building a local alternative supply chain from scratch — a matter of civic pride and
practical necessity for a city whose whole identity rests on self-sufficiency. B argues that pouring
scarce, precision-capable industrial capacity into chasing one stubborn, possibly unsolvable materials
problem is a bad trade against the far more provable, more urgent returns of keeping chamber and
machinery production running at full capacity. Neither side disputes that flight matters; they disagree
on whether it's worth what solving it would actually cost the rest of the city's economy.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an aviation
maintenance lead — someone whose professional identity and daily work are bound up in the fleet, and who
takes its grounding personally as an unfinished problem rather than a settled fact. Character B is
plausibly a fabrication-economy planner or plant leader, someone whose responsibility is allocating
Byrd's finite, precision-capable industrial capacity across everything the city actually needs, and who
has to weigh one sector's pride against the whole city's more provable priorities. This plays best as an
honest opportunity-cost argument between two people who each have a legitimate claim on the same scarce
resource.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's position wins the resource argument, formally or simply by attrition — the effort to chase a
substitute or rebuild the missing supply chain doesn't continue indefinitely. The aircraft go
permanently grounded, not from any lack of skill, but because the specific fix was judged, deliberately
and after real argument, not worth what it would cost the rest of Byrd's economy to keep pursuing.
Byrd's engineers are left knowing exactly what's wrong and exactly why they can't solve it anyway —
competence intact, the problem sitting one step outside anything their own hands can reach.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the grounding was a resourced-and-lost argument rather than an unexplained technical mystery,
the people who lost that argument make sure the record of exactly what's missing and why survives them
in unusually precise, deliberate form — full documentation, preserved specifically in case circumstances
or the outside supply chain ever change, rather than the vague institutional shrug a fleet that simply
broke down and was quietly forgotten would have left behind.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that documentation survives in full, precise form, the aviation maintenance crews left tending a
fleet that cannot fly evolve into something closer to permanent archivists than mechanics in the
ordinary sense — custodians of a standing, ready diagnosis rather than active operators of working
aircraft. If the missing material or its supply chain ever reappeared, this crew could resume flight
faster than a city that had genuinely lost the knowledge, a readiness that exists only because the
original argument was lost so carefully rather than simply abandoned.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Byrd's aviation fleet sits permanently grounded, its maintenance crews having quietly become the
institution's most precise archivists of a specific, well-documented failure — an odd, quiet corner of
civic expertise nobody else in the city has much reason to think about, but that stands ready the
instant circumstances change, holding the clearest institutional memory in the city of exactly why the
sky stopped being reachable at all.
