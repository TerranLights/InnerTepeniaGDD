## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Byrd's underground population grows past what its own resource extraction and fabrication economy can
feed on its own. A single isolated interior settlement has no realistic way to produce enough dietary
variety for a city this size, no matter how efficiently it manages what it can extract and grow locally.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A real supply relationship has to form along Hwy 1's own northern route if Byrd's population is going to
eat anything beyond the barest extracted minimum — Esperanza's established agricultural tradition
sending food and variety south, Byrd sending manufactured goods and minerals back north in return. But
Esperanza's own harvests vary with the seasons, and its harbor already handles trans-shipment for
Belgrano's goods heading toward Palmer City — Byrd is not the only demand already pulling on Esperanza's
limited, weather-dependent capacity.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a negotiation-turned-confrontation between someone
representing Byrd's urgent need for guaranteed, predictable food shipments, and someone representing
Esperanza's genuine agricultural and logistical limits. A's demand for a fixed, reliable quota is
entirely reasonable given how much of Byrd's population now depends on this single supply line arriving
on schedule. B's resistance is equally reasonable — Esperanza cannot promise a fixed quota against
harvests that vary with weather it doesn't control, and it already has a standing obligation to
Belgrano's own trans-shipment needs that came first. Neither side is being unreasonable or greedy; they
are simply optimizing for different, incompatible kinds of certainty.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an early Byrd
trade or logistics figure tasked with securing the city's food security, someone whose job is measured
in whether Byrd eats reliably. Character B is plausibly an Esperanza agricultural or harbor figure —
a farmer, cooperative representative, or trans-shipment coordinator — whose job is protecting Esperanza's
own limited capacity from being overcommitted to any single demanding partner. The conflict reads
naturally as an inter-city trade negotiation between two people each accountable to their own
community's real constraints.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The negotiation resolves toward B's terms more than A's: a durable, ongoing trade relationship forms,
but as a flexible arrangement tracking Esperanza's actual seasonal capacity rather than a fixed quota
Byrd can rely on absolutely. Byrd accepts a less certain deal than it wanted because a durable
relationship, even an imperfect one, matters more than holding out for guarantees Esperanza genuinely
cannot make. Hwy 1 becomes something Byrd treats as vital in both directions — not simply an export
route, but the corridor an entire population's dietary variety actually depends on.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the arrangement that formed is variable rather than fixed, Byrd has to build genuine domestic
food-storage and buffer-stock capacity underground to absorb the natural swings in what Esperanza can
actually ship season to season — a new civic practice of reserve stores and rationing discipline that
didn't exist when the city's food supply was a simpler, purely local problem.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Once buffer-stock discipline is established for food specifically, it becomes the template Byrd
reflexively applies to other critical goods it depends on the outside world for — not because anyone
decides to generalize the practice, but because the habit of stockpiling against variable supply, once
learned, doesn't confine itself to the problem that taught it. Self-sufficiency as an instinct, rather
than a single food-specific policy, traces one of its earliest roots to this one imperfect negotiation.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Byrd now runs on a durable but variable food-supply relationship with Esperanza, buffered by a domestic
reserve-stock discipline that has quietly become one of the city's own defining habits — treating
stockpiling as ordinary civic practice that reaches well beyond food alone, an early expression of the
same reflexive self-sufficiency that will go on to define how Byrd handles nearly everything it can't
produce for itself.
