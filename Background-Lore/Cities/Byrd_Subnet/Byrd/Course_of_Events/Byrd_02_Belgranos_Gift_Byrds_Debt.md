## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Reaching Byrd's remote interior coordinates requires a vehicle capable of crossing unmapped terrain
that no existing transport was ever built for. No such vehicle exists anywhere in Tepenia yet — not a
prototype, not a design on paper, nothing. If Byrd is ever going to be found, something entirely new
has to be built first.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Belgrano's engineers commit to inventing that vehicle from nothing — the Arrastradoras, Tepenia's first
native tracked overland vehicle, purpose-built for a single specific crossing nobody has ever attempted.
But inventing the machine only solves half the problem. Somebody still has to actually drive it, alone,
across ground nobody has mapped, with no way to know in advance whether the vehicle — untested outside
controlled conditions — will hold together for the distance required, or whether the crew sent to prove
it will survive finding out.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone who wants to field
the Arrastradoras for the actual crossing as soon as it is minimally functional, and someone who wants
extensive field trials completed first, before any crew's life depends on it. This shouldn't play as
impatience versus professionalism — A's urgency is grounded in something real (every season the
expedition doesn't leave is a season Byrd, if it exists at all, stays unfound, and the exile community's
need for a viable settlement site doesn't pause for engineering caution), and B's caution is equally
grounded (Belgrano's own reputation, and the crew's actual survival, ride on a machine that has never
once been tested under real conditions). Both are right about what they're protecting; they simply
can't both be protected fully at once.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Belgrano
organizational leader or expedition sponsor answering to the broader exile community's pressing need for
a viable settlement site — someone whose job is weighing the whole community's stakes, not just the
vehicle's. Character B is plausibly the Arrastradoras' own lead engineer or designer, someone whose
professional identity is bound up in the machine actually working as intended, and who has the most
direct, technical reasons to know exactly how much is still unverified. The conflict reads naturally as
sponsor-versus-inventor, two people who each built or backed something they don't want to see fail for
different reasons.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Belgrano commits to fielding the Arrastradoras for the real crossing before extensive validation trials
are complete — the argument resolves in favor of urgency, but not blindly: whatever partial testing time
remains gets compressed and intensified rather than skipped outright. The vehicle ships out under real,
acknowledged uncertainty. The expedition succeeds; the crossing is made, and Byrd is found.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Having watched an untested vehicle succeed against genuine, acknowledged odds, Belgrano does not treat
the outcome as vindication of speed — it treats it as a warning about how close the gamble actually
came. In the vehicle lineage's aftermath, Belgrano institutionalizes a far more rigorous, continuous
field-testing culture for everything it subsequently builds, refusing to let urgency substitute for
verification a second time. What had been ad hoc engineering becomes a defined discipline.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that field-testing discipline specifically requires continuous real-world performance data
rather than one-time trials, Belgrano's engineers make a lasting practice of staying in contact with
wherever its vehicles actually end up operating — tracking how the machines hold up in the hands of
their real, ongoing users rather than treating delivery as the end of the relationship. Centuries later,
once Byrd's own fabrication economy matures enough to manufacture and maintain the same vehicle lineage
itself, that inherited habit of ongoing contact is already there waiting, ready to become the genuine
working relationship between Belgrano's mechanic culture and Byrd's own manufacturing capability that
persists into the present day.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Belgrano is now home to a serious, continuous vehicle-testing culture born directly from the calculated
risk that found Byrd — a discipline built on staying in contact with its machines long after they leave
the workshop. Byrd itself does not yet exist as a functioning city, but the habit that will one day bind
it to Belgrano across the length of Hwy 1 is already forming, decades before either city has any reason
to know it.
