# Byrd — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material (Byrd's own present-day DLC 2 struggle and its guild-based faction structure are out of scope
here; these chains cover only the city's founding and its long pre-war economic development).
10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Byrd's Megasheet (the buried mechanized-fabrication
mega-city, the founding expedition, the Cradle manufacturing role). Post-settlement only; nothing here
is canon.

---

## 1. Buried Before It Was Built

An expedition sets out to find Byrd Station using nothing but old maps and transport manifests, with
no confirmation the site is even still accessible. **But** when the crew finally reaches the
coordinates, they find no surface structure at all — only a field of compacted snow over whatever
infrastructure lies beneath it. **Therefore** the earliest arrivals have to dig down before they can
build up, discovering and clearing the buried station rather than settling onto open ground the way
every other Tepenian exile community does. **But** starting a city underground before it exists on the
surface inverts the whole normal order of settlement. **Therefore** Byrd's founding becomes genuinely
unique in Tepenia — a community that built downward first, and only afterward developed anything
resembling a surface presence at all.

## 2. Belgrano's Gift, Byrd's Debt

Reaching Byrd's remote coordinates requires a vehicle capable of crossing unmapped interior terrain no
existing transport was built for. **But** no such vehicle exists yet anywhere in Tepenia. **Therefore**
Belgrano develops the Arrastradoras specifically for this crossing — Tepenia's first native tracked
overland vehicles, purpose-built for exactly this expedition. **But** inventing the vehicle is only
half the problem; someone still has to drive it across ground nobody has mapped. **Therefore** the
expedition succeeds, and centuries later, once Byrd's own fabrication economy matures, the city
becomes one of the primary sites still building and maintaining that same vehicle lineage — the
machine that found Byrd eventually becoming one of the things Byrd itself manufactures.

## 3. What the Robots Found Alone

The crossing to Byrd's coordinates is judged too dangerous to risk human lives on, given how little is
actually known about the route or what waits at the end of it. **But** the expedition still has to
happen if Byrd is ever going to be found at all. **Therefore** an all-robot crew drives the
Arrastradoras across the unmapped interior alone, accepting a risk no human crew would be sent to
take. **But** succeeding means the earliest, most formative memory of Byrd's own founding belongs
entirely to robots, with no human witness present for the actual discovery. **Therefore** Byrd's
founding story carries a genuine, distinctive weight from the start — a city whose very first moment
of existence was experienced only by the population that would go on to help build it.

## 4. Chambers From a Distant Schematic

Byrd's growing fabrication economy has real industrial capacity, but no particular reason yet to
specialize toward anything as demanding as robot creation itself. **But** Tepenia's whole
robot-creation infrastructure needs more manufacturing sites capable of producing fabrication-synthesis
chambers than currently exist, and Neumayer, across the continent in the Halley subnet, has already
designed the current-generation schematic. **Therefore** Byrd takes up chamber manufacturing directly
from that distant design, becoming one of the small number of active sites building the actual
apparatus that creates new robots nationwide. **But** manufacturing something this consequential
demands a level of precision ordinary industrial work doesn't require. **Therefore** the Chamber Works
becomes a specifically reinforced, carefully managed section of Byrd's own underground plant — treated
with a seriousness distinct from the rest of the city's industrial output.

## 5. Two Roads Out

Byrd's underground fabrication economy produces real, valuable output, but output nobody can reach
does the rest of Tepenia no good. **But** Byrd's remote interior position leaves it with no easy
natural route to anywhere else. **Therefore** two overland highways get built and maintained
specifically to solve that problem — Hwy 1 running north toward the Peninsula and South America
beyond it, Hwy 22 running east across the entire continent toward the Mirny subnet. **But**
maintaining two continent-spanning routes from one remote interior city takes enormous, continuous
investment. **Therefore** Byrd commits to it anyway, understanding early that its whole relevance to
the rest of the Federation depends entirely on those two roads staying open and functional.

## 6. What Esperanza Sends South

Byrd's underground population grows past what its own resource extraction and fabrication economy can
feed on its own. **But** a single isolated interior settlement has no realistic way to produce enough
dietary variety for a city this size. **Therefore** a real supply relationship forms along Hwy 1's own
northern route — Esperanza's established agricultural tradition sending food and variety south, Byrd
sending manufactured goods and minerals back north in return. **But** that relationship means Byrd's
own food security now depends on a supply line running the full length of the Antarctic Peninsula.
**Therefore** Hwy 1 becomes something Byrd treats as genuinely vital in both directions — not simply an
export route, but the corridor an entire population's dietary variety actually depends on.

## 7. The Fleet That Grounded Itself

Byrd's aviation fleet serves for generations as the city's only real connection to the rest of Tepenia
for people, not just goods. **But** the fleet eventually develops a problem Byrd's own considerable
engineering expertise can diagnose without being able to actually fix. **Therefore** the aircraft go
permanently grounded — not from any lack of skill, but because whatever specific material or component
the repair genuinely requires was never something Byrd could produce locally, and the supply chain that
once provided it has quietly stopped existing. **But** a city this capable at building complex
machinery being unable to fix its own planes reads, to outsiders, as a strange contradiction.
**Therefore** Byrd's engineers know exactly what's wrong and exactly why they can't solve it anyway —
competence intact, the actual problem sitting one step outside anything their own hands can reach.

## 8. St. Richard's Marker

Byrd's original founding expedition makes camp on the surface before anyone knows what actually lies
beneath the snow around them. **But** that first encampment, once the buried station is discovered and
the community moves underground, has no obvious reason to remain significant. **Therefore** residents
mark the site anyway, a modest memorial built where the first arrivals actually stood in genuine
uncertainty, before they knew what they'd find. **But** a site honoring uncertainty rather than
triumph is an unusual thing to commemorate. **Therefore** it becomes exactly that — not a monument to
discovery, but to the moment just before it, when nobody yet knew whether the expedition would
succeed at all.

## 9. Deeper Than Anyone Remembers

Byrd's own underground excavation eventually reaches sections far below anything the exile community
itself ever built. **But** those deepest layers predate not just Tepenia's own arrival, but the exile
era entirely — material from the original station's pre-exile operational period, sealed away long
before anyone now living was born. **Therefore** engineers extending the city's own tunnels
occasionally encounter genuine remnants of Upper Earth's own historical presence in Antarctica, sitting
quietly beneath a community that otherwise has little reason to think about it. **But** those
encounters happen rarely enough that most residents never personally experience one. **Therefore** the
deepest layers stay something closer to quiet civic legend than lived daily fact — real, occasionally
confirmed, mostly just a story residents tell about how much history actually sits beneath their own
feet.

## 10. Five Trades, One City

Byrd's earliest economy could have stayed simple — one or two industries meeting the community's most
immediate needs. **But** a city this isolated needs real self-sufficiency across far more domains than
a well-connected city ever has to manage alone. **Therefore** Byrd's economy grows into something
genuinely integrated — fabrication, freight dispatch, snow and infrastructure management, resource
extraction, and aviation maintenance all developing side by side, each depending on the others to
function. **But** running five distinct, interdependent sectors at once takes far more internal
coordination than any single specialized economy would need. **Therefore** Byrd becomes exactly the
kind of place its own isolation demanded — not one city with one industry, but several industries
learning to run as one continuous system, out of sheer necessity rather than any deliberate plan to
build it that way.

## 11. The Weight of All That Digging

Engineers notice a section of tunnel showing genuine structural strain, a minor but real settling
event — centuries of cumulative excavation finally interacting, visibly, with the West Antarctic Ice
Sheet's own known instability. **But** a structural engineer's push to slow or reassess excavation meets
real resistance from production leadership, afraid of disrupting a five-sector integrated economy that
took centuries to build. **Therefore** the city commits to genuine, ongoing structural monitoring instead
of either halting growth or ignoring the strain — expansion continues, but consciously managed for the
first time. **But** one round of monitoring doesn't settle a geological reality this large.
**Therefore** it becomes permanent — a quiet sixth concern threaded through Byrd's whole integrated
economy, the Ice Sheet's instability settling into a chronic, actively managed condition rather than
either a single crisis or something simply ignored.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#1, #2, and #3** trace Byrd's whole founding arc — the search, the vehicle that made it possible, and
the crew that actually made the crossing — giving the Megasheet's own central founding fact ("built
from below, then left alone") its full dramatized sequence. **#4 and #10** are the most structurally
important for Byrd's later significance, since together they establish both its single most
nationally consequential role (chamber manufacturing) and the broader integrated economy that role
grew out of. **#11** answers the Mega_Init's own still-open Ice Sheet instability question directly,
giving Byrd's underground economy a permanent, managed tension with its own physical foundation.
