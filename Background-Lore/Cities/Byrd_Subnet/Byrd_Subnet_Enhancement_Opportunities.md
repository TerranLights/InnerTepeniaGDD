### Byrd

**Files read:** `Byrd_Catalog.md` (Primary+Significant scope), `Byrd_Mega_Init.md`,
`Byrd_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` (all 10 existing chains). Sole city in its subnet; the final city
in the entire pass.

**Existing 10 chains already cover:** the founding expedition discovering the buried, surface-less
station (#1), Belgrano's Arrastradoras enabling the crossing and Byrd later manufacturing that same
vehicle lineage (#2), the all-robot crew that made the crossing alone (#3), the Chamber Works
manufacturing fabrication-synthesis chambers from Neumayer's schematic (#4), the two overland highways
(Hwy 1, Hwy 22) built to reach the rest of Tepenia (#5), the food-security supply relationship with
Esperanza along Hwy 1 (#6), the aviation fleet grounding itself for a reason Byrd's own engineers
understand but can't fix (#7), St. Richard's Marker honoring the first uncertain encampment (#8), deep
tunnels occasionally surfacing pre-exile Upper Earth remnants (#9), and the five interdependent economic
sectors running as one integrated system (#10). New angles below are ones the existing 10 don't touch.

1. **Division of industry → history.** The Mega_Init explicitly flags the Jamshedpur precedent as "the
   clearest available precedent for pairing Byrd's own established resource-extraction sector directly
   with its fabrication plants — raw material and finished product created in the same place, by design"
   — but chain #10 covers five sectors only as parallel, generically interdependent trades, never
   dramatizing this specific captive-mine-to-mill design. A chain about Byrd's own extraction operation
   being deliberately sited to feed directly into its fabrication plants — echoing Jamshedpur's specific
   model — would use this unused, explicitly flagged precedent.

2. **Resident character cultures.** The Catalog names Japan as holding "the single strongest real-world
   geography match found anywhere in this city's own research" — the *gangi* covered-arcade snow-country
   tradition, "a near-literal precedent" for Byrd's own tunneled understructure. None of the 10 chains
   credit any specific population; "the expedition," "the community," and "residents" all stay
   undifferentiated throughout. A chain crediting Japanese-descended engineers specifically with pioneering
   Byrd's own tunneled-passage design would use this explicitly flagged but unused population-specific
   expertise.

3. **Real-world historical precedent.** The Kiruna parallel is explicitly flagged as giving "direct
   real-world grounding for the West Antarctic Ice Sheet's own established structural instability" — a
   real town currently being relocated due to mining-caused subsidence, including a genuine mining-induced
   earthquake. None of the 10 chains touch ground instability at all; every chain assumes stable
   underground expansion. A chain about Byrd's engineers managing a genuine ground-stability crisis —
   echoing Kiruna's real subsidence precedent — would use this unused, explicitly flagged parallel.

4. **City personality / geography / geology → history.** The Catalog's own Geological composition entry
   notes "some areas volcanically active" within the West Antarctic marine ice sheet, but none of the 10
   chains touch this at all — every chain frames Byrd's geology purely in terms of ice, snow, and burial.
   A chain about Byrd's engineers discovering or managing a specific volcanically-active zone within their
   own excavation would use an entirely unaddressed geological fact, distinct from the ice-focused framing
   every existing chain shares.

5. **Other.** The Mega_Init's own "What's Actually Open" section flags, still unresolved: "Whether any
   overland highway connection was ever confirmed for *people*, as distinct from the freight-only Hwy
   1/Hwy 22 network." Chains #5 and #6 establish the highways as freight corridors, and chain #7 covers
   the aviation fleet's failure (the loss of the *people* connection), but none of the 10 chains directly
   address whether people ever traveled the overland routes before that failure made the question moot. A
   chain establishing (or explicitly ruling out) pre-war personal overland travel via Hwy 1/22 would use
   this explicitly flagged, still-open distinction.
