# Zhongshan — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Zhongshan's Megasheet (the unbroken Chinese
founding identity, the non-prying social contract, the "double life" civic dynamic). Post-settlement
only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. We Kept the Name

Sinian defectors choose exile alongside robots over continued loyalty to their own nation's ideology.
**But** that choice raises an immediate question of identity — do they abandon everything associated
with the nation they're breaking from, or hold onto what they still value while rejecting what drove
them out? **Therefore** Founding Elder Mèi Sun articulates the founding stance directly:
they kept the name, the language, the culture — they did not keep the regime. **But** drawing that
line clearly means constantly explaining the distinction to anyone who assumes rejecting the nation
means rejecting everything Chinese about themselves. **Therefore** "we kept the name; we did not keep
them" becomes Zhongshan's founding political creed, repeated and understood by every generation that
follows.

## 2. The Double Life

Later immigrants — Korean, German, Japanese, Russian, Australian, and more — arrive at Zhongshan to
find a fully-formed civic culture already in place, built entirely around its founding population's
own traditions. **But** those newcomers still carry their own heritage, their own customs, their own
sense of where they came from. **Therefore** a real, lived split develops — public daily rhythm
conducted entirely within Zhongshan's own established framework, personal heritage kept quietly at
home rather than performed publicly. **But** living that split consistently, generation after
generation, takes real discipline nobody has to formally teach. **Therefore** it becomes second
nature instead, absorbed simply by growing up watching how everyone else already does it.

## 3. Nobody Asks

Zhongshan's founding population, forged through the trauma of defection and exile, develops a genuine
aversion to prying into anyone else's personal history. **But** a city eventually filled with
newcomers carrying their own complicated pasts could easily default to curiosity about where everyone
came from and why. **Therefore** the founding generation's own instinct — what happens in your life is
your business, not a topic for casual conversation — hardens into a real, citywide social contract
rather than staying a private habit. **But** maintaining that norm requires everyone, newcomer and
founder alike, to actually hold to it consistently. **Therefore** Zhongshan becomes a city where a
resident can live for years beside someone without knowing basic facts about their past — not
distance, but a specific, shared form of respect.

## 4. Three Rings of Building

Zhongshan's founding generation builds directly onto the original station's own utilitarian
structures, adding only what the earliest exile years actually require. **But** as the population
grows and stabilizes, later generations want architecture that reflects genuine aesthetic refinement,
not just survival. **Therefore** a second layer forms around the founding core — exile-era
construction with real design intention behind it, distinct from the purely functional structures at
the center. **But** even that refined layer eventually runs out of room as the city keeps growing over
roughly two and a half centuries. **Therefore** a third, outer ring develops in a mature architectural idiom all its own,
working with the Larsemann Hills' exposed bedrock rather than against it — three visibly different
eras of building, none of them erasing what came before.

## 5. Táng Wǔ's Hands

Zhongshan's earliest craftspeople work mostly out of practical necessity — furniture, tools, basic
household goods. **But** a population this settled and this culturally continuous eventually wants
craft that reflects genuine skill and pride, not just utility. **Therefore** Táng Wǔ establishes
something more deliberate — a real, recognized artisan tradition applying precision and care to
material culture, treated as a civic institution worth taking seriously rather than mere trade work.
**But** building a tradition like that into something lasting takes more than one skilled craftsman's
own lifetime. **Therefore** the practice outlives him, passed down through successive artisans until
it settles into its own dedicated neighborhood — the Crossing Quarter, where the city's whole
identity of refined, unbroken continuity becomes something visitors can actually see and touch.

## 6. The Long Winter Suite

Zhongshan's classical musical tradition, carried forward on erhu, pipa, and guqin, develops slowly
over generations without ever producing one defining, universally recognized work. **But** a culture
this contemplative and patient eventually produces someone willing to attempt something genuinely
ambitious. **Therefore** Composer Táng Yuxuan writes a multi-movement suite structured around polar
night's full sixty-day arc, from onset to first light — deliberately long, unhurried, demanding real
patience from anyone who wants to actually understand it. **But** a work that demanding risks alienating
listeners looking for something easier to absorb. **Therefore** it becomes exactly the opposite —
the piece residents point to as the truest expression of what a Zhongshan composition is supposed to
be, precisely because it refuses to rush.

## 7. The Quarter With No Name

A Southeast Asian-inflected counterculture takes root in one corner of Zhongshan, louder and more
colorful than the city's own austere mainstream character. **But** Zhongshan's whole civic culture
runs on the same non-prying instinct that governs individual privacy — what happens in your own
neighborhood is your business. **Therefore** the mainstream simply lets the district exist, fully
aware of it and largely undisturbed by it, without ever formalizing it with an official name or
designation. **But** a district this vibrant naturally develops its own informal identity regardless
of official recognition. **Therefore** it becomes known simply as the Loud Quarter, colloquially,
permanently — a name residents use freely even though no civic authority ever bothered to make it
official, since making it official was never really the point.

## 8. What the Ice Gave Up

Zhongshan's coastal position on Prydz Bay offers real, if seasonal, access to krill and Antarctic
silverfish. **But** the bay freezes solid for much of the year, closing off the easy summer harvesting
window for months at a stretch. **Therefore** residents develop two parallel methods — net fishing
during the brief ice-free summer, ice-hole fishing through the winter — keeping the catch coming
year-round rather than accepting a long seasonal gap. **But** two different methods produce genuinely
different results, different textures, different volumes. **Therefore** Zhongshan's whole fermentation
and preservation culture grows up around reconciling the two, turning an inconsistent seasonal catch
into a steady, reliable staple regardless of which method produced it.

## 9. The Chronicler Nobody Named

Someone in Zhongshan begins writing, capturing the city's own daily rhythm and sensibility in a form
residents recognize immediately as genuinely theirs. **But** Zhongshan's whole culture treats personal
history as private, something not to be pried into even for someone whose work becomes genuinely
important. **Therefore** the writer's own identity never becomes public knowledge in any deliberate
way — known perhaps to a few, unconfirmed to everyone else, the work read and valued entirely apart
from who actually wrote it. **But** a work this significant to the city's own self-image would
normally invite exactly the kind of curiosity Zhongshan's culture resists. **Therefore** residents
settle into treating the writing itself as what matters, deliberately declining to chase down the
Chronicler's identity — consistent with everything else the city already believes about privacy.

## 10. Stone at the Center

Early construction at Zhongshan organizes itself around a genuine exposed bedrock outcrop, too solid
and too prominent to simply build over. **But** a landmark that just sits there, unused, is a wasted
piece of the city's own geography. **Therefore** residents start gathering near it informally, at
first for no particular reason beyond its convenient, central location. **But** informal gathering
spots either take on real meaning over time or quietly fade from use. **Therefore** the Standing Stone
becomes something closer to genuine civic ground — no formal designation, no official purpose, just
generations of residents who've kept meeting there long enough that its significance no longer needs
explaining to anyone who already lives in the city.

## 11. The City That Didn't Ask

Ji-Eun Kim arrives in Zhongshan from Sejong, her nanotech skill immediately obvious to the city's
technical community, and a workshop lead genuinely wants to bring her into the formal scientific
establishment. **But** Ji-Eun's own instinct is to work around institutions rather than through them,
and even a culture built on privacy can't fully resolve what formal integration would risk exposing.
**Therefore** the offer gets made once, clearly, and then simply left to stand — consistent with
everything Zhongshan already believes about not pressing further. **But** declining formal standing
doesn't mean declining the work itself. **Therefore** Ji-Eun does real, significant technical work in
the city anyway, entirely on her own terms, respected for what she produces without anyone needing the
rest of her story to respect it.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#1 and #3** are the most load-bearing — together they trace Zhongshan's whole civic character back
to the same founding-generation trauma, one producing a political creed, the other a lasting social
norm, both still governing the city centuries later. **#5 and #7** work as a natural pair, since both
dramatize the same underlying non-prying instinct expressed at civic scale — once toward a craft
tradition worth showcasing, once toward a district worth simply leaving alone. **#11** is the only chain
built around an already-confirmed, named companion character rather than a placeholder — giving Ji-Eun
Kim's own established Zhongshan chapter a dramatized shape consistent with her existing backstory.
