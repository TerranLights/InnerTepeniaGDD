## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Later immigrants — Korean, German, Japanese, Russian, Australian, and more — arrive at Zhongshan to find
a fully-formed civic culture already in place, built entirely around its founding population's own
traditions, unbroken since the city's earliest years.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Those newcomers still carry their own heritage, their own customs, their own sense of where they came
from — arriving into an already-settled civic framework doesn't erase what they brought with them, it
just leaves no obvious public place for it. A real, lived split develops — public daily rhythm conducted
entirely within Zhongshan's own established framework, personal heritage kept quietly at home rather than
performed publicly. But living that split consistently, generation after generation, takes real
discipline nobody has to formally teach — and at least one newer arrival, in the earliest years of this
adjustment, struggles openly and visibly with the split, unable or unwilling to simply compartmentalize
their own heritage the way the unwritten civic expectation seems to require.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a newer immigrant who wants
to express their own heritage more openly in Zhongshan's public life — a festival, a public custom, some
visible marker of where they came from — and a longer-established resident who, without hostility,
explains that Zhongshan's own civic culture simply doesn't work that way, that the established public
framework isn't a rule imposed on newcomers specifically but the same rhythm everyone, including the
founding population's own descendants, already lives inside. Neither side is wrong: A's desire for
visibility is entirely reasonable, and B's explanation reflects a real, already-existing civic norm that
predates A's own arrival and wasn't built to exclude anyone in particular.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a newly-arrived
immigrant — Korean, German, Japanese, Russian, or Australian, any would work — genuinely missing more
open, public expression of their own heritage and unfamiliar yet with Zhongshan's particular civic
rhythm. Character B is plausibly a longer-established resident, possibly one of the founding-generation's
own descendants, patiently explaining a norm they've simply always lived inside rather than one they
personally invented or enforce.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A doesn't get a public platform, but does find something workable: a private, home-based space to
practice their own heritage fully, exactly as the established civic pattern already allows for everyone
else. It becomes second nature instead, absorbed simply by growing up watching how everyone else already
does it — A settles into the same split B has always lived inside, not through instruction so much as
through lived example.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because A's own adjustment happened through direct, personal example from B rather than through any
formal onboarding process, Zhongshan never develops an explicit, written explanation of the "double life"
norm for newcomers — the practice keeps transmitting the same way it always has, resident to resident,
by lived demonstration rather than formal instruction.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the norm stays entirely informal and person-to-person rather than ever being codified, it remains
genuinely flexible in practice — individual residents can quietly negotiate their own private balance
between public rhythm and personal heritage without ever running up against a written rule, giving
Zhongshan's "double life" dynamic real, lived variation beneath its outwardly uniform public surface,
invisible to anyone who only ever sees the shared public rhythm itself.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Zhongshan's double life continues exactly as it always has — an entirely informal, unwritten norm passed
resident to resident by lived example, uniform in its public face while quietly allowing real individual
variation in how each resident actually lives it out at home.
