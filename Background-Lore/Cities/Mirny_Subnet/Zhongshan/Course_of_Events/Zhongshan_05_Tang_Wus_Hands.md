## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Zhongshan's earliest craftspeople work mostly out of practical necessity — furniture, tools, basic
household goods, produced for function rather than any particular pride in the making.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A population this settled and this culturally continuous eventually wants craft that reflects genuine
skill and pride, not just utility — the same civic identity that produces Zhongshan's whole "unbroken
refinement" character starts pushing against purely functional craftwork too. Táng Wǔ establishes
something more deliberate — a real, recognized artisan tradition applying precision and care to material
culture, treated as a civic institution worth taking seriously rather than mere trade work. But building a
tradition like that into something lasting takes more than one skilled craftsman's own lifetime — and
before Táng Wǔ can establish any lasting institution, he has to actually convince the wider community that
investing scarce time and material into refined craftwork, rather than more of the purely practical goods
the city has always needed, is worth the tradeoff.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A compromise emerges: Táng Wǔ's refined tradition gets formally established, but initially as a
part-time, secondary practice — craftspeople splitting their time between practical goods and refined
work rather than the community committing to full-time artisans immediately. The practice outlives him,
passed down through successive artisans until it settles into its own dedicated neighborhood — the
Crossing Quarter, where the city's whole identity of refined, unbroken continuity becomes something
visitors can actually see and touch.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the tradition began as a part-time practice rather than a full institutional commitment from the
start, its eventual growth into a dedicated neighborhood happened gradually, tracking the settlement's own
increasing material security — the Crossing Quarter's emergence becomes a genuine, readable indicator of
Zhongshan's own economic development over generations, rather than an arbitrary founding-era decision.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the Quarter's growth tracked the city's own material development so closely, later generations of
Zhongshan historians and residents come to read the Crossing Quarter's own expansion, era by era, as a
kind of informal economic history of the city itself — each new workshop or expansion roughly datable to
a period of genuine civic prosperity, giving the neighborhood a secondary function as an accidental,
physical record of Zhongshan's own economic story.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The Crossing Quarter stands as both Zhongshan's living artisan tradition and an accidental, physical
record of the city's own economic history — each expansion tracing back to Táng Wǔ's original, carefully
negotiated compromise between aesthetic ambition and practical necessity.
