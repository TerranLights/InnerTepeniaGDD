## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Zhongshan's coastal position on Prydz Bay offers real, if seasonal, access to krill and Antarctic
silverfish — a genuine dietary resource, but one the earliest fishing practice hasn't yet fully learned to
use across the whole year.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The bay freezes solid for much of the year, closing off the easy summer harvesting window for months at a
stretch — a structural limitation the earliest fishing practice, built around simple net fishing, has no
answer for once winter sets in. Residents develop two parallel methods — net fishing during the brief
ice-free summer, ice-hole fishing through the winter — keeping the catch coming year-round rather than
accepting a long seasonal gap. But two different methods produce genuinely different results, different
textures, different volumes — and once both methods are established, a real disagreement emerges among
Zhongshan's own food preparers over how to actually reconcile catches that don't behave the same way in
storage, preservation, or cooking, since treating them identically produces inconsistent, sometimes
disappointing results.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation, more culinary than personal, between
a preparer who wants to standardize preservation technique across both summer and winter catches for
consistency's sake, and another who insists the two catches genuinely require different treatment and that
forcing uniformity produces worse results than simply developing separate methods for each. This mirrors,
in miniature, the same "chaos that is actually structure" quality already established in Zhongshan's own

about whether consistency or quality should take priority.

household cook favoring a single, standardized preservation process for administrative or practical
simplicity. Character B is plausibly another preparer, more attentive to the actual differences between

genuinely need distinct handling.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's argument wins out through simple demonstrated results — dishes prepared with method-specific handling
consistently taste better than the standardized approach, and the standardized method gets quietly
abandoned. Zhongshan's whole fermentation and preservation culture grows up around reconciling the two,
turning an inconsistent seasonal catch into a steady, reliable staple regardless of which method produced
it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because method-specific handling won out on demonstrated merit rather than administrative convenience,
Zhongshan's food culture develops genuine technical specialization within its own preservation practice —
distinct, named techniques for summer-caught and winter-caught fish, each passed down and refined
separately rather than treated as interchangeable variations on one process.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because those two distinct techniques exist and keep developing separately, Zhongshan's own culinary
identity ends up with twice the technical depth a single, standardized method would ever have produced —
the same "chaos that is actually structure" quality that defines the rest of the city's cuisine, now
extended specifically into how the sea's own seasonal inconsistency gets turned into two genuinely
different, equally respected culinary traditions rather than one compromise.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Zhongshan's maritime food culture now runs on two distinct, technically developed preservation
traditions — one for each season's catch — a direct, lasting result of a preparer's insistence that
quality mattered more than administrative simplicity.
