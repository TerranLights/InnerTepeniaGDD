## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Zhongshan's founding population, forged through the trauma of defection and exile, develops a genuine
aversion to prying into anyone else's personal history — an instinct born directly from lived experience,
not yet a formal civic expectation.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city eventually filled with newcomers carrying their own complicated pasts could easily default to
curiosity about where everyone came from and why — ordinary human sociability tends toward exactly that
kind of question. The founding generation's own instinct — what happens in your life is your business, not
a topic for casual conversation — hardens into a real, citywide social contract rather than staying a
private habit. But maintaining that norm requires everyone, newcomer and founder alike, to actually hold
to it consistently — and the first real test comes when a well-meaning but simply curious newcomer, with
no awareness yet of the unwritten norm, asks a founding-generation resident directly about their own
defection, touching a wound the norm exists specifically to protect.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a quiet but genuinely painful confrontation between
a newcomer whose question comes from ordinary, well-intentioned curiosity rather than malice, and a
founding-generation resident whose defection carries real, unhealed trauma the question inadvertently
reopens. Neither character is at fault — A had no way of knowing the norm existed yet, and B's pain is
real regardless of A's intent — which is exactly what makes this the moment the norm needs to actually be
explained rather than simply assumed as common knowledge.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a recent immigrant,
genuinely unaware that a seemingly ordinary question about someone's past carries weight it wouldn't
carry almost anywhere else in Tepenia. Character B is plausibly a founding-generation defector, or their
close descendant carrying the story secondhand, for whom the question — however innocently asked — lands
as a real intrusion into something they've never been ready to discuss openly.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The moment doesn't end in lasting rupture — a third party, or B themselves once the initial hurt passes,
explains the norm directly to A, turning a painful misstep into the newcomer's actual introduction to how
Zhongshan works. Zhongshan becomes a city where a resident can live for years beside someone without
knowing basic facts about their past — not distance, but a specific, shared form of respect.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because this kind of painful misstep recurs often enough with each new wave of immigration, Zhongshan's
longer-established residents develop an informal but real practice of explaining the non-prying norm to
newcomers proactively, before a similar incident happens — not a formal rule, but a habit of quietly
briefing new arrivals early, precisely to spare both the newcomer and any founding-generation resident the
same unnecessary pain.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that proactive briefing becomes habitual, newcomers to Zhongshan increasingly arrive already
understanding the city's non-prying culture before they ever have the chance to violate it unknowingly —
meaning the norm, originally established and reinforced through painful individual incidents, gradually
transmits instead through deliberate, protective teaching, sparing later generations of both founders and
newcomers the same wound.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Zhongshan's non-prying social contract now transmits through active, protective teaching rather than
painful individual lessons — newcomers arrive pre-briefed, sparing both sides the kind of unintentional
wound that first made the norm's importance so clear.
