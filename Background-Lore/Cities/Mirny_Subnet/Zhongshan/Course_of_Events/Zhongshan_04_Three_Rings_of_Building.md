## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Zhongshan's founding generation builds directly onto the original station's own utilitarian structures,
adding only what the earliest exile years actually require — pure function, nothing built for its own
sake.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

As the population grows and stabilizes, later generations want architecture that reflects genuine
aesthetic refinement, not just survival — the founding generation's own children and grandchildren, no
longer facing the same immediate survival pressure, start wanting their city to actually look like
something rather than merely function. A second layer forms around the founding core — exile-era
construction with real design intention behind it, distinct from the purely functional structures at the
center. But building something aesthetically ambitious around a core built purely for utility means the
new generation's own architects have to decide how to relate the new work to the old — matching it,
contrasting it, or simply ignoring it — and that decision itself becomes a real point of disagreement
once actual construction plans are put forward.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither vision fully wins — the actual construction that proceeds keeps the founding core structurally
and visually distinct, exactly as B argued, but the new layer's own design language deliberately echoes
specific details (proportions, material choices) from the original structures, a compromise closer to A's
instinct for continuity. A second layer forms around the founding core — exile-era construction with real
design intention behind it, distinct from the purely functional structures at the center, but never
erasing what came before.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the compromise established both visual distinction and deliberate echo as the guiding design
principle, it becomes the standing architectural convention for the city going forward — any future
construction is expected to be honestly, visibly new while still carrying some legible relationship back
to what came before it, rather than either hiding its era or ignoring its predecessors entirely.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that convention holds as later generations keep building, even that refined layer eventually runs
out of room as the city keeps growing over 130 years — a third, outer ring develops in a mature
architectural idiom all its own, working with the Larsemann Hills' exposed bedrock rather than against
it, following the same established convention: visibly its own era, while still carrying legible
continuity back through the refined layer to the founding core beneath it.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Zhongshan now stands as three visibly different eras of building — utilitarian core, refined middle ring,
mature bedrock-integrated outer ring — none of them erasing what came before, each one honestly its own
era while carrying deliberate, legible continuity back through everything built before it.
