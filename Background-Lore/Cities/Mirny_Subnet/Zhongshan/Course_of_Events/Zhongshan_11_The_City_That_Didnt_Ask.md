# Zhongshan — The City That Didn't Ask
### Featuring Ji-Eun Kim and [Character B] (Ji-Eun confirmed; her counterpart left blank)

**Course of Events Suggestion #11**, translated from `Zhongshan_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` into
`z-template_-_city_histories_conflict_variant.md`. Grounded in Ji-Eun Kim's own confirmed, already-
established backstory (`Worldspace/Characters/Dolls/Still-Present_-_In-Game/recruitable/Ji-Eun Kim/README.md`): after
her first landing at Sejong, she relocated to Zhongshan, "a deeply Chinese city on Prydz Bay whose
cultural insularity and serious technical character suited her," and spent significant time there before
eventually moving to Concordia. This chain stays strictly within that already-established window — her
time in Zhongshan, not her departure or anything downstream of it — and does not invent any detail about
her Concordia arc, the concealment technology, or the person it was built for, all of which remain
outside this chain's scope. Character B is intentionally left blank — see the Conflict section's Design
Notes for what kind of character would make the following setting-condition become true.

---

## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Zhongshan's own non-prying social contract (chain #3) is fully in place — a resident can live for years
beside someone without knowing basic facts about their past, not out of distance but as a specific,
shared form of respect. The city's own technical/scientific sector runs deep and serious, a natural home
for anyone whose real value lies entirely in the work itself.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Ji-Eun Kim arrives from Sejong, carrying an exile's guardedness and a level of nanotech skill immediately
obvious to anyone who sees her work — and Zhongshan's own technical community takes real notice almost at
once.

---

## Conflict
###### Ji-Eun vs. [Character B] — role/stakes, not a name, for now.

**Character A (confirmed):** Ji-Eun Kim.

**Character B:** *(left blank)*

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a conflict about whether Ji-Eun's ability is
real — it plainly is. Ji-Eun's own established instinct is to work around institutions rather than
through them, having learned they will not protect her; formal integration into any technical body, even
inside a culture built on privacy, risks a kind of visibility she isn't willing to accept. Character B,
a Zhongshan technical institution or workshop lead, genuinely wants to bring her obvious talent into the
city's own formal scientific community — not suspicious of her, just unable to understand why someone
this capable would want to stay outside the structure that could give her real standing.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character B is plausibly a senior researcher or
technical administrator, sincere and unthreatened, whose own instinct — shaped by Zhongshan's culture —
is to extend a genuine offer rather than press for an explanation when it's declined.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Character B extends the offer once, clearly, and then simply lets it stand without pressing further —
consistent with everything Zhongshan's own culture already believes about privacy. Ji-Eun does
significant technical work in the city anyway, informally, on her own terms, never joining any formal
institution, respected for what she produces without ever being asked to explain herself.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Zhongshan gives Ji-Eun something genuinely rare — real technical belonging without exposure, a place
where her work speaks for her completely and nobody needs the rest of the story to respect it.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

The experience reinforces, at a formative moment, an instinct Ji-Eun already carries — that not needing
to explain herself is itself a kind of safety — the same underlying pattern chain #9's anonymous
Chronicler already models for the city more broadly, now lived out by one specific, confirmed resident
rather than described only as a general civic tendency.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Ji-Eun's time in Zhongshan continues on exactly these terms — genuinely valued, entirely on her own
terms, never pressed — for as long as she stays. What happens after she eventually leaves for Concordia
is already established elsewhere and stays outside this chain entirely.
