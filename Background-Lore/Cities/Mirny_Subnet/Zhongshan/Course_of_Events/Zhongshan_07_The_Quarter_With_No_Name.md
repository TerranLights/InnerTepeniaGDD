## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

A Southeast Asian-inflected counterculture takes root in one corner of Zhongshan, louder and more
colorful than the city's own austere mainstream character — a real, growing scene, distinct from the rest
of the city's established civic tone.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Zhongshan's whole civic culture runs on the same non-prying instinct that governs individual privacy —
what happens in your own neighborhood is your business — but a district this visible and this different
from the mainstream doesn't simply escape notice; it gets *seen* clearly, and someone within Zhongshan's
civic administration eventually has to actually decide, formally, how the city should respond to a
counterculture district that's grown large enough to be genuinely impossible to ignore. The mainstream
simply lets the district exist, fully aware of it and largely undisturbed by it, without ever formalizing
it with an official name or designation. But that "simply letting it exist" outcome isn't automatic — some
civic voices push, not out of hostility, for at least some administrative acknowledgment or oversight,
arguing that total non-engagement with a district this size is neglect rather than respect.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's argument prevails — no formal designation, no official name, no dedicated oversight body. The mainstream
simply lets the district exist, fully aware of it and largely undisturbed by it. A district this vibrant
naturally develops its own informal identity regardless of official recognition — it becomes known simply
as the Loud Quarter, colloquially, permanently, a name residents use freely even though no civic authority
ever bothered to make it official.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the district's own name emerged colloquially rather than through any formal process, its
boundaries stay genuinely fluid and informally understood rather than officially mapped — residents know
roughly where the Loud Quarter is and isn't without any civic document ever having drawn a formal line,
the same informal-but-real character that defines the rest of Zhongshan's own unwritten social contracts.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the district's boundaries were never formally fixed, it stays free to organically expand or
contract with its own community's actual growth over subsequent generations, rather than being constrained
by an administrative line drawn once and never revisited — giving the Loud Quarter a genuine, lasting
flexibility that a formally designated district, with fixed boundaries, would never have retained.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The Loud Quarter remains exactly what its own name suggests — informal, unofficial, and genuinely alive,
its boundaries as fluid as the community that fills them, protected rather than administered by the same
civic instinct that governs everything else Zhongshan chooses not to formally name.
