# Shirayuki — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Shirayuki's Megasheet (the diplomatically
allocated founding, the homemade Alt-Rock/Alt-Grunge/Alt-Pop fashion culture, "A Place Decided For You,
Made Into a Place You'd Choose"). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Decided at Jeju-do

Japan, Korea, and China need to settle who claims the unoccupied Bharati site before the exile era
ever begins, since leaving it uncontested risks China's own regional dominance defaulting into a third
uncontested claim. **But** a three-way negotiation this consequential could easily become contentious.
**Therefore** Ambassador Reiko Tashiro secures the allocation for Japan through careful, low-friction
diplomacy — China's own claim to its adjacent site was never actually at risk, meaning the concession
costs relatively little in real terms. **But** securing a claim through negotiation isn't the same as
having anyone actually living there yet. **Therefore** the allocation sits settled on paper for years
before a single Japanese exile ever sets foot on the site it was decided for.

## 2. A Place Decided For You

Japanese exiles arrive at the newly-allocated site to find a claim already secured, a decision already
made entirely without their input. **But** inheriting a diplomatically-settled destination is a
strange way to begin a new life — chosen for you, not by you. **Therefore** the earliest years carry a
genuine, if quiet, adjustment period: residents settling into infrastructure and a claim negotiated by
people who never had to live with the consequences themselves. **But** living somewhere long enough
changes your relationship to how you got there. **Therefore** what starts as an imposed circumstance
gradually becomes something residents actually inhabit on their own terms — the first step in the
city's whole long arc from decided-for to chosen.

## 3. Labs Into Galleries

The inherited Bharati Station infrastructure is built entirely for scientific research — labs,
dormitories, support buildings, nothing designed with art or culture in mind. **But** the growing
population wants more from its home than pure research function can provide. **Therefore** residents
adapt the buildings gradually rather than replacing them — former labs becoming gallery and studio
space, dormitory blocks repurposed toward the school system that will eventually draw students from
across Tepenia. **But** adaptive reuse means working around a building's original purpose rather than
designing fresh. **Therefore** Shirayuki's architecture ends up genuinely layered — research-station
bones doing an arts city's work, the throughline between its founding infrastructure and its present
character never actually broken.

## 4. Ashiato

Shirayuki's homemade fashion culture grows scattered and informal for a generation — individual
residents altering and personalizing their own clothes, with no shared name or identity connecting
them. **But** a scene this vibrant, spread across enough of the city, eventually starts to feel like
something more than isolated individual choices. **Therefore** Momoka Ishihara's grassroots collective
gives it a name — Ashiato, "footprint," a trail personally left rather than a look bought ready-made —
turning a loose aesthetic into something recognizable and namable. **But** naming a movement changes
how people relate to it, turning private expression into something closer to shared identity.
**Therefore** Ashiato becomes Shirayuki's own defining visual signature, deliberately imperfect
stitching and worn-in textures worn as marks of authenticity rather than flaws to hide.

## 5. Glacier-pop

Shirayuki's massive amateur music scene draws on softer, melodic Japanese folk instrumentation from
its founding population. **But** the Larsemann Hills cluster's own broader multinational influences —
Korean, Chinese, Russian, Australian — surround the city on every side, impossible to ignore entirely.
**Therefore** a genuine musical fusion emerges, blending the founding folk tradition with the
subnet's wider influences into something new, performed mostly in the same informal, friends-and-
neighbors spaces the city's whole social culture already runs on. **But** a fusion this eclectic risks
losing any distinct identity of its own. **Therefore** it doesn't — Glacier-pop becomes recognizably
Shirayuki's own sound precisely because of how naturally it blends, never quite reducible to any one
of its component influences.

## 6. Three Neighbors, Slow Warming

Shirayuki, Zhongshan, and the neighboring Korean-founded city arrive at adjacent sites through related
but distinct mechanisms — Zhongshan's organic proximity, Shirayuki's and its neighbor's shared
diplomatic engineering. **But** sharing a diplomatic history doesn't automatically make three separate
populations feel like neighbors in daily practice. **Therefore** the relationship between them starts
genuinely more formal and distant than their eventual closeness — cordial contact between three
communities who technically arrived together but haven't yet actually lived beside each other.
**But** proximity this close, sustained over generations, is hard to keep purely formal forever.
**Therefore** the three communities warm gradually into the closely-entangled cluster identity later
generations simply take for granted — legal separation slowly giving way to genuine, lived closeness.

## 7. Why Everyone Wants to Come Here

Shirayuki's schools earn a real reputation for genuine educational quality early in the city's
history. **But** academic reputation alone doesn't explain why students keep choosing Shirayuki over
comparably good schools elsewhere. **Therefore** something else takes hold alongside it — students
drawn by the schools stay on afterward, making art and music independent of their formal studies,
building exactly the amateur creative culture that then draws the *next* wave of students for the
culture itself, not the ranking. **But** a loop like that only keeps working if enough people actually
choose to stay each generation. **Therefore** Shirayuki's whole "people come for the soul of the
place" reputation becomes genuinely self-sustaining — built, quietly, by generations of students who
arrived for one reason and ended up staying for another.

## 8. Clean Streets, Shared Pride

Shirayuki's earliest civic planners could have treated street maintenance as purely functional
upkeep, nothing more. **But** a population working to make an imposed home genuinely their own finds
real meaning in visible, collective care for shared space. **Therefore** cleanliness becomes something
closer to civic pride than mere maintenance — streets kept genuinely, deliberately orderly, not out
of obligation but because residents actually want their city to look cared for. **But** sustaining
that standard requires everyone's ongoing buy-in, not just a few dedicated planners. **Therefore** it
becomes something residents actively teach newcomers, generation after generation, until visible pride
in shared space is simply what living in Shirayuki means.

## 9. Nobody's a Stranger Long

Shirayuki's population grows from students, artists, and researchers arriving from across Tepenia, few
of them knowing anyone when they first arrive. **But** a city that wants to feel like a genuine home
rather than a waypoint needs newcomers to actually integrate quickly. **Therefore** the city's whole
social culture develops around making that easy — genuine, uncomplicated friendliness that doesn't
require months of proving yourself before anyone actually opens up. **But** a culture this welcoming
could easily stay shallow, friendly on the surface without real depth underneath. **Therefore** it
doesn't — the same ease that makes new friendships easy to start also makes them easy to deepen,
consistent with a whole city built around residents choosing to be here rather than simply enduring it.

## 10. The Trail You Leave

Shirayuki's whole founding story is one of arrival into something already decided — a place, a name
for the site, a claim settled by other people before anyone who'd actually live there was consulted.
**But** residents eventually want something that expresses the opposite: a mark that's unmistakably
theirs, made rather than simply inherited. **Therefore** Ashiato's whole philosophy — a trail
personally left, imperfection worn as authenticity — becomes something bigger than a fashion movement,
read by residents as the city's own answer to its founding tension. **But** a philosophy like that only
means something if it's actually lived, not just worn. **Therefore** "leaving your own footprint"
becomes Shirayuki's real, felt civic value — the place decided for them, finally, visibly, made into
somewhere they chose.

## 11. One of the Ones Who Left

A young resident, raised fully inside Shirayuki's own culture, reaches the point most residents either
settle permanently or move on — and finds themselves genuinely unsure which way they're going. **But**
someone close to them has built their own life around staying, and finds the idea of leaving hard to
understand, not out of possessiveness but because staying was how they expressed their own choosing of
the city. **Therefore** a real, quiet conflict opens between them — not about whether Shirayuki is worth
loving, but about what leaving would actually mean. **But** the city's own founding tension was never
really about staying versus leaving — it was always about choosing, deliberately, rather than simply
drifting. **Therefore** the resident leaves anyway, consciously, framed as their own footprint left
behind rather than a rejection — and the one who stays comes to recognize it as genuinely consistent with
everything Shirayuki itself stands for.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#4 and #10** work as a pair — the concrete founding of the Ashiato fashion movement, and the deeper
civic meaning it comes to carry, giving the Megasheet's own central theme ("A Place Decided For You,
Made Into a Place You'd Choose") a direct, tangible expression rather than an abstract description.
**#7** is the most structurally important thread, since it dramatizes the self-reinforcing schools-to-
culture loop the Megasheet's own Cross-Reference Synthesis flags as a real but unconfirmed mechanism.
**#11** extends the city's own founding theme to its most personal scale yet — one resident's own choice
to leave, framed by the same "chosen, not simply inherited" logic that defines Shirayuki as a whole.
