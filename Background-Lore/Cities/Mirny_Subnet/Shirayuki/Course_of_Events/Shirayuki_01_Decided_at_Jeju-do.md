## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Japan, Korea, and China need to settle who claims the unoccupied Bharati site before the exile era ever
begins, since leaving it uncontested risks China's own regional dominance defaulting into a third
uncontested claim across the whole Larsemann Hills cluster.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A three-way negotiation this consequential could easily become contentious — three delegations, one
unclaimed site, and a regional power already positioned to simply absorb it by default if nobody actively
intervenes. Ambassador Reiko Tashiro moves to secure the allocation for Japan through careful, low-friction
diplomacy, working the negotiation privately rather than letting it become a public standoff. But
low-friction doesn't mean uncontested — China's own claim to its adjacent site was never actually at
risk in this negotiation, which gives China's delegation little material reason to fight hard, but it
does mean someone at the table has to be persuaded to actively give up a demographic opportunity they
could otherwise simply have let happen by inaction, and persuading someone to relinquish a passive
advantage is a different, subtler task than winning an active dispute.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a negotiation-table confrontation between Ambassador
Tashiro, working to secure the allocation actively rather than let it default, and a Chinese delegate who
has genuinely little to lose from simply staying quiet and letting China's own regional weight settle the
question without any deliberate action on their part. This isn't a fight over a contested resource — it's
a subtler negotiation about whether inaction itself counts as a legitimate claim, and Tashiro has to make
the case that an active, formal allocation serves everyone's longer-term interest better than an
uncontested default would, without ever framing it as taking something away from China specifically.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is, per established canon,
Ambassador Reiko Tashiro, the Japanese diplomat credited with securing the allocation. Character B is
plausibly a Chinese delegate at the Jeju-do court — not hostile, not obstructive, simply someone with no
strong personal incentive to actively concede anything, whose eventual cooperation has to be earned
through Tashiro's own patient diplomatic case-making rather than through any real confrontation.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Tashiro's case succeeds — the Chinese delegate agrees to the formal allocation rather than letting the
site default, and the claim is secured for Japan through careful negotiation rather than contest. But
securing a claim through negotiation isn't the same as having anyone actually living there yet — the
allocation sits settled on paper for years before a single Japanese exile ever sets foot on the site it
was decided for.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the allocation exists on paper years before any exile population arrives to occupy it, the site
sits formally claimed but physically untended — no maintenance, no habitation, no active use of whatever
existing station infrastructure sits there, for however many years pass between the diplomatic agreement
and the first actual arrivals.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That gap between paper claim and physical occupation becomes, decades later, a genuine point of local
historical curiosity once Shirayuki's own civic identity solidifies — residents researching the city's
founding discover the strange fact that "their" city legally existed, named and claimed, for years before
a single person who would actually build it had arrived, giving the later-developed "A Place Decided For
You" theme a concrete, documented historical detail to point to rather than remaining purely abstract
sentiment.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Japan's claim to the site stands secured, on paper, years ahead of any actual settlement — a legally
real city with no population yet, waiting for the exiles who will eventually arrive to find a decision
already made, entirely without their input, the true starting point of Shirayuki's whole founding story.
