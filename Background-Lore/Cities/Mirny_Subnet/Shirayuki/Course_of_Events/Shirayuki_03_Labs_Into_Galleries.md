## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The inherited Bharati Station infrastructure is built entirely for scientific research — labs,
dormitories, support buildings, nothing designed with art or culture in mind, functional architecture
serving a mission the founding Japanese population never chose.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The growing population wants more from its home than pure research function can provide — a city can't
sustain itself on laboratory space alone once the people actually living there start wanting places to
make art, teach children, and simply gather. Residents begin adapting the buildings gradually rather than
replacing them — former labs becoming gallery and studio space, dormitory blocks repurposed toward the
school system that will eventually draw students from across Tepenia. But adaptive reuse means working
around a building's original purpose rather than designing fresh — a lab converted into a gallery still
has a lab's own ventilation systems, fixed benches, and awkward sightlines, and someone actually doing the
conversion work has to decide how far to fight the building's original design versus simply working
within its limitations.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone converting a
specific former lab space into a gallery who wants to gut it fully — stripping out the fixed benches and
ventilation infrastructure to create an open, purpose-built art space — and someone who argues for working
within the building's existing bones, treating the lab's own leftover fixtures as texture rather than
obstacle. This is a practical, almost aesthetic disagreement rather than a moral one: A wants the finished
space to serve art fully and unapologetically; B wants the finished space to visibly carry its own
research-station history rather than erase it.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a visual artist or
gallery organizer, frustrated by having to design around a building that was never meant to hold what
they want to put in it. Character B is plausibly another artist, or possibly an early city planner with
some architectural sensibility, who sees genuine creative and historical value in letting the space's
research-station origin remain visible rather than scrubbed away.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's approach prevails for this specific conversion, and becomes the template followed afterward: fixed
benches get repurposed as display plinths, old ventilation conduits stay exposed rather than concealed,
and the finished gallery space reads unmistakably as a former lab doing new work rather than a lab
disguised as something else. Shirayuki's architecture ends up genuinely layered — research-station bones
doing an arts city's work, the throughline between its founding infrastructure and its present character
never actually broken.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the "keep the bones visible" approach becomes the template rather than a one-off choice, every
subsequent lab-to-gallery, dormitory-to-school conversion across the city follows the same principle —
adaptive reuse as a deliberate, citywide architectural philosophy rather than a series of unrelated
individual decisions made building by building.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that philosophy becomes citywide and consistent, Shirayuki's built environment develops a
genuinely recognizable visual signature — visitors and residents alike can identify a converted research
building on sight, by its particular blend of old scientific infrastructure and new creative or
educational use, giving the city an architectural identity distinct from anywhere else in Tepenia that
inherited research-station infrastructure and simply demolished or fully modernized it instead.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Shirayuki's whole built environment now carries a consistent, citywide architectural signature — former
labs, dormitories, and support buildings converted according to the same "bones stay visible" principle,
making the city's research-station origin and its present arts-and-education character legible together,
in the same buildings, rather than one having replaced the other.
