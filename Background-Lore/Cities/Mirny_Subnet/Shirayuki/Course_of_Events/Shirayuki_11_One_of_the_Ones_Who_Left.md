# Shirayuki — One of the Ones Who Left

## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

A young resident grows up fully immersed in Shirayuki's own established rhythm — homemade Ashiato
fashion, Glacier-pop drifting out of informal friends-and-neighbors spaces, clean streets kept out of
genuine shared pride, the same uncomplicated ease at making friends the whole city runs on. Like every
resident here, they've absorbed the city's own founding tension without necessarily naming it: a place
decided for other people, generations ago, that somehow still has to be actively chosen by everyone
living in it now.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

As they reach the point most residents either settle into Shirayuki permanently or move on — school
finishing, a path forward needing to be chosen — they find themselves genuinely unsure which way they're
going, in a way the city's own comfortable "stayed for the culture, not the ranking" story never quite
prepared them for.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a conflict about whether Shirayuki is worth
loving — both sides agree it is. Character A is the young resident themselves, drawn toward something
beyond the city without being able to fully articulate why, uneasy at the thought of leaving a place that
took generations to become genuinely chosen rather than simply inherited. Character B — a mentor, a close
friend, a parent — has built their own life fully around staying, and finds the idea of leaving
genuinely hard to understand, not out of possessiveness, but because staying is how their own version of
"choosing" Shirayuki was expressed.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a student or young
artist near the end of their formal schooling, competent and well-liked, without yet having had to make a
real, consequential choice about their own future. Character B is plausibly an older resident — a teacher,
an Ashiato-scene elder, a parent — whose own life stands as quiet, lived proof that staying can be a real,
active choice rather than a default.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The resident leaves — deliberately, consciously, framed explicitly (by themselves, and eventually by
Character B) as their own footprint left behind rather than a rejection of what Shirayuki gave them.
Character B doesn't get the outcome they hoped for, but comes to recognize the choice as genuinely
consistent with everything the city itself stands for.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Shirayuki loses another promising young resident to somewhere else in Tepenia — but per its own
established civic philosophy, this isn't treated as failure. Leaving, chosen deliberately rather than
simply drifted into, is understood as compatible with having genuinely chosen the place for however long
they were actually in it.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Whoever stays behind — friends, family, the wider Ashiato-scene community — carries a full, specific,
textured understanding of exactly what Shirayuki gave this one resident growing up: a real formative
childhood and adolescence, wherever they end up and whatever they eventually become known for, if
anything.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Shirayuki holds space for both those who stay and those who leave, its own founding tension — decided
for you, made into a place you'd choose — extended into something more mature: even leaving, done
deliberately, can be its own act of ownership rather than a rejection. Somewhere out there, one more
resident carries a full, lived Shirayuki upbringing with them, wherever the rest of their story goes.
