## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Shirayuki's earliest civic planners could have treated street maintenance as purely functional upkeep,
nothing more — a practical necessity, handled the way any city handles basic sanitation, with no
particular civic weight attached to it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A population working to make an imposed home genuinely their own finds real meaning in visible,
collective care for shared space — cleanliness becomes something closer to civic pride than mere
maintenance, streets kept genuinely, deliberately orderly, not out of obligation but because residents
actually want their city to look cared for. But sustaining that standard requires everyone's ongoing
buy-in, not just a few dedicated planners — and as the population grows beyond the founding generation who
first felt that meaning most directly, later arrivals who never personally experienced the "imposed home"
tension have less obvious, automatic reason to invest the same effort, and someone has to actually decide
whether to let the standard slip or find a way to pass the meaning behind it forward, not just the habit.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a newer resident or younger
generation member who maintains the city's cleanliness out of simple habit or social expectation, without
feeling any particular emotional weight behind it, and an older resident who worries that cleanliness as
mere habit, disconnected from the meaning that originally produced it, will eventually erode once habit
alone isn't enough to sustain it through a harder period. This isn't a conflict about whether cleanliness
matters — both agree it does — it's about whether unexplained tradition is actually durable, or whether
it needs its own origin story actively retold to survive.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a younger resident or
recent arrival, conscientious and cooperative but relating to the cleanliness standard as simply "how
things are done here" rather than as something with deeper civic meaning. Character B is plausibly an
older resident, possibly one who remembers or was raised on stories of the founding generation's own
adjustment period, worried that the practice is becoming hollow even as it remains, for now, perfectly
intact.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's concern prompts an actual, deliberate response rather than staying an abstract worry — older
residents start actively teaching newer ones not just the practice of civic cleanliness but the reasoning
behind it, folding the story of the city's own imposed founding directly into how the standard gets
passed on. It becomes something residents actively teach newcomers, generation after generation, until
visible pride in shared space is simply what living in Shirayuki means.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the practice now comes bundled with its own explicit origin story rather than being taught as
pure habit, newcomers arriving in Shirayuki learn the city's founding tension — imposed home, made chosen
— as one of the very first things anyone tells them, well before they'd otherwise encounter it through
formal history or civic education.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the founding story now travels alongside something as immediate and physically visible as street
cleanliness, it stays alive as lived, felt knowledge across generations rather than fading into the kind
of abstract historical fact most residents of most cities eventually stop actively thinking about — every
resident, simply by participating in the city's most ordinary daily habit, keeps re-encountering the
reason it started in the first place.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Shirayuki's clean streets now carry their own explicit origin story, passed to every newcomer alongside
the practice itself — civic pride and civic memory bound together so tightly that maintaining one keeps
the other alive, generation after generation, without either ever depending on unexamined habit alone.
