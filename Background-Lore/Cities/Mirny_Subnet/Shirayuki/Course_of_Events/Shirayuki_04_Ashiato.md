## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Shirayuki's homemade fashion culture grows scattered and informal for a generation — individual residents
altering and personalizing their own clothes, with no shared name or identity connecting them, each
person's own private practice.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A scene this vibrant, spread across enough of the city, eventually starts to feel like something more
than isolated individual choices — enough residents notice enough of each other's work that a genuine
sense of shared aesthetic starts forming, even without anyone yet naming it. Momoka Ishihara's grassroots
collective moves to give it a name — Ashiato, "footprint," a trail personally left rather than a look
bought ready-made — turning a loose aesthetic into something recognizable and namable. But naming a
movement changes how people relate to it, turning private expression into something closer to shared
identity — and not every individual maker who had been quietly doing their own thing wants their personal
practice folded into a named collective identity they didn't choose to join, even one built from
something they themselves helped create simply by making their own clothes.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between Momoka Ishihara, pushing to
formally name and organize the scene into a recognized collective identity, and an individual maker whose
own personal, private practice predates the collective and who resists being retroactively categorized
as part of a "movement" they never signed up to represent. This isn't hostility toward Ishihara's project
— the individual maker can genuinely respect what she's building — it's a question of consent: does
making clothes in a recognizable style automatically enroll you in whatever name gets attached to that
style later, or does naming a scene require the individual participants' own buy-in first.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is, per established canon, Momoka
Ishihara, proprietor of the influential grassroots collective credited with naming Ashiato. Character B
is plausibly one of the scene's earlier, more private practitioners — someone whose personal style
predates Ishihara's own organizing efforts, uneasy about a name and a collective identity being applied
to work they always considered simply their own.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Ishihara doesn't insist — the name stays, but its actual use becomes voluntary rather than automatic:
Ashiato as an identity anyone can claim for their own work, not a label retroactively applied to anyone
whose style happens to fit. Ashiato becomes Shirayuki's own defining visual signature, deliberately
imperfect stitching and worn-in textures worn as marks of authenticity rather than flaws to hide — but
worn by choice, by makers who actually claim the name for themselves.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because claiming the Ashiato name stays a voluntary, individual choice rather than an automatic
categorization, the movement develops a genuine internal culture of self-identification — makers actively
describe their own work as "Ashiato" or not, a small but real act of self-definition built into the
movement's own practice from its earliest formal days.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That culture of voluntary self-identification becomes the reason Ashiato never calcifies into a rigid,
gatekept style with fixed rules — since nobody can be told they're "doing it wrong" without first having
claimed the name for themselves, the movement stays genuinely open to reinterpretation generation after
generation, each new wave of makers free to define what claiming the name actually means for their own
work.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Ashiato stands as Shirayuki's defining fashion movement — not a fixed style anyone can be excluded from
or forced into, but a name makers choose to claim for their own work, staying genuinely open and
reinterpretable precisely because Ishihara never insisted otherwise when the name was first proposed.
