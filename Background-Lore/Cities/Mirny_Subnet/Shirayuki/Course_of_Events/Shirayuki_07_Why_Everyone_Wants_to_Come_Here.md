## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Shirayuki's schools earn a real reputation for genuine educational quality early in the city's history —
a straightforward, unremarkable fact about academic standards, nothing yet distinguishing it from any
other city with strong schools.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Academic reputation alone doesn't explain why students keep choosing Shirayuki over comparably good
schools elsewhere — something else takes hold alongside it. Students drawn by the schools start staying
on afterward, making art and music independent of their formal studies, building exactly the amateur
creative culture that then draws the *next* wave of students for the culture itself, not the ranking. But
a loop like that only keeps working if enough people actually choose to stay each generation — and at
some point, as Shirayuki's schools grow more prestigious and start drawing more ambitious, career-focused
students from further afield, the loop's own continued strength genuinely depends on whether those more
career-minded arrivals still choose to stay and build culture the way earlier generations did, or whether
they simply take the credential and leave.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a school administrator or
civic figure who wants to actively market Shirayuki's academic prestige to attract more high-achieving
applicants from across Tepenia, and a longtime resident or fellow educator who worries that leaning too
hard into prestige-driven recruitment will bring in students who came purely for the credential and have
no intention of staying to build anything afterward — quietly undermining the very loop that made the
schools worth attending for their culture in the first place. Both are trying to protect Shirayuki's
future; they simply disagree about what actually threatens it.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a school administrator
focused on institutional growth and reputation, genuinely proud of what the schools have achieved and
eager to expand their reach. Character B is plausibly a longtime resident, possibly a former student who
stayed and became part of the amateur creative scene themselves, whose entire understanding of what makes
Shirayuki special is bound up in the retention the loop depends on, not the admissions numbers alone.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A middle path emerges from the disagreement rather than either side simply winning: the schools keep
marketing academic quality, but deliberately fold the city's own creative culture directly into how they
present themselves to prospective students — recruiting materials that sell the "soul of the place"
alongside the credential, rather than the credential alone. Shirayuki's whole "people come for the soul
of the place" reputation becomes genuinely self-sustaining, built quietly by generations of students who
arrived for one reason and ended up staying for another.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the recruiting materials now explicitly foreground the amateur creative culture, prospective
students increasingly arrive already knowing about and interested in that culture specifically, rather
than discovering it only after enrollment — the schools' own admissions messaging becomes, in effect, the
first stage of the retention loop itself, screening in advance for exactly the kind of student most
likely to stay.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because incoming students now arrive pre-selected for interest in the creative culture, the amateur scene
itself grows faster and more confidently than it would have under pure organic discovery — new arrivals
integrate into existing creative circles more quickly, since they came looking for exactly that, further
strengthening the very reputation that drew them there in a tightening, self-reinforcing cycle.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Shirayuki's schools now recruit as much on culture as on credential, deliberately closing the loop that
once depended on chance retention alone — students arrive already primed to stay and build, making the
"soul of the place" reputation not just self-sustaining but actively, deliberately reinforced by the
institutions responsible for bringing new students in.
