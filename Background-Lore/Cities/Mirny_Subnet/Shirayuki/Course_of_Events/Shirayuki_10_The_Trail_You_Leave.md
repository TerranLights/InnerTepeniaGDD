## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Shirayuki's whole founding story is one of arrival into something already decided — a place, a name for
the site, a claim settled by other people before anyone who'd actually live there was consulted, still
felt as a real, if by now distant, civic memory.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Residents eventually want something that expresses the opposite of that inherited, decided-for origin: a
mark that's unmistakably theirs, made rather than simply inherited. Ashiato's whole philosophy — a trail
personally left, imperfection worn as authenticity — becomes something bigger than a fashion movement,
read by residents as the city's own answer to its founding tension. But a philosophy like that only means
something if it's actually lived, not just worn — and at some point, someone has to test whether "leaving
your own footprint" is a genuine, actionable civic value or merely a slogan borrowed from a clothing
scene, applied to something it was never really built to carry.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a civic figure who wants to
formally elevate "leaving your own footprint" into an official civic motto or stated value — something
taught, printed, made part of how the city presents itself — and someone from within the Ashiato scene
itself who worries that formalizing a philosophy born from informal fashion-making will hollow it out the
same way naming often risks hollowing out anything organic. This mirrors, at a civic scale, the exact
tension the Ashiato movement itself once navigated over whether claiming the name should be automatic or
voluntary — now playing out over whether the *idea* should become official doctrine or stay something
residents arrive at on their own.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic administrator
or educator, drawn to the phrase's genuine resonance and wanting to give newcomers something concrete and
memorable to understand the city by. Character B is plausibly a working Ashiato maker or member of
Ishihara's original collective, protective of the idea's informal, lived origin and wary of it becoming
civic branding rather than something residents actually have to discover and choose for themselves.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's caution shapes the outcome without blocking it outright: the phrase never becomes an official motto,
printed or mandated, but it does become something every longtime resident eventually tells every
newcomer, unofficially and personally, exactly the way the founding cleanliness practice and the Ashiato
name itself were both once passed on. "Leaving your own footprint" becomes Shirayuki's real, felt civic
value — the place decided for them, finally, visibly, made into somewhere they chose — precisely because
nobody ever forced it into an official slogan.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the value stays unofficial and personally transmitted rather than institutionally declared, its
exact meaning stays genuinely contested and personal from resident to resident — no single, authorized
definition of what "leaving your own footprint" actually requires, which means every resident who
encounters the phrase has to work out, for themselves, what their own footprint actually looks like.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the phrase's meaning stays genuinely open rather than fixed, it becomes something residents can
apply far beyond fashion — to their choice of career, their relationships, their own small civic
contributions — since nobody defined its boundaries narrowly enough to keep it confined to Ashiato alone.
The idea outgrows its own origin, exactly the way the founding tension it answers once outgrew the single
diplomatic decision that started it.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Shirayuki now carries "leaving your own footprint" as a genuinely lived, unofficial civic value — passed
resident to resident rather than declared from above, its meaning still open enough that every generation
gets to work out, for itself, what actually counts as making an inherited place genuinely one's own.
