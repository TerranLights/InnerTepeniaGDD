## What Tashiro Actually Traded For at Jeju-do

## The Collective That Gave a Look a Name

**"Momoka Ishihara" *(placeholder, not developer-chosen)***, proprietor of Shirayuki's most influential
grassroots fashion collective, is credited with turning scattered homemade Alt-Rock/Alt-Grunge/Alt-Pop looks
into "Ashiato" — a recognized local movement, distinct from mere individual style.

Before Ishihara's own intervention, per what's preserved of the city's own fashion history, hand-altered
garments — deliberately imperfect stitching, mismatched layering, worn-in textures worn as a mark of
authenticity — existed across the city as individual, uncoordinated personal choices rather than anything
resembling a shared movement. Ishihara's own collective began deliberately documenting and cross-referencing
these individual looks, per what's remembered of the effort, treating scattered personal style as a genuine
aesthetic tradition worth naming and articulating rather than simply letting it remain informal. The name
"Ashiato" — footprint, a trail personally left rather than a look bought ready-made — emerged from that
documentation work specifically, capturing what Ishihara's own collective had identified as the style's
actual unifying principle. The named movement that resulted gave individual residents' own previously
uncoordinated choices a shared identity they could recognize themselves as already part of.

## The Lab That Became a Gallery First

The real Bharati Station's own modern research infrastructure was adapted gradually by the founding Japanese
community rather than replaced — former research labs converted into gallery and studio space, dormitory
blocks repurposed toward the school system Shirayuki eventually became known for nationwide.

One specific former laboratory, per what's preserved of the founding-era conversion record, was the first
research space formally repurposed into gallery use — a pilot conversion undertaken before the wider,
facility-spanning adaptation that eventually gave the city its established "clean, orderly, art-filled"
architecture. The conversion required genuine structural modification, since the lab's own research-grade
environmental controls and fixed equipment mounts weren't designed for displaying or creating visual work.
Founding-era residents who championed the conversion, per what's remembered of the decision, argued the
gesture mattered beyond the practical gallery space it created — proof, early and visible, that the founding
community intended to make the inherited research infrastructure genuinely theirs rather than simply
occupying it unchanged. The pilot's own success is credited, per what's established about the city's own
subsequent development, with setting the precedent every later lab-to-studio conversion across the city
followed.

## The Night Glacier-Pop Actually Became a Genre

"Glacier-pop," Shirayuki's own musical fusion blending Japanese folk instrumentation with the subnet's broader
multinational influences, emerged from the city's massive amateur scene, performed primarily in informal,
friends-and-neighbors spaces rather than dedicated concert venues.

One specific informal gathering, per what's preserved of the scene's own early history, is remembered as the
moment the loosely related sound scattered across the city's amateur scene actually crystallized into
something musicians themselves began treating as a shared genre. Several performers, each independently
blending folk instrumentation with different national influences already present in the city's own
multinational population, ended up playing consecutive informal sets at the same gathering — and audience
members present, per what's remembered of the evening, began explicitly discussing the sets as variations on
the same underlying sound rather than as unrelated performances. Musicians who'd previously considered their
own work simply personal experimentation began, afterward, deliberately identifying it as part of the same
emerging tradition — the specific evening the scattered fusion work actually became a genre performers
recognized themselves as contributing to together.

Dormitory blocks inherited from the Bharati Station were repurposed toward the school system that would
eventually draw students nationwide — a gradual transition, not an immediate one, from housing built for a
small research staff to genuine educational infrastructure serving population well beyond Shirayuki itself.

The school's own first cohort of students arriving from outside the city, per what's preserved of the

rather than turning the students away, improvised additional accommodation within the existing repurposed
buildings, a genuinely makeshift arrangement that nonetheless held through that first cohort's own enrollment
without incident. The successful, if improvised, accommodation of that first outside cohort became the
concrete proof that justified further, more deliberate expansion of the school's own capacity — the

improvised accommodation for students nobody had originally planned space for.

## Batch 2 — Shortly Before and After the Amundsen Tower's Completion

### The Gallery That Hosted People Who'd Never Meant to Stop

*(Shortly before the Tower's completion.)* Shirayuki's own converted gallery spaces, built from the same
former Bharati Station research labs the founding community had repurposed generations earlier, hosted an
unusually steady stream of travelers late in the Tower's construction — crews rotating through the Larsemann
Hills cluster on their way toward or away from the project, drawn in by the same clean, art-filled downtown
that already drew visitors from across the subnet (`Shirayuki_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section IV).

Gallery staff, per what's remembered of the period, noted the visitors' own distinctive exhaustion without
needing to ask where they'd come from — travelers who lingered longer than an ordinary passerby might, in
rooms built for exactly the kind of unhurried looking Shirayuki's whole civic identity already prized. Nothing
about the galleries themselves changed to accommodate the traffic. The city's own established gift for making
strangers feel unhurried simply did, for a season, more of what it had always done.

### The Enrollment the School Wasn't Expecting From That Direction

*(Shortly before the Tower's completion.)* Shirayuki's school system, already drawing students from across the
subnet since the improvised first outside cohort generations earlier, received a specific, unusual wave of
enrollment inquiries late in the Tower's construction — families relocating temporarily to be nearer relatives
working the project, seeking schooling for children who'd otherwise have gone without formal enrollment during
the move (`Shirayuki_Full_Extrapolation.md`, school material).

The administration, per what's recalled of the response, handled the inquiries the same improvised way the
very first outside cohort had once been accommodated — makeshift arrangements within existing capacity rather
than turning families away for arriving outside the ordinary enrollment cycle. Most of the students left again
once their own families' circumstances changed. The precedent for accommodating irregular, short-notice
enrollment, already established once, simply held under a new kind of irregular circumstance nobody had
specifically anticipated.

### The Collection Ashiato Never Formally Named

*(Shortly before the Tower's completion.)* A specific run of garments within the city's own Ashiato tradition —

### The First Broadcast Glacier-Pop Ever Received Back

*(Shortly after the Tower's completion.)* Glacier-pop, performed as it always had been in informal, friends-

Musicians who'd built the genre out of scattered personal experimentation, per what's recalled of the reaction,

audience considerably larger than the room.

### The Trail Ashiato Left Somewhere It Had Never Walked

*(Shortly after the Tower's completion.)* The Ashiato fashion tradition — hand-altered garments worn as a
personally-left trail rather than a look bought ready-made — reached, for the first time, residents of cities

Ishihara's own collective, per what's remembered of the response, didn't treat the outside attention as reason
