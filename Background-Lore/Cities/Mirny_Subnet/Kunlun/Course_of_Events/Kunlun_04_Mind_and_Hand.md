## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Kunlun's earliest residents could easily have treated the astronomy and comms work as simply a job —
demanding, technically serious, but ultimately just labor performed for pay or purpose, the way most
Tepenian cities relate to their own defining industry.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A population this specifically self-selected — drawn here by genuine interest in the work itself, not
economic necessity, not inherited obligation, not a national claim someone else made for them — brings
something different to how they talk about what they do. The work gets reframed, gradually and
collectively, as identity rather than labor: nobody at Kunlun describes what they do as "a job." But a
reframing like that needs to be lived consistently, across real hardship and real tedium, to actually
take hold as genuine civic character rather than empty rhetoric repeated because it sounds good — a
population can *say* the work is who they are and still, under enough strain, start treating it like
ordinary labor the moment it stops being convenient to believe otherwise.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation that tests whether "the work is who
we are" actually holds up under real pressure, rather than simply asserting that it does. The most honest
version of this conflict pits someone who wants Kunlun to formally acknowledge that the work is,
functionally, still labor — deserving of shift limits, compensation structures, or some other ordinary
protection — against someone who insists that treating it that way would betray the entire reason
anyone came to Kunlun in the first place. Neither side is wrong: A is defending something real (burnout,
overwork, and exhaustion don't stop being real just because the labor is also loved), and B is defending
something equally real (a population that came here specifically to escape "just a job" thinking has a
legitimate stake in not quietly reintroducing it through the back door of good intentions).

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a newer arrival, or a
figure responsible for the wellbeing of the wider population, who has watched genuine exhaustion get
waved off as "not a big deal, it's not like it's a job" one too many times. Character B is plausibly one
of Kunlun's own founding-generation specialists, or someone who has internalized the city's ethos most
completely, for whom formal labor protections read as a philosophical retreat rather than a practical
kindness — a first crack in the very identity the population built itself around.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The dispute forces the ethos to actually be articulated out loud for the first time, rather than simply
assumed — and once stated plainly, it survives the test. It becomes genuine, load-bearing philosophy over
generations: dignity found through the work itself, not despite it, closer to a creed than a passing
attitude, with real safeguards against exhaustion built in without ever conceding that the work is
merely labor.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the ethos had to be defended explicitly rather than left as an unspoken assumption, Kunlun ends
up with something most Tepenian cities never bother to produce: an actual stated civic creed, articulated
in specific enough terms that it can be taught to newcomers rather than simply absorbed by osmosis over
years of living there.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the creed now exists in explicit, teachable form, it becomes part of how Kunlun evaluates
candidates during its own credentialing and vetting process — someone can hold every technical
qualification the observatory requires and still be judged, formally, on whether they can actually live
inside the stated philosophy, not merely perform the work it describes.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Kunlun now has a genuine, articulated civic creed — dignity through the work, not despite it — taught
deliberately to every new arrival, and used, alongside technical credential, as part of what determines
who actually gets to stay. What began as an unspoken cultural drift is now a philosophy the city can
recite, defend, and select for.
