## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Kunlun's observatory begins as a single instrument, adequate for its founding-era mission — modest in
scale, built to the specification of whichever founding specialists first staffed it, with no reason yet
to think of itself as anything but complete.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Successive waves of newly-arrived specialists, each brought in through Kunlun's own credentialed
recruitment and each carrying a different national instrumentation tradition with them, keep finding the
existing setup too limited for what they actually want to do — a detector built to one nation's own
standard doesn't interface cleanly with a technique developed under another's, and the single original
instrument can't accommodate every incoming tradition at once. A genuine expansion phase takes hold
early, coinciding with the population's own convergence into a real multinational specialist community.
But even that expanded facility eventually strains against Tepenia's own growing orbital-era
communications needs — the astronomy-first expansion never accounted for a comms-relay load that didn't
exist yet when it was designed, and someone now has to decide whether to expand the facility a second
time, on a completely different technical basis than the first.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone who oversaw or
benefited from the first expansion and wants the second one to follow the same basic model — organic,
specialist-driven, shaped by whoever's technical tradition happens to be arriving at the time — and
someone who recognizes that a comms-relay expansion serving the whole Federation's orbital infrastructure
needs a more deliberate, centrally-planned approach than the first, more improvised expansion had. This
is a conflict about institutional memory versus institutional growth: A trusts the process that worked
once already; B argues the stakes and scale have genuinely changed.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a veteran astronomer
who lived through, or was shaped by, Kunlun's first expansion phase, and who has real institutional
standing built on the success of that earlier, organically-driven model. Character B is plausibly the
same figure credited elsewhere with the later orbital-era relay expansion — meaning this cycle can
function as the internal argument that precedes and sets up that later, better-documented achievement,
giving it earned context rather than treating it as arriving fully formed.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The second expansion goes forward on the more deliberate, centrally-planned basis B argued for — but
built, at A's own insistence, with the same openness to incoming specialist traditions that made the
first expansion work, rather than a rigid, top-down blueprint that leaves no room for what future arrivals
might bring. The result is a facility that grew in two distinct, datable stages, each shaped by a
different logic, rather than one static instrument quietly accumulating readings across five centuries.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the second expansion deliberately preserved room for future specialist input rather than locking
the facility into a fixed final design, Kunlun's observatory now has a standing, physical expectation of
periodic future expansion built into its own architecture — allocated but unbuilt space, reserved
specifically for whatever the next wave of arriving expertise turns out to need.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That reserved, unbuilt space becomes a genuine point of pride and speculation within Kunlun's own civic
culture over the following generations — residents and new arrivals alike treat the empty allocation as
proof the observatory's story isn't finished, informally debating what the next expansion might actually
need to accommodate long before there's any concrete plan for it.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Kunlun's observatory now carries two completed expansion phases and a permanent, deliberately unbuilt
reserve for a third — an institution that has learned, through its own two-centuries-apart growth, to
plan for the specialist it hasn't recruited yet rather than assuming its current shape is its final one.
