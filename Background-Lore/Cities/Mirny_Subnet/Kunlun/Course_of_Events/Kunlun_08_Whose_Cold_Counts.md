## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Kunlun's higher elevation plausibly makes its actual conditions colder than Vostok's own historically
verified record — a matter of physical likelihood everyone on both sides quietly accepts, without it
ever having been formally tested against Vostok's own claim.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Kunlun has never had a confirmed surface measurement the way Vostok's record was actually recorded and
instrumentally verified at a functioning station, decades earlier, under conditions nobody at either
outpost can now fully replicate. The two cities end up in a real, if good-natured, standoff — Vostok
holding the official record, Kunlun holding the raw physical claim nobody can quite confirm. But a
standoff like that doesn't stay purely abstract forever in a population this small and this
achievement-minded — sooner or later, someone at Kunlun proposes actually settling it, installing the
instrumentation and running the verification Vostok's own record already had, and someone at Vostok has
to decide whether backing that verification effort serves their own city's interest, or risks handing
away the one outright title Vostok has always held.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** neither side has any real interest in escalating
this into something adversarial, so the conflict needs to stay genuinely good-natured even while it's
real — a confrontation between someone at Kunlun who wants the ambiguity resolved, one way or the other,
simply to know the truth, and someone at Vostok who has real, legitimate reason to prefer the ambiguity
stay exactly as it is, since Vostok's own record is the one thing about the city that isn't inherited,
disputed, or diluted by its own demographic history. This works best played as mutual, fond rivalry
rather than genuine hostility — both characters can like and respect each other and still each want their
own city's claim to hold.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Kunlun instrument
technician or junior astronomer, proud of the observatory's own physical extremity and genuinely curious
whether the numbers would actually bear it out. Character B is plausibly a Vostok researcher or
administrator — someone who, given Vostok's own established founding-legend tension (a population that
inherited a legacy it never built), has real personal reason to want at least one thing about the city
to remain uncomplicatedly, verifiably Vostok's own.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The verification effort happens — Kunlun installs the instrumentation, and Vostok's own researchers,
rather than blocking it, insist on being directly involved in confirming the methodology themselves,
trusting their own scrutiny more than an outright refusal to participate. The result settles nothing
outright: Kunlun's readings run colder in raw terms but fall short of a clean, uncontested surface
verification under Vostok's own original recording standard. The rivalry stays exactly what it's always
been — a genuine, mutual point of pride between two isolated outposts that otherwise have every reason to
think of themselves as kin.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because Vostok's own researchers were directly involved in the verification attempt rather than staying
outside it, the two outposts now have a jointly-authored, jointly-signed technical record of the whole
episode — the first formal scientific document either city has ever co-produced with the other, filed in
both archives.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That co-authored precedent — two outposts jointly running and signing off on a shared measurement effort —
becomes the template Kunlun and Vostok's researchers later reach for whenever a genuine joint scientific
question comes up between them, rather than treating each new collaboration as a novel arrangement to be
negotiated from scratch each time.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The cold-record standoff between Kunlun and Vostok remains formally unresolved, exactly as fondly
contested as it's always been — but the two outposts now have a standing, working model for how to run
joint research together, born directly from a rivalry neither side actually wanted to end.
