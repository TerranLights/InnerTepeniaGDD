## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Kunlun's early observatory serves astronomy first, with comms relay work handled as a secondary
function — a smaller installation, staffed lightly, adequate for whatever modest orbital traffic exists
at the time but never treated as a program in its own right.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

As Tepenia's own orbital-era presence grows, the demand for genuine, reliable deep-space communications
capacity grows well past what a secondary function can support — orbital assets now depend on Kunlun's
relay in ways the original, lightly-staffed installation was never designed to bear. Chief Astronomer
Renata Okonkwo-Sato pushes for a real, dedicated expansion of the comms-relay side of the facility,
scaling it to match the astronomy program rather than leaving it permanently subordinate. But that
expansion means real investment and real reorganization of a facility that's worked one way for
generations — reallocating funding, staff, and physical space away from the astronomy program that has
always been Kunlun's primary identity, toward a relay function most of the observatory's own veteran
staff still think of as secondary.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone protecting the
observatory's founding identity — astronomy first, always — and someone arguing that the facility's
actual usefulness to the wider Federation has quietly outgrown that identity. This isn't a conflict
about competence; both sides can be excellent at their own work. It's a conflict about what Kunlun is
actually *for*, going forward — a research institution that happens to also relay signals, or dual-purpose
infrastructure the whole orbital-era Federation now genuinely depends on. A's position defends continuity
and institutional identity; B's position defends the observatory's real, growing usefulness against
sentiment about what it used to be.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a senior astronomer,
possibly one of the observatory's founding-era veterans, who has built their whole career around the
primacy of the astronomy program and reasonably fears that a comms-relay expansion will permanently
dilute Kunlun's core scientific mission. Character B is, per established canon, Chief Astronomer Renata
Okonkwo-Sato herself, pushing the expansion through — meaning this cycle can plausibly be written as an
internal argument within the observatory's own leadership rather than an outside imposition, giving
Renata's push real, earned resistance to overcome rather than an uncontested administrative decision.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Renata's push succeeds. The comms-relay expansion is approved and built out to genuinely match the
astronomy program's own scale — a real turning point in Kunlun's institutional history, the observatory
growing into something serving two equally serious functions rather than one primary mission with a
minor addition.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

With comms-relay capacity now genuinely scaled to orbital-era demand, Kunlun's relay has enough spare,
reliable bandwidth for the first time to carry more than emergency or priority orbital traffic — including
routine requests from other extreme-isolation research outposts that previously had no reliable channel
at all.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That newly available spare capacity is what actually makes a sustained, practical resource-sharing
relationship with Vostok possible for the first time — equipment requests, reagents, and findings neither
extreme outpost can produce alone, now routed reliably through Kunlun's own expanded relay. The "two
loneliest outposts" relationship could not have existed in anything like its later, mutually-dependent
form before this specific expansion — it is a direct, dated consequence of Renata's own institutional win,
not an unrelated, separately-arising friendship.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Kunlun's observatory now runs two equally serious programs, astronomy and comms relay, and its expanded
relay capacity has become the literal infrastructure underwriting its whole relationship with Vostok — a
partnership built on spare bandwidth that didn't exist before Renata's own push succeeded, now treated
as simply how things have always worked.
