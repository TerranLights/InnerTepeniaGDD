## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Individual devotees at Kunlun each mark polar night's perfect calm privately, in their own way, with no
shared practice connecting them — the same devotion, observed alone, scattered across however many
devotees happen to be present in any given generation.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A community this small and this isolated has real reason to want something communal rather than purely
individual — private devotion, however sincere, offers no shared moment, no sense of practicing alongside
anyone else, in a population that already has almost nothing else resembling collective civic ritual.
Brother/Sister Ilkay begins working to formalize the observance, turning private stillness into something
the devotional population marks together, deliberately, as a shared civic occasion. But formalizing a
practice this personal risks losing exactly what made it meaningful to begin with — devotees who have
spent years perfecting their own private relationship to polar night's calm have real reason to worry
that turning it into a scheduled, shared event will flatten something that was never supposed to be
performed for or alongside anyone else.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone pushing to formalize
the observance into a shared communal ritual, and someone whose own private practice is genuinely,
legitimately threatened by that formalization — not because they're opposed to community, but because
they've built something real and personal that a shared version might not preserve. This shouldn't read
as reformer-versus-traditionalist; both sides value the same devotion, they simply disagree about whether
sharing it strengthens or dilutes it, and there's no way to test that disagreement except by actually
trying.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is, per established canon,
Brother/Sister Ilkay, the figure most associated with pushing to formalize the observance. Character B is
plausibly one of Kunlun's longest-practicing devotees — someone whose private polar-night ritual predates
Ilkay's own arrival, and who has genuine standing, earned through years of solitary practice, to question
whether Ilkay's proposed shared version can actually hold the same meaning, or whether it will simply
become a scheduled event people attend rather than a devotion people live.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Ilkay doesn't override the objection — the formalized version that actually gets adopted stays
deliberately minimal, shaped directly by the skeptical devotee's own input: structured enough to share,
unstructured enough that it never stops feeling like genuine individual devotion happening in the same
room at the same time, rather than a performed communal service.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the formalized observance was built through direct negotiation with its own strongest skeptic
rather than imposed over their objection, it carries real, lasting legitimacy among Kunlun's devotees in
a way a top-down version likely wouldn't have — devotees who might otherwise have stayed away, suspicious
of a "performed" version of something private, choose to participate instead, since the version that
exists is one they can recognize as still genuinely theirs.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because near-universal devotee participation becomes the norm rather than a hoped-for outcome, the shared
observance grows large and visible enough, generation after generation, that it starts drawing quiet
attention from Kunlun's scientifically self-selected residents too — not converts, but curious onlookers
and, occasionally, genuine participants, since a practice built to preserve rather than flatten individual
meaning turns out to be legible, and inviting, to people outside the devotional community as well.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Kunlun's polar-night observance is now a genuinely shared civic occasion, built carefully enough at its
founding to preserve what made the private version meaningful, and open enough in practice that it has
begun quietly drawing in residents who were never devotees at all — the rare moment on Kunlun's calendar
where the specialist and the devotional halves of the population share the same room for the same reason.
