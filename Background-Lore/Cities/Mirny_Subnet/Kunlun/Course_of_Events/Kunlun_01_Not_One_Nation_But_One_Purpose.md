## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Kunlun's earliest framing assumes one founding nation will simply define the whole city, the way nearly
every other Tepenian settlement works — an exile population from a single homeland, inheriting a single
station, carrying a single flag forward into however many centuries follow.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

An observatory and communications facility at this elevation, in this degree of isolation, turns out to
need expertise no single nation's own space program can fully supply on its own — deep-space tracking,
orbital telemetry, and comms-relay engineering are each their own narrow specialty, and no one exile
population happens to already contain all of them in sufficient depth. The population that actually
settles Kunlun starts re-resolving around function instead of origin: astronomers, comms engineers, and
specialists drawn specifically from whichever nations can show a real, verifiable credential — a national
space agency, a major observatory, a deep-space communications facility, genuine rocket heritage —
however many nations that ends up being, regardless of how the site was originally allocated on paper.
But a city recruited this way has no single dominant culture to organize around once the specialists
actually arrive — no shared founding language, no shared national holiday calendar, no inherited civic
mythology anyone can simply default to.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone charged with
administering Kunlun's founding the way every other Tepenian exile settlement gets administered — one
sponsor population, one national claim, one predictable bureaucratic template — and someone who
understands, from direct technical necessity, that the observatory's mission cannot actually be staffed
that way. This shouldn't read as a narrow bureaucrat versus a visionary scientist; A's position is
genuinely reasonable on its own terms, since the single-nation template is what the rest of the exile
administration already knows how to run, and departing from it means building an entirely new process
with no guarantee it holds together. B isn't simply "right" in some abstract sense either — B is making
a real gamble that a curated, function-first draw can actually cohere into a functioning settlement
rather than a fractured one, since nothing quite like it has been tried anywhere else in Tepenia.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Federation
resettlement administrator dispatched to establish Kunlun through the ordinary channel — someone whose
entire professional competence is built around the standard single-nation exile procedure, with genuine
institutional reasons to want that procedure followed here too. Character B is plausibly the
observatory's first technical director or chief astronomer, someone who has already tried and failed to
staff the facility's most demanding instruments from within one nation's own available exile population,
and knows firsthand the mission will fail under the standard template. Either could plausibly be the one
who ultimately prevails; which one does changes whether this cycle reads as institutional flexibility
winning out, or as a determined specialist forcing a necessary exception through sheer technical need.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The exception is granted. Professional and technical lineage becomes Kunlun's actual civic glue, a
specialist outpost the same way a real research consortium draws its staff from wherever the expertise
genuinely lives, not from one national program alone.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because Kunlun's population was never assembled through the ordinary single-nation channel, the
settlement has no default administrative mechanism for deciding who gets to join it later — no national
sponsor whose immigration quota simply expands over time the way most Tepenian cities absorb later
arrivals. In its place, Kunlun's founding administration formalizes something new: a credential-and-
vetting process, function's own equivalent of a national claim, that any later arrival has to pass
through before being accepted onto the ice.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That credentialing process, built to sort by demonstrated technical expertise, has no natural way to
evaluate the growing number of Ice Cold Buddhist devotees drawn to Kunlun specifically for its extremity
rather than for any scientific credential they can point to. The vetting apparatus built for astronomers
and engineers has to be quietly split into two separate tracks — one for verifiable technical competence,
one for demonstrated devotional seriousness — the first formal, administrative acknowledgment that
Kunlun's population was always going to be two different kinds of resident living side by side, not one.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Kunlun now admits its residents through two parallel, formally distinct pipelines — credentialed
specialists on one track, vetted devotees on the other — neither subordinate to the other, both required
to actually earn their place on the ice rather than simply inheriting it. The city's whole civic identity,
still running on function rather than flag, has quietly built its first real internal seam: two different
ways of belonging, sorted at the gate before anyone ever sets foot on the plateau.
