# Kunlun — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country. Nearly all of Kunlun's own established
history already falls in this window — the founding human population's departure, the curated
multinational convergence, and the observatory's own centuries of growth all predate the war. 10
candidate But/Therefore chains built from Kunlun's Megasheet (the specialist-drawn, 100%-robot
population, the observatory-and-comms city, Ice Cold Buddhism's second primary site). Post-settlement
only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Not One Nation, But One Purpose

Kunlun's earliest framing assumes one founding nation will simply define the whole city, the way most
Tepenian settlements work. **But** an observatory and comms facility this significant genuinely needs
expertise no single nation's own space program can fully supply on its own. **Therefore** the
population re-resolves around function instead — astronomers, comms engineers, and specialists drawn
specifically from nations with real space, astronomy, or deep-space communications heritage, however
many that ends up being. **But** a city built this way has no single dominant culture to organize
around. **Therefore** professional and technical lineage becomes Kunlun's actual civic glue, a
specialist outpost the same way a real research consortium draws its staff from wherever the expertise
genuinely lives.

## 2. The Considered Departure

Generations of human researchers at Kunlun live with the same chronic altitude toll Dome Fuji's own
population once endured. **But** unlike a slow, passive fade, Kunlun's own medical infrastructure
tracks the cumulative cost with real precision, generation after generation. **Therefore** the
evidence eventually becomes undeniable enough that the community itself, or a wider Federation safety
authority, makes a deliberate call: phase out human habitation entirely rather than let it continue at
mounting biological cost. **But** a considered decision like that means someone actually has to decide,
not simply let circumstance take its course. **Therefore** Kunlun's own transition to a fully robot
population carries a different weight than a quiet drift would — evidence-driven, consistent with a
city whose whole civic identity already runs on function and proof rather than sentiment.

## 3. Two Faiths, One Sky

Astronomers and comms specialists at Kunlun organize their whole lives around instrument and signal
schedules, purely task-driven. **But** Ice Cold Buddhism devotees drawn to the same extreme conditions
want something more than a working schedule to mark their presence here. **Therefore** devotees
develop their own formal practice layered onto the identical environment — observed stillness during
specific astronomical and atmospheric events, most significantly the perfect calm of polar night,
treated as devotion rather than simply a condition to work around. **But** scientist and devotee can
be standing at the very same instrument for entirely different reasons. **Therefore** Kunlun ends up
holding two genuinely different relationships to its own extremity side by side, neither one needing
to displace the other.

## 4. Mind and Hand

Kunlun's earliest residents could easily have treated the astronomy and comms work as simply a job,
demanding but ultimately just labor performed for pay or purpose. **But** a population this
specifically self-selected — drawn here by genuine interest in the work itself, not economic necessity
— brings something different to how they talk about what they do. **Therefore** the work gets reframed,
gradually and collectively, as identity rather than labor: nobody at Kunlun describes what they do as
"a job." **But** a reframing like that needs to be lived consistently to actually take hold as real
civic character rather than empty rhetoric. **Therefore** it becomes genuine, load-bearing philosophy
over generations — dignity found through the work itself, not despite it, closer to a creed than a
passing attitude.

## 5. The Tower That Never Comes Down

Kunlun's earliest comms infrastructure starts as purely functional — an antenna, satellite dishes,
nothing built for show. **But** the structure ends up towering over everything else in the city
regardless, visible from any approach long before anything else comes into view. **Therefore** it
becomes, without anyone deliberately designing it as one, the closest thing Kunlun has to a civic
monument — never built to represent the city, simply doing so by being the first and largest thing
anyone sees. **But** treating pure infrastructure as a symbol changes how residents relate to it.
**Therefore** generations of Kunlunites grow up orienting themselves, physically and emotionally,
around a structure that was never meant to mean anything beyond its own function.

## 6. Renata's Reach

Kunlun's early observatory serves astronomy first, with comms relay work handled as a secondary
function. **But** as Tepenia's own orbital-era presence grows, the demand for genuine, reliable
deep-space communications capacity grows well past what a secondary function can support. **Therefore**
Chief Astronomer Renata Okonkwo-Sato pushes for a real, dedicated expansion of the comms-relay side of
the facility, scaling it to match the astronomy program rather than leaving it permanently subordinate.
**But** that expansion means real investment and real reorganization of a facility that's worked one
way for generations. **Therefore** her push becomes a genuine turning point in Kunlun's own
institutional history — the observatory growing into something serving two equally serious functions
rather than one primary mission with a minor addition.

## 7. Brother Ilkay's Stillness

Individual devotees at Kunlun each mark polar night's perfect calm privately, in their own way, with no
shared practice connecting them. **But** a community this small and this isolated has real reason to
want something communal rather than purely individual. **Therefore** Brother/Sister Ilkay works to
formalize the observance — turning private stillness into something the devotional population marks
together, deliberately, as a shared civic ritual. **But** formalizing a practice this personal risks
losing what made it meaningful to begin with. **Therefore** the formalized version stays deliberately
minimal, structured enough to share, unstructured enough that it never stops feeling like genuine
individual devotion happening in the same room at the same time.

## 8. Whose Cold Counts

Kunlun's higher elevation plausibly makes its actual conditions colder than Vostok's own historically
verified record. **But** Kunlun has never had a confirmed surface measurement the way Vostok's record
was actually recorded and instrumentally verified. **Therefore** the two cities end up in a real,
if good-natured, standoff — Vostok holding the official record, Kunlun holding the raw physical claim
nobody can quite confirm. **But** neither side has any real interest in escalating the disagreement
into something adversarial. **Therefore** the rivalry stays exactly what it's always been — a genuine,
mutual point of pride between two isolated outposts that otherwise have every reason to think of
themselves as kin.

## 9. No Off Hours

Kunlun's whole population works in overlapping shifts dictated by the sky and the signal, neither of
which ever pauses for a shared evening. **But** bars, music halls, and restaurants built around a
single shared "off hours" wouldn't actually serve a population working around the clock. **Therefore**
Kunlun's leisure infrastructure develops to run continuously instead, since at any given moment some
fraction of the population is coming off shift or heading into one. **But** running that way means the
city never experiences the kind of shared civic rhythm most Tepenian cities take for granted.
**Therefore** Kunlun ends up with no collective "evening" at all — just a permanent, rolling overlap of
people working, resting, and socializing, all at once, all the time.

## 10. An Expansion Two Centuries Apart

Kunlun's observatory begins as a single instrument, adequate for its founding-era mission. **But**
successive waves of newly-arrived specialists, each bringing different national instrumentation
traditions with them, keep finding the existing setup too limited for what they actually want to do.
**Therefore** a genuine expansion phase takes hold early, coinciding with the population's own
convergence into a real multinational specialist community. **But** even that expanded facility
eventually strains against Tepenia's own growing orbital-era communications needs. **Therefore** a
second, later expansion phase scales the comms-relay function specifically, leaving Kunlun's
observatory as a facility that grew in distinct, datable stages rather than one static instrument
quietly accumulating readings across five centuries.

## 11. The Only Two Who Crossed

A Kunlun researcher studying the city's own human-to-robot transition learns that Dome Fuji is the only
other Tepenian city to have gone through the equivalent shift, and reaches out for the first time,
seeking their records as genuine comparative data. **But** Dome Fuji's own resident finds that framing
uncomfortable — for their city, the transition wasn't a policy decision to be catalogued, and being
treated as a data point feels like something significant getting flattened. **Therefore** a real
adjustment has to happen: Kunlun's own researcher acknowledges directly that the request matters to Dome
Fuji in a way it doesn't have to matter to Kunlun for the exchange to still be real. **But** neither city
actually changes its own fundamental register in the process. **Therefore** a modest, ongoing exchange
takes hold anyway — the only two cities in Tepenia that share this specific history, each now aware the
other exists, neither required to carry it the same way.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#1 and #2** are the most load-bearing — together they trace Kunlun's full transition from an assumed
single-nation founding to its actual re-resolved, function-first population, giving that identity an
earned developmental arc. **#6 and #10** work as a pair, since #6 dramatizes the specific decision
that #10 describes as one of the observatory's two historical expansion phases — the same event, told
once as institutional history and once through the person who pushed for it. **#11** is the only chain
that reaches beyond Kunlun's own subnet entirely, connecting it to the single other Tepenian city that
shares its most defining, unrepeated history.
