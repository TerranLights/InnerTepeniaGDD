## The Decision to Stop Sending Humans Up

Kunlun's human population's end was a considered decision, not a passive fade — a direct consequence of the
same reasoning that later became settled Federation policy: the combined altitude and cold at Dome A posed a
mounting, undeniable physiological cost no amount of medical infrastructure could fully offset.

The decision itself, per `Kunlun_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section I, was tracked and confirmed through Kunlun's
own altitude-legacy medical infrastructure — generations of accumulated data on chronic mountain sickness's
own cumulative toll, reviewed periodically rather than acted on impulsively. The specific review that finally
tipped the community, or the wider safety authority consulted, toward phasing out human habitation entirely
compared that accumulated medical data against the genuine scientific and communications value humans were
still providing at the station — and concluded, per what's preserved of the reasoning, that no research
output justified the mounting biological cost any longer. The phase-out itself was gradual rather than
immediate, existing human residents relocated rather than abandoned, consistent with a population whose whole
civic identity already ran on function and evidence rather than sentiment. The policy that resulted — human
presence at Kunlun understood as a genuine safety hazard, not an ideological exclusion — traces directly to
that one specific, evidence-driven review.

**"Brother/Sister Ilkay" *(placeholder, deliberately without a gendered or national marker resolved, not
developer-chosen)*** is credited with formalizing Kunlun's own polar-night stillness observance — formal,
observed periods of stillness during the −68°C perfect calm of polar night, treated by Ice Cold Buddhism

The practice began, per what's preserved of the devotional community's own account, as Ilkay's own private,

## The Reading Vostok Wouldn't Concede

Kunlun's own conditions are colder than Vostok's in the raw physical sense, given Kunlun's higher elevation —
but the claim was never settled by an equivalent confirmed surface measurement the way Vostok's own historic
record was. One specific attempt to close that gap produced a genuine, unresolved dispute between the two
cities' research communities rather than a clean resolution.

A Kunlun research team, working from atmospheric modeling suggesting conditions colder than any surface
measurement on record, proposed a joint verification effort with Vostok's own research staff — a shared
instrument deployment that could, in principle, produce a confirmed reading settling the comparison once and
for all. Vostok's own researchers, per what's preserved of the exchange, declined to participate, reasoning,
not unreasonably, that a joint effort risked producing a result that simply displaced their own city's
already-confirmed historic record without providing anything Vostok itself needed. Kunlun's own attempt to
verify independently, without Vostok's own instrumentation or expertise, produced modeled estimates that
remained just short of the confirmation standard a fully verified record required. The dispute was never
resolved, per what's established about the two cities' own ongoing relationship — Vostok holds the official
record, Kunlun holds the colder raw conditions, and neither side has ever fully accepted the other's own
framing of what that split actually means.

## When the Instruments Started Arriving From Everywhere

Kunlun's observatory underwent at least two significant expansion phases across its centuries of Tepenian
operation. The earlier of the two coincided with the population's own convergence into a genuinely
multinational specialist community, as newly arrived astronomers and comms engineers brought instrumentation
approaches from different national space-heritage traditions with them.

The convergence itself, per what's preserved of the observatory's own early institutional history, produced
genuine technical friction before it produced integration — instrumentation and calibration standards
developed independently across different national astronomy traditions didn't always interoperate cleanly,
and early staff meetings, per what's remembered of the period, involved real disagreement over which
tradition's own standards the observatory's combined instrumentation should be calibrated against going
forward. The resolution that emerged wasn't any single tradition winning out, but a deliberately hybrid
calibration protocol drawing pieces from multiple traditions at once — a genuinely collaborative compromise
that took considerably longer to establish than simply adopting one existing standard would have, but that
staff from every contributing tradition could actually work from afterward. The hybrid protocol, once
settled, became simply how Kunlun's observatory operated, with few current staff aware it had ever needed to
be negotiated at all.

## The Shift Where Both Reasons Were True at Once

Astronomers and comms specialists at Kunlun organize their time purely around instrument and signal
schedules — task-driven, functional. Ice Cold Buddhism devotees observe the same schedules for a different
reason entirely, treating the conditions themselves as devotional. One particular shared shift made both
readings visible in the same room at once.

A scheduled instrument calibration during a polar-night stillness period required both a task-focused
technician and a devotee observing the same stillness devotionally to work the same shift together — an
ordinary scheduling overlap, not a deliberate pairing. The technician worked through the calibration exactly
as any other shift required; the devotee present, per what's remembered of the account, maintained the same
formal stillness practice throughout, neither interfering with the technical work nor treating it as
incompatible with devotional observance. Both considered the shift entirely successful by their own separate
standards — the calibration completed correctly, the stillness observed faithfully — a small, concrete
instance of exactly the coexistence Section IV describes: the same task, the same extreme conditions, two
genuinely different relationships to both, neither one requiring the other to yield.
