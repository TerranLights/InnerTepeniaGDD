# Davis — Candidate Courses of Events

**Regenerated 2026-07-20** against Davis's conclusive settled identity — **"Breadbasket of Tepenia,"**
plus a genuine multi-disciplinary research hub (resolved 2026-07-16). Chains #5, #6, #7, and #10 have been
substantively rewritten; #1, #2, #3, #4, #8, and #9 needed no economic correction and are carried forward
with a fresh pass, incorporating texture from the Neo-Races Catalog and Cross-Reference Synthesis work
completed since the originals were drafted. Suggestion #11, "Food for Stone" (already correctly grounded
when it was drafted), is unchanged.

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Davis's Megasheet (the enabler-not-explorer
namesake, the Vestfold Hills' varied terrain, the breadbasket-plus-research civic character). Post-settlement
only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. The Man Who Enabled Others

The earliest exile community inherits a station named for a mariner who made other men's expeditions
possible and never sought credit for his own. **But** a founding population could easily have chosen
a more celebratory civic myth to build around instead. **Therefore** Founding Administrator Wren
Kalloway deliberately models the young settlement's self-image on John King Davis's own unglamorous
competence, rather than reaching for anything grander. **But** that choice means Davis will never
market itself the way flashier founding stories let other cities do. **Therefore** "enabler, not
protagonist" becomes Davis's genuine civic character from its earliest institutional decisions
onward — and centuries later, once Davis's economy settles into feeding much of the wider Federation
rather than being any city's destination in its own right, Kalloway's founding choice turns out to have
anticipated exactly what Davis would become, not merely described what it once was.

## 2. What the Lakes Remember

Davis's inherited terrain includes freshwater lakes, hypersaline basins, and landlocked marine pools,
each freezing and thawing on its own separate schedule. **But** none of that variety means much to
science without someone actually working out how to read it. **Therefore** researchers develop a
genuinely distinct methodology — paleoclimate reconstruction from layered lake sediment cores, different
bodies of water each preserving an independent record of the same shared history. **But**
cross-referencing multiple separate sediment records against each other is slower and more painstaking
than working from a single archive. **Therefore** Davis's research culture earns a real, specific
scientific identity of its own — water as archival medium, distinct from the ice-core science
practiced elsewhere in Tepenia. **But** a methodology this precise raises an uncomfortable question
closer to home: if the sediment cores can reconstruct the region's deep climate history, they could just
as easily reveal whether Davis's own lakes have shifted extent or salinity within living settlement
memory. **Therefore** Davis's own archive becomes doubly significant without anyone quite planning it
that way — valuable to the wider Federation's climate science, and quietly, uncomfortably capable of
documenting the city's own history back to itself, whether anyone goes looking or not.

## 3. Credit Declined

Naturalist Ratna Wirawan leads the team that works out the sediment-core methodology, doing the bulk
of the foundational research herself. **But** publishing under her own name alone would mean
overshadowing the whole team that helped build the method with her — and Wirawan's own instinct, shaped
by a relational, communally-minded upbringing where leadership is built through trust rather than
individual claim, makes solo credit feel less like an honor than a distortion of how the work actually
happened. **Therefore** she insists on crediting the group collectively, turning down opportunities to
claim sole authorship even when doing so would have been easy and expected. **But** that choice means
her own individual contribution stays less visible than it could have been. **Therefore** her decision
becomes, quietly, a defining local story — proof that Davis's whole "enabler" ethos isn't just inherited
from a namesake, but something its own people actively choose to live out, from instincts that predate
Davis itself.

## 4. Ice on Different Clocks

Prydz Bay's main harbor handles Davis's primary shipping traffic, but freezes over fully during the
harshest stretch of winter like any other Tepenian port. **But** the city's own scattered fjord
inlets, shaped by the same varied terrain that defines everything else about Davis, don't all freeze
and thaw on the same schedule as the main bay. **Therefore** the smaller fishing and maintenance
vessels that don't need the main harbor's full commercial capacity shift to working the fjords instead,
and sailors and dispatchers work out, over years of trial, which specific inlets stay navigable even
when the harbor itself ices over — a genuine logistical advantage tied directly to the terrain diversity
everyone already takes for granted. **But** exploiting that advantage means learning a genuinely
complicated, ever-shifting map of ice conditions. **Therefore** reading the fjords becomes one of
Davis's own specialized local skills, quietly essential and almost entirely invisible to anyone who's
never had to rely on it.

## 5. The Same Eye, Turned Sideways

*(Replaces "Paint What You See," which grounded its tension in Davis as "a working city organized
around quarries and fabrication shops" — superseded 2026-07-16.)*

Davis's whole civic and economic life already runs on one specific, trained skill: patient, careful
visual observation — reading a lake's ice, distinguishing one terrain feature from another, noticing
exactly what's changed since last season. **But** a trained eye like that doesn't stay obediently
confined to formal data collection once residents have spent years developing it. **Therefore** a
genuine naturalist visual-arts tradition takes root — the same people who sketch a lake's shoreline for
a sediment-core survey start sketching it again afterward, for no reason except that they're already
looking that closely and it would almost feel strange to stop. **But** a skill this directly useful for
research work could easily crowd out any purely aesthetic use of it, if Davis's civic culture valued
output over the practice itself. **Therefore** the tradition survives and stays genuinely valued in its
own right — proof that Davis's quiet competence was never purely functional to begin with, that looking
carefully is something its people do for its own sake as much as for the archive.

## 6. Pages From Elsewhere

*(Revised 2026-07-20 — the resource-competition driving this chain is now Davis's breadbasket/research
economy, not the superseded mining/fabrication framing.)*

Davis's population wants more cultural and intellectual life than a city built around patient fieldwork
and greenhouse labor naturally produces on its own. **But** building elaborate cultural institutions
competes directly with the same resources Davis's breadbasket economy and research heritage actually
need — labor, growing space, research funding, all already stretched thin by the responsibility of
feeding much of the wider Federation. **Therefore** residents turn to something quieter and cheaper
instead — a genuine, if informal, network for acquiring and circulating books smuggled out of Upper
Earth, read privately rather than performed publicly. **But** sustaining that kind of network takes
real, ongoing coordination between people who'd rather not draw much attention to it. **Therefore**
Davis develops a quiet, self-directed intellectual life that asks nothing from the wider city and needs
no outside recognition to keep functioning — echoing the same unglamorous, self-sufficient instinct
that defines everything else about Davis.

## 7. Building With the Land, Not Against It

*(Revised 2026-07-20 — drops the prior version's implicit mining/fabrication economic backdrop in favor
of Davis's actual breadbasket needs.)*

Most Tepenian cities engineer their architecture primarily to defend against wind. **But** Davis's own
terrain offers something most other cities don't have — genuine variety worth working with rather than
simply surviving, and a breadbasket economy that specifically needs sunlight exposure, lake access, and
sheltered growing sites more than it needs uniform wind-defense. **Therefore** early planners site
greenhouses, research stations, and residential clusters around lake access and fjord inlets
deliberately, letting the terrain's own natural features shape the city's layout instead of building
uniform wind-defense everywhere. **But** that approach means less standardized construction and more
site-specific engineering than a simpler, wind-first design would require. **Therefore** Davis ends up
with a genuinely varied, terrain-responsive built environment — proof that the city's relationship with
its own remarkable landscape, and its own breadbasket economy's actual needs, was collaborative from the
very start, not adversarial.

## 8. The Wish Nobody Says Out Loud

Davis's dominant civic ethos genuinely doesn't seek outside attention, content with quiet competence
over recognition. **But** a minority of residents privately notice just how objectively unusual the
Vestfold Hills' terrain actually is by any Tepenian standard — genuinely unusual enough that Davis's own
maritime economy, on Prydz Bay, could plausibly have supported an assertive, self-promoting port
identity instead, the way distant Vladivostok's real "Lord of the East" naming embraced exactly that
path. **Therefore** a quiet, rarely-voiced wish takes hold among that minority — not for fame exactly,
but something closer to knowing there was a real alternative civic identity available and watching the
city's founders choose the humble one anyway. **But** saying so openly would cut against the city's
whole dominant character. **Therefore** the wish stays mostly private, surfacing only occasionally, in
conversations between residents who suspect they're not the only ones who feel it but rarely confirm it
with each other directly — each of them quietly aware of the assertive city Davis could have been, and
wasn't.

## 9. Thirteen Flags, No Winner

Later immigration waves bring thirteen distinct nations to Notable tier at Davis, longer than any other
Mirny subnet city's own minority-heritage list. **But** with no single non-Primary community carrying
outsized weight, none of them can simply claim a defined cultural niche within the city the way a
larger single minority might elsewhere. **Therefore** minority-heritage life at Davis stays genuinely
dispersed — small threads of many traditions woven through daily life rather than concentrated into
one visible "guest culture" the way it plays out at Zhongshan or Janbogo. **But** that dispersal means
no single minority tradition ever gets the critical mass to become a defining civic landmark on its
own. **Therefore** Davis's cultural texture stays quietly, permanently plural — present everywhere in
small pieces, dominant nowhere.

## 10. The Harvest Outgrows the Archive

*(Replaces "Quarries Before Questions," whose entire premise — mining and fabrication growing to
outpace the founding research mission — was superseded 2026-07-16. This chain now carries the same
structural beat with the correct economic driver.)*

Davis's founding population arrives with a genuine research mission, inheriting decades of AAD
environmental study into the Vestfold Hills' unusual terrain. **But** a growing exile population needs
food, not just data — sheltered-agriculture and greenhouse cultivation prove far more urgently
necessary than any research program alone was ever built to provide. **Therefore** agricultural
production grows steadily alongside the research work, drawing more and more of the daily workforce as
the practical needs of an actual, growing breadbasket city outpace the founding mission's own original
scope. **But** that growth means the research heritage risks becoming a smaller and smaller share of
what Davis actually does day to day. **Therefore** the founding scientific mission survives as a
genuine, still-respected co-equal pillar rather than a shrinking afterthought — sustained deliberately,
not by accident, precisely because Davis's own leadership had already learned from Kalloway's founding
choice (Suggestion #1) that quiet, unglamorous competence is worth protecting even when it isn't what
pays the bills.

## 11. Food for Stone

*(Grounded in the corrected 2026-07-16 breadbasket/research identity — Davis's own agricultural surplus,
not the superseded mining/fabrication framing. Unchanged by this regeneration; already correctly
grounded when drafted.)*

Mirny's industrial and quarrying operation grows fast enough along Hwy 110 that informal, ad-hoc food
arrangements genuinely stop being adequate for its expanding workforce, at the same moment Davis's own
breadbasket surplus has nowhere established to go. **But** Davis's own enabler instinct risks letting
that surplus go quietly undervalued rather than genuinely, fairly traded. **Therefore** the two cities
negotiate a real, formal exchange — Davis's agricultural surplus for Mirny's materials and construction
goods. **But** a lopsided or informal arrangement would only repeat the same undervaluation Davis's own
civic character is prone to. **Therefore** it gets codified as a real, ongoing relationship instead, food
for stone flowing steadily along the same corridor connecting both cities.

## Worth Your Attention

**#2 and #3** still work as the strongest pair — the methodology Davis's researchers actually
developed, and the specific personal choice that turned it into a defining civic value rather than just
a scientific achievement. **#10** remains the most structurally important thread, now correctly
grounded: it traces the exact demographic-and-economic shift (research-first founding to
breadbasket-first present) that every other established fact about Davis's current character depends
on, and closes the loop with #1 by having Davis's own leadership consciously protect its research
heritage rather than let it erode by accident. **#5 and #7** are the two chains most directly repaired
by this regeneration — both now trace Davis's aesthetic and architectural character to the *correct*
economic root (breadbasket agriculture and the observational habits it demands), rather than the
superseded mining-city framing. **#11** remains grounded specifically in the corrected breadbasket
economy, giving Davis's identity a genuine outward-facing trade relationship with Mirny to sit alongside
#10's inward-facing account of how that identity took its current shape.
