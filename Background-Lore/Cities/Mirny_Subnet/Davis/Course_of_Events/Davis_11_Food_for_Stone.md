## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Davis's own corrected identity is settled — Tepenia's breadbasket, sheltered agriculture and greenhouse
cultivation making up its clear daily majority, alongside genuine ecological research. Along the same
Hwy 110 corridor, Mirny's industrial and quarrying operation is growing, its workforce expanding faster
than any informal local food arrangement can comfortably support. Neither city has yet formalized what
the other could actually use.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Mirny's industrial growth reaches a point where informal, ad-hoc food arrangements genuinely stop being
adequate — a real supply gap opens for its expanding workforce, at the same moment Davis's own breadbasket
surplus has nowhere established to go beyond its own subsistence needs.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a conflict about whether trade should happen —
both sides need what the other has. Character A, from Davis, carries the city's own established
"enabler, not protagonist" civic instinct (chain #1) into real economic terms — genuinely wary of quietly
undervaluing Davis's own surplus out of the same humility that defines the city's whole character,
mindful of the Catalog's own flagged concern that agricultural distribution is "a genuine justice
question, not merely a logistics detail." Character B, from Mirny, is focused practically on securing
reliable supply for a growing workforce, initially inclined toward whatever arrangement is fastest and
cheapest rather than one that's actually fair to Davis over the long term.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Davis agricultural
administrator or civic representative, competent and unshowy, uncomfortable with the idea of Davis's own
enabler instinct being quietly exploited rather than genuinely valued. Character B is plausibly a Mirny
industrial logistics figure, not unreasonable, just focused on their own city's urgent and growing needs.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A formal, genuinely fair exchange gets established rather than an informal or lopsided one — Davis's
agricultural surplus for Mirny's materials and construction goods, codified along Hwy 110 as a real,
ongoing relationship rather than an ad-hoc arrangement either city could quietly walk back later.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Davis's own corrected breadbasket economy gains real outward direction for the first time — not merely
feeding itself and running its own research quietly, but genuinely enabling another city's growth, the
same structural role Tyumen plays for extraction happening elsewhere, made concrete and specific.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

The fair-exchange precedent set with Mirny becomes the template Davis reaches for whenever allocation
questions arise with other cities too — directly answering, through lived practice rather than abstract
policy, the Catalog's own flagged concern that Davis's agricultural distribution be treated as a genuine
justice question.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Davis and Mirny hold a genuine, formalized exchange relationship — food for materials, running along
Hwy 110 — giving Davis's corrected economic identity real outward direction and giving Mirny reliable
food security, neither city's growth resting on the other's quiet undervaluation.
