## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Davis's founding population arrives with a genuine research mission, inheriting the AAD's physical
infrastructure and the Vestfold Hills' unusual terrain itself — long unstudied since the real program ended
centuries earlier *(corrected 2026-07-24, GPS-purposes-only pass; see `Specs/Davis.md`)* — a settlement
whose earliest institutional identity is built entirely around independently re-establishing that kind of
scientific work themselves.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A growing exile population needs food, not just data, and that need doesn't wait for a graceful
institutional transition — sheltered-agriculture and greenhouse cultivation prove far more urgently
necessary than any research program alone was ever built to provide, and people need to eat now,
regardless of what the founding mission's charter says the settlement is actually for. Agricultural
production grows steadily alongside the research work as a practical response, but every worker and
every allocation of resources that shifts toward growing food is one that doesn't go toward the founding
scientific mission, and the balance between the two starts to matter in a way it never had to during the
settlement's earliest, smaller years.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a research-mission figure
watching the founding scientific program's resources and workforce steadily shift toward the growing
agricultural sector, and an agricultural or civic-infrastructure figure responsible for actually feeding
a growing population that can't wait on research priorities. Character A isn't blind to the settlement's
real material needs, and Character B isn't dismissive of the research heritage's value — the
disagreement is about pace and proportion: how much of the mission's own resources should shift before
the balance between feeding people and understanding the terrain that feeds them tips too far, not
whether either one matters at all. This should resolve toward genuine partnership rather than a
concession from either side — Kalloway's own founding choice (Suggestion #1) already established that
Davis protects what's worth protecting even when it isn't what pays the bills.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a senior researcher
from the founding scientific program, watching a mission they've dedicated their working life to compete
for resources against an urgent, growing practical need. Character B is plausibly an early agricultural
or civic-infrastructure administrator, someone directly responsible for keeping a growing population fed,
for whom the research mission's needs are real but pressed against an equally real and immediate
shortage.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side "wins" a majority-versus-minority arrangement — instead, a deliberate institutional
commitment establishes agriculture and research as genuinely co-equal pillars of Davis's economy,
formally allocated resources side by side rather than one shrinking to accommodate the other's growth.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the two pillars are established as formally co-equal rather than one absorbing the other, Davis
ends up with two structurally interdependent institutions — a breadbasket economy and a genuine research
tradition — each drawing on the same terrain-literacy the other depends on, an arrangement that has to be
actively maintained through ongoing, deliberate resource allocation rather than settling into permanent
balance on its own.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the research core survives as a fully-resourced, co-equal institution rather than shrinking into
a protected minority, it remains available generations later as exactly the foundation the sediment-core
methodology breakthrough (Suggestion #2) needs to happen at all — a research tradition still funded,
staffed, and taken seriously enough that Naturalist Ratna Wirawan's later work has somewhere genuinely
institutional to happen, not a diminished remnant surviving on tolerance. Delete the 2nd-order change —
imagine agriculture had instead grown to fully dominate, research reduced to a token minority tradition
the way the superseded version of this chain once described — and there is no fully-resourced scientific
institution left for Suggestion #2's breakthrough to occur within at the scale it actually requires; the
later discovery depends specifically on this cycle's deliberate co-equal settlement.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Davis stands as Tepenia's breadbasket with a genuine, fully co-equal research tradition sitting directly
alongside its agricultural economy rather than diminished by it — sustained deliberately, not by
accident, precisely because Davis's own leadership had already learned from Kalloway's founding choice
that quiet, unglamorous competence is worth protecting even when it isn't what pays the bills.
