## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Davis's dominant civic ethos genuinely doesn't seek outside attention, content with quiet competence over
recognition — a self-image the whole population has largely internalized, inherited directly from the
enabler philosophy Wren Kalloway established generations earlier (Suggestion #1).

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A minority of residents privately notice just how objectively unusual the Vestfold Hills' terrain
actually is by any Tepenian standard — genuinely remarkable, arguably worth more acknowledgment than the
city's own founding ethos permits anyone to ask for. A quiet, rarely-voiced wish takes hold among that
minority: not for fame, just for simple acknowledgment that what Davis has here is genuinely
extraordinary. Saying so openly cuts directly against the city's whole dominant character, though — a
population that has spent generations defining itself by not seeking outside attention has no comfortable
vocabulary for admitting it privately wants some, even in the smallest, most modest form. The wish stays
mostly unspoken, but unspoken things between people who suspect they're not alone in feeling them have a
way of eventually finding each other.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** less an adversarial confrontation than a hesitant,
half-admitted conversation between two residents who both privately hold the wish and have never said so
to each other directly — one finally naming it out loud, the other reacting with a mix of relief at not
being alone and real discomfort at how directly it cuts against everything Davis is supposed to stand
for. This should feel like two people testing whether a shared, quiet betrayal of civic identity is
actually a betrayal at all, or just an honest, small human want the ethos never made room for.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a resident who
finally says the wish out loud, prompted by something small — a visiting outsider's genuine astonishment
at the terrain, perhaps, that makes the private feeling suddenly harder to keep private. Character B is
plausibly a longtime friend or colleague, someone steeped just as deeply in the enabler ethos, whose
reaction has to carry real ambivalence rather than simple agreement or simple reproach.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Nothing civic changes — no campaign, no petition, no shift in Davis's outward self-presentation. What
changes is smaller and more private: the two residents settle into an unspoken agreement that the wish is
allowed to exist between them, spoken occasionally, never pursued, never resolved into anything more
than what it already is.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the wish gets voiced but never acted on, it becomes something closer to a recognized, private
undercurrent within Davis rather than staying a single resident's isolated feeling — the specific
conversation becomes one others quietly have too, in similar pairs, spreading person to person as a known
but never publicly acknowledged sentiment.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the sentiment spreads privately, person to person, rather than through any public channel, Davis
develops a genuine split between its public civic voice (unified, modest, uninterested in outside
attention) and a private undercurrent of residents who know exactly how remarkable their home is and quietly
wish more people did — a split residents themselves rarely name directly, but one that shapes how
individual Davis residents react, differently from the city's official posture, whenever an outsider
does happen to be struck by the terrain. Delete the 2nd-order change — imagine the conversation had
stayed a genuinely isolated, one-time exchange — and there's no mechanism for a citywide private
undercurrent to exist at all; it depends specifically on the wish having spread person to person the way
this outcome allowed.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Davis holds two layers at once — a public ethos of quiet, attention-averse competence, fully intact, and
a private, never-quite-acknowledged undercurrent among a real minority of residents who privately wish,
just a little, that the rest of Tepenia understood what they already know about this place.
