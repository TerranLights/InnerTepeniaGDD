## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Davis's founding population arrives with a genuine research mission, inheriting decades of AAD
environmental study into the Vestfold Hills — a settlement whose earliest institutional identity is
built entirely around continuing that scientific work.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A growing exile population needs housing, materials, and infrastructure the research mission alone was
never built to provide, and that need doesn't wait for a graceful institutional transition — people need
shelter and tools now, regardless of what the founding mission's charter says the settlement is actually
for. Mining and fabrication grow steadily alongside the research work as a practical response, but every
worker and every allocation of resources that shifts toward quarrying and manufacturing is one that
doesn't go toward the founding scientific mission, and the balance between the two starts to matter in a
way it never had to during the settlement's earliest, smaller years.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a research-mission figure
watching the founding scientific program's resources and workforce steadily thin as industry grows, and
an industrial or civic-infrastructure figure responsible for actually housing and supplying a growing
population that can't wait on research priorities. Character A isn't blind to the settlement's real
material needs, and Character B isn't dismissive of the research heritage's value — the disagreement is
about pace and proportion: how much of the mission can be preserved while still meeting the city's
genuinely urgent practical needs, not whether the mission matters at all.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a senior researcher
from the founding scientific program, watching a mission they've dedicated their working life to shrink
in relative importance year over year. Character B is plausibly an early industrial or infrastructure
administrator, someone directly responsible for keeping a growing population housed, fed, and supplied,
for whom the research mission's needs are real but necessarily secondary to immediate survival
infrastructure.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

No formal victory for either side — industry keeps growing because it has to, becoming the majority of
daily economic activity, but a deliberate, if modest, institutional commitment gets made to preserve a
dedicated, permanently funded core of the founding research program rather than letting it dissolve
entirely into the industrial majority.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the research program survives as a deliberately preserved core rather than being fully absorbed
or abandoned, Davis ends up with two structurally distinct institutions running side by side — an
industrial majority economy and a smaller, protected scientific minority tradition — rather than one
having simply replaced the other, an arrangement that has to be actively maintained through ongoing
resource allocation rather than settling into permanent balance on its own.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the research core survives as a distinct, actively maintained institution rather than dissolving
into industry, it remains available generations later as exactly the foundation the sediment-core
methodology breakthrough (Suggestion #2) needs to happen at all — a research minority tradition still
funded, staffed, and taken seriously enough that Naturalist Ratna Wirawan's later work has somewhere
institutional to actually happen. Delete the 2nd-order change — imagine the research program had instead
fully dissolved into the industrial economy — and there is no scientific institution left for Suggestion
#2's breakthrough to occur within; the later discovery depends specifically on this cycle's deliberate
preservation.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Davis stands as an industrial city with a genuine, still-respected minority scientific tradition sitting
alongside its dominant economy rather than replaced by it — smaller than the founding mission once was,
never abandoned, and quietly load-bearing for everything Davis's research culture eventually becomes.
