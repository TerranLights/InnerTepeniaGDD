## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Prydz Bay's main harbor handles Davis's primary shipping traffic but freezes over fully during the
harshest stretch of winter, like any other Tepenian port — a predictable, citywide shutdown every year,
accepted as simply how winter shipping works.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The city's own scattered fjord inlets, shaped by the same varied terrain that defines everything else
about Davis, don't all freeze and thaw on the same schedule as the main bay — some stay navigable well
into the season the harbor itself is fully iced over. The smaller fishing and maintenance vessels that
never needed the main harbor's full commercial capacity in the first place are the ones positioned to
actually exploit it, if anyone's willing to test the ice. That fact sits unexploited for a while, because
acting on it means real risk: sending ships into unfamiliar, unmapped fjord channels during exactly the
season when ice conditions are least predictable, on nothing more than an educated guess that a
particular inlet might still be open. A shipping economy under real winter pressure has to decide whether
the potential advantage is worth the danger of testing it.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a sailor or dispatcher
convinced the fjords represent a genuine, exploitable logistical advantage worth the risk of testing, and
a more cautious harbor authority responsible for crew safety who sees an unmapped, unpredictable ice
channel as exactly the kind of risk a responsible operation shouldn't gamble with. Character B's caution
is entirely reasonable — ice misjudged badly enough kills people — and Character A isn't reckless, just
convinced the terrain diversity everyone already takes for granted (Davis's own defining trait) is being
wasted if nobody ever tests what it can actually do for the city's winter shipping capacity.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an experienced sailor
or fjord-familiar local, someone who's spent years quietly observing which inlets behave differently
from the main bay without yet having the institutional backing to act on it. Character B is plausibly a
harbor-safety official or shipping-dispatch authority, someone whose job is explicitly to weigh
economic advantage against crew risk and who has no reason yet to trust an untested claim over established
caution.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A limited, deliberately cautious trial run is authorized rather than either side's position winning
outright — a single fjord route tested under close supervision. It works, and works again over
successive winters of trial, gradually building a genuine, tested map of which inlets stay navigable
even when the harbor itself ices over.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the advantage was proven through cautious, incremental trial rather than a single dramatic
success, Davis develops a formal, ongoing practice of season-by-season fjord-condition surveying — not a
one-time discovery but a maintained, living body of knowledge that has to be actively renewed every
winter as ice conditions shift.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the knowledge has to be actively renewed rather than simply learned once, reading the fjords
becomes a genuine specialized profession at Davis rather than a piece of settled common knowledge —
skilled sailors and surveyors whose entire expertise is the yearly, ever-shifting map of which channels
hold, passing that specific, quietly essential skill down through direct apprenticeship rather than
written record, since the map itself never stays fixed long enough to fully codify. Delete the
2nd-order change — imagine the fjord routes had instead been mapped once and left as static knowledge —
and there's no reason a living, apprenticed profession would need to exist around it; that specialization
depends specifically on the knowledge needing constant renewal.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Davis holds a genuine, quietly essential logistical advantage over ports without its own varied terrain
— a maintained, generationally-apprenticed skill for reading which fjords stay open when the main
harbor doesn't, almost entirely invisible to anyone who's never had to rely on it.
