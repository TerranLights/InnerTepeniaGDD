## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Davis's inherited terrain includes freshwater lakes, hypersaline basins, and landlocked marine pools,
each freezing and thawing on its own separate schedule — a genuine natural resource sitting largely
unread, since none of that variety means much to science without someone working out how to interpret
it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Researchers recognize the lakes' layered sediment as a potential paleoclimate archive, but no
established methodology exists yet for actually reading it — Tepenia's dominant polar science tradition
runs on ice-core analysis, a completely different medium with its own established techniques that don't
transfer cleanly to water. Developing a genuinely new methodology from scratch means real professional
risk: years of painstaking, cross-referenced work against multiple separate sediment records that may or
may not agree with each other, with no guarantee the approach produces anything publishable at all. Some
researchers argue the safer, faster path is simply requesting ice-core equipment and expertise from
elsewhere in Tepenia rather than reinventing a whole methodology locally.

settlement's limited scientific budget and wary of betting it on an unproven approach.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Character A's approach is given a real trial rather than full institutional backing outright — enough
resources to attempt cross-referencing multiple lake sediment records against each other. It works: the
layered cores do preserve an independent, legible record of shared regional history, validating the
slower, painstaking method over the faster borrowed one.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the trial succeeds on its own terms rather than through institutional mandate, Davis's research
culture inherits a working method built specifically around cross-referencing multiple independent
water-based records against each other — a genuinely distinct scientific identity, water as archival
medium, structurally different from the single-archive ice-core science practiced elsewhere in Tepenia.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Davis's methodology depends on cross-referencing multiple records rather than reading one, the
city's research culture develops an unusually collaborative, team-dependent working style — no single
researcher can complete a full reconstruction alone, since the method's entire validity rests on
comparing independent records against each other. That structural need for collective work becomes the
same soil Suggestion #3's insistence on shared credit later grows from. Delete the 2nd-order change —
imagine Davis had instead adopted borrowed single-archive ice-core methods — and there's no structural
reason the research culture would need to be collaborative rather than individual; that need depends
specifically on the cross-referencing method this cycle established.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Davis's research culture stands on a genuinely distinct scientific identity — water as archival medium,
built through slow, cross-referenced, inherently collaborative work — earning the city a real,
specific reputation among specialists distinct from anywhere else in Tepenia that studies its own
climate history through ice instead.
