## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The earliest exile community inherits a station named for a mariner who made other men's expeditions
possible and never sought credit for his own — a namesake carrying a specific, quiet kind of legacy, not
yet translated into anything the young settlement has actually chosen for itself.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A founding population confronting the same blank-slate choice every exile community faces could easily
reach for a more celebratory civic myth instead — grand ambition, a heroic founding narrative, something
to rally a population around and market outward to the rest of Tepenia. Founding Administrator Wren
Kalloway pushes deliberately against that pull, proposing instead that the young settlement model its
self-image directly on the mariner's own unglamorous competence: enabler, not protagonist. That proposal
draws real resistance, because it isn't free — a founding identity this modest means Davis forfeits the
kind of flashy origin story that lets other Tepenian cities market themselves outward, attract attention,
trade partners, and migrants on the strength of a compelling founding legend.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between Kalloway (or a founding
figure like them) advocating for the humble "enabler" self-image, and an early economic or diplomatic

disadvantage — harder to attract migrants, trade attention, or Federation notice compared to cities
building around a heroic myth. This shouldn't play as vision versus small-mindedness; Character B's
concern is a legitimate, practical one about a young settlement's long-term competitiveness, and
Character A has to make a real case for why the quieter identity is worth that cost, not simply assert
that it's more honest.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly Wren Kalloway
themselves, in an early administrative or founding-council role with real authority over how the
settlement presents itself. Character B is plausibly an early trade liaison or resource-allocation
figure — someone whose job is literally to make the case for Davis to outsiders, and who has legitimate
professional reason to want a more marketable founding story to work with.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Kalloway's position wins, but deliberately, not by default — the settlement's founding institutions are
built explicitly around the enabler ethos, with Character B's concern noted and accepted as a real cost
rather than dismissed. "Enabler, not protagonist" becomes Davis's genuine civic character from its
earliest institutional decisions onward.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the choice was made deliberately, with the marketing cost openly acknowledged, Davis's earliest
institutions never build any apparatus for civic self-promotion at all — no founding-myth office, no
outward-facing origin narrative — leaving a genuine, structural gap in how the city presents itself to
the rest of Tepenia that persists for generations.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Davis never develops the institutional habit of self-promotion, the city's later, genuinely
remarkable achievements — the sediment-core methodology (Suggestion #2), the fjord-navigation expertise
(Suggestion #4) — spread by reputation among specialists who directly need them rather than through any
deliberate civic marketing effort, giving Davis's fame a narrower but more durable, expertise-driven
character than cities that promoted themselves from the start. Delete the 2nd-order change — imagine
Davis had built ordinary self-promotion institutions after all — and there's no structural reason its
reputation would spread through specialist networks specifically rather than general publicity; that
pattern depends on the promotional gap this cycle created.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Davis stands as a city that will never market itself the way flashier founding stories let other cities
do — its reputation, when it comes, arriving instead through the genuine respect of people who directly
need what Davis actually produces, exactly the kind of quiet, earned relevance the enabler ethos was
built to produce from the very start.
