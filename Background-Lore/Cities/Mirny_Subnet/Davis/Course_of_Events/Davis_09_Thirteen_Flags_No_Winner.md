## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Later immigration waves bring thirteen distinct nations to Notable tier at Davis, longer than any other
Mirny subnet city's own minority-heritage list — a genuinely plural population with no single
non-Primary community large enough to claim a defined cultural niche of its own the way a larger single
minority might elsewhere.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

With no single minority community carrying outsized numbers, none of the thirteen can simply claim
dedicated civic space, a named quarter, or a recognized public tradition the way a larger, more
concentrated minority elsewhere in the Mirny subnet manages to. That's a real frustration for at least
some of these communities — watching Zhongshan's or Janbogo's own visible "guest culture" traditions and
finding no equivalent path available at Davis, purely as a matter of arithmetic rather than any lack of
cultural richness. A push emerges from within one or two of the larger of the thirteen communities to
formally designate space or recognition specifically for their own tradition, on the reasonable argument
that they've been present long enough and contributed enough to deserve it.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

No single community gets dedicated, exclusive civic space — but a genuine compromise emerges: a shared,
rotating civic calendar of recognition, giving each of the thirteen communities a formally allocated
turn rather than any one of them a permanent claim, letting real acknowledgment happen without breaking
the citywide parity.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because recognition rotates rather than concentrates, Davis develops a formal civic institution — a
shared calendar of minority-heritage observances — that no other Mirny subnet city needs, since none of
them face the same thirteen-way parity problem. The rotation itself becomes a genuine, ongoing piece of
civic administration rather than a one-time settlement.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the rotating calendar requires residents to actively engage with traditions outside their own
heritage on a regular, structured basis, Davis's cultural texture develops genuine, working familiarity
across all thirteen communities rather than staying dispersed and mutually unfamiliar — residents end up
knowing real, specific things about traditions that aren't their own simply because the rotation puts
each one in front of the whole city on a predictable schedule. Delete the 2nd-order change — imagine no
formal rotation had been created and each community's recognition had simply stayed informal and
sporadic — and there's no structural reason residents would gain working familiarity with traditions
outside their own; that familiarity depends specifically on the rotation's existence.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Davis's cultural texture stays quietly, permanently plural — present everywhere in small pieces, dominant
nowhere, but now formally sustained through a rotating civic calendar that gives all thirteen communities
real, structured acknowledgment without ever collapsing the parity that makes Davis's plurality distinct
from Zhongshan's or Janbogo's own concentrated minority cultures.
