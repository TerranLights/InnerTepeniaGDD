## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Davis's landscape offers a range of terrain no other Tepenian city has to account for in the same
compact space, but a working city organized around quarries and fabrication shops has little
institutional reason to prioritize documenting its own scenery — the terrain is a resource and a
navigational fact, not yet treated as anything worth simply looking at.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A handful of residents — not commissioned, not professionally trained, just people who find themselves
looking closely at the lakes and fjords and rock formations they pass every day — start sketching and
painting what they see, purely because the landscape is there and worth the attention. That impulse runs
directly against the settlement's dominant practical culture, where time and resources are understood to
belong to the mining and fabrication economy that actually keeps the city running. It doesn't take formal
opposition to threaten the fledgling tradition, just the ordinary pressure of a population with genuinely
limited time, more focused on industrial output than on cultivating anything resembling a formal artistic
scene.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** less a dramatic confrontation than a quiet,
sustained tension between someone who keeps making time for this naturalist visual-arts practice despite

time as better spent elsewhere, given how much the young settlement genuinely needs from its working
population. Character B shouldn't be dismissive of art for its own sake — the concern is a legitimate one

attention.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an ordinary quarry or
fabrication worker who paints or sketches in whatever hours remain — not a professional artist, someone
for whom this is a private, sustained practice rather than a career. Character B is plausibly a close
family member or work supervisor, someone with a genuine, non-hostile stake in Character A's time and
labor going toward what the settlement visibly needs.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side wins outright — Character A doesn't abandon the practical work, and Character B doesn't
manage to fully redirect Character A's remaining hours. What settles instead is an unspoken accommodation:
the practice continues, unofficial and modest, tolerated rather than endorsed, precisely because it never
grows large enough to genuinely compete with the demands of the working economy.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the accommodation stays informal rather than becoming institutional, the naturalist visual-arts
tradition survives without ever developing dedicated civic infrastructure — no gallery, no formal
teaching structure, no allocated studio space — passed on instead purely through individual example,
one worker showing another what they've been sketching in their own off-hours.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the tradition has no institutional home, it never risks becoming formalized into something
separate from the working population that actually practices it — every generation's artists remain
quarry workers and fabricators first, meaning the tradition survives specifically as a genuine minority
practice within Davis's working population rather than splitting off into a distinct artistic class the
city would then have to fund or justify. Delete the 2nd-order change — imagine the practice had instead
gained a dedicated civic gallery and formal teaching structure — and there's a real chance it would have
grown large enough to actually compete with the industrial economy for legitimacy and resources, exactly
the collision the informal accommodation was built to avoid.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Davis holds a modest, genuine minority visual-arts tradition — never the city's main identity, sustained
purely through individual example rather than institutional support, and never quite disappearing either,
generation after generation of working residents who find, in whatever hours remain, something worth
looking at closely enough to paint.
