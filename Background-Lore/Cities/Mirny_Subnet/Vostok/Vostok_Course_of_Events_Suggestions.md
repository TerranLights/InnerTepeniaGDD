# Vostok — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country. Nearly all of Vostok's own established
history already falls in this window — the founding community's decline, the redistributed
population's arrival, and the Lake Vostok research program all predate the war. 10 candidate
But/Therefore chains built from Vostok's Megasheet (the coldest city on Earth, the archive too vast
for any one mind, the population that inherited a legend it never built). Post-settlement only;
nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Selected for the Isolation

Vostok Station's earliest exile administrators need a population that can actually tolerate the
site's extreme isolation, not merely endure it. **But** ordinary exile settlement doesn't naturally
sort people by psychological fit for total remoteness. **Therefore** the founding community forms
through deliberate selection — people who found genuine extreme isolation acceptable as the cost of
doing the science, chosen specifically rather than simply assigned. **But** selecting that narrowly
means the founding population stays small from the very start, with no natural mechanism to grow much
larger. **Therefore** Vostok's whole founding character locks in early as necessarily specialized and
necessarily thin — a choice that shapes everything the city becomes centuries before anyone could see
where it was heading.

## 2. The Quiet Thinning

Vostok's founding Russian community lives and works generation after generation under conditions few
people would choose voluntarily. **But** each generation's own descendants are free to leave for less
extreme cities elsewhere in Tepenia, and many eventually do. **Therefore** the population thins
steadily, not through any single crisis but through ordinary attrition — age, quiet departure, simply
not being replaced at the rate people leave. **But** a shrinking community still has to maintain the
same research infrastructure with fewer and fewer hands. **Therefore** by the time centuries have
passed, there's remarkably little of the founding population left — a decline so gradual nobody could
point to the moment it became irreversible.

## 3. Room Where There Was None

A displaced population needs somewhere to resettle after their own home site is removed from
habitability. **But** most Tepenian cities have no real capacity to absorb a substantial new arrival
all at once. **Therefore** Vostok, its own founding community thinned by generations of quiet decline,
turns out to have exactly the kind of demographic room this displaced population needs. **But** nobody
actually planned to send them to inherit Vostok's own scientific legacy specifically. **Therefore**
the resettlement happens simply because the gap existed, not because anyone chose this population to
carry Vostok's legend forward — a plain, unglamorous demographic fact that ends up defining the city's
whole present character.

## 4. A Legend Inherited, Not Built

The newly-arrived population finds an already-running, genuinely significant research operation in
progress at Vostok, built by people they have no ancestral connection to at all. **But** inheriting
infrastructure isn't the same as inheriting obligation — nobody who arrives owes the founders' project
anything. **Therefore** each new resident makes an individual choice about whether to stay and take
part in the Lake Vostok work or move on to somewhere less demanding. **But** the work itself turns out
to be genuinely compelling on its own terms, not merely something left behind to finish. **Therefore**
most who stay end up drawn into the research because it's worth doing, not out of any felt duty to
complete someone else's unfinished mission — the legend becomes genuinely theirs by choice rather than
by inheritance.

## 5. What the Ice Kept Secret

Vostok's geneticist spends years working through Lake Vostok's ancient, isolated ecosystem, looking
for something worth the research effort the archive represents. **But** engineering a genuinely novel
genetic technique from scratch would take far longer than the timeline her breakthrough actually
follows. **Therefore** she finds something already there instead — a natural DNA-repair mechanism
evolved by organisms surviving millions of years of extreme isolation, cold, and pressure, waiting to
be noticed rather than invented. **But** recognizing what she'd actually found meant seeing past
centuries of prior researchers' own accumulated assumptions about what the lake's ecosystem was even
for. **Therefore** her breakthrough becomes less a triumph of engineering and more a triumph of
looking closely enough at something everyone else had already walked past.

## 6. Notes Left for Whoever's Next

Vostok's human researchers keep a conventional day/night schedule while its robot researchers work
until a task is finished, then socialize with whoever else happens to be free — no shared shift, no
predictable overlap. **But** genuine scientific coordination usually depends on people comparing notes
directly, in the same room, at the same time. **Therefore** Vostok's research culture develops a real,
necessary habit of detailed handoff documentation instead — notes and logs left for whoever picks up a
given thread next, since waiting for a shared meeting simply isn't realistic here. **But** that habit
only works if everyone actually commits to writing things down clearly enough for a stranger to follow
later. **Therefore** careful, deliberate recordkeeping becomes as central to Vostok's science as the
research itself — the archive's own eventual complexity is a direct, cumulative product of exactly
this practice.

## 7. Equipment That Neither Outpost Can Make Alone

Vostok's genetics program and Kunlun's observatory both operate at the extreme edge of what a single,
isolated outpost can sustain on its own. **But** neither facility can produce every piece of
specialized equipment or reagent its own frontier-edge work requires. **Therefore** the two outposts
develop a genuine, practical resource-sharing relationship — Kunlun's comms relay carrying requests
for supplies neither site can make itself, Vostok offering computational modeling or cold-tolerant
research relevant to Kunlun's own extreme-environment engineering in return. **But** sustaining that
exchange means both sites trusting each other's judgment about what actually matters. **Therefore**
"two loneliest outposts" stops being just an emotional description and becomes a real, functioning
infrastructure partnership neither could easily replace.

## 8. Words That Outlived the Speakers

Vostok's founding Russian researchers label their equipment, archive sections, and facility
terminology in their own language as a simple, practical matter of course. **But** the population
that eventually inherits the city speaks primarily English and Japanese, with no living connection to
Russian at all. **Therefore** those original labels never get formally translated or replaced — too
embedded in the facility's own working structure to bother changing. **But** using inherited
terminology without understanding its origin means residents end up fluent in words they can't
actually parse. **Therefore** everyday life at Vostok settles into a strange linguistic layering —
ordinary conversation in one language, technical vocabulary in another nobody currently living there
actually speaks.

## 9. Yelena's Ledger

Among the last of Vostok's founding-era researchers, Archivist Yelena Voskresenskaya keeps her own
personal notes and cataloguing system running long after the wider community around her has thinned to
almost nothing. **But** a cataloguing system built by one person, for their own use, doesn't
necessarily make sense to anyone who comes after. **Therefore** her particular corner of the archive
becomes one of its oldest and most difficult layers to interpret — idiosyncratic, deeply personal,
genuinely valuable, and genuinely hard to parse all at once. **But** her work still gets preserved
rather than discarded, since nobody arriving later has the standing to simply throw out a founding-era
researcher's life work. **Therefore** her ledger survives as a permanent, half-legible monument to the
very last stretch of Vostok's original community — present in the archive, understood by almost no one.

## 10. Too Big for One Mind

Generations of researchers at Vostok each contribute their own genetic sequencing data, their own
terminology, their own areas of focus to the growing Lake Vostok archive. **But** centuries of
researchers working without ever coordinating directly with each other across that span means no
single research program's records fully align with any other's. **Therefore** the archive accumulates
into something genuinely too vast and internally inconsistent for any one mind, human or robot, to
hold completely. **But** the work still has to get done despite that fragmentation. **Therefore**
understanding gets distributed across the whole research population instead — no one person
comprehends the whole archive, but the community together, piece by piece, still manages to make it
function.

## 11. Right After the Expansion

Kunlun's comms-relay facility undergoes its major expansion, scaled to match the astronomy program
rather than staying permanently subordinate to it — giving Kunlun real spare capacity to reach outward
for the first time. **But** a Vostok researcher is genuinely uncertain what two such differently-focused
outposts could actually offer each other, extreme isolation aside. **Therefore** the two sites work out
a concrete first exchange rather than a vague gesture — Kunlun's comms carrying supply requests neither
site can source alone, Vostok offering computational modeling and cold-tolerance research relevant to
Kunlun's own engineering in return. **But** a partnership built on genuine exchange rather than
sentiment has to keep proving its own worth to last. **Therefore** it does — the relationship becomes
durable precisely because it started as something concrete and practical, dated to the direct aftermath
of Kunlun's own expansion rather than existing as an undated, always-assumed fact.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#2, #3, and #4** trace Vostok's full demographic arc — the founders' quiet decline, the
redistributed population's arrival, and their own deliberate choice to take up the work — giving the
Megasheet's own central "inherited legend" tension an actual causal sequence. **#5** is the richest
individual thread of the ten, since it's the only one built directly from already-sanctioned candidate
material for Vostok's own resident geneticist, giving her established backstory proposal a fuller
dramatized shape. **#11** resolves a genuine cross-city timeline constraint, anchoring chain #7's
Vostok-Kunlun partnership to a specific, consistent historical moment rather than leaving it undated.
