## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Vostok Station's earliest exile administrators need a population that can actually tolerate the site's
extreme isolation, not merely endure it — a genuinely different requirement from most Tepenian
settlements, where survival alone is usually the whole bar.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Ordinary exile settlement doesn't naturally sort people by psychological fit for total remoteness — the
usual process assigns exiles to sites based on nationality, timing, and available capacity, none of which
has anything to do with whether a given person can actually thrive, rather than merely survive, at the
most isolated research site in Tepenia. Someone responsible for staffing Vostok has to depart from the
ordinary process and propose actual, deliberate psychological selection instead — screening candidates
specifically for whether they find genuine extreme isolation acceptable as the cost of doing the science.
But departing from the standard assignment process means overriding the ordinary claims of exiles who
would otherwise have been sent here regardless of fitness, and someone within the standard administration
has real reason to resist a selection criterion that effectively tells some exiles they're unsuitable for
a posting they might otherwise have simply been assigned to.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone advocating for
deliberate psychological screening at Vostok specifically, arguing that ordinary assignment risks sending
people who will genuinely suffer or fail under conditions this extreme, and an ordinary exile
administrator committed to the standard, more impartial assignment process, wary of introducing a
selection criterion that could easily become a pretext for arbitrary exclusion. A's position is
compassionate and practical; B's is a legitimate defense of fairness and administrative consistency,
worried about what precedent a psychologically-screened posting sets elsewhere.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Vostok Station
administrator or an early Federation psychologist, directly responsible for the site's staffing and aware,
firsthand, of what extreme isolation actually does to people poorly suited to it. Character B is
plausibly a broader exile-resettlement administrator, responsible for the ordinary, standardized
assignment process across many sites, concerned that Vostok's exception could be cited to justify
selective, unequal treatment of exiles elsewhere.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A's case prevails, narrowly and with real limits attached — Vostok gets its exception, but bounded and
justified specifically by the site's uniquely extreme isolation rather than as a general precedent. The
founding community forms through deliberate selection — people who found genuine extreme isolation
acceptable as the cost of doing the science, chosen specifically rather than simply assigned.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the selection was granted only as a narrow, justified exception rather than a general policy,
Vostok's own founding population ends up smaller than an ordinary assignment process would have produced
— selecting narrowly for psychological fit necessarily excludes many exiles who might otherwise have been
sent here, leaving the settlement with no natural mechanism to grow much larger from the outset.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the founding population stays small by design rather than accident, Vostok's earliest civic
institutions — housing, governance, social structure — are built from the very beginning around a
population that will likely never scale the way an ordinarily-assigned settlement would, shaping
everything from the station's physical layout to its social expectations around permanent, deliberate
smallness rather than anticipated future growth.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Vostok's whole founding character locks in early as necessarily specialized and necessarily thin — a
choice, made through a real and contested administrative exception, that shapes everything the city
becomes centuries before anyone could see where it was heading.
