## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The newly-arrived population finds an already-running, genuinely significant research operation in
progress at Vostok, built by people they have no ancestral connection to at all — inherited infrastructure
and inherited purpose, neither one chosen.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Inheriting infrastructure isn't the same as inheriting obligation — nobody who arrives owes the founders'
project anything, whatever documented legacy was left behind for them to find. Each new resident makes an
individual choice about whether to stay and take part in the Lake Vostok work or move on to somewhere
less demanding. But the work itself turns out to be genuinely compelling on its own terms, not merely
something left behind to finish — and as word of the research's actual substance spreads among the
newly-arrived population, a real disagreement forms between those drawn in by the work's genuine
scientific interest and those who resent feeling any pressure, implicit or explicit, to continue a
mission that was never theirs to begin with.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a newly-arrived resident who
has genuinely fallen into the Lake Vostok work, fascinated by it on its own scientific merits, and another
resident from the same displaced population who resents the growing social expectation — informal, never
officially stated, but felt nonetheless — that everyone who ends up at Vostok should eventually take up

resident with some scientific or technical background, drawn into the research almost accidentally after
initial exposure to the documented handover material. Character B is plausibly another member of the same
displaced population, with no particular interest in Lake Vostok research, frustrated at feeling
implicitly judged by A's own enthusiasm and the wider community's growing admiration for those who do
take up the work.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The disagreement surfaces the implicit pressure openly enough that Vostok's community explicitly, publicly
disavows it — a resident choosing not to engage with the Lake Vostok research is affirmed, out loud, as
making just as legitimate a choice as one who does. Most who stay end up drawn into the research because
it's worth doing, not out of any felt duty to complete someone else's unfinished mission — the legend
becomes genuinely theirs by choice rather than by inheritance.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the community explicitly disavowed any expectation of participation, Vostok develops a genuinely
mixed civic population for the first time — a substantial cohort actively engaged in the Lake Vostok
research alongside an equally legitimate cohort with no connection to it at all, neither treated as more
authentically "Vostok" than the other.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because non-participation carries no informal stigma, Vostok's own civic identity develops room for
genuine diversity of purpose within a single small, isolated population — support roles, logistics,
maintenance, and ordinary civic life staffed by residents who never needed a scientific rationale to
belong there, giving the settlement a more resilient, less monocultural social fabric than a purely
mission-driven community would have had.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Vostok's population now genuinely divides between those actively carrying the Lake Vostok legend forward
by choice and those who simply live and work there for their own reasons — both equally at home, a city
whose central research mission no longer defines who actually belongs.
