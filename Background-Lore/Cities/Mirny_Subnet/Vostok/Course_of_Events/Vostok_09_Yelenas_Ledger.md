## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Among the last of Vostok's founding-era researchers, Archivist Yelena Voskresenskaya keeps her own
personal notes and cataloguing system running long after the wider community around her has thinned to
almost nothing — a solitary discipline, sustained well past the point where anyone else is left to share
it with.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A cataloguing system built by one person, for their own use, doesn't necessarily make sense to anyone who
comes after — Yelena's own idiosyncratic shorthand, private organizational logic, and personal annotations
were never designed to be legible to a stranger. Her particular corner of the archive becomes one of its
oldest and most difficult layers to interpret — idiosyncratic, deeply personal, genuinely valuable, and
genuinely hard to parse all at once. But her work still gets preserved rather than discarded, since
nobody arriving later has the standing to simply throw out a founding-era researcher's life work — and
once later generations of Vostok's own population take up the archive, one of its own newer researchers,
confronting Yelena's incomprehensible corner of the archive for the first time, has to actually decide
how much effort a stranger's personal, half-legible legacy is worth investing in trying to understand.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation, largely internal to the newly-
arrived research community, between a researcher determined to actually decode Yelena's own idiosyncratic
system, arguing the archive's oldest layer might hold genuine scientific value nobody else has ever
properly accessed, and a colleague who considers the effort a poor use of limited research time compared
to more immediately productive, better-documented work elsewhere in the archive. A's instinct honors the
founding legacy directly; B's skepticism is a reasonable, practical assessment of where limited effort is
actually best spent.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a newly-arrived
researcher with a particular interest in the archive's own history, perhaps drawn specifically to Yelena's
corner of it out of curiosity about the founding community she represents. Character B is plausibly a
more results-focused colleague from the same newly-arrived population, prioritizing immediately usable
data over historical or archival curiosity.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A commits real, sustained personal effort to the decoding project, working through Yelena's shorthand
slowly and largely alone, and eventually makes genuine, if partial, headway — not a full translation, but
enough to establish that at least some of Yelena's records hold data no other part of the archive
duplicates. Her ledger survives as a permanent, half-legible monument to the very last stretch of Vostok's
original community — present in the archive, understood by almost no one, but now, at least in part,
by someone.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because A's partial decoding work established that Yelena's records genuinely contain unique data, the
research community formally reclassifies her corner of the archive from "historical curiosity, low
priority" to "unique data source, worth continued investment" — a real, if modest, shift in how the
archive's own oldest layer gets treated going forward.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Yelena's corner is now formally treated as a genuine data source rather than mere historical
curiosity, subsequent researchers periodically return to it over the following generations, each
contributing incremental further decoding — a slow, cumulative, multi-generational project that never
fully completes, but steadily recovers more of what Yelena actually recorded, entirely because A's
original, unpressured effort proved there was something real worth recovering.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Yelena Voskresenskaya's ledger stands as a formally recognized, if still only partially decoded, unique
layer of Vostok's archive — no longer simply preserved out of respect for a founder's memory, but actively,
if slowly, still being worked through by researchers generations removed from her, precisely because one
of them once decided it was worth the effort.
