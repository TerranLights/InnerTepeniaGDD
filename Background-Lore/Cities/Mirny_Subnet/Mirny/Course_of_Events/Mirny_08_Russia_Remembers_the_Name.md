## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Mirny's founding population names the city for a real historic Russian sloop, carrying genuine
institutional pride into the earliest exile years — the name means something specific and remembered to
the founders who chose it, tied directly to Bellingshausen's own 1819–1821 expedition.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Later immigration waves eventually make China demographically Primary, with Russia settling into
Significant tier behind it — the numbers shift substantially over generations, exactly the kind of
demographic dilution that, in most Tepenian founding-nation stories, leaves the original naming heritage
as an empty label nobody quite remembers the reason for. The founding naming heritage could easily fade
into exactly that. But a smaller, still-present Russian-descended community doesn't have to let that
happen passively — someone within that community has to actually decide the story is worth actively
preserving, and has to do the work of preserving it, at a moment when the community's own numerical
influence over the city's broader civic direction is visibly shrinking and there's a real temptation to
simply let the founding story go quiet along with it.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation less between opposing sides than
between resignation and resolve within the same community — someone within Mirny's Russian-descended
population who sees the demographic shift as reason to stop investing energy in a story that fewer and
fewer residents will ever personally connect to, and someone who insists the story matters precisely
*because* fewer people carry it now, not despite that. This works best as a generational or personal
disagreement inside a shrinking community rather than an external threat — nobody outside is trying to
erase the naming heritage; the real risk is quiet neglect from within.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an older or more
practically-minded member of Mirny's Russian-descended community, tired of maintaining traditions that
feel increasingly disconnected from the city's actual demographic present. Character B is plausibly a
younger community member, or a dedicated local historian or archivist, who takes on the *Mirny* and
Bellingshausen's expedition as a deliberate personal project — treating it as institutional memory worth
preserving specifically because nobody else in the city is going to do it if this community doesn't.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's insistence wins out, materially — the story of the *Mirny* and Bellingshausen's expedition gets
actively documented, retold, and kept alive within the founding-era community rather than left to fade,
treated as institutional memory worth preserving regardless of numbers. The name stays genuinely
meaningful to a real, if numerically smaller, community — present and active rather than a hollow label
nobody remembers the reason for.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the preservation effort was deliberate and documented rather than informal, it produces an
actual, lasting artifact — some form of recorded history, oral archive, or small commemorative practice
tied specifically to the *Mirny* and Bellingshausen's voyage — something concrete that exists now and
didn't exist before, distinct from the naming heritage simply persisting as vague background sentiment.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because a concrete, documented artifact now exists rather than just an informal oral memory, it becomes
something the wider city — not just the Russian-descended community — can actually encounter and engage
with directly, whether through a small civic display, a taught piece of local history, or simply a
resource newcomers of any background can be pointed toward. The founding story stops being something only
the founding-heritage community keeps alive privately, and becomes, gradually, something the whole city
can access if it chooses to.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mirny's name carries real, documented meaning that outlasted its founders' own numerical decline — kept
alive deliberately by a shrinking community that refused to let it go quiet, and now accessible, in
concrete form, to any resident of any background curious enough to actually look.
