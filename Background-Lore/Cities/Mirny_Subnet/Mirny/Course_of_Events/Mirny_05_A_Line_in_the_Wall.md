## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

As Mirny's civic identity solidifies beyond pure survival, residents want some way to mark the city's own
precise position directly on the Antarctic Circle — a genuine geographic fact worth acknowledging, with
no existing precedent in the city for how to actually do it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A separate ornamental monument would sit oddly against a city whose whole built environment exists for
function, not decoration — Mirny has never built anything purely for display, and a freestanding marker
would be the first. Someone proposes folding the marker directly into the windbreak ring's own outer wall
instead, a plain, functional plate at the exact latitude. But that means physically altering the ring
itself — cutting into, or building onto, the exact structure the whole city depends on for survival — and
whoever maintains the windbreak's structural integrity has real, professional reason to resist any
modification to it, symbolic value or not, especially one requested by people with no engineering stake
in whether the wall still holds.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone advocating for the
marker's placement, for whom the windbreak wall is the only honest, meaningful surface Mirny actually has
to put it on, and the windwright or structural engineer responsible for the wall's integrity, for whom
any modification — however minor, however carefully planned — represents a real, unnecessary risk to the
one piece of infrastructure the entire city's survival depends on. This isn't sentiment versus
engineering exactly; it's a genuine disagreement about how much risk a purely symbolic addition is worth
taking on a structure with zero margin for failure.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic-minded resident
or early administrator pushing for some tangible acknowledgment of the city's exact geographic
significance. Character B is plausibly a windwright or structural engineer, quite possibly a successor
or apprentice in Bellandry's own line, whose entire professional responsibility is the wall's continued
integrity, and who has to be genuinely convinced — through careful design, not through appeals to
sentiment — that the marker won't compromise it.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The engineer signs off, but only after the marker's design is reworked entirely around structural
safety — a shallow, load-neutral plate mounted rather than cut in, positioned at a point in the wall the
engineer identifies as carrying the least structural stress. The marker gets built directly into the
windbreak ring's own outer wall, visible from both inside and outside the shelter, exactly as proposed,
but only in the specific form the wall's own guardian was willing to certify as safe.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the marker's final placement was determined by structural safety rather than visual prominence,
it ends up somewhere quiet and unremarkable rather than at any obvious civic focal point — exactly the
kind of location someone would only find by already knowing to look for it, rather than stumbling across
it while exploring the city.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the marker isn't somewhere a visitor would naturally find it, showing someone the marker becomes
a genuine, personal gesture among residents — something a longtime Mirny resident does deliberately for
someone they want to welcome properly, rather than something the city advertises or a visitor stumbles
onto alone. The marker becomes something residents actively point out to visitors rather than something
the city puts on display.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mirny's Antarctic Circle marker sits quietly embedded in the windbreak wall, exactly where the wall's
guardian judged it safe rather than where it would be most visible — quiet significance embedded in
plain infrastructure, shown to newcomers by residents who choose to share it, exactly the way Mirny
handles everything else worth being proud of.
