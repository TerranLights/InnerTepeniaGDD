## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Early settlers at Mirny face katabatic wind severe enough to be genuinely lethal, not merely
uncomfortable — the earliest structures are put up individually, each building fending for itself
against a wind the site's exact geography channels and accelerates.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Scattered, individually-defended buildings leave gaps the wind always finds a way through — a structure
strong enough to survive on its own still funnels dangerous gusts into the spaces between it and its
neighbors, and the individual-defense approach starts costing lives faster than the settlement can
absorb. Someone has to propose something more radical than reinforcing each building further: a dense,
concentric ring of buildings, two or three layers deep, shaped specifically to shed the wind rather than
resist it head-on, with the vulnerable industrial core sheltered entirely inside. But committing to a
plan like that means abandoning buildings already standing, redesigning the settlement's whole layout
from the ground up, and asking a population already stretched thin by survival to invest scarce labor
and material into a structure that won't fully prove itself for years.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone responsible for
the settlement's immediate survival, who has to weigh the very real cost of tearing up an imperfect but
functioning defensive layout against an unproven radical redesign, and the windwright proposing that
redesign, who has to make the case that continuing to reinforce individual buildings is a losing strategy
no amount of additional labor will actually fix. Neither side is being unreasonable — A is responsible
for keeping people alive through the next storm, not the one five years from now; B is responsible for
making sure there's a settlement left in five years at all. The tension is fundamentally about time
horizon, not about competence or vision.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an early settlement
leader or council figure charged with allocating the exile population's scarce labor and material toward
whatever keeps the most people alive through the immediate winter. Character B is, per established canon,
Chief Windwright Osric Bellandry — an engineer whose read of the wind's actual behavior convinces them
that the individual-defense approach is structurally doomed no matter how much effort goes into it, and
who has to win over a leadership more naturally inclined toward incremental, lower-risk fixes.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Bellandry's design is adopted — not unanimously, and not without real risk taken on faith, but adopted.
Mirny grows outward in rings rather than sprawl for the rest of its history — the windbreak's original
shape setting the terms for everything built after it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the ring design was adopted under real institutional risk rather than obvious, uncontested
consensus, Mirny's earliest civic memory preserves the decision itself as a formative act — the settlement
develops a lasting suspicion of purely incremental fixes to structural problems, a bias toward addressing
root causes directly even when doing so is disruptive, that traces back to this one specific, contested
choice.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That inherited bias toward root-cause solutions over incremental patchwork later shapes how Mirny's own
industrial planners approach the city's growing manufacturing role for the wider subnet — rather than
expanding production piecemeal as demand rises, they invest early in centralized, durable infrastructure
built to scale ahead of need, the same design philosophy that built the windbreak now applied to Mirny's
economic backbone.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mirny stands as a city built in concentric rings, its whole civic instinct now shaped by the windbreak's
founding lesson: structural problems get structural solutions, not patchwork ones, however disruptive the
real fix turns out to be — an instinct that will go on to shape far more than architecture alone.
