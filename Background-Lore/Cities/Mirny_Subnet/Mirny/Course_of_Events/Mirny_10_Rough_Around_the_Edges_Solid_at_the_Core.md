## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Mirny's entertainment options stay deliberately minimal — a handful of rough, unpolished bars, nothing
elaborate, built by and for a population with little spare time or energy to invest in anything more.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A working population enduring real hardship still needs somewhere to unwind together, and those modest
bars become genuinely important civic spaces regardless of how little polish they offer, valued for what
they provide rather than how they look. A visitor expecting anything resembling Casey's Splinters or
Mawson's own hospitality culture finds Mirny's version distinctly underwhelming by comparison — and when
enough visiting traders, administrators, or subnet colleagues make that comparison openly, sometimes
undiplomatically, it starts to feel to some Mirny residents like an actual civic slight rather than an
idle observation, however little the visitors themselves meant by it.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a Mirny resident, possibly
someone running one of the city's own modest bars, who feels genuinely stung by repeated unfavorable
comparisons to Casey's or Mawson's polish and wants to invest in upgrading Mirny's own venues to compete
on those terms, and another resident who insists that trying to compete on someone else's terms would
betray exactly the plain, function-first identity that makes Mirny what it is. Both positions come from
real civic pride, just aimed in opposite directions — one wants pride defended by matching the comparison,
the other wants it defended by refusing to accept the comparison as valid in the first place.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly the proprietor of one
of Mirny's own bars, personally exposed to visitor comments and genuinely tempted to invest real effort
and material into a more polished version of their venue. Character B is plausibly a longtime patron or
fellow proprietor, someone whose own sense of Mirny's identity is deeply tied to exactly the roughness
being criticized, arguing that "fixing" the bars would be solving a problem that was never actually
Mirny's to solve.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's position holds. The proprietor considers the upgrade and ultimately abandons it, convinced that
matching Casey's or Mawson's polish would mean building something that no longer actually served Mirny's
own population the way the rough version always has. Mirny never tries to compete on those terms — its
own bars stay exactly as rough as the city around them, honest rather than impressive, and residents
wouldn't have it any other way.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the deliberate decision not to compete gets talked through openly rather than simply defaulting
by inertia, Mirny's residents end up with an actual, articulated reason for their own entertainment
culture's plainness — not simply "we never got around to it," but a real, examined civic position that
can be explained to the next visitor who makes the same comparison.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that articulated position now exists, longtime residents start using it, half-jokingly, as a
genuine point of local pride when visitors comment unfavorably — turning what used to sting into a
running, self-aware civic joke about Mirny's own deliberate lack of polish, told by residents themselves
rather than only heard from outsiders.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mirny's bars remain exactly as rough as ever, but the roughness now carries a deliberately examined,
self-aware civic pride behind it rather than simple habit — a city that has actually talked through why
it doesn't want to be Casey or Mawson, and decided, on its own terms, that it doesn't.
