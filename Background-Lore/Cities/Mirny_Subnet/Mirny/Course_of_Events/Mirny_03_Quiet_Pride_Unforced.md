## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Mirny's daily life is genuinely rough — minimal entertainment, hard physical work, a city built to
survive rather than to charm anyone, with nothing in its early years resembling a deliberate civic
identity beyond staying alive and keeping the industrial yards running.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Hardship alone doesn't automatically produce the kind of civic pride that actually holds a community
together — plenty of hard places produce nothing more than exhausted endurance. Something else forms
alongside it at Mirny: a real, unforced sense of kinship among people who all understand exactly what
living here demands, without ever needing to perform that understanding for outsiders. But pride this
quiet is easy for visitors, and even for some of Mirny's own younger residents raised on stories of
"softer" cities elsewhere in Tepenia, to mistake for simple grimness — and once that mistake starts
getting repeated back to Mirny by outsiders often enough, someone within the city eventually has to
decide whether it's worth correcting, or whether correcting it would itself betray the whole point of
pride that was never built to be explained.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a younger or more
outward-facing resident who wants Mirny to actively push back against outsiders' "grim" read of the
city — through some visible gesture, campaign, or civic effort to be better understood — and an older or
more settled resident who believes explaining the pride at all would cheapen it. This is a conflict
about whether dignity requires visibility to be real, and it should resist an easy answer: A's
frustration at being constantly misread is legitimate, and B's instinct that some things lose their
meaning the moment they're performed for an audience is equally legitimate.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a younger Mirny
resident with more direct contact with the wider Federation — someone in a trade or communications role
that puts them regularly in front of outsiders, tired of watching Mirny get characterized as merely
bleak. Character B is plausibly an older, longer-tenured figure, possibly someone who helped keep the
city running through its hardest early decades, for whom the very idea of campaigning to be understood
misses what the pride was actually for in the first place.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A tastes real, and loses. No campaign, no civic gesture gets mounted — Mirny stays content to be misread,
exactly as B argued. The pride was never built for outside approval, only for the people who actually
have to live inside the ring every day.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the city deliberately chose not to correct outsiders' misreading, Mirny develops no formal
tourism, hospitality, or outward-facing cultural apparatus of the kind other Tepenian cities build to
manage their own reputations — an absence that becomes conspicuous by contrast once neighboring cities
in the subnet begin developing exactly that kind of infrastructure for themselves.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Without any outward-facing cultural apparatus competing for resources or attention, Mirny's civic
investment stays concentrated almost entirely on function — industrial capacity, infrastructure,
maintenance — since there's no parallel "how we present ourselves" budget or effort drawing labor and
material away from it. The city's own practical, unglamorous economic strength is, in part, a direct
consequence of never having built the machinery to manage its own image.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mirny remains, by deliberate and repeatedly reaffirmed choice, a city that doesn't explain itself to
outsiders — and that choice has left its civic resources concentrated entirely on substance over
presentation, a quiet, unforced kind of pride that shapes not just how residents feel about their home,
but where the city's own limited investment actually goes.
