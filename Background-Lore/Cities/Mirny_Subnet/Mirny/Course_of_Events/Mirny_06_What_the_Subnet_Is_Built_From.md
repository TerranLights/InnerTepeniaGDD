## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Mirny's industrial yards produce raw quarried material and manufactured goods from the earliest exile
years onward — a local economy, serving local needs, with no particular expectation that its output
matters much beyond the city's own windbreak ring.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A subnet with six member cities and one real highway network between them needs a steady, reliable
source of construction and maintenance materials to keep that network functional at all — and Mirny's
own quarries and yards turn out to be positioned, by simple geography, closer to the emerging Hwy 110
corridor than any comparable industrial capacity elsewhere in the subnet. Mirny's own industrial output
starts growing to serve exactly that regional need. But scaling from a local economy into the subnet's
literal construction engine means Mirny's own production planners now have to weigh local civic needs
against a growing set of external demands from cities that have no say in how Mirny allocates its own
labor and material — and the first time local and subnet-wide priorities genuinely collide over the same
finite output, someone has to decide which claim actually comes first.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone responsible for
Mirny's own local infrastructure needs — housing expansion, windbreak maintenance, the ordinary upkeep
of a growing city — and someone representing the wider subnet's dependence on Mirny's output for Hwy 110
itself, at a moment when the yards' finite capacity genuinely cannot satisfy both demands at once. Neither
side is being selfish: A is protecting the city that actually has to live inside whatever gets built or
neglected; B is protecting the literal physical connection tying six cities together, a connection Mirny
itself depends on just as much as anyone else in the subnet.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Mirny civic planner
or windbreak maintenance supervisor, focused on the city's own immediate structural needs and wary of
becoming, in effect, subordinate to demand from cities that don't share Mirny's own risks. Character B is
plausibly a highway administrator or subnet-wide logistics coordinator, quite possibly based at Mirny
itself given its hub role, whose job depends entirely on Mirny's output actually reaching the rest of the
network on schedule.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A formal production-allocation framework gets negotiated rather than either side simply prevailing — a
fixed baseline share of Mirny's own output permanently reserved for local civic needs before any subnet-
wide allocation is calculated, protecting the city's own maintenance and growth from ever being fully
crowded out. Mirny's own industrial output grows to serve the subnet's highway network, becoming the
literal construction engine for Hwy 110 and everything connected to it, without the city's own needs
being sacrificed to get there.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the allocation framework has to be formally negotiated and tracked rather than handled informally,
Mirny develops the subnet's first real administrative apparatus dedicated specifically to production
planning — a standing office whose entire job is balancing local and regional demand on Mirny's own
industrial output, something no other Mirny subnet city needs in quite the same form since none of them
carry the same doubled hub role.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that production-planning office already exists and already has established, working relationships
with every other subnet city's own logistics contacts, it becomes the natural body other cities turn to
whenever a new subnet-wide infrastructure question comes up — not because Mirny was granted any formal
authority over the rest of the subnet, but because the office built specifically to manage Mirny's own
output allocation is, by simple accident of function, the only administrative body in the subnet that
already talks regularly to all six cities at once.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mirny's hub status is now genuinely doubled in practice, not just in name — an administrative and
communications center, and the physical source of the materials holding the rest of the subnet's
infrastructure together, with a production-planning apparatus built to manage that second role that has
quietly become the subnet's own de facto coordination point for far more than construction materials
alone.
