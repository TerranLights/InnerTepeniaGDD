## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Mirny's earliest residents notice something the site's exact latitude makes possible — a few days near
each solstice where the sun grazes the horizon instead of fully rising or setting, a genuine atmospheric
rarity nobody in the founding generation had any particular reason to expect.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city this focused on function and survival has little established habit of pausing to notice anything
purely atmospheric — the earliest years leave essentially no time or attention for anything that doesn't
directly serve keeping people alive and the industrial yards running. The observation takes hold anyway,
informally at first, residents simply stepping outside to watch. But informal habits can fade as easily
as they start, especially in a population working brutal hours with genuinely little energy left over —
and as the founding generation ages and a busier, more industrially demanding city takes shape around
their children, someone eventually has to decide whether the habit is worth actively passing on, or
whether it simply quietly lapses the way most informal founding-era customs eventually do once the people
who started them are gone.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a low-stakes but genuinely felt confrontation
between someone focused entirely on Mirny's practical survival, for whom stepping outside to watch the
sun graze the horizon reads as an indulgence the city's harsh schedule can't really afford, and someone
who wants to actively teach the next generation to keep watching, arguing that a city built entirely
around wind and industry needs at least one thing that has nothing to do with either. Neither side is
wrong about their own priorities — A's practicality kept people alive through the founding years; B is
simply the first person to notice that survival alone isn't quite the same as a life worth living inside.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a founding-generation
parent or work-crew supervisor, entirely reasonable in prioritizing rest and labor over what looks, on
its face, like an unproductive habit. Character B is plausibly that same generation's child, or a younger
resident who first watched the solstice as a child themselves and refuses to let the habit lapse now that
they're old enough to actually organize others around it.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B wins, not through argument but through sheer persistence — deliberately bringing children, coworkers,
and eventually whole work crews outside at the right moment each solstice, until the observation becomes
something newcomers are told to expect and residents genuinely anticipate, generation after generation,
the one moment in Mirny's whole calendar that has nothing to do with wind, industry, or survival at all.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the tradition survived specifically through deliberate, person-to-person teaching rather than
official civic sponsorship, it never becomes a scheduled municipal event with fixed logistics — no
official viewing platform, no organized ceremony, just residents independently making sure whoever's
nearby knows the moment is coming. The observation stays genuinely informal even after becoming
universal, since formalizing it was never how it actually spread.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because there's no official viewing infrastructure, residents end up watching from wherever they happen
to be working or living when the moment arrives — industrial yard, windbreak wall, doorway — meaning the
solstice observation becomes something experienced individually, in dozens of small groups scattered
across the ring, rather than as one unified civic gathering. Mirny's one purely atmospheric tradition
ends up shaped by the same withdrawn, unforced character as everything else about the city.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mirny keeps its solstice observation exactly as informally as it began — passed hand to hand across
generations rather than organized from above, watched in scattered small groups across the ring rather
than as one gathering, the one moment in the city's calendar that stayed genuinely unforced precisely
because nobody ever tried to formalize it.
