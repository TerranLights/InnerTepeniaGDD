## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Decades after the windbreak's completion, Mirny runs on the ring's design as settled, working
infrastructure — trusted, unremarkable, no longer actively debated the way it was when Bellandry first
proposed it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

An unusually severe storm system tests the design directly — worse than anything the original engineers
explicitly planned for, worse than anything the current generation has personally lived through. The
outer ring's smooth, concentric shape does exactly what it was built to do, shedding the worst of the
wind before it ever reaches the sheltered core, and the industrial yards inside keep running through
conditions that would have shut down an ordinary city entirely. But surviving the test doesn't settle
anything on its own — in the storm's immediate aftermath, with damage assessments still coming in and
some peripheral structures genuinely battered, a real argument breaks out over whether the ring actually
held because the original design was sound, or whether the city simply got lucky this time and needs to
reinforce further before trusting the same design against whatever comes next.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation, in the immediate wake of a genuine
close call, between someone who reads the storm's outcome as full vindication of the original design and
someone who reads the same outcome as a warning that the margin was thinner than anyone realized. This
is a genuinely hard question to settle with the evidence actually available — both readings are
defensible from the same set of facts, and the real disagreement is about how much risk tolerance the
city should carry forward, not about what happened.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a structural engineer
or senior windwright, professionally and personally invested in the ring's original design, for whom the
storm's survival is proof the founding decision was correct. Character B is plausibly a more cautious
administrator or a resident who lost something in the storm's peripheral damage — someone with direct,
personal reason to distrust "it held" as the end of the conversation, pushing instead for further
reinforcement regardless of what the test technically proved.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The argument resolves into a genuine compromise rather than a clean win for either side: no fundamental
redesign, since the core structure plainly worked, but a formal program of targeted reinforcement at the
specific peripheral points the storm actually damaged — treating the test as validation of the design's
core logic and a real, useful map of its remaining weak points at once.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the reinforcement program targets specific, storm-identified weak points rather than the whole
ring uniformly, Mirny's engineers establish a formal practice of post-storm structural review after every
subsequent severe weather event — treating each major storm as free, real-world stress-test data rather
than simply an event to recover from.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That accumulating record of post-storm reviews becomes, over generations, Mirny's own informal but
genuinely rigorous body of structural knowledge about wind behavior at the site — more detailed and more
empirically grounded than the original windbreak's founding design ever had access to, since it's built
from centuries of real events rather than a single engineer's initial calculation.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mirny's windbreak ring is no longer just Bellandry's original design — it's a structure continuously
refined by generations of storm-tested data, each severe storm treated as a source of real engineering
knowledge rather than merely survived. The storm becomes local legend anyway, cited for generations
afterward as proof the original margin was never overbuilt caution, it was exactly what the city
eventually needed.
