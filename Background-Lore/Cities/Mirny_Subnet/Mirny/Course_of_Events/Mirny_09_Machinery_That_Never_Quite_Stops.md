## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Mirny's industrial yards run at genuine, near-constant activity from the earliest productive years
onward — the machinery that keeps the subnet supplied with construction material doesn't pause for
anything short of a genuine emergency.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A population living directly inside the same ring as that machinery has to actually coexist with the
noise and rhythm of near-continuous industrial work, since Mirny's own architecture folds residential
space directly into the sheltered inner ring rather than keeping it separate. The sound and motion of
running machinery becomes ordinary background to daily life rather than something residents notice or
resent — the city's own pulse, indistinguishable from simply being home. But not every resident arrives
already used to it — newcomers, visiting workers, or families relocated from quieter subnet cities find
the constant activity genuinely disorienting, and at some point a newcomer's open, repeated complaints
about the noise start actually mattering enough that someone has to decide whether Mirny owes new
arrivals any accommodation at all, or whether adjusting is simply the newcomer's own problem to solve.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a newcomer struggling
genuinely, not performatively, with the machinery's constant presence — sleep disruption, real
psychological strain — and a longtime resident or civic figure who has to decide how much the city's
built environment should bend to accommodate someone who chose, or was assigned, to move here. This
shouldn't be framed as the newcomer being weak or the resident being callous; Mirny's whole architecture
was never designed with acoustic comfort in mind, and there's a genuine, unresolved question about
whether that omission is simply an inherent cost of living in a working industrial city, or an oversight
actually worth correcting now that the population includes people who didn't grow up inside it.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a newly-relocated
resident — a technical worker, an administrator's family member, or someone reassigned from a quieter

Character B is plausibly a longtime Mirny resident or civic planner, personally unbothered by the
machinery's presence and genuinely uncertain whether accommodating one newcomer's difficulty is fair to
the rest of a population that's simply always lived this way.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A modest, concrete accommodation gets made rather than either extreme — some residential sections nearer
the ring's quieter, less mechanically dense stretches get informally designated as better suited to
newcomers still adjusting, without any formal citywide acoustic overhaul. "Does it ever stop" becomes one
of the most common questions newcomers ask, and one longtime residents never quite know how to answer,
since to them the machinery running is simply what Mirny sounds like.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because certain residential stretches are now informally understood as "newcomer-friendly," Mirny
develops its first real, if unofficial, internal geography distinguishing sections of the ring by lived
experience rather than by function alone — not a formal zoning system, but a genuine, shared local
knowledge about which parts of the city feel different to live in.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That informal geography becomes, over time, the closest thing Mirny has to a transitional community —
newcomers placed in the quieter stretches end up living alongside each other during their own adjustment
period, forming friendships and mutual-support networks specifically around the shared experience of
still getting used to the machinery, before most eventually move on to other parts of the ring once
they've acclimated.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mirny's ring now carries an unofficial but real internal geography — quieter stretches informally
understood as where newcomers land first, functioning as a kind of transitional community built entirely
around the shared, temporary experience of learning to live with a city that never quite stops running.
