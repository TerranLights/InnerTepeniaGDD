## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Later immigration into Mirny settles overwhelmingly around a Chinese-led demographic current, with no
American presence developing at any tier — a pattern unlike anywhere else in Tepenia, taking shape
gradually across generations of ordinary post-founding arrival rather than through any deliberate policy.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city this far from the rest of the country's dominant demographic pattern could easily feel like an
anomaly worth correcting — and at some point, a wave of visiting administrators, tradespeople, or
Federation officials from elsewhere in Tepenia start treating Mirny's demographic makeup as something
requiring explanation, or even active rebalancing, the way an unusual statistic on a report might draw a
reviewer's attention. Mirny's own residents never treat it as an anomaly themselves — the city's whole
practical, function-first culture has little use for worrying about how its demographics compare to
anywhere else. But outside pressure to "correct" or explain the imbalance doesn't simply evaporate because
Mirny itself is unbothered by it — someone eventually has to actually respond to it, one way or another,
when an outside body raises the question formally rather than just in passing.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between an outside Federation
administrator who genuinely believes Mirny's demographic pattern reflects some kind of correctable
imbalance worth actively addressing — perhaps through incentivized resettlement or targeted outreach —
and a Mirny resident or local administrator who sees the whole premise of the question as a category
error, imported from cities that actually care about matching some national demographic template. This
works best without villainizing the outside administrator — their concern is sincere and, from their own
frame of reference, reasonable; Mirny's response isn't defensive so much as genuinely baffled that anyone
would think this needs fixing.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Federation census or
resettlement official visiting from elsewhere in Tepenia, applying a standard they've seen work
elsewhere without first checking whether it actually fits Mirny's own civic character. Character B is
plausibly a local Mirny administrator or simply a long-tenured resident asked to respond on the city's
behalf — someone whose entire argument amounts to pointing out that nothing about daily life in Mirny is
actually broken, whatever the numbers alone might suggest to someone reading them from elsewhere.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The proposed rebalancing effort quietly dies, not through confrontation but through Mirny's own flat,
practical indifference to the premise — no counter-campaign, no formal rebuttal, just a population that
keeps doing exactly what it was already doing and lets the outside concern lose momentum on its own.
Outsiders visiting for the first time sometimes remark on the demographic pattern anyway, unprompted, and
residents grow used to a small, recurring moment of mild surprise — acknowledged, shrugged off, never
treated as anything requiring explanation.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the rebalancing effort visibly failed to gain any traction against Mirny's own indifference,
Mirny develops a modest but real regional reputation among Federation administrators as a city whose
demographic and cultural particulars simply aren't worth trying to standardize — a quiet, informal
precedent other administrators start citing whenever a similar "correction" is proposed elsewhere.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That informal precedent — "even the Mirny rebalancing effort went nowhere" — becomes a genuine, citable
argument later administrators use to push back against similarly unnecessary standardization efforts
proposed for other demographically unusual Tepenian cities, meaning Mirny's own flat refusal to be
bothered ends up quietly protecting other cities' own demographic particulars too, entirely without Mirny
ever intending to set any kind of example.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mirny's demographic pattern remains exactly what it always was, entirely unaddressed and entirely
unbothered — and the failed attempt to correct it has quietly become a small, informal piece of
Federation administrative folklore, cited elsewhere whenever someone proposes fixing something in another
city that was, in fact, never actually broken.
