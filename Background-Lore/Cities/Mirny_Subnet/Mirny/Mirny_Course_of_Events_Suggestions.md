# Mirny — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Mirny's Megasheet (the concentric windbreak-ring
architecture, the industrial core, the solstice observation tradition). Post-settlement only; nothing
here is canon.

---

## 1. Building Its Own Shelter

Early settlers at Mirny face katabatic wind severe enough to be genuinely lethal, not merely
uncomfortable. **But** scattered, individually-defended buildings would leave gaps the wind could
always find a way through. **Therefore** Chief Windwright Osric Bellandry designs something more
radical — a dense, concentric ring of buildings, two or three layers deep, shaped specifically to shed
the wind rather than resist it head-on, with the vulnerable industrial core sheltered entirely inside.
**But** committing to that shape means every future expansion has to work within the same defensive
logic. **Therefore** Mirny grows outward in rings rather than sprawl for the rest of its history — the
windbreak's original shape setting the terms for everything built after it.

## 2. The Ring Holds

Decades after the windbreak's completion, an unusually severe storm system tests the design directly —
worse than anything the original engineers explicitly planned for. **But** the outer ring's smooth,
concentric shape does exactly what it was built to do, shedding the worst of the wind before it ever
reaches the sheltered core. **Therefore** the industrial yards inside keep running through conditions
that would have shut down an ordinary city entirely. **But** surviving the test doesn't make residents
complacent about it. **Therefore** the storm becomes local legend anyway — proof, cited for generations
afterward, that the ring's original design was never overbuilt caution, it was exactly the margin the
city eventually needed.

## 3. Quiet Pride, Unforced

Mirny's daily life is genuinely rough — minimal entertainment, hard physical work, a city built to
survive rather than to charm anyone. **But** hardship alone doesn't automatically produce the kind of
civic pride that actually holds a community together. **Therefore** something else forms alongside it:
a real, unforced sense of kinship among people who all understand exactly what living here demands,
without ever needing to perform that understanding for outsiders. **But** pride this quiet is easy for
visitors to mistake for simple grimness. **Therefore** Mirny stays content to be misread — the pride
was never built for outside approval, only for the people who actually have to live inside the ring
every day.

## 4. Two Days a Year

Mirny's earliest residents notice something the site's exact latitude makes possible — a few days near
each solstice where the sun grazes the horizon instead of fully rising or setting. **But** a city this
focused on function and survival has little established habit of pausing to notice anything purely
atmospheric. **Therefore** the observation takes hold anyway, informally at first, residents simply
stepping outside to watch. **But** informal habits can fade as easily as they start. **Therefore** it
doesn't fade — generation after generation keeps watching, until it becomes something newcomers are
told to expect and residents genuinely anticipate, the one moment in Mirny's whole calendar that has
nothing to do with wind, industry, or survival at all.

## 5. A Line in the Wall

As Mirny's civic identity solidifies, residents want some way to mark the city's own precise position
directly on the Antarctic Circle. **But** a separate ornamental monument would sit oddly against a
city whose whole built environment exists for function, not decoration. **Therefore** the marker gets
built directly into the windbreak ring's own outer wall instead — a plain, functional plate at the
exact latitude, visible from both inside and outside the shelter. **But** folding a symbolic object
into purely structural architecture means it could easily go unnoticed by anyone not looking for it.
**Therefore** the marker becomes something residents actively point out to visitors rather than
something the city advertises — quiet significance embedded in plain infrastructure, exactly the way
Mirny handles everything else worth being proud of.

## 6. What the Subnet Is Built From

Mirny's industrial yards produce raw quarried material and manufactured goods from the earliest exile
years onward. **But** a subnet with six member cities and one real highway network between them needs
a steady, reliable source of construction and maintenance materials to keep that network functional at
all. **Therefore** Mirny's own industrial output grows to serve exactly that need — becoming, in
practice, the literal construction engine for Hwy 110 and everything connected to it, not merely a
local economy. **But** that role means Mirny's own industrial health is now something the whole
subnet quietly depends on. **Therefore** Mirny's hub status ends up doubled — an administrative and
communications center, and the physical source of the materials holding the rest of the subnet's
infrastructure together.

## 7. No Flag But China's

Later immigration into Mirny settles overwhelmingly around a Chinese-led demographic current, with no
American presence developing at any tier — a pattern unlike anywhere else in Tepenia. **But** a city
this far from the rest of the country's dominant demographic pattern could easily feel like an
anomaly worth correcting. **Therefore** Mirny's own residents never treat it as one — the city's whole
practical, function-first culture has little use for worrying about how its demographics compare to
anywhere else. **But** outsiders visiting for the first time sometimes remark on it anyway, unprompted.
**Therefore** Mirny residents grow used to a small, recurring moment of mild surprise from visitors —
acknowledged, shrugged off, never treated as anything requiring explanation.

## 8. Russia Remembers the Name

Mirny's founding population names the city for a real historic Russian sloop, carrying genuine
institutional pride into the earliest exile years. **But** later immigration waves eventually make
China demographically Primary, with Russia settling into Significant tier behind it. **Therefore**
the founding naming heritage could easily have faded into empty tradition once the population backing
it thinned. **But** Russia's own founding-era community keeps the story of the *Mirny* and
Bellingshausen's expedition alive deliberately, treating it as institutional memory worth preserving
regardless of numbers. **Therefore** the name stays genuinely meaningful to a real, if numerically
smaller, community — present and active rather than a hollow label nobody remembers the reason for.

## 9. Machinery That Never Quite Stops

Mirny's industrial yards run at genuine, near-constant activity from the earliest productive years
onward. **But** a population living directly inside the same ring as that machinery has to actually
coexist with the noise and rhythm of near-continuous industrial work. **Therefore** the sound and
motion of running machinery becomes ordinary background to daily life rather than something residents
notice or resent — the city's own pulse, indistinguishable from simply being home. **But** a visitor
unused to it finds the constant activity genuinely disorienting. **Therefore** "does it ever stop"
becomes one of the most common questions newcomers ask, and one longtime residents never quite know
how to answer, since to them the machinery running is simply what Mirny sounds like.

## 10. Rough Around the Edges, Solid at the Core

Mirny's entertainment options stay deliberately minimal — a handful of rough, unpolished bars, nothing
elaborate. **But** a working population enduring real hardship still needs somewhere to unwind
together. **Therefore** those modest bars become genuinely important civic spaces regardless of how
little polish they offer, valued for what they provide rather than how they look. **But** a visitor
expecting anything resembling Casey's Splinters or Mawson's hospitality culture finds Mirny's version
distinctly underwhelming by comparison. **Therefore** Mirny never tries to compete on those terms —
its own bars stay exactly as rough as the city around them, honest rather than impressive, and
residents wouldn't have it any other way.

## 11. What Feeds the Chambers

Sinheung's fabrication-synthesis-chamber production scales up to meet growing national demand, and the
resulting material request from Mirny is larger and more urgent than the city's existing quarrying
capacity — already committed to the subnet-local Hwy 110 role — was ever sized to handle. **But**
diverting capacity toward Sinheung's request risks leaving the Mirny subnet's own infrastructure
under-supplied, while refusing it risks national chamber production falling short. **Therefore** Mirny
commits to genuine expansion instead of choosing between the two — real investment sized to supply both
Hwy 110's local materials needs and Sinheung's national demand at once. **But** that kind of expansion
takes real, sustained commitment to actually deliver. **Therefore** it holds: Mirny's industrial identity
gains a second, nationally significant tier, the same rough, unglamorous quarry work now reaching all the
way to the robots whose fabrication depends on it.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#1 and #2** work as a pair — the windbreak's original design and a concrete later test that proves
its worth, giving Mirny's single most distinctive architectural fact an actual origin and a moment of
validation rather than treating it as always having simply worked. **#6** is the most structurally
significant thread, since it gives Mirny's own "subnet hub" role a physical, ongoing mechanism (the
literal source of the subnet's construction materials) rather than leaving the hub status as pure
administrative abstraction. **#11** extends that same structural significance nationally, giving the
2026-07-16 Mirny-Sinheung supply correction its first dramatized, load-bearing test.
