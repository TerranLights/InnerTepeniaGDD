## The Ring That Didn't Hold

Mirny's concentric windbreak-ring architecture (Physical Infrastructure Attribute #1) and its structural
reinforcement rated for sustained loading up to 40+ m/s wind events (attribute #3) read today as settled
doctrine — obviously the way to build against katabatic wind this severe. It wasn't obvious to the first
exile generation working the site. The genuinely substantial Soviet-era station they inherited (`Specs/
Mirny.md`'s Founding section) had stood for generations on conventional, square-cornered polar
construction, the same discipline used at nearly every other pre-exile station on the continent, and the
young city's first defensive perimeter — built fast, by people with every reason to trust the geometry
they already knew — copied that same squared shape at a larger scale.

**It held for less than two full winters.** A sustained katabatic event, worse than anything in the
station's own surviving pre-exile weather log by the account of the crew who rode it out, found the
perimeter's hard corners exactly the way Mirny's wind regime finds every hard corner: a place for lateral
force to concentrate instead of slide past. A section of the eastern wall came down against an occupied
storage annex, and though the annex had been evacuated hours earlier on a junior engineer's own
insistence, the wall's failure cost the young settlement weeks of stored material it couldn't easily
replace. That junior engineer — **"Osric Bellandry" *(placeholder, not developer-chosen)***, not yet
Chief Windwright, not yet the name later generations would credit with the whole ring architecture
(`Mirny_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section VI) — spent the following weeks arguing against the settlement's
own elders, who wanted the same design rebuilt on the reasoning that it had simply been unlucky. Bellandry
proposed something genuinely unfamiliar instead: a smooth, rounded outer shape with no corner for the
wind to catch at all, a design nobody at Mirny had tried before and few elsewhere in Tepenia had reason
to. The rebuilt ring was tested by an even worse storm within its own first year and held. Nothing about
attribute #1 as it stands today required that specific argument to have gone the way it did — a different
outcome, and Mirny's whole outer wall might still be shedding its own force into hard corners instead of
around a curve.

## The Redundancy Nobody Wanted to Pay For

The Relay Hardware Vaults carry a weight the Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding B spells out plainly: their
failure would cut Arcanet contact between Zhongshan, Casey, Davis, Vostok, and Kunlun at once, a genuine
single point of failure for the whole subnet's own internal contact. That vulnerability was identified and
argued over during the Vaults' own founding-era construction, not discovered later.

**"Zoya Marchenko" *(placeholder, not developer-chosen)***, a junior technician on the founding-era
construction team, argued during the Vaults' own design phase for building genuine hardware redundancy into
the relay system — duplicate pathways capable of carrying the subnet's own traffic if any single component
failed — against senior planners who considered the additional cost unjustified for a facility with no
historical failure to point to yet. Marchenko's own proposal was scaled back rather than rejected outright,
producing a partial redundancy compromise neither side found fully satisfying. She continued raising the
issue periodically in the years that followed, per what's preserved of the facility's own internal planning
notes, without ever securing the fuller redundancy she'd originally argued for. The compromise system that
was actually built is the same one the Vaults still run on — a single point of failure smaller than it might
have been without Marchenko's own founding-era advocacy, but never as resilient as she herself believed the
subnet's own most consequential facility deserved.

## The Apprentice Who Argued With the Wind

Windwright Apprentice Trials (`Mirny_Community_Infrastructure.md`) test new apprentices' work against the
actual wind, not a panel's opinion — a custom that traces directly from the structural-reinforcement
discipline established in Physical Infrastructure Attribute #3, and from the Windwright Guildhall's own
purely competence-based teaching culture, "never mythologized" (`Mirny_Community_Infrastructure.md`'s
Additions).

**One trial in particular is still told as a cautionary story inside the Guildhall.** **"Talia
Voskresenskaya" *(placeholder, not developer-chosen)*** — an apprentice a generation or so past
Bellandry's own founding-era redesign — refused the standard trial format, which tested a scaled sample
panel inside a controlled wind chamber built specifically to approximate, not replicate, the katabatic
regime outside. She argued the chamber was lying to every apprentice tested in it, understating gust
variability in a way that flattered marginal designs, and petitioned to have her own panel tested outside,
during a live storm, instead. Her instructors refused twice before relenting, on the condition that the
trial run during a moderate event rather than anything near the 40+ m/s threshold attribute #3 rates
structures against. The storm that actually arrived on the scheduled day ran well past moderate. Her panel
held — but by a narrower margin than the chamber's own simulation had predicted for a design of its
specifications, exactly the gap she'd argued existed. The Guildhall didn't formally rebuild its trial
process around her result, but chamber-tested apprentices since have, by informal custom, been asked at
least once during their own trial what the chamber isn't telling them — a question with Voskresenskaya's
own name still attached to it whenever it's asked.

## The Toast Nobody Wanted to Give

Quarry Return Toasts (`Mirny_Community_Infrastructure.md`) mark, by established custom, a quarry crew's
safe return from the harsher southern routes — the same routes Quarry-to-Yard Transit Sheds (Physical
Infrastructure Attribute #7) exist to service, waystations along a supply chain feeding both the
eastern-highway construction materials output and Sinheung's own chamber-fabrication supply (attribute
#10).

**The custom itself is remembered as beginning with a specific crew that came back two days late.** A
southern quarry team, working a route far enough from the sheltered industrial core that no real-time
contact with the yards was possible mid-run, was caught by an unforecast katabatic event severe enough to
force the crew to hole up rather than continue. With no word for two full days past their expected return,
the yard had begun the grim administrative work of accounting for a loss it assumed was real. **The crew
chief — "Danil Oyunbaatar" *(placeholder, not developer-chosen)*** — brought his team back on the third
day, all of them alive, having sheltered in a natural rock lee the storm itself had driven them toward.
What the yard held that evening wasn't the funeral rite it had quietly begun preparing for, and wasn't
simple celebration either — it became something in between, an informal toast that acknowledged both how
close the loss had actually come and how ordinary a hazard the southern routes represent for every crew
that works them. Every Quarry Return Toast since carries some version of that same double weight, whether
or not the crew being toasted ever came as close to genuine loss as Oyunbaatar's own did.

## What Sinheung Sent Back

Mirny's raw-material shipping relationship to Sinheung (Physical Infrastructure Attribute #10) is
established as a genuinely major, nationally significant supply chain — but Finding G (Physical
Infrastructure Attributes) proposes a second, quieter export riding the same route: Mirny's own extreme-
wind structural engineering expertise, plausibly transferable given Sinheung's own meaningful katabatic
exposure.

**That transfer traces to one specific, still-remembered exchange rather than any standing arrangement.**
An early batch of ore shipped south came back flagged — not rejected outright, but accompanied by a formal
query from Sinheung's own fabrication engineers, who'd found inconsistent structural performance in
chambers built from that specific delivery and wanted to know whether Mirny's own processing had changed.
It hadn't; the quarry face itself had simply shifted to a new, less-tested seam. Mirny's own Windwright
Guildhall, not the ore-processing yards, ended up fielding the query, since assessing structural
consistency under load was squarely a Windwright's own competence. A senior Windwright traveled to
Sinheung in person to walk the fabrication floor directly rather than answer by written report alone — the
first documented case of a Mirny engineer consulting on-site at another city over a materials question —
and left behind a specific testing protocol for flagging seam-to-seam variability before shipment, adopted
at the quarry face soon after. The ore dispute itself was resolved in weeks; the protocol it produced
outlasted it, a small, concrete instance of exactly the knowledge-transfer Finding G proposes as plausible,
now with an actual visit and an actual named consequence attached to it rather than remaining only a
structural possibility.

## The Kitchen That Refused to Merge

Dual-Kitchen Halls (Physical Infrastructure Attribute #18) — physically distinct but co-located Russian
and Chinese food service — are the clearest ground-level expression the Cross-Reference Synthesis's
Finding D identifies of Mirny's own "coexistence, not fusion" civic principle, operating at the same small
daily scale the Community Infrastructure file's Dual-Table Suppers describe.

**The arrangement wasn't the first thing tried.** During a genuinely lean stretch in the early exile
years, when materials for a second kitchen structure were scarce enough that duplicating facilities looked
like an indulgence the young settlement couldn't afford, an administrator proposed combining the two food
services into a single fused kitchen and menu — a practical cost-saving measure, argued on resource
grounds rather than any cultural one. The attempt lasted less than a season. Neither population's own
cooks were trained in the other's techniques, ingredient staples didn't substitute cleanly for each other,
and the resulting fused menu satisfied neither community — by the account passed down since, residents
from both sides simply ate less rather than eat food that had been merged badly out of necessity rather
than choice. **A Chinese-community cook, "Mei Lian Fu" *(placeholder, not developer-chosen)*, and her
Russian-community counterpart, "Pyotr Aksakov" *(placeholder, not developer-chosen)***, jointly petitioned
the settlement's own administration to reverse the merger — not back to full separation, since the
resource constraint that had prompted the experiment was still real, but to two genuinely distinct
kitchens sharing one roof and one hall, each cooked and run by people who actually knew the food. The
compromise held, and outlasted the resource shortage that had originally forced the question — becoming,
by the time later attributes like #18 were being cataloged as simply how Mirny operates, no longer
remembered as a compromise at all.
