## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

For years, Splinters' growing reputation stays mostly word of mouth, spreading gradually along the
Dumont d'Urville Sea and Indian Ocean coastline — well known among regular travelers, but not yet the
kind of place people organize a trip specifically around.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A particular night — a convergence of travelers arriving at the same time, a stretch of unusually good
weather holding an entire wind-window open longer than expected, a performer on stage who happens to be
having an exceptional night — draws more people through Splinters' doors at once than anyone's ever
seen there before. The venue, built for its previous, smaller scale of regular business, is suddenly
handling a crowd it was never actually staffed or stocked for. Something has to give in real time:
whether to turn people away at the door to protect the evening's quality, or let the place run over
capacity and trust that the staff and the space can absorb it.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation, happening fast and under real
pressure, between someone managing the door who wants to cap the crowd for safety and quality — a
completely reasonable, responsible instinct — and someone (staff, performer, or a regular who knows the
place best) who insists on letting the night run its course, betting that turning people away would kill
the exact energy that's making the night what it is. Neither side is being reckless or precious; it's a
genuine judgment call made under real time pressure, the kind of decision that only looks obviously
right or wrong in hindsight.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly whoever's actually
working the door or bar that night — someone with real operational responsibility for the space and a
sober read on what over-capacity actually risks. Character B is plausibly the performer on stage, or a
longtime regular with enough standing to make the call — someone whose read on the crowd's mood is
instinctive rather than procedural, betting on the night rather than managing it.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Character B's instinct wins — the door stays open, the crowd keeps growing, and the night runs
long and loud without the disaster Character A feared. It becomes, by the time it's over, the single
largest, most memorable night Splinters has ever hosted.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the gamble paid off rather than ending in disaster, Splinters' management quietly revises its
own operating instincts going forward — treating exceptional nights as worth accommodating rather than
capping by default, a real shift in how the venue plans for capacity from that night onward.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Splinters starts planning for the possibility of exceptional nights rather than simply capping
them, the venue's later full-city-block expansion (Suggestion #3) inherits a built-in tolerance for
surge crowds that a more capacity-cautious venue would never have designed for — Splinters becomes
structurally built to handle its own legend recurring, not just to accommodate steady average business.
Delete the 2nd-order change — imagine management had instead tightened capacity controls after the
near-miss — and the venue's later capacity to handle huge surges has no origin; it depends specifically
on the operating-instinct shift this night produced.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

That single night becomes local legend, retold and exaggerated with each retelling — the moment
residents point back to as proof Splinters had genuinely arrived as a regional phenomenon. Nobody agrees
anymore on exactly what happened that night, only that something did, and Casey was never quite the same
modest waypoint afterward.
