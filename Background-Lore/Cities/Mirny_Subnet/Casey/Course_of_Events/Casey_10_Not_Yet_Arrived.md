## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

For a generation, Splinters' fame feels like something that happens *to* Casey rather than something
Casey itself chose — travelers know the bar, not necessarily the city around it, and residents have
absorbed that framing without much resistance.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The generation that actually revived and grew Splinters — the people who did the work, night after
night, of building it into what it became — starts to notice something in themselves that doesn't match
the old framing: they're proud of it, not merely associated with it. That's a genuinely different feeling
than the passive "it happens to be here" self-description Casey has carried for a generation, and it
puts that older generation at odds, quietly at first, with residents who still describe the city the old
way — as a diffuse, function-first junction that happens to have a famous bar in it, nothing more
essentially theirs than that.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone who helped build
Splinters and has come to feel genuine ownership and pride in it, and someone who holds onto Casey's
older self-description — a diffuse junction city, defined by function rather than any one achievement —
and is uneasy about letting one landmark redefine the whole city's identity. This isn't pride versus
humility as a moral contest; Character B's caution is a real defense of something Casey earned
honestly (Suggestion #1's and #8's hard-won function-first neutrality), which a single celebrated
landmark could quietly erode if it becomes the city's whole story. Character A isn't asking to erase
that history, just to be allowed to feel what they've genuinely earned.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly one of the original
volunteers or long-serving staff from Splinters' growth years (compare Suggestion #2's Idris Wetherall
or Suggestion #3's early regulars) — someone with real, personal history invested in what the bar became.
Character B is plausibly an older civic figure or longtime resident with no particular tie to Splinters
itself, someone whose attachment is to Casey's founding function-first character and who worries what
gets lost if the city starts defining itself by one achievement instead of by what it does for
everyone.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither description displaces the other — residents start describing themselves, for the first time,
as being from the city with Splinters in it, while that pride never fully replaces Casey's older
self-description as a diffuse, function-first junction city. Casey ends up holding both at once.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because both self-descriptions survive side by side rather than one replacing the other, Casey develops
a genuine, dual civic vocabulary — residents fluidly switch between describing the city by what it does
(the junction) and by what it built (Splinters), depending on context, without treating either framing
as more authentic than the other.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that dual vocabulary becomes normal rather than contested, later generations of Casey residents
grow up without ever having to resolve the tension Character A and Character B argued over — they
simply inherit both framings as equally valid, a settled civic fact rather than an active debate, which
means Casey's eventual "did we ever feel we'd arrived" question gets answered not with a single verdict
but with a permanent, comfortable both/and. Delete the 2nd-order change — imagine one framing had won
outright instead — and there would be nothing for later generations to simply inherit unresolved; the
comfortable duality depends specifically on both descriptions having survived together.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Casey holds both identities at once, unresolved and unbothered by being unresolved — genuine ownership
over what it built, and an honest, settled awareness that the city was never only that one thing to
begin with.
