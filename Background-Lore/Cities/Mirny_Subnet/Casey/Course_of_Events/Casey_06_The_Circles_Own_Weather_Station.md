## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Casey inherits genuine Australian Antarctic Division meteorological and katabatic-monitoring
equipment, sitting almost precisely on the Antarctic Circle's own threshold — real scientific
infrastructure with no clear civic purpose yet attached to it beyond its original research mandate.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Inherited scientific infrastructure means nothing without a clear reason to keep using it seriously, and
the original Australian research mandate the equipment was built for doesn't automatically carry over
to an exile population with entirely different priorities. Two plausible futures open up for the
station: maintained as pure science, continuing the original meteorological research program largely
for its own sake, or repurposed toward something the growing settlement actually needs day to day. The
station's technical staff — the people who actually understand the equipment — don't agree on which
future is the right one, and the disagreement isn't trivial, because it decides what kind of institution
the station becomes for generations afterward.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a research-minded
technician who wants to preserve the station's original scientific mandate — continuing meteorological
study as legitimate science in its own right, not merely in service of something else — and a
safety-minded figure who sees the same equipment as wasted potential unless it's aimed directly at
keeping travelers alive on Casey's own katabatic wind-prone roads. This isn't a science-versus-ignorance
conflict; Character A's position is a genuine defense of research for its own sake, and Character B
isn't anti-science, just persuaded that unglamorous, applied purpose is worth more here than pure
inquiry. Both are right about what the equipment is capable of; they disagree about what it's *for*.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The applied case wins, but not by fully displacing the scientific one — the station's function is
redirected so that wind-warning data feeds directly into the city's travel-safety systems, while the
underlying meteorological research continues as the mechanism producing that data rather than being shut
down.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the redirection reframes rather than eliminates the science, Casey's technical sector grows up
with a specific, unusual dual character — real scientific rigor practiced entirely in service of an
unglamorous, practical purpose, distinct from stations elsewhere in Tepenia where meteorological
research and public safety infrastructure remain organizationally separate.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Casey's technical staff train from the outset to treat rigorous science and applied
travel-safety work as a single job rather than two separate disciplines, the station becomes a
model other Mirny subnet cities' own safety infrastructure eventually gets measured against —
Casey's technicians developing a specific, exportable expertise in translating raw meteorological data
into actionable travel warnings that other cities' purely research-focused stations were never built to
produce. Delete the 2nd-order change — imagine the station having simply been shut down in favor of
safety infrastructure built from scratch — and there's no rigorous scientific foundation underneath the
expertise other cities come to rely on; it depends specifically on science and safety having been fused
rather than one replacing the other.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Casey's technical sector stands as a working fusion of genuine science and unglamorous applied purpose
— wind-warning data feeding directly into the travel-safety culture that already defines the city, with
a level of translational expertise other Mirny subnet cities increasingly look to Casey to provide.
