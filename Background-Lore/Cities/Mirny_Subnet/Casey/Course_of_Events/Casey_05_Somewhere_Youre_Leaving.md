## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Travelers passing through Casey experience it as exactly what its own geography suggests — a junction,
a decision point, somewhere to stop before continuing on toward Concordia or back along the coast.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The people who actually live in Casey full time build entire lives there — families, careers, decades
of memory attached to specific streets and buildings — in the exact same place visitors only ever pass
through in an afternoon. A real gap opens between how Casey sees itself and how the rest of Tepenia
describes it: a lived, complete city on one side, a highway junction on the other. Neither description
is actually wrong, just differently angled — but the gap becomes something residents have to actively
manage, over and over, because every new traveler arrives already carrying the outside description as
if it were the whole truth.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a longtime Casey resident,
tired of correcting the same assumption for the thousandth time, and a traveler who says something
genuinely dismissive about the city — not out of malice, just out of the ordinary carelessness of
someone who's never had to think of a waypoint as anyone's home. This works best as a small, specific,
almost mundane disagreement rather than a dramatic one: the traveler isn't wrong about what they've
experienced, they've genuinely only ever seen Casey as a place to stop; the resident isn't wrong either,
they've built a whole life the traveler has no way of seeing in a single afternoon. The friction is
about two true things that don't fit together, not about one person being at fault.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a longtime resident —
someone in a public-facing junction role (a dispatcher, a Splinters regular, a highway-maintenance
figure) who deals with travelers constantly and has absorbed the outside description more times than
they can count. Character B is plausibly a one-time or infrequent traveler, someone without any
particular malice, whose comment about "just passing through" or "nothing really here" lands wrong
specifically because they have no idea it's landed at all.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side actually changes the other's mind in the moment — the traveler leaves still thinking of
Casey primarily as a junction, and Character A doesn't manage to fully close the gap in one
conversation. But something smaller and more durable happens: Character A settles on a specific way of
responding — direct, unresentful correction rather than either silence or real anger — that becomes,
informally, how residents handle the moment from then on.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the response that sticks is calm correction rather than resentment, Casey's public-facing
workers develop something close to a shared, informal script for the moment — a light, practiced way of
naming the gap without turning every traveler interaction into a grievance, passed on from established
staff to newcomers without ever being formally written down anywhere.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the correction becomes practiced rather than raw, it starts doubling as something closer to
local color — travelers who get the light version of the "we actually live here" correction remember it
fondly rather than awkwardly, and some repeat it themselves to other travelers, quietly spreading a more
accurate picture of Casey along the same coastal traveler network that made Splinters famous. Delete the
2nd-order change — imagine residents never settling into a shared, practiced response — and there's no
mechanism for the correction to travel outward at all; it depends specifically on the response having
become consistent and repeatable.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The gap between Casey's self-image and its outside reputation never fully closes, and residents have
stopped expecting it to — but the correction itself has become a small, practiced, almost affectionate
piece of local culture, one more thing regulars know to expect from the city with the junction that's
also somebody's home.
