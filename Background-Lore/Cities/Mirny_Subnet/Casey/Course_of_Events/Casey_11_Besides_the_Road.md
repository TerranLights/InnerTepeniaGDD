## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Casey's civic identity runs almost entirely on two things: the junction itself, and Splinters. Beneath
both, unremarked, sits something else — the sheer volume of travelers passing through before the long
interior drive to Concordia means a steady, constant demand for vehicle repair, resupply, and cold-weather
equipment, and Casey's workshops have quietly multiplied to meet it, one repair job at a time.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A workshop owner totals up, almost by accident, just how much of Casey's actual daily economic activity
now runs through vehicle repair and fabrication — and realizes it rivals Splinters' own contribution, even
though nobody outside the trade has ever thought of Casey that way.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a conflict about whether the workshop trade
matters — both sides can see the numbers now that someone's actually added them up. Character A, from
the workshop trade itself, wants real civic recognition and investment to match what the city already
pours into Splinters' own upkeep and reputation — tired of being essential, unglamorous background labor
next to something famous along an entire coastline. Character B, closer to Splinters or the city's
traveler-facing civic identity, isn't dismissive of the workshops, but worries that formally elevating a
second identity risks diluting the one thing Casey is actually known for everywhere else in Tepenia.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a senior mechanic or
workshop owner, practical and unsentimental, more interested in fair civic standing than in fame.
Character B is plausibly someone connected to Splinters' own management or Casey's traveler-facing civic
promotion, protective of the identity that actually put Casey on the regional map.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The city formally recognizes both. A genuine workshop-and-fabrication quarter gets real civic investment
alongside Splinters' own — not a rebrand, not a competition, just an honest acknowledgment that Casey
runs on two legs, not one.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Casey's economic character finally has real breadth beyond hospitality — a genuine vehicle-repair and
travel-equipment fabrication trade, standing alongside Splinters rather than living invisibly underneath
it.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

This becomes part of how Casey answers its own long-running destination-versus-waypoint tension (chains
#5, #10) — residents now have two distinct, genuine sources of civic ownership, not just Splinters' fame:
pride in a bar people travel for, and pride in a trade that quietly keeps the whole junction's actual
traffic moving.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Casey holds two real economic identities at once — Splinters' regional fame, and a quieter, equally
essential vehicle-repair and fabrication trade — both now genuinely part of how the city understands and
describes itself, neither one overshadowing the other the way it used to.
