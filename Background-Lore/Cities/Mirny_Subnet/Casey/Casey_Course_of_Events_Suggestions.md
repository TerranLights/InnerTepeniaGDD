# Casey — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material (Casey's own eventual destruction is out of scope here). 10 candidate But/Therefore chains
built from Casey's Megasheet (the Hwy 110/Hwy 2 junction, Splinters, the destination-vs-waypoint
tension). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Two Roads, No Center

Casey's junction position draws a wider, more mixed early wave of settlers than more geographically
isolated founding sites ever see. **But** a population arriving from six or more nations in comparable
numbers has no obvious candidate for a single dominant founding identity. **Therefore** Casey's
earliest civic institutions form around function instead — keeping the junction running — since no
national culture has the numbers to define the city on its own terms. **But** a city with no unifying
identity risks never developing much civic character at all. **Therefore** Casey's character forms
around what it does rather than who founded it, structurally as diffuse as its own function from the
very start.

## 2. What the Records Remembered

Casey's exile community inherits Australia's largest, best-equipped pre-exile station, along with
whatever paperwork and records happened to survive the transition. **But** most of that inherited
history is administrative and forgettable, nothing anyone thinks to revisit. **Therefore** when Idris
Wetherall finds old records referencing a modest social bar called Splinters at the original station,
it could easily have stayed a footnote nobody acted on. **But** he pushes for reviving it anyway, on
nothing more than the appeal of the name and what it once represented. **Therefore** a small, informal
gathering space named after forgotten history becomes the seed of what eventually grows into Casey's
single most famous landmark.

## 3. A Bar Too Big to Ignore

Splinters starts as a modest revival, nothing more ambitious than a decent place for locals to unwind.
**But** Casey's position as the last coastal city before the long interior drive to Concordia means a
constant stream of travelers passes through looking for exactly that kind of place. **Therefore** word
spreads along the whole Dumont d'Urville Sea and Indian Ocean coastline, and Splinters keeps expanding
to keep pace with its own growing reputation. **But** growing that large risks losing whatever made the
original revival feel worth doing in the first place. **Therefore** Splinters keeps its genre-spanning,
unpretentious character even at full-city-block scale — proof that popularity and genuine character
don't have to trade off against each other.

## 4. Long Odds Is Never Wrong

Casey's cardinal social rule holds that route information gets shared immediately and accurately,
since lives genuinely depend on it. **But** living up to that rule consistently, across an entire
career of forecasting katabatic wind windows, is a much harder standard to actually meet than to
simply state. **Therefore** Dispatcher Okonkwo-Hale builds a reputation, call after call, for never
once getting it wrong — a track record residents start treating as close to legendary. **But** a
reputation that clean invites real pressure to maintain it perfectly, forever. **Therefore** "Long
Odds" becomes not just a nickname but a genuine civic standard other dispatchers measure themselves
against, whether or not they can ever quite match it.

## 5. Somewhere You're Leaving

Travelers passing through Casey experience it as exactly what its own geography suggests — a
junction, a decision point, somewhere to stop before continuing on. **But** the people who actually
live there full time build entire lives, families, and careers in the same place visitors only ever
pass through. **Therefore** a real gap opens between how Casey sees itself and how the rest of Tepenia
describes it — a lived, complete city on one side, a highway junction on the other. **But** neither
description is actually wrong, just differently angled. **Therefore** Casey's residents grow used to
correcting outsiders without ever fully closing the gap, since the correction has to be made fresh
with every new traveler who arrives already thinking of the city as somewhere to leave.

## 6. The Circle's Own Weather Station

Casey inherits genuine Australian Antarctic Division meteorological and katabatic-monitoring
equipment, sitting almost precisely on the Antarctic Circle's own threshold. **But** inherited
scientific infrastructure means nothing without a clear reason to keep using it seriously. **Therefore**
the city finds one directly in its own daily function — wind-warning data feeding straight into the
travel-safety culture that already defines Casey's whole civic character. **But** treating weather
monitoring as safety infrastructure rather than pure science changes what the work is actually for.
**Therefore** Casey's technical sector grows up entirely in service of keeping travelers alive on the
roads, real science wearing an entirely practical, unglamorous purpose.

## 7. Whoever Passes Through, Fed and Warm

Hosting an enormous, constant stream of strangers could easily wear a city down into resentful,
transactional hospitality. **But** Casey's whole civic function depends on travelers actually wanting
to stop, not just needing to. **Therefore** the city develops something closer to genuine, practiced
warmth — efficient, unglamorous, and real, the specific skill of making a stranger feel worth
stopping for. **But** performing that warmth constantly, for people who'll be gone again within a day,
risks it curdling into performance rather than sincerity. **Therefore** Casey keeps it honest instead,
residents taking real pride in doing an unglamorous job extraordinarily well rather than treating
hospitality as a show.

## 8. A City That Never Picked a Side

Six nations arrive at Casey in comparable numbers, none dominant enough to set the civic tone alone.
**But** a city this evenly split could easily fracture into competing communities instead of one
functioning whole. **Therefore** Casey's early leadership deliberately avoids favoring any single
group's institutions or customs, organizing civic life around shared, practical necessity instead.
**But** staying neutral this consistently means the city never develops the kind of strong founding
mythology most Tepenian cities lean on. **Therefore** Casey ends up defined by what it does for
everyone equally rather than any one group's story — a genuine achievement of balance that reads, from
outside, as simply having no distinct identity at all.

## 9. The Night Splinters Filled Up

For years, Splinters' growing reputation stays mostly word of mouth, spreading gradually along the
coast. **But** one particular night — a convergence of travelers, a stretch of good weather, a
memorable act on stage — draws more people through the doors at once than anyone's ever seen there
before. **Therefore** that single night becomes local legend, retold and exaggerated with each
retelling, the moment residents point back to as proof Splinters had genuinely arrived as a regional
phenomenon rather than just a well-liked local bar. **But** legends grow bigger than the actual events
behind them. **Therefore** nobody agrees anymore on exactly what happened that night — only that
something did, and that Casey was never quite the same modest waypoint afterward.

## 10. Not Yet Arrived

For a generation, Splinters' fame feels like something that happens to Casey rather than something
Casey itself chose — travelers know the bar, not necessarily the city around it. **But** the
generation that actually revived and grew the place starts to notice they're proud of it, not merely
associated with it. **Therefore** a real shift takes hold — residents start describing themselves, for
the first time, as being from the city with Splinters in it, rather than simply living somewhere
travelers happen to stop. **But** that pride never fully displaces Casey's older self-description as a
diffuse, function-first junction city. **Therefore** Casey ends up holding both at once — genuine
ownership over what it built, and an honest awareness that the city was never only that one thing.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#2, #3, and #9** trace Splinters' full arc from a forgotten record to a genuine regional phenomenon,
giving the Megasheet's own central landmark an actual causal history rather than treating its fame as
a fixed, always-true fact. **#10** is the most emotionally significant thread, since it's the one that
gives Casey's own "did we ever feel we'd arrived" open question (raised directly in the Mega-Init) an
actual dramatized turning point rather than a static yes-or-no answer.
