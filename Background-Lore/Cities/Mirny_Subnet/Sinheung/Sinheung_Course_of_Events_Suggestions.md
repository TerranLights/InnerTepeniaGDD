# Sinheung — Candidate Courses of Events

**Naming note:** this city is now officially named **Sinheung (신흥)** — resolved by the developer
after this document's Megasheet source material was written, when the city was still tracked under
the placeholder label Sinheung (working title "Sinheung," a leftover tag tied to
the real-world Russian Progress Station its infrastructure was inherited from, never a name its own
founders chose). All other candidate names considered during that naming process remain on reserve for
future use — streets, districts, landmarks, public squares — rather than discarded. The chains below
use Sinheung throughout; thread #7 has been rewritten to reflect the actual naming rather than its
absence.

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Sinheung's Megasheet (the singularly
Korean-founded claim secured by treaty, the fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturing role, pride
earned through output rather than inheritance). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. A Claim Secured, Not Inherited

Korea's claim to this site comes from the same Jeju-do court negotiation that allocates Shirayuki to
Japan, not from any Korean real-world station ever having stood here. **But** a claim settled by
treaty, rather than grown organically the way most Tepenian cities' founding stories work, means
Korean exiles arrive to a place their nation had to formally negotiate for rather than simply inherit.
**Therefore** the founding population starts from a genuinely different footing than its cluster
neighbors — a home secured through diplomacy, not birthright. **But** a claim secured on paper still
has to be made real by the people actually living there. **Therefore** proving the claim was worth
securing becomes the founding generation's own unspoken task from the very first years onward.

## 2. Baek Ji-hoon's Conversion

The inherited Russian research infrastructure at Progress Station serves a scientific mission the
new Korean population has no particular connection to. **But** a growing exile city needs real
economic output, not just inherited equipment sitting idle or repurposed only partially. **Therefore**
Chief Engineer Baek Ji-hoon leads the deliberate conversion of that research infrastructure into a
genuine industrial fabrication economy — raw materials in, finished goods out, a shift in the city's
whole working identity. **But** converting research infrastructure into industrial capacity means
walking away from whatever the original scientific mission was pursuing. **Therefore** Sinheung's
founding research heritage fades as its fabrication economy takes over — a deliberate choice, not a
simple drift, credited directly to the engineer who pushed it through.

## 3. Proving It Through Output

Sinheung's founding population inherits a claim secured through diplomacy rather than earned through
organic settlement. **But** a claim like that could easily feel less legitimate, less truly theirs,
than a station a population settled and built up entirely on its own. **Therefore** residents lean
hard into demonstrated contribution instead — building something genuinely, visibly valuable to the
rest of Tepenia as a way of making the diplomatically-secured claim feel earned rather than merely
granted. **But** proving a claim through output means the standard never really lets up; there's
always more to demonstrate. **Therefore** a sharp, forward-looking civic ambition takes hold and
never fully settles — pride here stays active and ongoing, not something the city can simply rest on
once achieved.

## 4. Dae-ho's Achievement

Sinheung's fabrication economy grows into genuine industrial competence, but competence alone doesn't
yet make it nationally indispensable. **But** Tepenia's whole robot-creation infrastructure needs more
manufacturing sites capable of producing fabrication-synthesis chambers than currently exist.
**Therefore** Foreman Dae-ho Whitfield pushes the city's own engineers to pursue exactly that
capability — the specific technical achievement that turns an ordinary industrial center into
something touching the entire Federation's population growth. **But** securing that role means
competing with other cities' own industrial ambitions for the same opportunity. **Therefore** his
success becomes the city's single most consequential civic achievement — quietly load-bearing
infrastructure for the whole country, credited to one foreman's deliberate push rather than
happenstance.

## 5. What Davis Sends

Sinheung's growing fabrication economy needs a steady, reliable source of raw material to actually
function. **But** its own site offers limited natural material of the right kind for heavy
manufacturing. **Therefore** a genuine supply relationship forms with Davis, further down Hwy 110 —
raw materials trucked in, worked into finished goods here, a real economic dependency running between
the two cities. **But** relying on another city's supply chain means Sinheung's own industrial output
is only ever as reliable as Davis's ability to keep shipping. **Therefore** the relationship becomes
something both cities have to actively maintain, not a one-time arrangement either side can take for
granted once it's set up.

## 6. Three Parties, One Agreement

Zhongshan, Shirayuki, and Sinheung all trace back to the same Jeju-do negotiation among Japan, Korea,
and the Sinian Federation, before any of them ever occupy their allocated sites. **But** arriving at
adjacent physical locations doesn't automatically make three separate founding populations act like
established neighbors. **Therefore** the three communities settle into coexistence gradually, the same
diplomatic working relationship that divided the cluster among them at Jeju-do simply continuing once
they're actually living beside each other. **But** a relationship that starts as diplomacy takes real
time to become genuine neighborliness. **Therefore** the cluster's eventual close cooperation traces
back to three regional peers who'd already negotiated directly once before, continuing a working
relationship rather than building one from nothing.

## 7. The Meaning in the Name

Sinheung's founding generation needs to decide, at some point, what to actually call the place they're
building — not the station they inherited, something of their own. **But** choosing a name for a city
secured through diplomacy rather than settled by conquest or discovery means the name has to say
something true about how the place actually came to exist. **Therefore** they settle on Sinheung
(신흥) — "newly rising," a name that names the process itself rather than claiming false roots: not a
city that was always here, but one visibly, deliberately emerging into something real. **But** a name
that honest invites its own kind of pressure — you can't call yourselves "newly rising" forever without
eventually having risen into something. **Therefore** the name becomes, quietly, its own standing
promise — one more reason the whole city leans so hard into proving itself through output rather than
simply resting on the claim that secured its ground.

## 8. From Broad Industry to One Precise Thing

Sinheung's fabrication economy starts broad and general — whatever finished goods the wider subnet
happens to need. **But** general manufacturing doesn't give a city any particular claim to national
significance the way a genuinely specialized capability would. **Therefore** the economy narrows
deliberately over generations, engineers and planners pushing toward increasingly precise, high-value
specialization rather than staying a generic industrial center. **But** narrowing that far means
betting the city's whole economic identity on one specific capability actually paying off. **Therefore**
the bet pays off completely once fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturing becomes the city's
defining role — proof that demonstrated precision, not raw industrial breadth, was always going to be
the real path to significance.

## 9. The Weight of the Apparatus

Workers at Sinheung's fabrication plant spend years producing components without necessarily thinking
hard about their ultimate purpose — just another precise manufacturing job. **But** at some point, the
full implication of what the chambers actually do becomes impossible to ignore: this apparatus is how
new robots get created, anywhere in Tepenia, regardless of local manufacturing capability elsewhere.
**Therefore** a real shift in how workers understand their own labor takes hold — not just precision
manufacturing, but something closer to population itself passing through their hands. **But** that
realization changes how much pressure the work actually feels like it carries. **Therefore** the
plant's own culture develops an unusually serious, careful standard around chamber production
specifically, distinct from the rest of the city's ordinary industrial work.

## 10. Whitfield and Baek

Baek Ji-hoon converts Sinheung's inherited infrastructure into a genuine industrial economy; Dae-ho
Whitfield later secures its place among the nation's few chamber manufacturers. **But** the two
achievements happen far enough apart, generationally, that residents don't automatically connect them
as one continuous story. **Therefore** local institutional memory eventually does draw the line
directly — one figure who built the foundation, another who built the achievement the foundation made
possible, two halves of the same civic arc rather than two unrelated successes. **But** crediting a
whole city's defining accomplishment to two specific people risks flattening everyone else's
contribution along the way. **Therefore** both names get remembered less as individual heroes and more
as convenient anchors for a much longer, more collective story — proof of what Sinheung built,
credited to the two people history happened to remember doing it.

## Worth Your Attention

**#3 and #7** work as the most thematically central pair — the civic pride earned through output, and
the name itself standing as a promise the city has to keep proving true, together giving the
Megasheet's own central finding (a population that proves its place through demonstrated output
because it was never simply handed one) its clearest dramatized shape. **#2 and #4** trace the city's
full economic arc from inherited research infrastructure to nationally load-bearing chamber
manufacturer, the two decisions that actually built Sinheung's defining significance.
