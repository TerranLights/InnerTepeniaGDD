## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The inherited Russian research infrastructure at Progress Station serves a scientific mission the new
Korean population has no particular connection to — laboratories and equipment built for a purpose that
belonged to people who are no longer here, sitting only partially used.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A growing exile city needs real economic output, not just inherited equipment sitting idle or repurposed
only partially — a population can't sustain itself on a research mission it never chose and has no
particular stake in continuing. Chief Engineer Baek Ji-hoon leads the deliberate conversion of that
research infrastructure into a genuine industrial fabrication economy — raw materials in, finished goods
out, a shift in the city's whole working identity. But converting research infrastructure into industrial
capacity means walking away from whatever the original scientific mission was pursuing — and some among
the settlement, whether out of respect for the inherited work or simple caution about discarding
something potentially valuable, resist abandoning the research mission outright before anyone's
determined whether it held anything worth continuing.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between Baek Ji-hoon, pushing to
convert the research infrastructure decisively into industrial capacity, and someone with a more direct,
personal stake in the research mission itself — not necessarily hostile to industrial conversion in
principle, but unwilling to see it happen before someone has actually assessed whether the inherited
research holds anything genuinely worth preserving. This is a conflict about due diligence versus
necessity: B isn't simply sentimental about the old mission, and A isn't simply indifferent to what might
be lost — the real disagreement is about how much time the settlement can actually afford to spend
finding out before economic necessity forces the decision regardless.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is, per established canon, Chief
Engineer Baek Ji-hoon, credited with leading the conversion. Character B is plausibly one of the
settlement's own scientifically-inclined residents, perhaps someone with at least partial training or
interest in the original research mission, who wants a genuine assessment of the inherited work's value
before it gets converted or discarded, worried that economic urgency will bury something irretrievably.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Baek Ji-hoon grants a limited window — a defined, bounded period for B to assess the research mission's
value before conversion proceeds regardless of the outcome. The assessment turns up nothing the
settlement can justify prioritizing over its own survival, and the conversion goes forward as planned.
Sinheung's founding research heritage fades as its fabrication economy takes over — a deliberate choice,
not a simple drift, credited directly to the engineer who pushed it through.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the assessment happened formally, on the record, before the conversion proceeded, Sinheung ends
up with an actual archived account of what the original Russian research mission was pursuing and why it
was ultimately set aside — not lost knowledge, but consciously, deliberately preserved as historical
record even as the physical infrastructure itself gets repurposed.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that archived assessment exists, later generations of Sinheung residents — curious about the
city's own pre-fabrication history — have an actual, concrete document to consult rather than pure oral
legend or informed guesswork, giving the "research station that became an industrial city" story a real,
citable historical foundation rather than leaving it as vague founding-era myth.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Sinheung's fabrication economy stands fully established, its research-station origin fading from daily
practice but preserved, deliberately, as documented civic history — proof that Baek Ji-hoon's own
conversion was a considered choice, made with the original mission's value genuinely weighed and found
wanting, not simply overwritten without a second thought.
