## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Sinheung's founding population inherits a claim secured through diplomacy rather than earned through
organic settlement — a fact the population lives with rather than a problem yet actively addressed.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A claim like that could easily feel less legitimate, less truly theirs, than a station a population
settled and built up entirely on its own — and that quiet feeling of illegitimacy starts to surface,
openly, whenever Sinheung's residents interact with a cluster neighbor whose own founding felt more
"earned" by comparison. Residents lean hard into demonstrated contribution instead, building something
genuinely, visibly valuable to the rest of Tepenia as a way of making the diplomatically-secured claim
feel earned rather than merely granted. But proving a claim through output means the standard never
really lets up — there's always more to demonstrate — and at some point a resident, exhausted by a
civic culture that never seems to allow satisfaction, pushes back against the whole premise that the
claim needs continuous re-earning at all.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone exhausted by
Sinheung's own relentless, never-satisfied civic ambition, arguing the city has long since proven
whatever it needed to prove and deserves to simply rest on real, accumulated achievement, and someone who
insists the whole premise of "proving it, then stopping" misunderstands what the claim actually requires
— that legitimacy secured through ongoing demonstration can't simply convert into permanent, static
security the way an inherited claim would. Both are responding to something real: A's exhaustion is
legitimate after generations of unrelenting effort; B's insistence reflects a genuine, structural fact
about how this particular claim actually works, whether anyone finds it exhausting or not.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a mid-career worker or
civic figure, genuinely accomplished within the city's own industrial or fabrication economy, worn down by
a culture that never lets any achievement simply be enough. Character B is plausibly an older resident or
civic leader who has internalized the city's whole founding logic most completely, for whom the idea of
"resting" on the claim reads as a genuine, structural risk rather than a reasonable request.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

No resolution actually settles the underlying disagreement — B's position effectively wins in practice,
since the civic culture doesn't change, but A's protest gets formally acknowledged rather than dismissed,
producing the first honest, public admission within Sinheung's own civic life that the standard genuinely
never lets up, rather than pretending otherwise. A sharp, forward-looking civic ambition takes hold and
never fully settles — pride here stays active and ongoing, not something the city can simply rest on once
achieved.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the exhausting nature of the standard is now openly acknowledged rather than denied, Sinheung
develops an actual, if informal, civic vocabulary for talking about burnout and diminishing returns —
residents can name the fatigue directly, even while the underlying expectation to keep demonstrating
value remains fully in place.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that vocabulary exists and gets used openly, later generations of Sinheung workers develop
informal, mutual-support practices around managing the pressure — not formal labor protections, but a
real, shared cultural understanding among residents that acknowledging fatigue is not the same as
abandoning the standard, letting the city sustain its relentless ambition without that ambition simply
burning out the people carrying it.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Sinheung's civic ambition remains as sharp and unresting as ever, but the population has developed real,
if informal, ways of naming and managing the toll that ambition takes — proof through output remains the
city's whole civic logic, but it now coexists with an honest acknowledgment of what sustaining it actually
costs.
