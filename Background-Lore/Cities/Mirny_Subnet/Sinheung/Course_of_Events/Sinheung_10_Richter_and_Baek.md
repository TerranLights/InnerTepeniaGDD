## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Baek Ji-hoon converts Sinheung's inherited infrastructure into a genuine industrial economy; Dae-ho
Richter later secures its place among the nation's few chamber manufacturers — two real, significant
achievements, remembered separately, generations apart.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The two achievements happen far enough apart, generationally, that residents don't automatically connect
them as one continuous story — Baek Ji-hoon belongs to founding-era memory, Richter to a much later,
better-documented civic triumph, and nothing in how either is remembered originally links the two
together. Local institutional memory eventually does draw the line directly — one figure who built the
foundation, another who built the achievement the foundation made possible, two halves of the same civic
arc rather than two unrelated successes. But crediting a whole city's defining accomplishment to two
specific people risks flattening everyone else's contribution along the way — and once the two-figure
narrative starts circulating as Sinheung's own official founding-to-achievement story, some among the
generations of ordinary workers who actually built and ran the fabrication economy in between start to
resent being written out of it entirely.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a civic historian or
storyteller championing the clean, two-figure Baek-to-Richter narrative because it's genuinely more
compelling and easier to teach newcomers, and a representative of the intervening generations of ordinary
plant workers who feel — legitimately — that a story crediting only two names erases the collective

is wrong: A's narrative genuinely serves the city's ability to teach its own history simply; B's objection
correctly identifies what that simplicity costs.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic educator,
archivist, or storyteller responsible for how Sinheung's history actually gets taught and retold to new
generations. Character B is plausibly a descendant of one of the intervening generations of plant
workers, or a labor-history-minded resident, pushing back on behalf of everyone whose contribution the
clean two-name story leaves out.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The two-figure narrative survives, but reframed rather than abandoned — both names get remembered less as
individual heroes and more as convenient anchors for a much longer, more collective story, explicitly
taught alongside acknowledgment of the generations of ordinary workers whose labor the two names
represent rather than replace.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the reframing was formalized rather than left as an unresolved tension, Sinheung's civic education
develops an actual, deliberate two-layer teaching approach — the memorable, simple Baek-to-Richter story
for newcomers and children, paired explicitly with a fuller, collective account for anyone who wants the
complete picture, rather than one narrative fully displacing the other.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that two-layer approach becomes standard practice, Sinheung's own civic storytelling develops a
general, reusable pattern for handling every other achievement the city credits to specific named figures
going forward — a simple version for quick teaching, paired deliberately with a fuller, more collective
account preserved alongside it, avoiding the same erasure complaint recurring with the next figure the
city eventually chooses to honor.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Sinheung now teaches its own history in two deliberate layers — the memorable, simple story of Baek and
Richter, and the fuller, collective account beside it — proof of what Sinheung built, credited to the
two people history happened to remember doing it, without erasing everyone else who actually built it
alongside them.
