## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Sayowa's earliest exile community settles onto JARE's inherited research infrastructure with modest
ambitions — a small, research-descended town, content to exist at the scale its founders actually
brought with them, with no particular expectation of becoming anything more.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The town's own geography has other plans for it. Sayowa happens to sit exactly where the Mawson
subnet, the inland route to Concordia, and the Halley subnet all physically converge — a fact that has
nothing to do with the founders' intentions and everything to do with where JARE's station happened to
already be. Traffic, freight, and coordination begin accumulating here regardless of the population's
own size or ambition, the geography deciding Sayowa's importance long before its residents do. What
starts as a trickle of through-traffic and informal coordination requests grows, year over year, into
something the town's existing habits — modest, research-oriented, built for a population that never
expected to matter to anyone outside itself — can no longer simply absorb without deciding, one way or
another, what to do about it.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone who wants Sayowa
to deliberately invest in and grow to meet its accumulating junction role, and someone who fears that
investment will crowd out or subordinate the town's original research character. This shouldn't play as
progress-vs.-nostalgia — A's push is a genuine practical necessity (the traffic isn't going away, and
ignoring it risks real failures other cities would suffer from), while B's worry is equally legitimate:
JARE's research tradition is the actual reason this town exists at all, and industrial buildout
undertaken carelessly could turn a research community with a side function into a logistics node with a
research footnote. A good version of this conflict makes both positions load-bearing: neither side is
wrong about what Sayowa risks losing if the other one simply gets its way unchecked.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a practically-minded
coordinator or dispatcher — someone who deals daily with the accumulating freight and coordination
burden and has watched informal habits start to buckle under it. Character B is plausibly someone
descended from the founding population's own ex-JARE researchers (who personally carried that research
ethos into exile, not a surviving institution — see `Specs/Sayowa.md`, corrected 2026-07-24, GPS-purposes-
only pass), personally invested in the town's founding purpose,
who isn't opposed to growth in principle but wants it to happen in a way that protects rather than
erases what that research tradition originally built. Either could be JARE-descended themselves; the
conflict reads best if both genuinely belong to the town's own founding lineage rather than one being an
outside industrial interest imposing change on a resistant local.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Sayowa reorganizes around the junction deliberately rather than by accident — industrial and dispatch
infrastructure growing to match a role the town never set out to hold, but built in a way that keeps
JARE's original research footprint intact and separate rather than folding it into the new industrial
apparatus.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

That deliberate, negotiated reorganization is what produces Sayowa's own lasting civic geography: a
clean split between an industrialized fabrication-and-dispatch half and a residential (including the
preserved research) half, rather than the two simply blurring together the way an unplanned, purely
reactive buildout might have let them.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the split is physical and deliberate rather than incidental, civic and leisure life ends up
tucked structurally into the residential half rather than commanding any prominent space of its own —
not because Sayowa's people don't value it, but because the reorganization that created the split never
allotted it a competing claim on the industrial side in the first place.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Sayowa is now a city built in two matched halves — an industrial fabrication-and-dispatch engine on one
side, a residential and research-preserving community on the other — carrying a junction role the town
never asked for with infrastructure it built on purpose. But the very geography that forced this
reorganization gives every future dispute over the town's identity a permanent, physical shape: whichever
half a given argument favors, the other half's existence is never in question again.
