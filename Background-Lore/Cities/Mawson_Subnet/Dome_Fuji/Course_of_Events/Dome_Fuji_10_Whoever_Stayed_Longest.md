## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Ice Cold Buddhism arrives at Dome Fuji with a deliberately anti-hierarchical ethos — devotion, not
rank, is the only credential that matters, and no devotee is supposed to hold formal authority over
another.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A growing community still needs someone to actually steward Nyakkyo and manage the observance calendar
— the anti-hierarchical ideal doesn't make the practical work disappear. The role falls, informally and
without election, to whoever has simply been there longest — the devotees who've witnessed the most
Deepest Colds becoming de facto stewards through nothing but accumulated tenure. But deference earned
this way could easily calcify into an unofficial ruling class if nobody's watching for it, and as the
first generation of long-tenured stewards settles in, that risk stops being hypothetical.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a long-tenured
steward who has, gradually and perhaps not fully consciously, started acting with more authority than
pure deference-to-experience actually justifies — making calls, or expecting deference, beyond the
narrow work of Nyakkyo and the calendar — and a newer or mid-tenure devotee who pushes back, invoking
the faith's own founding anti-hierarchical principle before the drift calcifies into something harder
to undo. This works best if A isn't a villain seizing power deliberately — the drift is gradual, earned
respect slowly generalizing into something broader almost without anyone deciding it should — which
makes B's pushback a genuine risk rather than an easy call: challenging a respected elder, even a
gently overreaching one, isn't comfortable for anyone.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly one of the community's
very first stewards, someone whose authority has been real and useful for so long that its edges have
started blurring without any deliberate intent to grab more than was given. Character B is plausibly a
younger devotee who arrived with the faith's founding ideals fresh and unweathered, less willing than
longer-tenured devotees to let the drift pass unremarked simply out of respect for A's genuine, earned
standing.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

It doesn't calcify. B's challenge holds: newer devotees defer to stewards' experience specifically, not
their authority, and the moment a steward stops actively living the practice — stops making the
crossing, stops enduring the cold, stops actually showing up — the deference quietly stops too, without
confrontation or formal removal ever being required.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because deference is now understood to be tied to actively living the practice rather than to the title
itself, the community develops an ongoing, low-key habit of informally noticing whether a given steward
is still doing the work themselves — a built-in, unwritten accountability check rather than any formal
process of review or removal.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because standing is continuously, informally re-earned rather than granted once and kept forever,
stewards who age, degrade, or simply grow too worn down by the altitude to keep practicing at the same
intensity quietly and gracefully lose their standing without any confrontation or formal removal ever
being necessary — meaning Dome Fuji, uniquely among Tepenian institutions, has effectively solved
graceful succession through the logic of its own culture rather than through any written rule, policy,
or election.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Dome Fuji's stewardship remains genuinely fluid and self-correcting — authority quietly earned and
quietly relinquished, exactly as the practice itself demands, succession handled by culture rather than
by any formal mechanism the faith would otherwise have needed to invent.
