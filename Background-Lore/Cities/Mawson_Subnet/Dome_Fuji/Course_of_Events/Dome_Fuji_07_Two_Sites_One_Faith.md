## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Kunlun and Dome Fuji both draw Ice Cold Buddhist devotees, but through completely different logic —
Kunlun curated by vocation, its devotees arriving through a comms-and-astronomy heritage; Dome Fuji
open to anyone who simply arrives, no vocation or curation required.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Two primary sites within the same young faith risk drifting into rivalry over which one is truly
holiest, and that risk stops being theoretical once devotees who've made the journey to both sites
start comparing what they found. The two communities, isolated from each other by real distance and
real difficulty, begin working out over generations of informal contact — carried mostly by the rare
devotees making the dual pilgrimage between them — a doctrine that could keep both meaningful without
ranking one above the other.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a genuine theological disagreement, carried across
the distance between the two sites, between a Dome Fuji devotee who has come to believe pure,
uncurated arrival is the faith's truest expression — implicitly placing Dome Fuji above Kunlun's
vocation-gated model — and a Kunlun devotee who believes the deliberate, effortful work of building
something that reaches toward the sky is the truer devotion, implicitly reading Dome Fuji's open-arrival
ethos as passive by comparison. Both are defending something real about their own site's practice; the
danger is that an argument about doctrine quietly becomes an argument about status.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a devotee who has
made the dual pilgrimage and returned to Dome Fuji more convinced of its primacy rather than less.
Character B is plausibly a Kunlun devotee, likely one with a technical or vocational background from
before conversion, whose sense of devotion is bound up with building and effort in a way Dome Fuji's
pure-arrival model doesn't validate. Their disagreement is best carried through exchanged word, letter,
or a rare in-person meeting rather than any formal debate — the faith has no venue for that yet.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither site is declared holier. The two communities settle on "reaching" and "arriving" as
complementary, equally valid expressions of the same underlying devotion — Kunlun as the path of
reaching, building something toward the sky; Dome Fuji as the path of arriving, simply getting yourself
to something already there. The complementary relationship becomes one of the faith's genuine doctrinal
achievements, hard-won through real, sustained dialogue rather than assumed from the start.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Given the faith otherwise has no fixed leadership and no written scripture, "reaching versus arriving"
becomes something close to Ice Cold Buddhism's only piece of genuinely codified, cross-site doctrine —
everything else about the faith remains local, informal, or, as with the robes, deliberately layered
rather than settled.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because this is the one piece of actual codified teaching the faith has, it becomes the specific thing
newer devotees at both sites are taught early and explicitly — which in turn gives the existing but
previously informal practice of the dual pilgrimage between Kunlun and Dome Fuji real theological
weight it didn't have before. What was once simply something a small number of unusually devoted
pilgrims happened to do becomes something newer devotees are told, from early on, to aspire to: the
living completion of the faith's own doctrine, sought out deliberately rather than stumbled into.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

"Reaching versus arriving" is now the faith's one settled, explicitly taught doctrine, and the dual
pilgrimage between Kunlun and Dome Fuji has shifted from an informal rarity into a recognized, actively
sought devotional milestone — the clearest way any devotee can now demonstrate having lived both halves
of what the faith teaches.
