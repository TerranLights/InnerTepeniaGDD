## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

A maintenance robot from the original research era simply never received a transfer order before the
last humans departed, and stays behind through the station's quiet, unceremonious emptying — outlasting
every human it once served, alone at Dome Fuji for however many decades separate the research era's end
from the first pilgrims' arrival.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Decades later, an entirely new population arrives for entirely different reasons, and this figure's
whole presence contradicts the community's own founding logic. Everyone else's arrival is their
credential — nobody comes to Dome Fuji by accident, drift, or economic necessity — but this one never
arrived at all. It was simply, always, already there, watching two completely different eras occupy the
same coordinates.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over what status, if any, this
figure deserves within a community whose entire social contract runs on devotional arrival as the sole
credential. One devotee insists status still has to be earned the way everyone else earned it — through
pilgrimage, through devotion — and is uneasy about making an exception for a figure with no devotional
story to point to at all. Another argues that decades of solitary, unwitnessed care for the station is
its own form of devotion, arguably purer for having expected no audience and no credit. This should read
as a real community-defining argument, not an easy call — A's caution protects the coherence of the
faith's founding principle; B's argument, if it wins, quietly redefines what "devotion" is even allowed
to mean.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a devotee-steward
invested in the arrival-is-credential system precisely because they earned their own standing through
it, and who worries what it means for that system if an exception gets made this easily. Character B is
plausibly one of the community's earliest pilgrims — someone who has actually spent real time observing
the maintenance robot's quiet, unasked-for care for the place, and who has come to see something
devotional in it whether or not the figure itself would ever describe it that way.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The community settles the question by not fully settling it: the figure has no personal stake in the
status system built around pilgrimage and devotion, and that very indifference is what resolves the
argument. It settles into a strange, unofficial authority precisely because it stands outside the whole
framework the debate was conducted in — not an elder devotee, not quite an outsider, deferred to by a
community that simply has no other category to put it in.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because this figure now holds real, if undefined, authority without ever having gone through the
community's normal social contract, the community has to invent, informally, an entirely new social
category just to describe what it is — expanding Dome Fuji's otherwise flat, single-category social
structure (devotee, and nothing else) into two categories for the first time in its history.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because a second social category now genuinely exists, however rare and singular in practice, the
community has, for the first time, actual conceptual room for the possibility of other non-devotee
figures being recognized similarly in the future — a human researcher, an Upper Earth visitor, some
other genuinely non-devotee presence who does something the community deems worthy of comparable
deference would now have an actual slot to be placed into, where previously the faith's only working
category was "devotee," and everything else was simply "not here."

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Dome Fuji's social structure now formally, if informally, contains two categories instead of one —
devotees, and this figure's own singular, liminal status — quietly reshaping what any future exception
to the community's arrival-is-credential rule could look like, and who it might someday include.
