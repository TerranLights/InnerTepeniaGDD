## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Ice Cold Buddhism's earliest devotees, searching old records and transport manifests for sites that
embody the faith's still-forming theology, surface Dome Fuji's documented but long-abandoned altitude
record — the highest, coldest, quietest inhabited point anyone can find a paper trail for.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Nobody currently living knows what condition decades of vacancy have actually left the station in, and
the only way to find out is to go. A small expedition — fewer than twenty devotees — commits to making
the interior plateau crossing to see for themselves, prepared, going in, for ruin: collapsed structures,
scavenged equipment, a monument to neglect rather than to anything worth venerating.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a devotee-scout convinced
Dome Fuji's abandoned altitude record makes it genuinely sacred ground worth the crossing's real risk,
and a more cautious senior figure within the still-young faith who worries about spending scarce lives
and resources chasing an unconfirmed site built on nothing but old paperwork. This shouldn't resolve as
reckless-visionary-proven-right — B's caution is legitimate given how small and fragile the faith still
is at this point, and A's conviction, while ultimately vindicated, is still a genuine gamble at the
moment it's made, not a certainty either character could have known going in.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly one of the faith's
earliest, most restless converts — someone drawn to extremity for its own sake, the kind of figure who
reads "effectively 4,500 meters of physiological altitude, documented, abandoned" and hears a calling
rather than a warning. Character B is plausibly one of the faith's few established elders at this early
stage, responsible for the survival of a community that can't yet afford to lose twenty devotees to a
crossing that turns out to be for nothing.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The expedition goes ahead, A's judgment vindicated by what they find: Antarctic cold preserves rather
than destroys, and the station's infrastructure sits almost exactly as it was left, snow-coated and
undisturbed. The pilgrims don't find wreckage to mourn — they find stillness to interpret, and for a
faith built around cold as a spiritual state, an untouched monument to quiet, non-violent absence reads
less like tragedy and more like proof of concept.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The specific route this first expedition improvised across the plateau — the waypoints they marked to
survive their own uncertain crossing, chosen under real pressure rather than planned at leisure — gets
adopted wholesale by the pilgrims who follow. What was one party's improvised survival trail hardens,
trip by trip, into a known, marked, maintained path: the Waystones.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the crossing now has fixed, maintained waypoints instead of relying on each new party's own
improvisation, the risk calculus that made B's original caution rational drops sharply for everyone
who comes after — which is precisely what allows the trickle of individual pilgrimages the faith
depends on to actually sustain itself generation over generation, rather than remaining a single,
unrepeatable act of nerve.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Dome Fuji now has a known, marked pilgrimage route across the plateau, and a growing, steady trickle of
individual devotees making the crossing behind the first expedition's proof that the destination is
worth reaching — the beginning of the slow, one-at-a-time accretion that will eventually become Dome
Fuji's entire population.
