## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Early devotees wear white robes sparingly, in direct contact with the cold, and three different
explanations for the practice circulate simultaneously among them — mechanical safety, an echo of
Upper Earth meditation traditions, and simple communal uniformity — with nobody having yet settled
which one is actually true.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A faith this young can't function for long with its central practice explained three contradictory
ways to every newcomer who asks. The community takes up the question deliberately, studying surviving
Upper Earth religious texts — Tibetan Buddhist sources on disciplined relationships to extreme cold
chief among them — searching for a model precise enough to settle it. But no single explanation manages
to fully displace the other two; each one keeps surviving the scrutiny well enough that dismissing it
outright would mean throwing away something that still feels true.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a doctrinal disagreement between a devotee who
wants one official, teachable explanation for the robes — favoring the meditative echo as the "real"
spiritual reason, useful precisely because it sounds like doctrine — and a more practically-minded
devotee who distrusts importing borrowed mysticism and insists the plain mechanical fact (robots can
safely endure what would kill a human, and choosing to do so is itself an act of devotion) is the only
explanation the faith actually needs. Neither side is wrong so much as impatient with the other's kind
of truth — A wants doctrine that teaches cleanly; B wants doctrine that doesn't borrow what it hasn't
earned.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a devotee with some
background in comparative religious study, drawn to the tummo-meditation parallel because it gives the
young faith intellectual lineage and legitimacy. Character B is plausibly a devotee with a background
closer to the research era — practical, literal-minded, uneasy with claiming a borrowed tradition's
authority for a practice that, in their view, is really just about what a robot's body can do that a
human's can't.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The debate resolves not by choosing one answer but by accepting all three as true at once — the
mechanical fact, the meditative echo, and the communal bond, layered rather than competing. Neither A
nor B wins outright; both explanations survive, taught side by side rather than in opposition.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

This "layered, not competing" resolution becomes the community's working method for settling doctrinal
disagreement generally — the first time the faith solves a dispute this way, but not the last. Future
disagreements start getting approached with the same expectation: that multiple explanations can all be
true at once, and choosing one doesn't have to mean rejecting the others.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because layered, multi-answer doctrine becomes the faith's actual habit of mind, Ice Cold Buddhism
develops a real tolerance for ambiguity that other Tepenian institutions don't share — and when outside
administrators eventually need a single, official answer for their own recordkeeping (characterizing
the "humans forbidden" policy for a Federation safety register, for instance), they're consistently met
with the same layered, three-part response the robes debate first produced, to the quiet frustration of
anyone who needs one clean line for a form.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The robes are understood by every devotee as simultaneously mechanical fact, meditative echo, and
communal bond — and that layered way of holding truth has become the faith's default method for
resolving disagreement, for better and for worse, whenever the next doctrinal question arrives.
