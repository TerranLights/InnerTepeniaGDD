## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Devotees arrive at Dome Fuji across scattered individual pilgrimages, each on their own schedule, with
no shared calendar to mark time together as a single community.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A faith built around communal devotion to cold needs at least one moment everyone actually shares, not
just a shared location. The community fixes on the precise coldest point of polar night — the first
calm, clear night after the solstice — as a universal, mandatory observance. But committing to a
weather-dependent date means the observance can never be scheduled with total certainty in advance,
and the question of how to handle that uncertainty becomes its own genuine dispute.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between a devotee who wants a
fixed calendar date for Deepest Cold — predictable, easy to plan and coordinate a communal event around
— and a devotee who insists the observance has to track the literal coldest moment, however
unpredictable, because fixing an arbitrary date instead would substitute administrative convenience for
the actual condition the whole faith is organized around. This is a genuine values conflict, not a
scheduling squabble: A is protecting the community's ability to function together; B is protecting the
faith's core commitment to attending to what's actually true rather than what's simply convenient.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a devotee with
organizational instincts, someone who's tried and failed to coordinate a communal gathering around an
unpredictable date before and doesn't want to repeat the experience. Character B is plausibly a devotee
with a closer, more attentive relationship to the specific climate of the plateau — someone who has
already, informally, started tracking conditions well enough to sense that the "real" coldest night is
worth waiting for.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's position carries the day: Deepest Cold is tracked with genuine meteorological seriousness, the
exact date shifting year to year, rather than fixed to a convenient calendar slot. The uncertainty
itself becomes part of the anticipation rather than a flaw in the tradition — the whole community
watches for it together.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because Deepest Cold can't be called from a calendar, correctly predicting and announcing it becomes a
genuine, learned skill rather than an administrative task — and the devotees who prove reliably good at
reading Dome Fuji's specific conditions start being trusted with the responsibility of calling it for
everyone else.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That skill becomes one of the concrete, demonstrable things the community's tenure-based steward status
actually tracks — not simply "how long has this devotee been here," but "how many times has this
devotee correctly read the plateau well enough to call Deepest Cold." Misjudging the call — declaring it
too early, or missing the real night entirely — becomes a rare but real, quietly remembered failure that
specifically costs a steward's standing, giving the community's informal deference to its longest-tenured
devotees an actual earned mechanism behind it, not just accumulated time.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Deepest Cold now anchors Dome Fuji's entire shared calendar, and correctly calling it has become an
informal proving ground — one of the concrete, quietly tracked reasons some devotees are trusted with
stewardship and others aren't.
