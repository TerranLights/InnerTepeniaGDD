## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Nyakkyo stands at the dome's geometric high point, deliberately small, low, and empty (chain #4) — the
faith's central sacred site, tended with real reverence by a devotee population still relatively young in
its own religious history.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A fire — almost certainly electrical or heating-system in origin, given the structure's spare, minimal
design — damages Nyakkyo badly enough that the site can no longer function as it did. For a faith this
young, centered this completely on one specific structure, the loss lands as a genuine crisis.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a conflict about whether to rebuild — both sides
agree Nyakkyo has to be restored. Character A wants it rebuilt exactly as it was, treating any deviation
from the original small-low-empty design as a betrayal of the theological insight that made the site
meaningful in the first place. Character B isn't opposed to preserving the design, but worries that
simply replicating the past risks treating rebuilding as mere repair rather than a genuine act of
devotion in its own right — something the community should approach thoughtfully, not just mechanically.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a longtime steward
(echoing chain #10's tenure-based deference), protective of Nyakkyo's exact original form. Character B is
plausibly a newer devotee or a steward from a different site, open to treating the rebuilding itself as
meaningful rather than merely restorative.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The community rebuilds Nyakkyo identical in form — still small, low, empty — but the act of rebuilding
itself, undertaken deliberately and communally rather than delegated or rushed, becomes its own
devotional practice, not simply a repair.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Nyakkyo's own history changes shape — no longer just a site that was discovered and built once, but one
that was also lost and remade by the community's own hands, giving it a second, communally-earned
foundation layered directly onto the first.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

The fire and rebuilding become the faith's own version of a real, documented pattern in actual pilgrimage
religion — proof that devotion isn't only discovering and maintaining what already exists, but actively
remaking it when necessary, deepening the community's own ownership of their sacred site rather than
leaving it as something merely inherited.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Nyakkyo stands rebuilt, identical in form to before but carrying real, additional devotional weight — the
fire and its reconstruction now as much a part of the site's sacred history as its original discovery and
construction ever were.
