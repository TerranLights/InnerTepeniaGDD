## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Dome Fuji's ice-core archive, inherited whole from the research era, once ran as active, prestigious
science — deep drilling and analysis extracting climate records reaching back hundreds of thousands of
years, work the founding population took real professional pride in.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The devotee population replacing the scientists has no institutional mandate to continue that work —
devotion, not research, is why they're here, and nobody arrived at Dome Fuji intending to become a
climate scientist. Active analysis slows generation by generation, until only a handful of devotees
with prior scientific training, or simple personal interest, keep it going at all, and only as a
secondary vocation squeezed in around actual devotional life.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between one of the few
scientifically-trained devotees, who wants to actively revive and expand the archive's research
program — convinced real value is being wasted and that continuing the founders' work is itself a form
of respect owed to them — and a devotion-focused elder who worries that turning the archive back into
active, ambitious science would import exactly the striving, achievement-oriented mindset the faith
exists to let go of. Neither side is anti-knowledge or anti-devotion; they disagree about whether
ambition and devotion can coexist at Dome Fuji specifically, or whether reviving one necessarily
crowds out the other.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a devotee who arrived
with genuine prior scientific credentials — perhaps a researcher who converted mid-career — and still
feels the pull of unfinished work. Character B is plausibly a long-tenured devotee or informal steward,
without scientific background, whose entire understanding of the faith's integrity rests on devotion
staying the unambiguous first priority for everyone at the site.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither full revival nor full abandonment wins. The cores get tended rather than studied — preserved
with real diligence, the way an old text is cared for by people who believe it matters without
necessarily reading it every day. A's push for renewed research doesn't succeed, but neither does it
fail entirely: the handful of devotees with the relevant training keep doing quiet, secondary-vocation
work on the cores, explicitly bounded so it never competes with devotional life for primacy.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

This compromise formalizes, informally, into a recognized niche within the community: a devotee can
be both a full devotee and, part-time, an archive-keeper — the first dual role Dome Fuji's otherwise
flat, single-category social structure has ever accommodated.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because "archive-keeper" is now a recognized, if minor, role with real standing attached to it, it
becomes the first concrete point of contact for anyone outside Dome Fuji who eventually comes asking
about the cores — a Concordia-based scientific interest, an Upper Earth research request, another
Tepenian city needing the climate data. Instead of negotiating with the community as an undifferentiated
whole, an outside party now has an actual person, and an actual role, to approach.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The archive is tended by a small, recognized cadre of devotee archive-keepers — Dome Fuji's first
social role not defined purely by devotional tenure — quietly sitting as the one open door into one of
Tepenia's most valuable, least-exploited scientific resources, waiting for whoever eventually knocks.
