## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Mawson's genuine warmth and ease of making friends is already a lived civic trait — real, and already
noticed by anyone passing through — but nobody's yet built anything deliberate out of it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A hub city with real hospitality instincts and no particular use for them is leaving something on the
table. A hospitality-minded figure proposes something specific — cultivating Mawson as an actual
destination, not just a place people pass through on hub business — but turning an informal civic trait
into deliberate infrastructure takes real investment nobody's certain will pay off, and the city's hub
work already has a legitimate, well-established claim on the same limited resources.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between a hospitality-minded
civic figure pushing to invest real resources in dedicated hospitality infrastructure — betting on an
unproven return — and a hub-logistics administrator who views the proposal as a distraction from the
city's core, proven function: freight, dispatch, and continental coordination. Neither side is wrong
to worry: A is right that an untapped civic trait is being wasted, and B is right that Mawson's
survival has always depended on the hub work actually getting done. The conflict is a real budget-and-
priorities fight, not a personality clash.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a hospitality-
minded figure — perhaps someone already informally known for making visitors feel welcome — proposing
to turn instinct into institution. Character B is plausibly a hub-focused planner or administrator
whose whole professional investment is in keeping freight and dispatch running smoothly, skeptical of
diverting attention toward something as unproven as tourism.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A's proposal wins, but only on her own insistence and against real resistance: the earliest dedicated
hospitality infrastructure gets built anyway, becoming the foundation the city's later
honeymoon-destination reputation grows out of decades on. B's resistance isn't erased by the
outcome — it's simply outlasted.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because this first hospitality investment had to be fought for and justified against hub-function
priorities directly, it sets a lasting precedent: every hospitality investment that follows has to make
the same case for itself, rather than being assumed to deserve resources simply because the first one
succeeded.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because hospitality investment always has to actively justify itself rather than being taken for
granted, Mawson's leadership develops the deliberate, ongoing habit of weighing both civic priorities
side by side on their own merits, every time — the specific practice that later generations formalize
into full civic policy, deliberately protecting space for both hospitality and hub function rather than
letting either one crowd out the other by default.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mawson now has real, if modest, dedicated hospitality infrastructure that had to earn its place against
the city's hub priorities — establishing, from this first contested investment onward, the pattern of
Mawson's leadership actively weighing and defending both civic priorities rather than letting either
one win by default.
