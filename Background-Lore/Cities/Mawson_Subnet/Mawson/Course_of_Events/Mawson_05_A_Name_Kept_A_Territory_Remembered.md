## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Mawson's exile community inherits both the real explorer's name and the historical Australian
Antarctic Territory claim built around this stretch of coast, alongside genuine, felt St. Douglas
veneration passed down from the founding wave.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The demographic population actually living under that name and that claim shifts, generation by
generation, toward something Australia never anticipated. St. Douglas veneration and the old Territory
claim could easily have faded as irrelevant once the founding population's own numbers thinned enough
that fewer and fewer residents had any personal tie to either one.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between a civic figure who sees
continuing to formally venerate St. Douglas and the old Territory claim as empty, even slightly
awkward pageantry — a history fewer residents have any real tie to, better retired quietly and without
fuss — and a figure, of any heritage, who insists Mawson actively choose to keep the history alive on
purpose, arguing that letting an inherited legacy fade by simple neglect isn't neutral, it's its own
quiet kind of erasure. Neither position needs to be read as more legitimate than the other going in —
this is a genuine debate about what a city owes its own founding story once fewer people share it.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a pragmatic civic
administrator, uninterested in ceremony for its own sake and focused on where the city's attention
should actually go. Character B is plausibly a civic figure — not necessarily of Australian descent
themselves — who has thought seriously about what it means for a city to let its own founding story go
unmarked, and isn't willing to let that happen by default rather than by decision.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The city chooses, deliberately, to keep both St. Douglas veneration and the old Territory claim alive
— not as living political authority, but as honored civic history. Mawson ends up carrying its
founding legacy forward openly, on its own terms, rather than letting demographic change quietly erase
it by inattention.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the choice to keep the founding legacy alive was made deliberately and openly — explicitly as
history rather than as authority — Mawson develops an actual, distinct civic vocabulary for holding
onto founding-era symbols without those symbols implying any claim to present-day power.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that vocabulary already exists, and has already been tested once with St. Douglas and the
Territory claim, the city doesn't have to improvise a way of holding two truths at once when its
demographic balance later shifts decisively and China becomes demographic Primary. Mawson already has
the tested civic habit of honoring an inherited legacy as history while embracing a different present
as simply what the city has become — the exact resolution that later lets residents say "The Name That
Outlasted the Founders" with warmth rather than discomfort.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mawson has an established, deliberately chosen civic habit of separating honored history from present
authority — a habit that will be reused, and proven again, once the city's demographic composition
shifts decisively away from its founding nationality.
