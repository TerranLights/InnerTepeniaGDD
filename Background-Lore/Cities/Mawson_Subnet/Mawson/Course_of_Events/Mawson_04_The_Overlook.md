## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

As Mawson's hospitality reputation grows, newly-married couples start arriving with no fixed
itinerary, unsure what to actually do with a week or two at the edge of the continent beyond simply
being comfortable there.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A destination reputation needs something concrete for visitors to actually experience together, not
just a pleasant place to stay. A customary visit to the Prince Charles Mountains overlook above
Horseshoe Harbor takes hold, timed to whichever extreme — polar night or midnight sun — happens to be
in season during a given stay. But couples arrive at wildly different times of year, meaning no two
visits ever see quite the same sky, and the question of what to actually promise visitors about the
experience becomes a real, practical problem for whoever's guiding them.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between a hospitality worker who
wants to standardize the overlook visit into a polished, reliable, repeatable experience — a script
that guarantees every couple gets a satisfying visit regardless of season — and another hospitality
figure who argues that trying to standardize what nature happens to be doing that week betrays the
whole point of the place, and that visitors should be guided through whatever the sky actually gives
them rather than promised a fixed outcome. Both are trying to protect the same reputation; they
disagree about whether reliability or authenticity is the safer bet.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a newer hospitality
worker, trained to think in terms of consistent guest satisfaction and worried about unpredictable
outcomes reflecting badly on the city. Character B is plausibly a more experienced guide, personally
attached to the overlook and confident that its real value lies precisely in never being quite the
same twice.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's instinct proves right: the overlook becomes the honeymoon's defining shared memory precisely
because it's never quite the same experience twice — each couple gets a version of it nobody else's
honeymoon shared. The visit stays a fixed ritual, but its content is deliberately left unscripted.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the overlook's value depends specifically on its unrepeatable, season-dependent character,
Mawson's hospitality workers develop a distinct guiding skill around it — learning to talk each couple
through whatever the sky is actually doing that particular week, rather than delivering a fixed script.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That specific skill — reading a moment and helping visitors appreciate it, unscripted — becomes a
recognized specialty within Mawson's hospitality workforce, something later guides actively train for
and take pride in. A quiet internal hierarchy of skill and reputation starts forming inside the
hospitality sector itself, distinct from and parallel to the city's separate hub-logistics status
system.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The Overlook is now Mawson's signature honeymoon experience, deliberately kept unscripted, and a
handful of the city's hospitality workers have become genuinely known — sought out by name — for their
particular skill at reading and sharing whatever the sky is doing for each new couple.
