## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Australia founds Mawson and sets its whole operational and cultural tone — the AAD heritage, St.
Douglas veneration, the Territory claim, all part of a genuinely, visibly Australian founding
identity.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Later immigration waves eventually make China the demographic Primary, well ahead of the founding-wave
Australian presence. The gap between who founded the city and who actually lives there now becomes
impossible to ignore forever, no matter how gradually it accumulates — and the moment finally arrives
where the city has to decide, openly, what to do about a gap it can no longer pretend not to notice.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a figure invested in the
founding Australian-heritage identity — not hostile to the city's newer residents, but genuinely
uncertain what the demographic shift means for "their" city's identity going forward — and a civic
figure representing, or simply embodying, the new Chinese-Primary majority, who isn't trying to erase
the founding legacy but wants Mawson's present-day identity honestly named and claimed rather than
treated as an awkward footnote to someone else's founding story. Both positions are legible and
sympathetic: A's unease is a real, understandable response to watching something inherited change past
recognition; B's insistence on honest naming isn't erasure, it's a request to actually belong to the
place they live.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly descended from the
original exile wave, or a keeper of St. Douglas tradition, whose relationship to Mawson's identity has
always run through its founding story. Character B is plausibly a civic figure from within the new
demographic majority, established enough in the city's institutions to speak for more than just
themselves, but without any interest in dismissing what came before them.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Mawson's civic culture makes real, open peace with the gap: the founding legacy honored as history, the
present population's own character embraced as exactly what the city has become. "The Name That
Outlasted the Founders" becomes something residents say with genuine warmth rather than discomfort — a
fact about the city they've settled, not a tension they're still working through.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because this resolution was reached through real, named acknowledgment rather than quiet drift, Mawson
develops the specific civic habit of periodically and openly retelling the phrase and its meaning —
teaching new generations, of any heritage, the actual history behind it, so it never calcifies into
either empty slogan or unspoken grievance.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the story is actively retold rather than left to fade into unremarked civic wallpaper, it
becomes something Mawson's residents can point to explicitly when other Tepenian cities undergoing
their own demographic transitions look to Mawson as a working example of how the gap doesn't have to
become an unresolved wound — Mawson's own deliberately-told history functioning as a genuine,
exportable civic lesson rather than only a private peace the city made for itself.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

"The Name That Outlasted the Founders" is now an actively retold, explicitly taught piece of Mawson's
civic self-understanding — not just a peace the city quietly made with itself, but a story other
Tepenian cities navigating their own demographic change can look to directly.
