## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Mawson's position on Hwy 4, between the Sayowa Junction and Sinheung, makes it the Mawson subnet's
practical coordination center almost automatically — a role decided by geography before anyone in the
city chose it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city that busy, with that much genuine operational responsibility, could easily let its hub function
crowd out everything else about its civic character. As the coordination workload grows heavier year
over year, Mawson's leadership faces a real, concrete choice about whether to let the hospitality
culture growing up alongside the logistics work keep its own dedicated space and attention, or let it
quietly recede in favor of the work that keeps the subnet actually running.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between a logistics-focused
administrator arguing the hub's operational responsibilities should take clear institutional priority
— freight and dispatch failures have continent-wide consequences that a slow season at a guesthouse
never will — and a hospitality-sector figure arguing that letting hub responsibilities dominate
entirely would hollow out exactly what makes Mawson worth visiting or living in, and that civic space

are right about the stakes they're each defending; neither is wrong to worry.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a hub-operations
administrator responsible for keeping subnet-wide freight and dispatch running, under real, measurable
pressure to perform. Character B is plausibly a hospitality-sector leader — perhaps someone who
remembers, or was mentored by someone who remembers, the earlier fight to get hospitality infrastructure
built in the first place, and isn't willing to watch it quietly lose ground now.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Mawson's leadership deliberately protects space for both — resort warmth and hub function developed as
two equally real civic priorities, neither one subordinated to the other. Balancing both means neither
one ever gets the city's undivided attention, but Mawson becomes genuinely good at both without being
defined entirely by either — a city its own residents describe by what it does for people, not simply
by what moves through it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

This deliberate dual-priority balancing gets formalized into how the city trains and assigns its own
civic leadership: rather than treating hospitality and logistics as entirely separate career tracks,
Mawson develops a genuine institutional expectation that its administrators stay conversant in both
sides of the city's work.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Mawson's leadership is required to stay fluent in both domains, the city becomes unusually good
at noticing exactly when logistics work and hospitality work start interfering with each other before
it becomes a real problem — a practical, quiet interdependence-awareness at the leadership level that
simply wouldn't exist if the city's two identities had been allowed to stay administratively separate.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mawson's civic leadership is now expected to stay genuinely fluent in both hospitality and
hub-logistics work — an institutional habit that quietly primes the city to notice, and manage, exactly
how much its two identities actually depend on each other.
