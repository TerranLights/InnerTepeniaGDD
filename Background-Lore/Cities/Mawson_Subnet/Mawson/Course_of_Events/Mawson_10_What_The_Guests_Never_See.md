## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Honeymooning couples arrive at Mawson experiencing warmth, ease, and a genuinely relaxing stay — the
city's welcoming face, fully on display and, for most visitors, the entire story of what Mawson is.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Underneath that welcoming surface, the city is running real, continuous freight and communications
coordination for the entire Mawson subnet — dispatch, routing, and Arcanet traffic that never stops
just because visitors are here to enjoy themselves. Mawson's hospitality workers and its hub-logistics
workers end up operating, in practice, as two barely-overlapping halves of the same city, each rarely
thinking much about the other's daily work — until an ordinary disagreement between two of them makes
that gap in mutual understanding impossible to ignore.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between a hospitality worker
frustrated that the city's logistics side gets so little visible credit despite being, in her view,
what actually keeps Mawson relevant to the rest of the subnet — worried the city's public image has
drifted too far toward "resort" and forgotten what it actually does — and a hub-logistics worker
frustrated in the opposite direction, worried that if the hospitality side's reputation ever slipped,
outsiders would stop wanting anything to do with Mawson at all, dragging the hub's own credibility down
with it. Each is more aware of their own half's stakes than the other's; the conflict is really about
neither side having ever had to reckon with how much they depend on each other.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a hospitality
worker, proud of her sector's work and mildly resentful of how invisible the logistics side keeps it.
Character B is plausibly a hub-logistics worker, equally proud of his own sector and mildly dismissive
of hospitality work as secondary to the "real" job of keeping the subnet running.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Both halves recognize, once the stakes are actually said aloud between them, that they depend on the
same civic reputation to keep functioning — a hub nobody trusts, or a resort nobody visits, would each
undermine the other eventually. Mawson's two identities settle into quiet, permanent interdependence,
even though most visitors only ever see the half built to welcome them.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because this mutual dependency gets named and understood rather than left as an unspoken assumption,
Mawson's leadership starts deliberately cross-briefing both sectors on how the other one actually
affects them — hospitality workers gaining basic literacy in what the hub does, and vice versa, an
institutional practice extending down to the ordinary workforce for the first time.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because ordinary workers on both sides now have at least basic literacy in the other half's stakes,
Mawson becomes able to respond faster and more coherently than most cities would to any single incident
threatening either reputation at once — a hospitality scandal or a logistics failure gets treated, by
both halves, as everyone's problem rather than staying contained to the sector where it started.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mawson's hospitality and hub-logistics workforces now share at least a basic, deliberately cultivated
understanding of how much each depends on the other's reputation — a quiet, practical solidarity
between two halves of the city that used to barely think about each other at all.
