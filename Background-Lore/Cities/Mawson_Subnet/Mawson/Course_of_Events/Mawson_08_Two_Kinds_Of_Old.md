## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Mawson's founding-era legitimacy — the oldest continuous occupation in its subnet — is already
centuries deep by the time its honeymoon reputation starts forming.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The honeymoon reputation is something genuinely new, built deliberately within living memory rather
than inherited from the founding at all. Mawson ends up holding two entirely different kinds of civic
prestige at once — an ancient, inherited kind tied to St. Douglas and continuity, and a modern, actively
earned kind tied to hospitality and warmth. But residents could easily end up treating one as more
legitimate than the other, and as the honeymoon reputation's success grows more visible, that question
stops staying comfortably unspoken.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between a resident who privately
believes the ancient, inherited prestige is the "real" one — the honeymoon reputation, however
successful, comparatively shallow, recent, and commercial rather than earned through continuity — and a
resident, likely from the hospitality sector, who sees the honeymoon reputation as more legitimate
precisely because it was actively chosen and built by living people, not simply inherited by accident of
birth or lineage. This is a genuine philosophical disagreement about what makes something legitimately
part of a city's identity, not a petty status dispute.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an older resident, or
one invested in the founding legacy specifically — a keeper of St. Douglas tradition, perhaps — for
whom lineage carries real weight. Character B is plausibly a hospitality worker or a younger civic
figure who takes real pride in what's been actively built within living memory and doesn't see why
inheritance should automatically outrank achievement.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side wins the argument outright. Mawson's own established civic culture simply never picks a
winner between them — both kinds of prestige are treated as simply true, operating on different
timescales, neither one crowding out the other's claim to matter.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the city deliberately refuses to rank the two, Mawson develops actual civic occasions and
shared spaces that intentionally reference both timescales side by side — St. Douglas-era continuity
and honeymoon-era warmth invoked in the same breath — making the coexistence a lived, visible practice
rather than only an abstract civic stance.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because both kinds of prestige get physically and ceremonially intertwined rather than kept in separate
spheres, newcomers and visitors alike absorb Mawson's dual-timescale identity as simply how the city
is, without ever needing to be told there was once a real debate over which prestige mattered more —
the original tension resolves so thoroughly into ordinary civic texture that later generations
experience the coexistence as natural rather than as a choice their predecessors actually had to make.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mawson's ancient and modern kinds of prestige are now thoroughly woven into the city's everyday civic
life, experienced by residents and visitors alike as simply how Mawson is — the debate over which
mattered more forgotten precisely because it was so thoroughly resolved.
