## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The founding exile community reclaims Mawson Station's substantial but long-dormant infrastructure in 2564,
and — once resettled — never experiences the kind of *repeated* rebuilding-from-scratch other Tepenian
cities go through afterward: once reclaimed, Tepenian Mawson's own operation runs unbroken from that point
forward, infrastructure, procedure, and habit passed down whole within the exile community's own history,
generation after generation.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Unbroken continuity this long means every generation inherits not just infrastructure, but
expectations about how things are supposed to run. Mawson develops an unusually deep institutional
memory early — habits, procedures, and civic instincts passed down essentially unbroken since the
founding. But that kind of inherited weight could easily calcify into rigidity, and as the population
begins to diversify beyond its founding wave, the question of whether it will starts to matter in
practice rather than only in theory.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between someone who wants Mawson's
inherited procedures preserved close to exactly as handed down — worried that loosening them threatens
the very continuity that makes the city function — and someone pushing to adapt those same inherited
habits for a population that no longer matches the one they were built for. This shouldn't play as
rigid-traditionalist versus reckless-reformer: A's caution is protecting something genuinely valuable
(centuries of working Tepenian institutional memory don't survive carelessness), while B isn't trying to
discard that memory, only to keep it usable for people it wasn't originally written for.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a long-serving
station administrator or record-keeper, personally responsible for maintaining the procedures in
question and proud of how well they've held up. Character B is plausibly a newer civic figure —
perhaps from one of the immigration currents just beginning to reshape the city's demographics —
who respects what's been inherited but has direct, personal reason to notice where it no longer quite
fits everyone it's meant to serve.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither wholesale preservation nor wholesale replacement wins. Mawson's inherited habits get carried
forward as a living toolkit rather than a fixed code — taught to newcomers explicitly as "how we do
things here, and why," with room built in for those newcomers to make the practices their own rather
than simply inherit them unchanged. The continuity survives; its rigidity doesn't.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Mawson develops a deliberate civic habit of teaching its own institutional memory to new arrivals as
something adaptable rather than fixed — an actual practice, repeated with every new wave of settlement,
rather than a one-time compromise between A and B.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that teaching habit exists and repeats, later demographic shifts — arriving in waves far larger
than anything A or B could have anticipated — get absorbed into Mawson's institutional memory instead
of displacing it wholesale. The city's continuity keeps stretching to fit whoever's actually living
inside it, generation after generation, precisely because it was never treated as something finished.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Mawson's institutional memory is now explicitly a living inheritance, actively taught and actively
adapted with each new wave of arrivals — deep enough to give the city real stability, flexible enough
that no single generation's version of "how we do things here" is expected to be the last.
