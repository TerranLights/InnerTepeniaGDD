# Mawson — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Mawson's Megasheet (the oldest continuously
occupied site in its subnet, "The Name That Outlasted the Founders," the deliberately cultivated
honeymoon-destination reputation). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. The Station That Never Went Dark

Mawson Station's real-world operation runs continuously from 1954 onward, and the exile community
inheriting it never experiences the kind of total rebuilding-from-scratch other Tepenian cities go
through. **But** unbroken continuity this long means every generation inherits not just infrastructure,
but expectations about how things are supposed to run. **Therefore** Mawson develops an unusually deep
institutional memory early — habits, procedures, and civic instincts passed down essentially unbroken
since the founding. **But** that kind of inherited weight could easily calcify into rigidity.
**Therefore** it doesn't — the same continuity that gives Mawson its institutional depth also gives it
the stability to absorb real demographic change later without the whole civic structure cracking under
the strain.

## 2. Warrick's Transition

The earliest exile community operates, in practice, as a continuation of Australian Antarctic Division
routine — familiar, but not yet a genuine Tepenian civic institution in its own right. **But** a
station can't function as a real city while still organizing itself around a foreign government's old
operational chain of command. **Therefore** Founding Administrator Warrick Zhao steers the
community through a deliberate transition — Australian Antarctic Division habits reshaped into
something that actually belongs to the people living there now. **But** doing that means letting go of
some of the founding identity's own institutional authority. **Therefore** his own choice sets a
lasting precedent: Mawson's hub function, not any single national identity, becomes what the city
actually organizes itself around.

## 3. Mei-Ling's Idea

Mawson's genuine warmth and ease of making friends is already a lived civic trait, but nobody's yet
built anything deliberate out of it. **But** a hub city with real hospitality instincts and no
particular use for them is leaving something on the table. **Therefore** Hostess Mei-Ling Sorensen
proposes something specific — cultivating Mawson as an actual destination, not just a place people
pass through on hub business. **But** turning an informal civic trait into deliberate infrastructure
takes real investment nobody's certain will pay off. **Therefore** the earliest dedicated hospitality
infrastructure gets built anyway, on her insistence, becoming the foundation the city's later
honeymoon-destination reputation grows out of decades on.

## 4. The Overlook

As Mawson's hospitality reputation grows, newly-married couples start arriving with no fixed
itinerary, unsure what to actually do with a week or two at the edge of the continent. **But** a
destination reputation needs something concrete for visitors to actually experience together, not just
a pleasant place to stay. **Therefore** a customary visit to the Prince Charles Mountains overlook
above Horseshoe Harbour takes hold — timed to whichever extreme, polar night or midnight sun, happens
to be in season during a given stay. **But** couples arrive at wildly different times of year, meaning
no two visits see the same sky. **Therefore** the overlook becomes the honeymoon's defining shared
memory precisely because it's never quite the same experience twice — each couple gets a version of it
nobody else's honeymoon shared.

## 5. A Name Kept, A Territory Remembered

Mawson's exile community inherits both the real explorer's name and the historical Australian
Antarctic Territory claim built around this stretch of coast. **But** the demographic population
actually living under that name and that claim shifts, generation by generation, toward something
Australia never anticipated. **Therefore** St. Douglas veneration and the old Territory claim could
easily have faded as irrelevant once the founding population's numbers thinned. **But** the city
chooses instead to keep both alive deliberately, as honored civic history rather than living political
authority. **Therefore** Mawson ends up carrying its founding legacy forward openly, on its own terms,
rather than letting demographic change quietly erase it.

## 6. Whoever Arrives, Warmly

Immigration currents bring genuinely diverse populations to Mawson — Chinese, Japanese, South Korean,
Indonesian, alongside the founding Australian wave. **But** a city this demographically layered could
easily default to polite coexistence without any real warmth behind it. **Therefore** civic leadership
treats hospitality itself as something worth actively cultivating, not something that simply follows
from having lots of different people in one place. **But** building a genuine culture of welcome takes
sustained effort across generations, not one policy or one leader's initiative. **Therefore** Mawson's
famous ease of making friends becomes a real, earned civic achievement — deliberately built alongside
its demographic plurality, not an automatic byproduct of it.

## 7. The Hub That Chose Kindness

Mawson's position on Hwy 4, between the Sayowa Junction and Sinheung, makes it the Mawson subnet's
practical coordination center almost automatically. **But** a city that busy, with that much genuine
operational responsibility, could easily let its hub function crowd out everything else about its
civic character. **Therefore** Mawson's leadership deliberately protects space for the hospitality
culture growing up alongside the logistics work — resort warmth and hub function developed as two
equally real civic priorities, not one subordinated to the other. **But** balancing both means neither
one ever gets the city's undivided attention. **Therefore** Mawson becomes genuinely good at both
without being defined entirely by either — a city its own residents describe by what it does for
people, not simply by what moves through it.

## 8. Two Kinds of Old

Mawson's founding-era legitimacy — the oldest continuous occupation in its subnet — is already
centuries deep by the time its honeymoon reputation starts forming. **But** the honeymoon reputation is
something genuinely new, built deliberately within living memory rather than inherited from the
founding at all. **Therefore** Mawson ends up holding two entirely different kinds of civic prestige at
once — an ancient, inherited kind tied to St. Douglas and continuity, and a modern, actively earned
kind tied to hospitality and warmth. **But** residents could easily end up treating one as more
legitimate than the other. **Therefore** the city's own established lore never picks a winner between
them — both are simply true, operating on different timescales, neither one crowding out the other's
claim to matter.

## 9. The Name That Outlasted the Founders

Australia founds Mawson and sets its whole operational and cultural tone. **But** later immigration
waves eventually make China the demographic Primary, well ahead of the founding-wave Australian
presence. **Therefore** the gap between who founded the city and who actually lives there now becomes
impossible to ignore forever. **But** rather than suppressing that gap or treating it as an
unresolved wound, Mawson's civic culture makes real, open peace with it — the founding legacy honored
as history, the present population's own character embraced as exactly what the city has become.
**Therefore** "The Name That Outlasted the Founders" becomes something residents say with genuine
warmth rather than discomfort — a fact about the city they've settled, not a tension they're still
working through.

## 10. What the Guests Never See

Honeymooning couples arrive at Mawson experiencing warmth, ease, and a genuinely relaxing stay. **But**
underneath that welcoming surface, the city is running real, continuous freight and communications
coordination for the entire Mawson subnet — dispatch, routing, and Arcanet traffic that never stops
just because visitors are here to enjoy themselves. **Therefore** Mawson's hospitality workers and its
hub-logistics workers end up operating, in practice, as two barely-overlapping halves of the same
city, each rarely thinking much about the other's daily work. **But** both halves depend on the same
civic reputation to keep functioning — a hub nobody trusts, or a resort nobody visits, would each
undermine the other eventually. **Therefore** Mawson's two identities stay quietly, permanently
interdependent, even though most visitors only ever see the half built to welcome them.

## 11. The Part Nobody Points To

Someone researching Mawson's earliest exile-era records finds that the real transition from Australian
Antarctic Division routine to full civic life was genuinely harder than the smooth, celebratory version
everyone now tells. **But** a civic archivist wanting the harder history genuinely acknowledged meets
real concern from someone in the hospitality industry, worried about complicating a warm reputation
decades in the making. **Therefore** a real disagreement opens over how, or whether, to tell the fuller
story. **But** presenting only the smooth version does a disservice to what the earliest generation
actually lived through. **Therefore** a middle path holds — the harder history acknowledged honestly,
available to anyone who looks, without displacing the warmth that's genuinely, currently true. Mawson's
civic self-understanding gains a third layer few outsiders ever notice.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#3 and #4** work as a pair — the deliberate civic decision to cultivate a destination reputation, and
the concrete tradition that decision eventually produced, giving Mawson's honeymoon-destination status
an actual origin rather than treating it as always having simply been true. **#9** is the most
directly load-bearing thread of the ten, since it dramatizes the Megasheet's own central theme
("The Name That Outlasted the Founders") as a lived civic choice rather than a passive demographic
fact — deliberately leaving open, per the Megasheet's own note, whether any further correction to
Mawson's founding-nation demographic balance is ever made. **#11** answers the Mega_Init's own Jeju-
parallel question directly, giving the city's warmth genuine depth without contradicting #9's separate,
openly-settled demographic peace.
