## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Majyao's teahouse tradition, having grown into something genuinely famous throughout Tepenia
(Suggestion #2), still starts as something specifically hers, tied to her own establishment and her own
founding-era Korean heritage — other proprietors admire it, but none have yet claimed it as their own.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The practice of gathering somewhere warm and welcoming resonates with residents who have no connection to
that heritage at all — and as other proprietors across the growing city start opening their own
tea-and-hospitality spaces modeled on Majyao's example, a real question surfaces about whether they're
honoring her tradition or diluting it: does a practice this closely tied to one founder's heritage stay
meaningfully "hers" once it belongs to everyone?

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between a proprietor from Majyao's own
community, protective of the teahouse tradition's specifically Korean roots and uneasy watching it spread
detached from that heritage, and a proprietor from an entirely different background, genuinely
enthusiastic about adopting the practice and seeing no reason its origin should limit who gets to carry
it forward. This mirrors, at a smaller and gentler scale, the same tension Suggestion #5 traces for a
newer arriving practice — but here it's playing out over an already-beloved, already-citywide-admired
tradition rather than something newly arrived and unfamiliar, which raises the emotional stakes.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a proprietor or
community figure with direct ties to Majyao's own founding-era Korean population, invested in the
tradition's specific origin being remembered. Character B is plausibly a proprietor from any of Janbogo's
other founding communities, genuinely inspired by the teahouse model and eager to open their own version
of it.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Majyao herself, consulted informally, settles the matter simply by declining to gatekeep it — she
welcomes the spread rather than resisting it, on the grounds that a tradition about making people feel
at home shouldn't restrict who gets to offer that warmth to others, and her own endorsement takes the
wind out of any argument for keeping the practice exclusive.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Teahouse culture spreads well beyond Majyao's own walls, adopted and reinterpreted by proprietors from
every background the city's growing population contains — no longer one founder's establishment, but a
whole citywide practice with dozens of independent, differently-flavored versions.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the spread happened with Majyao's own visible blessing rather than through quiet, contested
imitation, the tradition's Korean origin gets openly and gratefully remembered even as its ethnic
specificity fades from most practitioners' daily awareness — residents can name where the custom came
from even while practicing a version that's stopped being recognizably "Korean" to most of the people now
carrying it forward.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

By the time the custom is genuinely citywide, it belongs to Janbogo itself rather than to any one founding
community — the same absorption pattern the city's whole culture keeps repeating, this time carried out
in the open, with the tradition's own founder having actively chosen to let it go.
