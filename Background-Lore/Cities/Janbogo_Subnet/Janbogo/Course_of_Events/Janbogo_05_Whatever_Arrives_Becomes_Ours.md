## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

A cultural practice arrives with one of Janbogo's later immigration waves — distinct, recognizably
foreign, tied to wherever it came from, practiced by its originating community much the way the founding
Chinese and French aesthetic traditions once were before they fused (Suggestion #4).

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The city's whole daily rhythm runs through shared spaces — the halls, the teahouses — where practices
from different origins constantly rub against each other, and this new practice is no exception: residents
with no connection to its origin start encountering it simply by sharing the same rooms, and some of them
start participating, adapting it in ways the originating community didn't necessarily anticipate or
initially welcome.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between a member of the practice's
originating community, protective of the tradition's authenticity and uneasy watching it get
reinterpreted by people with no connection to its meaning, and a resident from outside that community who
has genuinely taken up the practice and adapted it, seeing nothing wrong with a living tradition changing
hands. This shouldn't be framed as appropriation-versus-innocence — the originating community's protectiveness
is legitimate, especially this early, when the practice hasn't yet had generations to naturally diffuse
the way the teahouse tradition eventually did; the adapting resident's enthusiasm is equally genuine, not
careless disrespect.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a recent immigrant or
first-generation resident from the practice's originating community, still closely attached to its
original meaning and context. Character B is plausibly a longer-settled Janbogo resident from an entirely
different background, someone whose whole civic experience has already been shaped by the city's
established habit of adopting whatever reaches it.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

No formal resolution ever gets reached — the originating-community resident's concerns are heard but
never translate into any actual restriction, and the practice keeps spreading regardless, adopted,
adapted, and retold by residents with no connection to its origin at all, the same absorption pattern the
teahouse tradition and the aesthetic fusion both already went through.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The practice stops being recognizably tied to any one community and becomes, within a generation,
something residents across the city simply consider Janbogo's own — repeating the identical pattern the
Megasheet's own established civic character already predicts, now visible as a repeatable process rather
than a single historical fact.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because this is now the third or fourth time residents can point to the exact same absorption pattern
playing out — the teahouse tradition, the aesthetic fusion, and now this — the pattern itself becomes
something Janbogo residents consciously name and expect, rather than something that just happens to keep
occurring without anyone remarking on it; newcomers start being told, half-jokingly, that whatever they
bring with them will eventually stop being purely theirs.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Janbogo develops a genuine reputation, across the whole subnet, as the place where anything new
eventually just becomes Janbogo's own — no longer an unnoticed pattern but a named, expected civic trait,
repeated often enough that residents themselves now anticipate it every time something new arrives.
