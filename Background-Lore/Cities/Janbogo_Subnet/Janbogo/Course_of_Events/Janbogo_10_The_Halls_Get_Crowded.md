## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The original shielded commercial halls, built for a smaller population (Suggestion #3), have served
Janbogo well for years — but the city's role as subnet hub keeps drawing more residents and travelers,
and the halls, however soundly built, were never designed to absorb this much continued growth.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Real crowding starts straining the halls' daily function — foot traffic bottlenecks, overtaxed heating
and ventilation systems, and a growing sense among residents that the civic spaces they depend on
completely are becoming less pleasant, even less safe, to actually use. Expanding structures this large
and this integral to daily life risks disrupting the very civic rhythm they're meant to support, and
different stakeholders disagree sharply about how urgently, and how disruptively, to respond.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between a civic planner pushing for fast,
comprehensive expansion — arguing that the crowding is already a real safety problem that delay only
worsens — and a hall proprietor or resident advocate warning that fast, disruptive construction risks
shutting down the very spaces the population depends on completely, trading a slow-building crowding
problem for an acute, immediate one. The planner's urgency is legitimate: crowding this severe is a
genuine hazard, not just an inconvenience. The proprietor's caution is equally legitimate: a population
with nowhere else to gather can't simply tolerate their only civic space going dark for extended
construction.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic infrastructure
planner, perhaps working in the same tradition established by Architect Wu Lian-Marchetti's original hall
design (Suggestion #3), pushing for a fast, thorough response. Character B is plausibly a hall-based
proprietor or an informal resident representative, someone whose livelihood or daily life depends on the
halls staying functional throughout any renovation.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The proprietor's caution shapes the final approach: planners commit to a careful, staged expansion,
adding hall space in sections without ever fully shutting down what's already working, trading the
planner's preferred speed for the proprietor's preferred continuity.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Even careful, staged expansion still means years of genuine inconvenience for a population that depends
on the halls completely — construction noise, partial closures, and rerouted daily traffic become an
ordinary, extended feature of civic life throughout the whole expansion period.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the inconvenience was sustained and collectively endured over years rather than resolved quickly,
it becomes a genuine, shared civic memory in its own right — residents who lived through the expansion
develop a real, lasting sense of having personally weathered something together, giving the halls an
added layer of emotional significance beyond their practical function, earned specifically through the
shared difficulty of the renovation itself.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Janbogo's expanded halls now serve a much larger population comfortably — but the expansion itself
became a real, lived civic event residents remember collectively, proof, tested directly, of exactly how
central the halls had already become to Janbogo's whole way of life.
