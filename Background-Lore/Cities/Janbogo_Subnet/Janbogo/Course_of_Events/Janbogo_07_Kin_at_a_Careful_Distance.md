## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Janbogo and Sejong both trace back to Korean founding populations — Unified Korea and South Korea
specifically, related but genuinely distinct national origins — with only occasional, ceremonial contact
between the two communities so far, neither city having ever seriously considered what a closer
relationship might look like.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Geography and separate political history mean the two cities never grow up in each other's daily life —
but a specific occasion (a ceremonial visit, an anniversary, a shared observance) brings representatives
of both cities into direct, sustained contact for once, long enough that the question of whether this
relationship should stay occasional or become something deeper actually gets raised out loud, by someone
on one side or the other, rather than simply left unexamined.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a respectful but genuine disagreement between a
Janbogo delegate who wants to formalize and deepen ties with Sejong — proposing regular exchange,
scheduled visits, something closer to real ongoing kinship — and a Sejong counterpart who values the
warmth of the existing occasional contact but is wary of formalizing it, worried that manufactured
closeness between two cities with genuinely separate histories and separate day-to-day concerns would feel
performative rather than authentic. Neither character is rejecting the other's people; the disagreement is
about whether a shared ancestral thread obligates deeper involvement, or whether real affection can
coexist with real distance.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Janbogo civic figure
with a personal or family interest in Korean heritage, drawn to the idea of Sejong as more than distant
kin. Character B is plausibly a Sejong counterpart, equally invested in the shared heritage but more
attached to the practical, separate character each city has independently built for itself.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Sejong's counterpart's caution prevails — no formal exchange program or deepened institutional
relationship ever gets established, but the disagreement itself, aired honestly rather than left
unspoken, produces a clearer, mutually understood agreement: the relationship stays warm, occasional, and
voluntary, deliberately, rather than by simple neglect.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Contact between Janbogo and Sejong continues at the same limited but real level as before — occasional
ceremonial visits, warm but infrequent — except now understood by both sides as a deliberate choice
rather than an unexamined default, which changes how the relationship *feels* even though it doesn't
change how often it actually happens.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the distance is now a named, mutually agreed choice rather than an accident of circumstance,
neither city ever revisits the question again with any real urgency — the settled clarity itself removes
the recurring low-grade uncertainty that might otherwise have prompted the same debate to resurface every
generation, letting the relationship simply continue on its established terms indefinitely.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Janbogo and Sejong remain permanently, comfortably distant kin — acknowledged, valued, never quite close,
their distance now a settled, deliberate understanding rather than an unexamined default neither city had
ever actually discussed.
