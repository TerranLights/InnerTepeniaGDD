## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

A small teahouse opens downtown, run by a proprietor named Majyao Bisyugota, offering nothing more
ambitious than a warm room and good tea — a modest, personal venture with no particular plan to become
anything more than a livelihood.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Janbogo's whole climate makes a genuinely warm, welcoming indoor space rare and valuable, and word
spreads fast — travelers passing through the subnet's growing hub start seeking Majyao's place out
deliberately, not just stumbling into it. The sudden demand outpaces what a single small establishment,
built and staffed for a modest local clientele, was ever designed to handle, forcing a real decision about
whether and how to grow.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** an internal, personal conflict given external shape
— between Majyao herself, weighing whether to expand the teahouse to meet growing demand, and a
business-minded associate or family member pushing her toward exactly that: more seating, more staff, a
bigger space, treating the sudden fame as an opportunity too valuable to leave on the table. Majyao's
hesitation isn't false modesty; she has genuine reason to worry that scaling up would mean losing the
personal quality that made travelers seek the place out in the first place. The associate's push isn't
greed; growth genuinely could serve more travelers who need exactly what she's offering.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly Majyao Bisyugota
herself, the established proprietor, whose instincts about her own establishment carry real weight.
Character B is plausibly a business partner, a family member helping run the growing operation, or an
outside investor drawn by the teahouse's sudden reputation, genuinely well-meaning but focused on scale
rather than what actually made the place work.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Majyao's instinct wins, but not through simple refusal — she agrees to a careful, limited expansion,
enough additional space and staff to meet real demand without losing the personal attention that defined
the place, rejecting the associate's push for anything resembling a large-scale commercial operation.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

A single small establishment grows into something genuinely famous throughout Tepenia — the single most
visible cultural export a city this practically-minded ever produces — while still recognizably operating
on the same personal, intimate scale that first drew travelers to seek it out deliberately.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Majyao's teahouse became famous while staying small and personal rather than industrializing,
other proprietors across Janbogo take the model itself as the lesson, not just the tea — a citywide
teahouse culture starts forming around the same principle of warmth over scale, other establishments
opening in Majyao's image well before her own tradition ever formally spreads beyond her walls
(Suggestion #9).

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Majyao's teahouse stands as Janbogo's founding cultural landmark — famous, personal, deliberately never
scaled past what one proprietor could still genuinely oversee — already quietly shaping how other
proprietors across the city think about hospitality, long before the tradition becomes explicitly
citywide.
