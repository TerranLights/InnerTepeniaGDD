## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Janbogo's own founding population never sets out to build the subnet's central city — just a working
settlement on Jang Bogo Station's inherited infrastructure, with the polynya's maritime advantage already
quietly accumulating outside traffic (Suggestion #1), but no one yet treating that traffic as a deliberate
civic project.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The polynya's maritime access and the highway network's own geography keep routing more and more subnet
traffic through Janbogo by simple necessity — administrative requests, supply coordination, communications
relay work all start accumulating here, informally, because it's simply the most practical place for them
to happen. Eventually, the volume of ad hoc responsibility Janbogo is absorbing without any of it being
planned reaches a point where someone has to decide whether to keep absorbing it informally or start
treating it as an actual civic mandate.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between a Janbogo civic figure who wants
to formally embrace and organize the hub role the city has been informally accumulating — building
dedicated administrative and coordination infrastructure to match the responsibility already landing here
— and a figure wary of formalizing it, worried that officially declaring Janbogo the subnet's hub would
invite resentment from other cities who never voted for that arrangement, or obligations Janbogo never
actually agreed to take on. Both are responding to the same accumulated fact differently: one sees an
opportunity to finally match capacity to responsibility, the other sees the risk of turning quiet,
tolerated informality into contested, resented authority.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an administrative or
logistics figure already doing much of the unofficial coordination work by hand, frustrated at doing it
without proper support. Character B is plausibly a more politically cautious civic leader, aware of how
Fort McMurdo's own informal-influence resentment played out elsewhere in the Federation and wary of
Janbogo walking the same path.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A middle path wins: Janbogo builds out real administrative, supply, and communications capacity to match
what it's already absorbing, but deliberately avoids ever formally declaring itself the subnet's hub —
functional infrastructure without a formal title, letting the role keep growing through accumulated
practical necessity rather than an announced civic ambition.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Administrative, supply, and communications functions accumulate here gradually, one practical decision at
a time, rather than through any single deliberate plan to make Janbogo the hub — the city's capacity keeps
growing to match demand, but always framed internally as simply meeting need, never as claiming status.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the growth was never framed as a claim to status, enough accumulated function eventually looks,
from outside, exactly like deliberate design anyway — other subnet cities start treating Janbogo as the de
facto hub in practice, an outcome the cautious civic figure's own strategy inadvertently produced by
avoiding the label, rather than the formal declaration the strategy was originally meant to prevent.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

By the time anyone thinks to ask how Janbogo became the Janbogo subnet's own namesake hub, the honest
answer is that nobody ever really decided it — it just kept happening, one practical decision at a time,
until the informal reality and the outside perception finally matched.
