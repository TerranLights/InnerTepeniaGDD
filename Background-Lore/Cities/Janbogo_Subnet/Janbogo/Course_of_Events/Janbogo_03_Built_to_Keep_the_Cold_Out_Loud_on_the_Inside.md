## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Katabatic winds off the East Antarctic interior make outdoor life here a near-permanent challenge, not
an occasional storm to wait out — and Janbogo's growing population, still relying on smaller, individually
heated buildings like Majyao's teahouse, has no way for large numbers of people to gather together
indoors at once.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A growing population can't simply live indoors in isolation from each other, scattered across individual
small heated buildings the way the city's earliest years managed — but building something large enough
to function as genuine shared civic space means committing the young city to a structure on a scale
nothing in Janbogo has attempted before, with all the cost and risk that scale implies against a
population still establishing itself.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between an ambitious architect pushing
for a small number of vast, genuinely civic-scale shielded halls — betting the city's whole daily civic
life on a handful of protected megastructures — and a more conservative civic planner favoring several
smaller, more modest heated structures spread across the growing settlement, safer to build incrementally
and less catastrophic individually if one fails. The architect's ambition carries real risk: committing to
a small number of massive structures means the city's whole civic life depends on very few points of
potential failure. The planner's caution carries a real cost too: several smaller structures could never
match the sense of shared communal space the architect is actually trying to create.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly the figure later
remembered as, or a direct predecessor to, Architect Wu Lian-Marchetti, credited with designing the
shielded commercial halls. Character B is plausibly a civic planner or resource administrator, responsible
for a still-young settlement's risk tolerance, wary of betting too much civic infrastructure on
unprecedented scale.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The architect's vision prevails — vast, shielded commercial halls, bright and bustling inside, get built
as genuine community centers rather than mere shopping arcades — but built in a small number of
independently structurally sound halls rather than one single megastructure, the planner's caution
surviving as redundancy: if one hall ever needs to close, the city's civic life doesn't collapse with it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The halls become Janbogo's real center of gravity, the place daily life actually happens while the
outside stays dark and nearly empty — several distinct halls each serving as a genuine hub in their own
right, rather than one single overwhelming civic center.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because multiple distinct halls exist rather than one, different halls start developing slightly
different characters over time, shaped by whichever neighborhoods and populations cluster around each one
— an unplanned civic texture that gives Janbogo internal variety no single mega-structure could have
produced, each hall becoming its own recognizable piece of the city's identity rather than one
undifferentiated center.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Janbogo's daily civic life now runs through several distinct, structurally independent shielded halls,
each with its own developing character — vast, warm, bustling spaces the whole city depends on, built with
enough redundancy that no single structure's failure could threaten the city's entire way of life.
