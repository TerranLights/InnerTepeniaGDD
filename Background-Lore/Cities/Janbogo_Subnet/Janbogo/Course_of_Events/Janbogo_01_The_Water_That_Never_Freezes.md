## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The Terra Nova Bay polynya keeps a stretch of coastal water ice-free year-round, a genuine advantage no
other Ross Sea coastal city can match — but in the settlement's earliest years, that advantage is simply
a known fact, not yet something the young city has organized its life around.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A maritime advantage only matters if a city actually builds itself around exploiting it — and turning
the polynya from a passive fact into the settlement's central economic asset means committing real, scarce
early-settlement labor and capital to shipping infrastructure, docks, and supply coordination, at the
direct expense of other founding priorities the young city also genuinely needs. Not every founding
figure agrees that betting so heavily on one geographic feature, however unique, is the wisest use of a
struggling settlement's limited resources.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a resource-priority conflict between a founding
figure who wants to commit the settlement's early labor and capital heavily toward maritime
infrastructure — docks, shipping coordination, supply handling — to exploit the polynya while the
advantage is still uncontested, and a figure who wants a more balanced, diversified early investment,
wary of over-committing a young settlement to one geographic bet before its long-term value is proven.
Both are being reasonable: the first sees a rare, genuine strategic opening that won't wait; the second
sees the ordinary risk of any young settlement betting too much on one undiversified plan.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an early
maritime-minded settler or logistics figure, someone who immediately recognized the polynya's rarity and
wants to move fast to claim the advantage before circumstance or competition catches up. Character B is
plausibly a more cautious settlement administrator, responsible for balancing the founding population's
overall survival needs against any single, however promising, specialization.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The maritime advocate's position wins, but the caution isn't wasted — the settlement commits to building
its whole civic and economic life around the open water, shipping and supply coordination becoming the
central founding project, while the administrator's insistence on balance shapes *how* it's built:
maritime infrastructure development paced so it never fully starves the settlement's other basic needs.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Janbogo grows its whole civic and economic life around the polynya's open water — year-round access
nobody else on this coast has, developed steadily rather than all at once, giving the settlement a real,
functioning maritime advantage without the boom-and-bust instability an all-at-once commitment might have
produced.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Janbogo's maritime capability was built steadily rather than in one uncoordinated rush, it ends
up reliable in a way a faster, more improvised buildout might not have been — reliable enough that every
other Ross Sea coastal city's own supply chain eventually starts routing through Janbogo whether it plans
to or not, since Janbogo is simply the one node on this stretch of coast that consistently works.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The polynya alone is enough to make Janbogo the subnet's practical hub, decades before anyone formally
names it that — a steady, deliberately-paced maritime buildout having turned a geographic accident into
the coast's single most dependable node.
