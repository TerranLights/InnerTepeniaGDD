# Janbogo — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material (Janbogo's later role as Zukelli's surviving witness is out of scope here). 10 candidate
But/Therefore chains built from Janbogo's Megasheet (the Terra Nova Bay polynya, the teahouse
tradition, the shielded commercial halls, the city's habit of absorbing whatever reaches it).
Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. The Water That Never Freezes

The Terra Nova Bay polynya keeps a stretch of coastal water ice-free year-round, a genuine advantage
no other Ross Sea coastal city can match. **But** a maritime advantage only matters if a city actually
builds itself around exploiting it. **Therefore** Janbogo grows its whole civic and economic life
around that open water — shipping, supply coordination, year-round access nobody else on this coast
has. **But** being the one reliable access point means every other city's own supply chain eventually
starts routing through Janbogo whether it plans to or not. **Therefore** the polynya alone is enough
to make Janbogo the subnet's practical hub, decades before anyone formally names it that.

## 2. Majyao's Kettle

A small teahouse opens downtown, run by a proprietor named Majyao Bisyugota, offering nothing more
ambitious than a warm room and good tea. **But** Janbogo's whole climate makes a genuinely warm,
welcoming indoor space rare and valuable. **Therefore** word spreads fast — travelers passing through
the subnet's growing hub start seeking the place out deliberately, not just stumbling into it.
**But** a single small establishment can't easily absorb that kind of reputation without changing.
**Therefore** Majyao's teahouse grows into something genuinely famous throughout Tepenia, the single
most visible cultural export a city this practically-minded ever produces.

## 3. Built to Keep the Cold Out, Loud on the Inside

Katabatic winds off the East Antarctic interior make outdoor life here a near-permanent challenge, not
an occasional storm to wait out. **But** a growing population can't simply live indoors in isolation
from each other. **Therefore** architect Wu Lian-Marchetti designs something larger than any single
building — vast, shielded commercial halls, bright and bustling inside, functioning as genuine
community centers rather than mere shopping arcades. **But** committing to structures that large means
betting the city's whole civic life on a small number of protected spaces. **Therefore** the halls
become Janbogo's real center of gravity, the place daily life actually happens while the outside stays
dark and nearly empty.

## 4. Two Traditions, One Style

Janbogo's growing population brings genuinely distinct aesthetic sensibilities with it — Chinese and
French design habits arrive side by side, neither one dominant enough to simply replace the other.
**But** designing for indoor warmth against a climate this severe means the city needs a real, unified
visual identity, not two competing ones. **Therefore** the two traditions fuse, independently, into
something new — richly colored streets and interiors that belong to neither tradition alone.
**But** nobody plans this fusion deliberately; it just keeps happening, building by building.
**Therefore** Janbogo ends up with a genuinely original aesthetic identity, grown rather than designed,
that no other Tepenian city quite replicates.

## 5. Whatever Arrives Becomes Ours

A cultural practice arrives with one of Janbogo's later immigration waves — distinct, recognizably
foreign, tied to wherever it came from. **But** the city's whole daily rhythm runs through shared
spaces (the halls, the teahouses) where practices from different origins constantly rub against each
other. **Therefore** the practice doesn't stay separate for long — it gets adopted, adapted, and
retold by residents with no connection to its origin at all, the same way the teahouse tradition and
the fashion fusion both did. **But** repeating that pattern enough times means nothing arriving in
Janbogo ever stays purely foreign for very long. **Therefore** the city develops a genuine reputation,
across the whole subnet, as the place where anything new eventually just becomes Janbogo's own.

## 6. Han Soo-jin's Warning System

Katabatic winds arrive with real, dangerous force and little obvious warning to residents caught
outside. **But** a growing population moving between the shielded halls and their homes can't simply
avoid the outdoors entirely. **Therefore** meteorologist Han Soo-jin formalizes a real
forecasting and warning system, giving residents genuine advance notice before the worst gusts arrive.
**But** building a system reliable enough to actually trust takes years of careful, unglamorous data
work before anyone's willing to depend on it. **Therefore** her warning system becomes one of
Janbogo's quiet, load-bearing achievements — the specific reason outdoor katabatic fatalities stay
rare in a city that could otherwise expect them routinely.

## 7. Kin at a Careful Distance

Janbogo and Sejong both trace back to Korean founding populations — Unified Korea and South Korea
specifically, related but genuinely distinct national origins. **But** geography and separate
political history mean the two cities never grow up in each other's daily life. **Therefore** contact
between them stays limited but real — occasional ceremonial visits, warm but infrequent, the kind of
relationship a diaspora with a shared ancestral thread might keep without deep ongoing involvement.
**But** neither city ever pushes to deepen that relationship further. **Therefore** Janbogo and Sejong
remain permanently, comfortably distant kin — acknowledged, valued, never quite close.

## 8. The Hub Nobody Planned

Janbogo's own founding population never sets out to build the subnet's central city — just a working
settlement on Jang Bogo Station's inherited infrastructure. **But** the polynya's maritime access and
the highway network's own geography keep routing more and more subnet traffic through Janbogo by
simple necessity. **Therefore** administrative, supply, and communications functions accumulate here
gradually, one practical decision at a time, rather than through any single deliberate plan to make
Janbogo the hub. **But** enough accumulated function eventually looks, from outside, exactly like
deliberate design. **Therefore** by the time anyone thinks to ask how Janbogo became the Janbogo
subnet's own namesake hub, the honest answer is that nobody ever really decided it — it just kept
happening.

## 9. A City That Never Says No

Majyao's teahouse tradition starts as something specifically hers, tied to her own establishment and
her own founding-era Korean heritage. **But** the practice of gathering somewhere warm and welcoming
resonates with residents who have no connection to that heritage at all. **Therefore** teahouse
culture spreads well beyond Majyao's own walls, adopted and reinterpreted by proprietors from every
background the city's growing population contains. **But** that spread means the tradition stops
being recognizably "Korean" to most of the people now practicing it. **Therefore** by the time the
custom is genuinely citywide, it belongs to Janbogo itself rather than to any one founding community —
the same absorption pattern the city's whole culture keeps repeating.

## 10. The Halls Get Crowded

The original shielded commercial halls, built for a smaller population, start straining under real
crowding as Janbogo's role as subnet hub keeps drawing more residents and travelers. **But** expanding
structures this large and this integral to daily life risks disrupting the very civic rhythm they're
meant to support. **Therefore** planners commit to a careful, staged expansion, adding hall space
without ever fully shutting down what's already working. **But** even careful expansion means years of
genuine inconvenience for a population that depends on the halls completely. **Therefore** the
expansion becomes a real, lived civic event residents remember collectively — proof, tested directly,
of exactly how central the halls had already become to Janbogo's whole way of life.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#1 and #8** work as a pair — the polynya's concrete strategic advantage, and the gradual, undesigned
accumulation of hub function it produced, together explaining Janbogo's centrality without needing any
single deliberate founding decision. **#2, #5, and #9** trace the same "absorbs and re-originates"
civic trait (the Megasheet's own Cross-Reference Finding 2) across three different scales — one
teahouse, one general pattern, one full citywide adoption — giving that trait a concrete developmental
arc rather than stating it as a fixed fact.
