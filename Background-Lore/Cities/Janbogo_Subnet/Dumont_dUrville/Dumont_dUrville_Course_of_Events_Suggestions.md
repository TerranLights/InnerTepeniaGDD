# Dumont d'Urville — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Dumont d'Urville's Megasheet (the francophone
island city, its negotiated coexistence with the Adélie rookery, its downtown music-and-eatery
culture). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. A Language That Never Needed Defending

French exiles found Dumont d'Urville's earliest civic institutions in French — the natural
administrative language of the people setting them up. **But** later, larger waves of Chinese and
American arrivals come for the practical opportunities of a coastal post, with no personal attachment
to St. Jules or Adélie Land at all. **Therefore** French could easily have faded once its founding
population became a demographic minority. **But** because civic life itself keeps running in French
day to day, every new arrival picks up functional French simply by participating in ordinary life, no
formal preservation effort required. **Therefore** the language survives as a living civic default,
carried forward by people with no ancestral stake in it whatsoever.

## 2. Founded in the Middle of Someone Else's Home

Early settlers arrive at Petrel Island already home to one of Antarctica's largest Adélie breeding
grounds — a population no arriving community could displace even if it tried. **But** building a
functioning settlement means needing space the rookery already occupies. **Therefore** the earliest
civic decisions aren't about clearing ground, they're about negotiating it — where structures can go,
where the colonies stay undisturbed, which paths are actually theirs to build. **But** that
negotiation has to be renewed every breeding season, never settled once and forgotten. **Therefore**
Dumont d'Urville grows up treating coexistence as an ongoing relationship rather than a founding
problem solved and closed.

## 3. New Orleans at 1/20th Scale

Dumont d'Urville's downtown core is small, dense, and expensive by necessity — there's only so much
buildable ground on the island. **But** a population living somewhere this physically demanding needs
real reasons to stay rather than simply endure it. **Therefore** live music becomes an ordinary,
expected feature of almost every eatery downtown, not a special occasion but daily civic texture —
small, packed, informal venues rather than one dedicated performance industry. **But** sustaining that
kind of culture at genuine scale, night after night, takes real commitment from a small population.
**Therefore** it becomes something residents actively protect rather than a pleasant accident — the
specific thing that makes staying somewhere this demanding worth it.

## 4. Heat as a Statement

Wind here is more severe than almost anywhere else documented in Tepenia, and keeping buildings
livable takes real engineering effort. **But** downtown residents want more than bare survival — they
want the core of the city to actually feel alive, not just insulated. **Therefore** heating units run
visibly between buildings, not hidden inside them, turning basic infrastructure into something closer
to a public display of vitality. **But** that visible warmth costs more than the minimum needed to
simply keep people from freezing. **Therefore** Dumont d'Urville's downtown ends up defined by
warmth as a deliberate outward statement — proof the city is thriving, not just surviving, right where
anyone passing through can see it.

## 5. The Bridge Nobody Could Agree On

As the island's population grows, connecting reliably to the mainland becomes a real civic question.
**But** every proposed solution runs into a genuine engineering problem — a permanent structure faces
real aerodynamic flutter risk and pack ice pier loads; a seasonal crossing means real unreliability
part of the year. **Therefore** the debate over what to actually build drags on, generation after
generation, with genuine engineering brainstorms proposed and shelved rather than one design ever
winning outright. **But** the island still needs some way across regardless of the debate's outcome.
**Therefore** Dumont d'Urville simply keeps adapting — ice roads some years, boats in others — never
fully resolving the question, treating "how do we cross" as a live, ongoing negotiation rather than a
problem anyone expects to finally close.

## 6. Reading Into the Wind

For years, St. Jules Day is observed quietly, however individual families choose to mark it. **But** a
city this thoroughly shaped by its founder's own landing wants something more shared and deliberate.
**Therefore** a public reading takes hold — surviving fragments of Jules Dumont d'Urville's 1840
expedition journal, read aloud downtown, outdoors, regardless of the wind. **But** holding it outside,
in the exact conditions the observance is honoring, means the reading itself becomes a small trial
every year. **Therefore** that discomfort becomes the whole point — a deliberate act of standing in
the same wind St. Jules once did, not a comfortable ceremony held indoors out of convenience.

## 7. Watching Quietly

Robots in Dumont d'Urville develop a genuine tradition of careful, patient penguin-colony observation
— watching without disturbing, simply attending to the rookery's rhythms. **But** for a long stretch,
this stays a private, individual practice, not something shared. **Therefore** as word spreads of how
many residents already do this quietly on their own, it starts happening in loose company instead of
isolation — people and robots observing together without needing to talk through it. **But** turning
a private habit into something communal risks losing the quiet that made it meaningful in the first
place. **Therefore** the practice stays deliberately unstructured — no formal ritual, no set schedule,
just Dumont d'Urville's closest thing to a shared contemplative practice, kept exactly as informal as
it started.

## 8. Buildable Rock

Petrel Island gets chosen as a settlement site for one plain, unglamorous reason: its rock is solid
enough to build on, unlike so much of the surrounding ice and unstable ground. **But** a site chosen
purely for practicality carries no built-in promise of future significance. **Therefore** Dumont
d'Urville's earliest years pass with little expectation that the settlement will ever be more than a
modest, functional post. **But** modest and functional, sustained long enough, still accumulates real
weight of its own. **Therefore** the city's whole later identity forms without ever needing a grand
founding myth — proof, eventually, that "the ground here happened to hold" is a perfectly sufficient
origin story.

## 9. Everyone Learns French Eventually

New arrivals drawn by practical opportunity rather than any attachment to France initially see no
reason to bother with the city's French administrative habits. **But** every actual civic transaction
— permits, disputes, ordinary daily business — runs through institutions still conducted in French.
**Therefore** newcomers who meant to get by without it find themselves picking up functional French
anyway, not through lessons but through sheer repeated necessity. **But** nobody ever forces the
issue directly. **Therefore** French persistence at Dumont d'Urville ends up self-perpetuating,
proven out generation after generation by new arrivals who show up skeptical and end up fluent anyway.

## 10. The Island Economy of a Small, Alive Place

Dumont d'Urville's population stays genuinely small by Tepenian standards, capped hard by the island's
own limited buildable ground. **But** a small population still needs enough economic and cultural
depth to feel like a real city rather than an outpost. **Therefore** residents lean hard into what the
island can actually sustain — a dense, concentrated downtown doing the work a much larger population
would spread across more space. **But** concentrating everything into one small core means the city
has less room to absorb shocks than a larger, more spread-out place would. **Therefore** Dumont
d'Urville's whole civic character forms around protecting that one small, vital core — understanding,
long before anything ever tests it, that the downtown's health and the city's health are the same
question.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#1 and #9** work as a pair — the mechanism behind French's persistence (Finding 1's own "functional
use, not ceremony" insight) and a concrete generational incident proving it. **#3 and #10** are the
most load-bearing for the city's later identity, since together they establish, well before any crisis
ever tests it, that downtown's music-and-eatery culture is genuinely load-bearing to the whole
island's civic life — not decoration, but the thing residents already understand keeps them here.
