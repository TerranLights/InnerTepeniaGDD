## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Petrel Island gets chosen as a settlement site for one plain, unglamorous reason: its rock is solid
enough to build on, unlike so much of the surrounding ice and unstable ground — a decision made on pure
engineering practicality, with no accompanying narrative about the site's significance.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A site chosen purely for practicality carries no built-in promise of future significance — and as
Dumont d'Urville's earliest years pass with little expectation the settlement will ever be more than a
modest, functional post, some among the founding population start to chafe at that modesty, wanting the
city's origin story to carry more weight than "the ground here happened to hold." The gap between the
site's plain practical reasoning and what some residents want the city to eventually mean becomes a real
point of friction.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between a resident who wants to
retroactively dress up the settlement's founding with a grander narrative — emphasizing St. Jules's own
legendary landing, downplaying the plain engineering logic that actually chose the site — and a resident,
perhaps an original settler or their direct descendant, who insists on keeping the founding story
honest: the site was chosen because the rock held, nothing more mythic than that. This is a conflict
about civic self-image, not facts in dispute — both know what actually happened; they disagree about
whether the plain truth is enough to build a city's identity on.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a younger civic figure
or booster, eager for Dumont d'Urville to have a founding story that competes with other Tepenian cities'
more dramatic origins. Character B is plausibly an original settler or engineer directly involved in the
site-selection decision, for whom plain practicality isn't something to be embarrassed about.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The honest account wins, not through formal decree but through simple persistence — the plain
"the rock held" story keeps getting told the way it actually happened, generation after generation,
while attempts to mythologize the founding never take root, since there's no actual dramatic event to
mythologize beyond an engineering assessment.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Dumont d'Urville's whole later civic identity forms without ever needing a grand founding myth — modest
and functional, sustained long enough, accumulates real weight of its own, and residents stop feeling
the absence of a dramatic origin story as a lack.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the city's identity was never staked on a single grand founding claim, its later civic
achievements — the language that persisted on its own, the music culture that grew out of scarcity, the
negotiated coexistence with the rookery — end up carrying the weight a founding myth would have carried
elsewhere, each one adding to the city's sense of itself independently rather than in service of one
central legend.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Dumont d'Urville's whole later identity rests on an accumulation of modest, unglamorous, genuinely
earned facts rather than any single claim to importance — proof, eventually, that "the ground here
happened to hold" is a perfectly sufficient origin story, needing nothing grander layered on top of it.
