## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

French exiles found Dumont d'Urville's earliest civic institutions in French — the natural
administrative language of the people setting them up, used because it's the only language most of the
founding population actually shares, not out of any deliberate cultural preservation project.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Later, larger waves of Chinese and American arrivals come for the practical opportunities of a coastal
post, with no personal attachment to St. Jules or Adélie Land at all — and as their numbers grow past the
founding population's own, French's future as the city's working language stops being a settled question.
Nothing forces the newcomers to learn it; nothing formally requires civic business to stay in French.
The language could easily fade the moment its founding population becomes a demographic minority, the
same way founding-era customs elsewhere in Tepenia sometimes thin out once their originating community
no longer holds a numerical majority.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a practical, low-heat disagreement between a
founding-population civic administrator who wants to formally codify French as Dumont d'Urville's
required administrative language, and a newer arrival — genuinely uninterested in cultural politics, just
trying to get a permit approved or a dispute settled — who pushes, informally, for civic business to
accommodate whichever language is actually convenient for whoever's asking. This isn't a
preservation-versus-assimilation culture war; the administrator's motive is administrative continuity, not
ethnic gatekeeping, and the newcomer's motive is simple practicality, not disrespect. The tension is over
whether language policy needs to be formally decided at all, or whether it can just keep working
informally.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an early civic clerk
or administrator from the founding French population, someone whose daily work is already conducted in
French and who sees formalizing that as simple continuity rather than exclusion. Character B is plausibly
a newly arrived Chinese or American tradesperson or laborer, present for practical coastal-post
opportunity, whose only interest in the language question is getting ordinary business done without
friction.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

No formal codification ever happens — the administrator's push for an official language requirement
quietly loses momentum, not through defeat but through irrelevance, because ordinary civic transactions
just keep running in French regardless of whether anyone mandates it, since that's simply the language
the existing institutions already operate in.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because civic life itself keeps running in French day to day — with no formal policy either requiring or
forbidding it — every new arrival ends up picking up functional French simply by participating in
ordinary life, permits and disputes and daily transactions doing the teaching no classroom ever needed to.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the language is now carried forward by unplanned, everyday necessity rather than formal policy,
it becomes something no single civic decision could actually threaten — there's no policy to repeal, no
mandate to resent, just an accumulating population of French-speakers with no ancestral stake in the
language at all, which paradoxically makes its position more secure than formal codification ever could
have made it.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

French survives at Dumont d'Urville as a living civic default, carried forward by people with no
ancestral stake in it whatsoever — not preserved deliberately, but reproduced automatically by the same
ordinary participation that first taught it to a skeptical newcomer.
