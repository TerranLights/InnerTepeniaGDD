## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Dumont d'Urville's downtown core is small, dense, and expensive by necessity — there's only so much
buildable ground on the island, and every structure competes for space that could otherwise house more
people or more storage, more of the plain infrastructure a coastal post actually needs.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A population living somewhere this physically demanding needs real reasons to stay rather than simply
endure it — and as the earliest, purely-functional downtown fills in, a genuine disagreement surfaces
over what the island's scarce buildable ground should actually be spent on: more of the practical
infrastructure the settlement clearly needs, or space given over to something as seemingly frivolous as
music venues, when every square meter is this contested.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a resource-allocation conflict between a civic
planner who wants the island's remaining buildable ground reserved for practical infrastructure —
storage, housing, administrative space — and a downtown proprietor or resident advocating for space
given over to small music-and-eatery venues, arguing that a population that only survives has already
lost something worth protecting. The planner isn't wrong that space is genuinely scarce and every square

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The proprietor's side wins a modest but real concession — not a dedicated entertainment district, which
the island's space genuinely can't support, but permission for eateries to fold small, informal
performance space directly into their existing footprint, music sharing the same square meters as food
rather than requiring space of its own.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Live music becomes an ordinary, expected feature of almost every eatery downtown, not a special occasion
but daily civic texture — small, packed, informal venues rather than one dedicated performance industry,
precisely because the compromise never allowed music its own separate space to grow into.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because music was forced to share space with food rather than existing separately, sustaining the culture
at genuine scale, night after night, takes real, continuous commitment from proprietors who could
otherwise use that same floor space more simply — turning the practice into something residents actively
protect rather than a pleasant accident, since letting it lapse would mean giving back space that took
real negotiation to win in the first place.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Dumont d'Urville's downtown is now defined by music and food sharing the same rooms, the same tables — a
compromise born of genuine space scarcity that became, over time, the specific thing that makes staying
somewhere this demanding worth it.
