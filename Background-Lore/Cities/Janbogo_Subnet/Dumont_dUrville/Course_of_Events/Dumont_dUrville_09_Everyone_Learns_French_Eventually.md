## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

New arrivals drawn to Dumont d'Urville by practical opportunity rather than any attachment to France
initially see no reason to bother learning the city's French administrative habits — French is simply
the language the founding institutions happen to run in, nothing they feel obligated to adopt personally.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Every actual civic transaction — permits, disputes, ordinary daily business — runs through institutions
still conducted in French, with no accommodation offered for residents who'd rather conduct business
otherwise. A newly arrived resident, confident they can get by without bothering to learn the language,
runs directly into that wall: a specific, ordinary piece of civic business they need resolved, and no way
to resolve it without engaging with French-speaking institutions on their own terms.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a low-stakes but genuinely frustrating standoff
between a newly arrived resident trying to resolve ordinary civic business — a permit, a property dispute,
a licensing matter — without French, and a civic clerk or administrator who isn't being obstructive on
purpose but simply has no French-free process to offer, since none was ever built. Neither side is the
antagonist: the newcomer's frustration at an unadvertised language barrier is legitimate, and the clerk's
inability to accommodate them isn't malice, just the plain fact that the institution was never designed
around anyone else's convenience.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Chinese or American
newcomer, drawn by the coastal post's practical opportunities exactly as Suggestion #1 describes, with no
prior interest in or attachment to French. Character B is plausibly an ordinary civic clerk from the
founding population, conducting business the only way the institution has ever worked, with no
particular investment in making a point of it.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The newcomer resolves the immediate business the only way actually available — by picking up enough
functional French, on the spot and out of sheer necessity, to get the transaction done, not through
lessons or deliberate study but through direct, repeated exposure forced by the situation itself.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The newcomer, now demonstrably capable of functional French gained purely through necessity, becomes a
small, visible proof-of-concept other skeptical new arrivals hear about — evidence that the language
barrier, while real, isn't actually the obstacle to settling in that it first appears to be.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that proof keeps circulating informally among new arrivals — friend to friend, workplace to
workplace — nobody ever needs to force the issue directly through formal policy; the expectation that
"you'll pick it up" becomes self-perpetuating folk wisdom passed newcomer to newcomer, doing the work a
mandatory language requirement never had to.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

French persistence at Dumont d'Urville is now proven out generation after generation by new arrivals who
show up skeptical and end up fluent anyway — nobody ever forcing the issue directly, the pattern simply
repeating itself through ordinary necessity every time a new skeptic arrives.
