## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Wind here is more severe than almost anywhere else documented in Tepenia, and keeping buildings livable
takes real, continuous engineering effort — heating infrastructure treated, in most Tepenian cities, as
purely functional, hidden wherever possible to keep exposed piping and units from adding to maintenance
burden.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Downtown residents, having already committed real civic effort to making the core of the city feel alive
rather than merely insulated (Suggestion #3), start pushing the question further: if a small, dense
downtown is already worth fighting for space to keep vibrant, why should its infrastructure be hidden the
way basic survival systems are everywhere else? A proposal starts circulating to run heating units
visibly between buildings downtown, instead of concealing them the standard way — turning basic
infrastructure into something closer to a public display.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between a civic infrastructure
engineer who considers visible heating a wasteful, purely cosmetic expense — costing more than the
minimum needed to simply keep people from freezing, at a time when every unit of energy and maintenance
labor matters — and a downtown advocate who argues the visible warmth is itself a form of infrastructure,
proof to residents and visitors alike that the city is thriving rather than just surviving. The engineer's
concern about waste is legitimate on a small, resource-constrained island; the advocate's insistence that
morale and outward vitality have real civic value is equally legitimate, especially for a city already
built around giving residents reasons to stay rather than merely endure.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic infrastructure
engineer or resource administrator, the same kind of practically-minded figure who might have opposed
Suggestion #3's music-venue space concession on similar grounds. Character B is plausibly a downtown civic
booster or the same proprietor-class figure who won that earlier concession, now pushing infrastructure
itself to serve the same vitality-first civic instinct.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The advocate's proposal wins, but scoped specifically to downtown rather than the whole city — visible
heating units become standard practice in the dense core alone, while outlying, more purely residential
or storage areas keep their infrastructure hidden and minimal, the engineer's cost concerns satisfied by
containing the more expensive visible approach to the one district where the civic case for it is
strongest.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Heating units start running visibly between buildings throughout downtown specifically, not hidden inside
them — a literal, physical marker separating the vitality-focused core from the rest of the city, visible
to anyone passing through exactly where anyone passing through would see it.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the visible heating is geographically concentrated rather than citywide, downtown itself becomes
legible at a glance as the city's living, thriving center — visitors and new arrivals learn to read the
exposed infrastructure as a literal map of where Dumont d'Urville's civic life actually happens, long
before they learn the island's layout any other way.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Dumont d'Urville's downtown is now defined by warmth as a deliberate outward statement — proof the city
is thriving, not just surviving, visible precisely where anyone passing through can see it, while the
rest of the island keeps its infrastructure ordinary and unremarked.
