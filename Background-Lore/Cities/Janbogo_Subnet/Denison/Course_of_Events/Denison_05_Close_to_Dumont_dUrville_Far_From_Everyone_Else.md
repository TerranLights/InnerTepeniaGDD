## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Denison sits roughly 110km along the coast from Dumont d'Urville, genuinely close by Ross Sea coast
standards, while the stretch of Hwy 183 back toward Cape Adare and the cities further north runs
dramatically longer — a plain geographic fact nobody in the founding generation set out to make
meaningful.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

As both cities grow past their earliest founding years, the asymmetry stops being a passive geographic
fact and starts actively shaping behavior: trade caravans, civic delegations, and ordinary travelers
default to the short, practical run down to Dumont d'Urville far more often than the long haul north,
simply because it's easier — and a real, frequent relationship starts accumulating almost by default,
while contact with Cape Adare and the cities beyond it stays occasional, dependent on travelers with
specific reason to make the longer trip.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between a Denison civic figure who
wants to actively invest in maintaining ties with Cape Adare and the more distant northern cities — on
the grounds that a subnet city shouldn't let geography alone dictate who it considers its community — and
a more pragmatic figure who sees no reason to spend scarce civic effort courting distant relationships
when Dumont d'Urville already offers everything a close, functioning neighbor provides. Neither side is
wrong: the first is right that geographic convenience isn't the same as genuine subnet solidarity, and
the second is right that Denison's limited resources are better spent where the relationship already
works.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a subnet-minded civic
official or delegate, someone whose role or temperament inclines toward seeing the whole Janbogo subnet
as Denison's community rather than just its nearest neighbor. Character B is plausibly a trade or
logistics figure whose daily work is defined by what actually moves efficiently along Hwy 183 — someone
for whom "closer" has a very concrete, practical meaning.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The pragmatist's position effectively wins, not through any formal decision but through simple accumulated
behavior: no deliberate civic effort ever gets committed to courting the more distant cities, and the
close, practical relationship with Dumont d'Urville keeps deepening on its own momentum, trade route by
trade route, visit by visit.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Denison's own residents begin to think of Dumont d'Urville, specifically, as the neighbor — the city
whose news, gossip, and civic events actually register in daily conversation — while Cape Adare and the
cities further north fade into something closer to distant acquaintance, known about rather than known.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Denison's everyday sense of "who's part of our world" narrows specifically around Dumont
d'Urville rather than the subnet as a whole, later civic comparisons, complaints, and even rivalries
Denison's residents raise about "how other cities do things" end up drawn almost entirely from Dumont
d'Urville's example — the city becomes Denison's default point of comparison for practically everything,
simply because it's the only other city close enough to actually watch closely.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Denison's mental map of the Janbogo subnet is now lopsided, oriented almost entirely southward — Dumont
d'Urville treated as the one neighbor genuinely known, while the rest of the subnet remains present only
in the abstract, a pattern set early enough that most residents never think to question it.
