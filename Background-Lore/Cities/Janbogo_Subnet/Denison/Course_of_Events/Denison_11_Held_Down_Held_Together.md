## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Denison's fused, load-sharing architecture (chain #1, Wei Zhang's design) handles Cape Denison's wind at
the structural level — one continuous, aerodynamically coherent system, celebrated as the city's defining
engineering achievement. Smaller-scale, human-level wind adaptation — chains, handrails, weighted
roofing at the specific points where the system still briefly exposes someone to open wind — hasn't been
addressed at all.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A resident is caught in an unexpectedly severe gust at one of the fused structure's own transition
points — a loading dock, an access hatch, a brief exposed junction between sections — proving that
structural elegance at the macro scale doesn't automatically solve every hazard at the human scale.

engineer, protective of the fused-structure legacy, genuinely proud rather than defensive. Character B is
plausibly a maintenance worker or ordinary resident, practical rather than critical, simply focused on
what actually keeps people safe at ground level.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The city adopts the granular adaptations — chains and handrails bolted at specific vulnerable points,
roofs weighted down at exposed sections — layered onto the existing fused structure rather than replacing
or competing with it, a practical addition that complements the original engineering achievement instead
of undermining it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Denison's engineering identity becomes genuinely twofold — the celebrated, macro-scale fused structure,
and a quieter, less celebrated network of granular, human-scale adaptations at every point the system
still exposes someone briefly to the wind.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

The episode becomes a quiet lesson passed to each new generation of Denison engineers: an elegant,
systemic solution still needs practical, ground-level backup, and pride in the large achievement is never
a reason to neglect the small, human-scale details the big design doesn't fully reach.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Denison's built environment now holds both scales of wind-adaptation at once — the celebrated, unified
fused structure, and a genuine network of granular, practical details at every remaining exposed point —
together forming the full, complete picture of what surviving Cape Denison's climate actually takes.
