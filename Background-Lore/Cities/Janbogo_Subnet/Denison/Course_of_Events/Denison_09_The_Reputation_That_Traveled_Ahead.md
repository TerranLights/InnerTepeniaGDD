## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Word of Cape Denison's extremity spreads across Tepenia long before most people ever have reason to
visit — the windiest permanently inhabited place on the continent becomes a kind of shorthand for
severity itself, repeated well past the point where anyone repeating it has actually experienced it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The reality inside the fused structure is, day to day, considerably more livable than the reputation
suggests — the same interlinked architecture that hides the wind's severity from view also means daily
life indoors is warm, functional, and unremarkable in a way the outside reputation never captures.
Visitors arrive consistently braced for something worse than what they find, and just as consistently
leave surprised — a mismatch that starts to matter once enough visitors, expecting a harsher experience,
start openly questioning whether Denison's reputation is honest.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between a visitor who feels
genuinely misled — expecting daily hardship and finding, instead, a functional, even comfortable
interior life — and a Denison resident who refuses to correct the exaggerated reputation, seeing no
obligation to manage outsiders' expectations about a city they're free to actually visit and judge for
themselves. The visitor's frustration is reasonable: reputations that don't match reality erode trust.
The resident's refusal is equally reasonable: undercutting the reputation would mean giving up the very
extremity Denison takes open pride in, over something as minor as one visitor's mismatched expectations.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a visiting trader,
journalist, or civic delegate arriving with secondhand impressions of Denison formed entirely from
reputation rather than firsthand account. Character B is plausibly an ordinary resident, perhaps one
already invested in the city's wind-statistics recitation custom (Suggestions #3 and #6), for whom
maintaining the fearsome reputation is inseparable from civic pride itself.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The resident wins the argument simply by declining to engage with it as a problem — no correction is
ever issued, no reputation-management effort ever mounted, and the visitor eventually leaves having
gotten no concession beyond the resident's calm insistence that Denison never promised anything about
what daily life would feel like, only about what the wind itself measures.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The gap between Denison's fearsome reputation and its actually-functional daily life becomes something
residents openly, collectively acknowledge rather than something anyone feels obligated to explain away —
a known, accepted mismatch rather than an unresolved misunderstanding.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because residents now openly acknowledge the gap rather than hide it, it becomes something they take
quiet, active enjoyment in maintaining — deliberately leaning into the fearsome reputation with outsiders
while privately treating the comfortable daily reality as a kind of inside joke, a private comfort visitors
never expect to find.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Denison's gap between fearsome reputation and livable daily reality is now something its own residents
quietly, deliberately maintain rather than a misunderstanding anyone bothers correcting — a private
comfort held alongside a public reputation, both kept fully intact at once.
