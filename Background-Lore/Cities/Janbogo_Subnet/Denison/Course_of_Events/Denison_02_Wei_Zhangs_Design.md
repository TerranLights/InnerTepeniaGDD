## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Following the founding generation's commitment to build Cape Denison as one continuous, load-sharing
structure rather than separate buildings (Suggestion #1), the settlement's chief wind-engineer inherits
the actual technical burden of making that commitment real — turning a founding decision into a working
structural principle nobody in Tepenia has ever had to formalize before.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Every existing engineering approach available to her — imported know-how, precedent from neighboring
subnet cities, anything the settlement's founders brought with them — assumes buildings remain
functionally separate units, linked at most by insulated connective tunnels. None of it scales to what
Cape Denison's wind actually demands: a structure where every section shares load with its neighbors
rather than merely sitting beside them. She has no textbook to adapt from, only a young settlement's
limited labor and materials, and a design commitment that leaves no room to discover partway through
construction that the underlying engineering principle doesn't actually work.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between the engineer pursuing a
genuinely unproven load-sharing principle to its full, rigorous conclusion, and a construction-side
foreman or resource administrator pushing to fall back on the known, if insufficient, tunnel-linkage
approach used elsewhere the moment early results look uncertain. This isn't a visionary-versus-coward
story — both sides are managing real risk. The administrator's caution is justified: a young settlement
that guesses wrong on its central structural principle may not have the labor or materials to recover
from the mistake. The engineer's insistence is equally justified: a known-insufficient design guarantees
failure against Cape Denison's specific wind severity, just failure on a longer, slower timeline. The
conflict is about which uncertainty is more dangerous to gamble the settlement's survival on.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly the figure later
remembered as, or a direct predecessor to, Chief Wind-Engineer Wei Zhang — someone
credentialed enough to be trusted with the settlement's founding structural design, but still working
out the actual mathematics of load-sharing interlinkage in real time, under real construction pressure.
Character B is plausibly a settlement administrator responsible for a founding population's limited
labor pool, whose job is specifically to prevent the settlement from over-committing to an ambitious,
unproven design before its feasibility is confirmed.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The engineer's approach prevails, but not on the strength of theory alone — it survives several real,
partial structural failures during early construction, each one forcing a redesign rather than a
retreat to the safer alternative. What emerges by the end isn't the most ambitious version of her
original proposal, but a load-sharing structural principle validated the hard way, through practice and
correction rather than clean calculation from the outset.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the principle survived real failure and correction rather than being adopted on faith, it
becomes something the settlement can actually document and teach — not a single engineer's private
intuition, but a codified structural discipline with recorded reasoning behind every rule, passed to the
next generation of builders as Denison keeps expanding in the staged phases Suggestion #1 already set in
motion.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That codified, teachable rigor — not merely the fact that Denison survived its wind — is what later
makes the city a plausible site for work demanding genuinely exacting, interlinked-systems precision:
manufacturing fabrication-synthesis chambers for the nationwide Cradle infrastructure (Suggestion #4).
A city that simply endured its environment wouldn't attract that kind of industry; a city that turned
survival into a documented engineering discipline would.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Denison now possesses not just a fused structure but a formal engineering legacy behind it — a
load-sharing discipline proven through real failure, taught deliberately rather than inherited by
accident, credited to the engineer who first pushed it through. The city's reputation for precision has
started to extend beyond its own walls, well before anyone outside Denison has reason to test it.
