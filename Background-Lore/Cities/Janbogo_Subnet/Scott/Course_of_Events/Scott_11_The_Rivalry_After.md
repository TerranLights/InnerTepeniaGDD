# Scott — The Rivalry, After
### Featuring [Character A] and [Character B] (placeholder figures — unnamed)

**Course of Events Suggestion #11**, translated from `Scott_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` into
`z-template_-_city_histories_conflict_variant.md`. Grounded in the Mega_Init's own "New, surfaced by
this research pass" question: whether Fort McMurdo's formal capital designation strained the
established Scott/Fort McMurdo relationship. Chain #7 answers only Scott's own private, internal
reaction (a quiet sting that fades); it never tests whether the actual day-to-day sibling dynamic chain
#9 describes changed in practice. This suggestion tests the relationship itself, directly. Character
fields are intentionally left blank — see each Conflict section's Design Notes for what kind of
conflict, and what kind of character(s), would make the following setting-condition become true.

---

## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Scott and Fort McMurdo hold their established sibling dynamic (chain #9) — genuinely close, genuinely
different, equal parts collaboration and friendly competition. Fort McMurdo's capital status is now
formally designated by name, and Scott's own residents have privately absorbed the quiet sting of it
(chain #7), the sting already fading on its own.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The annual Hut Point Independence Day gathering (chain #5) arrives for the first time since the formal
designation, putting residents of both cities back in the same shared space — the first real, direct
test of whether anything about the relationship actually changed.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a hostile conflict — a quiet, private tension
neither side names directly. Character A, a Scott resident, arrives at the gathering slightly guarded,
unconsciously testing whether Fort McMurdo residents will act differently now that their city carries an
official title Scott never held or sought. Character B, a Fort McMurdo resident, is genuinely oblivious to
any of this mattering — nothing about their own daily relationship to Scott changed when the designation
became official, and they're confused by Character A's unfamiliar reserve.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an ordinary Scott
resident, not bitter, just quietly carrying the same private wariness chain #7 already establishes.
Character B is plausibly an ordinary Fort McMurdo resident, warm and unguarded, a longtime participant in
the same yearly gathering with no reason to expect anything different this time.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The gathering proceeds exactly as it always has — the same teasing, the same warmth, the same shared
reverence at Hut Point. Character A's guardedness dissolves over the course of the evening, proving,
directly and socially rather than just privately, that the formal title changed far less in practice than
Scott had quietly worried it might.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The sibling dynamic chain #9 already establishes is now tested and confirmed, not merely assumed —
lived proof, shared between both cities' residents, that the relationship survived the formalization
intact.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Chain #7's private, fading sting stops being something Scott residents simply get over quietly on their
own — it fades because it gets actively, socially disproven, a more concrete resolution than time alone
provides.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The Scott/Fort McMurdo sibling relationship is now proven, not just privately assumed, to have survived
the capital formalization intact — tested directly through a shared, lived moment rather than left as
sentiment on Scott's side alone.
