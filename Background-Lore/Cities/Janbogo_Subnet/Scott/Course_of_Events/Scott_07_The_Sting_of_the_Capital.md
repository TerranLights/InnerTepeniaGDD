## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Fort McMurdo's influence grows for generations without any formal title attached to it — an arrangement
Scott can live beside without feeling actively diminished by it, its own complementary economic identity
(Suggestion #6) already well established and genuinely secure.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

When that influence finally gets formalized into an official national capital designation, the asymmetry
between the two cities stops being informal and becomes permanent, official record — the news lands as a
real, if quiet, sting among Scott's residents, confirmation of something they'd always half-resented being
reminded of, and reactions to the news split visibly within Scott itself, between residents who want to
mark the moment with public acknowledgment and residents who'd rather the city simply not dwell on it.

**Character A:** *(left blank)*

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between a Scott resident who wants some
kind of civic acknowledgment of the capital announcement — even just an honest public conversation about
what it means for Scott — and a resident who wants the city to visibly, deliberately not react, worried
that any public acknowledgment at all would look like sour grapes or petty resentment toward a neighbor
Scott has no real grievance against. The first resident isn't looking to stir up bitterness; unspoken
feelings left entirely unaddressed can fester worse than an honest, brief acknowledgment. The second
resident isn't in denial; they're protecting Scott's own established equanimity, worried that dwelling on
the asymmetry publicly would manufacture a wound that mostly wasn't there before anyone drew attention to
it.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an ordinary resident, or
a civic figure with reason to speak publicly on the news, wanting Scott's own feelings acknowledged rather
than swallowed. Character B is plausibly a civic leader or longtime resident invested in Scott's public
image and its genuinely secure sense of self, wary of a reaction that reads as envy.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A brief, low-key acknowledgment wins out over silence — a short, plainly-worded civic statement noting the
announcement without editorializing on it, satisfying the first resident's wish for honesty while denying
the second resident's fear of anything resembling public sulking; the moment gets named once, briefly, and
then the city moves on.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Scott's own established Positive Outlook temperament keeps the sting from curdling into lasting
resentment — because the feeling was named once, briefly and honestly, rather than either suppressed
entirely or dwelt on publicly, it has nowhere further to build, and ordinary civic life resumes almost
immediately.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the sting was processed openly and briefly rather than either suppressed or amplified, Scott's
residents develop a specific, shared civic habit for handling future moments of this kind — acknowledge
honestly, briefly, once, then move on — a template later drawn on whenever some new comparison to Fort
McMurdo's greater prominence resurfaces, rather than each new instance reopening the same unresolved wound.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The sting fades faster than it might have elsewhere — an official name finally put on an imbalance that
had simply always existed, not a loss of anything Scott actually wanted, processed through a brief, honest
civic acknowledgment rather than either denial or dwelling.
