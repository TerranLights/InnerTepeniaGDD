## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

As Fort McMurdo's informal influence starts becoming impossible to ignore across the Federation, some
outside observers assume Scott, sitting right beside it, might eventually make its own bid for regional
prominence — a natural enough assumption given the proximity, but one that has never actually reflected
how Scott's own residents think about their city.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A visiting delegation or outside political figure, genuinely expecting Scott to have ambitions of its own
given its position beside a rising regional power, raises the question directly with Scott's civic
leadership — proposing, in good faith, that Scott position itself for a larger regional role now, while
Fort McMurdo's rise creates opportunity for its neighbors too. The proposal isn't hostile or presumptuous;
it's a reasonable outside read of the situation that simply doesn't match Scott's own settled self-
understanding, and it forces Scott's leadership to articulate, out loud, something that's mostly gone
unspoken until now.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** less a hostile confrontation than a genuine
persuasion attempt — an outside figure making a real, well-intentioned case for why Scott should seize this
moment, against a Scott civic leader who has to explain, clearly and patiently, why the premise itself
doesn't apply. The outside figure isn't wrong that opportunity exists; they simply don't yet understand
that Scott's founding character (Suggestions #1, #2, and #8) was never built around wanting that kind of
influence in the first place. The Scott leader's task isn't to defeat an adversary, but to make legible, to
someone genuinely puzzled by it, a civic identity that doesn't map onto the ambition the visitor is taking
for granted.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an outside political
figure or delegation member, perhaps from another subnet, acting in good faith on a reasonable but
mistaken assumption about Scott's ambitions. Character B is plausibly a senior Scott civic leader, someone
old enough to personally remember, or to have inherited direct institutional memory of, the founding
modesty decision and the later test it survived (Suggestion #8).

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The Scott leader's explanation lands — no bid for regional prominence ever materializes, the outside
figure leaves having genuinely understood, rather than merely accepted, why Scott isn't interested,
and the proposal quietly closes without residue on either side.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Scott watches its neighbor's rise with genuine equanimity rather than competitive ambition — the
successfully deflected proposal becomes, itself, a small but real confirmation that Scott's civic identity
can withstand direct outside pressure to be something other than what it's chosen to be.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Scott's leadership has now had to articulate its own lack of ambition clearly enough to persuade a
genuinely skeptical outsider, that explanation itself becomes something Scott's civic culture keeps and
reuses — a settled, ready answer passed down for the next time some outsider makes the same reasonable but
mistaken assumption, meaning each subsequent version of this same conversation takes less effort to resolve
than the last.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

"The city that never wanted to be the capital" becomes as real a piece of Scott's civic character as
anything it actively built — defined, in part, by the prominence it simply never wanted, and now backed by
a settled, well-rehearsed answer for anyone who assumes otherwise.
