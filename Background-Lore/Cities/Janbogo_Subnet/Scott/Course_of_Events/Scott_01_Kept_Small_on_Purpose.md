## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Scott Base's real infrastructure was always deliberately modest, and the exile community inheriting it
sits just three kilometers from Fort McMurdo, whose own industrial base and strategic position are
already visibly outgrowing anything Scott's founders inherited.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The exile community faces an early, unavoidable choice: grow aggressively the way Fort McMurdo clearly
intends to, or accept staying small. Matching Fort McMurdo's growth would mean competing directly with a
neighbor that already has the industrial base and strategic position to win that race easily — but
accepting smallness carries its own real cost, forfeiting whatever prestige, resources, and influence a
larger, more ambitious Scott might eventually command.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a founding-era conflict between a settler who wants
Scott to compete seriously with Fort McMurdo — investing hard in industrial capacity, chasing comparable
growth, refusing to simply concede the region's prominence to their neighbor — and a settler advocating
deliberate modesty, arguing that a smaller, genuinely well-run community serves its people better than a
losing race against a neighbor with every structural advantage. The ambitious settler's instinct isn't
vanity; conceding the field before even trying carries real long-term costs. The modesty advocate isn't
settling for less out of timidity; they're making a clear-eyed bet that contentment and quality of life
matter more than raw competitive standing.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an ambitious
founding-era settler, perhaps with an industrial or engineering background, who arrived expecting Scott
to build something comparably significant to its neighbor. Character B is plausibly the figure later
remembered as, or a direct predecessor to, Community Organizer Fiona Māui, already inclined toward
a domestic, quality-of-life-first vision for the settlement.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The modesty advocate's position wins the founding generation's actual commitment — not through the
ambitious settler being definitively refuted, but through the practical recognition, shared eventually by
most of the community, that a genuinely decent, quiet place is an achievable goal in a way that
out-competing Fort McMurdo simply isn't.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Scott's founding generation deliberately chooses modesty as an active civic commitment rather than a
passive default — early civic planning, infrastructure investment, and public messaging all start
reflecting a genuinely decent, quiet place rather than a rival industrial center trying to catch up to its
neighbor.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the founding generation had to actively choose and defend modesty against a real, live
alternative rather than simply drifting into smallness by default, later generations inherit modesty as a
value worth actively maintaining, not a circumstance to passively accept — meaning every later temptation
to grow bigger (Suggestion #8) gets weighed against a founding choice residents can actually point to and
name, rather than an unexamined status quo.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

"Kept smaller by choice, not necessity" becomes something Scott residents actively maintain, generation
after generation, rather than a circumstance they were simply born into — a founding decision residents
can point to directly whenever the question of growth resurfaces.
