## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Hut Point belongs, technically, to Scott — the hut sits inside the city's own limits, tended informally
by the resident who first took up the role of its keeper (Suggestion #4) — while Fort McMurdo, three
kilometers away, has no formal claim to the site at all.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Fort McMurdo's own residents feel the same connection to the Saint the hut honors, since he belongs to the
whole peninsula, not one city's property line — and on Tepenian Independence Day, without any advance
coordination between the two cities, residents of both start showing up at Hut Point independently, each
group assuming they have the more legitimate claim to be there, creating an awkward, unplanned overlap
neither city's civic leadership anticipated.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict, gentle rather than hostile, between a
Scott resident who feels a proprietary claim to Hut Point as their own city's sacred site, and a Fort
McMurdo resident who feels every bit as entitled to be there, having grown up on the same peninsula with
the same reverence for the same history. Neither is trying to exclude the other out of malice — both are
simply working from an assumption of primary ownership that the other person's presence quietly
contradicts. The tension is less about who's right and more about whether the site actually needs one
exclusive owner at all.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Scott resident,
perhaps someone close to the Keeper of Hut Point, who initially bristles at unexpected Fort McMurdo
attendance on what feels like Scott's own day. Character B is plausibly a Fort McMurdo resident equally
sincere in their own attachment to the site, arriving without any sense they're intruding on anything.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The initial awkwardness resolves not through any formal negotiation but through the two residents simply
talking to each other at the site itself, realizing neither actually wants exclusive claim — they leave
that first shared observance having quietly agreed, just between themselves, to make it a shared moment on
purpose next year, rather than an accidental overlap to be managed or avoided.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

An unmandated tradition simply starts happening — residents of both cities gathering at Hut Point on
Tepenian Independence Day to lay candles, flowers, and personal tokens, nobody having organized it
top-down, built directly out of that first year's accidental, then quietly welcomed, overlap.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the tradition started as two individuals' personal choice rather than a civic decree, it carries
no formal machinery that could let it quietly lapse through simple neglect of paperwork — its survival
depends entirely on residents continuing to choose it each year, which means every year it repeats becomes
its own small, renewed act of choosing the other city as genuinely welcome, rather than an inherited
obligation nobody thinks to question.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The Independence Day gathering at Hut Point repeats every year, unforced, becoming the one regular
occasion the two neighboring cities genuinely share rather than merely tolerate — a tradition two
strangers' quiet agreement built, sustained ever since by nothing more official than residents choosing,
year after year, to keep showing up together.
