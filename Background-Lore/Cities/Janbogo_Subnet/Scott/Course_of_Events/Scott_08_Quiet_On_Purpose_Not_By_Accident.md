## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Scott has, by now, a genuine, working complementary technical economy (Suggestion #6) alongside its
deliberately modest, family-oriented civic character (Suggestions #1 and #2) — a settled, comfortable
identity that has never yet been seriously tested against real outside temptation.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A genuine economic opportunity arises — outside investors, impressed by Scott's proven calibration and
monitoring expertise, propose expanding the city's industrial footprint well beyond its modest scale, with
real capital and real long-term prosperity attached to the offer. Taking it would mean abandoning the
deliberate smallness the city has maintained since its founding — but turning down real economic
opportunity isn't free either, and the offer forces Scott's civic leadership to confront, for the first
time with real stakes attached, whether its founding modesty was ever meant to survive genuine temptation.

**Character A:** *(left blank)*

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between a civic leader who wants to accept
the investors' offer, arguing that real prosperity for Scott's residents shouldn't be turned down out of
mere sentimental attachment to a founding-era decision nobody currently living actually made, and a civic
leader defending the founding modesty commitment as a genuine, still-relevant value worth real sacrifice to
protect. The pro-growth leader's case is legitimate: declining real prosperity is a real cost, not a free
gesture. The modesty defender's case is equally legitimate: a value that only holds when nothing tempts it
was never really a value at all, only an unexamined default — this is the one moment that actually tests
which one Scott's founding choice was.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic leader with
direct exposure to the outside investors, perhaps someone from Scott's own technical-economy sector
(Suggestion #6), genuinely persuaded by the offer's real merits. Character B is plausibly a civic leader
invested in Scott's founding character, perhaps a descendant or institutional heir of Fiona Māui-Larsen's
original vision.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Scott's civic leadership declines the offer — a close, genuinely contested decision, not a foregone
conclusion, settled specifically on the grounds that growth of this scale would abandon the deliberate
smallness the city has maintained since its founding, choosing to stay the quiet, family-oriented place
it's always been over the prosperity the offer would bring.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Turning down real economic opportunity on principle isn't free — it means real prosperity Scott could
plausibly have had, permanently forgone, a cost residents are honest with themselves about rather than
pretending the decision was costless.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the decision cost something real and everyone involved knew it at the time, it becomes a defining
civic moment residents point back to specifically *because* it was hard — not a comfortable, easy
affirmation of values nobody had to sacrifice for, but proof, demonstrated under genuine pressure, that
Scott's modesty was always an active choice defended against real temptation, not a passive default nobody
bothered to change.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Scott's founding modesty commitment now carries the weight of having survived a genuine, costly test —
"kept small on purpose" is no longer just a founding story, but a value residents have watched their own
civic leadership actually choose, at real cost, within living memory.
