## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Scott and Fort McMurdo sit close enough that residents of each regularly interact with the other, close
enough to feel like neighbors rather than distant cities — a proximity that has produced practical
cooperation (the shared Erebus watch, Suggestion #6's economic complementarity) but not yet any real,
acknowledged sense of what the two cities actually think of each other.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The two places could hardly feel more different day to day — one a constant, loud operational hub, the
other overwhelmingly residential and quiet — and as ordinary cross-city interactions accumulate (shared
work, shared Independence Day observance at Hut Point, ordinary commerce), residents on both sides start
half-teasing each other about whose city actually offers the better life, comments that could easily curdle
into real friction if either side pushes too hard or takes offense too easily.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a genuinely good-natured but real ribbing match
between a Fort McMurdo resident, proud of their city's scale, energy, and importance, and a Scott resident,
equally proud of their city's quiet, family-oriented character — each one convinced their own way of life
is better, neither one actually wanting to trade places, but both willing to needle the other about it. The
conflict needs to walk a real line: genuine enough that both sides mean what they're saying, light enough
that it never threatens the underlying goodwill that makes the two cities' cooperation possible in the
first place.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an ordinary Fort McMurdo
resident with routine reason to interact with Scott — a coordination-station worker, a tradesperson, a
frequent visitor. Character B is plausibly the corresponding Scott resident on the other end of that same
routine interaction, equally invested in defending their own city's very different appeal.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither resident concedes the argument, and neither expects to — the exchange ends the way it always does,
in mutual, affectionate needling rather than any real resolution, both walking away exactly as convinced of
their own city's superiority as when the conversation started, and both, notably, still on entirely good
terms.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

A genuine, lived friendly rivalry forms between the two cities, equal parts collaboration and competition —
not a single dramatic incident, but a repeated, low-stakes pattern of exactly this kind of exchange playing
out across countless ordinary interactions between the two populations.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the rivalry keeps expressing itself through harmless, repeated teasing rather than any genuine
grievance, it never curdles into real hostility — the pattern itself becomes self-reinforcing, since
neither city actually wants what the other has, meaning every fresh exchange of gentle mockery just
confirms, rather than threatens, how comfortable both cities actually are in their own very different
skins.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Scott and Fort McMurdo settle into something closer to siblings than rivals — genuinely different,
genuinely close, neither one able to fully imagine living the other's way, their difference expressed
constantly and safely through exactly the kind of good-natured needling neither city ever seems to tire of.
