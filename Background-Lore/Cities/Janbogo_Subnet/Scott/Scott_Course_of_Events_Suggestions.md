# Scott — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Scott's Megasheet (the deliberately modest
neighbor beside Fort McMurdo, Hut Point's shared remembrance tradition, the quiet family-oriented
civic character). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Kept Small on Purpose

Scott Base's real infrastructure was always deliberately modest, and the exile community inheriting it
faces an early choice: grow aggressively the way Fort McMurdo, three kilometers away, clearly intends
to, or stay small. **But** matching Fort McMurdo's growth would mean competing directly with a
neighbor that already has the industrial base and strategic position to win that race easily.
**Therefore** Scott's founding generation deliberately chooses modesty instead — a genuinely decent,
quiet place rather than a rival industrial center. **But** choosing smallness on purpose means
defending that choice against every later temptation to grow bigger. **Therefore** "kept smaller by
choice, not necessity" becomes something Scott residents actively maintain, generation after
generation, rather than a circumstance they were simply born into.

## 2. Fiona's Neighborhood

Scott's earliest exile-era decades could easily have drifted toward whatever civic shape its
inherited infrastructure happened to suggest. **But** Community Organizer Fiona Māui-Larsen sees an
opportunity to shape something more deliberate — a genuinely family-oriented residential community,
not just a smaller, quieter version of an industrial city. **Therefore** she pushes early civic
planning toward housing, leisure, and modest gathering spaces rather than heavy infrastructure
investment. **But** prioritizing domestic life over economic ambition means Scott will never compete
with its neighbor on raw output. **Therefore** her choices set the whole tone the city carries
forward — a place defined by what it's like to live there, not by what it produces.

## 3. What Falls From Erebus

Mount Erebus's constant volcanic activity scatters material across the Hut Point Peninsula, useful
raw matter nobody's yet organized a way to actually collect. **But** Fort McMurdo's own industrial
capacity is already stretched across its Dry Valleys processing operation and doesn't need another
collection job added to it. **Therefore** Scott develops its own small, contained industry instead —
gathering the fallen volcanic material and delivering it to a trucking facility across McMurdo Sound
for onward processing. **But** it's a modest operation, nothing like Fort McMurdo's scale. **Therefore**
it becomes exactly the kind of niche Scott's whole civic character favors: real, useful, and
deliberately unambitious.

## 4. The Keeper of Hut Point

Robert Falcon Scott's 1902 expedition hut sits inside the city's own limits, one of the oldest
surviving human-made structures on the continent. **But** a structure that significant needs someone
actually tending it, not just left to weather on its own. **Therefore** Wiremu Halvorsen takes on the
role of Keeper, maintaining the site between observances and making sure its condition never becomes
an afterthought. **But** the role carries real responsibility with no formal institutional backing
behind it, just personal commitment. **Therefore** the Keeper position becomes one of Scott's own
quietly essential civic roles — unglamorous, unpaid in any grand sense, and absolutely necessary to
keeping the city's most sacred site intact.

## 5. Two Saints, One Peninsula

Hut Point belongs, technically, to Scott. **But** Fort McMurdo's own residents feel the same
connection to the Saint the hut honors, since he belongs to the whole peninsula, not one city's
property line. **Therefore** an unmandated tradition simply starts happening — residents of both
cities gathering at Hut Point on Tepenian Independence Day to lay candles, flowers, and personal
tokens, nobody having organized it top-down. **But** a tradition nobody organized could just as easily
fade as it started. **Therefore** it doesn't — it repeats every year, unforced, becoming the one
regular occasion the two neighboring cities genuinely share rather than merely tolerate.

## 6. Measuring What Fort McMurdo Builds

Fort McMurdo's industrial base grows fast, heavy, and increasingly complex, badly in need of precise
calibration and monitoring work its own operational focus doesn't prioritize. **But** duplicating Fort
McMurdo's own heavy-industry approach would put Scott in direct, unwinnable competition with its
neighbor. **Therefore** Scott develops a genuinely complementary technical niche instead — precision
equipment calibration and environmental monitoring, including a real share of the ongoing Erebus
watch. **But** staying complementary means Scott's own economic identity will always be defined partly
in relation to its neighbor rather than entirely on its own terms. **Therefore** Scott settles into a
role it's genuinely good at and genuinely needed for, even knowing it will never be the more famous
half of the partnership.

## 7. The Sting of the Capital

Fort McMurdo's influence grows for generations without any formal title attached to it — an
arrangement Scott can live beside without feeling actively diminished by it. **But** when that
influence finally gets formalized into an official national capital designation, the asymmetry between
the two cities stops being informal and becomes permanent, official record. **Therefore** the news
lands as a real, if quiet, sting among Scott's residents — confirmation of something they'd always
half-resented being reminded of. **But** Scott's own civic character was never built around wanting
the title in the first place. **Therefore** the sting fades faster than it might have elsewhere — an
official name finally put on an imbalance that had simply always existed, not a loss of anything Scott
actually wanted.

## 8. Quiet On Purpose, Not By Accident

A genuine economic opportunity arises — outside investors interested in expanding Scott's own
industrial footprint well beyond its modest scale. **But** taking the offer would mean abandoning the
deliberate smallness the city has maintained since its founding. **Therefore** Scott's civic leadership
declines, choosing to stay the quiet, family-oriented place it's always been over the growth the offer
would bring. **But** turning down real economic opportunity on principle isn't free — it means real
prosperity Scott could plausibly have had, permanently forgone. **Therefore** the decision becomes a
defining civic moment residents point back to, proof that Scott's modesty was always an active choice
defended against real temptation, not a passive default nobody bothered to change.

## 9. Three Kilometers, Two Worlds

Scott and Fort McMurdo sit close enough that residents of each regularly interact with the other,
close enough to feel like neighbors rather than distant cities. **But** the two places could hardly
feel more different day to day — one a constant, loud operational hub, the other overwhelmingly
residential and quiet. **Therefore** a genuine, lived friendly rivalry forms between them, equal parts
collaboration and competition, residents of each half-teasing the other about which kind of city
actually has the better life. **But** that rivalry never curdles into real hostility, since neither
city actually wants what the other has. **Therefore** Scott and Fort McMurdo settle into something
closer to siblings than rivals — genuinely different, genuinely close, neither one able to fully
imagine living the other's way.

## 10. Not the Capital, and Fine With It

As Fort McMurdo's informal influence starts becoming impossible to ignore across the Federation, some
observers assume Scott, sitting right beside it, might eventually make its own bid for regional
prominence. **But** nothing in Scott's founding character was ever built around wanting that kind of
influence — the deliberate modesty goes back to the earliest exile-era decisions. **Therefore** no
such bid ever materializes; Scott watches its neighbor's rise with genuine equanimity rather than
competitive ambition. **But** equanimity this consistent, this early, eventually becomes its own kind
of identity. **Therefore** "the city that never wanted to be the capital" becomes as real a piece of
Scott's civic character as anything it actively built — defined, in part, by the prominence it simply
never wanted.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#1 and #8** work as a pair — the founding decision to stay modest, and a later, real test of whether
that decision would hold against genuine temptation, giving Scott's smallness an actual defended shape
rather than treating it as a static, unchallenged fact. **#5** is the most emotionally resonant thread
of the ten, since it's the only one that gives the Hut Point tradition an actual origin moment rather
than describing it as something that simply always happened.
