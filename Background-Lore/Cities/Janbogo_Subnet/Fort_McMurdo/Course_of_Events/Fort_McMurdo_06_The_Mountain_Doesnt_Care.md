## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Generations of Fort McMurdo residents grow up under Erebus's permanent gas-and-ash plume, treating the
active volcano as background weather rather than an ongoing threat — an easy familiarity with forces
larger than human or robot agency that outsiders sometimes find unsettling.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

That easy familiarity is tested once, hard, when the monitoring program Dr. Marco Conti built
registers a real spike in seismic activity — not an eruption, but close enough that evacuation planning
has to move from theoretical exercise to actual, live preparation for the first time in living memory.
The city's whole unbothered self-image is suddenly a genuine question rather than a settled fact:
nobody currently in the city has ever had to find out whether that calm was earned competence or just
untested luck.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation, under real time pressure, between
someone reading the monitoring data as cause for immediate, disruptive evacuation measures and someone
arguing for continued calm pending clearer signal — both drawing on the same data, disagreeing on how to
weigh false-alarm cost (halting the city's coordination role, a cost that ripples across the whole
Federation) against catastrophic-miss cost (not evacuating in time). This shouldn't play as
caution-is-right-and-panic-is-wrong or vice versa; the monitoring program itself was never tested against
a real ambiguous reading before, so neither position has more actual evidence behind it than the other —
just different tolerances for being wrong in different directions.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a volcanologist or
monitoring-station technician working under or trained by Conti himself, closest to the raw data
and most aware of exactly how ambiguous a "spike, not yet an eruption" reading really is. Character B is
plausibly a city administrator or coordination-station lead who has to weigh evacuation against Fort
McMurdo's ongoing responsibility to the rest of the subnet's logistics — someone whose job is
specifically to not let the city's operational reliability break down over an uncertain signal. The
conflict is between expertise close to uncertain data and authority responsible for the wider
consequences of acting, or not acting, on it.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A partial, staged evacuation plan is activated — non-essential personnel and residents near the most
exposed sections of the city moved to secure zones, the coordination stations themselves kept running at
reduced capacity rather than shut down entirely — a genuine middle path forced by the standoff rather
than a clean win for either side. The spike recedes without an eruption, and Erebus settles back to its
familiar baseline rumble before the staged evacuation is even fully complete.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The partial-evacuation protocol, improvised under pressure this one time, gets formally written down and
adopted as Fort McMurdo's first actual volcanic-response procedure — a specific, staged plan replacing
what had previously been purely theoretical planning that nobody expected to actually need.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the city now has a real, tested-in-practice procedure rather than an unproven theory, the near-
eruption stops being remembered as a moment of institutional near-failure and instead becomes local
legend — proof, retroactively, that the city's unbothered relationship with a live volcano was earned
readiness all along. That retelling depends specifically on the procedure the crisis produced; without
it, the same incident would just as easily be remembered as the time the city got lucky.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Fort McMurdo now has a tested, formally adopted volcanic evacuation procedure and a piece of local legend
to go with it — the incident becomes local legend rather than local trauma, even though everyone who
lived through it privately knows how close that verdict came to going the other way.
