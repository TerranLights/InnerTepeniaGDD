## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Fort McMurdo's earliest coordination stations are staffed almost evenly by Chinese and American
personnel, with neither side holding a clear majority — a near-fifty-fifty founding population with no
single dominant national culture to set the terms of daily operations.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

That near-parity means early disputes over whose engineering standards, whose administrative language,
and whose scheduling conventions the comms stations should run on have no easy majority-rules answer.
Every shift change threatens to refight the same argument, since neither bloc can simply outvote the
other and neither is willing to defer. The disputes start eating into the coordination stations' actual
operational reliability — exactly the function the rest of the subnet is coming to depend on, even this
early.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a senior figure from each
bloc, each genuinely convinced their own national engineering and administrative conventions are simply
the more reliable ones — not out of chauvinism, but because each was trained inside a coherent system
that works fine on its own terms and breaks down only when forced to interoperate with an equally
coherent, different one. This should avoid making either side's preference arbitrary or petty; the
conflict is that two genuinely functional systems can't both run the same stations at once, and neither
side has a principled reason to be the one who yields.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** two coordination-station shift leads or
technical supervisors, one from each national bloc, each responsible for keeping their own shift running
smoothly and each frustrated that the other's incoming shift keeps undoing scheduling or procedural
choices made on the previous one. Neither is a national representative in any formal sense — they're
working engineers whose personal competence is genuinely being undermined by the standoff, which is
exactly what eventually pushes them toward a practical rather than ideological solution.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Out of practical necessity rather than idealism, the two blocs settle on judging procedures — and,
implicitly, people — by output instead of origin: whichever standard, whichever convention, actually
keeps a shift running with the fewest errors wins that argument, regardless of which flag it came from.
It's adopted as a narrow, specific fix for a specific operational headache, nothing more ambitious than
that at the time.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The output-over-origin standard, once adopted for scheduling and engineering convention disputes,
doesn't stay confined to those disputes — new hires and new procedures across every department start
getting judged the same way by default, since it's now the only tie-breaking principle the coordination
stations have that both founding blocs already agreed to.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That compromise, adopted to end one specific dispute, outlives the dispute itself and calcifies into
something residents actually believe in rather than merely tolerate: the meritocratic, "status earned by
what you could do" culture the city is known for by the Interwar's later years didn't arrive as a
founding ideal — it was a workaround for a stalemate between two nations neither willing to yield to the
other, which by the time of Suggestion #2's stayed-behinds dispute, nobody in the city remembers being a
workaround at all.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Fort McMurdo's meritocratic culture is now fully load-bearing civic identity rather than a remembered
compromise — the specific historical origin of "status earned by output, not origin" has receded into
the background, indistinguishable, for most residents, from the city simply always having been this way.
