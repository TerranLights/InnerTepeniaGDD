## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

In its early industrial years, Fort McMurdo tries to do everything itself — extraction and processing
both, concentrated entirely on Ross Island, close to the original McMurdo Station-era core.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Ross Island's own volcanic basalt turns out to be a poor substitute for what the exposed bedrock and
sediment across McMurdo Sound in the Dry Valleys can actually supply — genuinely unlike ice-covered
ground anywhere else on the continent, and rich in mineral resources Ross Island itself doesn't have.
Raw extraction gradually relocates across the Sound, reached by seasonal sea-ice road or by sea, while
Fort McMurdo itself specializes down into pure processing and coordination, mirroring Calgary's own
real division between a headquarters city and its extraction site. That division is a deliberate
efficiency decision, not an accident of geology alone — but it creates a real structural vulnerability:
two sites now depend on each other across an unstable, seasonal crossing that neither one fully
controls.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between someone responsible for Fort
McMurdo's own processing capacity, who wants the Dry Valleys extraction operation kept tightly
integrated with — and answerable to — the city's own scheduling and infrastructure, and someone running
the Dry Valleys side itself, who wants deliberate independence from Fort McMurdo's own logistics so a
single bad ice season on the crossing can't stall extraction entirely. Neither side is wrong: integration
would make coordination easier day to day, but at the cost of a shared failure point; independence
protects extraction from the city's own problems, but at the cost of the two sites drifting apart
operationally. The disagreement should read as two competent operators optimizing for different kinds of
risk, not as one side being short-sighted.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Fort McMurdo
processing-and-coordination administrator, invested in keeping the city's own comms-station scheduling
authority (the same apparatus behind its de facto capital status) extended over the Dry Valleys
operation. Character B is plausibly a Dry Valleys site foreman or extraction lead, physically removed
from Fort McMurdo's daily politics and more concerned with keeping their own operation running
regardless of what happens across the Sound. The conflict plays out as an early test of exactly how much
authority Fort McMurdo's coordination culture can extend over infrastructure it doesn't physically sit
on top of.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The independence side wins the argument, formally: the Dry Valleys operation is built out with its own
resilient, separately-resourced logistics chain, deliberately not fully dependent on Fort McMurdo's own
infrastructure. It's framed at the time as a hedge, not a vote of no confidence in the city's
coordination — a decision made a generation before anything actually tests it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The Dry Valleys operation develops its own semi-autonomous administrative culture, distinct from Fort
McMurdo's own comms-station apparatus — its own scheduling habits, its own chain of authority for
day-to-day decisions, checking in with the city proper only for larger allocation questions.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

When the convergence-era demand spike (Suggestion #7) strains Fort McMurdo's own processing capacity to
its limit, the Dry Valleys side — never dependent on the city's own infrastructure, and by then run
through a genuinely separate administrative culture with its own decision-making habits — keeps running
at full pace regardless, without needing to wait on any instruction from Fort McMurdo's overwhelmed
coordination stations. The split proves its worth not through luck, but because the earlier decision to
build real administrative independence, not just physical distance, is what actually lets the Dry
Valleys side act on its own during the crisis.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Fort McMurdo and its Dry Valleys extraction operation now run as two genuinely distinct administrative
cultures joined by a single seasonal crossing — a deliberate division of labor whose real payoff, a
functioning hedge against the city's own future overload, won't be fully appreciated until the
convergence boom actually arrives.
