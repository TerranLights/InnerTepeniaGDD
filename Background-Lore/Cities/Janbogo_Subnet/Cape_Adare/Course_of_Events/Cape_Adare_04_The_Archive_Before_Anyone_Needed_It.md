## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Borchgrevink's 1899 hut sits at the literal center of the growing city, the physical reason it exists
where it does — a modest wooden structure the whole settlement has quite literally grown up around.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A structure that old, made of simple materials, isn't going to last forever on reputation alone —
someone eventually has to reckon with the fact that the single object anchoring Cape Adare's whole sense
of itself is fragile, aging, and completely unmonitored. But funding careful archival work — measurements,
photographs, replica plans — in a practical, trade-focused gateway economy is a hard sell without an
obvious crisis to justify it. There's no visible emergency yet, only a quiet, structural risk nobody has
prioritized.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone pushing for
dedicated, funded heritage-documentation work well before any visible damage justifies the expense, and a
civic administrator responsible for allocating a growing gateway city's limited resources toward more
immediately pressing needs — port capacity, housing, trade infrastructure. The administrator isn't
dismissing history out of philistinism; they're weighing a real, present cost against a hypothetical
future loss that hasn't happened yet and might never. The advocate isn't being sentimental; they
understand, correctly, that waiting for visible damage means waiting too long.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an early historian or
proto-archivist — the figure later remembered as, or a direct predecessor of, Elena Manalo —
making the case for preventive documentation nobody else sees the urgency for yet. Character B is
plausibly a civic budget administrator or trade-infrastructure official, genuinely sympathetic in
principle but accountable to competing, more visible demands on the same limited resources.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The advocate wins a modest, ongoing allocation — not the full archival program eventually associated with
Manalo's name, but enough to begin systematic measurement, photography, and replica planning of
the hut well before any specific threat exists, establishing archival documentation as a recurring
line item rather than a one-time favor.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

A dedicated, if modest, heritage-documentation tradition takes root as an ongoing civic practice rather
than a one-off project — successive archivists, eventually including Manalo herself, inherit a
standing (if perpetually underfunded) mandate rather than having to establish the whole practice from
nothing.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the practice is now inherited rather than newly argued for each time, the fight over its funding
never fully goes away — it recurs instead, every budget cycle, as successive civic administrators
question anew why scarce resources go toward documenting an old wooden hut. Manalo and her own
successors have to keep making the same case, generation after generation, precisely because the earlier
victory secured the practice's existence but never secured it a permanent, unquestioned budget.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Cape Adare now has a standing, if perpetually contested, heritage-documentation tradition — the specific
reason a physical record of Borchgrevink's hut exists at all when the structure itself is eventually
lost, and the origin point of a recurring civic argument successive archivists will keep having to win.
