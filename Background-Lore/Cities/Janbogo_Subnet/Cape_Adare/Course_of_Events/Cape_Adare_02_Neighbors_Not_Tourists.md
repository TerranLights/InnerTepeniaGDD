## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Early residents share the Adare Peninsula with one of the largest Adélie rookeries on the continent — a
quarter-million breeding pairs, far too vast to displace even if anyone wanted to, simply an environmental
constant the settlement has to build its life around.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Treating a population this size purely as scenery or hazard leaves an obvious gap in how people actually
live alongside it day to day — birds wander into settled areas, injured or orphaned chicks turn up near
human structures, and nobody has agreed on what, if anything, residents are supposed to do about any of
it. Individual penguin-keeping quietly starts happening anyway, informally, without any shared standard
for which birds get taken in, which stay wild, and where the line actually sits — a practice that could
easily start damaging the wider rookery's own undisturbed breeding grounds if it keeps spreading
unchecked.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a resident who has taken in
and grown attached to individual penguins informally, treating it as ordinary neighborly kindness, and
someone responsible for (or simply protective of) the wider rookery's ecological stability, worried that
uncoordinated, well-meaning intervention at scale could genuinely disrupt breeding patterns the whole
settlement's long-term coexistence depends on. Neither side should be simply right or wrong — the
individual keeper's affection is real and the birds involved are often genuinely orphaned or injured, but
the ecological concern is equally real and not merely bureaucratic caution.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an ordinary resident
who has personally taken in one or more penguins and built a real emotional bond with them, resistant to
being told their private kindness needs outside rules. Character B is plausibly an early naturalist or
proto-Rookery-Keeper figure — someone who might later be recognized as Tomás Widjaja himself, or a
predecessor whose informal observations Widjaja eventually formalizes — arguing for coordinated norms
before individual practice, however well-intentioned, accumulates into real ecological harm.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The dispute settles into a genuinely negotiated set of norms, not a ban and not unrestricted freedom:
which birds are appropriate to take in (injured, orphaned, or otherwise unable to rejoin the colony) and
which must stay wild, formalized over time by Widjaja into the city's first real unofficial code around
penguin-keeping.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Widjaja's norms give Cape Adare its first informal but genuinely functioning civic role tied specifically
to the rookery — an unofficial but widely respected authority residents actually defer to on questions of
where the line between "neighbor" and "keeper" sits, filling a gap the city's own consensus-built
institutions (Suggestion #1) never explicitly addressed.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that authority exists and is trusted, Cape Adare's relationship to the rookery never calcifies
into either strict institutional control or total laissez-faire — the negotiated middle Widjaja
established becomes the model the city reaches for generations later when other resident practices near
the rookery need similar informal governance, from acoustic-music gatherings held close to breeding
grounds to how heritage-tourism traffic near the hut (Suggestion #4) gets routed to avoid disturbing
nesting areas.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Cape Adare now lives inside a genuinely negotiated relationship with its rookery — not conquest, not pure
sentiment, but two populations that learned, through Widjaja's own established norms, to actually share
the ground, with an informal but respected civic role now standing behind that arrangement.
