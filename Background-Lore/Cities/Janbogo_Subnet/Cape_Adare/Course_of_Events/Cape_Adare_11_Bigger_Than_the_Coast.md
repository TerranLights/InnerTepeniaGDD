## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Cape Adare already carries real symbolic weight as the site of a regional Ross Sea coast self-governance
gathering (chain #7) — proof the city's "first place" status means something beyond sentiment. But
nothing at genuinely national scale has ever used the site; Cape Adare remains, in national terms, simply
a large, unhurried gateway city.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A genuinely national-level dispute or decision point arises, requiring representatives from well beyond
the Ross Sea coast — even beyond the Janbogo subnet entirely. With no standing national capital to host
it, a delegate proposes Cape Adare specifically: not for convenience, but because its symbolic status as
the place where Antarctica's human story began, combined with its total lack of founding-nation dominance
or political ambition, makes it uniquely, credibly neutral ground.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a conflict about whether the matter itself is
important — both sides agree it genuinely is. Character A, from Cape Adare's own civic leadership, is
uneasy about the responsibility and scrutiny a national-scale gathering would bring to a city that has
spent generations deliberately protecting its own "big city, small-town feel" (chains #3, #9) — worried
that hosting something this significant risks undoing exactly what makes Cape Adare worth protecting.
Character B, an outside delegate proposing the site, argues Cape Adare's own lack of ambition is
precisely the qualification — a city that isn't trying to become anything more than what it already is
makes it the one place every side can trust not to use the hosting itself for leverage.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a senior Cape Adare
civic administrator, protective of the city's hard-won unhurried character, genuinely torn between civic
pride and civic caution. Character B is plausibly a delegate from elsewhere in the Federation, respectful
rather than pushy, making a case grounded in exactly what Cape Adare already is rather than what it could
become.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Cape Adare agrees to host — but strictly on its own terms. The gathering is contained and temporary,
kept away from crowding the historic core around the hut, run with the same deliberate pacing the city
already applies to its own growth (chain #9), rather than allowed to reshape the city's ambitions going
forward.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The gathering happens, genuinely national in consequence — and Cape Adare returns immediately afterward
to its ordinary, unhurried civic life. The event becomes a single, bounded, historic moment rather than a
first step toward the city trying to become any kind of political center.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Residents come to cite the gathering afterward as proof of something specific: that mattering nationally
and staying exactly what Cape Adare already chose to be aren't actually in tension — the city's core
"big city, small-town feel" identity (chain #3) reinforced by real, national-scale evidence rather than
threatened by it.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Cape Adare now holds two distinct kinds of historical weight — the founding "first place" significance
that predates the city itself, and a second, genuinely national-scale event it hosted once, deliberately,
and then just as deliberately let go of — real proof the city can matter enormously without needing to
become something else.
