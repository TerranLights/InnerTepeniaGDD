## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

As the exile-era communities scattered across the Ross Sea coast start needing more coordination than
informal contact provides, Cape Adare is known regionally as the place where Antarctica's human story
began — a genuine symbolic weight, but nothing anyone has yet put to practical use.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Someone proposes using Cape Adare's own symbolic weight — the first place — to host a formal regional
gathering, a real step up from lending the site sentiment alone. That proposal isn't uncontested: lending
a site this kind of gravity means treating "being first" as something more than a shared feeling, and not
every community scattered along the coast agrees that symbolic weight should translate into practical
authority, or that Cape Adare — a city with no dominant founding nation and no established leadership of
its own — is even positioned to host something this significant.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a representative from
another Ross Sea coast community advocating that Cape Adare's symbolic status makes it the natural,
neutral host for formalizing regional coordination, and a skeptic — possibly from Cape Adare itself —
questioning whether a city with no established leadership or governing tradition of its own is actually
equipped to host something this consequential, however symbolically fitting the location. This should
read as a genuine practical concern, not petty resistance: hosting matters, and "meaningful location"
and "capable host" aren't automatically the same thing.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a delegate from another
exile community along the coast, drawn to Cape Adare's symbolic weight and pushing for it as neutral,
meaningful ground specifically because it belongs to no single founding nation. Character B is plausibly
a Cape Adare resident or civic figure — shaped by the city's own consensus-built institutions
(Suggestion #1) — genuinely unsure the city's loose, negotiated governance style can handle hosting a
formal inter-community gathering without simply defaulting to the same slow consensus process that works
for local matters but might not scale to something this significant.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The skeptic's concern gets addressed rather than overridden: Cape Adare agrees to host, but explicitly as
neutral ground rather than as an active participant shaping the outcome — its own consensus-built civic
institutions (Suggestion #1) turn out to be exactly what makes it trustworthy as host, since a city with
no entrenched leadership of its own has nothing to gain by tilting anyone else's business in its favor.
Representatives convene at Cape Adare and formalize an early aspect of regional self-governance among the
Ross Sea coast communities.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Cape Adare picks up a second, quieter kind of historical weight — not just where Antarctica's human story
began, but a place other communities chose, on purpose, when something needed to be made official. The
city's own civic self-image starts incorporating "trusted neutral host" alongside "first landing site."

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that reputation as trusted neutral host now exists, Cape Adare becomes the default candidate
whenever the wider region later needs neutral ground again — a standing role the city didn't seek but
increasingly finds itself expected to fill, distinct from and layered on top of St. Carsten's Landing
(Suggestion #6) as a second, separate reason outside communities hold the city in particular regard.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Cape Adare now carries two distinct kinds of regional significance — the place where things began, and
the place other communities trust to host what needs deciding — a dual reputation built entirely on
symbolic weight the city never had to fight for, only prove worthy of once asked.
