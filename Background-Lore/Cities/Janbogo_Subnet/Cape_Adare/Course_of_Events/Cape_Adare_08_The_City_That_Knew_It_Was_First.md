## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

St. Carsten's Landing has become Cape Adare's signature holiday, its hybrid structure of historical
commemoration and communal celebration well-established and beloved — but every other "first" in the
city's history remains scattered, undated, and half-remembered, never having received the same treatment.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city this thoroughly built around precedence rarely stops at commemorating just one moment. Once
residents have a proven template for turning a historical first into a real civic celebration, they start
looking for other candidates — but the city's actual history offers no shortage of loosely-remembered
"firsts," most of them undocumented well enough to say with confidence exactly when they happened or
whether they even deserve the honor. The city needs some way to sort genuine historical weight from mere
nostalgia dressed up as significance.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between an enthusiastic advocate
pushing for a specific candidate "first" (the first joint civic decision, the first building beyond the

happens to have a compelling story attached. This is a dispute about historical standards, not personality
— both care about the city's history, but disagree on how rigorous the bar for "worth a holiday" should
be.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a community organizer
or storyteller, personally attached to a specific candidate first and eager to see it formally
recognized the way St. Carsten's Landing was. Character B is plausibly an archivist or historian in the
Manalo tradition, wary of civic sentiment overriding actual documented significance, and
protective of the credibility the city's existing observance already carries.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The skeptic's standard wins out as the process, not as a single verdict: a formal vetting process gets
established, requiring genuine documentation and demonstrable civic significance before any candidate
first earns its own observance — not every proposed first survives that bar, but the ones that do carry
real weight rather than borrowed sentiment from St. Carsten's Landing's own success.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The city ends up with a small, curated calendar of firsts — the first joint civic decision, the first
building beyond the hut itself, the first season the rookery and the settlement truly learned to coexist —
each one vetted by the same instinct that made St. Carsten's Landing matter in the first place, rather
than an ever-expanding list of increasingly minor anniversaries.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the vetting process itself now exists as a standing civic institution, it becomes the mechanism
Cape Adare reaches for generations later whenever a genuinely significant event needs formal historical
recognition, including — eventually — deciding how to properly commemorate hosting the region's Second
Gathering (Suggestion #7), rather than that moment simply passing into vague folk memory the way the
original landing anniversary nearly did before St. Carsten's Landing was formalized.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Cape Adare now maintains a small, rigorously vetted calendar of civic observances beyond St. Carsten's
Landing alone, with a standing process for evaluating future candidates — a city that didn't just
commemorate being first once, but built a lasting, disciplined civic habit out of recognizing its own
history properly.
