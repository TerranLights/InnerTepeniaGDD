## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Cape Adare's accessibility keeps drawing new arrivals faster than most Ross Sea coast cities ever see —
the same gateway advantage that shaped its founding (Suggestion #1) continues pulling in growth long
after the city is well established, with the pacing mechanisms built to protect its community-first
character (Suggestion #3) now facing their first genuinely large-scale test.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A specific, concrete opportunity arrives — a substantial wave of new arrivals or a major trade expansion
proposal — that would let Cape Adare grow dramatically faster than its existing pacing mechanisms are
built to accommodate. The city's whole self-image depends on staying unhurried and community-first, the
opposite of what runaway growth usually produces, but turning the opportunity away means deliberately
sacrificing real, measurable growth a more purely ambitious gateway city would simply take.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a civic leader responsible
for enforcing the city's existing growth-pacing mechanisms and an ambitious trade or infrastructure
advocate who sees the arriving opportunity as too valuable to turn down, however well-intentioned the
city's usual caution has been. This shouldn't be growth-is-bad-versus-growth-is-good; the advocate has a
genuine point that opportunities this size don't come often, and the civic leader isn't reflexively
anti-growth — they're specifically protecting a character the city has already invested real effort in
building (Suggestion #3), and they have real evidence that character is worth protecting.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic planner or
zoning administrator, the direct institutional inheritor of the pacing mechanisms established in
Suggestion #3, personally responsible for enforcing limits that are about to be tested at real scale for
the first time. Character B is plausibly a trade delegate or ambitious civic figure genuinely convinced
the opportunity in front of them is too significant to reflexively decline, framing the existing pacing
policy as untested against a challenge this large.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Civic leaders deliberately pace the opportunity rather than either fully accepting or flatly rejecting
it — spreading the new construction and integration out over a much longer timeline than the opportunity's
backers wanted, protecting the historic core around the hut from being crowded by expansion, accepting a
slower, smaller version of the opportunity rather than the trade delegate's original full-scale proposal.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The decision becomes Cape Adare's first genuinely large-scale, high-stakes test of its own pacing
mechanisms — proof, under real pressure rather than routine circumstances, that the policy established in
Suggestion #3 can actually hold when a serious opportunity is on the table, not merely when growth
pressure stays modest.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the pacing mechanism visibly held under real pressure this once, it stops being treated as an
untested civic ideal and becomes something residents and future administrators actually trust to hold
again — Cape Adare's civic leadership gains genuine confidence to keep turning away future opportunities
on the same principle, secure in the knowledge the approach has already been tested and hasn't failed.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Cape Adare ends up genuinely large and genuinely unhurried at once, its pacing mechanisms no longer just
policy but proven, tested practice — a city that has now demonstrated, under real pressure, that it will
keep choosing intimacy over unchecked opportunity whenever the two come into conflict.
