## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

For years after the Southern Cross expedition's historic 1899 overwintering, Cape Adare marks the
anniversary only informally, if at all — individual families and residents choosing, or not choosing, to
acknowledge the date in whatever way happens to occur to them.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city whose whole reason for existing traces to that landing eventually wants something more deliberate
than scattered, private observance — a growing sense, as Cape Adare's own civic identity solidifies
around "the place where Antarctica's human story began," that the anniversary deserves a shared, public
form rather than staying a matter of individual choice. But formalizing it means deciding, for the first
time, what the observance is actually supposed to involve — and no consensus yet exists on whether that
should mean solemn commemoration, community celebration, or something else entirely.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between someone advocating a solemn,
historically reverent form for the new civic holiday and someone advocating a warmer, communal
celebration in keeping with the city's own established unhurried, community-driven character. Both are
right about something real: the landing genuinely deserves historical weight, and Cape Adare genuinely
isn't a city built around solemnity as its default register. Neither position should be framed as more
legitimate than the other — the resolution has to actually blend them rather than pick a winner.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a historian or civic
elder invested in treating the observance with the gravity the founding moment deserves, possibly
connected to the same early heritage-documentation tradition behind the archive (Suggestion #4).
Character B is plausibly a community organizer or ordinary resident who wants the city's defining holiday
to actually feel like Cape Adare — warm, communal, unhurried — rather than a solemn civic exercise nobody
enjoys attending.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

February 17th is formally adopted as St. Carsten's Landing, a real civic holiday built around exactly the
blend the disagreement forced into being: a historically grounded core (readings, commemoration of the
1899 landing itself) wrapped inside a genuinely communal, warm civic celebration — solemn where the
history demands it, festive everywhere else.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The holiday's specific hybrid structure — historical core plus communal celebration — becomes Cape
Adare's template for civic observance generally, the model residents and organizers default to whenever
some other historically significant date later needs its own formal recognition.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that hybrid template already exists and already works, residents start actively looking for
other "firsts" in the city's own scattered, half-remembered history worth giving the same treatment —
not simply repeating St. Carsten's Landing, but applying its proven structure to new candidates, which is
exactly the impulse that later produces a small, deliberately curated calendar of additional civic
observances (Suggestion #8) rather than one holiday standing alone.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

St. Carsten's Landing is now Cape Adare's defining annual civic ritual — the one day even residents with
no personal stake in "firstness" take part in commemorating anyway — and its proven hybrid structure has
become the template the city reaches for whenever it wants to formalize a piece of its own history.
