# Cape Adare — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material (Cape Adare's own eventual destruction is out of scope here). 10 candidate But/Therefore
chains built from Cape Adare's Megasheet (the site of Borchgrevink's 1899 hut, the gateway city with
no dominant founding nation, the huge Adélie rookery, acoustic folk culture). Post-settlement only;
nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Whichever Ship Got Here First

Cape Adare's location makes it the most accessible Ross Sea landfall from open ocean. **But** that
means its founding population was never one organized settling group — just whoever happened to reach
it first. **Therefore** the earliest civic institutions form out of genuine improvisation between
communities with nothing in common but timing. **But** improvised cooperation between strangers with
no shared founding story could easily have stayed thin and transactional. **Therefore** it doesn't —
the very fact nobody arrived with an established claim to lead means every early institution gets
built by consensus from the start, setting the tone for a city that never developed one dominant
civic voice.

## 2. Neighbors, Not Tourists

Early residents share the Adare Peninsula with one of the largest Adélie rookeries on the continent —
too vast to displace even if anyone wanted to. **But** treating a quarter-million birds purely as
scenery or hazard leaves an obvious gap in how people actually live alongside them daily. **Therefore**
individual penguin-keeping quietly takes hold, formalized over time by residents like Rookery-Keeper
Tomás Widjaja into unofficial norms about which birds get taken in and which stay wild. **But** those
norms have to coexist with an undisturbed rookery ecosystem the city depends on continuing to thrive.
**Therefore** Cape Adare settles into a genuinely negotiated relationship — not conquest, not
sentiment, just two populations that learned to actually share the ground.

## 3. A Big City That Learned to Feel Small

Cape Adare's population grows into one of Tepenia's largest, well past a million residents. **But**
nothing about that scale comes from one unifying founding identity residents can rally around.
**Therefore** the city has to work, deliberately and continuously, to keep daily life feeling
unhurried and community-driven rather than letting sheer size flatten it into anonymity. **But**
that effort competes directly with the practical pressures of running a genuine gateway city — ships,
trade, constant new arrivals. **Therefore** "big city, small-town feel" becomes an actual civic
project residents take pride in sustaining, not a natural accident of modest size.

## 4. The Archive Before Anyone Needed It

Borchgrevink's 1899 hut sits at the literal center of the growing city, the physical reason it exists
where it does. **But** a structure that old, made of simple materials, isn't going to last forever on
reputation alone. **Therefore** a dedicated heritage-documentation tradition takes root early —
measurements, photographs, replica plans — long before anyone has a specific reason to think the hut
is actually at risk. **But** funding careful archival work in a practical, trade-focused gateway
economy is a hard sell without an obvious crisis to justify it. **Therefore** Archivist Elena
Manalo and her successors have to keep making the case, generation after generation, that
some things are worth documenting before you need to.

## 5. Every Instrument Someone Brought

Cape Adare's mixed, no-single-tradition population arrives carrying whatever instruments and musical
habits happened to travel with them. **But** with no dominant culture to set the terms, no single
tradition ever gets to define the city's sound. **Therefore** guitars, violins, cellos, and tagelharpas
end up sharing the same rooms, cross-pollinating into something genuinely new rather than any one
inherited style winning out. **But** blending that freely risks producing something shallow, a
sound with no real roots anywhere. **Therefore** the resulting folk-acoustic culture becomes something
residents are unexpectedly proud of — proof that a city built from scattered arrivals could still grow
something that sounds, unmistakably, like itself.

## 6. St. Carsten's Landing

For years after the Southern Cross expedition's historic 1899 overwintering, Cape Adare marks the
anniversary only informally, if at all. **But** a city whose whole reason for existing traces to that
landing eventually wants something more deliberate. **Therefore** February 17th is formally adopted as
St. Carsten's Landing, a real civic holiday rather than a loose date people vaguely remember.
**But** formalizing it means deciding, for the first time, what the observance is actually supposed to
involve. **Therefore** the holiday grows into the city's defining annual ritual — the one day even
residents with no personal stake in "firstness" take part in commemorating it anyway.

## 7. The Second Gathering

As the exile-era communities scattered across the Ross Sea coast start needing more coordination than
informal contact provides, someone proposes using Cape Adare's own symbolic weight — the first place —
to host a formal gathering. **But** lending the site that kind of gravity means treating "being first"
as something more than sentiment. **Therefore** representatives convene at Cape Adare to formalize
some early aspect of regional self-governance, deliberately choosing the location for what it means,
not just its convenience. **But** hosting something that significant changes how the city sees itself
afterward. **Therefore** Cape Adare picks up a second, quieter kind of historical weight — not just
where Antarctica's human story began, but a place other communities chose, on purpose, when something
needed to be made official.

## 8. The City That Knew It Was First

St. Carsten's Landing becomes Cape Adare's signature holiday, but a city this thoroughly built around
precedence rarely stops at commemorating just one moment. **But** every other "first" in the city's
history is scattered, undated, half-remembered. **Therefore** residents start deliberately identifying
and formalizing other historic firsts worth marking — the first joint civic decision, the first
building beyond the hut itself, the first season the rookery and the settlement truly learned to
coexist. **But** not every candidate "first" turns out to deserve its own holiday. **Therefore** the
city ends up with a small, curated calendar of firsts, each one vetted by the same instinct that made
St. Carsten's Landing matter in the first place.

## 9. Loud City, Quiet Growth

Cape Adare's accessibility keeps drawing new arrivals faster than most Ross Sea coast cities ever see.
**But** the city's whole self-image depends on staying unhurried and community-first, the opposite of
what runaway growth usually produces. **Therefore** civic leaders start deliberately pacing
development — spreading new construction out, protecting the historic core around the hut from being
crowded by expansion — rather than simply accommodating growth as fast as it arrives. **But** slowing
growth on purpose means turning away opportunities a more ambitious gateway city would take.
**Therefore** Cape Adare ends up genuinely large and genuinely unhurried at once, by continuous,
deliberate choice rather than by accident.

## 10. Elena's Insistence

Archivist Elena Manalo inherits a heritage-documentation tradition nobody has ever had to
defend before. **But** a new generation of civic administrators, focused on trade and growth, starts
questioning why scarce resources go toward measuring and photographing an old wooden hut instead of
funding the port. **Therefore** she has to make the case publicly, for the first time, that Cape
Adare's whole identity depends on this work continuing. **But** winning the argument once doesn't
settle it permanently — the same question resurfaces with every new budget cycle. **Therefore**
defending the archive becomes a recurring civic ritual of its own, quietly as much a part of the
city's character as St. Carsten's Landing itself.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#4 and #10** work as a matched pair — the archival tradition's founding, and the ongoing effort to
keep it funded and valued, giving Cape Adare's established heritage-consciousness real, lived
friction rather than treating it as automatic. **#1 and #3** together trace the city's whole
demographic arc: a founding with no organizing story, and the deliberate civic work required to keep
that lack of a unifying identity from ever curdling into anonymity at real scale.
