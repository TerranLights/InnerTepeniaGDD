## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Zukelli's food and music scene grows famous enough that Janbogo's own residents start taking real notice —
frequent crossings (Suggestion #6) mean Janbogo residents experience Zukelli's hospitality culture
directly and often, not merely by reputation.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Janbogo has its own established teahouse tradition and its own civic pride to defend — and as Zukelli's
reputation for hospitality grows more widely known across the subnet, some Janbogo residents start to
feel their own, equally real hospitality tradition is being overshadowed by comparison, a genuine, if
good-natured, sting to civic pride that surfaces directly whenever the two cities' residents cross paths
and start comparing notes.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a spirited, good-natured argument between a Zukelli
resident, confident their city's food-and-music culture is simply the superior hospitality tradition on
this stretch of coast, and a Janbogo resident, equally convinced their own teahouse tradition offers
something Zukelli's louder, more famous scene doesn't. Neither actually wants to concede the point, and
neither should — the disagreement works precisely because both traditions are genuinely, independently
excellent, and the "rivalry" is really just two proud communities enjoying the chance to defend something
they love.

restaurant-and-music-scene figure, perhaps someone connected to Elisa's Room's own reputation. Character B

but equally deep-rooted appeal.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side concedes, and the argument becomes a recurring, expected feature of cross-bay visits rather
than a one-time dispute — each city privately convinced its own hospitality culture is the real one,
neither ever quite willing to concede the point, repeated warmly every time residents of the two cities
meet.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

A genuine, good-natured rivalry develops between the two cities — residents on both sides start actively
comparing the two traditions, sometimes half-jokingly ranking specific venues or teahouses against each
other, keeping both traditions sharply, mutually aware of the other's continued excellence.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because both cities keep visiting each other constantly regardless of who's supposedly winning the
argument, the rivalry never curdles into anything harder — proximity and continued mutual attendance
(sustained directly by the Crossing tradition, Suggestion #6) keep functioning as a built-in check against
the rivalry ever souring, since neither side can actually maintain resentment toward people they keep
sharing meals and music with.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Zukelli and Janbogo settle into exactly the kind of relationship centuries of close contact tends to
produce — cooperative, competitive, and affectionate all at once, neither quality ever fully replacing the
others.
