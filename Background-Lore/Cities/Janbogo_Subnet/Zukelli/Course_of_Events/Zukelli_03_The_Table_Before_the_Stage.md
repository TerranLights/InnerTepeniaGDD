## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Zukelli's tangled, densely-built circumstances leave little room for grand civic architecture — no
sprawling public squares or dedicated civic halls the way other Tepenian cities might build, simply
because the organically grown layout (Suggestion #1) never set that kind of space aside.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A population living this close together, this informally, needs somewhere to actually gather — but with
no grand civic space available and no easy way to carve one out of an already dense, tangled built
environment, the question of where and how a growing population actually gathers becomes a genuine, unmet
civic need, one some residents want addressed through deliberate new construction and others think the
city should simply let solve itself.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between a civic-minded resident pushing
for the city to deliberately carve out and build genuine shared civic space — however difficult, given the
existing density — and a restaurant or eatery proprietor arguing that the city already has exactly what it
needs, informally, in the rooms where people already gather to eat, and that trying to build something
more formal would waste effort duplicating what already works. The civic advocate isn't wrong that a
dedicated shared space carries real symbolic and practical value; the proprietor isn't wrong that Zukelli's
density makes new construction of that kind genuinely difficult and possibly unnecessary.

gather in their space regardless of any formal civic plan.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The proprietor's position effectively wins by default — no dedicated civic space ever gets built, the
civic advocate's proposal quietly abandoned once it becomes clear how much disruption it would require,
and the city's actual daily gathering keeps happening exactly where the proprietor said it already was:
around food and shared tables.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Food and hospitality become the real center of civic life almost by default — restaurants and shared
meals filling the role other cities give to public squares or civic halls, not through any deliberate
civic decision but through the simple absence of any built alternative.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because civic life concentrated specifically around food rather than around any purpose-built gathering
space, whatever cultural expression grows out of that concentrated activity develops in direct proximity
to it — music grows directly out of the same rooms, the same tables, becoming an adjacent expression of the
identical hospitality instinct rather than a separate cultural tradition competing for space of its own.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Zukelli's civic life now runs entirely through food and hospitality, with music growing directly out of
those same rooms and tables — a civic culture built entirely without a dedicated public square, because
none was ever built, and none turned out to be needed.
