## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

A modest restaurant opens with a small stage tucked into one corner, nothing unusual by Zukelli's own
standards — one of many establishments already blending food and informal performance the way Suggestion
#3 established as ordinary civic pattern citywide.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Its proprietor, Elisa Faranda, treats the music and the food as genuinely equal parts of what she's
offering, rather than one as background to the other — an unusual level of commitment that starts drawing
real, sustained attention as her place develops a genuine reputation, first locally, then across the whole
subnet, with travelers from Janbogo specifically making the trip for it. That growing reputation puts real
pressure on Elisa to decide how to handle sudden demand a small establishment was never built to absorb.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** an internal conflict given external shape, echoing
Janbogo's own Majyao's Kettle (Suggestion #2 of that city's chains) but distinct in its specific stakes —
between Elisa herself, protective of the exact intimate quality that made her place special, and a business
partner or eager investor pushing her to expand, arguing that a reputation this strong, reaching all the
way to Janbogo, is being wasted on a room too small to meet its own demand. Elisa's hesitation isn't false
modesty; she has direct, personal knowledge that scaling up risks losing the very quality visitors are
actually traveling for. The partner's push is genuine business sense, not greed; turning away eager
travelers from another city is a real, visible cost.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly Elisa Faranda herself,
the established proprietor, whose personal judgment about her own establishment carries real weight.
Character B is plausibly a business partner, family member, or investor drawn by the venue's sudden
cross-subnet fame, well-meaning but focused on capturing the opportunity rather than preserving what
created it.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Elisa's instinct wins decisively — she declines every proposal to expand, scale, or relocate to a larger
space, choosing instead to simply let demand exceed capacity rather than compromise the room's actual
character, even at the direct cost of turning away travelers who made the trip specifically for her.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

A single restaurant can't scale its reputation without losing what made it special — so it doesn't try;
Elisa's Room stays exactly what it always was, deliberately small, becoming famous precisely by refusing to
become anything else.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Elisa's Room stayed deliberately small rather than absorbing the demand directly, the overflow of
travelers who can't get in has to go somewhere — other Zukelli eateries, previously ordinary, start
picking up visitors originally drawn by Elisa's reputation alone, spreading the same cross-subnet
attention across multiple venues rather than concentrating it in one place, and quietly strengthening the
whole city's music-and-food reputation rather than just one restaurant's.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Elisa's Room stands as Zukelli's single most famous venue, deliberately, permanently small — its overflow
demand having spread genuine cross-subnet attention across the wider hospitality scene it originally grew
out of, rather than concentrating fame in one place alone.
