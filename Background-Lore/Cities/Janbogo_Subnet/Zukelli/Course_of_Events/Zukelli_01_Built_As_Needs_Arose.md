## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Zukelli's earliest settlers build shelter and infrastructure wherever the immediate need is, with no
master plan guiding the layout — a founding pattern nobody objects to while the settlement is still small
enough for everyone to hold its shape in memory.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city built this way risks becoming genuinely unnavigable as it grows past what any one person can hold
in memory — and as Zukelli's population expands past its earliest years, that risk stops being
theoretical. Newer residents start getting lost in their own city, and a real argument surfaces over
whether to intervene before the problem compounds further: impose some retroactive order on the layout
now, while it's still comparatively manageable, or accept that fixing it after the fact would cost more
than simply living with it.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between a civic-minded resident pushing
for some retroactive planning intervention — official signage, a rationalized grid overlay in newer
districts, anything to arrest the compounding disorientation — and a resident, likely one whose own
livelihood or home would be disrupted by any retroactive reorganization, arguing that imposing order now
would cost more, in demolished structures and dislocated lives, than simply letting the city keep growing
the way it always has. The planner's concern is legitimate: unchecked, the problem only gets worse. The
resident's resistance is equally legitimate: retroactive planning in an already-dense, organically grown
city means real, concrete harm to real people's homes and businesses.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a newer civic
administrator or urban-minded resident, frustrated by the layout's growing unnavigability and drawn to
more conventional planning solutions seen elsewhere in Tepenia. Character B is plausibly a longer-settled
resident or small business owner, whose home or livelihood is built directly into the existing tangled
layout and who has real, concrete stakes in it staying exactly as it is.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The resident's resistance wins — no retroactive planning intervention ever goes forward, the compounding
disorientation accepted as the price of not disrupting real homes and livelihoods, and the tangled street
pattern keeps compounding anyway, generation after generation.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because no top-down fix was ever imposed, the problem the planner warned about doesn't resolve itself
either — a city too disorienting for its own residents to navigate isn't actually functional, and the
unresolved tension between growth and navigability keeps pressing on the city's daily life.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the problem was never solved from above, it gets solved from below instead — Zukelli develops its
own internal logic, not a grid imposed from above but a lived-in coherence only residents ever fully
learn, growing directly out of the same unresolved pressure the planner's failed proposal never actually
addressed.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Zukelli's tangled layout now carries its own internal, lived-in coherence — exactly the kind of organic
growth that reads as chaos to outsiders and home to everyone who actually lives there, a solution the city
found for itself only after a top-down fix was deliberately rejected.
