## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Italian exiles set Zukelli's earliest civic institutions in place, shaping the whole city's founding
character — hospitality-first civic values, food-and-music culture, the whole organically-grown
sensibility already established in Suggestions #1 through #5, all rooted in that founding population's
own instincts.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Later waves of Chinese and American immigration eventually outpace the founding population demographically,
settling Italy into Significant tier rather than Primary — and as the numbers shift, some of the founding
Italian community start to worry openly about what happens to the civic template they built once they're
no longer the city's largest population, while newer arrivals wonder whether they're now expected to
simply inherit customs that were never originally theirs.

**Character A:** *(left blank)*

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between a founding-population Italian
resident, anxious that Zukelli's civic character will fade once Italian demographic dominance ends, and a
newer Chinese or American resident who has already, personally, embraced the city's existing customs —
crossing to Janbogo, eating at Elisa's Room, contributing to the music scene — and finds the anxiety
somewhat beside the point, since they already consider these customs genuinely their own. The founding
resident's worry is understandable: demographic dilution has eroded founding culture elsewhere in
Tepenia. The newer resident's confidence is equally well-founded: they aren't merely tolerating Zukelli's
customs, they've actually adopted them as their own daily practice.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an older resident from
Zukelli's founding Italian population, personally remembering when the community was still numerically
dominant. Character B is plausibly a newer Chinese or American resident, arrived well after the founding
era, who has genuinely and personally taken up the city's existing customs without any sense of
performing an inherited role.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The conversation itself becomes the resolution — the founding resident, seeing firsthand how thoroughly
the newer resident has actually internalized Zukelli's customs, rather than merely observing them from
outside, has their anxiety directly and personally answered, not through any formal reassurance but simply
by witnessing it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The early institutions, already deeply embedded in daily practice by the time demographic dilution
happens, keep functioning exactly as before — carried forward by a population that inherited them through
ordinary participation rather than through any continued numerical majority.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the founding-era civic character survives through lived practice rather than demographic
dominance, Zukelli's civic identity becomes something no future population shift can easily threaten
either — a durability that specifically depends on the customs already having been embedded deeply enough,
before the demographic shift happened, to survive being carried forward by people with no ancestral
connection to their origin.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The founding-era civic character persists regardless of population, carried forward by a population that
inherited it through ordinary participation rather than through any continued numerical majority — proven,
not merely assumed, by the direct, personal reassurance one anxious founding-era resident actually
received.
