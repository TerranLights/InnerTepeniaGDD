# Zukelli — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material (Zukelli's own eventual destruction and everything that followed it is entirely out of scope
here; these chains cover only the living city). 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Zukelli's
Megasheet (the tangled, organically-grown hospitality city, its centuries-long relationship with
Janbogo across the bay, its huge genre-diverse music scene). Post-settlement only; nothing here is
canon.

---

## 1. Built As Needs Arose

Zukelli's earliest settlers build shelter and infrastructure wherever the immediate need is, with no
master plan guiding the layout. **But** a city built this way risks becoming genuinely unnavigable as
it grows past what any one person can hold in memory. **Therefore** the tangled street pattern keeps
compounding anyway, generation after generation, since fixing it retroactively would cost more than
simply living with it. **But** a city too disorienting for its own residents to navigate isn't
actually functional. **Therefore** Zukelli develops its own internal logic instead — not a grid
imposed from above, but a lived-in coherence only residents ever fully learn, exactly the kind of
organic growth that reads as chaos to outsiders and home to everyone who actually lives there.

## 2. Marked by Neighborhood

Covered connections and heating-pipe conduits thread between Zukelli's buildings, sheltering movement
from the katabatic winds. **But** with no citywide plan behind any of it, finding your way through
someone else's neighborhood means genuinely not knowing where you are. **Therefore** individual
neighborhoods start marking their own passages — color, pattern, whatever convention a given block
happens to settle on — an organic, bottom-up system nobody mandates and everybody eventually
understands. **But** the system only works if you already know somebody who can teach you to read it.
**Therefore** newcomers learn Zukelli's geography the same way they learn everything else about the
city: through relationships, not through any signage a stranger could simply follow alone.

## 3. The Table Before the Stage

Zukelli's tangled, densely-built circumstances leave little room for grand civic architecture, but
plenty of room for something smaller and warmer. **But** a population living this close together,
this informally, needs somewhere to actually gather. **Therefore** food and hospitality become the
real center of civic life almost by default — restaurants and shared meals filling the role other
cities give to public squares or civic halls. **But** hospitality this central to daily life doesn't
stay quiet for long. **Therefore** music grows directly out of the same rooms, the same tables,
becoming an adjacent expression of the identical hospitality instinct rather than a separate cultural
tradition competing for space.

## 4. Elisa's Room

A modest restaurant opens with a small stage tucked into one corner, nothing unusual by Zukelli's own
standards. **But** its proprietor, Elisa Faranda, treats the music and the food as genuinely equal
parts of what she's offering, rather than one as background to the other. **Therefore** the place
develops a real reputation, first locally, then across the whole subnet — travelers from Janbogo
specifically making the trip for it. **But** a single restaurant can't scale that reputation without
losing what made it special. **Therefore** Elisa's Room stays exactly what it always was, deliberately
small, becoming famous precisely by refusing to become anything else.

## 5. Green Against the Dark

Zukelli's engineers face the same katabatic winds and Antarctic dark every neighboring city contends
with. **But** pure survival infrastructure — heat, shelter, insulation — leaves the city feeling
purely functional, nothing like the warmth its hospitality culture is already building toward.
**Therefore** elaborate plantlines and elevated bridger-footroads get engineered into the built
environment, artificial but genuinely effective, giving residents something resembling equatorial
nature to walk among. **But** maintaining living green space against Antarctic conditions takes real,
continuous engineering effort most cities wouldn't bother with. **Therefore** Zukelli commits to it
anyway, turning basic infrastructure into something closer to a permanent, deliberate contrast with
the dark just outside the heating envelope.

## 6. The Crossing

Zukelli and Janbogo sit close enough across the same bay that visiting back and forth becomes
practical almost from the start. **But** neither city ever formalizes the relationship into anything
official — no treaty, no scheduled exchange, just informal, constant contact. **Therefore** a genuine
tradition takes hold over generations: residents crossing casually, visits blurring into each other
until it stops being clear which city is hosting which. **But** that kind of informality only survives
if both sides keep choosing to maintain it. **Therefore** the Crossing becomes something both cities
actively sustain rather than a geographic accident — proof that proximity alone doesn't explain a
relationship this warm, only the centuries of choosing to keep crossing that bay explains it.

## 7. Rivals Who Never Really Competed

Zukelli's food and music scene grows famous enough that Janbogo's own residents start taking real
notice. **But** Janbogo has its own established teahouse tradition and its own civic pride to defend.
**Therefore** a genuine, good-natured rivalry develops — each city privately convinced its own
hospitality culture is the real one, neither ever quite willing to concede the point. **But** the
rivalry never curdles into anything harder, since both cities keep visiting each other constantly
regardless of who's supposedly winning. **Therefore** Zukelli and Janbogo settle into exactly the kind
of relationship centuries of close contact tends to produce — cooperative, competitive, and
affectionate all at once, neither quality ever fully replacing the others.

## 8. A Template That Outlasted the Founders

Italian exiles set Zukelli's earliest civic institutions in place, shaping the whole city's founding
character. **But** later waves of Chinese and American immigration eventually outpace the founding
population demographically, settling Italy into Significant tier rather than Primary. **Therefore**
Zukelli could plausibly have seen its founding civic template fade along with the founding population's
numbers. **But** the early institutions were already deeply embedded in daily practice by the time
that dilution happened. **Therefore** the founding-era civic character persists regardless, carried
forward by a population that inherited it through ordinary participation rather than through any
continued numerical majority.

## 9. What You Brought to the Table

Zukelli's population arrives from genuinely varied backgrounds, each bringing its own musical habits
and traditions along. **But** with hospitality already the city's organizing civic principle, none of
those traditions stay confined to their own community for long. **Therefore** Zukelli's music scene
grows huge and genre-diverse specifically because it develops through restaurants and shared tables
rather than separate, community-specific venues — different traditions mixing simply because
everyone eats in the same rooms. **But** that kind of constant mixing risks producing something with
no distinct identity of its own. **Therefore** it doesn't — the blend becomes recognizably Zukelli's
own sound, distinct from anywhere else on the coast, precisely because of how thoroughly it mixed.

## 10. Zukellesi, Wherever You Go

"Zukellesi" starts as nothing more than the ordinary demonym for anyone born or living in the city.
**But** Zukelli's specific hospitality-and-warmth civic character becomes distinctive enough,
recognized widely enough across Tepenia, that the word starts meaning something beyond simple
residency. **Therefore** people elsewhere start using "Zukellesi" informally to describe anyone who
carries that same warmth and hospitality instinct, whether or not they were ever actually a resident.
**But** a word that spreads past its original meaning risks losing its connection to the actual city
behind it. **Therefore** Zukelli's own residents keep the distinction alive themselves, quietly proud
that their city's name became shorthand for a whole way of treating people, even as the word travels
further than most of them ever will.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#3, #4, and #9** trace the food-and-music civic identity across three scales — the general civic
pattern, one specific famous venue, and the population-wide mixing that produced it — giving Zukelli's
established hospitality culture an actual developmental arc. **#6 and #7** work as a pair, giving the
Megasheet's own "cooperative, competitive, affectionate, entangled" description of the Janbogo
relationship two concrete, lived mechanisms (informal visiting, good-natured rivalry) rather than
leaving it as an abstract summary.
