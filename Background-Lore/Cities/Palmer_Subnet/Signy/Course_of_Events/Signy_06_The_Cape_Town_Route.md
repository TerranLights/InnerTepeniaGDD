## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Signy's founding population arrives partly via a route no other Palmer subnet city shares — a South
African arrival current running through Cape Town to the South Orkneys, distinct from the founding
patterns that shaped every neighboring settlement.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A single arrival route this different from everyone else's founding story could easily leave the South
African-descended community feeling disconnected from the rest of the subnet's shared founding
narrative — no natural point of comparison, no neighboring city that arrived the same way to trade
stories with. That isolation becomes a live question inside the city itself once the community is
established enough to start asking what its own identity is actually going to be: something held apart
as its own distinct heritage, or something folded into the wider founding population around it. Being
the only city with this specific founding current at all means there's no external model to follow
either way — Signy has to work this out for itself.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** an internal disagreement within Signy's founding
population, not a conflict with any outside community — there's no neighboring city sharing this
arrival route to have the argument with instead. One side wants the South African thread formally
preserved and marked as its own distinct heritage: language, custom, and memory of the Cape Town route
kept recognizably intact, out of the same loyalty that makes any exile community want to hold onto
where it actually came from. The other side worries that a city this small can't sustain a genuinely
separate cultural track without fragmenting what's still a very young, still-forming settlement, and
would rather see that heritage become simply part of everyone's shared civic character rather than a
distinct thing requiring its own space.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an elder or
organizer from the South African-descended founding cohort, personally invested in making sure the Cape
Town route's specific memory doesn't simply dissolve into a broader founding story that doesn't
actually include it. Character B is plausibly a civic-unity-minded figure — not necessarily hostile to
that heritage, but responsible for holding a small, newly-settled population together, worried that
formal separateness now becomes real division later.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither preservation-as-enclave nor simple absorption wins outright. Signy's South African-descended
community settles into treating its own distinctiveness as simply part of the city's particular
character — a demographic thread unique to Signy, worn without needing to explain or apologize for it,
present everywhere in ordinary civic life rather than set apart in its own institutions. Signy's civic
identity develops with real independence as a result, shaped by a founding story genuinely its own
rather than echoing any neighbor's.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the South African thread gets woven into ordinary city life rather than preserved separately,
its influence shows up diffusely rather than in any single dedicated institution — turns of phrase at
the dock, particular fishing and food customs, surnames and small domestic habits that trace back to
that founding current without anyone needing to flag them as such. The heritage becomes ambient rather
than exhibited.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that heritage was folded in rather than marked as separate, later generations — and visitors —
sometimes fail to recognize how significant the Cape Town route actually was to Signy's founding,
precisely because nothing about the city singles it out as distinct. The very success of the
integration makes the thing it integrated easy to overlook: a founding current unlike any other Palmer
subnet city's own story, hiding in plain sight inside a city that never treated it as anything but
ordinary.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Signy's civic identity stands genuinely apart from every neighboring Palmer subnet city, built on a
founding current — the Cape Town route — that belongs to Signy alone. That distinctiveness runs through
the whole city's ordinary life rather than living in any one preserved tradition, real enough to shape
who Signy actually is, and easy enough to miss that even the city's own residents rarely stop to name
it.
