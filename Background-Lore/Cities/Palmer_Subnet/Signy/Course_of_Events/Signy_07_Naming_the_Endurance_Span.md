## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The bridge connecting Signy's two islands has served its practical function for years without any
formal name — residents simply cross it, twice a day, calling it nothing more specific than "the
bridge."

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The South Orkney Islands sit close enough to the historic 1916 Endurance expedition's own open-boat
route that Signy's connection to that story runs deeper than most Tepenian cities' St. Ernest
veneration — this isn't a general spiritual thread borrowed secondhand, it's a history that happened,
geographically, more or less where Signy itself now stands. Residents eventually move to name the
bridge directly after that history: the Endurance Span, honoring the same act of persistence the city's
strongest spiritual thread already reveres. But naming a piece of daily, unremarkable infrastructure
after something this historically weighty risks feeling like an odd mismatch — grand history draped
over a structure people cross every day without a second thought.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over whether the name actually
fits, surfacing once it's proposed rather than before. One side wants the weighty historical name
specifically because the crossing is daily and ordinary — the whole point, to them, is that residents
live inside the history rather than merely visiting it once a year. The other side worries that
invoking a genuinely desperate, life-or-death crossing for something as mundane as the commute to the
dock either trivializes the history or saddles an ordinary structure with more solemnity than daily
life can comfortably carry. Both sides take St. Ernest veneration seriously; they disagree about what
honoring it well actually looks like.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly someone closely tied
to Signy's St. Ernest veneration specifically — a keeper of that tradition, or simply someone who feels
the Endurance story's proximity to Signy's own geography deeply and wants it made visible in the city's
everyday landscape. Character B is plausibly a more practically-minded resident or administrator, not
dismissive of the history but wary of overreach, preferring a plainer, more functional name for
something people are going to cross in a hurry, in bad weather, without ceremony, most days of their
lives.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The historical name wins out, adopted as the Endurance Span — and B's worry doesn't come true.
Residents grow into the name rather than feeling burdened by it: the daily crossing becomes, without
anyone forcing the comparison, a small, safe echo of the same journey the name commemorates. The weight
of the history and the routine of the commute end up sitting comfortably together rather than in
tension.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the name took hold and the comparison felt natural rather than imposed, small informal habits
start forming around the crossing itself — residents pausing for a beat before stepping onto the
bridge, a particular phrase some exchange in passing, nothing official or mandated, just the kind of
small personal ritual that accumulates around a place once people start feeling something when they use
it.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because those informal daily habits already exist, they become the raw material later generations draw
on when the city wants something more deliberate than a name and a private habit — an actual annual
observance. The vocabulary and instinct for how to mark this specific crossing meaningfully are already
sitting in the community's daily practice by the time anyone proposes a formal holiday, making that
later observance feel like a natural extension of something Signy was already quietly doing, not an
invented tradition grafted on from outside.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The bridge is the Endurance Span now, its name fully absorbed into how Signy talks about and
experiences its own daily crossing. Small, informal personal rituals already surround the act of
crossing it — a foundation, not yet a formal civic institution, but exactly the kind of groundwork a
future annual observance would need in order to feel earned rather than invented.
