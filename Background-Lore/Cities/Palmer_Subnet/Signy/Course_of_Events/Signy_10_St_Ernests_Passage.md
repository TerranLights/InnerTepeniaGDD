## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Generations after the Endurance Span is named, residents want something more than the bridge itself to
mark their connection to the historic 1916 crossing — a name and a daily habit of crossing it feel, by
now, like they've earned something more deliberate.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A bridge alone, however meaningfully named, isn't quite the same thing as an actual observance — a name
commemorates once, passively, every time someone happens to notice it, where an observance asks
something of people on purpose, once a year. A specific commemorative practice takes hold in response:
St. Ernest's Passage, timed to the anniversary of the Endurance crew's own historic open-boat journey,
held at the bridge itself. But a holiday this specific to one historical event risks feeling
disconnected from anyone who isn't already familiar with the full story — and Signy's population,
generations on, includes plenty of people who never learned it firsthand.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over who the observance is actually
for. One side wants St. Ernest's Passage kept as it started — a solemn, insider observance for people
who already understand its full weight, worried that explaining the story over and over, every year, to
people who don't already know it cheapens or flattens something that's supposed to be genuinely felt.
The other side is thinking about the city's own newer residents and its own children, worried that a
holiday nobody explains eventually becomes a holiday nobody but the oldest residents actually
understands — meaningful in theory, hollow in practice, for everyone the tradition hasn't already
reached.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a longtime keeper of
the tradition — someone who has observed St. Ernest's Passage since it began and feels protective of
its solemnity, wary of turning a genuine commemoration into a rehearsed lesson. Character B is plausibly
someone responsible for integrating newcomers into Signy's civic life — a teacher, a dockside organizer,
someone who has watched newer residents or younger Signians stand at the edges of the observance without
understanding what it's actually for.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The two roles don't end up in competition — the observance absorbs both. St. Ernest's Passage becomes
something residents actively explain to newcomers rather than assume everyone already knows,
functioning as both commemoration and, every year, a small act of teaching the story forward again to
whoever hasn't heard it yet.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the explaining itself becomes a built-in part of the observance rather than an afterthought, a
concrete pedagogical shape forms around it — a designated storyteller or elder retells the Endurance
crossing at the start of each year's Passage, with newer residents and children specifically brought
forward to hear it before the wider community observance begins.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that formal retelling happens every year, without fail, it becomes, over generations, Signy's
primary living mechanism for passing down its own founding-era history at all — not just the Endurance
story specifically, but the wider habit of oral historical transmission that the retelling models.
Given Signy's own weak, radio-based connection to wider Federation archives and record-keeping, St.
Ernest's Passage ends up mattering more to how Signy remembers itself than any written archive would
for a city this disconnected — an annual holiday functioning, in practice, as the city's actual
historical record.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

St. Ernest's Passage stands as Signy's central annual civic-spiritual event, its yearly retelling doing
double duty as the city's primary means of preserving and passing down its own founding history —
genuinely vital for a city whose isolation leaves it without the archival and communication resources a
better-connected city could otherwise depend on to remember itself.
