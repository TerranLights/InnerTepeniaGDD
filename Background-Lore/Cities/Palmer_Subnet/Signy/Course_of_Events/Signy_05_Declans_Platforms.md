## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Signy's fishing economy depends entirely on how much of the surrounding water residents can actually
reach from the coastline itself — and the coastline, however rich the waters beyond it, sets a hard
limit on how far that reach can extend.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Platform Engineer Declan Ferreira-Whitcombe proposes a way past that limit: floating extension
platforms, built to push the fishing economy's reach out to sea wherever the surrounding geography
allows it. The idea promises real growth for a city whose whole economy runs on what it can pull from
the water. But the Scotia Sea's own harsh, gale-driven conditions mean maintaining floating
infrastructure this exposed takes real, sustained engineering attention — nothing built out there stays
safe by accident, and the crews who would actually work the platforms are the ones who'd pay for any
shortcut.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between ambition and safety that
neither side can honestly dismiss. Declan's case is real: the platforms mean genuine growth for a city
that badly needs it, proof Signy can extend its own reach deliberately rather than simply accepting
what the coastline gives it. The opposing case is equally real: whoever actually works the platforms
once they're built is the one exposed to the Scotia Sea's conditions, not the engineer who designed
them, and a rushed or under-tested build risks lives for a return that hasn't yet been proven. This
should read as two people who both want the platforms to succeed, disagreeing hard about what "ready"
actually means.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly Declan himself,
confident in the engineering and eager to see the fishing economy's reach actually grow rather than
stay theoretical. Character B is plausibly a fishing crew leader or foreman — someone who will have
people working these platforms in weather Declan gets to observe from land, and who wants real input
into the design and a genuine safety standard before any crew sets foot on one.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The platforms get built, but only after the foreman's demands are folded directly into the design and
the working process — proper safety protocols, a maintenance rotation crews trust rather than tolerate,
fisher input baked into the engineering rather than added afterward. Once proven reliable, the
platforms become one of Signy's genuine points of civic pride: concrete proof that a city this small
and this remote could extend its own reach on purpose, rather than simply living within the coastline's
natural limits.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the platforms were built through genuine collaboration between engineering and the fishing
crews rather than handed down from one side to the other, Signy develops something new: joint
fisher-engineer maintenance teams, workers who aren't purely fishers or purely engineers but something
in between, trained across both skill sets because the platforms themselves demanded it.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that combined fisher-engineer expertise now exists as a standing workforce, it becomes the pool
Signy draws on whenever anything exposed to the same harsh open-water conditions needs building or
repairing — not just the platforms themselves, but eventually the bridge's own maintenance, the
wind-and-hydro system's most exposed components, anything the Scotia Sea threatens. A skill set built
to solve one specific problem quietly becomes the city's general answer to the hardest maintenance
problem it has: keeping anything built out in the open weather actually working.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Signy's fishing economy now reaches well beyond its own coastline, sustained by floating platforms the
whole city takes real pride in. The joint fisher-engineer workforce that maintains them has become,
without anyone planning it that way, the city's go-to expertise for any infrastructure exposed to the
open Scotia Sea — a resource the platforms created that now serves purposes well beyond fishing alone.
