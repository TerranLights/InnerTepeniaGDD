## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Signy's earliest supply ships arrive on an irregular, weather-dependent schedule, never quite
predictable — the community has no way to know exactly when the season's first ship will actually
reach the dock, only that it will, eventually, when conditions allow.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A small, isolated settlement depending entirely on that supply has real reason to watch closely for its
arrival, and that reason becomes a shared practice: waiting for the season's first ship turns into
something residents mark deliberately, gathering to watch together rather than each household simply
checking the horizon on its own schedule. But a practice this informal, born purely out of practical
necessity, could easily fade once the supply chain becomes more established and the ship's arrival
stops being genuinely uncertain — there'd be nothing forcing the habit to continue past the point it
stops being strictly needed.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement, once supply becomes more reliable,
over whether the communal watch is still worth keeping at all. One side sees a practice that's outlived
its practical function — time and labor that could go back to ordinary work instead of a whole
community pausing together for something that's no longer genuinely uncertain. The other side isn't
arguing practicality at all; they're arguing that the value the watch provides was never purely
practical to begin with, and that dropping it the moment it stops being strictly necessary would cost
the city something real that has nothing to do with supply logistics.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a younger or more
efficiency-minded resident, or a logistics-minded administrator, for whom continuing a now-unnecessary
communal ritual reads as simple inertia rather than meaningful practice. Character B is plausibly an
older resident, or someone who has come to value what the gathering itself does for the community — the
shared attention, the sense of a whole city waiting on the same thing together — independent of whether
the ship's arrival is still actually in doubt.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The community chooses, deliberately rather than by default, to keep the practice going. The Long Watch
persists as genuine civic ritual — residents still gathering to watch for the season's first ship long
after doing so together stops being strictly necessary, the choice to continue made consciously rather
than simply left unquestioned.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because that choice was made deliberately — a practical necessity kept on purpose after the necessity
itself faded — Signy establishes something broader than just one surviving custom: a working civic
instinct that a practice earning its keep once doesn't have to justify itself again every year on pure
efficiency grounds. What started as pure necessity becomes the city's first clear example of preserving
something for what it means, not just for what it does.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that instinct is now established rather than merely implicit, it becomes the reference point
later invoked whenever Signy has to decide, about some other aging habit or practice, whether to let it
lapse once its original function fades. The Long Watch stops being just one surviving tradition among
many and becomes the city's working precedent — the example residents point back to when arguing that a
meaningful practice deserves to outlast the necessity that first created it.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The Long Watch endures as one of Signy's genuine civic rituals, residents still gathering for the
season's first ship's arrival with the same attention their earliest, most supply-anxious ancestors
once needed out of pure necessity. The city now carries, as an established working instinct, a
demonstrated willingness to keep meaningful practices alive past the point where necessity alone would
justify them.
