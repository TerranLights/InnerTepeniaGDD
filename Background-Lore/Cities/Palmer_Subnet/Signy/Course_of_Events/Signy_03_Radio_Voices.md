## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Signy's own remote position leaves it the weakest Arcanet connection point anywhere in the Federation
— simply too far south, too far from anything, for the network to reliably reach. Whatever the rest of
Tepenia takes for granted about staying digitally connected, Signy cannot assume for itself.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city this isolated still needs some way to reach the rest of the country, so residents build their
whole communications infrastructure around old-fashioned radio instead — a technology that doesn't
depend on the same network the Arcanet does, and doesn't care how far south Signy happens to sit. But
committing to radio means accepting, permanently, a slower and less reliable channel than
Arcanet-connected cities take for granted — messages that arrive in minutes elsewhere might take hours
or days to reach or leave Signy, if they get through cleanly at all.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over how much to keep investing in
the losing battle for a stronger Arcanet signal versus committing fully to radio as Signy's real,
permanent answer. This isn't reformer-versus-obstructionist — both sides want Signy genuinely connected
to the rest of Tepenia. One side worries that formally settling for radio means accepting second-class
status, a city that looks, from the outside, permanently behind everyone else. The other side thinks
that worry is exactly the trap: continuing to chase an Arcanet signal that physically cannot reach this
far wastes effort Signy could instead spend building real expertise in the technology that actually
works here.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic figure
concerned with how Signy is perceived by the rest of the Federation — someone who has felt the sting of
being the subnet's weakest link and wants that fixed, or at least not quietly accepted. Character B is
plausibly whoever ends up running Signy's actual radio equipment day to day, someone with hands-on
experience of what the technology can and can't do, impatient with effort spent chasing a signal that
isn't coming and confident that radio, properly invested in, is a perfectly real answer rather than a
consolation prize.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's practical case wins out. Signy commits its communications effort and resources fully to radio
rather than continuing to chase Arcanet parity with the rest of the Federation, and the city simply
adjusts its own expectations around the limitation that follows — messages take longer here, and
everyone understands that as normal, not as an unsolved problem waiting for someone to fix it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because radio becomes the deliberate, permanent center of Signy's outside communication rather than a
stopgap, the people who operate it stop being generalists filling in on equipment nobody else wants to
touch and start becoming genuine specialists — a small, recognized class of radio operators with real
technical standing in the city, based out of Signy Island's dock-side comms setup.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that operator class exists and holds real expertise, they become, in practice, the only
reliable channel between Signy and the rest of Tepenia — anyone in the Federation who genuinely needs
to reach Signy, or Signy anyone else, ends up going through them specifically. That concentration of
access gives Signy's radio operators a disproportionate civic importance for a small handful of people:
not formal political power, but a quiet gatekeeping role over the city's one working link to the
outside world, one that outlasts whoever first builds the equipment.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Signy's connection to the rest of Tepenia runs entirely through radio, staffed by a small, genuinely
skilled operator class the city has come to depend on for anything that needs to leave the island or
arrive from outside it. The city has stopped measuring itself against Arcanet-connected neighbors'
speed of contact — slower is simply what reaching Signy means, and the people who make that slower
connection work at all now hold a quiet importance the city's earliest settlers never planned for.
