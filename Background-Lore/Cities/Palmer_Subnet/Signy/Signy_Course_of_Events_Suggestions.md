# Signy — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material (Signy's own wartime survival through isolation, and the post-war siligel shortage that
isolation later causes, are both out of scope here). 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from
Signy's Megasheet (the two-island structure, the floating fishing platforms, the weak Arcanet link,
St. Ernest veneration). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Two Islands, One Bridge

Signy's earliest settlers find their site split naturally between two landmasses — small Signy Island,
with the one usable shipping dock, and much larger Coronation Island beyond it. **But** a city split
across two islands with no way to move easily between them risks never functioning as one place at
all. **Therefore** a bridge gets built connecting them, letting residents live on the larger island
while still working the smaller one daily. **But** committing to one single crossing means the whole
city's daily rhythm now depends on that one structure holding. **Therefore** the bridge becomes,
immediately, one of Signy's most vital pieces of infrastructure — the literal, physical link holding
the city's two halves together as one functioning whole.

## 2. Whatever the Wind Provides

Signy's remote, gale-exposed position offers no realistic connection to any wider power grid. **But**
a growing settlement still needs reliable electricity for its docks, its radio equipment, and its
homes. **Therefore** residents build a local wind-and-hydro system matched specifically to Signy's own
conditions — abundant, near-constant wind rather than the sunlight a lower-latitude island might rely
on instead. **But** a system this small and this isolated has no outside backup if something fails.
**Therefore** maintaining it becomes a genuine, ongoing civic priority, residents treating their own
local power generation as something the whole community has to actively sustain together, not
something they can simply take for granted.

## 3. Radio Voices

Signy's own remote position leaves it the weakest Arcanet connection point anywhere in the Federation
— too far for the network to reliably reach. **But** a city this isolated still needs some way to
communicate with the rest of Tepenia. **Therefore** residents build their whole communications
infrastructure around old-fashioned radio instead, a technology that doesn't depend on the same
network the Arcanet does. **But** relying on radio means accepting slower, less reliable contact than
Arcanet-connected cities take for granted. **Therefore** Signy simply adjusts its own expectations
around that limitation — messages take longer here, and everyone understands that as normal rather
than as a problem waiting to be fixed.

## 4. Naledi's Research

Signy's founding population inherits a genuine biological-science mission from its real-world BAS
station heritage. **But** a small, remote community has limited capacity to sustain ambitious ongoing
research indefinitely. **Therefore** Dr. Naledi van Zyl-Osei takes up the work anyway, continuing the
original marine-and-terrestrial biology mission into the exile era, her own background drawing
directly on Signy's uniquely South African founding-wave presence. **But** sustaining serious research
in a city this isolated means working largely without the resources a better-connected city's
scientists could draw on. **Therefore** her research develops its own resourceful, self-reliant
character — genuine science conducted with whatever the island itself actually provides.

## 5. Declan's Platforms

Signy's fishing economy depends entirely on how much of the surrounding water residents can actually
reach from the coastline itself. **But** the coastline alone limits how far that reach can extend.
**Therefore** Platform Engineer Declan Ferreira-Whitcombe designs floating extension platforms,
pushing the fishing economy's reach out to sea wherever the surrounding geography allows it. **But**
maintaining floating infrastructure against the Scotia Sea's own harsh conditions takes real, ongoing
engineering attention. **Therefore** the platforms become one of Signy's genuine points of civic
pride — proof that a city this small and this remote could still extend its own reach, deliberately,
rather than simply accepting the coastline's natural limits.

## 6. The Cape Town Route

Signy's founding population arrives partly via a route no other Palmer subnet city shares — a South
African arrival current running through Cape Town to the South Orkneys. **But** a single arrival
route, distinct from every neighboring city's own founding pattern, could easily leave that population
feeling disconnected from the rest of the subnet's shared founding story. **Therefore** Signy's South
African-descended community instead treats that distinctiveness as simply part of the city's own
particular character — a demographic thread unique to Signy, worn without needing to explain or
apologize for it. **But** being the only city with this specific founding current means fewer natural
points of comparison with any other Palmer subnet community. **Therefore** Signy's own civic identity
develops with real independence, shaped by a founding story genuinely its own rather than echoing any
neighbor's.

## 7. Naming the Endurance Span

The bridge connecting Signy's two islands serves its practical function for years without any formal
name. **But** the South Orkney Islands sit close enough to the historic 1916 Endurance expedition's
own open-boat route that Signy's connection to that story runs deeper than most Tepenian cities'
St. Ernest veneration. **Therefore** residents eventually name the bridge directly after that
history — the Endurance Span, honoring the same act of persistence the city's strongest spiritual
thread already reveres. **But** naming a piece of daily infrastructure after something this
historically weighty risks feeling like an odd mismatch. **Therefore** it doesn't — residents come to
see their own daily crossing as a small, safe echo of the same journey the name commemorates, the
weight and the routine sitting comfortably together.

## 8. The Long Watch

Signy's earliest supply ships arrive on an irregular, weather-dependent schedule, never quite
predictable. **But** a small, isolated community depending on that supply has real reason to watch for
its arrival closely. **Therefore** waiting for the season's first ship becomes something residents mark
deliberately, watching together rather than each household simply checking on its own. **But** a
practice this informal could easily fade once the supply becomes more reliable over time. **Therefore**
it doesn't — The Long Watch persists as genuine civic ritual, residents gathering to watch for the
first arrival of the season long after it stops being strictly necessary to do so together.

## 9. Nobody's In a Rush

Signy's earliest daily rhythm follows the same wildlife cycles the surrounding waters and coastline
run on — fishing seasons, breeding cycles, weather windows. **But** a growing settlement could easily
try to impose a faster, more standardized schedule on top of those natural rhythms instead. **Therefore**
residents let the wildlife cycles set the pace regardless, work and rest genuinely paced by the
island's own natural timing rather than any imported urgency. **But** a civic rhythm this unhurried
looks, to visitors from busier cities, like the whole place is simply behind. **Therefore** Signy
never bothers correcting that impression — nobody here is in a real rush, and residents feel no need
to prove otherwise to anyone passing through.

## 10. St. Ernest's Passage

Generations after the Endurance Span is named, residents want something more than the bridge itself to
mark their connection to the historic 1916 crossing. **But** a bridge alone, however meaningfully
named, isn't quite the same as an actual observance. **Therefore** a specific commemorative practice
takes hold — St. Ernest's Passage, timed to the anniversary of the Endurance crew's own historic
open-boat journey, held at the bridge itself. **But** a holiday this specific to one historical event
risks feeling disconnected from everyone who isn't already familiar with the full story. **Therefore**
the observance becomes something residents actively explain to newcomers rather than assume everyone
already knows — St. Ernest's Passage functioning as both commemoration and, every year, a small act of
teaching the story forward again.

## 11. Not Everything the Sea Provides

A specific fish population central to Signy's own catch declines sharply one season, without warning or
any clear human cause — the kind of ordinary ecosystem unpredictability the city had quietly stopped
expecting. **But** the decline forces a real disagreement: whether it's a genuine wake-up call demanding
real contingency, or ordinary fluctuation not worth overreacting to. **Therefore** the city settles on a
modest middle path — a small stored reserve and a wider spread of species targeted across the floating
platforms, a real but bounded hedge rather than a full reorganization. **But** the fish populations
recover on their own not long after. **Therefore** the hedge stays anyway, absorbed quietly into how the
city simply operates from then on — Signy's own understanding of its self-sufficiency shifting from an
unexamined assumption into something more honestly earned.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#1 and #7** work as a pair — the founding decision to bridge Signy's two islands, and the later
naming that gives that same bridge its full civic and spiritual weight. **#4 and #6** together trace
Signy's uniquely South African demographic thread from its founding-era arrival route to a specific,
living expression of it in one resident researcher's own work. **#11** is the only chain that tests
Signy's central, load-bearing assumption — that the sea simply provides — directly, giving the city's
established post-war self-sufficiency theme a genuine pre-war precedent rather than leaving it
unexamined until the war forces the question.
