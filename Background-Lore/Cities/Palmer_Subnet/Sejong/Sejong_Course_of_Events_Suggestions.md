# Sejong — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material (Sejong's own eventual destruction is out of scope here). 10 candidate But/Therefore chains
built from Sejong's Megasheet (the dense multinational island city, the boundary-zone architecture,
diplomatic coordination as civic art form). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Nowhere to Spread

Sejong's exile community settles on King George Island alongside nearly a dozen other national
communities, all sharing the same small, crowded landmass. **But** the island simply doesn't have the
room for any of them to sprawl the way cities elsewhere in Tepenia do. **Therefore** Sejong builds
vertically instead — dense, stacked construction rather than spreading outward into territory that
doesn't exist. **But** building up rather than out means every structural decision has to account for
neighbors on every side, not just one. **Therefore** verticality and constant negotiation become the
same problem solved twice — Sejong's whole built environment and its whole diplomatic culture growing
out of the identical physical constraint.

## 2. Absorbing Without Announcing It

Several smaller national footholds dot King George Island alongside Sejong's own growing settlement.
**But** none of them ever grows large enough to sustain full independent city status the way Sejong
does. **Therefore** their populations gradually fold into Sejong's own civic structure as the city
becomes the island's largest, most organized settlement — each community keeping a distinct
neighborhood identity within Sejong rather than a separate municipal one. **But** absorbing multiple
distinct populations without erasing their character takes real, deliberate accommodation. **Therefore**
Sejong ends up holding genuine internal diversity most cities never develop — not one population, but
several, sharing one civic address without ever fully merging into one.

## 3. The Boundary Zones

Sejong's territory meets its island neighbors' own footholds at multiple points, none of them clearly
defined at first. **But** leaving those meeting points ambiguous invites exactly the kind of ongoing
friction a dozen adjacent communities can't afford. **Therefore** the boundary zones develop
deliberately — architecturally transitional areas rather than hard walls, genuine negotiated space
where Sejong's own territory gives way to whichever neighbor happens to be adjacent. **But**
maintaining that kind of soft, negotiated boundary takes continuous diplomatic attention, never fully
settled once and forgotten. **Therefore** boundary-zone maintenance becomes one of Sejong's own
defining, ongoing civic skills — a form of coordination built entirely out of necessity, refined
constantly rather than resolved once.

## 4. A Tower Nobody Owns

King George Island's dense harbor traffic needs reliable maritime signaling, the same practical
requirement any coastal settlement faces. **But** building that signal tower under any single national
community's exclusive control risks turning a shared necessity into a contested asset. **Therefore**
the combined lighthouse-and-signal spire gets built as genuinely neutral infrastructure instead —
visible from every community's own footholds, serving all of them equally, claimed exclusively by
none. **But** neutral infrastructure still needs someone to actually build and maintain it.
**Therefore** its construction and upkeep become one of the few projects every island community
contributes to jointly — proof that shared necessity can produce genuine cooperation even among
communities otherwise competing for the same limited ground.

## 5. Competing for the Same Sun

King George Island's comparatively mild climate makes certain stretches of ground genuinely more
desirable than others. **But** with a dozen communities sharing one small island, favorable ground is
never abundant enough for everyone to simply have their own. **Therefore** a real, ongoing competitive
tension develops around exactly the terrain everyone wants most — the mild climate that should be a
shared blessing instead becoming a specific, persistent source of friction. **But** that tension never
escalates into anything the island's practical coexistence can't absorb. **Therefore** Sejong and its
neighbors settle into an uneasy but functional equilibrium — real competition for the best ground,
managed well enough that daily life and shared infrastructure keep working regardless.

## 6. What Han Ji-woo Negotiated

Sejong's earliest years require some way to actually formalize how the island's communities will share
land and resources, before informal arrangements harden into resentment. **But** no single community
has the standing to simply dictate terms to all the others. **Therefore** Coordinator Han Ji-woo takes
on the work of negotiating genuine, mutual boundary-zone agreements directly with each neighboring
community, one relationship at a time. **But** securing agreement from a dozen separate communities
takes far longer and far more patience than negotiating with just one. **Therefore** the process
stretches across years rather than resolving quickly — a foundational, unglamorous diplomatic
achievement that later generations inherit without necessarily appreciating how much work it actually
took.

## 7. Hangul, Kept Alive

Sejong's own Korean founding population dilutes steadily as later immigration reshapes the city's wider
demographic balance. **But** the city's own name and civic identity trace directly back to King Sejong
the Great's creation of Hangul — a heritage worth preserving regardless of population share. **Therefore**
Hangul instruction and cultural continuity persist deliberately, carried forward by dedicated
educators even as the founding nation's own numbers shrink relative to the growing multinational whole.
**But** sustaining a heritage tradition for a shrinking founding population takes real, ongoing
commitment from people increasingly outnumbered by newer arrivals. **Therefore** the tradition survives
anyway — smaller in scale, genuinely maintained, never treated as obsolete just because the numbers
backing it have changed.

## 8. Building Toward the Same Point

Early construction on Sejong's own territory happens somewhat haphazardly, each new structure added
wherever space allows. **But** a genuinely shared landmark — the island's one neutral signal spire —
gives builders something to actually orient around. **Therefore** Sejong's layout gradually organizes
itself in relation to that shared point, boundary zones settling into place around it like spokes
meeting at a hub rather than scattering arbitrarily. **But** a layout this organic takes generations to
fully settle into a legible shape. **Therefore** by the time it does, residents navigate the resulting
geometry instinctively, the same unplanned-but-coherent order every dense, negotiated city eventually
develops.

## 9. Everyone's Distinct, Nobody's Alone

A resident of one of Sejong's absorbed neighborhood communities could easily feel like a guest inside
someone else's city, tolerated rather than truly belonging. **But** Sejong's own founding logic never
organized itself around one dominant culture assimilating everyone else. **Therefore** each community
retains real, visible distinctness — food, language, custom — while still participating fully in
Sejong's shared civic life, neither erased nor kept at arm's length. **But** holding that balance
requires constant, deliberate effort from a city that could easily default to picking a single
dominant identity instead. **Therefore** Sejong keeps choosing the harder path, generation after
generation — plural without being fractured, unified without demanding sameness.

## 10. The Accord

Years of informal, ad-hoc boundary-zone negotiation eventually produce something residents want to mark
formally — the original land-use and resource-sharing agreements that made coexistence physically
workable in the first place. **But** commemorating a practical agreement risks implying the deeper
social tension it never fully resolved was settled too. **Therefore** The Neighbors' Accord takes
shape deliberately narrow — a holiday honoring the practical success specifically, without claiming the
underlying competition for good ground was ever fully put to rest. **But** drawing that distinction
takes real honesty about what the city has and hasn't actually achieved. **Therefore** the holiday
becomes something genuinely earned rather than aspirational — Sejong celebrating exactly what it
accomplished, and quietly living with what it didn't.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#3 and #6** trace Sejong's defining civic skill — boundary-zone diplomacy — from its earliest,
most urgent necessity to the specific negotiator credited with formalizing it. **#4 and #8** work as a
pair, giving the Megasheet's own proposed shared landmark (the lighthouse spire) both a founding reason
to exist and the concrete effect it has on the city's later physical geometry.
