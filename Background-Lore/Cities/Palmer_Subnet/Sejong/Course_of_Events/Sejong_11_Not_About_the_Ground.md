## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Sejong's practical coexistence works. The boundary zones hold, the shared signal tower stands neutral and
jointly maintained, The Neighbors' Accord is celebrated honestly for what it actually achieved. But
between two specific adjacent communities, something from the original boundary-zone negotiations never
actually got resolved — a sense, quietly held on one side, that their own community came out of Han
Ji-woo's careful, years-long process with less favorable ground than the negotiation's own stated
fairness should have produced.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A joint project — repairs to a shared piece of infrastructure, or a minor boundary adjustment neither
community can avoid — forces the same two communities back into the kind of close, direct coordination
the original negotiation required. The old grievance, never actually spoken aloud in all the years since,
resurfaces the moment they're back at the same table.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a conflict about whether the original Accord was
procedurally fair — it was, by every formal measure. Character A carries the older, quieter conviction
that fair process and fair outcome aren't the same thing, and that their own community's comparative
disadvantage, however small, was never actually acknowledged, just processed. Character B genuinely
believes the matter was settled, in good faith, generations ago, and finds it disorienting — not
dismissive, genuinely disorienting — to learn the other side never fully accepted that.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an elder or a
community representative old enough to remember (or to have inherited a clear account of) the original
negotiation firsthand. Character B is plausibly a counterpart from the neighboring community, similarly
senior, whose own understanding of the Accord's fairness has simply never been tested until now.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Nothing is renegotiated. The joint project proceeds. But Character B, for the first time, actually
acknowledges the asymmetry out loud — not a formal apology, not a reopened settlement, just a real,
spoken admission that the original process, however fair by its own standards, still left one side with
less. Character A accepts the acknowledgment as what it is: real, but not a fix.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The exchange becomes a known, quietly referenced precedent — not a change to any boundary or agreement,
but a small, real model for how Sejong's communities handle a grievance procedural fairness never
actually settled: naming it honestly, without pretending naming it fixes anything.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Over time, residents start pointing to this specific incident whenever the difference between Sejong's
functional coexistence and its genuine emotional resolution comes up — living proof, rather than abstract
principle, that the two really are different things, and that the city has learned to hold both
honestly rather than collapsing one into the other.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Sejong's coexistence remains exactly what the Full Extrapolation proposed it should be — genuinely both
a real success and a real, incomplete reckoning — except now with one concrete, lived precedent residents
can actually point to, instead of only the abstract, citywide shape of the tension.
