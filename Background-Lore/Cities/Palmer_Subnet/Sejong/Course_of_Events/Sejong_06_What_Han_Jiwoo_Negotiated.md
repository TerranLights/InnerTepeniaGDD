## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Sejong's earliest years require some way to actually formalize how the island's communities will share
land and resources, before informal, day-to-day arrangements harden into resentment nobody chose but
everyone inherits.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

No single community has the standing to simply dictate terms to all the others — any settlement imposed
unilaterally would be resisted by everyone it wasn't imposed by. So a coordinator from Sejong's own
community takes on the work of negotiating genuine, mutual boundary-zone agreements directly with each
neighboring community, one relationship at a time, rather than trying to broker one grand multilateral
settlement all at once. But securing agreement from a dozen separate communities takes far longer and far
more patience than negotiating with just one — the process stretches across years, and by the time most
communities have settled, one holdout remains, unwilling to accept terms comparable to what everyone else
has already agreed to.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a negotiation between the coordinator (the
"Han Ji-woo" role) and the leader of the one community still refusing to settle, after every other
community has already accepted comparable terms. This shouldn't read as one side being reasonable and the
other simply obstinate: the holdout's resistance is legible if their community arrived early, holds a
genuinely strong prior claim to their particular stretch of ground, or fears that accepting terms designed
for a dozen communities at once erases whatever advantage their own earlier arrival should have earned
them. A wants uniform, fair terms that hold the whole agreement together; B wants recognition that "fair"
applied identically to everyone isn't actually fair to a community with a materially different history.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is the coordinator figure — patient,
methodical, personally invested after years of exactly this same negotiation with every other community on
the island, running low on both time and goodwill by the time this last conversation happens. Character B
is plausibly the elder or founding representative of whichever community has the strongest independent
claim to being on the island before most others — a Russian or Polish community works well here, given
their real-world stations' own comparatively early historical presence on King George Island.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The holdout community becomes the last to sign — but not on identical terms to everyone else. It wins one
specific, meaningful concession: permanent recognition of a particular boundary landmark or use-right tied
to its own earlier claim, something none of the other agreements needed to include. With that concession
granted, the full set of boundary-zone agreements across the island is finally complete.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Having personally completed all dozen agreements, the coordinator's role stops being a temporary task and
becomes a permanent civic office — a standing "Coordinator" position tasked with maintaining what was
negotiated, rather than a one-time assignment that simply ends once the last signature is collected.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the Coordinator's office persists as a standing institution rather than disbanding once the
original agreements were signed, it becomes the office later, larger negotiations — a shared island
landmark's construction, eventual formal commemoration of the original agreements — get routed through,
rather than each new island-wide question starting its own negotiation from nothing.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The office of Coordinator, first held personally by the negotiator who closed all twelve agreements, is
now a permanent fixture of Sejong's civic structure — a foundational, unglamorous diplomatic achievement
later generations inherit as simple fact, largely without appreciating how much patient, one-relationship-
at-a-time work it actually took to build.
