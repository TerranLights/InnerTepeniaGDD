## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Sejong's territory meets its island neighbors' own footholds at multiple points around its edges, none of
them clearly defined — informal lines everyone has simply been observing by habit rather than by any
actual agreement.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Leaving those meeting points ambiguous invites exactly the kind of ongoing friction a dozen adjacent
communities sharing one small island can't actually afford — a fence moved a few meters here, a path
worn slightly wider there, no single incident serious enough to demand a reckoning but each one adding to
a running tally of small, unaddressed grievances. Someone eventually has to propose an actual answer, and
the answer that gets proposed is itself contentious: whether these edges should be drawn hard and final,
once and settled, or kept soft and negotiable, revisited as circumstances change.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over what kind of boundary actually
solves the problem — someone who wants a fixed, surveyed, permanent line (the appeal of finality: no more
ambiguity, no more small daily disputes) against someone who wants the boundary left deliberately soft and
periodically renegotiated (the appeal of flexibility: circumstances on a crowded island keep changing, and
a line fixed today will be wrong again within a generation). A's position is reasonable because ambiguity
really has been causing real friction; B's position is reasonable because a hard line, once drawn, has no
way to adapt to a growing or shifting population without a fight every time it needs to move.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly someone who has
personally been burned by an ambiguous dispute — a builder or household whose own plans were disrupted by
an undefined edge, now converted to wanting permanent clarity. Character B is plausibly an early
coordinator or go-between figure, someone already informally doing the work of talking neighboring
communities through their disagreements, who understands from direct experience that the island's
communities need room to keep adjusting, not a line that locks today's balance of power in place forever.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The soft-boundary model wins the argument, but not by simply overriding the case for clarity — the
compromise is to make the boundary zones themselves genuinely, formally negotiated space: architecturally
transitional areas rather than hard walls, with an agreed process for revisiting them rather than an
agreed permanent line. Ambiguity gets replaced not with finality, but with a defined, ongoing practice for
managing exactly the kind of change B was arguing a fixed line could never accommodate.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the boundary zones now require ongoing management rather than a one-time survey, the informal
go-between role B occupied during the negotiation turns into something closer to a standing responsibility
— a recognized liaison function, however unofficial at first, that specific individuals are known to hold
between Sejong and each of its adjacent communities.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that liaison function persists and gets exercised repeatedly, it becomes the practical training
ground for the kind of citywide negotiator the island's whole coexistence eventually depends on — the
specific skills of patient, one-relationship-at-a-time boundary diplomacy get learned and refined in this
role long before anyone holds a title broad enough to negotiate on behalf of the whole city at once.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Boundary-zone maintenance is now a recognized, ongoing civic function rather than an ad hoc habit —
practiced by a small handful of individuals whose accumulated skill at managing soft, renegotiated edges
quietly makes them the most experienced diplomats Sejong has, long before any of them holds a formal
citywide office for it.
