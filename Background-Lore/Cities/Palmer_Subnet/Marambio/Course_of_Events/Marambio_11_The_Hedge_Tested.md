# Marambio — The Hedge, Tested
### Featuring [Character A] and [Character B] (placeholder figures — unnamed)

**Course of Events Suggestion #11**, translated from `Marambio_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` into
`z-template_-_city_histories_conflict_variant.md`. Grounded in the Mega_Init's own explicitly flagged,
still-open question drawn from the Gander parallel: whether Marambio's runway centrality was ever
genuinely threatened by an emerging rival logistics option, the way Gander's real chokepoint status
evaporated once jets gained non-stop range. Chain #10 treats the shipyard as a proactive hedge against a
hypothetical risk; this suggestion is about that hedge actually being tested by a real threat. Character

## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Marambio's runway is the Palmer subnet's central aviation hub, moving people and goods between Tepenian
cities. The shipyard, developed later as a deliberate hedge against over-reliance on any single function
(chain #10), still plays second fiddle in most residents' minds — the airfield remains the assumed core
of what Marambio *is*, the shipyard a sound but secondary insurance policy nobody's actually had to cash
in.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Newer aircraft, with meaningfully greater range, begin making direct point-to-point flights between more
distant Tepenian cities practical without a refueling or transfer stop at Marambio — the same
technological shift, in miniature, that ended Gander's real-world indispensability once jets could cross
the Atlantic non-stop. Marambio's runway isn't obsolete, but it's no longer the unavoidable chokepoint it
once was, and traffic through it visibly, measurably thins.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a conflict about whether the shift is real —
both sides can see the traffic numbers. Character A represents aviation-identity loyalty: Marambio has
always been the airfield city first, and this figure wants the city to fight for that centrality directly
— lobbying for routing requirements, defending the runway's primacy as a matter of civic identity as much
as economics. Character B represents the shipyard's own quieter confidence: the hedge chain #10 already
built exists precisely for a moment like this, and the right response isn't to resist the shift but to
lean into it, letting the airfield's role recalibrate to what it's actually still needed for — internal
Tepenian logistics specifically — while civic investment and attention shift increasingly toward the
port's genuinely global reach.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a veteran
airfield administrator or air-traffic figure, someone whose own career and sense of Marambio's identity
was built entirely around the runway's centrality. Character B is plausibly a shipyard administrator or
a planner connected to the original dual-mode hedge decision (chain #10) — not dismissive of the
airfield's value, but genuinely unbothered by its diminished centrality, having expected something like
this all along.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The city doesn't fight the technological shift. Investment and civic attention shift decisively toward
the shipyard, while the airfield's role is openly recalibrated to what it's actually still essential for
— internal Tepenian logistics between cities, not a chokepoint the wider Federation has no alternative
to.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Marambio's own civic self-image begins to shift for the first time — from an airfield city that happens
to also run a shipyard, toward a city that genuinely holds two co-equal functions at once. Chain #10's
hedge stops being a cautious contingency nobody had tested and becomes something the city can point to
and credit directly for the outcome.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Over successive generations, Marambio's whole civic identity settles into genuine dual-primacy rather
than airfield-first-with-a-secondary-shipyard — the same integrated, non-split relationship to both
functions already found, in miniature, in the city's own Duisburg-descended population, now becoming the
city's general, shared self-understanding rather than one population's particular civic register.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Marambio holds its two functions as genuinely equal pillars, having already lived through — and
absorbed — the exact kind of single-function vulnerability chain #10 was originally built to hedge
against. Where Gander's real-world story ends in quiet decline once its chokepoint status vanished,
Marambio's doesn't, precisely because the hedge existed and was allowed to matter when the moment
actually came.
