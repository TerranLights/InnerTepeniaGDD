## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Marambio's fossil discovery starts as a scientific curiosity, interesting mainly to the researchers
directly involved — a specialist concern sitting quietly alongside a civic calendar built entirely around
practical, function-first observances.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The full scale of what the Eocene record actually reveals — a warmer, forested Antarctica existing long
before any ice — turns out to be significant enough that researchers and their advocates start to feel it's
something the whole city should mark, not just a specialist footnote. But pushing for a formal civic holiday
means asking for calendar space and legitimacy in a city whose holidays and public rituals have always
centered on tangible, work-honoring observances — and a scheduling-minded civic administrator, protective of
Marambio's operational rhythm, resists adding any observance built around abstract scientific significance
rather than demonstrated function.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A compromise, not a clean win for either side: the holiday is adopted, but designed deliberately to fit
Marambio's practical character rather than override it — a quiet, modest science-appreciation day, not a
full operational shutdown, timed to the anniversary of the discovery that first confirmed the fossil
record's full scope, observed with minimal disruption to airfield and shipyard schedules.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the holiday was designed minimally and quietly rather than as a grand civic event, it never
develops the kind of large-scale public ceremony or parade culture a more central holiday might — it stays
a modest, specific tradition built around quiet reflection and small exhibits rather than a citywide
celebration demanding real civic investment.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because it never grew into a major event, The Warm Ground comfortably coexists on Marambio's calendar
alongside any later aviation-focused observance (see Suggestion #7) without competing for the same civic
attention or resources — letting the city hold two very different commemorations side by side without
either one crowding out the other, a direct consequence of how modestly this one was built in the first
place.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Marambio's calendar now includes The Warm Ground, a small, deliberate science-appreciation holiday honoring
the fossil record specifically — modest by design, coexisting easily alongside the city's practical,
function-first civic rhythm rather than competing with it.
