## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Generations of later immigration steadily dilute Argentina's own founding population at Marambio,
eventually down to the deepest erosion documented anywhere in the Palmer subnet — the nation that built and
staffed the original airfield reduced to a tiny fraction of the city that now runs it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The operational discipline built into the city's earliest institutions doesn't dilute at the same rate its
founding population does — Marambio keeps running with the same rigorous, function-first character
generations after Argentina itself becomes a tiny minority presence, the culture persisting independent of
who's actually staffing the operation now. For most of that time nobody in Marambio's own established
culture particularly notices or names the persistence — it simply continues, unremarked. But eventually
someone does notice, and how differently two residents react to noticing it is what actually surfaces the
question: is this inherited discipline still meaningfully *theirs* to keep, or is it just an old habit
nobody's bothered to examine?

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a newer-generation resident
who questions why the city still enforces a specific, demanding operational standard with no remaining
demographic connection to the nation that created it, and a veteran of the system who insists the standard
should be kept regardless of ancestry. This isn't a nationalism-versus-progress conflict — neither
character has any real tie to Argentina by this point. It's a question about whether inherited institutions
need remembered justification to keep functioning, or whether demonstrated results are justification
enough on their own. A wants either a reason or a relaxation; B has no interest in the reason, only in the
results.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a younger dockworker or
airfield staffer, part of the now-dominant later-immigration demographic, chafing at standards that read to
them as arbitrary military-style rigor with no living connection to explain it. Character B is plausibly a
veteran instructor or shift supervisor, themselves of no Argentine descent either, who came up entirely
through Marambio's own training tradition (see Suggestion #1's voluntary training track, or Suggestion #2's
charter) and simply doesn't see ancestry as relevant to whether the standard is worth keeping.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The disagreement gets tested rather than argued out: A's push to relax a specific procedure gets tried, and
the corner-cutting produces a real, if contained, near-miss during a shipment or flight — not catastrophic,
but serious enough to prove the standard was never empty ritual. A concedes the practical point. But neither
side ever traces the practice back to Fariña or to Argentina specifically in the process — the argument gets
settled entirely on the discipline's present-day function, with its actual origin never entering the
conversation at all.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the resolution came entirely through demonstrated function rather than through anyone tracing the
practice back to its founding-era origin, Marambio never develops the kind of self-aware civic narrative
Janbogo has built around its own founding-culture persistence — the discipline's "why" stays functionally
justified (it works, therefore keep it) but historically unexamined (nobody asks where it actually came
from).

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the discipline's origin stays unexamined rather than named, Marambio never builds any ceremonial or
archival institution — no founders' museum, no oral-history program — dedicated to preserving *why* the
standard began. If the practical justification for the discipline (the airfield and shipyard actually
needing it) were ever removed, the city would have no independent memory left to explain the practice on
any other terms — the inheritance would simply have nothing left to fall back on.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Marambio's rigor now runs entirely on demonstrated present-day function rather than on any remembered
connection to its Argentine military origin — Fariña's name and the discipline's actual founding source
essentially forgotten even as the practice itself lives on, unexamined, exactly as it always has.
