## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The reclaimed airfield runs well on inertia for its first years once repaired — routines and habits set by
the founding ex-Air Force exiles carrying the operation along without anyone having to actively decide to
keep it that way.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Inertia alone doesn't sustain an operation this demanding indefinitely, and it's already showing its
limits: a growing civilian population is arriving alongside the founding ex-Air Force exiles, and that
civilian population has no automatic reason to adopt military-grade operational standards just because
they happen to be living next to a runway. Comandante Ezequiel Fariña sees the drift starting and responds
by deliberately extending the base's own disciplined culture across the whole settlement — not just
runway procedure, but treating operational rigor itself as a civic value every resident should hold,
whether or not they work the airfield directly. That's a real overreach in the eyes of a civilian
population that came to Seymour Island to build a life, not to be folded into someone else's chain of
command.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between Fariña himself, extending
military-grade discipline into civilian civic life as a deliberate policy, and a civilian figure who
resents having standards designed for uniformed service applied to people who never enlisted in anything.
This shouldn't play as authoritarian villain versus righteous rebel — Fariña's motivation is genuinely
protective (he's watched infrastructure decay before and knows what happens to a runway nobody disciplines
themselves to maintain), and the resister's motivation is genuinely legitimate (civilians who fled one kind
of imposed structure shouldn't have to accept another, even a well-intentioned one, without being asked).
Discipline imposed without buy-in rarely survives its own imposer — so the interesting version of this
conflict is one where Fariña has to actually win people over, not simply command them.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is Fariña — a founding-era Air Force
officer whose authority is real but whose jurisdiction, strictly speaking, ends at the base's own personnel.
Character B is plausibly a respected civilian settler-organizer, someone the exile community already
listens to on questions of how to build their new life, who sees Fariña's push as a boundary violation even
while privately admitting the airfield genuinely does need the standards he's asking for.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Fariña doesn't win through authority — he wins through demonstration. A genuine operational crisis (a
weather emergency, an equipment failure, a strained resupply) puts the settlement's safety directly in the
hands of whoever follows procedure correctly under pressure, and the outcome makes unmistakably clear that
disciplined habit, not good intentions, is what actually keeps people alive on Seymour Island. The civilian
resistance doesn't collapse in an instant, but it erodes for real reasons rather than through command — and
Fariña's standards take root anyway, becoming genuinely embedded civic character rather than merely
enforced policy.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the standards took root through lived demonstration rather than command, the settlement's own
founding council writes operational rigor directly into Marambio's earliest civic charter — not as military
law imposed from outside, but as a self-authored civilian commitment, the community formally choosing to
hold itself to the standard Fariña first demonstrated rather than having it imposed indefinitely from
above.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the commitment is written into the settlement's own self-authored charter rather than resting on
Fariña's personal authority, it survives his eventual death or departure without any crisis of succession —
new arrivals are acculturated to it through the charter and the settlement's own institutions, not through
any individual's continued enforcement, letting the discipline persist as genuinely impersonal civic
character rather than as one man's legacy that could have simply faded with him.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Marambio's operational discipline is now a self-authored civic institution, formally written into the
settlement's own founding charter and credited to Comandante Ezequiel Fariña — no longer dependent on any
single person's continued presence to enforce it, and already structurally positioned to outlast whoever
happens to be running the city at any given time.
