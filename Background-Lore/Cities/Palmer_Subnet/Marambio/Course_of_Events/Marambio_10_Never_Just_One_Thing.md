## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Marambio's early planners could have let the airfield remain the city's whole logistics identity, content
with being simply an aviation hub — a plausible, simpler path the settlement hasn't yet committed to
abandoning.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Relying on one single mode of transport leaves a city vulnerable to anything that changes the specific value
of that one function, and a strategically-minded figure raises the case explicitly — arguing Marambio needs
a second logistics mode as insurance against exactly that kind of vulnerability, whatever form it might
eventually take. But that collides with practical reality: a young, still-developing settlement genuinely
can't easily fund and staff two major infrastructure projects at once, and committing to both risks doing
neither one well enough to matter, jeopardizing the airfield's own hard-won operational rigor in the
process.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a planner arguing for
deliberate diversification as long-term insurance, and a resource-conscious administrator worried that
splitting the young settlement's limited investment across two infrastructure projects risks a half-built
failure on both fronts instead of one solid success. A's case rests on foresight about vulnerabilities that
haven't happened yet and might never happen — a genuinely harder case to win than B's, which only has to
point at the settlement's real, current resource limits. This conflict works best when B isn't simply
short-sighted, but making the more immediately defensible argument.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a strategically-minded
planner or administrator, someone thinking in terms of the settlement's long-term durability rather than its
immediate needs. Character B is plausibly a resource-conscious administrator, potentially overlapping with
the airfield-protective figure from Suggestion #4, who measures any proposal against what the young
settlement can actually afford to build well right now.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The diversification argument wins, but not recklessly — the two sides settle on a staged, deliberate
build-out rather than an all-at-once gamble: the shipyard gets developed in careful phases alongside,
never instead of, continued airfield investment, satisfying B's caution about doing both badly while still
committing to A's hedge against future vulnerability.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the shipyard build-out proceeds in careful, staged phases rather than as a single rushed initiative,
Marambio develops real, transferable planning and project-management expertise in large-scale phased
infrastructure development — a genuine institutional competency, not just an incidental outcome of building
the shipyard.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because Marambio now has genuine, demonstrated experience managing large-scale, staged infrastructure
investment, it becomes the Palmer subnet's natural reference point whenever any other city considers a
comparable major development decision — entrenching Marambio's reputation for considered, durable strategic
planning well beyond its own two logistics modes, a reputation that traces specifically to how the
diversification was built, not merely to the fact that it happened.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Marambio's dual airfield-and-shipyard identity now rests on genuine, deliberate strategic diversification —
proven durable specifically because it was built in careful stages rather than rushed or left to chance,
giving the city a reputation for considered infrastructure planning that extends well past its own borders.
