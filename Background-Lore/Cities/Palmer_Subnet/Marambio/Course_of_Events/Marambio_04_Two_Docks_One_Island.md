## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Marambio's identity starts and stays airfield-first for its earliest decades, the runway the obvious center
of civic life — everything about the settlement's self-understanding, from its training culture to its
civic pride, organized around aviation.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Seymour Island's own position, facing the South American mainland, makes it a genuinely useful shipping
location too — one the city's early planners hadn't fully developed while their attention was on the
airfield. As the shipping potential becomes impossible to ignore, a real push begins to build out a
substantial shipyard alongside the runway. But developing it means diverting labor, funding, and civic
attention away from the airfield operation that has defined Marambio's identity and disciplined culture
from the very start — and running two major logistics operations at once risks leaving neither one with the
undivided attention that made the airfield work as well as it does.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side wins outright. The settlement resolves the standoff structurally: the shipyard gets built, but
under its own separate, equally rigorous command structure rather than folded into the airfield's existing
one — Marambio applies its established disciplined-institution model twice, once for each operation,
instead of stretching one structure across both. Both sides get what actually mattered to them: the
airfield keeps its undivided rigor, and the shipyard gets built for real rather than left undeveloped.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the two operations run under separate but equally rigorous command structures rather than one
diluted structure, Marambio ends up training and recruiting two genuinely distinct specialist workforces
instead of one generalist one — real occupational specialization by domain, aviation crews and shipyard
crews, becomes a defining feature of Marambio's labor structure going forward.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the two workforces develop and train separately, each cultivates its own distinct point of civic
pride rather than sharing one undifferentiated identity — the aviation crews continuing to trace their
lineage back to the base's founding discipline, the shipyard crews building pride around the newer,
harder-won recognition of their own operation's equal importance. That divide in professional identity is
what later gives Marambio's civic calendar its own reason to eventually hold two separate, complementary
commemorations rather than one shared holiday trying to represent both halves at once.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Marambio is now genuinely a dual-mode hub, airfield and shipyard fully developed side by side, each running
under its own disciplined command structure and staffed by its own specialist workforce — two logistics
identities operating with real, undivided rigor rather than either one existing in the other's shadow.
