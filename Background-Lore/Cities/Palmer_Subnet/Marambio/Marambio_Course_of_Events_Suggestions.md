# Marambio — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material (Marambio's own eventual destruction is out of scope here). 10 candidate But/Therefore
chains built from Marambio's Megasheet (the dual airfield-and-shipyard hub, the Eocene fossil record,
the Air Force institutional discipline). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. A Runway Already Built

Marambio's exile community inherits a fully working Argentine Air Force aviation hub, operational
since 1969 — a genuine functional head start rather than an improvised settlement. **But** inheriting
working infrastructure doesn't automatically mean inheriting the discipline that kept it running.
**Therefore** the earliest years focus on maintaining and adapting the existing operation rather than
building from nothing, a genuinely different founding experience than cities that had to improvise
their whole infrastructure from scratch. **But** infrastructure without institutional culture behind it
eventually degrades. **Therefore** Marambio's founding generation has to deliberately carry the
operational rigor forward, not just the runway and hangars themselves.

## 2. Fariña's Discipline

The inherited airfield runs well on inertia for its first years, but inertia alone doesn't sustain an
operation this demanding indefinitely. **But** a growing civilian population arriving alongside the
original Air Force personnel has no automatic reason to adopt military-grade operational standards.
**Therefore** Comandante Ezequiel Fariña deliberately extends the base's own disciplined culture across
the whole settlement, treating operational rigor as a civic value rather than a purely military one.
**But** discipline imposed without buy-in rarely survives its own imposer. **Therefore** it takes root
anyway, becoming genuinely embedded civic character rather than merely enforced policy — the founding
figure credited with making Marambio's whole culture, not just its airfield, run with real precision.

## 3. Whoever Runs It Now

Generations of later immigration steadily dilute Argentina's own founding population at Marambio,
eventually down to the deepest erosion documented anywhere in the Palmer subnet. **But** the
operational discipline Fariña built into the city's earliest institutions doesn't dilute at the same
rate its founding population does. **Therefore** Marambio keeps running with the same rigorous,
function-first character generations after Argentina itself becomes a tiny minority presence — the
culture persisting independent of who's actually staffing the operation now. **But** nobody in
Marambio's own established culture seems to have particularly noticed or named this persistence.
**Therefore** it simply continues, unremarked, a founding institutional template quietly outliving its
own founders without anyone treating that fact as significant.

## 4. Two Docks, One Island

Marambio's identity starts and stays airfield-first for its earliest decades, the runway the obvious
center of civic life. **But** Seymour Island's own position, facing the South American mainland, makes
it a genuinely useful shipping location too, one the city's early planners hadn't fully developed.
**Therefore** a real, substantial shipyard grows up alongside the airfield, humans and robots loading
and unloading ships together regardless of weather. **But** running two major logistics operations at
once means neither one gets the city's undivided attention. **Therefore** Marambio becomes genuinely
skilled at both simultaneously — a dual-mode hub whose real significance was never fully captured by
its own earlier, airfield-only reputation.

## 5. Ingrid's Dig

Marambio's flat, exposed terrain hides something most residents initially pay no attention to —
Seymour Island's exposed sedimentary layers, carrying fossils tens of millions of years old.
**But** a working aviation and shipping hub has little obvious reason to prioritize paleontological
research. **Therefore** Paleontologist Ingrid Solberg-Vidal pursues the cataloguing work anyway,
building a genuine scholarly record of the fossil find largely on her own initiative. **But**
convincing a function-first city to value that kind of research takes real, sustained advocacy.
**Therefore** her cataloguing eventually earns real civic recognition — proof that Marambio's own
buried geological archive deserved serious attention alongside its more obviously practical industries.

## 6. The Warm Ground

Marambio's fossil discovery starts as a scientific curiosity, interesting mainly to the researchers
directly involved. **But** the full scale of what the Eocene record actually reveals — a warmer,
forested Antarctica existing long before any ice — is significant enough that it starts to feel like
something the whole city should mark. **Therefore** a quieter, science-appreciation holiday takes
hold, honoring the fossil record specifically and timed to whichever discovery first confirmed the
full scope of Seymour Island's paleontological significance. **But** a holiday celebrating ancient
geology sits oddly next to Marambio's own more obviously practical civic character. **Therefore** it
persists anyway, a small, deliberate reminder that the ground itself has its own history, older and
stranger than anything the exile community actually built.

## 7. Founders' Flight

The anniversary of the original 1969 Argentine Air Force arrival could easily pass unmarked, one date
among many in Marambio's institutional history. **But** a city whose whole founding infrastructure was
a runway has a natural, fitting reason to commemorate exactly that. **Therefore** Founders' Flight
takes hold as a real civic holiday, an aviation-focused observance honoring both the original arrival
and the exile generation's own founding flight onto Seymour Island. **But** a holiday this specific to
aviation risks underrepresenting the city's later shipyard identity. **Therefore** the two holidays end
up complementing each other — Founders' Flight looking at what the exile generation built, The Warm
Ground looking at what was already there long before any of them arrived.

## 8. Side by Side in Any Weather

Marambio's shipyard runs constant, demanding work regardless of weather, humans and robots loading and
unloading ships together through rain, clear skies, or snow. **But** work this constant and this
physically demanding naturally throws people together for long, shared stretches. **Therefore**
genuine relationships form between human and robot dockworkers, some formalizing into committed
partnerships, some residents specifically drawn to Marambio because robot society treated them better
than human society ever had. **But** relationships this ordinary in Marambio's own daily life could
easily go unremarked by the city's own established culture. **Therefore** they don't — smoke breaks
during the rare quiet stretches become genuine, valued social time, human-robot partnership simply
part of the shipyard's own unremarkable daily texture.

## 9. Bulk Here, Precision There

Marambio and Esperanza both handle real shipping traffic through the same Weddell Sea corridor. **But**
the two cities' own infrastructure and civic character point in different directions — Marambio built
for scale, Esperanza organized around its family charter with shipping as a secondary function.
**Therefore** a natural division settles between them: Marambio handling bulk, high-volume cargo
through its large-scale shipyard, Esperanza handling smaller-scale, more specific and often documented
shipments. **But** maintaining that split takes both cities actually respecting the other's particular
strength rather than competing for the same traffic. **Therefore** the arrangement holds, generation
after generation, each city doing what its own infrastructure and culture actually suit it for.

## 10. Never Just One Thing

Marambio's early planners could have let the airfield remain the city's whole logistics identity,
content with being simply an aviation hub. **But** relying on one single mode of transport leaves a
city vulnerable to anything that changes the specific value of that one function. **Therefore**
developing the shipyard alongside the airfield becomes, deliberately, a hedge against exactly that
vulnerability — two logistics modes instead of one, neither entirely dependent on the other remaining
relevant. **But** running both well takes real, sustained investment in two different kinds of
infrastructure at once. **Therefore** Marambio's centrality ends up genuinely durable, resting on
diversification rather than any single irreplaceable function — a considered strategic strength, not
an accident of geography.

## Worth Your Attention

**#2 and #3** trace Marambio's founding-culture arc directly — the deliberate choice that built the
city's operational discipline, and its own quiet persistence long after the founding population
thinned to almost nothing. **#4 and #10** work as a pair, giving the Megasheet's own newly-confirmed
dual airfield-and-shipyard identity both a developmental origin and the strategic logic behind why it
mattered.
