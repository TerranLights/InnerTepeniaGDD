# Palmer City — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material. Palmer City's own inciting incident and destruction are explicitly out of scope here; these
chains cover only the living city's earlier history, before any of that. 10 candidate But/Therefore
chains built from Palmer City's Megasheet (the founding-day-of-treaty city, the jazz-records origin,
the Upper Earth tourism economy). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. The Day We Didn't Wait

The Falkland Treaty is signed, formally establishing Tepenia's exile terms. **But** waiting for
further instruction or a more organized settlement plan would mean losing the momentum of the moment
itself. **Therefore** a group of robots, human partners, and human ideological supporters simply
begins building the same day the treaty is signed — Palmer City founded not out of careful planning,
but out of people who refused to wait even one day longer than they had to. **But** founding a city on
pure momentum means figuring out everything else — governance, economy, identity — after the fact
rather than before it. **Therefore** Palmer City's whole later character forms retroactively, shaped
by people already living there rather than planned in advance by anyone.

## 2. Needle Down

Early settlers cataloguing the original Palmer Station buildings find a cache of old jazz records,
left behind by whoever worked there before. **But** records with no player, no venue, and no
established audience could easily have stayed a curiosity nobody acted on. **Therefore** someone plays
them anyway, for the whole young settlement, within the first year or two of founding — an
impromptu, informal event that becomes something residents want to repeat. **But** repeating a single
lucky discovery risks it staying a one-time novelty. **Therefore** it doesn't — the informal gathering
becomes the seed of "Needle Down," Palmer City's own defining musical holiday, later drawing on jazz,
rockabilly, and electroswing alike.

## 3. Everyone's People Arrived First

Palmer City's founding population organizes itself around relationship to robots rather than
nationality — no single nation's claim to have arrived "first" the way most Tepenian cities' founding
stories work. **But** a city with no dominant founding nation still needs some shared civic myth to
hold together. **Therefore** the community settles on something more inclusive instead: the idea that
everyone's people arrived here first, since the founding population itself was never sorted by
nationality to begin with. **But** later arrivals, joining generations after the actual 2564 founding,
don't literally share that founding-day history. **Therefore** the myth stretches to include them
anyway — more generous than the literal facts strictly support, becoming the story the city needs
rather than the story it technically has.

## 4. A Window Facing Out

Palmer City's tourism economy starts small — the occasional Upper Earth visitor curious about a
nation officially considered illegitimate. **But** Upper Earth's own restrictive stance on certain
freedoms, particularly around robot personhood and cohabitation, makes visiting somewhere without
those restrictions genuinely appealing to some of its own citizens. **Therefore** Palmer City leans
into exactly that gap, building a genuine tourism economy around simply being what it already is —
ordinary Tepenian life, dressed up as spectacle for visitors to whom that ordinary life is the
forbidden thing. **But** hosting visitors from a nation that officially disdains you requires real,
careful legal infrastructure. **Therefore** the Machu Picchu Base visa-and-tracking system develops
alongside the tourism economy itself — hospitality and sovereignty assertion growing up together as
one continuous project.

## 5. Forty-Three Flags

Palmer City's founding population represents a genuine cross-section of nations, but not literally
every nation on Tepenia's own master list. **But** the city's whole civic myth already insists everyone
belongs here regardless of origin. **Therefore** a deliberate expansion eventually brings all
forty-three master-list nations into genuine Notable-tier presence, turning the city's inclusive myth
into demographic fact rather than aspiration alone. **But** completing that expansion means Palmer
City's population no longer traces neatly back to any single founding-era wave. **Therefore** the city
becomes, deliberately and by design, the one place in Tepenia where literally every available exile
nation has real presence — not a token gesture, but the direct extension of everything the city
already claimed to be.

## 6. The Petrograd Room

Palmer City's earliest casinos and entertainment halls serve their function well enough without any
particular grandeur. **But** a city building its whole identity around spectacle eventually wants
somewhere that actually looks the part. **Therefore** the city's grandest hall gets built — named,
half-jokingly and half-reverently, after St. Petersburg's own imperial ambition, the closest thing
Palmer City has to declaring "we willed this into existence and it worked." **But** a name that
grand invites real expectations about what the space actually delivers. **Therefore** it becomes
exactly what its name promises — Palmer City's most prestigious venue, the setting reserved for its
most significant visitors and its most memorable nights.

## 7. Little Burgundy Quarter

The district around the original station buildings, where the found jazz records first got played,
grows organically as Palmer City's population settles in around it. **But** a neighborhood that
significant to the city's founding story could easily lose its character as ordinary development
spreads through it. **Therefore** residents deliberately preserve its identity as the city's living
music quarter, an open homage to Montreal's own historic jazz neighborhood, keeping live venues dense
and concentrated rather than letting them scatter across the wider city. **But** protecting one
district's character means directing growth elsewhere instead. **Therefore** Palmer City ends up with
a genuinely distinct musical heart — one small area carrying disproportionate cultural weight relative
to its physical size.

## 8. What the Rain Allows

Palmer City sits in the one part of Tepenia where above-freezing temperatures and real rain are simply
normal, expected weather. **But** most Tepenian architecture assumes cold and snow as the default
condition to design around. **Therefore** Palmer City's builders develop something genuinely different
— elevated walkways and structures suited to rain and storm-window hazards rather than pure cold,
letting visitors experience the city's rare weather without full exposure to its worst moments.
**But** designing around weather nobody else in Tepenia has to consider means solving problems no
other city's engineers have already worked out. **Therefore** Palmer City's built environment ends up
genuinely unique nationwide — a direct architectural response to being the one place in the country
where it actually, unpredictably rains.

## 9. An Agreement Neither Side Announces

Palmer City's tourism economy grows large enough that informal, ad-hoc visitor arrangements stop being
workable. **But** neither Tepenia nor whatever Upper Earth authority oversees Machu Picchu Base wants
to formally, publicly acknowledge the arrangement they're both benefiting from. **Therefore** a quiet,
practical understanding gets reached instead — a visa-on-arrival, tracked-stay system, genuinely
significant diplomacy conducted without either side making a public announcement about it. **But** an
unannounced agreement still has to actually function reliably for both parties to keep honoring it.
**Therefore** it becomes, quietly, the first real sustained point of negotiated contact between
Tepenia and the world that exiled it — significant precisely because nobody involved ever calls it
that.

## 10. Records From an Older Expedition

Palmer City, already an established, functioning settlement, receives a request for historical records
and maps to help locate an even earlier lost site far across the continent. **But** a city this focused
on its own present-tense spectacle and nightlife has little obvious reason to prioritize archival
support for someone else's expedition. **Therefore** the city's own cultural custodians provide the
material anyway, treating the request as a natural extension of Palmer City's own founding-first
identity — a place that arrived early enough to have useful history now being useful to someone else's
search. **But** lending irreplaceable records means trusting they'll actually help. **Therefore** the
contribution becomes a quiet point of civic pride — proof that being first sometimes means being
useful to whoever comes looking later, not just being remembered for arriving early.

## 11. Nothing Unusual Yet

An Upper Earth visitor treats a robot hospitality worker dismissively during an ordinary shift — nothing
that rises to any formal incident, over in a moment, easy for everyone else on shift to let pass. **But**
a young resident who witnessed or experienced it directly won't let it go, since letting it pass quietly
means the city's founding conviction is worth less in practice than it claims to be. **Therefore** they
insist on documenting it anyway, through whatever internal channel the hospitality industry keeps for
exactly this. **But** the report goes nowhere consequential — no structural change, no real consequence
for the visitor, just a quiet mark nobody outside the building will ever see. **Therefore** the
insistence itself becomes the real outcome: a formative, unresolved conviction the resident carries
forward quietly for the rest of their life, a clear-eyed correction to the assumption that Palmer City's
founding principle gets honored in practice every single time it matters.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#1 and #3** work as a pair — the literal founding-day origin, and the more generous civic myth the
city eventually builds on top of it, giving the Megasheet's own flagged gap (a birthright story more
inclusive than the actual founding facts) an honest, dramatized shape. **#4 and #9** trace Palmer
City's tourism economy from its earliest informal roots to genuine, quiet diplomacy — the two halves of
what the Megasheet's own Full Extrapolation identifies as the city's real structural relationship to
Upper Earth. **#11** is the quietest and most consequential thread of the eleven, without naming anyone
or referencing the war directly — it builds real pre-war texture for exactly the kind of resident the
Megasheet's own open questions suggest Palmer City's most notable individual eventually turned out to be.
