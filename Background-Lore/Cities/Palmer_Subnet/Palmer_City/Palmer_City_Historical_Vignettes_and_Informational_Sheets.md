## The First Address in the Federation

100 Miles Davis Boulevard and the original customs-office site (Physical Infrastructure Attribute #3) is,
per established lore, the literal first official address in the Federation — the physical threshold where
the earliest exiled robots and their human partners were actually received, before Tepenia had anything
resembling formal governance to receive them with.

*(An informational sheet on the site's founding-era function, not a single arrival.)* The earliest customs
processing here, per what's established about Tepenia's founding, was necessarily improvised — there was no
existing bureaucracy to inherit, no prior institution to model the intake process on, only the practical
necessity of somehow accounting for who had arrived and what they'd brought with them. What later became a
formal address and a named boulevard began as something closer to a single desk and whoever happened to be
staffing it that day. The site's own significance grew backward, in a sense — its status as "the first
official address" wasn't decided in advance, but recognized only once Tepenia had enough subsequent history
to know that this particular spot was where its own story with recorded arrivals had actually started.

## What the Boardwalk Was Actually For

The Boardwalk (attribute #8) — an elevated walkway purpose-built to let visitors experience Palmer City's
rare above-freezing, rainy weather — doubled as prime nightlife real estate, a genuinely unusual pairing of
infrastructure functions no other Tepenian city's own climate would make sensible.

*(An informational sheet on the design logic itself, not a single evening.)* Nowhere else in Tepenia could a
walkway be built specifically to showcase weather as an attraction rather than a hazard to be managed around
— every other Tepenian city's own storm-window and hazard infrastructure exists to protect residents from
conditions Palmer City alone could afford to treat as scenery. The Boardwalk's own nightlife function grew
naturally out of this novelty: visitors already drawn to the elevated walkway to experience actual rain, wet
snow, and above-freezing air found themselves gathering there after dark as well, until the Boardwalk's own
identity as prime nightlife territory became inseparable from its original purpose as a weather-viewing
platform. Residents describe the pairing as simply obvious in retrospect — a city whose entire civic identity
was already built on celebration would naturally turn its rarest environmental feature into another reason to
gather rather than merely another engineering problem to solve.

## Running Water in a Frozen Country

*(An informational sheet on the city's defining engineering novelty, not a single storm.)* Running-water and
drainage infrastructure engineered for regular above-freezing conditions, and wet-weather structural design
accounting for rain, wet snow, and maritime icing hazards (attributes #1–2), made Palmer City a genuine
engineering outlier — a physical capability no other Tepenian city examined in this project ever needed to
build for at all.

Engineers trained elsewhere in Tepenia, per what's established about the city's own unusual climate,
routinely found Palmer City's own infrastructure needs counterintuitive on arrival — drainage systems and
structural drainage capacity were simply never part of standard Tepenian civic engineering training, since
every other city's own precipitation arrived exclusively as snow. Palmer City's own maritime subpolar climate
— the mildest of any Tepenian city, no polar night or midnight sun at this latitude — meant its builders
solved problems most of the rest of the country never had to think about: liquid water pooling, freeze-thaw
cycles at building foundations, and icing hazards created specifically by moisture and subfreezing
temperatures occurring together. The resulting infrastructure became, in its own way, as distinctive a
regional signature as the city's jazz clubs or casino economy — proof that Tepenia's founding city was also,
quietly, its most unusual engineering environment.

## Where the Records Were First Played

Little Burgundy Quarter (attribute #7), the district around the original station buildings, was Palmer
City's densest live-music neighborhood — the specific place where the city's own found jazz records were
first played, before they became any kind of citywide cultural touchstone.

Per Finding G, Little Burgundy and the Petrograd Room represent two genuinely different registers of the same
underlying tourism and entertainment economy — Little Burgundy dense, communal, and historically the city's
actual founding cultural soul, distinct from the Petrograd Room's own higher-status, more exclusive register.
Residents who trace their own family history to the Quarter describe an ordinary, almost accidental
originating moment: whoever first played the found records did so in Little Burgundy simply because that's
where people already gathered, not because the district was deliberately chosen as culturally significant.
The significance came later, retroactively, the same pattern already visible in how 100 Miles Davis
Boulevard's own founding status was recognized rather than planned. What Little Burgundy actually offered,
that made it the natural first venue, was density — enough people close enough together that music played
once could plausibly be heard, remembered, and repeated by enough witnesses to become tradition rather than a
single forgotten evening.

## The Street Built to Perform Sovereignty

Rue de la Réception (attribute #5), the formal ceremonial approach street used for visiting dignitaries and
Upper Earth tourism arrivals, is architecturally distinct from Miles Davis Boulevard's own historic, lived-in
register — a street built specifically to stage arrival rather than simply to house it.

Per Finding C, Rue de la Réception is the literal architectural expression of Palmer City's own established
"sovereignty theater" — the observation that the city's hospitality was never merely hospitality, but doubled
as an ongoing, repeated demonstration of Tepenian jurisdiction, every visa-tracked Upper Earth visitor a small
act of submission to Tepenian law simply by walking the formally designated approach. The street's own design
choices, per what's established about ceremonial arrival infrastructure generally, would have emphasized
this function deliberately — a controlled, singular route rather than the organic, informal access Miles
Davis Boulevard itself represents, ensuring that every formal visitor experienced their own arrival as
something staged and witnessed rather than simply as ordinary travel.

## A City No Faction Could Claim

Whatever civic and administrative infrastructure reflected Palmer City's own established faction-neutral
status (attribute #11) gave the city a genuinely unique civic-legal position among Tepenian cities — a
settlement no single faction could claim, because every faction's predecessors had passed through it first.

*(An informational sheet on that neutrality's own institutional expression, not a single dispute.)* Per
Finding E, this civic-neutrality infrastructure is the institutional expression of a doubly-justified
cosmopolitanism — the founding population's own relationship-based organizing principle, and the later,
independently-arrived-at 43-nation demographic reality, both pointing the same direction. A city with two
genuinely independent justifications for universal belonging, per this reading, plausibly needed real
administrative machinery to enact that neutrality in daily practice, not merely cultural sentiment —
arbitration mechanisms, no-single-claim governance structures, the practical apparatus that let representatives
of every faction conduct business in Palmer City without any of them ever needing to treat it as contested
ground. Residents describe the arrangement as something visitors from more contested parts of the subnet
often found genuinely disorienting at first — a city where asking "whose side is this place on" simply
wasn't a meaningful question to ask, by design, since its founding.
