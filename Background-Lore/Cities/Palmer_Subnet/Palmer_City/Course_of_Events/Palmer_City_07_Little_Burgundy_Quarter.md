## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The district around the original station buildings — where the found jazz records first got played —
grows organically as Palmer City's population settles in around it, its live-music character emerging by
accident rather than design.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A neighborhood this significant to the city's founding story could easily lose its character as ordinary
development spreads through it — nothing about organic growth guarantees the district stays what it
happened to become. Residents deliberately act to preserve its identity as the city's living music
quarter, an open homage to Montreal's own historic jazz neighborhood, keeping live venues dense and
concentrated rather than letting them scatter across the wider city. But protecting one district's
character this deliberately means directing the growth that district can no longer absorb somewhere
else instead — and somebody still has to decide, in practice, where "somewhere else" actually is.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement that plays out like a genuine zoning

protective measures so the district's identity survives the city's own continued growth around it.
Character B, based in an adjacent area, isn't against preservation in principle but is frustrated that
protecting one neighborhood simply displaces the growth pressure onto theirs instead, without any
corresponding benefit or say in the decision.

absorbing growth redirected away from the historic quarter without having chosen that role for themselves.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Preservation wins — the quarter's character is protected, its live venues stay dense and concentrated, and
Palmer City ends up with a genuinely distinct musical heart, one small area carrying disproportionate
cultural weight relative to its physical size.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because growth was explicitly redirected rather than simply avoided, the neighborhoods that absorbed it
develop their own compensating identity in response — commercial and hospitality development concentrates
there instead, giving Palmer City a functional split: one small district as cultural heart, a separate,
larger zone as the city's commercial and spectacle engine.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the city's musical and commercial identities now live in physically separate districts, residents
and visitors alike develop a habitual nightly circuit between them — starting in the high-spectacle
commercial district and ending the night in Little Burgundy's more intimate venues, or the reverse — a
customary rhythm that only exists because the two districts specialized apart from each other (2nd-order),
not simply because preservation happened at all.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Palmer City now has two distinct, functionally specialized cultural zones — Little Burgundy Quarter as its
small, dense, protected musical heart, and a separate commercial district absorbing the growth redirected
away from it — with a customary nightly migration between the two woven into the city's own everyday
social rhythm.
