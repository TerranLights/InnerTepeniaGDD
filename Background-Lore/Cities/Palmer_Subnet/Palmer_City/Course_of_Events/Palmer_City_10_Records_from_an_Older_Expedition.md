## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Palmer City, already an established, functioning settlement, receives a request for historical records and
maps to help locate an even earlier lost site far across the continent — the Belgrano-led effort searching
for what will become Byrd Station.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city this focused on its own present-tense spectacle and nightlife has little obvious reason to
prioritize archival support for someone else's expedition — the request competes for attention and
institutional energy against everything Palmer City's civic life is actually oriented toward. Whether to
treat the request seriously, or simply let it go unanswered amid everything more immediately pressing,
becomes a real, live question rather than an automatic yes.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over where the young city's limited
institutional attention should go. One side is invested in Palmer City's history meaning something beyond
its own present-tense spectacle — a cultural-custodian figure who sees the founding-first identity as
carrying a real obligation to be useful to others, not just something to celebrate about itself. The other
side is invested in the city's actual present-day economic engine — tourism, entertainment, the spectacle
that pays the bills — and sees diverting scarce institutional energy toward someone else's expedition, with
no clear benefit to Palmer City itself, as a low-value distraction the young settlement can't really
afford.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly one of Palmer City's own
early cultural-custodian figures, personally invested in preserving and honoring the city's founding story
as something more than decoration. Character B is plausibly an entertainment-industry or civic-economic
figure — a venue operator or tourism administrator — who would rather the city's limited institutional
energy stay focused on its own present-day economy.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The custodians provide the material anyway, treating the request as a natural extension of Palmer City's
own founding-first identity — a place that arrived early enough to have useful history now being useful to
someone else's search.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the records genuinely help locate the lost site, Palmer City's cultural-custodian role gains real,
externally demonstrated credibility for the first time — no longer just an internal institution honoring
its own founding story, but one with a proven track record of being useful beyond the city's own borders.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that credibility is now real and demonstrated rather than merely claimed, other expeditions,
historians, and city projects elsewhere in Tepenia begin treating Palmer City's archives as a legitimate
resource worth consulting for their own future questions — a reputation that starts drawing further outside
requests, gradually turning what began as a one-off act of civic generosity into an ongoing, expected role.
This depends specifically on the contribution's demonstrated success (2nd-order), not merely on the records
having been sent at all.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Palmer City's cultural-custodian institution has, through its proven contribution to finding an earlier
lost site, become an externally credible historical resource beyond the city's own borders — a quiet,
growing reputation that keeps drawing further outside requests its way, alongside everything else the city
is known for.
