## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Palmer City's founding population organizes itself around relationship to robots rather than nationality
— no single nation can claim to have arrived "first" the way most Tepenian cities' founding stories work,
because the founding population itself was never sorted by nationality to begin with.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city with no dominant founding nation still needs some shared civic myth to hold together — the absence
of a "we got here first" story doesn't remove the need for one, it just leaves the slot empty. The
community settles on something more inclusive instead: the idea that everyone's people arrived here
first, since nobody's arrival was ever tracked by nation in the first place. But later arrivals — joining
generations after the actual 2564 founding, with no ancestors present on that day at all — don't literally
share that founding-day history, and as those numbers grow, the gap between the myth's claim and the
founding record's actual narrow roster of nations becomes something the city eventually has to reckon
with, one way or another.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over whether the founding myth should
stay literal or stretch to accommodate everyone who's arrived since. One side descends from the actual
founding-wave nations and feels that extending "arrived first" to people who plainly didn't cheapens what
the true founders actually did — waiting the myth's meaning thin until it means nothing in particular.
The other side belongs to one of the later-arrived nations and argues that restricting the myth to
literal descent betrays the very principle the city was founded on: Palmer City never organized itself by
national priority to begin with, so measuring belonging by arrival date imports exactly the logic the
founding population rejected. Both sides can point to something real and consistent with the city's own
history — this shouldn't resolve as one side simply being right.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a descendant of one of
the narrow Primary/Significant-tier founding nations, personally attached to what their specific ancestors
actually did on day one. Character B is plausibly a resident from one of the thirty-three nations added
in later waves, invested in the city's civic identity but aware, at least privately, of standing slightly
outside its literal founding story.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The myth stretches to include everyone anyway — more generous than the literal facts strictly support,
becoming the story the city needs rather than the story it technically has. Character B's reading becomes
the city's official self-understanding, not because Character A's objection gets refuted so much as
because the city, practically, needs a story flexible enough to keep holding a constantly growing
population together.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the myth is now explicit civic doctrine rather than an assumed background fact, Palmer City begins
deliberately marking the formal arrival of newly Notable-tier nations with welcome customs that echo
founding-day observance itself — a new community's first substantial presence gets treated, ritually, as
its own small founding, rather than as a later addendum to someone else's.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Repeated across enough generations and enough newly welcomed nations, that ritual erodes the *public*
distinction between founding-wave and later-arrival residents almost entirely — nobody introduces
themselves by which wave their people arrived in. But the erosion is public, not universal: a quieter,
rarely voiced awareness of being a later arrival dressed in a myth of universal belonging persists
privately for some residents descended from the later-added nations, unresolved beneath the city's
confident official story. This particular residue only exists because the welcome-ritual habit
(2nd-order) made the *public* myth so seamless that any private gap between story and literal fact stands
out more, not less.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Palmer City's founding myth is now lived civic practice, actively renewed every time a new nation's
arrival gets ritually folded into the city's story — genuinely more generous than its own founding record,
and functioning exactly as the unifying story the city needs it to be, even as a quiet, private awareness
of the gap between myth and fact persists for some, unaddressed and unspoken.
