## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Palmer City's tourism economy starts small — the occasional Upper Earth visitor, curious about a nation
officially considered illegitimate, making the crossing more out of novelty than any real pattern of
demand.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Upper Earth's own restrictive stance on certain freedoms — particularly around robot personhood and open
cohabitation between humans and robots — makes visiting somewhere without those restrictions genuinely
appealing to some of its own citizens, not as novelty tourism but as something closer to relief. Palmer
City leans into exactly that gap, building a real tourism economy around simply being what it already is
— ordinary Tepenian life, dressed up as spectacle for visitors to whom that ordinary life is the forbidden
thing. But hosting visitors from a nation that officially disdains you can't run indefinitely on
improvisation once the numbers grow — real, careful legal infrastructure becomes necessary the moment
informal hospitality starts carrying genuine political and legal stakes.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over whether formalizing the visitor
arrangement protects the city or threatens what makes it work. One side sees an unmanaged flow of
visitors from a hostile nation as a real vulnerability — no tracking, no legal clarity about what happens
if something goes wrong, no assertion of Tepenian jurisdiction beyond polite assumption. The other side
worries that turning hospitality into an administered system contradicts the very openness the tourism
economy runs on — that visitors drawn here partly by the absence of Upper Earth's restrictions might balk
at a new set of rules and forms greeting them on arrival instead.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an early civic
administrator or someone from the founding ideological-supporter contingent, attentive to the political
exposure of hosting a hostile nation's citizens without any legal structure behind it. Character B is
plausibly a hospitality or entertainment-industry figure — a casino or venue proprietor whose livelihood
depends on visitors feeling genuinely welcome, worried that visible bureaucracy will chill the very traffic
it's meant to protect.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Both concerns get folded into one system rather than one side simply prevailing: the Machu Picchu Base
visa-and-tracking arrangement develops alongside the tourism economy itself, hospitality and sovereignty
assertion growing up together as one continuous project — visitors are genuinely welcomed and genuinely
tracked, at the same time, by design.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the system had to satisfy both sides of the original disagreement at once, it ends up
deliberately asymmetric: Tepenian jurisdiction applies in full the moment a visitor arrives, but the actual
apparatus that enforces it — the tracking, the paperwork, the legal machinery — is built to stay
essentially invisible to the visitor's own experience. The state feels the system fully; the tourist barely
feels it at all.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the tracking stays deliberately backstage, most Upper Earth visitors leave Palmer City never
having consciously registered how completely they submitted to Tepenian law during their stay — the
frictionless hospitality experience gives them no reason to notice. That means whenever a dispute or
incident involving a visitor does eventually surface, the visitor in question is often genuinely startled
to discover Tepenian jurisdiction has real teeth over them — a structural surprise that traces directly to
how invisibly the system was built to run, not merely to the fact that a legal system exists at all.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Palmer City's tourism economy now runs on a deliberately two-layered arrangement — full, real Tepenian
legal authority over every visitor, wrapped inside a hospitality experience engineered to make that
authority invisible until the moment it's actually invoked. Hospitality and sovereignty keep growing as
one project, exactly as they began.
