## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Palmer City runs at its usual glamorous, thriving pace — casinos, the Petrograd Room, Little Burgundy
Quarter's live venues, an economy that depends on hosting Upper Earth visitors who submit to Tepenian law
while present, whatever their own government officially believes about robot personhood. A young
resident, raised on the city's own founding conviction — that everyone belongs here, that personhood was
never up for debate among the people who built this place — works an ordinary hospitality-industry job,
the kind most residents eventually take, and has never had real reason to doubt that conviction holds.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

During an unremarkable shift, an Upper Earth visitor treats a robot employee dismissively — not
violently, nothing that rises to any kind of incident report by the letter of the law, just the small,
casual condescension of someone who doesn't actually believe the robot in front of them is a person. It's
over in a moment. Nobody else on shift seems to think it's worth mentioning.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a conflict about whether what happened was
wrong — both sides agree it was. Character A, having witnessed or experienced the moment directly, wants
it taken seriously: documented, reported, treated as the real violation it was, because letting it pass
quietly means the city's founding conviction is worth less in practice than it claims in principle.
Character B, plausibly a shift supervisor responsible for keeping the tourism economy running smoothly,
isn't defending the visitor — but genuinely believes that escalating every small, unprovable moment like
this would be untenable for an economy built entirely on hosting people who don't uniformly respect what
the city stands for; some absorption, in their view, is simply the cost of the arrangement.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly the affected robot
employee themselves, or a human or robot coworker who witnessed it and won't let it go. Character B is
plausibly a longtime hospitality manager, neither cynical nor cruel, just accustomed to a version of this
calculation that the young resident hasn't yet had to make peace with.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Nothing structural changes. The resident insists anyway, filing what documentation exists through
whatever internal channel the hospitality industry keeps for exactly this — and the report goes nowhere
consequential, the visitor facing nothing beyond a quiet mark nobody outside the building will ever see.
The insistence itself, not any institutional victory, is the actual outcome.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The resident carries the moment forward as a formative, unresolved conviction — not disillusionment
exactly, but a clear-eyed correction to the assumption that the city's founding principle would simply
be honored in practice every time it mattered. The gap between what Palmer City claims and what it can
actually enforce, moment to moment, becomes personally real to them for the first time.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That early, unresolved hardening becomes a quiet, permanent part of who they are — the specific internal
resource they'll draw on, without fanfare, the next time they encounter anything shaped like it, at
whatever scale that turns out to be, years or decades down the line.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Palmer City continues exactly as it was — glamorous, hospitable, economically dependent on visitors who
don't uniformly respect what the city was founded to protect — but now holds, quietly, one more resident
who already knows firsthand that the city's protections are real but imperfect, and who will not be
surprised by whatever comes next the way someone without this particular history might be.
