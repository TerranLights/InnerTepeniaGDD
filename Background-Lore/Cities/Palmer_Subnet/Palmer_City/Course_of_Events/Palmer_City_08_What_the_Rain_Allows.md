## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Palmer City sits in the one part of Tepenia where above-freezing temperatures and real rain are simply
normal, expected weather — a genuine outlier against the rest of the Federation's cold-and-snow baseline.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Most Tepenian architecture assumes cold and snow as the default condition to design around, and importing
that assumption wholesale into a place that actually rains creates real, recurring hazards — flooding at
ground level, storm-window damage, structures built to shed snow rather than manage sustained
precipitation. As the settlement grows, those hazards stop being occasional nuisances and start being a
genuine, recurring problem that standard Tepenian building practice simply wasn't designed to solve.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a practical, resource-driven disagreement between
innovation and continuity. One side wants to design genuinely new, rain-specific architecture from first
principles — elevated walkways, storm-hazard-suited structures — even though it means solving problems no
other Tepenian city's engineers have already worked out, with no existing playbook to borrow from. The
other side wants to keep adapting the standard cold-weather Tepenian designs everyone already knows how
to build, faster and cheaper, even at the cost of recurring flood and storm damage — a young settlement
without resources to spare on unproven experimentation.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an architect or engineer

its own terms. Character B is plausibly a resource-conscious builder or administrator prioritizing speed
and continuity with familiar Tepenian construction practice, unwilling to gamble scarce founding-era
resources on an unproven approach.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Character A's approach ultimately wins out, likely after a storm or flood visibly damages buildings built
to standard cold-weather specifications, forcing the issue past debate. The city commits to genuinely
rain-specific design — elevated walkways and storm-window-hazard-suited structures, letting visitors and
residents experience the city's rare weather without full exposure to its worst moments.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because Palmer City had to solve rain-specific engineering completely from first principles, it develops
and retains a body of specialized architectural and engineering expertise unlike anywhere else in Tepenia
— not just a distinctive look, but genuine institutional know-how, carried forward through the people who
worked the problem out.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that expertise is genuinely unique nationwide, Palmer City's builders occasionally get sought out
whenever another Tepenian project unexpectedly runs into water or moisture problems of its own — a modest
but real technical-export niche the city didn't have before, existing only because the specialized
knowledge itself took root (2nd-order), not simply because it happens to rain in Palmer City.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Palmer City's built environment is nationally unique — elevated, storm-hazard-suited architecture
developed from scratch — and the specialized engineering tradition behind it has become a modest, genuine
point of expertise the rest of Tepenia occasionally has real reason to draw on.
