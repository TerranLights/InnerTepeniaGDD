## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Palmer City's founding population represents a genuine cross-section of nations — but not literally every
nation on Tepenia's own master list. Whole nations that eventually settle elsewhere in the Federation have
no meaningful presence here yet.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The city's whole civic myth already insists everyone belongs here regardless of origin — and living only
partway up to that claim becomes an increasingly visible gap the longer the myth gets repeated without
the demographics to fully back it. A deliberate expansion effort takes shape, aiming to bring all
forty-three master-list nations into genuine Notable-tier presence, turning the inclusive myth into
demographic fact rather than aspiration alone. But completing that expansion on purpose, rather than
letting it happen organically over centuries, means Palmer City's population will no longer trace neatly
back to any single founding-era wave — the city's demographic character becomes, from this point forward,
as much a product of deliberate policy as of history.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over whether the city should actively
engineer its own demographic completeness or simply let the myth remain aspirational. One side wants to
make the inclusive myth demonstrably, provably true — actively recruiting or welcoming nations not yet
present, treating "everyone belongs here" as a promise the city owes its own founding principle. The other
side is wary of turning an organically cohered identity into a managed one — worried that deliberately
engineering demographic completeness is a different, colder thing than a population that simply happened
to end up this way, and that manufactured inclusivity risks feeling performative rather than earned.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic planner, or
someone from the ideological-supporter tradition, invested in making Palmer City's inclusivity real rather
than merely rhetorical. Character B is plausibly a longtime resident or cultural figure — perhaps
connected to the Little Burgundy Quarter's music scene — worried that a manufactured demographic
completeness dilutes whatever let the city's culture cohere organically in the first place.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The expansion goes forward. All forty-three master-list nations achieve genuine Notable-tier presence,
making Palmer City the one Tepenian city where literally every available exile nation has real presence —
not a token gesture, but the direct, deliberate extension of everything the city already claimed to be.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the expansion was pursued as deliberate policy rather than left to organic drift, Palmer City
develops — for the first time — a small civic apparatus dedicated to newcomer integration: sponsor
pairings between established residents and newly arriving communities, and modest civic offices tasked
with tracking how each new nation's presence actually takes root, distinct from every other Tepenian
city's more organic growth pattern.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because integration was actively facilitated and tracked rather than left to chance, Palmer City ends up
holding an unusually thorough documentary record of exactly when and how each of its forty-three nations
established real presence — a level of demographic paper trail no other Tepenian city has, existing only
because the sponsor-and-tracking apparatus (2nd-order) was built to produce it, not because the expansion
itself happened.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Palmer City is now, by design as much as by history, the one Tepenian city with genuine presence from all
forty-three master-list nations — administered through the very civic infrastructure built to make that
completion happen, and holding an unusually thorough record of its own demographic assembly that no other
Tepenian city possesses.
