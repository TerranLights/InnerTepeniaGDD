## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Esperanza's founding generation never gets around to formalizing its own defining promise into one
official document — the compact lives in intention and early practice, but nothing anyone could point to
as a single, authoritative charter.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A principle this central to the city's whole identity still needs some way to actually persist across
generations, and as Esperanza grows past its founding cohort, the absence of a formal document starts to
feel more exposed. It survives, for now, through the birth registry itself, oral tradition, and simple
lived practice — reinforced every time a name gets entered, every time a child is cared for the way the
compact promises. But an unwritten covenant is more fragile than a formal charter in one specific way: it
depends entirely on people actually continuing to live it out, generation after generation, with nothing
written down to fall back on if that living practice ever falters.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone who wants the
founding promise finally written down into one authoritative charter — arguing that a growing city needs
something more durable than lived practice alone, especially once it has to explain itself to outsiders
or defend itself to a wider Federation that runs on documents — and someone who believes formalizing it
would kill the very thing that makes it work. The anti-formalization argument isn't nostalgia for its own
sake: a written charter can be filed away and stop being lived, while an unwritten covenant survives only
by being continuously, actively practiced — which is arguably the whole point. Both positions carry real
risk; the conflict should make clear that there is no safe option, only a choice about which kind of
fragility Esperanza is willing to live with.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic administrator
or someone newly responsible for representing Esperanza to the wider Federation, who has run into the
practical cost of having nothing written to point to. Character B is plausibly a registry-keeper or an
elder closely tied to the compact's lived, oral tradition — someone who has watched the promise work
precisely because it demands continuous practice, and who fears a written document would let people treat
the promise as already fulfilled just by its existence.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

No single charter gets ratified. Instead, Esperanza's whole civic culture ends up organized, without
anyone designing it this way on purpose, around making sure the promise stays real in practice rather
than trusting any single document to carry it forward alone — the formalization push loses, but not
because it was wrong to worry, only because the city finds its own answer more convincing than a filed-
away document would have been.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

As a partial concession to the formalization argument's legitimate underlying worry, a separate,
explicitly non-binding written chronicle gets compiled anyway — a descriptive record of the compact's
history and practice, useful for explaining Esperanza to outsiders, but deliberately never granted the
authority a true charter would carry.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that chronicle exists but was never meant to be binding, it becomes a recurring, low-grade source
of civic disagreement in its own right — generations later, people periodically propose updating it,
formalizing it, or treating it as more authoritative than it was ever meant to be, and each time the
community has to reaffirm, deliberately, that the chronicle describes the promise rather than replacing
it. What began as a one-time compromise becomes an ongoing, accepted feature of how Esperanza keeps
relitigating its own relationship to writing things down.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Esperanza has no single founding charter, and by now understands that fact as a deliberate feature of its
own civic character rather than an oversight. A non-binding chronicle exists alongside the living
practice it describes, occasionally provoking the same old argument about formalization in miniature —
a city that has, in effect, chosen to keep having this conversation rather than settle it once.
