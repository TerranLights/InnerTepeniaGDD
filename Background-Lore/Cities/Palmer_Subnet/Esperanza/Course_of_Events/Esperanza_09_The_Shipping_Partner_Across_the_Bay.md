## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Esperanza and Marambio both handle Weddell Sea trans-shipment, splitting the corridor as genuine equal
partners rather than competitors — a working arrangement neither city had to fight for, simply the shape
their shared geography settled into early on.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The two cities' own civic characters point in different directions underneath that shared arrangement —
Marambio built for volume and scale, Esperanza organized entirely around its family charter, with shipping
as one function among several rather than its defining purpose. A natural division starts to emerge
between them as a result: Marambio handling bulk, high-volume cargo, Esperanza carrying smaller-scale,
more specific and often carefully documented shipments. But an informal division like that only holds if
both sides keep respecting it — and the first time a particular shipment doesn't obviously belong to
either category, the arrangement has no established rule to settle the question.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a dispute between a Marambio-side logistics figure
looking to grow their city's own throughput by moving into a lucrative category of shipment that happens
to fit Esperanza's usual documented, smaller-scale niche, and an Esperanza-side shipping coordinator
defending that niche as more than just a business line — it's the specific corner of the partnership that
respects Esperanza's own civic character. Character A's push shouldn't read as bad faith; expanding
market share is a rational move for a volume-built city, and nothing in the original informal division was
ever written down as binding. Character B's defense is equally legitimate: losing that specific category
would mean Esperanza's shipping role stops reflecting anything distinct about the city at all, reducing it
to simply "the smaller partner."

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a growth-minded
logistics official from Marambio, thinking in terms of scale and efficiency and not particularly
attached to the informal understanding that's held for years. Character B is plausibly an Esperanza
shipping coordinator or port liaison who understands the corridor split isn't just commercial
convenience but a reflection of what makes each city's contribution distinct.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The dispute forces the question the informal arrangement had never actually answered, and the answer that
emerges favors the original division. The partnership holds, generation after generation, precisely
because both cities recognize, once forced to say it out loud, that neither one actually wants to become
the other — Marambio doesn't want the overhead of Esperanza's documentation-heavy niche, and Esperanza
doesn't want the operational scale that would come with chasing Marambio's volume.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Having had to actually articulate the division under pressure, the two cities do something Esperanza
almost never does internally: they write it down. A formal inter-city shipping protocol gets drafted and
signed, spelling out which categories of cargo route through which port — an external agreement, notably,
rather than an internal civic charter, which is a distinction Esperanza's own civic culture treats as
meaningfully different.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because a working, formally written protocol now exists between the two cities, it becomes the template
other Palmer subnet cities reach for when they need to divide shared infrastructure of their own — not
because Esperanza and Marambio set out to create a model, but because a functioning written agreement is
a rare and useful enough object that neighboring cities facing similar disputes borrow its structure
rather than negotiate one from scratch.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Esperanza and Marambio's shipping partnership now rests on a formal, written protocol rather than
unspoken habit — an external agreement other cities have started borrowing as a template, standing in
quiet contrast to Esperanza's own continued refusal to formalize its internal founding compact the same
way.
