## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Esperanza's mining economy demands rotational shifts — weeks working the mines, then a week or two back
home — a rhythm the settlement adopts out of sheer economic necessity, without yet fully reckoning with
what it does to daily family life.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

That rhythm leaves real, recurring gaps in daily family life that someone has to fill, and robots take up
the primary caregiving and teaching role during fathers' absences — not as a stopgap arrangement, but as
the genuine, functioning shape of the city's actual family structure from early on. The trouble is that a
household built this way looks unusual against the more conventional assumptions most new arrivals and
outside visitors still carry about what a family is supposed to look like, and Esperanza's own settlers
haven't yet had to defend the arrangement to anyone outside the city who might read it as deficient rather
than simply different.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between an outside evaluator applying
conventional, mainland-shaped assumptions about family structure, and a local figure defending the
father-plus-robot-caregiver household as a legitimate, functioning family rather than a problem to be

genuinely tasked with checking on child welfare across Tepenia's cities has a real job to do, and a
household structure this unusual would reasonably draw a second look from someone applying standard
metrics never designed with Esperanza's actual conditions in mind. The local defender's task is convincing
them the metrics themselves are the wrong instrument here, not that nothing should be checked at all.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The evaluation resolves in the household structure's favor — but more importantly, Esperanza stops
treating the question as open at all. Father-plus-robot-caregiver becomes officially, unremarkably normal
here: the accepted, unapologetic shape of home, understood as grown directly out of the city's own real
conditions rather than as a deficiency anyone still needs to explain away.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the question got asked and answered formally rather than left as an unspoken assumption,
Esperanza ends up with something most cities never bother producing: an actual, documented account of why
its family structure looks the way it does, written for an outside audience rather than only understood
internally.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That documented account outlives its original evaluative purpose. Other Tepenian cities with their own
rotational-labor economies — wherever work pulls parents away for extended stretches — start citing
Esperanza's account as a working precedent when their own family structures draw similar outside
scrutiny, turning what began as a defensive answer to one visiting official into an exported template
other cities lean on when they need to make the same case for themselves.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Esperanza's family structure is no longer merely locally accepted — it's externally documented,
defended, and precedent-setting. The rotational-mining rhythm that once looked like an awkward compromise
against conventional expectations has become, on the record, a legitimate and even influential model of
what family can look like under real Tepenian conditions.
