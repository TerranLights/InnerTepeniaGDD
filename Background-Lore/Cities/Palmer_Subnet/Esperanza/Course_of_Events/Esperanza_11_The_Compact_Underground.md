## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Esperanza's mines run genuinely dangerous, physically demanding rotational shifts, staffed
disproportionately by fathers rotating between the mines and their own households. Mining safety is
treated as ordinary operational policy — real, but administratively separate from the founding compact,
which lives in a different part of the city's civic imagination entirely.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A serious mining accident forces the two facts into direct contact for the first time. A father is
injured or killed underground — and his own child is, immediately and unavoidably, one of the specific
children the founding compact exists to protect. The abstraction collapses into a single, concrete case
the whole city can see clearly.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a genuine disagreement about cost, not about
whether the accident matters — everyone agrees it matters. Character A represents the mining operation's
own economic reality: real safety reform means real, sustained reductions in output, straining
Esperanza's contribution to the wider Tepenian supply chain other cities and expansion efforts actually
depend on. Character B represents the founding compact taken literally and seriously: if protecting
children is what Esperanza exists for, protecting their fathers from preventable underground risk isn't
optional, whatever it costs. Neither side is being cynical — this is a real trade-off between two things
Esperanza genuinely values.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a mining operations
administrator or an official responsible for Esperanza's own output commitments to the wider industrial
supply chain. Character B is plausibly a figure connected to the founding compact's own civic
institutions — someone who maintains the birth registry, or simply a resident close enough to the
accident to insist the city's own stated values actually mean something in practice.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The city commits to real, costly safety reform — rewritten standards, slower output, real ongoing
expense. It's framed explicitly, publicly, as an extension of the founding compact itself, not as
ordinary labor policy quietly updated after an incident.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Esperanza's material output to the wider Tepenian industrial supply chain drops, measurably, and stays
lower than it was. Other cities and expansion efforts that depended on Esperanza's mining contribution
feel the difference, even if most of them never learn exactly why.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Rather than quietly reversing course once the immediate grief fades, Esperanza holds the new standard.
The mines become, permanently, a place where "protecting fathers because protecting fathers is how we
protect children" is treated as literally true rather than merely sentimental — the founding compact's
most costly, most concretely-paid-for expression anywhere in the city's own history.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Esperanza's mines continue producing real, valuable material for Tepenia's wider industrial supply
chain — just at a permanently lower, safer output than before, a choice the city keeps making
deliberately rather than one imposed on it once and forgotten. The founding compact now has a specific,
economically costly proof point behind it, distinct from the birth registry's ceremonial weight: a real
trade-off the city actually pays, not just a value it declares.
