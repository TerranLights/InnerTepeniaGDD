## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Esperanza's mining operation produces real, valuable material — inputs for siligel synthesis, building
materials, fabrication components — running alongside, but not yet clearly connected to, the city's
sentimental founding-compact identity as the Guarded City.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city this focused on its own founding compact could easily start treating the mining economy as merely
secondary — necessary income, but not what Esperanza is actually *about* the way the family charter is.
That drift starts to show: civic attention, celebration, and pride cluster around the registry, the
charter, the children, while the mines get treated as unglamorous background labor that happens to pay
for all of it. The trouble is that treating the two as separate, ranked identities — one sentimental and
central, one merely functional — misreads what's actually keeping the sentimental identity alive at all.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone invested in
Esperanza's sentimental civic identity — the registry, the charter, the children — who has started
treating the mines as beneath that identity's dignity, and someone from the mining operation itself who
resents watching the very labor that funds the family charter get civic short shrift. This shouldn't play
as the civic idealist simply being wrong to value the charter — the charter genuinely is Esperanza's
emotional core — but as a blind spot: valuing the sentimental identity while failing to credit the
unglamorous economic engine that makes sustaining it possible at all. The miners' side has a legitimate
grievance, not just wounded pride.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic or
ceremonial figure closely tied to the registry or the founding-compact tradition — someone whose whole
sense of Esperanza's identity runs through its sentimental, family-centered side. Character B is
plausibly a mine foreman or senior miner, someone who has spent a career doing the extraction work that
actually keeps the settlement solvent and who is tired of that work being treated as civically invisible
next to the charter it quietly makes possible.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The two threads end up genuinely intertwined rather than ranked — the material output is recognized,
explicitly and publicly, as funding and sustaining the very family-charter city built around it, with
Esperanza's economic contribution feeding directly into the wider Tepenian industrial supply chain. The
mines become something residents take real pride in, not despite the founding compact's more sentimental
civic identity, but as its necessary, unglamorous foundation.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Recognizing the mines as foundational rather than secondary produces its own civic ritual: returning
mining shifts start being marked with the same deliberate, public acknowledgment the registry gives to a
newly recorded name — not a private homecoming alone, but a small recognized civic moment, tying the
economic engine to the same reverence the family charter already enjoys.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because mining labor now carries real, visible civic honor rather than quiet necessity, it becomes an
aspirational role for young Esperanzans in a way it wasn't before — chosen with pride rather than settled
into out of economic obligation alone. Over successive generations, that shift in why people choose
mining work changes who actually ends up doing it, drawing in residents who might once have looked past
the mines entirely for something with more obvious civic prestige.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Esperanza's civic soul and its mining economy are no longer read as separate, ranked identities — they're
one intertwined system, each publicly honored in its own right. The unglamorous extraction work the
family charter's survival actually depends on is now, itself, a source of genuine civic pride rather than
invisible labor behind the city's more sentimental self-image.
