## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The founding compact is already settled — discovered through the sealed archive's own preserved birth
records (see Course of Events #2), the council has resolved that Esperanza exists specifically to protect
the children of those who sacrificed everything for exile. But a settled principle isn't yet a settled
practice: nobody has decided who actually does the protecting, day to day.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

With human settlers stretched thin building housing and running the mines, and robots making up a real
share of the young settlement from the outset, a practical question forces itself onto the founding
council: what role do robots actually play in making the compact real? A fine principle on paper doesn't
feed, shelter, or watch over a single child unless someone specific is actually assigned to do it — and
the compact itself, per its own robot-and-human authorship, was never framed as a human-only promise that
robots merely enable from the sidelines.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone who believes
child-rearing and protection should stay a human, family-led responsibility — robots providing
infrastructure and support, but not direct caregiving — and someone who insists the founding compact was
explicitly a *robot-and-human* agreement, not a human promise robots simply make possible, meaning robots
have as much standing and obligation to be direct caregivers as any human does. Neither side is wrong: A's
caution reflects a real, hard-won instinct about keeping something human-scaled and intimate in a
settlement that could easily let its founding purpose calcify into bureaucracy; B's position is the more
literal, faithful reading of what the compact's own dual authorship actually promised.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a human parent or early
civic organizer, protective of the compact's original intimacy and wary of diluting it. Character B is
plausibly a robot present since the founding council itself — someone with direct, earned standing to
insist the agreement's own authorship already settled this question, if anyone actually reads it that way.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Robots are formally included as direct caregivers and protectors, not merely support staff — codified
into the earliest version of what will become Esperanza's own child-welfare practice. Not robots replacing
human parents, but standing beside them with real, direct responsibility for a child's welfare, exactly as
the compact's own dual authorship implied from the start.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

This gives Esperanza's own birth registry (see Course of Events #2) a robot dimension from its very
origin — robots aren't merely record-keepers of a human institution, they hold real, formal standing as
protectors alongside the families whose sacrifice founded the city.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

This becomes part of why Esperanza's human-robot relationship reads as genuinely distinctive nationally —
not simply the general "egalitarian, skewed toward robots" baseline most Tepenian cities share, but
something more specific: a civic relationship literally built around shared, formal responsibility for
children, unlike anywhere else in Tepenia.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Esperanza's founding compact is now not just a settled principle but a settled practice — robots and
humans both formally, directly responsible for the city's children, the compact's own robot-and-human
authorship made real in daily civic life rather than staying an abstract founding document.
