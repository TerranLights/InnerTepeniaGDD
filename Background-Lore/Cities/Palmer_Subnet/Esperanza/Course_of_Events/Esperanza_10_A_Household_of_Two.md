## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

One father, working the mines on rotation, and the robot caregiver raising his child during his absences
have settled into a genuine, functioning rhythm together — not an arrangement either of them chose in the
abstract, just the shape their actual daily life takes.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

That rhythm works fine on its own terms, until a visit from outside disrupts it — a family connection or
old friend from beyond Esperanza, seeing the household up close for the first time, reacts with quiet
surprise, and then quiet concern, assuming a household missing a second human parent must be missing
something the child needs. The father finds himself explaining, patiently and without defensiveness, that
this is simply what a family looks like here — real, complete, and built out of Esperanza's own actual
conditions rather than any deficiency. But explaining it, once, in words, never quite closes the gap
between how the city understands itself and how a visitor from outside first reads it.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a low-stakes but genuinely wearing conflict between a
father defending the legitimacy of his own household and a visitor whose doubt comes from real affection
and worry rather than hostility. This isn't a confrontation with a villain — the visitor's concern is
plausibly sincere, rooted in a more conventional idea of what a child needs that most of Tepenia outside
Esperanza still quietly assumes. The father's task isn't to defeat an argument but to make something felt
rather than merely stated — the gap between them is experiential, not factual, and words alone may not be
able to close it.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly the father himself —
someone who has lived the household's actual functioning long enough to be confident in it without needing
outside validation, but who still cares what this particular visitor thinks. Character B is plausibly a
relative, an old friend, or someone from the father's own life before Esperanza — someone whose doubt
carries real personal weight precisely because they aren't a stranger, unlike a bureaucratic evaluator
would be.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Explaining in words gets the father only partway there, so he stops arguing and starts simply letting the
visit run its course — inviting the relative to actually stay embedded in the household's real daily
rhythm rather than hearing about it secondhand. The skepticism doesn't vanish, but it softens considerably
under direct exposure to how the arrangement actually works day to day; the small, recurring moment of
having to explain — now more show than tell — becomes, itself, a quiet, ordinary piece of what it means
to raise a child in Esperanza.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Having learned that demonstration works where argument doesn't, the father changes how he handles the
next skeptical visitor, and the one after that — leading with showing rather than explaining. Word of that
approach spreads informally among other rotational-mining fathers raising children the same way, less as
deliberate advice-giving than as the kind of practical wisdom that circulates naturally among people
facing the same recurring situation.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that "show, don't argue" habit spreads specifically through word of mouth among fathers who've
lived it, and because those same visitors carry their softened impressions back out with them, Esperanza's
reputation beyond its own borders slowly shifts too — not because outsiders stop being surprised at first,
but because more of them now arrive already having heard, secondhand, that the households are worth
watching before judging. The city's outside reputation improves gradually, as a direct downstream effect
of individual fathers' habit rather than of the household structure's existence alone.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Father-plus-robot-caregiver households in Esperanza still draw occasional surprise from outside — that
gap never fully closes — but the city's own fathers now have a shared, practiced way of meeting that
surprise: not defending the arrangement in the abstract, but simply letting it be seen. The small,
recurring moment of explaining has become, quietly and without anyone designing it that way, one more
ordinary thread in what raising a child in Esperanza actually involves.
