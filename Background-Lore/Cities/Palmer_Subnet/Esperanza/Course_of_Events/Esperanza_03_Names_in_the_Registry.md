## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Esperanza's earliest families want some way to formally mark each child born into the community's
founding promise — some act that makes the abstract civic compact feel real and specific to one actual
new life.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A promise this significant, the founding generation quickly realizes, needs more than a casual
acknowledgment to actually feel real — a birthday wish, a community announcement, isn't enough weight for
what the compact claims to guarantee. So the birth registry becomes something closer to sacred civic
record than routine paperwork: every name entered is treated as a formal, deliberate act of extending the
founding compact to one more child, not administrative bookkeeping. But a registry that matters this much
quickly becomes something residents genuinely worry about losing — a single document now carrying more
civic and emotional weight than any one physical object comfortably should.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over how to actually protect
something this precious once its value is undeniable. One side wants the registry kept as a single,
tightly guarded physical document — centralized, controlled access, minimal handling, the safest way to
prevent tampering or accidental loss. The other side argues that concentrating all the risk in one
irreplaceable object is itself the danger, and pushes instead for duplication and redundancy — copies,
cross-referenced oral tradition, trained memory — so no single point of failure can erase it. Both sides
are protecting the same thing for the same reason; they simply disagree about which kind of vulnerability
is worse, and the disagreement is sharpened by how new and untested the settlement's own institutions
still are.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an early
records-custodian type — someone temperamentally suited to guardianship, who reads any duplication of the
sacred document as dilution of its meaning as much as a security measure. Character B is plausibly a
settlement organizer with a more practical, resilience-minded streak — someone who has already seen how
easily a young colony can lose things it can't afford to lose, and who trusts redundancy over reverence
alone to actually keep a promise intact across generations.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither approach wins outright — the settlement settles on both. The registry's careful, guarded
maintenance becomes one of Esperanza's own defining civic habits, treated with a level of reverence most
cities reserve for something far more ceremonial than a simple list of names, while its contents are also
quietly reinforced through repetition, oral tradition, and lived practice, so the promise it records
doesn't depend on the physical document alone.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Maintaining the registry this carefully requires someone whose actual, ongoing civic role is tending
it — checking entries, reinforcing the oral tradition around it, making sure the reverence around the
document translates into real, sustained practice rather than a one-time founding gesture. An informal
registry-keeper role emerges, distinct from general settlement administration.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that keeper role exists and carries real civic weight, it becomes something families actively
want for their own children to grow into — a respected, trusted civic position rather than a clerical
chore nobody wants. Tending the registry becomes a small but genuine mark of standing within Esperanza,
generations before anyone would have predicted a simple record-keeping job could carry that kind of
civic prestige.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Esperanza now treats its birth registry as functionally sacred — guarded as a physical object, reinforced
through oral tradition as a living practice, and tended by a recognized civic role that carries real
social standing. The founding promise no longer lives only in memory or sentiment; it has a maintained,
defended, actively reproduced institutional home.
