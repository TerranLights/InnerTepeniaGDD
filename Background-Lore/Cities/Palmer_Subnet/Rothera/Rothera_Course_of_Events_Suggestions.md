# Rothera — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material (Rothera's own wartime survival through decentralization is out of scope here; these chains
cover the pre-war decisions and reasoning that made that survival possible, without depicting the war
itself). 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Rothera's Megasheet (the decentralized industrial
city, the underground vaults, the Palmer subnet's industrial spine). Post-settlement only; nothing
here is canon.

---

## 1. A Runway and a Legacy

Argentine and Chilean arrivals settle Adelaide Island first, ahead of the British institutional
population that eventually inherits BAS's own well-developed station and rare paved runway. **But** an
early population with no particular claim to the site's later British-institutional character could
easily have set the city's whole tone before that institutional identity ever took hold. **Therefore**
the founding sequence layers instead of erasing — the earlier Argentine and Chilean presence persisting
alongside, not displaced by, the British institutional population and the larger American wave that
follows. **But** three distinct founding currents arriving in sequence risk producing a city with no
single coherent identity at all. **Therefore** Rothera settles its identity around function instead —
what the city does together matters more than which wave arrived first.

## 2. Spread Out On Purpose

Rothera's earliest industrial planners could concentrate the city's fabrication capacity in one
efficient, centralized complex, the way most industrial cities naturally do. **But** Chief Engineer
Malcolm Ashgrove-Pryce sees a real risk in that approach — a single concentrated facility is also a
single point where everything could be lost at once. **Therefore** he lays out Rothera's whole
industrial plan deliberately spread across Adelaide Island's much larger physical scale, decentralized
by design rather than efficiency. **But** that choice costs real coordination overhead a centralized
plant wouldn't need. **Therefore** Rothera commits to the tradeoff anyway, betting long-term resilience
against short-term efficiency — a considered strategic choice, not an accident of geography.

## 3. Digging Into the Mountain

Adelaide Island's mountainous terrain offers something few other Peninsula cities' geography allows —
genuine capacity for large-scale underground excavation. **But** excavating space this large takes
years of sustained effort with no immediate payoff to justify the investment. **Therefore** Vault
Architect Dorotéia Uribe-Calloway pioneers the underground program anyway, arguing the capacity is
worth building before anyone has an urgent reason to need it. **But** convincing a function-first city
to invest heavily in space nobody's using yet takes real, sustained persuasion. **Therefore** the vault
program takes hold gradually, growing across generations into something far larger than its own
earliest, modest beginnings — capacity built patiently, well ahead of any specific need for it.

## 4. What Rothera Builds

Rothera's inherited infrastructure gives it real fabrication capacity, but no obvious specialty to
focus that capacity on. **But** a subnet with six member cities and one highway network between them
needs a steady, reliable source of vehicles and transit-infrastructure components to keep that network
running. **Therefore** Rothera's own industrial output grows to serve exactly that need — highway-
maintenance vehicles, aircraft and runway parts, general construction materials, distributed via Hwy 1
to every other Palmer subnet city. **But** that role means the whole subnet's infrastructure now
quietly depends on Rothera's own industrial health. **Therefore** Rothera becomes, in practice, the
Palmer subnet's own construction engine — literally building the network it belongs to, not merely
producing generic goods for it.

## 5. Two Shifts, One City

Rothera's human workers settle into 8–10 hour shifts, while its robot workers run 10–12 hour shifts —
two overlapping but distinct working rhythms across the same industrial city. **But** a population
running on genuinely different schedules risks splitting into two barely-overlapping social worlds.
**Therefore** shared leisure time becomes something residents actively protect — humans and robots
stopping at the same bars and pubs when the workday ends, regardless of whose shift just finished.
**But** sustaining that shared social rhythm across mismatched schedules takes real, ongoing effort.
**Therefore** Rothera's whole after-work culture ends up genuinely integrated — working-class,
unpretentious, and consistently shared between humans and robots rather than quietly segregated by
shift.

## 6. Founders' Airstrip

The inherited BAS runway gives Rothera real aviation capacity from its earliest years, alongside the
institutional competence that came with it. **But** a city whose whole civic character runs on
function over presentation has little natural instinct toward ceremony. **Therefore** Founders'
Airstrip still takes hold as a genuine holiday, honoring the inherited runway and the institutional
discipline it represents — observed, fittingly, with a maintenance-crew open house rather than anything
more elaborate. **But** even a modest holiday needs some reason to persist beyond its first
observance. **Therefore** it does, becoming the closest thing Rothera has to civic ceremony — plain,
functional, and genuinely valued precisely because it never tries to be more than what it is.

## 7. The Standing Works

Rothera's decentralized industrial network takes decades to fully mature, expanding site by site
across Adelaide Island until the whole system is genuinely, completely operational. **But** a
transformation this gradual has no single dramatic moment to mark as its completion. **Therefore**
residents eventually settle on one anyway — the day the last major fabrication site came fully online,
formalized into The Standing Works, a holiday marking the industrial network's own completed reach
across the island. **But** celebrating industrial infrastructure risks feeling hollow without real
civic investment behind it. **Therefore** it doesn't — residents treat the observance as genuine pride
in something they built together, patiently, across generations, rather than empty ceremony for its
own sake.

## 8. Between BAS and Halley

Rothera's British Antarctic Survey heritage traces back to the same institutional lineage as Halley's
own founding, despite the real geographic distance separating the two cities across different subnets.
**But** shared institutional origin doesn't automatically produce an ongoing relationship once two
cities settle into their own separate lives. **Therefore** a genuine cultural connection persists
anyway — occasional contact, shared reference points, a felt kinship between two cities that both trace
back to the same real-world program even though they answer to entirely different subnets now.
**But** maintaining that connection across real distance takes more effort than a close neighbor
relationship would. **Therefore** it stays real but modest — acknowledged and valued, never as close or
constant as either city's relationship with its own immediate neighbors.

## 9. Built On, Not Replaced

Rothera's earliest industrial infrastructure serves the founding population's own modest needs.
**But** each new generation's demands outpace what the previous generation built, the same way any
growing industrial city's needs eventually do. **Therefore** each stage of Rothera's development builds
directly onto the existing capacity rather than clearing it away and starting fresh — new fabrication
sites added alongside old ones, new vault sections extending existing excavation. **But** building this
way means later engineers constantly have to work around decisions earlier generations made for
reasons that no longer fully apply. **Therefore** Rothera's whole industrial base grows into a genuine
layered composite over time — accumulated rather than replaced, every era's contribution still visibly
part of what the city has become.

## 10. Testing the Design

A serious industrial accident — a fire, a structural failure — breaks out at one of Rothera's
fabrication sites, the first real test of what happens when something actually goes wrong. **But**
because the city's industrial capacity was deliberately spread across the island rather than
concentrated, the accident stays contained to its own site rather than threatening the wider network.
**Therefore** the rest of Rothera's production continues largely uninterrupted, proving Ashgrove-Pryce's
original decentralization argument directly, decades after he first made it. **But** one contained
accident doesn't retroactively justify every cost the decentralized design has demanded all along.
**Therefore** the incident still becomes local legend — the moment residents saw, concretely, exactly
what the decentralization was actually for.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#2 and #10** work as a pair — the deliberate founding-era decision to decentralize, and a later
incident that proves the reasoning sound without needing to invoke the war at all. **#3 and #4** trace
Rothera's two defining infrastructural facts (the underground vaults, the subnet-wide construction
economy) back to genuine, patient, unglamorous investment rather than treating either as simply given.
