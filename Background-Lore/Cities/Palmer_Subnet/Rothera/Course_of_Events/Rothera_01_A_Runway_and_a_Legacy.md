## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Argentine and Chilean arrivals settle Adelaide Island first, well ahead of the British institutional
population that eventually inherits BAS's own well-developed station and rare paved runway — a founding
sequence with no single population in permanent control of what the settlement becomes.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

An early population with no particular claim to the site's later British-institutional character could
easily have set the city's whole tone before that institutional identity ever took hold — first arrival
carrying no automatic guarantee of lasting cultural primacy, but no automatic disqualification from it
either. As the British institutional population settles onto the inherited station, and the larger
American wave arrives after that, the question of whose imprint actually defines Rothera stops being
hypothetical: three distinct founding currents, arriving in sequence, each with some claim to having
shaped the place, and no obvious mechanism yet for deciding whose claim counts for more — or whether any
one of them should.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over what kind of city Rothera is
going to be — specifically, whether the settlement's civic character should be anchored to the population
that arrived first, or to the population that inherited the site's actual institutional infrastructure
(the station, the runway, the operational discipline that came with both). This shouldn't read as a
grievance between rivals; nobody here is trying to erase anybody. A wants the founding sequence itself to
matter, on the reasonable grounds that arriving first and building the settlement's earliest institutions
ought to count for something. B isn't arguing against A's history — B is arguing that inherited capacity
and demonstrated competence at running it are what a function-first population should actually organize
around, arrival order or not.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a figure from the
Argentine or Chilean founding wave — someone whose family's claim to the settlement predates the station's
British-institutional character, arguing that founding-wave heritage deserves formal civic recognition.
Character B is plausibly a station-trained administrator or engineer, inheriting BAS's operational
competence directly, arguing that the runway and the fabrication capacity it represents — not who got
there first — should be what organizes the city's identity going forward.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither claim wins outright. The founding sequence layers instead of erasing — the earlier Argentine and
Chilean presence persists alongside, not displaced by, the British institutional population and the
American wave that follows — and Rothera's identity settles around function instead of precedence: what
the city does together, working the fabrication yards and keeping the runway operational, comes to matter
more than which wave arrived first or which population built which institution.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because civic standing ends up organized around what work a person does rather than which founding wave
they descend from, Rothera develops an early, informal practice of integrating newcomers by trade and
shift assignment rather than by national or founding-wave affiliation — a person's place in the city
becomes legible through their role in the fabrication yards long before it's legible through their
family's arrival date.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because civic status was never tracked by founding-wave heritage, Rothera never develops the kind of
formal genealogical or founding-family record-keeping some other Tepenian cities take real pride in — no
registry of "first families," no civic ceremony built around descent. National and founding-wave identity
persists, but privately, in individual households, rather than publicly, in the city's own institutions.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Rothera is a city where a person's standing is legible through what they build and maintain, not through
which wave their family arrived on — a genuinely layered founding population with no formal heritage
registry to sort itself by, its national character preserved at home rather than staged in public.
