## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Adelaide Island's mountainous terrain offers something few other Peninsula cities' geography allows —
genuine capacity for large-scale underground excavation, sitting unused beneath a young, function-first
industrial settlement with no established reason yet to dig into it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Excavating space at this scale takes years of sustained effort with no immediate payoff to justify the
investment — labor, materials, and time a function-first city could spend on capacity it actually needs
today. Vault Architect Dorotéia Uribe-Calloway pushes for the underground program anyway, arguing the
capacity itself is worth building before anyone has an urgent reason to need it — that waiting for a
concrete justification means starting the excavation only once it's already too late to matter.
Convincing a city organized entirely around demonstrated function to invest heavily in space nobody's
using yet takes more than one good argument; it takes sustained, repeated persuasion against a
population's own reasonable instinct to spend its labor where the need is already visible.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over whether to commit real, scarce
labor to capacity with no current application. This is not a story about a shortsighted skeptic blocking
a visionary — A's caution is exactly the same function-first instinct that will come to define Rothera's
civic character, and diverting labor from urgent needs toward speculative excavation is a genuinely
costly choice, not an obviously foolish one. B isn't claiming to know what the vaults will be needed for;
B is arguing that some capacity is worth having on principle, before the need that justifies it ever
announces itself. The tension is between two legitimate readings of what "function-first" actually
means — serving only demonstrated need, or building toward capability nobody's proven necessary yet.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a resource-allocation
figure — an early city administrator or fabrication-site foreman responsible for where scarce labor
actually goes, skeptical of committing crews to a project with no measurable output. Character B is
plausibly Uribe-Calloway herself, or an engineer working with her, arguing from something closer to
principle than data — a belief that Adelaide Island's rare excavation capacity is worth claiming now,
while there's no competing urgent demand for the same labor, rather than fighting for it later against
whatever else the city needs by then.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

No single mandate settles it. The vault program takes hold gradually instead — funded and staffed in
small, renewable increments rather than one large commitment — growing across generations into something
far larger than its own earliest, modest beginnings. Capacity gets built patiently, well ahead of any
specific need for it, but only because each generation's allocation has to be re-argued and re-won on its
own terms.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the program only survives by winning fresh buy-in each generation rather than resting on a single
founding mandate, it develops a standing practice of proving the vaults' worth through actual peacetime
use — cold storage for surplus fabrication stock, archival space, staging areas for materials awaiting
distribution — rather than sealing off each new section as idle, purpose-held space.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because each vault section has to earn its keep with a real peacetime function before the next generation
will fund more excavation, the growing vault network ends up genuinely woven into Rothera's everyday
industrial workflow — load-bearing to daily operations — rather than existing as a separate, mothballed
shelter system waiting for a crisis that may never come.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Rothera's underground vaults have grown, generation by generation, into an actively used layer of city
infrastructure — storage, archives, staging space — genuinely entwined with daily operations rather than
sealed away, with a durable civic habit of continuous justification built into how the excavation keeps
expanding.
