## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The inherited BAS runway gives Rothera real aviation capacity from its earliest years, alongside the
institutional competence that came with it — a genuine civic asset in a city that otherwise defines
itself by its fabrication yards, not its skies.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city whose whole civic character runs on function over presentation has little natural instinct toward
ceremony — nothing in Rothera's own values obviously calls for marking the runway's anniversary with
anything more than routine maintenance. And yet the runway represents something the city didn't build
itself, inherited whole from BAS along with the operational discipline it carries — the kind of thing a
function-first population might still want to acknowledge, if it can find a way to do so that doesn't
feel like empty ceremony grafted onto a city that doesn't naturally reach for one.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over how elaborate Rothera's new
holiday should actually be. A sees an opportunity — a proper civic ceremony, with parade elements,
monuments, or formal rhetoric, could give Rothera something its more culturally ornate subnet neighbors
already have, a chance to be seen as more than just a working city. B resists, not out of indifference to
the runway's importance, but because embellishment feels like a betrayal of exactly what makes Rothera
Rothera — the maintenance-crew open house format isn't a placeholder waiting to be dressed up, it *is* the
appropriate form the observance should take.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic figure with an
eye toward Rothera's standing relative to its subnet neighbors — someone who wants the city to have a
"proper" holiday it can point to, comparable to what other, more ceremonially inclined cities already
have. Character B is plausibly a maintenance-crew figure or a function-first traditionalist, someone who
actually works the runway day to day and sees the open-house format not as a modest compromise but as the
only honest way Rothera could mark this occasion at all.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The modest format wins, deliberately, not by default — actively chosen and defended rather than simply
persisting because nobody got around to changing it. Founders' Airstrip becomes the closest thing Rothera
has to civic ceremony: plain, functional, and genuinely valued precisely because it never tries to be
more than what it is.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the format was actively defended rather than left to inertia, the open house gets real structure
built into it over successive years — specific maintenance tasks and demonstrations rotating annually,
crews walking visitors and apprentices through actual runway and aircraft maintenance work, turning the
holiday into a de facto annual apprenticeship pipeline for aviation-maintenance trades.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that apprenticeship pipeline consistently produces more trained aviation-maintenance workers than
Rothera's own modest runway actually needs, other Palmer subnet cities' aviation programs — Marambio's
larger hub most of all — come to rely on Rothera-trained personnel to fill their own ranks. Rothera ends
up quietly growing aviation-maintenance talent for a subnet role it doesn't itself occupy.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Founders' Airstrip has become both civic ceremony and unofficial trade school, and Rothera quietly
supplies trained aviation-maintenance labor across the Palmer subnet, most visibly to Marambio's larger
hub — a modest holiday that turned into genuine subnet infrastructure of its own.
