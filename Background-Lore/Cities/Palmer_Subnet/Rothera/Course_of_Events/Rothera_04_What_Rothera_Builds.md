## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Rothera's inherited infrastructure gives it real fabrication capacity — but no obvious specialty to focus
that capacity on, a broad general-purpose catalog with nothing yet that the rest of the Palmer subnet
particularly depends on it for.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A subnet with six member cities and one highway network between them needs a steady, reliable source of
vehicles and transit-infrastructure components to keep that network running — someone has to build the
highway-maintenance vehicles, the aircraft and runway parts, the construction materials the whole Palmer
subnet's physical connectivity depends on. Rothera's own industrial output grows to serve exactly that
need, distributed via Hwy 1 to every other Palmer subnet city. But settling into that role means the
whole subnet's infrastructure now quietly depends on Rothera's own industrial health specifically — a
single city's output becoming the thing every other city's roads, runways, and construction projects
ultimately run on.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement about whether the whole subnet
should formally commit to depending on one city for its transit infrastructure. This isn't a fight
between a visionary and an obstructionist — A's case for consolidating production in Rothera is a real
efficiency argument (redundant small-scale production across six cities is wasteful, and Rothera's
dispersed fabrication network is already well-suited to the job), and B's caution is a real structural
argument (specializing the whole subnet's infrastructure around a single supplier recreates, at the
economic scale, exactly the concentration risk Rothera's own industrial planners rejected at the physical
scale). Both sides are arguing from the same resilience-minded values; they just disagree about which
kind of concentration is the dangerous one.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a delegate from another
Palmer subnet city — Marambio or Palmer City, say — advocating for the collective efficiency of a single
reliable supplier over six cities each maintaining their own thin, duplicated production lines. Character
B is plausibly a Rothera figure, perhaps someone institutionally close to the city's own decentralization
doctrine, wary of committing Rothera's economy to a role that makes the entire subnet's infrastructure
dependent on one city's continued good health.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Rothera commits to the specialization. The subnet organizes around it in practice — Rothera becomes, in
effect, the Palmer subnet's own construction engine, literally building the network it belongs to, not
merely producing generic goods for it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

To answer B's original concern directly, Rothera formalizes a strategic-reserve practice for
subnet-critical parts — deliberately maintained buffer stock, distributed across its own already-dispersed
fabrication sites, so that no single Rothera site going offline could leave another Palmer subnet city
without replacement components.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that reserve practice makes Rothera's supply so reliable, the other Palmer subnet cities let their
own small redundant production capacity for these same components lapse entirely — trusting Rothera's
buffer stock rather than maintaining a fallback of their own. The very safeguard built to reduce the
subnet's dependence on Rothera ends up deepening it instead, the rest of the subnet now relying on
Rothera even more completely than before.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Rothera is now the confirmed, indispensable construction engine of the Palmer subnet, its internal
redundancy keeping supply reliable — but that very reliability has left the rest of the subnet more
dependent on Rothera specifically, not less, than anyone arguing for the specialization originally
intended.
