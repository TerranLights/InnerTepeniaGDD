## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Rothera's earliest industrial infrastructure serves the founding population's own modest needs —
functional, sufficient, and built at a scale that has no reason yet to anticipate what the city will
eventually become.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Each new generation's demands outpace what the previous generation built, the same way any growing
industrial city's needs eventually do — more fabrication capacity, more storage, more throughput than the
founders' original infrastructure was ever designed to carry. That growing gap forces an actual choice
each time it opens: clear away what's already standing and build fresh to the new generation's own
specifications, or find some way to build around what's already there. Working around older
infrastructure means living with decisions earlier engineers made for reasons that may no longer fully
apply — awkward angles, outdated materials, load paths designed for a smaller city than the one now
straining against them.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between demolishing and building
around, playing out at a specific site where an older structure is genuinely getting in the way of what a
newer generation needs. A's case is a real engineering argument — working around an outdated structure
costs more in design compromise and ongoing maintenance than simply clearing it and starting fresh would.
B's case isn't nostalgia; it's a structural conviction that continuity itself has value, that what's
already invested — the labor, the materials, the accumulated adaptation of the site to its own
conditions — is worth preserving even at a real efficiency cost. Neither side is wrong about the tradeoff;
they value the two sides of it differently.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a modernizing engineer
or site planner, frustrated by the real cost of designing around a structure that no longer serves its
original purpose well, arguing for a clean replacement. Character B is plausibly an institutional-memory
figure — perhaps someone who trained under, or reveres, the original founding engineers — who sees
continuity with what came before as something worth defending on its own terms, not merely a sentimental
attachment to old concrete.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's position wins out, and not as a one-off exception — it hardens into explicit, articulated policy. New
construction across Rothera is required, wherever feasible, to integrate with existing structures rather
than raze them: new fabrication sites added alongside old ones, new vault sections extending existing
excavation, each era's contribution still visibly part of what the city becomes rather than erased by the
next one.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because "build onto, don't replace" becomes codified policy rather than habit, Rothera develops a
distinct, visible architectural signature — mismatched eras of construction technique and material
sitting adjacent to each other across the fabrication network, plainly readable as layered history rather
than blended into anything uniform.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that layered signature is visibly, distinctively Rothera's own, it draws outside interest —
engineers and students from other Palmer subnet cities visiting specifically to study the adaptive-reuse
technique, and carrying the "build onto, don't replace" design philosophy back to their own cities when it
applies. Rothera becomes, informally, a working reference case for the wider subnet's own engineering
practice — an intellectual export distinct from the highway-vehicle parts the city is already known for
producing.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Rothera's industrial base is now a visibly layered composite by explicit design policy, distinctive
enough to draw outside engineers who study it and later apply its adaptive-reuse philosophy elsewhere in
the Palmer subnet — a city built on rather than replaced, and now teaching that same habit to others.
