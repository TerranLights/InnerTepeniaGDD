# Juan Carlos — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note:** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during the Second
Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no post-war/present-day
material (Juan Carlos's own eventual destruction is out of scope here). 10 candidate But/Therefore
chains built from Juan Carlos's Megasheet (the tertulia tradition, Tepenia's first bureaucratic
archive, the quiet island city with an outsized invisible responsibility). Post-settlement only;
nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Quiet Enough to Choose Itself

Juan Carlos's exile community settles on Livingston Island without the dense cluster of immediate
national neighbors Sejong's own founders have to contend with. **But** a city with no competing
communities to define itself against still has to decide what it actually wants to become.
**Therefore** the community develops its own culture — language use, food, temperament — with real
coherence and on its own terms, never having to constantly renegotiate its identity against a dozen
adjacent populations the way Sejong does. **But** that quiet coherence means Juan Carlos never
develops the kind of loud, contested civic identity a more crowded founding produces. **Therefore**
the city settles into something genuinely rare in Tepenia — a founding population that got to simply
decide who it was, rather than having identity forced into shape by constant negotiation.

## 2. The First Ledger

The young Federation needs some way to track who's entering and leaving its territory, who's applying
for Palmer City visas, who's seeking permanent immigration. **But** no existing Tepenian institution
is built to handle that kind of ongoing administrative recordkeeping. **Therefore** Juan Carlos, quiet
and coherent enough to take on steady institutional work, becomes the site of Tepenia's first real
bureaucratic archive — connected directly to the Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority, keeping
watch specifically on anyone who once held a position in their home country's Upper Earth government.
**But** archival work this foundational sets a precedent for how seriously the young Federation treats
its own records. **Therefore** what starts as ordinary administrative necessity becomes, without
anyone planning it that way, the seed of Tepenia's entire institutional memory.

## 3. Mateo's Obsession

Juan Carlos's earliest archive keeps exactly the records the Federation's own paperwork requires,
nothing more. **But** Archivist Mateo Iglesias wants the archive to be genuinely comprehensive, not
merely adequate — a personal ambition that goes well beyond what his actual mandate demands.
**Therefore** he pushes the recordkeeping far past minimum requirements, cataloguing detail after
detail nobody specifically asked for, driven by nothing but his own conviction that thoroughness
matters for its own sake. **But** that kind of personal ambition takes real, sustained effort to
sustain across an entire career. **Therefore** the archive he leaves behind carries a depth and
completeness considerably beyond what a purely dutiful bureaucrat would have produced — his own
personal obsession quietly shaping the whole institution's later reputation.

## 4. An Argument Worth Having

Juan Carlos's residents want more from their social life than ordinary gathering. **But** a fully open,
unstructured meeting risks becoming shapeless — conversation without direction or real substance.
**Therefore** the tertulia takes hold instead — a hosted gathering, guest list and implied topic
included, real intellectual expectation built into the invitation itself. **But** structuring
conversation this deliberately risks making it feel performative rather than genuine. **Therefore** it
doesn't — argument becomes something residents genuinely enjoy pursuing as its own worthwhile
practice, not a contest to be won or a tension to be avoided, exactly the register a well-run tertulia
is supposed to strike.

## 5. Everything Moves Inland

Juan Carlos's own island archive grows increasingly valuable as the Federation matures, but stays
physically isolated and administratively separate from the rest of Tepenia's growing infrastructure.
**But** a young nation building its first real communications network has real reason to consolidate
its most important records somewhere more centrally protected. **Therefore**, around the same broader
wave of institutional capability-building that produces the Arcanet, Tepenia's scattered early records
get copied and relocated to a single, better-protected central archive deeper in the continent's
interior. **But** moving the practical function elsewhere doesn't automatically move the meaning it
carried for the city that built it. **Therefore** Juan Carlos loses its literal recordkeeping role
without losing the civic identity that role had already given it.

## 6. The Federation's Memory

Juan Carlos's archive function relocates inland, taking the practical work with it. **But** the city's
own residents don't simply stop thinking of themselves as record-keeping, memory-holding people just
because the physical function moved. **Therefore** a quiet, proud self-image persists anyway,
expressed now through the tertulia tradition rather than literal archives — the same underlying
impulse toward preserved, spoken, and argued knowledge as a civic good, carried forward in a different
form. **But** claiming a title this significant without the literal function to back it up risks
sounding like empty nostalgia. **Therefore** residents wear it lightly instead, half-joking about being
"the Federation's memory" — real historical truth worn with wry, self-aware pride rather than solemn
civic reverence.

## 7. Watching Who Left

Juan Carlos's archive carries a specific, sensitive responsibility — tracking anyone who once held
office in an Upper Earth government, now living in Tepenia. **But** that kind of watch-keeping is a
delicate task, easy to handle carelessly in ways that could feel like surveillance rather than
legitimate security. **Therefore** the work develops its own careful, methodical culture — precise,
unemotional, handled with the same dutiful competence the city applies to everything else, never
treated as an opportunity for suspicion or overreach. **But** doing sensitive work discreetly means
most residents never fully appreciate how consequential it actually is. **Therefore** the watch-list
function stays exactly what its own careful handling makes it — quietly essential, almost entirely
invisible to the people living alongside it every day.

## 8. A City That Never Had to Compete

Spain's founding population settles Juan Carlos without the same density of immediate national rivals
Sejong's own founders face. **But** later immigration still arrives, gradually building toward the
USA's own eventual demographic Primary status across the wider Palmer subnet pattern. **Therefore**
Spain's founding presence dilutes more gently than most Palmer subnet founding nations — settling at
Significant tier rather than being pushed down to Notable, an unusually strong founder retention.
**But** that retention isn't really about Spain fighting to hold ground. **Therefore** it happens
simply because Juan Carlos's whole founding process was quieter and less contested from the start —
demographic gentleness following naturally from the same uncrowded circumstances that shaped
everything else about the city.

## 9. Records Kept, Rarely Discussed

Juan Carlos's archivists go about their daily work — cataloguing, filing, maintaining records —
without much sense that what they're doing is historically significant. **But** the work they're
quietly building is, in fact, becoming the seed of Tepenia's entire institutional memory. **Therefore**
a real gap opens between the city's own modest self-perception and its actual, growing importance to
the wider Federation — ordinary administrative work that nobody in the room fully recognizes as
consequential while it's happening. **But** a gap like that doesn't necessarily bother anyone, since
the work still gets done competently either way. **Therefore** Juan Carlos simply keeps doing what it
does — methodical, unglamorous, and quietly foundational — without ever quite reckoning, from the
inside, with how much weight its own ordinary routine actually carries.

## 10. What Spain Kept

Spanish exiles bring language, food, and social temperament with them to Livingston Island, expecting
those traditions to eventually dilute the way founding cultures do almost everywhere else in Tepenia.
**But** without a dense cluster of competing national communities constantly renegotiating the city's
identity, there's simply less pressure pushing Spanish heritage to blend or fade. **Therefore** it
persists with unusual clarity — recognizable, coherent, still genuinely present generations later,
distinct from how founding cultures thin out elsewhere in the Palmer subnet. **But** persistence isn't
the same as dominance — later arrivals still shift the city's overall demographic balance regardless.
**Therefore** Juan Carlos ends up holding both at once: a demographically shifting population, and a
founding culture that never had to fight as hard as most to remain genuinely legible within it.

## 11. The Labor Beneath the Argument

Juan Carlos runs two economies at once — the celebrated archive-and-tertulia tradition, and the
unglamorous fishing fleet that actually feeds the city, staffed disproportionately by its UK- and
France-descended residents. **But** nobody has ever framed the gap between the two as a problem, since
the two simply run in parallel, unremarked. **Therefore** it takes a hard coincidence to force the
question into the open — a civic anniversary observed on schedule the same week a fishing crew is lost to
Drake Passage weather, the city's fluency at ceremony suddenly, visibly, never having been extended to
the labor that keeps it fed. **But** meeting that gap honestly means changing what the city's single most
prestigious institution actually contains, not just offering a one-off memorial. **Therefore** hosting
rights at the tertulia — previously implicitly archive-and-argument-coded — are extended on record to
fishing captains and crews, maritime loss and labor becoming legitimate subjects for the same hosted,
serious argument the tradition already takes seriously, closing a gap the city itself never noticed it
was carrying.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#2 and #5** trace Juan Carlos's full institutional arc — from founding archive to national
relocation — giving the Megasheet's own central premise (a quiet city that became the origin point of
Tepenia's institutional memory) an actual causal sequence. **#6** is the most emotionally resonant
thread, since it's the only one that gives the city's own self-mythologized identity a concrete shape
surviving past the practical function that originally earned it. **#11** is the only chain that reaches
outside the archive/tertulia register entirely, giving the city's other, unglamorous economy — and the
populations who actually work it — a real stake in Juan Carlos's most prestigious civic institution.
