## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Juan Carlos's earliest archive keeps exactly the records the Federation's own paperwork requires, nothing
more — a dutiful, adequate administrative function, sized precisely to its mandate and no larger.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

One archivist wants the archive to be genuinely comprehensive, not merely adequate — a personal ambition
that goes well beyond what the actual mandate demands, pushing recordkeeping past minimum requirements,
cataloguing detail after detail nobody specifically asked for. That drive doesn't sit well with everyone
around it: sustaining this kind of ambition takes real, ongoing effort across an entire career, effort that
has to be justified, funded, and tolerated by people who see the archive's job as already finished the
moment the Federation's own requirements are met.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between an archivist convinced that
thoroughness matters for its own sake, and a colleague or overseer who thinks that conviction is excessive —
a waste of limited institutional effort, or worse, a genuine risk, since cataloguing detail "nobody asked
for" edges close to overreach for an archive already handling sensitive material about former Upper Earth
officials. A should not read as simply right and B as simply small-minded: B's caution about scope-creep and
unmandated intrusion is a legitimate institutional concern, not petty gatekeeping, even though history ends
up vindicating A's instinct.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A maps onto the proposed placeholder
figure Archivist Mateo Iglesias from `Full_Extrapolation` — a founding-era archivist driven by personal
conviction rather than official instruction. Character B is plausibly a fellow archivist, supervisor, or
Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority liaison who is professionally responsible for keeping the archive's
scope proportionate to its mandate, and who has real, defensible reasons — cost, discretion, the risk of an
already-sensitive watch-list function drifting into open-ended surveillance — to want A reined in.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A's ambition outlasts B's caution. The archive he leaves behind carries a depth and completeness
considerably beyond what a purely dutiful bureaucrat would have produced — one archivist's personal
obsession, sustained across a whole career, quietly shaping the entire institution's later reputation.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the extra cataloguing goes well past the archive's administrative mandate, it starts capturing
incidental material that has nothing to do with visas or customs at all — guest lists from the city's own
unnamed hosted gatherings, informal notes on the city's own social and cultural rhythms — turning the
archive, almost by accident, into a cultural record as much as a bureaucratic one.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the archive now holds that cultural material alongside its official records, whatever copy of it
eventually reaches Tepenia's later, centralized interior archive carries far more texture and human detail
than a purely administrative record ever would have — meaning the deep archive at the heart of the game's
later lore inherits its richness from one archivist's personal ambition, not from any policy that ever asked
for it.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Juan Carlos's archive is now genuinely comprehensive — administrative and cultural both — carrying a depth
its own founding brief never called for and that its later fate elsewhere in Tepenia will end up quietly
depending on.
