## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Juan Carlos runs two economies at once. One is celebrated: the archive, and the tertulia tradition it
fed into — hosted, guest-listed argument as the city's defining civic art form, the thing visitors and
residents alike point to when asked what Juan Carlos *is*. The other is simply worked: the fishing fleets
that actually feed the city and fund a real share of its trade, staffed disproportionately by the same
UK- and France-descended residents whose real-world civic ancestors were fishing-port people first, and
only secondarily anything else. Nobody has ever framed this as a problem. The two economies simply run
in parallel, unremarked.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A civic anniversary — the kind the city has held before without incident, honoring the archive's
founding or the tertulia tradition's own long history — falls in the same week a fishing crew is lost to
a bad stretch of Drake Passage weather. The city observes its planned ceremony on schedule, full civic
warmth intact. The fishing families bury their dead quietly, without anything comparable. The contrast
is not manufactured by anyone; it simply becomes impossible, that week, not to notice.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a conflict about which economy matters more —
both sides already agree the fishing fleet is materially essential. The real disagreement is about
recognition: Character A argues that a city this practiced at ceremony, this fluent in honoring its own
history, has simply never extended that fluency to the labor that keeps it fed, and that the omission is
no longer excusable now that it's been made visible. Character B isn't defending the imbalance
cynically — more plausibly, they're a tertulia host or archive-tradition figure who never noticed the gap
because the city's own celebrated identity was never built to look for it, and who has to reckon
honestly with that blind spot rather than simply conceding the point performatively.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a fishing captain or a
family member of the lost crew, UK- or France-descended, articulate enough to make the case publicly but
speaking from real, recent grief rather than abstract civic theory. Character B is plausibly a
long-standing tertulia host or someone connected to the archive's own civic-memory tradition — sympathetic,
not a villain, genuinely startled to realize how completely the city's celebrated register has managed
to exclude an entire half of its own daily life.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Rather than a one-off memorial offered and forgotten, the fishing community is brought directly into the
city's existing register of civic recognition: hosting rights at a tertulia, previously an implicitly
archive-and-argument-coded honor, are extended on record to fishing captains and crews, with the losses
of the sea treated as a legitimate subject for the hosted, serious argument the tertulia tradition already
takes so seriously.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The tertulia's own implicit subject matter widens. What was, in practice, an archive-and-argument-coded
tradition now formally includes maritime labor and loss as a legitimate topic worthy of the same hosted,
guest-listed seriousness — a genuine expansion of what "belongs" in Juan Carlos's single most prestigious
civic institution, not merely a symbolic gesture bolted on beside it.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Over successive generations, being a fishing captain in Juan Carlos stops reading as work that simply
funds the city's more celebrated identity from the outside, and starts reading as a full, native part of
that identity itself — UK- and France-descended maritime families gaining the same kind of standing
within the tertulia tradition that archive-descended families had always simply assumed as their own.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Juan Carlos's civic prestige now genuinely spans both of its economies rather than resting almost
entirely on one. The tertulia remains the city's defining institution, but its subject matter — and its
hosts — now include the maritime labor that always sustained the city materially, closing a gap the
city itself never noticed it was carrying until a single hard week forced it into view.
