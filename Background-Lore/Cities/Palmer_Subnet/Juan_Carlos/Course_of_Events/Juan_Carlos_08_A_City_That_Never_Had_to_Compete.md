## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Spain's founding population has settled Juan Carlos without the same density of immediate national rivals
Sejong's own founders face — a founding with genuine breathing room, not yet tested by any real wave of
later arrivals.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Later immigration still arrives, gradually building toward the USA's own eventual demographic Primary
status across the wider Palmer subnet pattern — and its arrival raises a real question nobody in Juan Carlos
has had to answer yet: should the city do anything deliberate to manage how new arrivals fold into a
community that has, until now, never had to negotiate its identity against anyone?

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over whether Juan Carlos should build
deliberate civic apparatus to manage integration as new immigrants arrive, or simply let it happen without
intervention. A worries that without some deliberate structure, new arrivals will stay socially siloed,
producing exactly the kind of unmanaged demographic tension the city has never had to deal with before. B
trusts the same quiet, uncontested dynamic that shaped the founding to keep working on its own, and worries
that any formal assimilation policy would be the first genuinely contested civic decision in the city's
history — importing exactly the kind of friction Juan Carlos has always been spared. Neither is being
alarmist; they're weighing foresight against faith in what has worked so far.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a local civic
administrator or planner, professionally inclined to build institutions ahead of need rather than react
after the fact. Character B is plausibly a longtime resident or host of the city's own gathering tradition, someone whose whole
experience of the city has been its quiet, self-organizing character, skeptical that formal policy could
improve on what informal custom has already done well.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's instinct holds. No deliberate integration campaign is ever built. Spain's founding presence dilutes more
gently than most Palmer subnet founding nations — settling at Significant tier rather than being pushed down
to Notable — not because Spain fought to hold ground, but simply because Juan Carlos's whole founding
process was quieter and less contested from the start, and that same quiet extends naturally to how later
arrivals are absorbed.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because there was never an active integration campaign, Juan Carlos never develops the kind of formal civic
apparatus for immigrant assimilation that likely exists in more demographically contested Palmer cities —
no naturalization ceremonies, no dedicated cultural-integration office, nothing built specifically to manage
the transition.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because there is no dedicated on-ramp institution, new arrivals' actual introduction to civic life happens
primarily through the city's own unnamed gathering tradition — being invited to one becomes the de facto,
informal marker of having genuinely arrived socially in Juan Carlos, folding each new wave of demographic
change directly into the city's existing intellectual culture rather than into any separate immigrant track.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Juan Carlos's demographic composition keeps shifting gently with ordinary immigration, but with no formal
assimilation apparatus ever built — newcomers are absorbed directly into the city's existing social fabric,
chiefly through its own unnamed gathering tradition, rather than through any dedicated integration institution.
