## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The young Federation needs some way to track who is entering and leaving its territory, who is applying for
Palmer City visas, who is seeking permanent immigration — an ordinary administrative need with nowhere yet
built to hold it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

No existing Tepenian institution is built to handle that kind of ongoing administrative recordkeeping — the
young Federation has plenty of urgent, visible priorities, but nothing resembling a standing bureaucracy for
paperwork nobody has thought to prioritize yet. That gap forces a decision nobody particularly wants to
make: where, physically, does a function this sensitive and this permanent actually belong? The obvious
answer — site it somewhere prominent, close to the Federation's growing centers of activity — runs headlong
into a quieter argument that a function tracking former Upper Earth officials might be better served by
somewhere less exposed to the ordinary jostling of a busier, more contested city.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a siting dispute between someone who wants this new,
foundational function built somewhere visible and central, and someone who wants it built somewhere quiet
and structurally insulated. Neither side is wrong to want what they want — A is reasoning that an
institution this important to the young Federation's future deserves proximity to power and attention; B is
reasoning that sensitive, personal recordkeeping (particularly a watch on former Upper Earth officials) is
safer and more durable when it isn't entangled in a city that's still busy negotiating its own contested
identity. This should read as two legitimate institutional philosophies in tension, not a visionary against
an obstructionist.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a mainland or Palmer
City-based early Federation administrator, invested in building the country's first visible institutions in
its most prominent subnet city, where they can be staffed, funded, and overseen easily. Character B is
plausibly a Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority official who already works with discretion as a matter
of course, and who has reason to trust Juan Carlos's already-evident quiet, coherent temperament — someone
who has seen the city's own uncontested founding firsthand and reasons that steadiness, not visibility, is
what this particular function actually needs.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's argument wins. Juan Carlos — quiet and coherent enough to take on steady institutional work without the
distraction of a more contested civic identity — becomes the site of Tepenia's first real bureaucratic
archive, built with a direct connection to the Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority and a specific
mandate to track anyone who once held office in an Upper Earth government now living in Tepenia.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the site was chosen for its remove rather than its prominence, the archive is built to answer
directly to the Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority rather than through any ordinary subnet
administrative chain — an unusual, semi-independent reporting line with no real precedent anywhere else in
the young Federation.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that bypass structure works — because insulating a sensitive function from ordinary administrative
politics turns out to be exactly the right call — later Federation officials setting up other sensitive
functions elsewhere (customs enforcement, internal audits) borrow the same reporting shape as their
template, not because Juan Carlos ever intended to set one, but because it was the only fully-trusted,
functioning example the young Federation had to copy.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Juan Carlos now houses the Federation's first foundational bureaucratic archive, operating with an unusual
degree of administrative independence that traces directly to why the site was chosen in the first place —
and, without anyone planning it that way, has quietly become the working template later Federation
institutions imitate when they need a function insulated from ordinary politics.
