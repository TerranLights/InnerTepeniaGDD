## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Juan Carlos's archive carries a specific, sensitive responsibility — tracking anyone who once held office in
an Upper Earth government, now living in Tepenia — a mandate with real weight and no established precedent
for how carefully or how aggressively it ought to be run.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

That kind of watch-keeping is a delicate task, easy to handle carelessly in ways that could feel like
surveillance rather than legitimate security — and precisely because there's no precedent, the archive's
own early staff don't automatically agree on how it should be run. Left unresolved, that disagreement is the
kind that shapes the function permanently, for better or worse, depending on which instinct wins out early.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a dispute over how assertively to run a sensitive
watch-list function. A wants the function run more aggressively — treating former Upper Earth officials as
inherent security risks worth active investigation, reasoning that the stakes are too high for a purely
passive record. B insists that overreach undermines both the exiles' trust and the archive's own long-term
legitimacy, arguing for precise, dispassionate, minimal-intrusion recordkeeping instead. A's instinct isn't
paranoid — the population being tracked genuinely includes people with real Upper Earth government ties; B's
caution isn't naive — a young Federation built partly by exiles has obvious reasons not to replicate the
surveillance culture many of them fled.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a security-minded
official, perhaps a Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority liaison, for whom the watch-list's whole purpose
is defeated by excessive restraint. Character B is plausibly a senior archivist embodying the city's own
established Head/Compliant/Competency temperament — someone whose instinct is methodical precision over
visible suspicion, and who understands that the function's long-term value depends on the people it tracks
trusting it enough not to conceal anything from it.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's restraint prevails. The work develops its own careful, methodical culture — precise, unemotional,
handled with the same dutiful competence the city applies to everything else, never treated as an
opportunity for suspicion or overreach. Doing sensitive work this discreetly means most residents never
fully appreciate how consequential it actually is; the watch-list function stays exactly what its own
careful handling makes it — quietly essential, almost entirely invisible to the people living alongside it
every day.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the function stays this discreet and restrained, it earns a level of trust from the very population
it monitors that a more suspicion-driven approach never could have — former officials living in or near
Juan Carlos have little reason to conceal or falsify their own histories, since the archive has given them
no reason to fear it.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that trust exists, the watch-list archive ends up holding an unusually complete and honest record —
people not concealing their pasts means the data itself is more reliable than an adversarial monitoring
system would ever have produced, quietly raising the real intelligence value of exactly the material Upper
Earth eventually destroys the city to deny.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Juan Carlos's watch-list function remains quietly invisible to most residents, but has earned real,
functioning trust from the very population it monitors — producing an unusually honest, high-fidelity record
whose eventual value to a hostile power is directly proportional to how well the city ever ran the function
in the first place.
