## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Juan Carlos's exile community settles on Livingston Island without the dense cluster of immediate
national neighbors Sejong's own founders have to contend with — a founding with real breathing room, not
yet shaped by anything but its own choices.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city with no competing communities to define itself against still has to decide what it actually wants
to become — the absence of outside pressure doesn't answer the question of identity, it just removes the
easy shortcut of defining itself in opposition to someone else. That open question turns inward: without
a dozen adjacent populations to negotiate against, the founders have to negotiate the question among
themselves instead — how closely to hew to the culture they left behind, how much room to leave for
something genuinely new to take shape under Antarctic conditions nothing in their old life prepared them
for.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** an internal disagreement among the founders
themselves, since there's no outside community to argue with instead. One side wants to preserve the
culture they brought with them as faithfully as possible — language, food, social custom, kept
recognizably intact as a form of continuity with everything the exile cost them. The other side sees an
opening precisely because there's no crowded field of competing identities to answer to: a chance to build
something new, shaped by Livingston Island's actual conditions rather than replicated from a homeland
they're never returning to. Both positions come from the same loyalty to what was lost, expressed in
opposite directions — one wants to keep it whole, the other wants to let it grow into something the old
country never had room to become.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an older member of the
founding generation, personally invested in preserving what's recognizable of home life as an act of
fidelity to what was sacrificed. Character B is plausibly a younger or more forward-looking settler, less
attached to replication and more interested in what the community could become now that nothing outside
is forcing its hand.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side wins outright — the community settles into a genuine synthesis, developing its own culture
with real coherence and on its own terms: recognizably descended from what the founders brought, but never
merely a transplanted replica, and never renegotiated by outside pressure the way Sejong's identity
constantly is.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the synthesis came from working the disagreement out through open conversation rather than one
side simply prevailing, that becomes the founders' habitual method for settling future cultural questions
too — disagreements about how to live get worked out through hosted discussion, not majority vote or
deference to whichever family speaks loudest.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because cultural questions keep getting settled this way, no single founding family or faction ends up
perceived, generations later, as having "won" the city's cultural direction. Juan Carlos's identity keeps
feeling collectively authored rather than imposed — a durable structural fact that traces specifically to
how that first disagreement got resolved, not merely to the fact that a disagreement happened at all.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Juan Carlos has settled into something genuinely rare in Tepenia: a founding population that got to
simply decide who it was, rather than having identity forced into shape by constant outside negotiation —
and a working habit, already established, of settling its own internal disagreements through open,
hosted conversation rather than authority or majority rule.
