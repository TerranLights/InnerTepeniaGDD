## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Juan Carlos's archivists go about their daily work — cataloguing, filing, maintaining records — without much
sense that what they're doing is historically significant. It reads, from the inside, as ordinary
administrative labor.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The work they're quietly building is, in fact, becoming the seed of Tepenia's entire institutional memory —
and that growing importance doesn't stay invisible forever. Word of the archive's genuine comprehensiveness
reaches beyond Juan Carlos itself, and outside attention raises a question the city has never had to answer:
should this function finally be treated, funded, and staffed like the nationally important institution it's
quietly become?

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over whether to formally elevate an
institution that has been quietly outgrowing its own modest reputation. A, recognizing the archive's actual
importance, wants it elevated — proper funding, proper staffing, formal recognition matching its real
significance. B resists that elevation, out of a mix of genuine institutional humility and a practical
concern that greater visibility invites exactly the kind of outside interference and oversight a discreet,
sensitive watch-list function is safer without. Both are right about something real: A about the gap between
importance and resourcing, B about what attention actually costs an institution built to work quietly.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a visiting Federation
official or auditor, encountering the archive's actual depth for the first time and reacting with genuine
alarm at how underfunded something this important has been allowed to remain. Character B is plausibly a
senior local archivist, embodying the city's own Head/Compliant/Competency temperament — someone whose
instinct is to keep doing the work well rather than draw attention to how well it's being done.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's resistance wins. No formal elevation happens. A real gap persists between the city's own modest
self-perception and its actual, growing importance to the wider Federation — but the gap doesn't
particularly bother anyone, since the work still gets done competently either way. Juan Carlos simply keeps
doing what it does — methodical, unglamorous, and quietly foundational — without ever quite reckoning, from
the inside, with how much weight its own ordinary routine actually carries.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the elevation never happens, the archive stays administratively modest and understaffed relative to
its actual importance — meaning its genuine comprehensiveness keeps depending on a small number of
committed individuals rather than becoming distributed institutional practice, leaving the whole function
more fragile than its output would ever suggest.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the function's continuity depends this heavily on so few individuals rather than any real
institutional redundancy, what those individuals personally know and carry — beyond whatever gets formally
filed — ends up mattering disproportionately to the archive's real survival. The institution was never
staffed generously enough to make personal, individually-held knowledge dispensable, which is exactly why,
generations later, what actually survives Juan Carlos's destruction is one archivist's personal backup, not
the formal system itself.

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Juan Carlos's archive remains administratively modest and quietly understaffed relative to its actual,
growing importance — its comprehensiveness resting disproportionately on a handful of committed individuals,
whose personal, informally-held knowledge ends up mattering more to the function's real continuity than any
formal institutional redundancy ever does.
