## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Spanish exiles bring language, food, and social temperament with them to Livingston Island, expecting those
traditions to eventually dilute the way founding cultures do almost everywhere else in Tepenia — a founding
culture with no particular reason yet to think it will be the exception.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Without a dense cluster of competing national communities constantly renegotiating the city's identity,
there's simply less pressure pushing Spanish heritage to blend or fade — and that unexpected staying power
raises a real question inside the community itself: now that dilution isn't inevitable after all, should
Spanish heritage be actively protected and formalized to make sure it stays dominant, or left to persist on
its own terms as the city's demographics keep shifting regardless?

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over whether a founding culture that's
persisting more easily than expected should be formally institutionalized to protect that persistence, or
left informal. A, a cultural traditionalist, worries that persistence without protection is fragile — that
as new immigrants keep arriving, only active institutionalization (formal language status, protected
cultural designation) can guarantee Spanish heritage doesn't eventually erode after all. B, a pluralist,
argues that formalizing one founding culture's dominance would be the first genuinely contested civic
decision the city has ever had to make — and that it isn't fair or necessary given how much demographic
change is already underway regardless. Both take the city's founding character seriously; they disagree
about whether protecting it requires formal defense.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Spanish-heritage
cultural figure — perhaps a tertulia host known for foregrounding Spanish language and custom — invested in
guaranteeing the culture's centrality rather than trusting to chance. Character B is plausibly a newer
arrival, or a resident with mixed heritage, who has personally benefited from the city's uncontested,
non-hierarchical civic character and doesn't want to see it replaced with a formally defended cultural
hierarchy.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither formal protection nor genuine erosion wins outright. Spanish heritage persists with unusual
clarity — recognizable, coherent, still genuinely present generations later — but purely through everyday
practice rather than any institutional defense. Persistence isn't the same as dominance: later arrivals still
shift the city's overall demographic balance regardless, exactly as they would have anyway. Juan Carlos ends
up holding both at once — a demographically shifting population, and a founding culture that never had to
fight as hard as most to remain genuinely legible within it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because Spanish heritage's persistence was never formalized into law, curriculum, or official designation,
the specific things that actually carry it forward stay informal and portable — the tertulia, particular
food and language habits practiced socially — rather than anything codified that a change in demographics or
policy could later strip away.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the culture's persistence was never formalized, its later relative decline — as the USA's own
demographic weight grows toward Primary status across the wider Palmer subnet pattern — never triggers any
political backlash or contested "culture war" moment the way a formally-protected culture losing ground
might. The transition stays as quiet and uncontested as the founding itself, extending Juan Carlos's whole
uncontested civic character all the way into its own long-run demographic future.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Juan Carlos holds a demographically shifting population and a founding Spanish culture that persists through
everyday, informal practice rather than official protection — a legacy that, having never been formally
defended, never becomes formally contested either, keeping even its long-run demographic evolution as quiet
and uncontested as the city's own founding.
