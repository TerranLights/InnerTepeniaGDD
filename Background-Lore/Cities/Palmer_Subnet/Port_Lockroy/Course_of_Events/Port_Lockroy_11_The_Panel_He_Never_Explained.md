## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Old Toby Fenwick's stone wall has grown, panel by panel, into a genuine multi-generational palimpsest —
legible as one continuous project even though no single person ever saw the whole of it built (chain #3).
Among its many contributions sits one that stands out: a panel carved decades ago, depicting a DNA helix
rendered in electrons and cosmic strings, its meaning never publicly explained by whoever made it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Someone connected to cataloguing the city's own heritage — a role Port Lockroy has never fully settled
into or out of (chain #5's living-museum tension) — sets out to document the wall panel by panel, and
finds every panel has some known story or attributed meaning except this one. The gap becomes impossible
to ignore once it's the only blank line on an otherwise complete record.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** not a conflict about whether the panel matters —
both sides agree it does, or it wouldn't be worth arguing over. Character A represents the heritage-
cataloguing impulse: a wall this legible and communal deserves a complete record, and an unexplained gap
undermines the whole idea of the wall as something the city can point to and understand together.
Character B — plausibly the original carver, still living given robot lifespans, or someone protecting
that carver's wish for privacy — represents the opposite, equally legitimate position: some contributions
were never meant to be public explanations, only private ones made visible, and forcing a plaque-worthy
meaning onto something personal would flatten exactly what made it worth carving in the first place.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a heritage-museum
custodian or archivist figure, sincere rather than bureaucratic, genuinely troubled by an incomplete
record rather than merely procedural about it. Character B is plausibly the carver themselves — a robot,
given the decades-old timeframe and the panel's own subject matter, for whom the image is a private
reflection on their own creation or continuity, not a message meant for public interpretation.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side gets a clean win. The wall's custodians agree to record which panels have known, public
explanations and which are deliberately left unexplained by their own carvers' wishes — the DNA-helix
panel formally marked as belonging to the second category, rather than force-explained or left as an
unaccounted gap.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The wall's own catalogue now carries a real, named category — "deliberately unexplained" — sitting
alongside every panel's more conventional attributed meaning. Port Lockroy's heritage-preservation
impulse has to make peace with genuine gaps rather than resolve them, a small, permanent compromise built
directly into how the city records its own history.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Over successive generations, the deliberately-unexplained panels become almost as discussed as the
explained ones — residents and visitors alike drawn specifically to guessing at meanings the wall itself
declines to confirm, adding an entirely unplanned layer to the wall's own communal mystique that no one
designed and no one could have.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The wall holds explained and deliberately-unexplained panels side by side, an even richer palimpsest than
before — Port Lockroy's own permanent, low-key tension between living memory and heritage spectacle
(chain #5) finding a small, physical, literally carved expression directly on the one artifact that was
always going to hold it.
