## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Port Lockroy's postal role has been growing the same way most of the city's other functions do —
gradually, incrementally, without any single deliberate decision ever having made it the city's defining
identity.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A subnet-wide correspondence network doesn't organize itself — someone has to keep expanding the routes,
keep adding capacity, keep the whole system actually working as demand grows, year after unglamorous year.
Generations of residents quietly make that investment, one incremental improvement at a time, never
holding a ceremony to mark any of it. But enough accumulated, unglamorous investment eventually starts
looking, from outside, exactly like deliberate design — and that gap, between what actually happened and
what it now looks like happened, becomes something someone eventually notices and wants to do something
about.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement about who gets credit for something
that was never actually planned. Character A, looking at a genuinely impressive result, wants to claim it
as the product of deliberate leadership — whether their own or a predecessor's — because a clean story of
foresight and strategy is more useful, more flattering, and easier to build authority on than "it just
grew." Character B knows, because they actually did the unglamorous work or remember who did, that no such
plan ever existed, and resists having generations of ordinary incremental effort erased in favor of a
tidier, more mythologized account crediting one person's vision.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an ambitious civic
figure, record-keeper, or someone hoping to bolster their own or a predecessor's legacy by retroactively
presenting the postal hub's growth as strategic foresight. Character B is plausibly a working-level
postal or logistics figure, or an archivist, who has direct, unglamorous knowledge of how the network
actually got built — grounded in Port Lockroy's own established culture of quiet, undramatic accumulation
rather than declared destiny.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The honest account wins out. By the time anyone thinks seriously to ask how Port Lockroy became the
subnet's postal center, the answer that sticks — the one actually recorded and repeated — is the true one:
nobody ever formally decided it. It simply kept growing, the same way Old Toby always quietly suspected it
would.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the honest, unglamorous account becomes the accepted civic understanding rather than the
mythologized one, Port Lockroy develops a genuine institutional habit of self-effacement — official and
informal accounts of the postal hub's development explicitly credit "generations of residents" rather
than any single leader or decisive moment, a framing that starts shaping how the city talks about its
achievements generally, not just this one.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That self-effacing framing eventually becomes something Port Lockroy has to work against rather than
simply live comfortably inside. When the city later needs to make a case for formal subnet-wide
recognition, investment, or authority commensurate with its now genuinely real importance, it finds itself
institutionally unpracticed at claiming credit — generations spent insisting "nobody planned this, it just
grew" leave it without a ready vocabulary for asserting its own significance, even once that significance
is undeniable.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Port Lockroy's postal-hub identity is now genuinely mature and undeniable, remembered honestly as the
accumulated, unglamorous work of generations rather than any deliberate master plan — but that very
honesty has become a mild institutional liability, leaving the city quietly unpracticed at asserting the
formal recognition its real importance would otherwise justify.
