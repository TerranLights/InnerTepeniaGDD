## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Port Lockroy's original purpose, long before any exile ever arrived to live here, was watching the South
Atlantic for naval activity — a specific, narrow wartime function from an era nobody currently living
remembers firsthand.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A purpose that specific, tied to a threat that no longer exists, could easily fade out of civic memory
once the war it was built for is centuries in the past — nobody currently alive has any personal reason
to keep thinking about it. That risk becomes a live question once someone actually raises it: should the
community deliberately do something to keep the site's original purpose alive, or let it recede
naturally into the kind of history nobody bothers to actively remember.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement about whether deliberately reviving a
dead threat's memory is meaningful commemoration or empty theater. Character A wants an actual, structured
practice — an annual vigil, something concrete enough to survive the generations that never lived through
the reason for it — worried that without deliberate effort, the site's whole original reason for existing
just quietly disappears. Character B isn't dismissing the past so much as doubting the specific method:
reenacting a wartime watch in a time of ordinary peace, with no real threat to actually watch for, risks
becoming a hollow performance that insults the memory of people who kept watch when it actually mattered —
worried the ritual would mean less, not more, once staged rather than lived.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic-minded resident
invested in institutional memory, perhaps someone who feels a personal pull toward the site's founding
purpose even without any direct connection to it. Character B is plausibly a pragmatic peer or younger
resident, skeptical of ceremony for its own sake, wary of performing vigilance that costs nothing and
risks nothing — grounded in Port Lockroy's own established tension between living memory and staged
heritage spectacle.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The vigil gets instituted — but shaped, in the arguing, by exactly the concern that nearly stopped it:
kept modest and quiet rather than triumphant or theatrical, closer to an actual overnight watch than a
festival. It doesn't feel like empty theater. It becomes genuinely meaningful, a quiet acknowledgment that
the whole site's existence traces back to people watching for something, long before anyone lived here at
all.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the vigil keeps real emotional weight instead of curdling into obligation, it starts attracting
genuine, voluntary participation across generations — not just descendants of anyone connected to the
original watch-post function, but newer residents choosing to take part because the observance actually
means something to them personally, not because a calendar tells them to.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That voluntary, cross-generational participation turns Founders' Watch into something Port Lockroy
starts extending outward rather than keeping strictly internal — couriers and traders passing through on
the right night get invited to take a turn at the watch themselves, making it a small, modest point of
subnet-wide familiarity with Port Lockroy specifically as a place that keeps watch, well before the
postal-hub identity has matured into anything comparable.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Founders' Watch is now a firmly established, genuinely meaningful civic institution — voluntary,
cross-generational, and modestly known beyond Port Lockroy itself as an early marker of the city's
character, standing on its own well before its later postal destiny gives it a second reputation to grow
into.
