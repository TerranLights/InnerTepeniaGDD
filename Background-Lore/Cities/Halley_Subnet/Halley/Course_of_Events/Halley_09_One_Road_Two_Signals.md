## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Hwy 59 is built, from early on, to serve two purposes at once along a single shared route: a physical
highway connecting Halley outward, and the Arcanet cable corridor running south to Amundsen Station.
The combined design works well, and for a long stretch of years nobody has reason to question it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Maintaining both functions along the same physical route means any disruption to the highway
threatens Halley's communications link to the Pole too, not just its travel route — a shared
vulnerability nobody has had to actually confront yet. Then the ice shelf's own ongoing drift finally
forces the issue: the highway's Halley-end anchor point has to be relocated, and engineers face a
genuinely doubled project no other Tepenian highway effort has ever had to manage — rerouting the
physical road and preserving the cable's continuity together, as one combined undertaking rather than
two separate ones.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a safety-versus-cost conflict over whether to
finally split the two functions apart. A wants to split the physical road and the Arcanet corridor onto
genuinely separate routes going forward — safer, since one disruption can no longer threaten both
functions at once, but far more expensive to build and maintain than the current combined design. B
argues the combined corridor is the only version Halley can actually afford, given how much of the
city's finite labor and resources already go toward the propulsion fleet, the resupply relationship
with Belgrano and Sanay, and everything else the moving city has to fund at once — and that duplicating
infrastructure is a luxury a city with Halley's particular resource constraints simply can't justify,
however much safer it would be in the abstract.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a communications or
Arcanet-relay engineer, someone who deals directly with what it actually means when a route carrying
both physical and informational traffic goes down at once, and who has seen the doubled-disruption risk
up close. Character B is plausibly a civic resource planner or budget-holder, someone accountable for
weighing this specific investment against everything else Halley's limited surplus has to cover.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's resource argument prevails. Halley commits to keeping the highway and the Arcanet corridor
combined, and the anchor-point relocation goes forward as a single engineering effort — road and cable
rerouted together, on the same new alignment, rather than split apart.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Keeping the corridor combined requires a workforce that doesn't quite exist yet as a distinct trade:
crews trained in both physical roadwork and Arcanet cable maintenance together. Halley develops one,
purpose-built for exactly this dual-function upkeep — a combined engineering discipline that has no
real equivalent anywhere else in the subnet, where road crews and cable crews have never had reason to
be the same people.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That specialized combined-trade crew becomes, over time, one of Halley's more exportable skills. When
other subnet infrastructure projects run into comparable dual-function challenges, they end up
requesting Halley's own crews specifically — giving Halley a quiet, practical form of subnet-wide
reputation and occasional demand for its technical labor that has nothing to do with the Sanay-nexus
naming arrangement, and everything to do with a workforce that only exists because the corridor stayed
combined.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Hwy 59 remains a single combined corridor, carrying both highway traffic and the Arcanet cable link
south together, exactly as it always has. Its upkeep now belongs to a uniquely specialized Halley
engineering trade — one that has, in turn, become a quiet source of subnet-wide reputation and
occasional export demand for the city's technical labor, an unplanned benefit of a decision made purely
to save money.
