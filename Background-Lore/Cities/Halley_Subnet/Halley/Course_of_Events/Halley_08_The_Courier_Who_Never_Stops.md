## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

An itinerant Atlantic-subnet courier becomes known to Halley's port the same way she's known at every
other stop along her circuit — a familiar, welcome, but never-settling presence, passing through again
and again without ever claiming one city as hers.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Halley's own residents, watching her pass through so many times over so many years, start treating her
almost as one of their own anyway — an affection that grows precisely because she keeps returning, even
though returning was never a promise. That growing attachment eventually produces something concrete:
an offer, made in good faith, that she give up the circuit and take a fixed post at Halley's port
instead. But accepting would undo the exact thing that makes her valuable in the first place — her
worth as a courier depends entirely on continuous movement between ports, not on being anyone's
resident specialist.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict between someone whose entire identity
and value is bound up in never settling, and someone who has grown genuinely attached and wants to
convert an informal, itinerant relationship into a stable, institutional one. B's motive isn't only
sentimental — a permanently posted courier-liaison would also be more reliable for Halley's own
coordination needs than gambling on catching her whenever she happens to pass through. A's refusal
isn't rejection of Halley specifically; accepting anywhere would mean giving up the exact thing she is.
This should read as mutual respect colliding with incompatible needs, not as one side being wrong.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is the itinerant courier role
itself, grounded in Salagéa Aparast as established. Character B is plausibly a Halley harbor or
logistics coordinator — someone with real, personal fondness for her built up over years of her
regular visits, who also has a legitimate institutional stake in wanting a dependable courier presence
rather than an irregular one.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

She declines the post, explaining her reasoning plainly rather than simply vanishing from the
conversation — that the value Halley itself has come to rely on from her only exists because she never
stops moving. Halley's port accepts the answer without bitterness, formally choosing to keep the
relationship exactly as informal and voluntary as it's always been, rather than losing her goodwill
over an offer that was never going to be accepted.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Unable to get her to settle, Halley's port administration redirects the impulse behind the offer into
something more generalizable: a semi-official waystation courtesy, extended not just to her but to any
itinerant courier passing through — guaranteed provisioning, priority dock access, and standing terms
that ask nothing of anyone's mobility in return.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Once that waystation courtesy exists as standing policy, word of it travels the same courier circuits
Salagéa herself runs — and other itinerant Atlantic-subnet couriers start making Halley a more frequent
stop specifically because of it. Halley doesn't gain one settled resident out of the whole episode; it
gains a subnet-wide reputation as the friendliest port on the circuit for people who, like Salagéa,
have no intention of ever settling anywhere.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Halley never got its own Notable Figure out of the failed offer — but it gained something that fits
its own established character more naturally: a reputation, earned and maintained, as the port where
itinerant couriers are treated best precisely because nobody there expects them to stop being
itinerant. The same instinct that let Halley trade its Arcanet nexus for a name now shows up again here
— giving up the concrete goal (a settled courier) in favor of something less tangible but, in the end,
more valuable (a whole circuit's goodwill).
