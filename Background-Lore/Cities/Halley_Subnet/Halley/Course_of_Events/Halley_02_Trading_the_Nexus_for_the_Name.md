## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Halley has the labor, the skill, and the collective will to build the subnet's own Arcanet relay
nexus with its own hands — a project residents expect, going in, to be theirs: built by Halley,
staffed by Halley, controlled by Halley.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The bedrock survey settles the site question before the labor question ever gets asked: Sanay's ground
is the only stable footing the nexus can actually be built on, full stop, no matter whose hands do the
building. Halley commits its own crews to the project anyway — building, plank by plank and cable by
cable, an installation it will not itself house, on ground it does not control. Then the second
question arrives right behind the first: the subnet still needs a name, and everyone up and down the
coast has been calling it "Halley" for so long already that inventing a new one would be its own kind
of absurd. The city that built the nexus by hand is about to watch both the nexus and the subnet's
name settle in different places.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation over whether labor should convert
into leverage. A has put real, sustained sweat equity into a project that will sit on someone else's
ground, and believes that ought to buy Halley something concrete — operational say, a standing role in
how the nexus is run, some claim beyond having simply done the work. B isn't arguing in bad faith:
geography settled where the nexus sits before anyone laid a single cable, and renegotiating control now
would mean reopening a site decision that was never actually a choice, straining a fragile, still-young
subnet coordination arrangement over a fight nobody can actually win. Neither side is wrong about the
facts; they disagree about what the facts obligate.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly the lead of Halley's
own construction crew — someone who has spent years personally overseeing the nexus build and feels its
completion in her hands, not just on a ledger. Character B is plausibly a Sanay-based technical
administrator or subnet-wide coordination figure, someone whose job is keeping fragile inter-city
arrangements from fracturing over disputes that, however sympathetic, threaten to unwind more than
they'd resolve. The conflict reads best as two people who each respect the other's claim and still
can't agree on what it's worth.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The dispute resolves the way the source events describe: Halley keeps the subnet's naming honor, and
Sanay keeps the nexus's actual operational control — a split nobody formally negotiates so much as
lets happen, because by the time the naming question is settled, pressing further on the control
question would cost more good will than anyone's willing to spend on it. The trade goes unrenegotiated
for generations, not because it was ever declared final, but because nobody minds it enough, out loud,
to reopen it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The episode leaves behind more than a settled dispute — it leaves a working precedent for how the
subnet resolves "who built it" against "who holds it" whenever the two diverge again. Naming credit
and operational control start getting treated, informally but consistently, as two separable
currencies rather than one — a norm future infrastructure disputes across the subnet default to
without anyone having to relitigate the original nexus argument each time.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that separable-currency norm becomes the subnet's default, later infrastructure disputes
across Halley, Sanay, and Belgrano get resolved unusually cleanly compared to other subnets — but the
norm has a quieter cost. Since renegotiating a settled credit/control split now reads as
norm-breaking, not just unusual, the original construction crew's descendants inherit a low-grade,
never-quite-vocalized resentment that has nowhere legitimate to go. The norm that keeps the subnet's
infrastructure disputes calm is the same norm that keeps this one specific grievance permanently
unaddressed.

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The Halley Subnet runs on a generations-old, unwritten rule: who names a thing and who controls it
don't have to be the same city, and pressing to make them the same reads as bad form. Halley holds the
name the whole subnet uses and none of the switch that runs its technical heart — a settled fact on
the surface, and underneath it, in the families whose hands actually built the nexus, a dormant,
un-litigated grievance that has never once been the reason for open conflict, only ever the reason
one might eventually happen.
