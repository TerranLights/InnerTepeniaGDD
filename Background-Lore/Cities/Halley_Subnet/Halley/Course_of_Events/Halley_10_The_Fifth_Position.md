## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Generations of continuous relocation have put real, cumulative distance between Halley's current
position and its original founding site — the city is several moves deep into its own history, the
founding ground now a distant, half-abstract reference point rather than anywhere residents actually
go.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The founding position was never actually protected — it was simply left behind, generation after
generation, as the city kept moving on schedule. Sitting closer to the calving edge with every passing
year, it finally reaches the point Position Day's own survey records have been tracking toward for a
long time: confirmation arrives that the original site has calved into the sea entirely, gone rather
than merely abandoned. Losing it changes nothing about the city's actual day-to-day survival — Halley
moved on from that ground generations ago, and nothing practical depends on it anymore. But the loss
still lands, and it lands as something the city has to actually respond to, even with nothing at stake
but meaning.

## Conflict

**Character A:** *(left blank)*

**Character B:** *(left blank)*

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a conflict over whether to mark the loss at all.
A wants some form of formal commemoration — a monument, a documented expedition to record the exact
site before it's fully gone, an official period of acknowledgment — treating the founding ground's
disappearance as something that deserves real civic weight. B resists, not from indifference, but
because Halley's whole founding philosophy has always been to move on without regret, and any large
ceremony risks reading as backsliding into the sentimentality the city's own culture was built to
reject — echoing the same instinct that kept Halley from ever writing a formal contingency plan for its
own failure. Neither side is being sentimental for its own sake or coldly dismissive for its own sake;
they disagree about what a city that trades concrete ground for forward motion owes its own past.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a figure close to
Position Day's own tradition — perhaps akin to the "Position Keeper" role already proposed for Halley
(`Full_Extrapolation` §VIII), someone whose whole civic function is tracking exactly this kind of fact
and who feels its weight more directly than most residents do. Character B is plausibly a pragmatic
civic leader or engineer, someone whose authority and self-image are bound up in Halley's
forward-only confidence, wary of anything that might reintroduce doubt.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither a large official monument nor total silence wins out. The compromise is small and specific:
that year's Position Day survey log receives a formal, permanent addendum — a brief, deliberately
worded entry marking the founding site's confirmed loss — and a modest marker is placed at Halley's
current position, dedicated to "the ground we started from," rather than at the vanished site itself.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

That small addendum doesn't stay a one-time entry. Over subsequent years, Position Day's annual survey
ritual comes to include a brief, recurring reference to the calved founding site as a standing part of
the ceremony — not a fresh reckoning each time, but a short, familiar beat folded into a ritual
residents already gather for every year regardless.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that recurring beat exists, generations who never lived through the actual loss grow up with
the founding site's disappearance already folded into an ordinary yearly civic habit, rather than
inheriting it as urgent grief needing its own reckoning. The loss gets fully metabolized into routine —
in effect proving Character B's original instinct right in the long run, that Halley really can absorb
even its most symbolically weighty loss without lasting sentimental damage — but only because
Character A's small, contested compromise gave that process somewhere structured to actually happen.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The founding site is fully gone, and has been for long enough that its absence no longer registers as
loss to most residents at all — just one familiar beat in a yearly ritual they've always known. What
began as a genuine argument over how much weight the city's own past deserves resolved into proof, a
generation later, that Halley's whole philosophy held even under its most symbolically weighty test —
precisely because someone once insisted the loss be marked, however small the marking turned out to be.
