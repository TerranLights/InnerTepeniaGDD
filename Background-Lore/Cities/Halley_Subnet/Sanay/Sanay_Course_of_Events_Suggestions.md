# Sanay — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note (revised):** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during
the Second Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no
post-war/present-day material. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Sanay's Megasheet (the
subnet's real technical backbone and working port, "Competence Without Commentary," bedrock
endurance, the Trade Yard). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Endurance, Not Exemption

A sustained, subnet-wide storm system — the worst in a generation — batters Sanay's port and
shipyards directly, bedrock foundation or not. **But** unlike a city built on unstable ice, Sanay's
foundation holds even as real, visible damage piles up across the exposed dockside infrastructure.
**Therefore** Sanay comes out the other side of the storm hurt but still functioning, still shipping,
still working, while neighboring cities lose weeks of capacity outright. **But** outside observers
keep wanting to frame that as luck or exemption — as though Sanay simply got spared the worst of it.
**Therefore** the city's whole civic self-image forms around correcting that assumption: it wasn't
spared, it endured, and the difference matters enough to become the defining fact residents insist on.

## 2. The Nexus Nobody Named After Us

Sanay's own workforce builds and maintains the subnet's actual Arcanet relay nexus, woven right into
the same bedrock as its port. **But** "Halley subnet" was already the name everyone used long before
the nexus existed. **Therefore** the technical heart of the whole subnet's communications sits inside
Sanay while the honor of the name sits somewhere else entirely. **But** this isn't a one-time
insult — Sanay also does the real, unglamorous heavy lifting for the subnet's shipping while a
different city gets treated as the more prominent name. **Therefore** "does the work, someone else
gets the name" becomes a pattern repeated twice at two different scales, training Sanay's whole civic
psychology into treating uncredited function as simply how things are.

## 3. Function Over Sentiment

With the shipyards and the residential districts both damaged and limited resources to go around,
Sanay's leadership faces a real choice about what gets rebuilt first. **But** the port's continued
function determines whether the whole subnet keeps receiving South African shipments at all.
**Therefore** rebuilding priority goes to the shipyards, fast and first, ahead of resident comfort.
**But** that decision has to be made and defended openly, not just assumed. **Therefore** "function
over sentiment" stops being an abstract civic value and becomes a specific, remembered decision — the
moment Sanay chose, out loud, what it was actually willing to sacrifice for.

## 4. The Cost Nobody Complains About

Years after the shipyards are fully restored, residents of the cliffside neighborhoods are still
living with unrepaired damage the port itself never had. **But** complaining would mean questioning
"function over sentiment," the value the whole city takes pride in. **Therefore** the cost stays
mostly unspoken — felt, not voiced, cutting along a purely functional line (dockworker versus
everyone else) that doesn't match any of Sanay's other social divisions. **But** unspoken doesn't mean
unnoticed. **Therefore** a resident who finally does say something out loud forces the city to
confront, for the first time, whether its proudest civic value has actually been paid for evenly or
not.

## 5. The Rhythm They Set Together

South African freighters arrive at Sanay first; from there, goods split toward nearby cities or
onward to Troll's airfield for wider distribution. **But** the two cities initially schedule their
own operations independently, each optimizing for itself — freight sitting at Sanay's docks while
Troll's runway capacity goes half-used, or the reverse, costing both cities real time and money.
**Therefore** representatives from both cities negotiate a shared, coordinated schedule for the first
time, formally tying Sanay's unloading rhythm to Troll's flight capacity. **But** that coordination
means neither city can unilaterally change its own operations without consulting the other anymore.
**Therefore** Sanay and Troll become genuinely, formally interdependent — a relationship built to
save both cities money that neither one can quietly walk back once it's in place.

## 6. The Trade Yard

South African freighter crews arriving at Sanay's port initially move through the city with no
formal boundaries on where they go or who they deal with. **But** a city with real reasons to stay
guarded even while profiting from Upper Earth contact can't leave that access completely open
indefinitely. **Therefore** a specific, bounded dockside zone gets formally established — freighter
crews welcome to dock, unload, and conduct business, but within clearly defined limits rather than
free run of the city. **But** drawing that line means Sanay has to decide, deliberately, how much
contact is worth the trade. **Therefore** the Trade Yard becomes Sanay's own answer to a question
every Upper-Earth-facing Tepenian city eventually has to face, distinct from Palmer City's far more
open approach just a subnet away.

## 7. Orchards We Forgot We Grew

Sanay's founding mission was genuine scientific research — the actual SANAE program the city
inherited its infrastructure from. **But** the port and shipyard economy that grows up around that
research eventually dwarfs it completely. **Therefore** the scientific mission survives only through
a shrinking number of dedicated researchers, still doing real work but no longer anything close to
what the city is actually known for. **But** eventually the last researcher who remembers doing it
the founding-era way retires, or dies, without an obvious successor. **Therefore** SANAE's legacy
finishes its transition from living work into pure institutional memory — present in place names and
ceremonial vocabulary, invisible in what anyone actually spends their working day doing.

## 8. What the Budget Decided

The port's revenue starts dwarfing what Sanay's original SANAE-descended scientific mission brings in,
and both compete for the same limited civic investment. **But** a city built directly on a research
station's own infrastructure doesn't abandon its founding mission without a real argument over
priorities first. **Therefore** a formal budget dispute plays out — port infrastructure versus
continued science funding — testing where Sanay's actual loyalties lie. **But** the numbers make the
outcome nearly inevitable: the port is what actually keeps the whole city running. **Therefore**
science funding survives, but capped, a small, formally protected minority share — the SANAE heritage
shifting from primary civic mission to preserved tradition well before anyone thinks to call it that.

## 9. German Tide

Every other Halley subnet city's later immigration eventually resolves into an American Primary
population. **But** Sanay sits close enough to Neumayer's own substantial German community for a
different current to reach it first and hardest. **Therefore** German immigration, not American,
becomes Sanay's dominant later wave, diluting the founding South African population down to Notable
tier by an entirely different route than its neighbors took. **But** nobody at the time treats this
as remarkable — it simply happens, the way demographic tides happen everywhere. **Therefore** Sanay
ends up genuinely unusual within its own subnet's pattern, a fact its own residents never make a
point of discussing, consistent with a city that doesn't consider its own story exceptional even when
the numbers say otherwise.

## 10. Competence Without Commentary

A visiting official or journalist, impressed by how Sanay came through the great subnet-wide storm
(see #1), publicly frames the city
as a fortunate exception — spared where others weren't. **But** that framing directly contradicts
everything Sanay's own civic identity is built on. **Therefore** a local councilor responds in kind,
publicly and specifically declining the "exceptional" label, insisting on endurance rather than luck
in terms plain enough that the correction itself becomes locally famous. **But** correcting the
record once doesn't stop outsiders from making the same assumption again elsewhere. **Therefore** the
councilor's specific refusal becomes something close to civic doctrine — "Competence Without
Commentary" stops being just a description of how Sanay behaves and becomes something residents can
point to and quote.

## 11. The Record Nobody Meant to Get Wrong

Sanay's early dock economy staffs freighter-unloading crews with humans and robots working side by
side, roles assigned by practical fit rather than any formal hierarchy favoring either. **But** during
a dockside emergency — cargo shifting dangerously mid-unload — it's a robot dockworker's split-second
judgment call that actually prevents disaster, while the official incident report, written under old,
unconscious Upper-Earth-carried habits, credits the human supervisor on duty instead. **Therefore** the
robot crew and their human allies push to have the record corrected — not out of resentment, but a
simple insistence on accuracy. **But** fixing one report doesn't fix the pattern; smaller versions of
the same unconscious miscrediting keep recurring in the weeks after. **Therefore** Sanay's dock
leadership stops correcting incidents one at a time and instead formally reweights operational
authority on the docks — not toward perfect parity, but a deliberate, conscious tilt calibrated
specifically to counteract the bias it had just caught itself making, becoming the exact "slightly
better for robots" baseline the docks still run on generations later, later generalized project-wide as
Tepenia's own default human-robot standard.

## Worth Your Attention

**#11** is the highest-stakes addition here despite being purely local in setting — per the Catalog's
own note, this is where Tepenia's project-wide default human-robot labor baseline was first
established, making this chain's origin story load-bearing well beyond Sanay's own borders.

**#3 and #4** work best read together — the decision that founds "function over sentiment" as a value,
and the uneven cost that decision quietly produces, giving the Megasheet's own Finding 2 (a real,
unremarked-upon asymmetry) an actual before-and-after shape. **#6** is the most immediately useful for
any future Upper Earth-facing questline, since it gives Sanay's trade posture a concrete origin
distinct from Palmer City's, exactly the contrast the Megasheet's own synthesis flags as worth
keeping in mind.
