## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Sanay's own workforce builds and maintains the subnet's actual Arcanet relay nexus, woven right into
the same bedrock as the port itself rather than sitting apart in its own dedicated facility — a
genuine, technically demanding piece of national infrastructure that happens to live inside Sanay's
city limits.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

"Halley subnet" was already the name everyone used long before the nexus existed, so the technical
heart of the whole subnet's communications sits inside Sanay while the honor of the name sits
somewhere else entirely. It isn't a one-time insult, either — Sanay also does the real, unglamorous
heavy lifting for the subnet's shipping while a different city gets treated as the more prominent name.
"Does the work, someone else gets the name" repeats at two different scales inside the same city's
lived experience, and the repetition is what starts to matter: once is a fact nobody needs to think
about twice; twice starts to look like a pattern somebody at the nexus facility eventually notices
out loud.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone who wants Sanay's
uncredited technical role formally acknowledged and someone who believes seeking that acknowledgment is
itself a mistake. This is the project's own open question (Finding 1's 4th-order effect) given an
actual dramatic shape: is Sanay's lack of complaint genuine equanimity, or a resentment the city's own
values keep anyone from voicing? Neither side should be simply right. A has a real grievance and a
reasonable case that quiet competence has limits — recognition can mean real things, like investment,
staffing, or leverage in subnet-level decisions. B isn't in denial or servile; B's resistance is a
genuine belief that campaigning for credit is exactly the kind of behavior that would make Sanay into
a city known for grievance instead of a city known for getting things done — and a practical worry that
drawing outside attention to a strategically vital nexus facility is its own kind of risk.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a younger engineer
or administrator at the nexus facility, not yet fully absorbed into the older civic habit of not
minding, who drafts something concrete — a petition, a formal proposal — asking for the subnet's
naming or funding structure to reflect where the actual technical backbone lives. Character B is
plausibly a veteran coordinator or civic elder who has spent a career inside "Competence Without
Commentary" as a lived practice, not just an inherited slogan, and who has both an ideological and a
practical reason to want the question dropped rather than escalated.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The petition circulates informally through the nexus facility and the wider civic administration, but
it never reaches a clean vote or a clean refusal — no one openly kills it, it simply never arrives at a
decision point, buried under the ordinary press of running a working port and a working relay nexus at
the same time. The push for formal credit dissolves through attrition rather than defeat, and the
pattern the trigger described — uncredited function treated as simply how things are — holds.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The engineers and administrators who pushed hardest for recognition are also, disproportionately, the
ones who start looking for postings elsewhere in the years that follow — not in protest, exactly, just
a quiet drift toward cities like Halley or Concordia where the same technical skill set comes attached
to more visible standing.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That drift is small but consistent enough, over time, to change who the nexus facility's workforce
actually is: increasingly self-selected toward people who are personally comfortable with anonymous,
uncredited technical work, since the ones who weren't have gradually filtered themselves out. The
"Competence Without Commentary" culture stops being just an inherited civic story and becomes, quite
literally, who gets hired and who stays.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Sanay still hosts the nexus, still uncredited, and the question of whether that arrangement is
genuinely fine or quietly resented has, in effect, answered itself by attrition — the people most
likely to have kept pushing the question are elsewhere now, and the workforce that remains is
increasingly built from people who never found the arrangement worth pushing on in the first place.
