## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The port's revenue has grown to dwarf what Sanay's original SANAE-descended scientific mission brings
in, and the two now compete openly for the same limited civic investment every budget cycle.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city built directly on a research station's own infrastructure doesn't abandon its founding mission
without a real argument over priorities first — the imbalance in revenue doesn't automatically translate
into an imbalance in what the city believes it owes its own origins. That tension forces a formal budget
dispute into the open: port infrastructure versus continued science funding, argued as a genuine test of
where Sanay's actual loyalties lie rather than settled by default.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone arguing for
science funding on grounds of obligation and ongoing value, and someone arguing that resources should
track economic reality. B's position shouldn't read as philistine — the port genuinely is what keeps a
quarter-million residents fed and employed, and diverting real money from it has real human costs of its
own. A's position shouldn't read as pure nostalgia — the strongest version of this argument points to
research that still produces genuine value (bedrock and geological understanding relevant to the city's
own infrastructure, for instance), not just sentimental attachment to the founding mission. The dispute
is strongest when both characters are arguing from legitimate stewardship of the city, not from
personal stakes in one budget line winning.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a research
administrator or senior scientist — potentially the same figure, or a colleague of the figure, from
Suggestion #7's fading mentorship line — making the formal case for continued investment. Character B is
plausibly a budget officer or senior port administrator whose job is balancing the city's books against
what the port itself actually requires to keep functioning at the scale the whole subnet depends on.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The numbers make the outcome nearly inevitable: the port is what actually keeps the whole city running,
and no argument about heritage can outweigh that fact in a straight competition for funds. Science
funding survives, but capped — a small, formally protected minority share rather than a competitive
claim renewed each cycle. The SANAE heritage shifts from primary civic mission to preserved tradition,
codified in a budget line well before anyone thinks to call it that.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the allocation is written as a protected floor rather than merely tolerated as this year's
leftover, it becomes politically difficult to touch — future administrations can't simply zero it out or
quietly starve it further without formally amending the charter that protects it.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That specific mechanism — a small, legally protected floor rather than an open-ended, unprotected
allocation — becomes the model Sanay's civic government reaches for whenever some other underfunded but
valued civic function later needs the same kind of protection. The science-funding fight doesn't just
settle one dispute; it hands the city a reusable piece of governing machinery it didn't have before.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Science funding at Sanay is small but untouchable, protected by a specific charter mechanism that
outlived the dispute that created it. The fight over the founding mission's budget ended up giving the
city something more durable than either side's original argument: a permanent tool for protecting
whatever it decides to protect next.
