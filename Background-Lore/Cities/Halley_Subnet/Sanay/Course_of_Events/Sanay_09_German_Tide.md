## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Every other Halley subnet city's later immigration eventually resolves into an American Primary
population. Sanay, so far, is following no different a track than its neighbors — its founding South
African population still anchors the city's official identity, even as newer arrivals steadily
change who actually lives there.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Sanay sits close enough to Neumayer's own substantial German community for a different current to reach
it first and hardest. German immigration, not American, becomes Sanay's dominant later wave, diluting
the founding South African population down to Notable tier by an entirely different route than its
neighbors took. Nobody at the time treats this as remarkable — it simply happens, the way demographic
tides happen everywhere — until enough time passes that the shift is undeniable: a city whose place-names
and founding ceremonies are South African in flavor now has more residents with German heritage than
any other single background, and generations who grew up inside that new majority start to notice the
mismatch for themselves.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a now-majority population
asking for visibility proportional to its actual numbers, and a keeper of the founding identity worried
that granting it means overwriting what anchors the city's official self-image. This is a version of
exactly the tension the Durban parallel points to — numerical majority against institutional legacy —
and it plays best if neither side is asking for the other's erasure. A isn't trying to rename the city
or replace its South African foundations; A wants the city's public civic life to actually reflect who's
living in it now. B isn't denying the demographic reality; B is protecting something that, though
numerically outnumbered, still functions as the anchor the whole city's official identity is built on,
and worries that formally elevating a newer identity means the older one quietly stops being anyone's
priority to maintain.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a civic or cultural
figure from within the now-majority German-descended population — someone proposing a specific,
concrete change (a public holiday, a cultural institution, formal bilingual civic naming) rather than an
abstract complaint. Character B is plausibly a keeper of the SANAE-founding heritage, potentially
connected to the same institutional lineage as Suggestion #7's research tradition or Suggestion #8's
protected heritage funding — someone whose civic role depends on the founding identity remaining
Sanay's clear anchor.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The dispute resolves the way Durban's own history resolved it: layered rather than replaced. Sanay
formally adds recognized German-heritage civic elements alongside its existing SANAE-derived ones,
without displacing the founding identity from its anchoring role. Sanay ends up genuinely unusual within
its own subnet's demographic pattern, and — for the first time — the city has actually examined that
fact out loud instead of simply living inside it unremarked-upon.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Formally layering the two heritages produces a specific new civic practice — a joint annual observance,
or a bilingual (Afrikaans-inflected and German) naming convention applied to new civic institutions going
forward — giving the city an actual, deliberate mechanism for holding two heritages at once rather than
letting them simply coexist by accident.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that mechanism now exists and has already been tested once, it becomes the template Sanay's
civic government reaches for the next time a demographic shift raises the same underlying question —
turning what used to be a permanently unspoken curiosity into a reusable civic tool, on the books and
ready, rather than something the city would otherwise have had to work out from scratch all over again.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Sanay now carries a formally dual-layered civic heritage — South African founding, German demographic
majority — examined and settled once rather than left permanently unspoken. True to form, the city
still doesn't make a public point of any of it; it simply works, quietly, the way everything else about
Sanay does.
