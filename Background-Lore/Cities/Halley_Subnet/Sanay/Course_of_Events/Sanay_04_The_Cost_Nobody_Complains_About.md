## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Years after the shipyards are fully restored, residents of the cliffside neighborhoods are still living
with unrepaired damage the port itself never had to carry. The imbalance is old news by now — nobody's
raised it in a long time.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Complaining would mean questioning "function over sentiment," the value the whole city takes pride in
and formally committed itself to in the open hearing that settled it. So the cost stays mostly
unspoken — felt, not voiced, cutting along a purely functional line, dockworker versus everyone else,
that doesn't match any of Sanay's other social divisions: not national heritage, not human-or-robot,
just whether your daily environment happened to be the thing that got fixed first. Unspoken doesn't
mean unnoticed, though — the residents living with it every day haven't stopped noticing, they've just
stopped saying so.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone finally willing to
say the unspoken cost out loud and someone invested in keeping it unspoken — not out of malice, but out
of a real fear that reopening the question threatens something the city worked hard to earn. A isn't
a complainer by temperament; the whole point of this suggestion is that voicing this is genuinely
difficult inside Sanay's own civic culture, so A's decision to speak should read as costly, not casual.
B's resistance is equally legible: reopening the allocation question means re-litigating a resolution
that took real civic effort to settle once already, and risks reading as an attack on "function over
sentiment" itself rather than a request to finally pay its bill.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an ordinary cliffside
resident — not a professional advocate, which is what makes the moment land, since it's not someone
whose job is complaining but someone for whom staying quiet has simply run out. Character B is plausibly
a civic figure whose own standing is tied up in the "function over sentiment" identity — a port
administrator, or the same kind of figure who argued for shipyards-first in Suggestion #3 — now facing
the fact that the value they successfully defended has an actual human bill still outstanding.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The resident's public statement forces the city to confront, for the first time, whether its proudest
civic value has actually been paid for evenly. It doesn't produce full parity — the numbers still favor
the port — but it produces a concrete, if modest, response: a dedicated residential-repair allocation,
carved directly out of port revenue, formally established rather than left to the ordinary budget cycle
to maybe get around to.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

That allocation needs its own line item, its own oversight, and its own eligibility criteria — meaning
Sanay's civic budget now formally distinguishes, for the first time, between port-adjacent
infrastructure and residential infrastructure as separate categories with separate claims on revenue.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Once that budget category exists, "dockworker" and "resident" become, for the first time, functional
identities with actual bureaucratic weight — categories people get sorted into on paperwork, not just
categories people happen to notice about their own daily lives. A genuinely novel axis of local
distinction, tracking neither national heritage nor human-or-robot status, enters Sanay's civic
bookkeeping and stays there.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The cost is no longer entirely unspoken. A formal, if modest, remedy exists, defended by a resident who
had to spend real social capital to force it into being — and Sanay's civic administration now tracks
its own population along a line that didn't exist as an official category before this cycle: which side
of the docks you live on.
