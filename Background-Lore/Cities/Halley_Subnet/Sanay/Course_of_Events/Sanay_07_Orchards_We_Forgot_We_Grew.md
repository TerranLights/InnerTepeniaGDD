## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Sanay's founding mission was genuine scientific research — the actual SANAE program the city inherited
its infrastructure from. That mission still has real, active practitioners, even as the port and
shipyard economy around it has grown into something much larger.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The port and shipyard economy that grows up around the founding research eventually dwarfs it
completely, and the scientific mission survives only through a shrinking number of dedicated
researchers — still doing real work, but no longer anything close to what the city is actually known
for. That shrinkage isn't sudden; it's generational, each cohort of researchers a little smaller than
the last, until the mission depends on a handful of specific, aging people rather than an institution
that could survive any one of them retiring.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** this cycle's conflict is smaller and more personal
than most of Sanay's other turning points — a mentor trying to pass on a dying tradition, and a
plausible successor who has every practical reason not to take it up. It shouldn't read as the
successor being wrong or ungrateful; the port economy genuinely offers more money, more stability, and
more social standing than a shrinking research program can compete with. The mentor's grief is real,
but so is the protégé's rational self-interest — this is a conflict where both people can be entirely
correct about their own situation and still end up at odds.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly one of the last
SANAE-tradition researchers, close to retirement, who has spent years trying to identify and train a
successor. Character B is plausibly a talented young researcher or technician — human or robot — with
genuine aptitude for the work, who ultimately has to weigh loyalty to a fading institution against a
much more secure and rewarded career in Sanay's dominant port economy.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The protégé chooses the port economy — not out of disloyalty, but because nothing about staying in the
research program actually competes with what the alternative offers. The mentor's last real attempt to
pass the tradition on fails, and eventually the last researcher who remembers doing it the founding-era
way retires, or dies, without an obvious successor. SANAE's legacy finishes its transition from living
work into pure institutional memory.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

With no living successor to carry the specific methods and findings forward, the risk isn't just that
the work stops — it's that the actual technical record of decades of research could be lost outright
rather than merely deprioritized. A small civic or archival office takes on the deliberate task of
formally documenting and preserving what remains of the SANAE-era research materials and methodology
before the last people who understand them are gone.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That archive, built specifically because the living tradition had already ended, becomes the sole
surviving usable resource on the SANAE research mission — not scattered institutional memory or
ceremonial vocabulary, but an actual, deliberately preserved body of work. Anyone who later wants to
reference or revive any piece of the founding mission — a historian, a diaspora researcher, a city
official chasing an old finding — has exactly one place to go, and it only exists because someone made
the specific choice to build it before it was too late.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The SANAE research mission is fully retired as living practice. What survives is a small, deliberately
preserved archive, maintained by whoever inherited the task of building it — genuine institutional
memory, specific and citable, rather than the vaguer heritage vocabulary the city might otherwise have
been left with.
