## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

South African freighter crews arriving at Sanay's port move through the city with no formal boundaries
on where they go or who they deal with — dockside business happens wherever it happens to happen,
governed by custom and convenience rather than any written rule.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city with real reasons to stay guarded even while profiting from Upper Earth contact can't leave that
access completely open indefinitely — the informal arrangement works fine right up until someone in a
position of civic responsibility actually sits down and asks what unrestricted freighter-crew access to
the rest of the city could cost Sanay if circumstances ever turned. That question, once asked seriously,
doesn't have an easy answer that satisfies everyone: closing off contact entirely would cost the city
real trade revenue, but leaving it open indefinitely means carrying a risk nobody's actually agreed to.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone whose livelihood
depends on frictionless volume and someone whose responsibility is the city's exposure and security.
This isn't a xenophobia-versus-openness conflict — both characters can fully accept that Upper Earth
trade is worth having; the argument is about where the boundary sits, not whether one should exist. A's
case is that every procedural friction point costs real time and money, and that Sanay's whole
livelihood runs on this trade continuing smoothly. B's case is that a Federation city doing business
with a civilization that officially disdains it can't afford to treat unrestricted access as a
permanent given, no matter how profitable it currently is.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a dockside merchant or
logistics figure — someone whose income is directly tied to freighter turnaround speed and who
experiences any proposed boundary as friction with a real cost. Character B is plausibly an early port
security or administrative official, someone tasked specifically with the city's exposure risk rather
than its trade volume, for whom an unbounded arrangement is a standing liability regardless of how well
it's gone so far.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A specific, bounded dockside zone gets formally established — freighter crews welcome to dock, unload,
and conduct business, but within clearly defined limits rather than free run of the city. The Trade Yard
becomes Sanay's own answer to a question every Upper Earth-facing Tepenian city eventually has to face,
distinct from Palmer City's far more open approach just a subnet away.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

A bounded zone with defined protocols doesn't run itself — it needs dedicated staff who understand both
the procedures and the freighter crews they're managing. A small, specific professional role emerges at
Sanay that doesn't exist anywhere else in the city: Trade Yard clerks and liaisons, whose job is
managing Upper Earth contact specifically, day in and day out.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Years of that specialized, repeated contact make the Trade Yard's own liaison staff, almost by accident,
the most practically experienced body of Upper-Earth-relations expertise in the entire country — not
because Sanay set out to build diplomatic specialists, but because nobody else in Tepenia has this much
sustained, structured, day-to-day contact with an Upper Earth population to draw the same expertise from.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The Trade Yard is now a settled, bounded institution, staffed by a small class of specialists nobody
outside Sanay thinks to call specialists — and the city that never seeks credit for anything has quietly
become the country's most experienced authority on managing Upper Earth contact, without ever
mentioning it to anyone who hasn't already noticed.
