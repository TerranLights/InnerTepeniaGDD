# Neumayer — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note (revised):** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during
the Second Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no
post-war/present-day material. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Neumayer's Megasheet
("Measured, Not Debated," hydraulic-leg architecture, the uncredited Amundsen Tower schematics,
precision as civic virtue). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. The Schematic Nobody Signed

Neumayer's inherited AWI equipment and engineering talent make it the natural place to design the
structural plans for what becomes Tepenia's single greatest infrastructure project. **But** the
actual construction happens at the South Pole, far from Neumayer, under a different city's name.
**Therefore** the completed Amundsen Tower carries no trace of Neumayer in its public story, even
though the whole thing exists because of work done here first. **But** the engineers who did that
work know exactly what they built. **Therefore** The Schematic becomes a quietly bittersweet civic
holiday — marking a triumph almost nobody outside the city even knows to credit.

## 2. Doing It Again, Quietly

Decades after the Tower, Neumayer's R&D labs take on the design work for the current-generation
fabrication-synthesis chamber — the actual mechanism behind every new robot created in Tepenia.
**But** the chamber gets built and operated at two other cities entirely, carrying their names, not
Neumayer's. **Therefore** the exact pattern that made the Tower an uncredited legacy repeats itself,
this time for something touching population growth itself. **But** by now the city has lived through
this once already. **Therefore** what could have felt like a second injustice instead confirms
something residents already suspected: this isn't a single historical irony, it's simply what
Neumayer does — build the thing, hand it off, let someone else's name travel with it.

## 3. Measured, Not Debated

Built on the Ekström Ice Shelf's floating, unstable ice, Neumayer faces the same foundational problem
every ice-shelf city in the subnet does. **But** Halley, just up the coast, answers it through
continuous relocation and open civic debate about when and where to move. **Therefore** Neumayer
chooses differently: hydraulic-leg architecture and constant instrumentation, adjusting to the ice
through precise measurement rather than collective deliberation. **But** that choice means treating
consensus-building itself as less trustworthy than the numbers. **Therefore** "Measured, Not Debated"
becomes not just an engineering method but Neumayer's whole civic temperament, precision substituting
for argument in domains far beyond the ice.

## 4. The Institute That Refused to Dissolve

Most exile communities let their founding institutions fade into general civic life once survival no
longer depends on them. **But** a city organized around rigor as near-religious virtue finds that
kind of quiet dissolution intolerable. **Therefore** the Alfred Wegener Institute is formally,
deliberately preserved as its own distinct entity within the city rather than folded into general
governance. **But** maintaining a separate institutional structure for centuries takes real, ongoing
effort nobody strictly needs to spend. **Therefore** Neumayer ends up governed by two overlapping
systems — general civic administration and a formally continuous research institute — because letting
either one absorb the other never felt acceptable.

## 5. Backup Before Anyone Asked

Neumayer's climate and atmospheric data record grows, generation after generation, into one of
Tepenia's longest unbroken scientific datasets. **But** a precision-first institution doesn't wait
for a threat to start protecting what it values. **Therefore** the record gets transmitted to
multiple redundant off-site locations as standard, unremarkable practice, long before the Long Night
War is ever a possibility anyone plans around. **But** that means the precaution looks, for
generations, like effort spent on a danger that never seems to come. **Therefore** when a real
accident finally does strike — a fire sweeping through the primary records hall, decades of
instrumentation and paper archives going up with it — the single asset the city might have lost
completely turns out to be the one thing that was never actually at risk at all, sitting safely in
redundant storage exactly where precision-first habit had already put it.

## 6. The Feeling Nobody Campaigned For

Generations after the Tower, a visiting historian or reporter stumbles onto Neumayer's own
meticulous internal records of exactly what the city contributed. **But** going public with the full
story would mean campaigning for recognition the city has never sought. **Therefore** the temptation
arises, briefly, to correct the record loudly — to make sure the rest of Tepenia finally knows.
**But** the city's whole established instinct runs the other way: keep the documentation complete and
exact, and let that completeness be the answer, not a public campaign. **Therefore** Neumayer declines
the moment, quietly, the same way it's declined every other moment like it — recognition offered,
politely set aside in favor of the record simply being right.

## 7. Two Cities, One Ice Shelf, Opposite Answers

A shared subnet-wide engineering crisis — a foundation failure, a supply emergency touching both
cities' unstable ground — forces Halley and Neumayer engineers into the same room for the first time
in years. **But** the two cities have spent generations solving the identical underlying problem in
opposite ways: Halley through confident, consensus-driven motion, Neumayer through exacting,
individually-verified measurement. **Therefore** the collaboration starts as friction, each side
distrusting the other's whole method. **But** the crisis doesn't care which philosophy is more
elegant, only which one works. **Therefore** the two approaches end up genuinely complementing each
other under pressure, giving both cities a rare, concrete reason to respect a philosophy they'd
otherwise never have tested against their own.

## 8. The Second Set of Books

As Tepenia's regional administrations increasingly rely on documentation to settle real disputes —
land claims, resource allocation, credit for engineering work — Neumayer's reputation for rigorous,
redundant recordkeeping starts drawing outside requests for material nobody else kept as carefully.
**But** that rigor was never built to serve anyone's convenience but the city's own R&D and
institutional integrity. **Therefore** Neumayer has to decide, for the first time, whether to
formally open its archive to outside verification requests or keep it strictly internal. **But**
refusing would leave real disputes unresolved that its own records could plausibly settle.
**Therefore** the city opens a limited, formal channel for outside requests — becoming, quietly, one
of the Federation's most trusted informal sources of independent verification.

## 9. A Toast, Precisely Poured

Visitors from working-class glitch-coolant cultures elsewhere expect variety, or raw potency, or both.
**But** Neumayer's own coolant culture runs on neither — every batch engineered for exact, reproducible
effects, nothing left to chance. **Therefore** a skeptical outside drinker, expecting something
livelier, tries it expecting to be unimpressed. **But** the precision turns out to produce an
experience just as real as anything rowdier, simply more exact about what it delivers. **Therefore**
Neumayer's coolant culture gains a small, genuine reputation beyond its own borders — proof that
"Measured, Not Debated" works as well applied to a drink as to a foundation.

## 10. Legs That Never Panic

A routine seismic and ice-movement reading shows a genuinely unusual shift in the Ekström Ice Shelf's
drift — the kind of number that would trigger real alarm somewhere less prepared for it. **But**
Neumayer has been continuously monitoring exactly this kind of data for generations, treating it as
ordinary information rather than a warning sign in waiting. **Therefore** the hydraulic legs adjust,
the instruments log the event, and the city keeps functioning without so much as a public
announcement. **But** a visitor from elsewhere, hearing about the reading after the fact, is
startled that nobody thought it worth mentioning. **Therefore** the exchange becomes a small, telling
moment of cultural contrast — what reads as a near-miss anywhere else is, at Neumayer, simply
Tuesday's data.

## 11. A Private Correction

An engineer who worked directly on the current fabrication-synthesis chamber's schematic meets, by
chance, someone from Sinheung or Byrd — one of the two cities that actually builds the chamber today —
who mentions the chamber's design with real pride, crediting their own city's own workmanship without
any idea the underlying schematic came from somewhere else entirely. **But** the engineer knows exactly
whose hands actually drew the plans this stranger is unknowingly praising secondhand. **Therefore** a
sharp, private impulse rises — to correct the record on the spot, to say plainly where the design
actually came from. **But** correcting it here, to one stranger, in passing, wouldn't change anything
citywide; it would just be one uncomfortable personal moment, not the kind of civic campaign Neumayer's
whole culture has already, deliberately, declined to run. **Therefore** the engineer says something
small instead — not a correction, not a claim, just a quiet acknowledgment that the design has an
interesting history — and lets the moment pass, keeping the fuller truth exactly where it's always
lived: privately, personally, unspoken but not forgotten.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#11** is the personal-scale counterpart to chain #6's civic-scale story — where #6 shows the city as a
whole declining a public recognition moment, this is one ordinary engineer's private version of the same
choice, made alone, with nobody watching, exactly the individual angle the Mega_Init's own Bletchley Park
research question explicitly invites and chain #6 doesn't reach. **#1 and #2** are the most structurally important — together they confirm the Megasheet's own
Cross-Reference finding that the Tower wasn't an isolated irony but a standing pattern, giving that
pattern two concrete, dated instances instead of one. **#6** is the most emotionally significant,
since it's the only thread that actually tests the city's stated equanimity about its own uncredited
legacy rather than simply asserting it.
