## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Neumayer's inherited AWI equipment and engineering talent make it the natural place to design the
structural plans for what becomes Tepenia's single greatest infrastructure project. The work happens here
first, on this ice shelf, in these labs, drafted by people who know exactly what they're building toward.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The actual construction happens at the South Pole, far from Neumayer, under a different city's name. The
completed Amundsen Tower carries no trace of Neumayer in its public story, even though the whole thing
exists because of work done here first — and the engineers who did that work know it, precisely and in
detail, in a city that has never treated precise knowledge as something you're allowed to sit on quietly.
That gap — between what the city's own records can prove and what the rest of Tepenia will ever be told —
starts to feel less like an old grievance and more like an open question nobody's actually decided yet.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone who wants the
omission corrected outward — some public accounting, a plaque, a line in the Tower's own history, sent to
Fort McMurdo or wherever such things get recorded — and someone who believes going public would be a
mistake, not out of shame but out of a settled conviction that this isn't how Neumayer answers an injury.
Neither side should read as simply right. A's frustration is legitimate: watching your city's defining
achievement travel under someone else's name, forever, is a real cost, not a precious one. B's caution is
equally legitimate: campaigning for credit risks turning quiet, durable pride into something contingent on
whether the outside world ever agrees to grant it — and a city built on measurement over persuasion has
good reason to distrust persuasion as a strategy for anything that matters this much.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a younger AWI engineer
or archivist, close enough to the original schematic work (or its direct records) to feel the omission
personally, without yet having internalized the older civic habit of letting completeness stand in for
credit. Character B is plausibly a senior Institute figure — someone old enough to have watched this exact
argument happen before, or to have inherited the instinct from someone who did — whose objection isn't
indifference but a settled theory of what kind of dignity is actually available to a city like this one.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side fully wins. No letter goes to Fort McMurdo, no public campaign is launched — but the
argument itself gets formally answered rather than just dropped: the city institutes The Schematic, a
dedicated civic holiday marking the day the design work was completed and handed off to the actual
construction site. Not a correction aimed outward. A commemoration aimed inward — quietly bittersweet,
marking a triumph almost nobody outside the city even knows to credit, but marked all the same, every
year, on purpose.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because The Schematic is a formal civic holiday and not just a private mood, it needs actual annual
content — and the Institute settles on a public reading and cross-check of the original schematic
archive against whatever's been published elsewhere about the Tower since. What starts as commemorative
ceremony becomes, every year, a working audit: a fixed occasion for verifying the city's own record
against the outside world's version of events.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that audit happens every single year, not once, it starts surfacing things the original
commemoration was never designed to find: discrepancies, half-forgotten contributions, other pieces of
Neumayer's engineering work that quietly left the city under someone else's name the same way the Tower
did. The Schematic stops being about one triumph and starts accumulating, year by year, into a running
tally the city didn't set out to build.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Neumayer marks The Schematic every year — half memorial, half audit — and what began as a holiday about
one uncredited achievement has quietly turned into standing evidence of a pattern: this isn't a single
historical irony the city can grieve once and set down. It's a documented, growing, and still-open ledger
of a habit the city has never fully reckoned with.
