## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Generations after the Tower, a visiting historian or investigative reporter, doing unrelated research in
the AWI archive, stumbles onto Neumayer's own meticulous internal records documenting exactly what the
city actually contributed — to the Tower, and, by now, to more than the Tower.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Going public with the full story would mean campaigning for recognition the city has never sought — and
the temptation to do exactly that arises anyway, briefly but genuinely, because the visitor is eager to
publish and the archive itself makes an unusually persuasive case. But the city's whole established
instinct runs the other way: keep the documentation complete and exact, and let that completeness be the
answer, not a public campaign. That instinct now has to hold against real outside momentum for the first
time, not just against the city's own private temptation.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between the outside historian or
reporter, who wants to publish the full story publicly, and an internal Institute figure who wants it kept
out of the public record — or published in a form the city can live with. This needs real legibility on
both sides, not a simple truth-teller-versus-gatekeeper framing. A's motive isn't just professional
ambition — they genuinely believe letting an injustice go uncorrected, when the evidence to correct it
sits right there in the archive, is itself a kind of failure, and that quiet suffering shouldn't be
rewarded with more silence. B's motive isn't shame or secrecy — it's a real fear that trading the record's
current form (owned entirely by the city, on the city's own terms) for a public narrative authored by
someone else, however well-intentioned, means losing control of how the story gets told and inviting
exactly the kind of outside attention "Measured, Not Debated" has always been organized to avoid.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Federation historian
or archivist-journalist, someone whose whole professional instinct is that documented injustice deserves
correction. Character B is plausibly a senior AWI Institute official, someone whose job includes deciding
what the Institute formally releases, and whose caution comes from institutional responsibility rather
than personal discomfort.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neumayer declines the moment, quietly — the same way it's declined every moment like it. Institute
leadership asks the historian not to run the full public exposé they'd originally planned. Moved, in the
end, by the archive's own quiet, complete conviction rather than by pressure, the historian agrees to a
narrower, scholarly publication instead — recognition offered, politely set aside in favor of the record
simply being right.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The historian's now-restrained, academic-only publication doesn't disappear — it enters specialist
circulation and becomes the seed of a small but real scholarly reputation for Neumayer among Federation
historians and archivists specifically, rather than the general public. Within a narrow expert circle, the
city becomes known as an unusually reliable and unusually generous primary-source archive.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that narrow scholarly reputation keeps quietly growing — one visiting researcher telling another,
one citation leading to the next — a slow, word-of-mouth academic pipeline eventually forms, and it
becomes the actual channel through which outside researchers start bringing Neumayer genuine verification
requests. Without the historian's specifically restrained, specifically academic publication, no such
channel would exist for anyone to have heard of in the first place.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Neumayer still has no public reputation as the Tower's true origin. But among a small, steadily growing
circle of Federation historians and archivists, word has quietly spread that its archive is among the most
complete and most generously shared primary sources on record — a narrow, specialist kind of recognition
that finally exists, without the city ever having campaigned for the broader kind.
