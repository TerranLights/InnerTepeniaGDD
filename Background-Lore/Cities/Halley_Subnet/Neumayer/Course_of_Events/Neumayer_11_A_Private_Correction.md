## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Neumayer has long since made its own civic peace with the Tower situation — and, by extension, with the
fabrication-synthesis chamber's own repeat of the same pattern. Individually, though, that peace was
never actually tested at the personal scale; it's a civic posture, not necessarily something every single
engineer who touched the work has had to individually reckon with, one on one, with a stranger who
doesn't know the truth.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

An engineer who worked directly on the chamber's current schematic meets someone from Sinheung or Byrd —
one of the two cities that actually manufactures the chamber today — purely by chance, in an ordinary
social setting. The visitor mentions the chamber with real, unguarded pride, crediting their own city's
craftsmanship, genuinely unaware the underlying design came from somewhere else entirely.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** this isn't a conflict between two people so much as
a conflict inside one person, briefly externalized. Character B genuinely means no harm — they have no
idea they're praising someone else's work to that someone's own face. The tension is entirely
Character A's own: a sharp, private impulse to correct the record, weighed against everything Neumayer's
own culture has already taught them about what that correction would actually accomplish (nothing
citywide, only an uncomfortable moment for a stranger who did nothing wrong). The strongest version of
this scene has Character A visibly, briefly, hesitate — the moment where the choice is actually live —
before deciding.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a mid-career or senior
engineer, someone with enough personal history on the chamber project to feel the moment land, but not
so senior that their own reaction reads as institutional policy rather than a private feeling. Character B
is plausibly an ordinary visitor or worker from Sinheung or Byrd, entirely sincere, with no idea of the
weight in what they've just said.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Character A doesn't correct the record. They say something small instead — an acknowledgment that the
design "has an interesting history," offered lightly enough that Character B doesn't register it as
anything more than a passing comment. The fuller truth stays exactly where it's always lived: private,
personal, unspoken.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The moment doesn't become a story Character A tells others — it becomes something they simply carry,
quietly, the way the rest of Neumayer already does at civic scale. No campaign starts. No record gets
corrected. The only thing that changes is that Character A has now personally, individually made the
choice the city as a whole already made generations ago.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Word of the exchange — not the content, just the fact that it happened — reaches a few of
Character A's own colleagues, the way small personal moments do in a close-knit engineering culture.
Rather than becoming a cautionary tale or a rallying point, it becomes one more quiet, private data point
confirming something the city's own engineers already privately understood: that this exact scene, in
some form, has probably happened to nearly all of them at some point, individually, never discussed as a
group.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Neumayer's civic peace with its own uncredited legacy turns out to be built, in practice, on hundreds of
small, private, individually-made choices just like this one — never coordinated, never discussed as
policy, each engineer arriving at the same quiet restraint on their own terms. The city's famous
equanimity isn't a single institutional decision handed down from above; it's this, repeated, person by
person, for generations.
