## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

As Tepenia's regional administrations increasingly rely on documentation to settle real disputes — land
claims, resource allocation, credit for engineering work — outside requests start reaching Neumayer for
archival material nobody else kept as carefully, drawn by the city's quiet reputation for rigorous,
redundant recordkeeping.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

That rigor was never built to serve anyone's convenience but the city's own R&D and institutional
integrity, and the mismatch becomes impossible to ignore as the requests keep arriving. Neumayer has to
decide, for the first time, whether to formally open its archive to outside verification requests or keep
it strictly internal — while every refusal has a real, visible cost, leaving genuine disputes elsewhere in
the Federation unresolved that the city's own records could plausibly settle.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between an AWI archivist who opposes
opening the archive to outside requests, and a national-level administrator or inter-city mediator pushing
Neumayer to open a formal channel. This shouldn't read as obstruction versus public good. A's opposition is
substantive: turning an internal research resource into a tool other cities can invoke in their own
disputes risks selective interpretation, political pressure to slant findings, and workload the Institute
was never staffed for — a real threat to the archive's own integrity, the thing that made it valuable in
the first place. B's push is equally substantive: real, otherwise-unresolvable disputes are piling up
across the Federation, and Neumayer may genuinely be the only city holding records precise enough to settle
them fairly — refusing isn't neutral, it has its own victims.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a senior records
custodian within the Institute, professionally and personally invested in the archive's integrity as an
end in itself. Character B is plausibly an inter-city coordination figure — perhaps connected to Fort
McMurdo's own coordination apparatus — whose job is watching disputes across the Federation pile up
unresolved and looking for whatever might actually settle them.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The city opens a limited, formal channel for outside verification requests rather than staying strictly
internal or fully public — becoming, quietly, one of the Federation's most trusted informal sources of
independent verification, without ever becoming a general public archive.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

To protect the archive's integrity, exactly the concern the archivist originally raised, the new channel
is built with a specific structural safeguard: the staff who answer outside verification requests are
institutionally separated from and cannot be lobbied directly by whichever city or party is asking, with
requests routed and answered blind to who's requesting and why — a firewall process, not an open door.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that anonymized, firewalled process exists and builds a track record over time, Neumayer's
verification findings start getting cited as neutral evidence in Federation-level disputes specifically
because they're structurally insulated from the parties involved — a status the open-door version of the
channel, without the firewall, would never have earned. The city inadvertently becomes something close to
an informal evidentiary authority for the whole Federation, a role well beyond simple archive-sharing.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Neumayer now runs a small but structurally insulated formal-verification office inside the Institute, and
its findings are increasingly treated across the Federation as close to neutral evidentiary fact — an
authority the city never sought and didn't fully anticipate when it opened that first limited channel.
