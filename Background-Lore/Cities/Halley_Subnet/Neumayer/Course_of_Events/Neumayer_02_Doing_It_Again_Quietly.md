## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Decades after the Tower, Neumayer's R&D labs take on the design work for the current-generation
fabrication-synthesis chamber — the actual mechanism behind every new robot created in Tepenia. The city
carries The Schematic as an established, settled civic memory by now; this new project is just another
job, the kind of thing Neumayer's labs do as a matter of course.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The chamber gets built and operated at two other cities entirely — Sinheung and Byrd — carrying their
names, not Neumayer's. The exact pattern that made the Tower an uncredited legacy repeats itself, this
time for something touching population growth itself, something more intimate to Tepenian life than a
single piece of infrastructure ever was. And because the city has lived through this once already, the
recognition lands differently than it did the first time — less like a fresh wound, more like a second
data point demanding to be explained.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone who wants Neumayer to
finally, formally seek credit this time — reasoning that a second uncredited contribution to something as
significant as the Cradle's own manufacturing core is too important to let pass the way the Tower did —
and someone who thinks that fight is not just unwinnable but the wrong fight, arguing the city should stop
treating each instance as a fresh injustice and instead formally *name the pattern itself*. Both positions
should be legible: A isn't naive, they've simply decided that twice is a decision, not a coincidence, and
someone has to be the one who says so. B isn't defeatist — they're arguing that fighting city-by-city for
credit on individual projects will never catch up with how fast Neumayer's labs actually work, and that
the more durable, more honest response is to stop treating "getting credited" as the goal at all.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a younger engineer on
the Mark IV design team itself, close enough to this specific project to feel its erasure directly, and
old enough to remember The Schematic as precedent. Character B is plausibly a senior Institute
administrator whose job includes deciding what the city formally says about itself — someone with a
functional, structural reason to think in terms of durable civic policy rather than one project at a time.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The Institute council votes, deliberately and on the record, against pursuing formal credit for the Mark
IV chamber — but not as a dismissal of A's grievance. The vote instead formally recognizes, for the first
time in the city's own governance, that this is a standing pattern rather than an isolated wrong: what
could have felt like a second injustice instead becomes confirmation of something residents already
suspected. This isn't a single historical irony. It's simply what Neumayer does — build the thing, hand
it off, let someone else's name travel with it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Naming the pattern formally requires the city to actually track it formally — the council establishes a
standing internal registry tasked specifically with documenting every technology, schematic, and design
Neumayer originates and exports without carrying its own name forward. What used to be informal civic
memory becomes an administrative function with its own budget line and its own staff.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that registry exists and runs continuously rather than being reconstructed from memory each time
someone asks, it becomes the thing outside researchers and historians actually rely on decades later when
they come looking for verifiable, corroborated history of who really built what across Tepenia — not
personal testimony or oral tradition, but a maintained, citable record built for an entirely different
original purpose.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Neumayer now maintains a formal, standing registry of its own uncredited contributions — the Tower, the
Mark IV chamber, and whatever comes after — administered as routine Institute business rather than
grievance. The city has stopped waiting to be individually wronged before it responds; it has simply
started keeping the ledger, on the working assumption that there will be more entries.
