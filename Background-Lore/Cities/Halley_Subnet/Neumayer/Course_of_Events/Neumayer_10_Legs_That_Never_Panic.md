## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Neumayer's continuous seismic and ice-movement monitoring, running for generations as routine
instrumentation, quietly registers a genuinely unusual shift in the Ekström Ice Shelf's drift — the kind
of reading that would set off real alarm in a city less prepared for it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Neumayer has been continuously monitoring exactly this kind of data for generations, treating it as
ordinary information rather than a warning sign, so the hydraulic legs simply adjust, the instruments log
the event, and the city keeps functioning without so much as a public announcement. But a visitor from
elsewhere, hearing about the reading only after the fact, is genuinely startled that nobody thought it
worth mentioning at the time — and says so, treating the silence itself as the anomaly, even though nobody
in Neumayer did anything they'd consider unusual at all.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between an outside visitor — a
journalist, a Federation inspector, or an official from another subnet city — who believes withholding
this kind of reading, even passively, is a transparency failure that ought to have a formal disclosure
requirement, and a Neumayer monitoring engineer or Institute official who resists that push. This isn't
whistleblower versus cover-up — nobody in Neumayer hid anything, they simply never treated it as public
information. A's alarm is legitimate: from outside, a reading this significant going unannounced looks like
exactly the kind of gap that causes real harm elsewhere in Tepenia. B's resistance is equally legitimate:
announcing every reading that clears the hydraulic legs' normal adjustment tolerance would mean treating
routine operational data as a matter of public concern by default, manufacturing exactly the kind of
unwarranted panic the city's whole instrumentation-first method exists to avoid.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Federation inspector
or a journalist from a city with a very different relationship to public risk information — someone whose
own professional standards assume that significant readings get announced as a matter of course. Character
B is plausibly a senior monitoring-station engineer, someone for whom this specific class of judgment call
has always been a purely internal, professional matter, never previously questioned by anyone outside the
Institute.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The two negotiate a specific compromise rather than either side fully prevailing: Neumayer agrees to a
narrow, technical disclosure threshold — only readings that cross an actual, defined safety-relevant
magnitude get reported outward, through a specific channel to subnet authorities — formalizing, for the
first time, a judgment call that used to live entirely inside one engineer's professional discretion.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because that threshold is now written down and explicit rather than an implicit professional judgment
call, it becomes possible, for the first time, for outside parties — Halley, Fort McMurdo's coordination
office, national inspectors — to actually audit Neumayer's monitoring decisions against a fixed standard,
rather than simply trusting the city's word that nothing worth mentioning had happened.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because outside audit against a fixed written threshold is now possible, that threshold itself becomes a
recurring subject of dispute — every time a Neumayer engineer judges a reading to fall just under the
line, outside parties start second-guessing that specific call in a way nobody could have done when the
whole system ran on unquestioned internal discretion. The dispute exists only because the line was written
down in the first place.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Neumayer's monitoring judgment, once entirely a matter of unquestioned internal discretion, now operates
against a written, externally auditable disclosure threshold — genuinely safer and more accountable, but
also, for the first time, a running site of dispute every time a reading falls close enough to the line
that outsiders feel entitled to weigh in on a call the city used to make alone.
