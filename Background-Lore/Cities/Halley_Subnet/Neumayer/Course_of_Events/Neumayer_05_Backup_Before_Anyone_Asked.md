## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Neumayer's climate and atmospheric data record grows, generation after generation, into one of Tepenia's
longest unbroken scientific datasets — a slow, unglamorous accumulation that nobody treats as urgent,
because nothing has ever actually threatened it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A precision-first institution doesn't wait for a threat to start protecting what it values, so the record
gets transmitted to multiple redundant off-site locations as standard, unremarkable practice, long before
the Long Night War is ever a possibility anyone plans around. But maintaining that redundancy costs real,
recurring resources — storage, transmission, upkeep — and because the danger it guards against never
seems to arrive, the precaution starts to look, across successive budget cycles and successive
generations, like effort spent defending against a disaster that simply isn't coming.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a recurring institutional argument between someone
who insists on continuing to fund the redundant, multi-site backup program indefinitely, and someone who
wants to scale it back in favor of resources going toward the city's active, living R&D mission. This
should read as a genuine opportunity-cost dispute, not recklessness versus paranoia. A's position treats
protecting irreplaceable data as close to sacred, regardless of whether it's ever paid off — that's simply
what precision-first practice requires. B isn't cavalier; they're responsible for finite resources and are
right that, on the record so far, the backup program has cost a great deal and prevented nothing anyone
can point to. The argument should recur, unresolved, across more than one generation, exactly because
neither side is being unreasonable.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a long-serving archivist
or records custodian within the Institute, someone whose entire professional identity is built around the
backup program's continuation. Character B is plausibly a resource-allocation official — an Institute
administrator or city-hall counterpart — responsible for balancing the Institute's budget against its
active research output, for whom an ever-expanding archive-maintenance line with no incidents to show for
it is a legitimate, recurring target.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The disagreement recurs, unresolved, generation after generation — until a fire sweeping through the
primary records hall finally settles it decisively. Decades of instrumentation and paper archives in the
primary hall go up with the blaze, but the redundant, distant backups the skeptics kept wanting to defund
turn out to hold every dataset intact, untouched. The one asset the city might have lost completely turns
out to be the one thing that was never actually at risk at all.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The fire's aftermath triggers a full inventory review — and the review finds a gap the original backup
policy never covered. The redundancy program was scoped narrowly to "the record": the climate and
atmospheric dataset specifically. Other genuinely valuable material that lived in the same hall —
unpublished engineering notebooks, early instrumentation prototypes, Tower-era planning documents never
formally classified as part of "the record" — burned with no copy anywhere. The fire proves the backup
policy worked exactly as designed, and simultaneously exposes exactly how narrow that design always was.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because that specific gap gets found and documented, the backup mandate is formally expanded — no longer
scoped to "the record" alone, but rewritten to cover every category of output the Institute produces,
research notebooks and prototypes included. The expansion happens because of what the inventory review
found, not because the original archivist-versus-budget argument was ever revisited; that argument had
already been settled in the archivist's favor before the gap was discovered.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Neumayer's redundant-backup culture is now total rather than narrow — not just the climate record, but
every category of Institute output sits under a standing, institute-wide redundancy mandate, born
specifically from the one category of loss the original, narrower policy still failed to catch.
