# Belgrano — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note (revised):** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during
the Second Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no
post-war/present-day material (Belgrano's own later "Boneyard Times" decline is out of scope here).
10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Belgrano's Megasheet (the airbase that never stood down,
mate ritual, garage/mechanic culture, the Byrd-founding expedition). Post-settlement only; nothing
here is canon.

---

## 1. Command Without an Institution

*(Refined 2026-07-25, GPS-purposes-only pass: the real 1979-era Argentine Air Force station was kept
structurally maintained by a rotating succession of national operators across the First Interwar Period —
caretaker-level upkeep, not an active command presence — see `Specs/Belgrano.md`. The command structure
below is established by ex-Air Force personnel *among the exiles themselves*, not surviving pre-war staff.)*

Ex-Air Force exiles reclaiming the structurally sound but operationally dormant base establish a
military-style command structure from scratch, drawing on their own training. **But** a growing civilian population forms around it that the
chain of command has no formal jurisdiction over.
**Therefore** the ranking officer extends military-style command to cover the whole settlement rather
than let a separate civic government grow up alongside it. **But** that decision means every later
arrival — Argentine or not — inherits Air Force discipline as the only civic template Belgrano ever
offers. **Therefore** "function before comfort" becomes contagious well past the founding population,
outlasting the demographic majority that originally carried it.

## 2. The Ritual That Didn't Need Orders

Every other piece of Belgrano's founding character — chain of command, maintenance rotations, martial
bearing — gets formally taught to newer, non-Argentine arrivals through actual institutional channels.
**But** mate-sharing was never an Air Force policy to begin with, just a family habit brought from
home, so there's no formal channel teaching it at all. **Therefore** newcomers pick it up anyway,
informally, faster and more completely than they absorb the taught discipline, simply by sitting in
rooms where it happens. **But** that makes it the one piece of "Belgrano culture" nobody can point to
an official source for. **Therefore** the mate ritual becomes, within a generation or two, the thing
newer residents feel most personally belongs to them — precisely because nobody ever formally handed
it to them.

## 3. Yolanda's Workaround

A real spare-parts shortage hits the airbase during an otherwise ordinary Interwar supply slowdown —
shipments delayed, official channels too slow to help. **But** Chief of Maintenance Yolanda Petrescu
knows every garage crew in the outer city, official Air Force personnel or not, capable of
fabricating or salvaging what the base actually needs. **Therefore** she organizes an unofficial
parts-sharing rotation across those crews, with no new authority formally granted her, simply because
the work needs doing and she's the one who knows who can do it. **But** the workaround proves more
reliable than the official supply chain it quietly replaced. **Therefore** it never gets formally
dismantled once the shortage ends — becoming the informal backbone of what eventually grows into
Belgrano's own spare-parts fabrication economy.

## 4. Records Nobody Called an Archive

Flight charts, survey photography, and weather data pile up for generations as ordinary institutional
paperwork, filed the way any working airbase files its working documents. **But** an expedition
eventually needs exactly the kind of records nobody thought to treat as historically significant.
**Therefore** those routine files become, retroactively, the maps that make the Byrd-founding
crossing possible. **But** a later administrative reorganization, decades after the crossing, nearly
clears the old flight charts and survey photography out as obsolete clutter, since nobody left
remembers why any of it mattered anymore. **Therefore** it takes one records clerk's stubborn
refusal to include anything from that era in the purge to keep the material intact at all — the habit
of keeping good records surviving on nothing but one person's judgment call, long after the reason it
first mattered has faded from memory.

## 5. The Flight That Made Belgrano Famous

Belgrano's own archives suggest something worth finding across the ice; its own garage culture can
build and maintain the vehicle capable of reaching it. **But** no city this far out has ever
attempted an overland crossing that ambitious before. **Therefore** an all-robot crew sets out in
Belgrano-built Arrastradoras, with material support shipped in from Palmer City and Rothera, on a
route nobody else in Tepenia had mapped. **But** succeeding means the discovery belongs, in the
public record, to the expedition rather than to the maintenance and archival work that made it
possible. **Therefore** Expedition Day ends up commemorating the departure, not the decades of
unglamorous recordkeeping and engine repair actually responsible for it.

## 6. Whose Discipline Is It

Belgrano's founding Argentine population sets the base's whole operational character. **But**
generations of German, American, British, and Brazilian immigration eventually outpace it so
thoroughly that Argentina slips to the lowest recognized demographic tier in the city. **Therefore** a
real generational argument surfaces: do residents whose own heritage isn't Argentine at all still get
to call the discipline they were raised in genuinely theirs, or does it stay symbolically Argentine
regardless of who actually lives here now. **But** nobody with the standing to settle it wants to be
the one who tells newer families their inherited discipline isn't really theirs. **Therefore** the
question resolves itself the practical way, by simple use rather than formal ruling — the discipline
belongs to whoever keeps living it, Argentine surname or not.

## 7. The Garage That Built the Sound

Belgrano's mechanic culture means there's reliably somebody who can fix anything with an engine.
**But** that same hands-on culture, once the institution's formal discipline starts loosening, has
nowhere else to put its energy after hours. **Therefore** garage sessions meant for engine repair
start doubling as music sessions — the same tools, the same calluses, a different kind of noise.
**But** what starts as informal noise develops real technical range, the way any skill practiced
constantly does. **Therefore** Belgrano's garage rock scene becomes something the rest of the Halley
subnet actually travels to hear, mechanic culture and music culture revealed as the same culture all
along.

## 8. Logistics, Not Solidarity

Belgrano, Esperanza, and Marambio share Argentine founding heritage, which could plausibly mean a
close cultural bond between them. **But** Belgrano's own institution never had much bandwidth or
interest in anything beyond the operational. **Therefore** the actual pre-Split-Brain relationship
stays limited to shipping schedules, weather advisories, and trade coordination — genuinely useful,
genuinely thin. **But** the shared heritage doesn't disappear just because nobody's built anything
more out of it. **Therefore** three Argentine-founded cities end up quietly, permanently adjacent to
a deeper connection none of them ever got around to making.

## 9. The Vote That Wasn't Held

As Belgrano's civilian population grows well past its original military personnel, a real proposal
surfaces — from newer residents, or from visiting Federation administrators — to replace the extended
Air Force command structure with an ordinary civilian government, the way most Tepenian cities are
run. **But** a community that's grown up entirely inside that command structure can't actually picture
what would replace decades of embedded institutional habit, or who would run it. **Therefore** the
proposal never gathers enough real support to reach an actual vote — not defeated so much as never
taken seriously enough to hold one. **But** letting the question die quietly is itself a decision,
even without a vote to mark it. **Therefore** Belgrano's institutional character carries forward
unchanged, confirmed less by anyone choosing it outright than by nobody managing to seriously propose
anything else.

## 10. Bandera Without Argentina

Argentina founds the base, sets its entire operational character, and names the city after Manuel
Belgrano. **But** later immigration — German, American, British, Brazilian — eventually outpaces the
founding population so thoroughly that Argentina ends up the lowest recognized demographic tier in
the whole city. **Therefore** an observance honoring the city's own Argentine namesake could easily
have faded along with the founding population's numbers. **But** the name itself, and the discipline
it represents, had already become the city's whole civic character independent of who actually lived
there. **Therefore** Bandera Day persists as plain civic pride in the name, celebrated by a population
that is, numerically, barely Argentine at all anymore.

## 11. The Ground That Moved

Belgrano's entire civic importance rests on one thing: its runway, built on flat coastal ice rather than
the stable bedrock its Halley subnet neighbors enjoy. **But** during a routine structural survey,
engineers detect real, measurable ice movement beneath a section of that runway — not yet a crisis, but
the first hard evidence that the ground under the city's whole reason for mattering isn't as fixed as
everyone had simply assumed. **Therefore** base command mobilizes an urgent, resource-intensive
reinforcement effort, pulling in essentially the entire garage and mechanic workforce to shore up what
engineering alone can't fully solve. **But** even once the immediate danger passes, no amount of
reinforcement can make ice-shelf ground behave like bedrock — the underlying instability doesn't go away,
it's only managed. **Therefore** Belgrano absorbs a permanent, institutional watchfulness about its own
foundation, a specific concrete anxiety sitting underneath the base's confident "function before comfort"
bearing — the first hard evidence of the unsettled self-image the city will carry for the rest of its
history.

## Worth Your Attention

**#11** grounds Belgrano's Feeling/Reactive Enneagram read — "a city wrestling with its own image and
continuity" — in a specific, concrete pre-war event, rather than leaving that psychological trait to
emerge only from the post-war Boneyard Times material this pass is scoped away from. **#2, #3, and #4** are the most load-bearing — each turns an already-proposed Full-Extrapolation fact
(the mate ritual's institutional independence, Petrescu's maintenance rotations, the ordinary-records-
turned-archive) into an actual causal event rather than a static description, and all three converge
on the same underlying idea the Megasheet's own synthesis already named: Belgrano's own capability
keeps turning out to be a byproduct of habits nobody designed for that purpose in the first place.
