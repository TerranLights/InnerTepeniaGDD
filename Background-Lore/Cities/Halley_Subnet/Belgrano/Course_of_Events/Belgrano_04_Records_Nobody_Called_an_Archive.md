## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Flight charts, aerial survey photography, and weather data pile up for generations as ordinary
institutional paperwork, filed the way any working airbase files its working documents — nobody thinks
of any of it as historically significant, because none of it was ever generated to be.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

An expedition eventually needs exactly the kind of records nobody thought to treat as anything but
routine, and those ordinary files become, retroactively, the maps that make the Byrd-founding crossing
possible. Decades later, once the crossing itself has become the thing Belgrano is actually remembered
for, a later administrative reorganization goes looking through the base's older paperwork for space to
reclaim — and the old flight charts and survey photography from that era land squarely in the pile
marked for disposal, since nobody left on staff remembers why any of it mattered in the first place.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone executing an
entirely reasonable administrative purge — freeing storage space, clearing paperwork nobody can
explain the continued relevance of — and someone whose objection to that purge can't actually be
justified on paper. This shouldn't play as villain-clears-history-vs.-hero-saves-it. A's purge is
genuinely sound institutional housekeeping, not carelessness; B can't point to a policy, a directive,
or even a fully articulate reason for the refusal, only an instinct that this specific material still
matters, and that instinct is the whole of B's case.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a records-management
officer or administrator tasked with modernizing or streamlining an aging filing system, working from
entirely legitimate space and efficiency pressures. Character B is plausibly a records clerk — someone
with no particular standing, no dramatic personal connection to the Byrd-founding crossing, just years
of quiet, unglamorous familiarity with exactly which drawers hold what, and a professional's stubborn
discomfort with throwing away anything they can't fully account for.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The clerk simply refuses to include anything from that era in the purge — no appeal, no formal
objection filed, just a quiet, repeated declining to sign off on that specific portion of the disposal
list until the reorganization moves past it and finds space elsewhere. The material survives entirely
on the strength of one person's judgment call, made without ever being asked to justify it to anyone.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the material survives through personal refusal rather than any policy decision, there is now
no institutional record anywhere explaining *why* these particular files were kept — a preserved
collection with no accompanying rationale, only an unlogged decision nobody thought to write down.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Later clerks, facing their own routine purges of old material, have nothing to consult about this
precedent except the bare fact that it was kept — no memo, no policy, no explanation. Lacking any
reason to do otherwise, they default to the same conservative instinct on their own uncertain cases,
without ever formalizing an actual records-retention policy. Belgrano ends up quietly accumulating an
ever-larger body of preserved "just in case" material across generations, never organized into a real
archive, never officially declared historically significant — simply, repeatedly, not thrown away.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Belgrano sits on generations' worth of preserved but uncatalogued institutional paperwork — kept
alive by nothing but a chain of individual clerks' inherited caution, each one unaware they're
continuing a habit rather than following a rule, an accidental archive that has never once been
formally recognized as one.
