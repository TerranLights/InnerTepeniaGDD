## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Belgrano, Esperanza, and Marambio share Argentine founding heritage — a fact that, on its face, could
plausibly mean a close cultural bond between the three cities, the kind other shared-heritage cities
elsewhere in Tepenia have built into something real.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Belgrano's own institution never had much bandwidth or interest in anything beyond the operational, so
the actual pre-Split-Brain relationship between the three cities stays limited to shipping schedules,
weather advisories, and trade coordination — genuinely useful, genuinely thin. The shared heritage
doesn't disappear just because nobody's built anything more out of it, and periodically, someone
notices the gap between what the relationship could be and what it actually is, and says so.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone actively proposing
to build the relationship into something more than logistics — a cultural exchange, a shared civic
observance, formal sister-city ties — and a Belgrano counterpart whose institutional priorities and
genuinely limited operational bandwidth make them resist committing resources to it, not from hostility
to the idea but from correct triage. A believes something real is being left on the table between three
cities that could mean more to each other; B isn't dismissing that, only recognizing, accurately, that
it isn't what Belgrano's coordination structure exists to do.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a coordination-desk
counterpart from Esperanza or Marambio, or a Belgrano resident with personal ties to one of the other
two cities, genuinely invested in turning shared heritage into a living connection. Character B is
plausibly a Belgrano coordination-desk officer, the same kind of figure who runs the shipping schedules
and trade coordination day to day — not opposed to the idea in principle, just unable to justify
spending the base's limited attention on something outside its operational mandate.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The proposal gets formally logged and quietly deprioritized rather than actively rejected — filed as an
open item behind the base's actual operational obligations, never explicitly voted down, never formally
closed out either. It simply doesn't happen, and nobody with the standing to force the question does.
Three Argentine-founded cities end up quietly, permanently adjacent to a deeper connection none of them
ever got around to making.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the proposal dies by deprioritization rather than formal rejection, it never gets formally
closed out — it remains, technically, an open item in Belgrano's own institutional record: an
unanswered inter-city correspondence, an unactioned coordination-desk request, rather than a decided
matter.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because it's technically still open rather than closed, later coordination-desk staff occasionally
rediscover it in the files — and not knowing it was ever deliberately deprioritized rather than actively
decided against, independently re-evaluate it from scratch, sometimes proposing the exact same idea
again, unaware it already happened once before. The cycle repeats every so often without ever
resolving, because there's no record of anyone having actually decided against it — only a record of it
not getting done.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Belgrano, Esperanza, and Marambio remain exactly as institutionally adjacent and culturally
unconnected as before — except now there's a quiet, recurring institutional pattern where the idea of
building something more between the three cities periodically resurfaces, gets rediscovered by someone
who doesn't know it's been proposed before, and lapses again, an idea Belgrano's own records can never
quite finish or fully forget.
