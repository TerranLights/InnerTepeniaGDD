## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Belgrano's civilian population has grown well past its original military personnel, still governed
entirely by the extended Air Force command structure that has run the whole settlement since the
earliest founding years.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A real proposal surfaces — from newer residents, or from visiting Federation administrators — to
replace the extended command structure with an ordinary civilian government, the way most Tepenian
cities are run. It gathers genuine interest, enough to force an actual hearing. But a community that
has grown up entirely inside that command structure can't actually picture what would replace decades
of embedded institutional habit, or who would run it, when the question is put to them directly. The
proposal starts losing momentum not through opposition but through an inability to answer its own most
basic follow-up question.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone actively
championing civilian government — on principled grounds of self-determination and simple normalcy,
aligning Belgrano with how the rest of Tepenia runs — and someone who isn't defending military rule on
ideological grounds at all, but who, when pressed to actually specify an alternative, honestly can't
produce one. This shouldn't play as reformer-vs.-reactionary. A's case is principled and legitimate; B's
resistance is closer to embarrassment than conviction — a genuine, structural absence of civilian
administrative muscle memory, not a defense of the status quo for its own sake.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a newer resident with
no military background, or a visiting Federation administrator uncomfortable with an aviation base
running an entire city's civil affairs indefinitely. Character B is plausibly a respected, moderate
figure within the command structure — not a hardliner — who, asked in good faith to sketch what a
civilian government here would actually look like, finds they genuinely cannot, and says so.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A hearing is convened to discuss the proposal seriously, and it visibly fizzles — not voted down, just
unable to produce a workable next step, the room quietly understanding by the end that the matter is
settled without anyone formally settling it. Belgrano's institutional character carries forward
unchanged, confirmed less by anyone choosing it outright than by nobody managing to seriously propose
anything else that could actually work.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the matter was never formally decided, "why does Belgrano still run this way" becomes a
question with no citable answer or decision-point anyone can appeal to reopen — no minority report, no
dissenting vote count, no formal record of the arguments that were actually raised.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because there's no formal record to challenge, raising the idea again later carries an unusually high
social cost — anyone doing so is implicitly arguing against an outcome nobody can actually point to,
making them look naive or unaware of "how it already went" rather than principled. This quietly
discourages the proposal from ever being seriously raised a second time, regardless of how legitimate
the underlying case might still be.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Belgrano's Air Force-descended command structure continues to run the whole city, its legitimacy now
resting on an unchallengeable non-decision rather than a settled argument — and the absence of any real
record of that non-decision makes the question increasingly difficult for anyone to seriously reopen,
regardless of how much further the city grows past the population that would once have justified it.
