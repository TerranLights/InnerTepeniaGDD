## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Belgrano's mechanic culture means there's reliably somebody who can fix anything with an engine — a
skill kept sharp by constant, real operational demand, exercised every working day across the base and
the outer-city garages alike.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

That same hands-on culture, once the operational day's formal duties end, has nowhere else to put its
energy in the evenings — the same crews, the same tools, the same calluses, sitting idle after hours
in a settlement with little else built for leisure. Garage sessions meant for engine repair start
doubling as music sessions on personal time: the same hands, the same shared space, a different kind of
noise. What starts as informal after-hours noise develops real technical range, the way any skill
practiced constantly does, whether or not it was ever meant to be a skill at all. As the sessions grow
louder and more frequent, they start drawing outside attention — and outside complaints.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between someone responsible for a
garage or maintenance bay's actual operational condition — worried about tool wear, off-the-books
injury liability, and the base's reputation for discipline being associated with rowdy after-hours
noise — and someone who sees the same sessions as a legitimate, low-cost use of shared skill and

the same calluses and the same care go into both kinds of work.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a garage or
maintenance-bay supervisor accountable for the space and its equipment during and after hours, with a
real professional stake in nothing going wrong on their watch. Character B is plausibly a younger
mechanic who has become the de facto organizer of the after-hours sessions — someone with real standing
in the garage during the day, using that same standing to defend what happens there at night.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A specific incident forces the question — an unexpectedly large, unexpectedly well-behaved crowd
turning up for one evening's session, impossible for the supervisor to ignore or credibly frame as a
problem. Rather than write new policy either way, the supervisor simply doesn't shut it down when they
clearly have the standing to. The sessions continue exactly as they were, legitimized by nothing more
than a choice not to intervene.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the sessions get tolerated rather than formally sanctioned, they stay physically embedded
inside actual working garages and maintenance bays rather than migrating anywhere purpose-built —
Belgrano never develops dedicated music venues; every performance happens in a space still primarily
defined by its operational function, tools and half-repaired engines sharing the room with instruments.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because performances happen in real working garages rather than dedicated venues, visiting audiences
from elsewhere in the Halley subnet experience Belgrano's music as inseparable from its mechanic
culture — the smell of oil, the presence of actual project vehicles mid-repair becomes part of what a
show even is. Outside acts that later try to imitate "the Belgrano sound" in cleaner, purpose-built
spaces get quietly written off by subnet audiences as missing the entire point.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Belgrano's garage rock scene draws real subnet-wide travel and attention — but it still lives entirely
inside actual working mechanic garages, never formalized into its own venues, making the sound and its
setting inseparable, and quietly setting the terms by which every future imitation elsewhere in the
Halley subnet gets judged, and found wanting.
