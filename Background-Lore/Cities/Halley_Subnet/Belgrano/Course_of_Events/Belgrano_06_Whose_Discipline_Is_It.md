## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Belgrano's founding Argentine population set the base's whole operational character — the discipline,
the bearing, the function-before-comfort baseline every resident inherits, taken for granted as simply
what Belgrano is.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Generations of German, American, British, and Brazilian immigration outpace the founding population so
thoroughly that Argentina slips to the lowest recognized demographic tier in the city — a founding
nation that built the whole civic character now numerically the smallest group living inside it. A
real generational argument surfaces: do residents whose own heritage isn't Argentine at all still get
to call the discipline they were raised in genuinely theirs, or does it stay symbolically Argentine
regardless of who actually lives here now. Nobody with the standing to settle the question wants to be
the one who tells newer families their inherited discipline isn't really theirs — and the question sits,
unresolved, getting more pointed with each generation that passes without an answer.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a newer-heritage resident
who has lived Belgrano's discipline their entire life and bristles at any suggestion it isn't fully,
legitimately theirs, and an Argentine-heritage resident who isn't hostile to that claim exactly, but is
anxious about a real, specific history — actual exile, actual founding — being flattened into generic
civic mythology nobody remembers the particulars of. Neither side is wrong: A doesn't want their whole
identity treated as borrowed; B doesn't want the last living thread to an actual historical event to
dissolve into vague civic pride divorced from what it commemorates.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a younger resident of
German, American, British, or Brazilian heritage — someone whose family has been in Belgrano for
generations and has no other civic identity to fall back on. Character B is plausibly an
Argentine-heritage elder or family culture-keeper, one of the shrinking number of residents who still
has a personal, family-level connection to the original exile and founding, watching that connection
thin out faster than they're comfortable with.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

No formal ruling ever gets made. Whatever forum the argument surfaces in — a community meeting, a
council session, an open dispute that draws an audience — it doesn't end in a vote or a declaration; it
simply stops being actively litigated, because everyday practice keeps answering the question without
anyone having to decide it. The discipline belongs, in the end, to whoever keeps living it, Argentine
surname or not — settled by use, not by ruling.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because there's never a formal ruling, no institution or document in Belgrano actually defines what
"belonging to the discipline" requires — no citizenship-of-culture test, no naturalization ritual,
nothing an outsider could complete to formally prove themselves. Belonging is demonstrated only through
accumulated everyday behavior, never certified by anyone.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because there's no formal test, claims to genuine Belgrano standing become impossible to fake quickly
but equally impossible to formally revoke — someone born into the city who visibly abandons the
discipline quietly loses standing in others' eyes with no mechanism ever invoked to strip it, while a
newly arrived family that throws itself into the operational culture can earn real standing surprisingly
fast, purely through demonstrated behavior. An unwritten, constantly renegotiated reputation economy
forms around authenticity, with heritage and paperwork equally beside the point.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Belgrano's discipline genuinely belongs to whoever lives it, exactly as the resolution intended — but
with no formal anchor to that belonging, status inside the city's own culture runs entirely on an
unwritten reputation economy that rewards continuous demonstrated commitment and grants nobody, heritage
or not, a permanent or secure claim.
