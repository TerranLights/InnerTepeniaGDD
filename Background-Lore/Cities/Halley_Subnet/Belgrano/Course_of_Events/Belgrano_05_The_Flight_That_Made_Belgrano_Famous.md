## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Belgrano's own routine records suggest something worth finding across the ice, and its own garage
culture can build and maintain a vehicle capable of reaching it — the raw ingredients for an
achievement, sitting unused, because no city this far out has ever attempted an overland crossing this
ambitious before.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

An all-robot crew sets out in Belgrano-built Arrastradoras, with material support shipped in from
Palmer City and Rothera, on a route nobody else in Tepenia had ever mapped. The crossing succeeds — and
succeeding means the discovery belongs, in the public record that starts forming almost immediately, to
the expedition itself: the crew who made the crossing, the vehicles that carried them, the moment of
arrival. Nobody outside the base's own records staff has much reason to ask what made the crossing
possible in the first place, and the records staff aren't the ones anyone's asking.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation over credit — not over whether the
crossing was an achievement (nobody disputes that) but over what the city's memory of it should center.
This shouldn't play as glory-hound-vs.-humble-workers. A's case is genuine: physical risk, technical
skill, and real danger were involved in the crossing itself, and that deserves to be honored plainly.
B's case is equally genuine: none of it happens without decades of unglamorous recordkeeping and engine
maintenance that nobody thinks to credit precisely because it never looked like an achievement while it
was happening.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a member of the
expedition crew itself, or someone advocating on the crew's behalf for how the city commemorates the
crossing — invested, reasonably, in the visible, singular act of daring being the thing remembered.
Character B is plausibly a maintenance officer or records clerk (thematically continuous with the kind
of figure in Suggestion #4) arguing that the holiday, if there's going to be one, should honor the
groundwork rather than the departure — not out of resentment toward the crew, but out of genuine belief
that the wrong lesson gets taught if the city only remembers the glamorous half of the story.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The expedition's version wins, not through any dramatic argument but by simple weight of what's easier
to commemorate: a departure has a date, a crew, a route — a shape a holiday can actually be built
around. The unglamorous decades of recordkeeping and engine repair have no comparable single moment.
Expedition Day is established to commemorate the departure, not the archival and maintenance work that
actually made the crossing possible.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the holiday enshrines the departure rather than the groundwork, Belgrano's civic memory
quietly distorts its own self-understanding over the following years — new residents growing up with
Expedition Day come to understand the city's greatest achievement as a single act of daring, not as
the slow accumulation of institutional habit that actually made it possible.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the celebrated version of the story centers heroic action, younger residents aspiring to matter
the way the expedition crew mattered gravitate toward visible, singular achievement — wanting their own
Expedition Day moment — rather than toward the patient, unglamorous maintenance and archival work the
city actually depends on most, a quiet generational undervaluing of exactly the kind of competence
Belgrano's whole survival has always run on.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Belgrano proudly celebrates Expedition Day as its defining civic achievement — but the holiday's own
framing has begun quietly warping how the city understands itself, prizing the visible, singular act of
daring over the invisible, cumulative competence that actually built it, and actually still holds it
together.
