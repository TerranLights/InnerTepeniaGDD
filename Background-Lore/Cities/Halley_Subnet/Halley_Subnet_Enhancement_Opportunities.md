# Halley Subnet — But/Therefore Enhancement Opportunities

**Part of the City History Enhancement Opportunities pass** — see
`Cities/City_History_Enhancement_Opportunities_Tracker.md` for the overall purpose, per-city method, and
progress index across all subnets. This file holds the Halley subnet's own flagged ideas only.

**Scope note, carried over from every city's own Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md:** all flagged ideas
below are meant for *pre*-Long Night War, Second Interwar Period content only — no war damage, no
post-war material. Flagged ideas that would require war-era framing are out of scope for this pass.

### Sanay

**Files read:** `Sanay_Catalog.md`, `Sanay_Mega_Init.md`, `Sanay_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` (all 10
existing chains).

**Existing 10 chains already cover:** bedrock-vs-ice-shelf storm endurance (#1), the Arcanet-nexus
naming-credit gap (#2), the "function over sentiment" rebuild decision (#3) and its uneven human cost
(#4), Sanay-Troll shipping coordination (#5), the Trade Yard's bounded Upper-Earth-contact zone (#6),
SANAE's science mission fading into pure heritage (#7-8), the German demographic tide diluting the
founding South African population (#9), and the "Competence Without Commentary" civic doctrine (#10).
The four Mega_Init real-world picks (Rotterdam, Guangzhou/Canton System, Silicon Valley, Durban) are
already fully absorbed into these. New angles below are ones the existing 10 don't touch.

1. **Division of industry → history.** Vision Notes establishes that Sanay's docks/truckyards/business
   district run on roughly equal human-robot labor with robots holding *slightly better* positions —
   and the Mega_Init's own Synthesis Notes flag this as the specific place-of-origin for what later
   became Tepenia's project-wide default human-robot baseline ("egalitarian, skewed toward robots").
   None of the 10 existing chains dramatize how or why that specific labor arrangement actually formed
   at Sanay's docks. Given how load-bearing this fact is for wider project canon, a chain tracing its
   actual origin (a specific staffing decision, a specific crisis it solved) seems like real missed
   value, not just local color. **DRAFTED 2026-07-17 as Course of Events Suggestion #11, "The Record
   Nobody Meant to Get Wrong"** — see `Sanay_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` and
   `Course_of_Events/Sanay_11_The_Record_Nobody_Meant_to_Get_Wrong.md`.

2. **Resident character cultures.** The Catalog itself explicitly flags an unused seed: the German/UK
   "the schedule is sacred" punctuality culture versus the Brazilian population's "people matter more
   than the schedule" relational-warmth culture, sitting in real, ongoing tension. This is distinct from
   existing chain #4 (a labor/rebuild-cost asymmetry between dockworkers and everyone else) — this would
   be a values/communication-style clash between two demographic populations specifically, not a
   resource-allocation dispute.

3. **Real-world historical precedent.** Real-world dock labor was historically hired via the "shape-up"
   system — a chaotic, often exploitative daily hiring call where workers gathered and a foreman picked
   faces from the crowd — before union-negotiated seniority and rotation systems replaced it as a
   genuinely non-ideal-but-workable fix. Sanay's own established "equitable rotation of undesirable
   shifts" governance (Catalog, German entry, "Fairness and justice") reads as the settled outcome of
   exactly this kind of struggle, but none of the 10 chains show the rockier system it plausibly
   replaced or the specific dispute that forced the change.

4. **City personality / geography / geology → history.** Sanay's whole civic mythology already runs on
   bedrock-vs-ice-shelf construction, but only as a *survival* story (chain #1, itself a pre-war storm,
   not war damage). None of the 10 chains treat Sanay's bedrock-construction expertise as an *exportable
   skill*. A chain where a neighboring ice-shelf city (Halley or Neumayer) facing its own foundation
   crisis has to call on Sanay's engineers would give the geological distinction a second,
   complementary use beyond "we survived the storm."

5. **Other.** The Catalog separately flags the Brazilian population's Día de los Muertos-adjacent
   death/remembrance practices against the German population's pragmatic register as "another real point
   of internal cultural contrast worth designing around later." A chain about reconciling two different
   mourning registers into one shared civic memorial practice — for the storm dead from chain #1, an
   ordinary workplace accident, or any other pre-war loss — would use this directly.

   relationship rather than declaring a winner. None of the 10 chains show Abowasa actually developing

   Head-center precision as a civic *habit* rather than just a developer-research note — a chain about

**Files read:** `Halley_Catalog.md`, `Halley_Mega_Init.md`, `Halley_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md`

**Existing 10 chains already cover:** the founding commitment to continuous propulsion (#1), the

1. **Division of industry → history.** Halley's entire economic identity is "pure highway-gateway town,
   no significant independent economy" (Catalog City-Type). None of the 10 chains explore civic *ambition*
   around that fact — only its infrastructure consequences (chain #3's no-docks/no-airstrip choice). A
   chain about a push, from a growing population, to develop something closer to independent local
   industry — and the city either resisting or accommodating it without abandoning the gateway identity —
   would be a genuinely different angle than the existing infrastructure-only chains.

2. **Resident character cultures.** The Catalog identifies a genuine, ready-made hook the Synthesis Notes
   themselves call out as usable: Canada's real Watson Lake "Sign Post Forest" precedent, a
   marker-leaving tradition where travelers and residents leave physical markers behind — turning a city
   that can't stay in one place into one that still keeps a permanent record of who passed through. None
   of the 10 chains use this directly; it's distinct from Position Day (chain #4, a survey record of the
   city's *own* position) since this would be about individual travelers' and residents' own personal
   markers, not civic surveying.

3. **Real-world historical precedent.** The Synthesis Notes identify three genuinely distinct cultural
   postures toward the same ground instability: Germany/UK's heavy-engineering response (Wadden Sea
   polders, Fens reclamation), France's Camargue-derived "live with it, don't fight it" pastoral
   coexistence, and USA/Canada's adaptability-plus-hospitality pragmatism. None of the 10 chains
   dramatize these as an actual disagreement — a real values clash over how to respond to a specific
   ground-instability problem, with residents from different populations genuinely arguing past each
   other before landing on (or failing to land on) a shared approach.

4. **City personality / geography / geology → history.** Halley's established faction, "The
   Methodologists," crystallizes the city's whole outlook as "rigor is whatever survives being wrong
   about the ground under your feet" — a real, named civic philosophy. Chains #1 and #6 show this
   philosophy *applied*, but none of the 10 show it being *founded* — the actual moment it went from an
   individual attitude to a named, articulated civic school of thought residents could point to and
   invoke, the same way Sanay's "Competence Without Commentary" got its own origin moment (Sanay chain
   #10). **DRAFTED 2026-07-17 as Course of Events Suggestion #11, "Wrong, and Then What"** — see
   `Halley_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` and `Course_of_Events/Halley_11_Wrong_and_Then_What.md`.

5. **Other.** The Synthesis Notes flag that USA and Canada hold "outsized influence on Halley's actual
   lived culture despite Brazil (11.69%) and UK (10.57%) each holding a larger or comparable population
   share" — because only USA/Canada have a developed cultural response to Halley's *second* impermanence
   axis (gateway-not-destination), not just the ground-instability one. None of the 10 chains dramatize
   that imbalance becoming visible or contested — a moment where Brazil or UK's numerically larger
   population pushes back on a gateway-culture default they had no real hand in shaping.

   via a dedicated route separate from the Sinheung chamber freight (#6) or the Halley-bound goods (#7).
   None of the 10 chains dramatize the establishment of *that* specific route — a supply line to a
   pilgrimage city with genuine religious significance, not just another freight destination. A chain about
   how that route got established, and what it meant to be the city literally keeping a sacred city fed,
   would use a flagged connection none of the existing chains touch. **DRAFTED 2026-07-17 as Course of
   Events Suggestion #11, "No Leverage in It"** — see `Troll_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` and
   `Course_of_Events/Troll_11_No_Leverage_in_It.md`.

2. **Resident character cultures.** The Catalog explicitly names France's Courchevel-echoing "aviation as
   skilled, even glamorous spectacle" as "a genuine counter-note" and "a second, smaller register" against
   the dominant freight-precision civic identity — but no chain uses it. All 10 existing chains treat
   aviation purely as logistics infrastructure (fleet capacity, fuel, scheduling, chamber cargo). A chain
   built around a celebrated stunt-flying or air-show tradition specifically drawing on France's glamour
   register would give Troll's aviation identity a texture the current chains never explore.

3. **Real-world historical precedent.** The Mega_Init explicitly flags Shannon Airport's real cautionary
   history — an airfield's economic centrality can evaporate the moment the specific technological
   necessity behind it changes — as "a risk implicit in Troll's own aviation-dependent economy even if
   nothing currently threatens it." None of the 10 chains address this: #4 and #5 both assume continued,
   growing centrality and invest accordingly. A chain where city leadership deliberately hedges against
   that Shannon-style obsolescence risk — diversifying into some non-aviation economic capacity as
   insurance — would use a real precedent the project's own research already surfaced and left unused.

4. **City personality / geography / geology → history.** Troll Airfield sits at roughly 3,000m in
   dramatic nunatak/mountain terrain (Heimefrontfjella visible in the distance), but none of the 10 chains
   touch the sheer physical difficulty of operating a high-altitude runway itself — thin-air aircraft
   performance, weather risk, technical landing demands (echoing Leadville's and Courchevel's own real
   operating challenges, both already used for other purposes in the Catalog). A chain about solving a
   genuinely difficult high-altitude operational problem — rather than the governance/economic angles the
   existing 10 focus on — would ground the elevation itself in something load-bearing.

5. **Other.** The Synthesis Notes flag Russia's weak City-Type match as notable in itself: "a first —
   usually only Brazil lands in that bucket." Russia is Troll's second-largest population (9.88%) yet
   carries a "mountain-hardy, elevation-mastery" register that doesn't map onto the freight-precision civic
   value USA, UK, and Germany converge on. None of the 10 chains explore what it's like to be a
   substantial population whose own cultural register sits outside the thing the city is publicly *for* —
   distinct from France's counter-note (#2 above), since France's register is still aviation-flavored,
   while Russia's isn't tied to aviation or freight at all.
