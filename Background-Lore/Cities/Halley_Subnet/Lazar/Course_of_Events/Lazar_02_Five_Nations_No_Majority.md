## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Novolazarevskaya's Russian founding population, already blended with the absorbed Maitri overflow,
sets the city's early character. It's a single, coalesced culture by this point, Russian in flavor if
not in exclusivity, with no rival community large enough to contest it.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Four other nations arrive during the Second Interwar Period in comparable numbers, all at once, none
of them large enough to dominate the others or even to clearly outweigh what's already there.
Overnight, in demographic terms, "majority" stops meaning anything simple in Lazar — five roughly
equal blocs where every other Tepenian city has one clear founding population and, at most, a
handful of smaller arrivals to contend with. And because the city was already unified before these
four arrived, they aren't settling into a divided city they might split further — they're settling
into an already-fused whole that has no single default anyone can assimilate into or push against.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** with no majority to defer to, someone still has
to decide practical things — what language civic notices get posted in, what calendar of holidays the
city observes, whose administrative conventions become the default when a decision has to be made
today. A wants to settle that practically: pick one bloc's conventions as the working default, purely
for efficiency, the way any large administration eventually has to standardize on something. B
resists, not out of loyalty to any single bloc, but because picking any one default recreates exactly
the majority-versus-minority dynamic Lazar has never had — and once created, that dynamic is very
hard to undo. Neither side is wrong: running five languages and five customs in parallel really is
inefficient, and standardizing on any one of them really would quietly promote it above the other
four.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a newly arrived
administrator or civic organizer from one of the four newer national communities, pragmatic and
overworked, genuinely just trying to make the paperwork function. Character B is plausibly an
established civic figure descended from the original Novolazarevskaya-Maitri population, someone
whose actual job is keeping peace among all five blocs and who understands, better than A does yet,
that "efficient" and "fair" aren't the same thing in a city built the way Lazar was.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A's push for a single administrative default never gets voted down in any dramatic way — it simply
never accumulates enough support to happen. Every time it looks close to settled, the balance
between the five blocs shifts again, just enough that no proposal can hold its coalition together
long enough to pass. Administrators default, out of sheer necessity, to running things in parallel —
multiple languages, multiple institutions, redundant translation as ordinary practice rather than
exception. The newcomers never form a single "new arrivals" bloc against "the old settlement,"
because they are exactly as divided among themselves as they are from anyone else. Lazar's
demographic dilution never cleanly separates into old settlement versus new arrivals at all —
everyone simply becomes part of an already-fused city that was never going to hand any one group the
default position to begin with.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Civic administration institutionalizes redundancy as an actual, ongoing practice rather than a
temporary accommodation — documents produced and maintained in multiple languages as standard
procedure, parallel community institutions (chambers of commerce, civic associations) coexisting by
design rather than by oversight, a genuine administrative cost the city simply absorbs because no
cheaper, single-track alternative was ever politically available.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That habit of never building one system when several parallel ones will do stops being a purely
linguistic or administrative workaround and starts becoming a genuine civic instinct, applied well
beyond language — the same five-way math that makes no single tongue the default makes no single
power source, transit line, or supply route the default either. Redundancy, first practiced as a
compromise nobody chose on purpose, becomes the reflex Lazar reaches for whenever it builds anything
at scale.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Lazar is a genuinely plural megacity whose administrative and, increasingly, its infrastructural
culture runs on deliberate redundancy rather than convergence — not because any planner designed it
that way from the start, but because five roughly equal populations never once let a single solution
win by default, and the habit outlived the argument that produced it.
