## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The former-Maitri expansion is in the middle of its Interwar-boom growth, built fast and cheap
because speed and available space matter more than durability when nothing has actually put the
difference to the test yet. Nobody is watching closely enough to worry.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A major industrial fire sparks in one of the expansion's dense commercial blocks and spreads far
faster than anyone expects, precisely because of how quickly and cheaply the district was built. The
expansion suffers real, serious damage — burned structures, disrupted commerce, displaced workers —
while the old core's heavier, deliberately robust construction standard keeps the fire from ever
threatening it directly. The damage is bad enough that simply rebuilding the burned district the same
fast, cheap way risks setting up the exact same disaster to happen again.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a dispute over how to rebuild, pitting safety
against urgency. A wants the burned block rebuilt to a genuinely more durable standard, closer to the
old core's own, to prevent a repeat. B needs the district rebuilt fast, because every week of delay
is a week of lost income for displaced workers and shuttered businesses who can't afford to wait for
the "right" way to be built. Neither side is being unreasonable — A has just watched exactly what
cheap, fast construction costs when something goes wrong, and B is looking at real people who need
their livelihoods back now, not after a lengthy, expensive rebuild that stricter codes would require.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a fire-safety
inspector or a structural engineer, possibly trained in or influenced by the old core's own
construction tradition, pushing hard for durable standards after seeing the fire firsthand. Character
B is plausibly a former-Maitri commercial developer or a coalition of small-business owners from the
burned district, whose stake in a fast rebuild is not greed but survival.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side gets what it actually wanted. The burned block gets rebuilt to a modestly improved
standard — better than what burned, well short of the old core's full durability — a stopgap
compromise that satisfies nobody outright but that both sides can live with under the time pressure
they're both facing. Lazar has its first real civic argument about whether growth should keep
outrunning durability, and the argument doesn't get resolved so much as deferred, waiting for the
next expansion phase to raise the exact same question all over again.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the modest, compromise standard was never actually settled as formal policy — only improvised
once, under pressure, for one specific district — it becomes an informal reference point invoked and
re-litigated every time a new commercial growth phase begins, rather than a fixed rule anyone can
simply point to and follow.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

With the standard never formally settled, Lazar's own construction industry splits into two
competing professional camps — builders who default to fast-and-cheap practice, and builders who
push durable-standard methods — each winning contracts depending on which mood is politically
ascendant whenever a given expansion phase begins. The rivalry outlives any individual fire or
dispute, becoming a permanent, recurring feature of how the city actually gets built.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The former-Maitri expansion carries a visible patchwork of building standards from successive,
half-resolved durability arguments, its construction trade split into competing schools of practice
that resurface with every new growth phase. Commerce keeps booming; the underlying question of how
fast is too fast to build safely stays open, a recurring feature of the city's growth rather than a
crisis it ever fully settles.
