## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The old Novolazarevskaya core carries, as its defining civic pride, an unbroken record: since Tepenian
Lazar's own 2564 founding, this district has never once gone dark, and that continuity has become the
core's whole self-image — quiet, reliable, proven by longevity rather than by anything it's had to fight
for.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

That pride has gone largely untested for generations — until a sustained, region-wide grid failure
sweeps across much of the Halley subnet, knocking out power at other cities and nodes for days at a
stretch. For the first time, the old core's tradition of continuous, self-maintained infrastructure
faces a real exam, not against its own legend, but against a direct, simultaneous comparison to how
everyone else's systems are holding up under the exact same stress.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** the old core's grid is intact and everyone
else's isn't — so the question is what the core owes the rest of the subnet during the crisis. A
wants to extend real capacity outward to hard-hit areas, arguing it's civically and morally
indefensible to sit on an untouched grid while people elsewhere freeze in the dark. B resists sharing
freely, not out of callousness, but because the core's reliability has never actually been tested
under real strain either — B genuinely doesn't know how much slack the system has, and fears that
extending capacity carelessly could be the thing that finally breaks the unbroken record everyone is
counting on. Both positions are defensible: A is arguing from obligation, B from the very real risk of
turning a proven system into an untested one at the worst possible moment.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a subnet-wide
grid or emergency-coordination official, stationed in Lazar precisely because of its role as the
Halley subnet's distribution hub, whose job during the crisis is keeping as much of the wider subnet
functioning as possible. Character B is plausibly the old core's own utilities or maintenance chief —
someone whose entire professional identity is bound up in the district's unbroken record, and who is
the one person who will personally answer for it if extending power outward is what finally ends it.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

After real argument, B agrees to a narrow, carefully rationed extension — not a full share of the
core's grid, but enough to keep a defined set of critical facilities elsewhere in the subnet running:
hospitals, emergency shelters, essential coordination points. The core's own grid stays intact. The
compromise means the old core spends the crisis doing exactly what its own folklore always claimed it
would, and this time in front of the entire subnet at once: it is, quite literally, the only part of
Halley still lit — illuminating not just itself, but the specific lifelines it deliberately chose to
extend outward.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The crisis-tested, rationed power-sharing arrangement doesn't stay a one-time improvisation. In its
aftermath, it gets formalized into an actual standing interconnection protocol between the old core's
grid and the rest of the Halley subnet — defined criteria for what gets prioritized, how much capacity
can be extended without risking the core's own supply, and who has the authority to activate it.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the old core is now formally recognized as the subnet's backup power anchor — not just
informally proud of never having gone dark, but contractually responsible for helping others avoid
it — later infrastructure investment gets justified and funded partly on that regional-anchor role.
Lazar's own engineering sector starts explicitly designing new generation and transit capacity with
subnet-wide resilience in mind, not merely the city's own needs, a scope its own institutions never
had reason to plan for before this one crisis wrote it into policy.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The old core's "never gone dark" pride is no longer just personal civic folklore — it is now formal,
standing infrastructure policy, with the district serving as the Halley subnet's recognized power
anchor of last resort, and Lazar's engineering culture increasingly oriented toward resilience at a
regional scale rather than a purely local one.
