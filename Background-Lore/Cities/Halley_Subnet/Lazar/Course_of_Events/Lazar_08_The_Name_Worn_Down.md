## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

"Novolazarevskaya" is still the city's formal, official name, even though the founding Russian
population that gave rise to it is by now just one of five roughly equal national groups, no longer
any kind of majority. The name has outlasted the demographic fact it once described.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The full name is simply too unwieldy for a now-plural population's daily use. Over generations,
ordinary speech wears it down — not through any deliberate decision, but through the accumulation of
countless small everyday shortcuts, until "Lazar" is what everyone actually says, whatever the formal
records still read. Somewhere in that slow erosion, someone idly notices the shortened form's
accidental resemblance to "Lazarus" — a story about being raised from death by an outside
intervention — and the observation starts to circulate, half-joke, half-curiosity, alongside the name
itself.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a dispute over whether the city should keep
correcting the informal name back to its full form in official contexts, or simply accept and adopt
what everyone already says. A treats the drift toward "Lazar" as a small erosion of the founding
heritage worth defending in principle, even in a city that no longer has a founding majority to
defend it for. B has only ever known the short form, and pushes to make it official — not out of
disrespect for the city's origins, but because the Lazarus resonance strikes B as something genuinely
worth telling on purpose rather than quietly correcting out of records. Neither side is fighting over
the name for its own sake; they're fighting over what kind of relationship the city should have with
its own history — preserved as written, or claimed and reshaped by whoever's actually living in it now.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an archivist,
records-office administrator, or an elder-generation figure descended from the original
Novolazarevskaya population, invested in the full name as a mark of respect for a heritage that's
already a minority position demographically. Character B is plausibly a younger civic or cultural
figure — quite possibly from one of the four newer national communities, or simply someone born into
the already-fused city — who sees no contradiction in embracing the shortened name and its accidental
irony at the same time.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Informal usage wins, the same way it always does — not through any single decisive vote, but through
sheer accumulated weight, bureaucratic resistance overwhelmed by ordinary use exactly as the full name
itself was worn down syllable by syllable. Official records stop fighting the shortened form and begin
accepting it as the city's de facto name. The Lazarus association goes public along with it, becoming
shared, half-joking civic folklore rather than a private linguistic curiosity — the city that saved
itself, on its own strength, ends up carrying a name that quietly tells the opposite story, and
residents keep repeating the resemblance anyway, delighted rather than troubled by the irony.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

With the Lazarus echo now openly acknowledged rather than merely noticed, it becomes raw material
that local storytellers, tavern conversation, and informal civic naming actually start using on
purpose — worked into local sayings, invoked in toasts, occasionally borrowed for the names of streets
or gathering places. The accidental resemblance graduates from a coincidence people repeat into
cultural material people actively reach for.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the Lazarus association becomes genuinely woven into how Lazarians talk about themselves, it
starts subtly shaping how the city frames its own accomplishments — any major achievement the city
manages through its own effort gets described, half-ironically, in the borrowed language of
miraculous salvation, even though the actual story being told is the opposite of a rescue narrative.
Residents narrate self-made success in the vocabulary of being raised from the dead, fully aware of
the irony and fond of it anyway.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

"Lazar" is the fully accepted, official-enough name of Tepenia's largest city, its Lazarus echo openly
and affectionately claimed as civic folklore, woven directly into how residents narrate their own
city's self-made successes — a linguistic accident that has become genuine, load-bearing cultural
material, ironic in a way most residents enjoy rather than examine too closely.
