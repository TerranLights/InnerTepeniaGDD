## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Lazar's sheer scale has already produced a level of infrastructure redundancy no other Halley subnet
city can match — multiple independent power sources, multiple transit lines, overlapping supply
routes — mostly as an accident of scale rather than as anyone's deliberate defensive strategy.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A sustained, subnet-wide blizzard system, the worst in generations, tests every city's resilience at
once. Smaller Halley subnet cities start losing power, transit, or supply access for days at a
stretch. Lazar's redundant systems keep enough of the city running that no single failure ever fully
halts it — but "enough running" is not the same thing as "untouched." Real, visible disruption is
happening somewhere in the city at literally every point during the storm, moving from district to
district as different systems buckle and get compensated for in turn.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** during a multi-day emergency, someone has to
decide, district by district, which failing system gets the city's redundant backup capacity routed
to it first, and by what criteria. A, an emergency logistics coordinator, wants to allocate by
objective, citywide criteria — most people served, most critical services protected. B, a specific
district's representative, is fighting fiercely for that one district's priority — not out of pure
self-interest, but because B is the person actually accountable to the residents freezing in the
dark right now, not to an abstract citywide average. This is systemic optimization against localized
accountability, and both positions are legitimate: A's approach genuinely saves more people overall;
B's objection is that "overall" is cold comfort to whoever's district loses the coin flip.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an emergency
logistics coordinator, quite possibly drawn from the same coordination tradition established
elsewhere as Lazar's ground-logistics inheritance, working from citywide data under real time
pressure. Character B is plausibly a civic representative from a specific neighborhood — perhaps the
former-Maitri expansion, given its faster growth and comparatively less redundant local
infrastructure — advocating hard for that one district's people.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The two positions don't resolve into a clean win for either side — they get hashed into a working
district-by-district triage system under real storm pressure, built from A's objective criteria but
with genuine, grudging input from district representatives like B factored into the rotation. It
works: no district goes fully dark for the storm's entire duration, but every district takes its turn
absorbing disruption. Lazar comes through the worst weather event in living memory with a reputation
less for being untouched and more for being unstoppable — damaged in patches, functioning as a whole,
throughout the entire ordeal, precisely because neither pure systemic logic nor pure local advocacy
won outright.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The improvised triage protocol worked out under storm pressure gets formally written down and adopted
afterward as Lazar's standing emergency-rotation protocol — a codified system defining which district
gets priority under what specific criteria, rather than remaining a personality-dependent judgment
call to be re-litigated from scratch during the next crisis.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because there is now a formal, public rule for how disruption gets shared out during emergencies,
individual districts start proactively investing in their own supplementary local backup measures
specifically for the periods the published rotation rule tells them, in advance, they're likely to be
hit hardest — a self-reinforcing, decentralized resilience culture that grows directly out of the
rule's predictability, not merely out of the city's inherited scale.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Lazar's citywide redundancy is no longer just an accident of scale — it now pairs a codified, publicly
known emergency-rotation protocol with individual districts actively building their own supplementary
resilience around it, deepening the city's "unstoppable, not untouched" reputation into something
actively maintained rather than merely structurally inherited.
