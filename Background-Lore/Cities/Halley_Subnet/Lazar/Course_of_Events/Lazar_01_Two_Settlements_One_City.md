## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Novolazarevskaya's population has outgrown its own footprint and begun overflowing into the
unoccupied Maitri site nearby. Nobody has decided this is a merger — it's just people building where
there's room to build, spilling past an old boundary that was never much more than a line on a
survey map to begin with.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Overflow isn't the same thing as unity. For a full generation, the two areas keep functioning as
separate, if adjacent, communities — their own informal leadership, their own daily rhythms, their
own sense of which side of the gap a person belongs to. But shared infrastructure, shared labor, and
shared daily life keep eroding the distance between them anyway, faster than anyone is formally
deciding it should. Water lines cross the gap before anyone asks permission. Work crews split their
week between both sides without thinking about it as crossing a border. The erosion has no single
author and no single date — it is simply happening, continuously, underneath whatever the two
communities still tell themselves about being separate.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement over whether the ongoing,
undirected merging of the two settlements should be formally named and dated — turned into an actual
event with a ceremony, a treaty, a founding-day observance — or left to keep happening the way it
already is, without anyone drawing a line under it. This isn't a fight about whether unification is
good; both sides already live inside it daily. It's a fight about whether unification should have an
author. A wants a clean, namable moment because ambiguity feels like avoidance — someone should stand
up and say "this is now one city," with all the practical clarity that gives everyone else. B resists
specifically because naming the moment means also deciding, in that same moment, whose institutions,
whose customs, and whose leadership get treated as the default going forward — a fight neither
settlement is currently equipped to win cleanly, and one B would rather not force while the ordinary,
unglamorous work of actually living together is going fine on its own.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a record-keeper,
surveyor, or minor administrative figure on either side — someone whose job is made harder every day
by the absence of a clean line to point to, and who wants the ambiguity resolved for practical, not
sentimental, reasons. Character B is plausibly an elder or informal community figure from either
settlement who has watched enough slow, working coexistence to distrust anyone's attempt to formalize
it prematurely, suspecting (correctly) that formalization always ends up favoring whichever side gets
to write the document.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side wins outright. A's push for a formal, dated founding moment keeps almost happening and
keeps stalling — overtaken, every time, by some more urgent shared problem that needs solving today
rather than a ceremony that can wait. B never has to win the argument; B only has to keep it
postponed long enough for the two areas to finish becoming one place in practice, without anyone ever
having formally declared it. By the time anyone circles back to the question, there is no longer a
clean seam left to draw a line across. Lazar becomes Tepenia's only city with no single founding
story — a coalescence completed a full century before its later demographic waves, and nobody can
name the day it happened, because there wasn't one.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Without a founding event to anchor them, the city's earliest administrative records stay genuinely
patchy through this whole period — property claims, labor assignments, and infrastructure
responsibilities that crossed the old Novolazarevskaya/Maitri line were never formally reconciled
onto a single register, because no single register was ever created to reconcile them onto. The city
runs fine day to day; its paper trail simply doesn't match its lived reality.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That patchwork of unreconciled early records becomes, generations later, the specific practical
headache that eventually forces the question A originally raised back onto the table — not out of
sentiment this time, but because a growing megacity genuinely cannot plan large-scale infrastructure,
resolve boundary-adjacent property disputes, or assign civic responsibility cleanly without some
administrative baseline to work from. The absence B successfully preserved becomes, later, the exact
gap someone else eventually has to fill.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Lazar is a single, functioning, thoroughly coalesced city with no clean founding date and no
formally reconciled early property or administrative record — a city that grew together rather than
being built together, still carrying the loose ends of that undirected process into whatever comes
next.
