## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Lazar has long since become Tepenia's largest city by a wide margin — its own commercial density,
population, and economic weight visibly dwarf every other Tepenian city. Formal political authority,
however, sits elsewhere, with a much smaller seat of government. For most of Lazar's history this
mismatch is simply background fact, unremarked upon in daily civic life.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A major resource-allocation decision — the kind with real, direct consequences for Lazar's own
infrastructure — gets made by the formal seat of government without any real consultation of Lazar's own
civic leadership. The decision itself might be defensible on its own terms, but the process behind it
wasn't: nobody with Lazar's own perspective was meaningfully in the room.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** an institutional dispute rather than a personal one
— a genuine disagreement about how national decisions ought to get made, not a grudge between two
individuals. Character A represents the formal capital's own position: legitimate constitutional
authority doesn't scale with population or commerce, and ceding influence based on economic weight alone
would set a dangerous precedent. Character B represents Lazar's own frustration: a decision this
consequential, landing on a city this size, without real consultation, is a genuine governance failure
regardless of whose formal authority technically covers it. Neither side is acting in bad faith — this is
a real structural tension the city's own growth created, not a personality conflict.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a senior official from
the formal seat of government, defending the decision (and the process that produced it) on procedural
grounds. Character B is plausibly a prominent Lazar civic leader — an administrator, a business figure
with genuine standing, or a coalition representing multiple sectors of the city — pressing the case that
Lazar's own scale deserves a real voice in decisions that affect it directly.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Lazar's leadership pushes back formally and loudly, through whatever channels actually exist for it —
petitions, public argument, direct appeals to the capital. The case is well-made and hard to dismiss on
the merits. It doesn't matter. Practical economic weight and formal political authority turn out to be
genuinely different things, and no amount of forceful argument converts one into the other.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The original decision stands unchanged. What does change is Lazar's own institutional memory of the
exchange — civic leadership now has a specific, citable precedent for exactly how little formal leverage
the city's own size actually buys it, rather than a vague, untested assumption about how national
decision-making works.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Rather than escalating the fight or quietly accepting the outcome, Lazar's civic culture absorbs the
episode into its own self-understanding: the city that's biggest but not in charge, articulated now as a
known, named condition rather than an unexamined background fact. Future disputes over Lazar's own
influence get measured against this one, the reference case everyone already knows the ending to.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Lazar carries forward its own outsized economic and demographic weight exactly as before — but now with
a permanent, unresolved civic posture layered underneath it: the largest city in the country, and also,
unmistakably, not the one in charge, a friction the city has learned to name but never actually resolves.
