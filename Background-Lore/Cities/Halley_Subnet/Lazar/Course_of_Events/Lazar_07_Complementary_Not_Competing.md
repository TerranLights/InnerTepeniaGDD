## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Neumayer, off the Halley subnet's main line, has precision engineering and almost nothing else. Lazar
has a genuine megacity's worth of commerce and population and no reason to specialize the way Neumayer
does. The two cities occupy separate, non-overlapping niches from the start, without either one
trying to become the other.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The difference settles into a working pattern almost by default: Neumayer's small population comes to
rely on Lazar as the nearest real market for anything it has no interest in producing itself, while
Lazar's own growing technical sector develops an ongoing, informal professional relationship with
Neumayer's engineers. But the arrangement works so easily, and requires so little active negotiation,
that nobody with actual responsibility for it ever bothers to define or protect it. It becomes exactly
the kind of relationship that's easy to take for granted, precisely because it has never once caused
a problem.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a moment where the taken-for-granted arrangement
gets tested by its own asymmetry. A, from Neumayer's side, has grown anxious about how undocumented
the relationship is — precisely because Neumayer has so much more riding on it than Lazar does; a
disruption on Lazar's end could hurt Neumayer badly, while the reverse barely registers. A pushes for
some kind of formal guarantee. B, from Lazar's side, sees no need to formalize something that has
never once failed, and worries that adding process to a relationship this smooth would only invite the
kind of friction it's never had. Both are right in their own terms: A's asymmetric dependency is real
and worth worrying about; B's instinct that formalizing an easy thing tends to make it worse is also,
often, true.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Neumayer engineer
or trade representative who has done the math on just how much of the small city's economy actually
runs through Lazar, and doesn't like how little of that dependency is written down anywhere. Character
B is plausibly a Lazar civic or commercial figure, perhaps in the logistics or distribution sector,
who has simply never had reason to think about Neumayer as anything but a reliable, minor professional
relationship.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

A's push for formal recognition ultimately fizzles — not because A loses the argument decisively, but
because the relationship keeps working exactly as informally as it always has, even during the moment
of anxiety that prompted the argument in the first place. With nothing actually breaking to prove A's
point, the impulse to formalize never gathers enough support to become policy, and things settle back
into precisely the undocumented convenience they were before. The bond persists for generations as
quiet, undramatic mutual usefulness — the kind neither city's own records make a point of naming,
because neither side ever needed to.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the near-miss moment of anxiety never resulted in a formal arrangement, a handful of
individual professionals — particularly within Lazar's engineering sector — start personally,
informally keeping track of and maintaining the relationship themselves: specific point-of-contact
figures who simply know each other, rather than any city-level guarantee standing behind the
connection.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

The relationship's continuity ends up resting on those specific personal ties rather than any
institutional guarantee — a structural fragility completely invisible in any official record, since
officially nothing was ever wrong. If and when those particular individuals eventually retire, move
on, or are no longer in a position to maintain the connection, the relationship has to be quietly
rebuilt person-to-person all over again, with no formal mechanism to fall back on in the meantime.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Lazar and Neumayer's relationship continues exactly as informal and mutually useful as it has ever
been, its real continuity resting on the accumulated trust of a small number of individual
professionals rather than any institutional guarantee — durable in practice, but quietly more fragile
than either city's own records would ever suggest.
