## The Gap That Closed Itself

Lazar's own twin-district layout — the older, denser Old Core Institutional Quarter and the newer, sprawling
Overflow District (Physical Infrastructure Attributes #1–2) — began as two genuinely separate settlements
with real physical distance between them, the inherited Novolazarevskaya core on one side and the unoccupied
former Maitri site on the other.

*(An informational sheet on the coalescence itself, not a single incident.)* Unlike Abowasa's own comparable
merger, driven early by a single deliberate piece of connective infrastructure, Lazar's own twin-settlement
coalescence happened at megacity scale and over a considerably longer span — successive generations of
ordinary urban growth, incremental building outward from both districts, rather than any single planned
bridging project. Connective development infrastructure filled the gap gradually rather than at any
identifiable moment, meaning nobody currently living in Lazar can point to a specific year the two districts
became one continuous urban area the way Abowasa's own residents at least loosely associate their own merger
with the Joint Household Kitchens. The result, today, is a city whose twin origin remains visibly legible in
its layout — the Old Core's own density is architecturally distinct from the Overflow District's own sprawl
— even though functionally, administratively, and socially, the seam between them stopped mattering
generations ago.

## What Size Actually Bought Lazar

Lazar's survival under heavy Long Night War bombardment — damaged but never destroyed, unlike smaller cities
hit with comparable force — is established as a direct product of the city's own sheer scale (attribute #9),
a form of resilience unique to Lazar among Tepenian cities specifically because no other city was ever large
enough for structural redundancy at this scale to matter.

The uneven damage distribution across the Old Core and the Overflow District (attribute #10) is, per Finding
A, the single physical fact underlying two of the city's richest existing observations at once — the Old
Core's relative preservation, consistent with the broader subnet pattern of older cores surviving better than
newer expansions, and the Overflow District's own combination of visible vitality and visible war damage
existing in the same space. Residents who lived through the bombardment itself describe the experience less
as any one district being spared and the other sacrificed, and more as the sheer redundancy of a two-million-
plus population city meaning that no single strike, or even a sustained campaign of strikes, could plausibly
reach every part of the city at once — survival less through the war overlooking Lazar and more through Lazar
simply being too large for total destruction to be logistically achievable at all.

## The Hall That Never Stopped Counting

The Unbroken Operations Hall (attribute #11), per Finding B, is the physical, walkable monument to Lazar's
own self-generated survival story — the already-established Lazarus name-echo that inverts the Biblical
story, since Lazar survived through its own accumulated scale, not through any external rescue.

**"Ilana Marchetti" *(placeholder, not developer-chosen)*** has worked the Hall's own continuous-operations
record for longer than most of her colleagues have been alive, maintaining the unbroken count marking every
day the city has operated without a full administrative interruption since its actual Tepenian founding —
including, notably, through the war itself. She describes her own role in almost bureaucratic terms, the
counting itself mattering more than any individual entry, but acknowledges what the unbroken record actually
represents to residents who visit the Hall specifically to see it: proof, in the driest possible
administrative form, that the city's survival wasn't a matter of luck or outside intervention but of its own
scale simply outlasting whatever tried to end it. She's occasionally asked whether the count has ever come
close to breaking — her answer, consistently, is that the Hall's own records don't distinguish between days
that were easy to log and days that very nearly weren't, which she considers the entire point of keeping an
unbroken count at all.

## Meltwater Season

The Schirmacher Oasis's genuine summer meltwater lakes (attribute #6) are a distinctive resource and visual
feature unique to Lazar within the Halley subnet — ice-free terrain, exposed bedrock, and seasonal liquid
water existing together in a way no other Halley subnet city's own terrain allows.

*(An informational sheet on the seasonal pattern itself, not a single summer.)* The lakes form reliably each
warm season and recede predictably as temperatures drop again, a cycle residents across both the Old Core and
the Overflow District have long since built informal seasonal habits around — foundation and structural
engineering on the exposed bedrock (attribute #5) accounts for the meltwater directly, meaning the lakes'
seasonal presence doesn't threaten infrastructure the way unmanaged water might elsewhere. What the lakes
mostly represent, for a megacity population otherwise surrounded by permanent ice and snow across the rest of
the subnet, is simply visible seasonal change of a kind most other Halley subnet residents never get to see
directly — open water, however briefly, in a part of the world defined almost everywhere else by how
thoroughly it stays frozen year-round.

## What the Junction Markets Actually Inherited

The Hwy 7 / Belgrano Extension Junction Markets (attribute #7) are, per Finding E, the direct commercial and
physical expression of an already-resolved institutional question — Novolazarevskaya's real DROMLAN aviation-
hub legacy surviving as function rather than as form, translated into highway logistics since Lazar isn't
among Tepenia's own fixed eight airports.

*(An informational sheet on the Markets' own commercial character, not a single transaction.)* The Junction
Markets occupy the actual highway convergence point that inherited the old aviation hub's regional logistics
role — massive commercial density, holographic advertising, and constant activity (attribute #8) built
directly around freight and trade passing through rather than around any single anchor institution. Traders
who've worked the Markets for years describe the site's own energy as inseparable from its function: this is
where Lazar's own megacity-scale demand meets the physical highway infrastructure carrying goods in and out,
and the visible commercial density is less spectacle than simple consequence of how much genuinely needs to
move through this one convergence point to keep a city this size fed and supplied.

## Neumayer's Nearest Real Market

The Neumayer Trade Exchange (attribute #12) is, per Finding C, the concrete physical facility implementing an
already-proposed relationship — Neumayer relying on Lazar as its own nearest genuine megacity market, a
complementary, low-friction arrangement rather than anything resembling Sanay's own more transactional Trade
Yard protocols.

Neumayer traders making the trip describe the Exchange in practical terms: Lazar's own sheer commercial scale
offers access to goods and services no smaller Halley subnet city, including Neumayer itself, could
plausibly supply from its own local economy alone. The relationship runs comfortably in one direction without
apparent friction, per what's currently established — Neumayer genuinely needs what Lazar's own megacity
economy can provide, and Lazar's own scale means absorbing Neumayer's comparatively modest trade volume
barely registers as a strain. Per Finding C, this same relationship is flagged for confirmation from
Neumayer's own side once that city's material receives its own Findings pass, rather than being treated as
fully settled from Lazar's side alone.
