## The Planner Who Gave the Merger a Single Government

Lazar's own twin-district layout — the older, denser Old Core Institutional Quarter and the newer, sprawling
Overflow District (Physical Infrastructure Attributes #1–2) — coalesced gradually over roughly a century
during the First Interwar Period (`Lazar_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section I), long before anyone thought to
mark the moment it finished physically. The moment it finished *administratively* is more specific.

**Civic Planner "Ingrid Osei-Baranova" *(placeholder, not developer-chosen)*** is credited with formally
overseeing the coalescence — not the physical merger itself, which happened through ordinary incremental
growth nobody deliberately directed, but the specific administrative act of recognizing Novolazarevskaya and
the former Maitri overflow as one governed city rather than two settlements that happened to share a border.
Before her own tenure, per what's established about the era, the two districts maintained separate, if
increasingly redundant, local administrations — a genuine inefficiency once the physical seam between them
had already stopped mattering in practice. Osei-Baranova's own proposal to formally unify the governing
structure met resistance from officials in both districts reluctant to cede their own administrative role,
but was eventually adopted specifically because maintaining two governments over one continuous urban area
had become, by her own argument, a cost nobody could still justify. The single governing structure she
established is the same one Lazar's megacity administration still runs on today — the last piece of the
merger to actually happen, decided by one person's proposal rather than emerging on its own the way the
physical coalescence had.

## The Proposal the Rest of the Subnet Called Excessive

The maglev and elevated transit network (attributes #3–4) is the only such system found anywhere in this
pass — transit infrastructure scaled to Tepenia's single largest population. When it was first proposed, that
scale was exactly what other Halley subnet administrators objected to.

Lazar's own ground-level transit, in the megacity's earlier growth years, had begun failing visibly — commute
times between the Old Core and the expanding Overflow District stretching long enough that the strain was
genuinely affecting the commercial activity Section III of the Full Extrapolation credits as Lazar's core
economic engine. When Lazar's own transit planners proposed a maglev system — infrastructure investment on a
scale no Tepenian city had previously attempted — administrators from smaller Halley subnet cities,
petitioned for input given the subnet-wide resource implications, argued the proposal was disproportionate,
a solution sized for problems no city besides Lazar actually had. Lazar's own delegation countered that no
other city's population came close to generating the sustained demand that would justify it either — the
scale of the problem and the scale of the solution were the same argument, not two separate ones. The
proposal was approved specifically on that logic: a system this large was only ever going to make sense in a
city this large, and objections calibrated to ordinary transit needs simply didn't apply to a population
Lazar's size.

## The Hall That Never Stopped Counting

The Unbroken Operations Hall (attribute #11), per Finding B, is the physical, walkable monument to Lazar's
own self-generated survival story — the already-established Lazarus name-echo that inverts the Biblical
story, since Lazar survived through its own accumulated scale, not through any external rescue.

**"Ilana Marchetti" *(placeholder, not developer-chosen)*** has worked the Hall's own continuous-operations
record for longer than most of her colleagues have been alive, maintaining the unbroken count marking every
day the city has operated without a full administrative interruption since its actual Tepenian founding —
including, notably, through the war itself. She describes her own role in almost bureaucratic terms, the
counting itself mattering more than any individual entry, but acknowledges what the unbroken record actually
represents to residents who visit the Hall specifically to see it: proof, in the driest possible
administrative form, that the city's survival wasn't a matter of luck or outside intervention but of its own
scale simply outlasting whatever tried to end it. She's occasionally asked whether the count has ever come
close to breaking — her answer, consistently, is that the Hall's own records don't distinguish between days
that were easy to log and days that very nearly weren't, which she considers the entire point of keeping an
unbroken count at all.

## The Summer the Lakes Ran High

The Schirmacher Oasis's genuine summer meltwater lakes (attribute #6) are a distinctive resource and visual
feature unique to Lazar within the Halley subnet — ice-free terrain, exposed bedrock, and seasonal liquid
water existing together in a way no other Halley subnet city's own terrain allows. Foundation and structural
engineering on the exposed bedrock (attribute #5) accounts for the meltwater directly, and that engineering
was tested by one specific unusually warm season.

An anomalously warm summer produced meltwater volumes measurably beyond what any prior season on record had
reached, pushing several lakes past their own typical seasonal boundaries and bringing standing water closer
to Overflow District foundations than the engineering had ever needed to account for before. Structural
engineers spent the season conducting emergency assessments rather than routine monitoring, and while no
foundation actually failed, the close margin prompted a formal revision to the bedrock engineering
tolerances afterward — accounting for meltwater volumes beyond anything the original founding-era design
had planned around. Residents who lived through that specific summer still reference it when discussing
whether the Oasis's own meltwater pattern is genuinely as reliable and predictable as it's usually described —
proof, for at least one season, that "predictable" had a real upper limit nobody had previously needed to
find.

## The Freight Coordinators Who Moved to the Highway

The Hwy 7 / Belgrano Extension Junction Markets (attribute #7) are, per Finding E, the direct commercial and
physical expression of Novolazarevskaya's real DROMLAN aviation-hub legacy surviving as function rather than
form — since Lazar isn't among Tepenia's own fixed eight airports, the institutional habit of coordinating
large-scale regional logistics had to relocate to a different physical mechanism entirely.

The specific transition, per what's established about the era, wasn't automatic — Novolazarevskaya's own
founding-era logistics coordinators, trained in aviation-hub scheduling and freight coordination, had no
airfield left to run once Tepenia's fixed eight-airport network was established without Lazar among them.
Rather than the institutional knowledge simply dissolving, the same coordinators redirected their own
scheduling and freight-routing expertise toward the highway convergence forming at Hwy 7's meeting point with
the Belgrano Highway Extension — the same coordination skills, aviation logistics translated directly into
ground-transit logistics, applied to a genuinely different physical infrastructure. The Junction Markets grew
up around that redirected coordination rather than the other way around — commercial density following the
logistics expertise that had relocated there, not the reverse.

## The Order Neumayer Couldn't Fill Itself

The Neumayer Trade Exchange (attribute #12) is, per Finding C, the concrete physical facility implementing
Neumayer's reliance on Lazar as its own nearest genuine megacity market — a relationship that traces to a
specific early gap Neumayer's own small, specialized economy couldn't close on its own.

Neumayer's own precision-engineering institutions, in an early stretch of the relationship, required
components and materials at a volume and variety their own small population's local economy had no way to
supply — orders that, per what's preserved of the Exchange's own founding records, initially went unfilled
for long enough to become a genuine operational concern for Neumayer's own Instrument-Craft Workshops.
Neumayer's own trade representatives approached Lazar specifically because its established megacity commercial
density was the nearest source large enough to reliably fill orders of that scale, and the arrangement that
followed — regular, low-friction supply runs absorbing easily into Lazar's own enormous existing trade
volume — became the Exchange's own standing function. What began as Neumayer solving a specific supply
shortfall became, over time, simply the accepted shape of the relationship: Neumayer's own precision economy
depends on access Lazar's own scale can extend without ever particularly noticing the demand.
