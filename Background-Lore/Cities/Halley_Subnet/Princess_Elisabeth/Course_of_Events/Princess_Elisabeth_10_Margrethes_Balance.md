## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Chief Engineer Margrethe Ollivier-Tanaka, her own surname blending Belgian and Japanese heritage like the
city's demographic split, inherits responsibility for keeping the zero-emissions systems balanced across a
whole generation. She has become, by reputation and by fact, the single most essential person to the city's
survival.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The job demands constant, exacting attention with genuinely no room for error — the kind of pressure most
engineers eventually burn out under, and Margrethe is not immune to it. A colleague within the engineering
corps, watching her carry that weight alone year after year, starts pressing her to distribute the
knowledge and the workload before the pressure breaks her or, worse, before some critical piece of what she
alone knows is lost. Margrethe hesitates. Distributing the work means distributing the authority and
recognition that have come to define her professional standing — the city, without quite meaning to, has
built her into a kind of institution unto herself, and she is not sure who she is professionally without
that.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between Margrethe herself, weighing
whether to remain the singular indispensable expert — professionally prestigious, personally exhausting, and
quietly precarious for the whole city if anything ever happens to her — and a colleague or protégé within
the engineering corps pushing her to formalize and distribute the role, applying the city's own founding
"no fallback" logic to human expertise itself: however skilled one person is, the city cannot safely depend
on any single irreplaceable individual. This is best played as an internal-professional conflict rather than
adversarial — B is not attacking Margrethe's competence, but arguing that her competence is exactly the
problem if it exists nowhere else.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is Margrethe herself, whose stake is
both practical (exhaustion, real risk of burnout) and personal (professional identity built around being
irreplaceable). Character B is plausibly a peer or a promising junior engineer within her own corps — someone
who respects her work enough to want it protected structurally, rather than resting entirely on her
continued ability to personally carry it.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Margrethe designs and formalizes a staggered, overlapping shift system specifically so no single team — or
person — ever has to carry the full pressure alone for long. It works exactly as intended. But formalizing
the system also does what B predicted and what Margrethe quietly feared: it turns her own outsized personal
contribution into ordinary institutional structure rather than personal legend, and her own name starts to
recede from how the city talks about the system she built.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Her overlapping-shift model doesn't stay confined to her own department — it becomes the structural template
later adapted for other essential, high-stakes roles across the city, including, eventually, the underground
reserve's maintenance crew (see Suggestion #7), spreading her influence into infrastructure she personally
never touched.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because her model becomes a general administrative template rather than staying tied to her name, decades
later, when city administrators explain why essential work is always structured this way, they cite
institutional custom and established policy — not Margrethe specifically. Her formalization succeeds so
completely, and spreads so far beyond her own department, that even the specific memory of why the practice
started fades, leaving only the practice itself as unquestioned civic habit.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Overlapping-shift culture is now baked into how Princess Elisabeth organizes any essential, high-stakes
labor city-wide. Margrethe's own name survives as little more than a footnote, known mainly within the
engineering corps itself, even inside the very system she personally built — the "leave no mark" ethic
(Suggestion #1), quietly, applied to how the city remembers the engineers who kept it alive.
