## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Princess Elisabeth's zero-emissions systems prove so effective that word of them spreads to other Halley
subnet cities weighing their own infrastructure investments. The city's engineering, built purely out of
local necessity, starts to acquire an outside reputation it never set out to earn.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The design was built specifically for this site's unique total wind and solar exposure — nothing that
simply transplants elsewhere without real adaptation. But the interest doesn't fade once that caveat is
explained; if anything, other cities want exactly the kind of hands-on adaptation work only Princess
Elisabeth's own engineers can actually provide. So the city's engineers find themselves consulting on other
cities' projects for the first time, translating a design built purely out of necessity into something
outsiders can actually learn from and adjust to their own terrain. It is a genuine point of civic pride —
until the consulting work forces an uncomfortable realization: teaching the design to outsiders means
admitting large parts of it were never formally documented at all, just built, adjusted, and maintained by
feel, passed down informally from one engineer to the next.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between an outside city's delegation
or engineer, eager to move quickly and walk away with usable, transferable blueprints, and one of Princess
Elisabeth's own veteran engineers, reluctant to formalize decades of tacit, improvised knowledge into
something that looks like a tidy, principled design document. This should not read as helpful expert vs.
difficult gatekeeper. A's urgency is legitimate — their own city has real infrastructure needs waiting on
this consultation. B's reluctance is legitimate too: much of what actually keeps Princess Elisabeth's
systems running was never a single clean decision, but years of trial, error, and adjustment, and B is
genuinely uneasy about presenting improvisation as if it were doctrine — both out of professional honesty
and a quiet worry that a rushed, oversimplified export could fail somewhere else and reflect badly on
everyone involved.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a visiting engineer or
official from another Halley subnet city under real pressure to deliver results quickly. Character B is
plausibly one of Princess Elisabeth's own senior engineers — someone who has spent a career maintaining the
system by feel and experience rather than by written procedure, for whom being asked to write it all down
is both a professional challenge and, quietly, the first time anyone has asked them to take their own
undocumented expertise seriously as something worth preserving.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The consulting requests, and the friction they create, force the issue: Princess Elisabeth's engineering
corps undertakes the first systematic documentation of its own founding achievement, turning generations of
tacit, improvised knowledge into an actual written record — ironically, only once someone outside the city
finally asked for it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The new documentation doesn't just sit in an archive — it becomes the basis for a formal training
curriculum. Princess Elisabeth's engineering corps begins deliberately training a new generation of
specialists against a real, written standard for the first time, rather than relying purely on informal
mentorship and years of accumulated feel.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That formal training tradition — born specifically from having to document the undocumented for outsiders —
becomes the institutional root the city later draws on whenever it faces a similar problem elsewhere in its
own infrastructure: a precedent for treating specialized, concentrated knowledge as something that must be
deliberately taught and inherited, rather than something the city can simply assume will always be there.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Princess Elisabeth is now known subnet-wide as a design-exporter, its engineers regularly consulted by other
Halley cities. Documentation and formal teaching have become part of the city's civic identity for the first
time — no longer a city that only knows how to build and maintain, but one that has also learned, somewhat
reluctantly, how to explain itself.
