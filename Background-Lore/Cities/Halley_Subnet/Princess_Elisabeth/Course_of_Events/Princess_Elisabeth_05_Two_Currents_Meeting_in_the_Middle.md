## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Princess Elisabeth's unusually eastern position draws both the subnet's usual Atlantic-side American
current and a genuinely distinct Japanese current no other Halley city absorbs in comparable strength. Two
roughly-equal Primary populations live here side by side, neither one the city's obvious default culture.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Two roughly-equal Primary populations means neither one gets to simply define the city the way a single
dominant nation would elsewhere — every shared decision, every civic norm, has to actually be negotiated
rather than assumed. Daily civic life settles into constant, low-key negotiation between two cultural
currents that never fully merge and never fully separate either. That negotiation stays practical and
demographic; it never develops into anything spiritual or ceremonial the way Dome Fuji's own crossroads
status did. But as the city grows, some residents start pushing for the arrangement to be made more formal
and durable — a real institution, not just an accumulated habit that could in principle erode.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between an organizer from one of the
two currents who wants the informal balance formalized — a joint civic council, an explicit language or
representation policy, something durable that survives any one generation's goodwill — and a counterpart
from the other current who resists formalizing anything, not out of complacency but out of a specific fear:
that whichever community organizes fastest and most effectively would end up with genuine leverage the
moment the balance became a matter of official structure rather than lived practice. Both should be
defending the same underlying value — real parity between the two currents — while disagreeing sharply
about whether formal structure protects that parity or quietly threatens it.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a community organizer or
civic figure from the American current, drawn to formal institutions as the natural way to make an
arrangement permanent. Character B is plausibly a counterpart from the Japanese current, wary of exactly
the kind of formal power-seeking that official structures tend to invite, preferring the informal
negotiation precisely because it currently can't be captured by whoever moves first.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

B's position prevails, not through victory in any formal vote but through simple accumulated caution — no
joint council or formal representation policy is ever established. The two currents' negotiation stays
exactly what it has always been: constant, low-key, informal, and practical. Princess Elisabeth becomes a
genuine crossroads that never once thought of itself as sacred, and, just as deliberately, never once
thought of itself as formally governed either — just lived-in.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Without any formal shared institution to anchor decisions, city administration instead develops an
unwritten but remarkably consistent practical habit: significant civic decisions are, by informal custom,
never made by a single administrator alone, but always drawn from both currents in practice, even though no
law or charter ever requires it.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That unwritten dual-input norm becomes so deeply ingrained that it structurally has no place for a third
voice at all — and Belgium, the city's actual founding-operator nation, already diluted to Notable tier by
both larger currents, finds its remaining civic influence quietly locked out of exactly the kind of paired
decision-making that has become the city's default. It is the paired norm itself, not the original
demographic dilution, that finishes marginalizing the founders' own voice in civic life.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Princess Elisabeth's civic life now runs on a consistent, unspoken two-current pairing norm — informal,
flexible, and genuinely effective at keeping American and Japanese influence in real balance. But the norm
has an unintended cost: Belgian-heritage civic voice, never formally excluded by anyone's decision, has been
quietly sidelined by the very system built to keep the peace between the city's two larger currents.
