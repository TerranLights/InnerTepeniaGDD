## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Princess Elisabeth sits closer to Dome Fuji than any other Halley subnet city, right along the route
pilgrims traveling that distance would need to cross. Passing pilgrim traffic is a fact of geography here,
nothing more — the city itself has no religious tradition of its own tied to Dome Fuji or Kunlun's sacred
status.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Geography alone keeps pushing pilgrims through the city regardless of what Princess Elisabeth itself
believes, and the city settles into a modest, informal role anyway — the last practical stop before the
long stretch toward the mountain, useful without ever asking to be meaningful. But usefulness like that
does not stay neutral forever. Repeated contact, year after year, starts to create real, if quiet,
connections between residents and passing pilgrims — shared meals, traded supplies, the kind of familiarity
that builds without anyone deciding it should. Some residents start to wonder whether the city's purely
practical role should become something more deliberate.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a pilgrim-facing resident —
someone who has spent years hosting, guiding, or supplying pilgrims informally and wants to formalize and
expand that role into real infrastructure (proper lodging, marked routes, maybe some petitioned connection
to Dome Fuji's own pilgrimage tradition) — and a civic figure wary of that expansion, worried it would
divert the city's scarce attention and resources away from its actual founding purpose, or that importing
even a modest borrowed religious meaning would blur an identity Princess Elisabeth built for itself out of
demographic and engineering fact, not imported belief. Both sides should be sympathetic: A is responding to
something genuinely felt and worth taking seriously; B is protecting an identity the city earned honestly
and doesn't want diluted by something it never actually holds as its own.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a longtime innkeeper,
guide, or supplier who has built real relationships with pilgrims over years of informal hospitality.
Character B is plausibly a civic administrator or one of the engineering-tradition figures central to the
city's founding identity (see Suggestion #1 and #6), for whom the "leave no mark" ethic extends naturally
into not claiming meaning the city hasn't actually earned.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side gets exactly what they asked for. The city does not build formal pilgrimage infrastructure or
seek any ceremonial status of its own — B's caution holds on that front. But it also does not pull back from
the relationships A has spent years building; instead, the informal hospitality is quietly, permanently
absorbed into ordinary civic life, unofficial but consistent generation after generation. Princess Elisabeth
becomes a genuine, permanent fixture of Dome Fuji's own pilgrim routes — a waypoint that never once needed
to be sacred itself to matter to the people passing through it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The steady, informal pilgrim traffic settles into a modest but real recurring exchange economy — guiding,
temporary lodging, supplies sold or bartered specifically to travelers passing through — a small income and
contact stream distinct from, and separate from, the city's core zero-emissions engineering economy.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That pilgrim-facing economic niche becomes, over generations, one of the few channels through which cultural
currents beyond Princess Elisabeth's own American and Japanese populations reach the city at all — travelers
connected to Dome Fuji's own pilgrimage networks passing through regularly enough to leave a thin, occasional
third thread in an otherwise two-current city, without that thread ever becoming a settled population or a
third demographic current in its own right.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Princess Elisabeth's waypoint role is now fully settled and economically real — a permanent, unofficial
fixture of Dome Fuji's pilgrim routes, still never sacred, still never formalized into pilgrimage
infrastructure or ritual of its own. What it has instead is a modest, ongoing point of outside cultural
contact that neither of this cycle's two positions fully anticipated: not a shrine, not indifference, but a
quiet, permanent habit of hospitality that has become part of how the city sees itself.
