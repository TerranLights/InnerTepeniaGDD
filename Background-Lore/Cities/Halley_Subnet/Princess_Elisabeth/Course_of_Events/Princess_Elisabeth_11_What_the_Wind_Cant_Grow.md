## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Princess Elisabeth's engineering consultation reputation has grown steadily (per Suggestion #3), giving
the city real leverage and standing it never had at founding. Quietly, unremarked upon, the city has also
always depended on imported food and raw materials — the same wind-scoured nunatak that makes its energy
systems so effective grows nothing and yields nothing extractable at all.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

City leadership moves to formalize the growing consulting relationships into a real, structured trade
agreement — energy expertise offered in direct, explicit exchange for guaranteed food and material
shipments, rather than the informal, undiscussed purchasing arrangements the city has always quietly
relied on. Putting the exchange in writing means naming the dependency plainly, for the first time.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement about honesty versus mythology, not
about the trade's practical terms. Character A wants the formal agreement — clear terms, guaranteed
supply, a real negotiating position built on the city's genuine expertise. Character B is uneasy not
because the trade is a bad idea, but because formalizing it means the city's own "leave no mark"
self-image — total, self-generated independence — has to publicly admit an exception it's always quietly
lived with but never had to say out loud. Neither is wrong: A is being practical; B is protecting
something real about how the city understands itself, even if that self-understanding was never entirely
accurate.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a trade negotiator or
senior administrator focused on securing the city's practical future. Character B is plausibly a figure
more invested in the city's founding mythology specifically — perhaps someone descended from the original
founding generation, or simply a resident who takes the "leave no mark" identity personally rather than
just operationally.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The formal agreement goes through. Admitting the dependency doesn't change how the city actually
survives — it only changes whether the city says so plainly. Character B's discomfort doesn't prevent the
trade; it simply marks the moment the admission became unavoidable.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

With the trade formalized and public, residents start talking about the city's own self-sufficiency
differently — no longer as a single, total claim, but as something more specific: complete independence
in energy, the one thing that could have actually killed the founding generation, alongside open,
unembarrassed dependency in everything else.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That sharper, more honest self-understanding becomes the version residents actually teach newcomers and
visiting consultants — not "we need nothing from anyone," but "we solved the one problem that would have
killed us, and never pretended to solve the rest." The founding mythology doesn't shrink from this
correction; it gets more specific, and more genuinely earned for the precision.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Princess Elisabeth carries forward its hard-won energy expertise and its growing consulting reputation
exactly as before — but now paired with an openly acknowledged trade relationship for food and materials,
and a civic self-image that no longer quietly papers over what it has always actually depended on. Self-
sufficient in what mattered most; honestly dependent in everything else.
