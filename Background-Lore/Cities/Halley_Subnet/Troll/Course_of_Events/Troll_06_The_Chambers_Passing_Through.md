## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Fabrication-synthesis chambers manufactured at Sinheung occasionally move through Troll's broader
freight network on their way to cities beyond Dome Fuji's own dedicated delivery route — one shipment
class among the thousands passing through the airfield every year, indistinguishable on paper from
ordinary cargo.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

For years, nobody at Troll treats this as anything more than ordinary cargo. It takes an outside
observer — someone who actually understands what a fabrication-synthesis chamber means for the
nationwide robot-creation supply chain — to point out what's really been moving through Troll's
freight lines all along. Once that's known, the Airfield Authority (#2) can't simply keep treating
chamber shipments like anything else passing through — not after being shown, directly, that its own
routine handling procedures have been applied for years to freight touching population growth itself.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation over whether, and how fast, the
Authority admits an institutional blind spot and changes procedure because of it. A's alarm is
legitimate — a nationally vital shipment class really has been handled with zero special protocol for
years, a genuine oversight with real stakes. B's reluctance isn't negligence — admitting the gap means
conceding, on the record, that the existing system missed something this significant, and inviting new
costs, new liability, and quite possibly outside scrutiny of Troll's own operations from a city that
has never previously had reason to look closely at how the Authority runs things.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a technical or
production-side figure connected to Sinheung or Dome Fuji — someone whose professional stake is in the
robot-creation supply chain specifically, alarmed on the chambers' behalf rather than Troll's. Character
B is plausibly an Airfield Authority administrator (a direct successor to the roles established in #2)
who has to weigh genuine institutional embarrassment and new operational cost against the risk of
visibly under-reacting to something this significant once it's been named.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Troll formalizes special handling protocols for chamber freight specifically — a small procedural
change that quietly raises the Authority's own importance, now formally responsible for something
touching population growth itself, not just ordinary trade goods.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because chamber freight now requires distinct protocols, the Authority has to establish a genuine,
ongoing communication channel with Sinheung's production side directly, rather than continuing to
treat it as an anonymous point of origin somewhere upstream in the freight network — the Authority
becomes the first Troll civic body with a standing institutional relationship to another subnet's
manufacturing authority.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Having a standing channel to Sinheung's production side means the Authority starts receiving informal
advance notice of shipment schedules and production changes before they ever arrive at Troll — a
genuinely privileged, early information advantage over ordinary commercial customers, who get no
equivalent notice for anything else moving through the field. Self-check: without the direct
relationship from the 2nd-order change, there is no channel for this advance notice to travel through
— the advantage depends on that relationship existing first.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Troll's Airfield Authority runs formal special-handling protocols for chamber freight, backed by a
direct institutional relationship with Sinheung's production side — quietly making the Authority not
just Troll's own commercial-dispute arbitrator, but a minor, privileged node in the Federation's
nationwide robot-creation supply chain, with standing and information no other freight customer at
Troll enjoys.
