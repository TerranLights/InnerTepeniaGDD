## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Public civic life at Troll increasingly organizes around the airfield's rising economic importance,
leaving less and less room for the older Norwegian-heritage observances in anything the city presents
outwardly.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A smaller group of older residents never actually stops keeping St. Roald's veneration alive — just
more privately than before, in individual households and small gatherings rather than anything public.
A visitor expecting Troll's public identity to be its whole identity is surprised to find this quieter,
older tradition still very much alive underneath the airfield-forward surface. But that private
persistence never translates back into public prominence — it survives, but it doesn't reassert itself.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a smaller, more personal confrontation than the
formal civic-calendar fight in Suggestion #3 — this one is about whether Troll's outward self-
presentation gets to simply omit a living tradition because it's no longer publicly prominent. A isn't
malicious in leaving the older practice out of how the city presents itself; from where they stand, it
genuinely looks extinct or purely historical. B's confrontation isn't really about winning back public
prominence — it's about refusing to be erased from an account of the city that claims completeness
while missing something real that's still actually practiced.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a younger civic
promoter or someone preparing outward-facing material about Troll for visitors or trade delegations —
sincere, not dismissive, simply unaware. Character B is plausibly one of the older residents who has
kept St. Roald veneration alive privately, confronting A directly, in person, once the omission becomes
visible — not to demand a policy change, but to be seen and counted accurately.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The private tradition gets genuinely acknowledged — the visitor or promoter figure comes away
correcting their own account of the city — but it doesn't translate into public prominence. Troll ends
up carrying two genuinely real identities at once, one loud and public, one quiet and private, neither
one fully replacing the other.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the private tradition gets acknowledged rather than dismissed, a small number of younger
residents — not previously involved, with no family connection to the founding-era households — start
quietly seeking out the older residents specifically to learn the practice, out of genuine interest
sparked by the incident rather than the tradition simply continuing to age out alongside its current
keepers.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

This creates, for the first time, real intergenerational transmission of St. Roald veneration outside
family lines — no longer something only descendants of the founding exile families quietly keep among
themselves, but something a handful of newcomers deliberately choose to adopt. Self-check: without the
younger residents actively seeking it out from the 2nd-order change, the tradition would simply age out
with its current keepers as before — the transmission depends directly on that change, not on the
original acknowledgment alone.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Troll's civic identity remains publicly airfield-first. But St. Roald veneration survives as a
genuinely living private practice, no longer confined to the bloodlines of the founding families and no
longer simply running out the clock on its own extinction — quietly renewed rather than merely
persisting on borrowed time.
