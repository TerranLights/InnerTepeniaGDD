## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

The newly-chartered Airfield Authority (#2) needs someone to run day-to-day truck dispatch between the
fuel depot, the runway, and the city's own trucking companies — an operational role nobody has held
before, because the coordination it requires never had to be anyone's specific job until now.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Every shipping company operating at Troll wants priority scheduling, and the Authority's own charter
doesn't specify how disputes between equally legitimate commercial claims should be resolved. The
dispatch coordinator ends up making those calls herself, shipment by shipment, with no fixed formula
to fall back on. That kind of discretionary power invites real, sustained pressure from every
direction — each company wanting to be her priority, each with a plausible case for why.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a sustained test of whether one person's
unwritten, case-by-case judgment can hold up against a single, persistent commercial actor determined
to convert pressure into guaranteed priority. A's position is that consistent, evenhanded judgment —
even without a written formula — is the only fair way to run dispatch when every claim is legitimate on
its own terms. B's position is not unreasonable either: in the absence of any written rule, why
shouldn't the company willing to push hardest, escalate furthest, or offer the most get priority
treatment? The charter's silence makes both readings defensible.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is the Authority's own newly-
hired dispatch coordinator — the role that, per the source material, comes to be filled by (and
identified with) Freya Nakamura-Olsen — someone new enough to the position that her authority isn't
yet settled or assumed, having to earn a reputation for fairness under real-time pressure rather than
inheriting one. Character B is plausibly a liaison for the largest or most demanding shipping company
at Troll, standing in dramatized form for the broader pressure "every direction" represents — someone
whose job is specifically to press for their employer's advantage, repeatedly, by whatever legitimate
means available.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The coordinator holds her ground — not by refusing every request, but by applying visibly consistent
judgment that no single company, however persistent, can reliably bend in its own favor. She becomes,
without ever seeking the role, one of the few people in Troll whose daily judgment calls actually shape
how the city's whole freight economy runs.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because her authority proves workable purely on the strength of personal judgment, the Authority
starts extending the same kind of unwritten discretionary latitude to the other operational roles it
hires afterward — fuel management, storage allocation, broader scheduling — rather than writing fixed
formulas for each. Her example becomes the Authority's default staffing philosophy, not a one-off
exception born of a single unclear charter clause.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because discretionary judgment becomes the Authority's general operating norm rather than an isolated
accommodation, later attempts to write down formal procedures — including the special chamber-freight
handling protocols proposed in Suggestion #6 — run into real friction, since so much of what the
Authority actually does was never written down as rule in the first place, only practiced. Self-check:
without discretionary judgment having become the Authority's default philosophy in the 2nd-order
change, a later formalization effort would have far less entrenched practice to reckon with — the
friction depends on that default having taken hold first.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Troll's Airfield Authority runs, in practice, less on its own written charter than on the accumulated
personal judgment of the specific people who came to hold its key operational roles — Freya foremost
among them. A system that works reliably for as long as the right individuals hold those roles, and
creates real friction whenever anyone later tries to write down rules for what has always run on
trusted discretion instead.
