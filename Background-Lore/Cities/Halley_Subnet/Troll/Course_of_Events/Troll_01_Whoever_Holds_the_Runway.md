## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Troll's founding identity runs on Norwegian Polar Institute heritage and the 1939 Queen Maud Land
claim — a real, if quiet, civic story the 2564 exile families chose to learn (from preserved journals, logs,
and records) and carry forward as their
own, after inheriting the genuinely functional station and airfield built alongside it, kept maintained
through the First Interwar Period by a rotating succession of national operators *(refined 2026-07-25,
GPS-purposes-only pass — a chosen historical identity, learned from documentary records, not a living
institution handed down; see `Specs/Troll.md`)*. For its first generations, this is simply what Troll *is*: a city
that remembers where it came from.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The airfield inherited alongside that heritage turns out to matter more to the rest of Tepenia than
the heritage itself ever did. As Troll's role in the Halley subnet's freight economy grows — goods
landing at Sanay and Belgrano, trucked overland via Hwy 7, flown out across the whole country from
Troll's own runway — the airfield becomes the city's real center of gravity, eclipsing the founding
story generation by generation, not through any single decision but through the sheer accumulating
weight of what the runway does for the rest of the country compared to what the old story means to
anyone outside Troll itself. But eclipsing isn't the same as erasing — the older identity doesn't
vanish, it just stops being what the rest of Tepenia sees first when it looks at Troll.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation over how Troll represents
*itself* to the outside world — its official correspondence, its welcome materials for visiting
traders and delegations, the story it leads with. This isn't a fight over whether the heritage still
matters privately (that's a separate, later question — see #8); it's specifically about which story
gets to be the city's public face going forward. Avoid framing either side as simply wrong: A's
position is that continuing to lead with a founding story most of the country no longer connects to
Troll actively costs the city recognition and leverage it has functionally already earned; B's
position is that discarding the founding story as mere marketing strategy treats something residents
still genuinely value as disposable the moment it stops being useful.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a logistics or
civic-relations figure whose daily work is representing Troll to outside trading partners and other
cities — someone who experiences, firsthand and repeatedly, the gap between how the rest of Tepenia
actually values Troll (as a chokepoint) and how Troll still describes itself. Character B is
plausibly a keeper of the founding-era record — an archivist, a descendant of the original exile
families, someone whose civic role or personal identity is bound up in the Polar Institute story
specifically, for whom "the airfield matters more now" reads as a demotion of something that was
never merely instrumental to begin with.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Troll's outward-facing identity shifts decisively to the airfield: official correspondence, trade
signage, and how the city introduces itself to visitors all foreground "Whoever Holds the Runway"
over the founding story. But the heritage keeper doesn't lose everything — in exchange for ceding the
public-facing argument, they secure a lasting institutional home for the older story: a protected
archive, formally recognized rather than left to survive on individual memory alone.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Troll's civic administration splits what used to be one undivided job into two distinct functions: an
external-relations office built around the airfield-forward identity, handling trade correspondence
and outside visitors, and a separate cultural-stewardship office responsible for the archive and the
older story — a formal division of civic labor the city never needed while one office could plausibly
speak for both identities at once.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because cultural stewardship now exists as its own discrete, budgeted office rather than something
individuals maintain on their own initiative, it becomes chronically the smaller and poorer-funded of
the two — the airfield-forward office generates the revenue that justifies its own budget, while the
archive office has no equivalent revenue case to make. But its permanence is no longer contingent on
any one person's personal advocacy the way it once was; a formal office, however thin its funding,
persists in a way an individual keeper's private effort never reliably would.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Troll now presents itself to the rest of Tepenia almost entirely through the airfield — the story the
country actually recognizes it by. The older Norwegian heritage survives underneath, institutionally
secure but thinly funded, its own dedicated office now permanently established even if permanently
overshadowed — no longer dependent on any one family's memory to outlast the generation that holds it.
