## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Halley depends entirely on overland resupply routed through Sanay and Belgrano, both of which depend
in turn on Troll's own freight network reaching them reliably — a chain of dependency that has simply
always worked, and so has never had to be examined closely by anyone at either end.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

During one of Troll's own rapid growth years, a genuine capacity crunch — too much freight, not enough
scheduled flights — creates real delays reaching the subnet's more remote destinations. A shipment
bound for Halley via Sanay sits queued at Troll for weeks longer than expected, a delay Halley has no
way to see coming and no way to influence. Halley has no seat in Troll's own scheduling decisions
despite depending on them completely — the dependency chain, examined for the first time under real
strain, turns out to run entirely one way.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation over whether downstream, wholly
dependent cities get any formal say in decisions made entirely at Troll. A's grievance is concrete and
immediate — a real shipment sat queued for weeks with real consequences at the far end of the chain,
and no channel existed to even raise the problem in advance. B's position isn't callous — Troll's own
scheduling has to serve its own growth and its many commercial priorities first, and conceding
scheduling input to every downstream city it happens to supply would make coordinating the airfield at
all considerably harder. Play this as an unresolved structural tension, not a clean villain-and-victim
exchange.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a Halley-based
logistics liaison or civic representative, dispatched to Troll specifically because of this incident,
carrying Halley's frustration but with no actual leverage to back a formal demand. Character B is
plausibly a Troll-side scheduling figure — an Airfield Authority (#2) dispatcher or administrator
defending the city's operational autonomy, who may be sympathetic to Halley's situation personally
while still having no institutional mandate to change how scheduling actually works.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The Authority acknowledges the incident but does not concede formal scheduling input to Halley — the
delay stands as documented, undeniable proof that Troll's ordinary operational choices already reach
well outside the city itself, well before any larger crisis ever forces the point directly.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the incident becomes known, documented proof rather than a grievance that gets resolved and
closed, Sanay and Belgrano — the two intermediate cities also affected by the same delay — start
keeping their own informal records of Troll-caused disruptions for the first time, building a
paper trail rather than simply absorbing each disruption as it comes and moving on.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Having a documented pattern gives Sanay, Belgrano, and by extension Halley something concrete to point
to the next time a Troll capacity crunch threatens their supply — turning what had been a single,
unresolved grievance into an ongoing, evidence-backed relationship of scrutiny toward Troll's Airfield
Authority, even though no formal change to who controls scheduling has actually happened. Self-check:
without the record-keeping habit from the 2nd-order change, there would be no accumulated pattern to
point to — later leverage depends on that habit existing first, not merely on the original incident.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Troll retains full internal control over its own freight scheduling. But Sanay, Belgrano, and Halley
now watch its operational choices far more closely than before, armed with their own growing,
documented record of what it costs them whenever Troll's growth outpaces its own capacity — a standing,
unresolved tension between Troll's scheduling autonomy and its downstream dependents' vulnerability.
