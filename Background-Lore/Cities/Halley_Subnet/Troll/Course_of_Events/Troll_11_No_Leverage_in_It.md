## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Troll's freight network has grown into a genuine web of leverage-based relationships — scheduling
priority disputes, fee structures, the Airfield Authority's own contested control over who gets what.
Every route the city runs connects, in some way, to strategic or economic advantage. A dedicated supply
route to Dome Fuji exists within that network, but has never been examined closely against the logic
governing everything else.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A routine review of the Airfield Authority's own route portfolio — the kind of ordinary administrative
audit any growing freight operation eventually runs — turns up the Dome Fuji route as a genuine anomaly.
Dome Fuji has nearly no Arcanet presence, no strategic leverage to offer, and no way to apply pressure if
the route were ever quietly deprioritized or cut. By every metric the Authority uses to evaluate its other
routes, this one shouldn't exist in its current, fully-resourced form.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** an internal administrative disagreement rather than
an external threat — someone applying the Authority's own stated logic consistently, arguing the route
should be reassessed or reduced like any other underperforming asset; someone else insisting the route
stays exactly as it is, precisely because Dome Fuji's isolation makes it genuinely dependent in a way no
leverage calculation should be allowed to touch. Character A isn't wrong on the Authority's own terms —
the route really doesn't fit the model. Character B isn't arguing from sentiment alone, either; they're
naming something true about what the city actually does versus what it says it does.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a newer Authority
administrator or auditor, applying the organization's own stated principles rigorously and in good faith.
Character B is plausibly a more senior figure — someone who remembers when the route was established, or
simply someone with enough standing to make the case stick without needing to justify it in leverage
terms at all.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The route survives the review untouched. No formal policy gets written to explain why; it simply
continues, exactly as it always has, outside the leverage-based logic the Authority applies to everything
else it manages.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the route survived a direct, deliberate audit rather than simply being overlooked, its exceptional
status becomes something the Authority's own senior staff now know about explicitly, rather than an
unexamined default. The exception is no longer accidental — it's been tested, and chosen, on purpose.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Word of the review passes quietly through Troll's own administrative culture — not as a scandal or a
policy shift, but as a small, specific data point about the city's own character. Residents who work
closely with the Authority start treating the Dome Fuji route as a known, if unspoken, exception to how
Troll otherwise does business — proof, when anyone bothers to look closely, that the city's own
"competence is leverage" self-description was never the complete picture.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Troll's freight empire continues running on leverage, control, and contested advantage exactly as
before — except for one route, kept alive for no strategic reason at all, that the city's own
administration has now looked at directly, understood clearly, and chosen not to change. A small,
permanent crack in the city's own hardened self-understanding, present indefinitely, never quite
resolved into either full contradiction or full consistency.
