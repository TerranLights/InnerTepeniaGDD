## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

As Troll's freight-hub economy grows, multiple shipping companies and even neighboring cities start
pressing competing claims over scheduling priority, storage rights, and fee structures at the
airfield — each claim reasonable on its own terms, none of them adjudicated by anyone with the
standing to actually settle the question.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

No single existing civic body has the standing to arbitrate between all of them — ordinary city
government was built to run a city, not referee commercial disputes between companies and other
cities that don't answer to it. A dedicated Airfield Authority gets proposed, distinct from ordinary
city government, specifically to manage access and resolve disputes. But granting that Authority real
teeth — actual enforcement power, not just a mediation forum anyone can walk away from — means every
commercial interest operating at Troll would answer to a body none of them fully controls, and neither
city government nor the shipping companies currently competing for priority are eager to be the ones
who concede that ground first.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a chartering fight over how much real power the
new Authority should actually have. This should not read as government-good, business-bad — A's
position (a strong, independent Authority) is motivated by the practical need for an arbitrator whose
rulings actually stick, since a toothless mediation board solves nothing; B's position (a narrowly
scoped Authority) is motivated by a reasonable fear that an independent body with real enforcement
power becomes, in effect, a second government commercial operators have no say in shaping or
replacing.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a city government
figure — a council member or administrator who has personally had to referee unresolvable priority
disputes with no formal standing to do so, and wants a body built with enough independence and
authority to actually make rulings stick. Character B is plausibly a representative of the freight
companies themselves, or a coalition speaking for several of them — someone whose livelihood depends
on Troll's scheduling, wary of ceding real power to any body they don't sit inside and can't outvote.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The Authority gets chartered with real teeth: genuine enforcement power over scheduling, storage
rights, and fee disputes, formally distinct from ordinary city government. The commercial side loses
full autonomy over its own priority claims, but gains something it didn't have before — a predictable
process that applies to every company the same way, rather than whoever shouts loudest or has the best
relationship with whichever office happens to be listening that week.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the Authority now sits governance-independent from ordinary city government, staffing it
can't simply mean assigning the job to an existing city bureaucrat — it requires recruiting people
with genuine logistics and dispute-arbitration judgment, people whose actual job is running the
Authority and nothing else. A new class of career civic officeholder appears at Troll that didn't
previously exist in the city's structure: the Authority administrator, answerable to neither an
elected council seat nor any single company's payroll.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That new officeholder class, existing outside both electoral politics and any single commercial
payroll, starts to develop its own institutional culture and its own career track inside the
Authority specifically — people begin building working lives there, accumulating loyalty to the
Authority's own continuity and reputation rather than to city government or to any shipping company
that might currently be pressuring them. Self-check: without the new officeholder class existing
first, this loyalty has no institution to attach to — it depends directly on the 2nd-order change,
not merely on the original chartering fight.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Troll's governance now runs on two tiers: elected civic government handling ordinary city affairs, and
a permanent, independently staffed Airfield Authority wielding real enforcement power over anyone with
a stake in the runway — answering, by design, to neither government nor commerce alone, and already
developing its own institutional identity separate from both.
