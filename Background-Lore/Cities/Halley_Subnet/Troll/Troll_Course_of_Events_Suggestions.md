# Troll — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note (revised):** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during
the Second Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no
post-war/present-day material. Troll's own established present-day fate (the fractured, multi-faction
airfield conflict that anchors DLC 5) is out of scope here; these chains instead trace how the
airfield became the economically central, genuinely valuable asset that later made such a conflict
worth having in the first place. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Troll's Megasheet
(Norwegian founding heritage, the intermodal freight economy, the working-class prosperity it
produced). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Whoever Holds the Runway

Troll's founding identity runs on Norwegian Polar Institute heritage and the 1939 Queen Maud Land
claim — a real, if quiet, civic story. **But** the airfield inherited alongside that heritage turns
out to matter more to the rest of Tepenia than the heritage itself ever did. **Therefore** as
Troll's role in the subnet's freight economy grows, the runway itself becomes the city's real center
of gravity, eclipsing the founding story generation by generation. **But** eclipsing isn't the same
as erasing. **Therefore** Troll ends up defined publicly by its airfield's growing economic weight
while its older Norwegian identity survives underneath, diminished but not gone.

## 2. One Authority for Every Claim

As Troll's freight-hub economy grows, multiple shipping companies and even neighboring cities start
pressing competing claims over scheduling priority, storage rights, and fee structures at the
airfield. **But** no single existing civic body has the standing to arbitrate between all of them.
**Therefore** a dedicated Airfield Authority gets formally chartered, distinct from ordinary city
government, specifically to manage access and resolve disputes. **But** granting that Authority real
teeth means every commercial interest operating at Troll now answers to a body none of them fully
controls. **Therefore** Troll ends up governed, in practice, by two overlapping systems — ordinary
civic government, and a separate, increasingly powerful Airfield Authority everyone with a stake in
the runway has to work through.

## 3. A Landing and a Runway

For generations, St. Roald's Landing is Troll's only real civic holiday, honoring Amundsen and the
founding Polar Institute heritage. **But** as the airfield's rising economic importance comes to
define the city's public identity, the older observance starts to feel disconnected from what
residents actually live through day to day. **Therefore** a newer holiday, Runway Day, forms around
the airfield itself — its founding, its growth, what it means to the city now. **But** older
residents refuse to let the Landing simply fade in Runway Day's favor. **Therefore** Troll ends up
keeping both, deliberately, side by side — one of the only Tepenian cities to formally commemorate
both who it used to be and what it's become, rather than letting one identity quietly replace the
other.

## 4. Building the Fleet

Troll's role as a freight hub creates real, growing demand for more cargo capacity than the aircraft
inherited from the original Polar Institute station can handle. **But** a small city inheriting one
research station's infrastructure has neither the budget nor the justification to simply buy a full
modern fleet outright. **Therefore** the city pursues a mixed strategy: leasing capacity from outside
carriers during peak shipping seasons while slowly investing in aircraft of its own as revenue
allows. **But** relying on leased outside capacity means Troll's freight throughput is only ever as
reliable as carriers with no particular loyalty to the city. **Therefore** Troll spends years working
to make its own fleet self-sufficient, precisely so its economic centrality never depends on anyone
else's goodwill.

## 5. What Fuel Can't Wait For

Troll's growing cargo fleet depends entirely on a steady fuel supply trucked in via Hwy 7. **But** a
serious disruption elsewhere in the shipping chain leaves the airfield's fuel reserves running
dangerously low for the first time in the city's history. **Therefore** the city invests in
significantly expanded fuel storage capacity, enough to weather future disruptions without grounding
flights. **But** maintaining that much stored fuel safely requires real, ongoing investment neither
the city nor any single shipping company wants to bear alone. **Therefore** the cost gets split
between city government and the airfield's largest commercial users, tying Troll's own civic budget
directly to the health of its freight economy for the first time.

## 6. The Chambers Passing Through

Fabrication-synthesis chambers manufactured at Sinheung occasionally move through Troll's broader
freight network on their way to cities beyond Dome Fuji's own dedicated delivery route. **But** for
years, nobody at Troll treats this as anything more than ordinary cargo, one shipment among
thousands. **Therefore** it takes an outside observer — someone who actually understands what a
fabrication-synthesis chamber means for the nationwide robot-creation supply chain — to point out
what's really been moving through Troll's freight lines all along. **But** once that's known, the
Airfield Authority (#2) can't simply treat chamber shipments like anything else passing through.
**Therefore** Troll formalizes special handling protocols for chamber freight specifically — a small
procedural change that quietly raises the Authority's own importance, now responsible for something
touching population growth itself, not just ordinary trade goods.

## 7. Downstream to Halley

Halley depends entirely on overland resupply routed through Sanay and Belgrano, both of which depend
in turn on Troll's own freight network reaching them reliably. **But** during one of Troll's own
rapid growth years, a genuine capacity crunch — too much freight, not enough scheduled flights —
creates real delays reaching the subnet's more remote destinations. **Therefore** a shipment bound for
Halley via Sanay sits queued at Troll for weeks longer than expected, a delay Halley has no way to see
coming and no way to influence. **But** Halley has no seat in Troll's own scheduling decisions
despite depending on them completely. **Therefore** the incident becomes hard, direct proof, well
before any larger crisis forces the point, that Troll's ordinary operational choices already have
real teeth reaching well outside the city itself.

## 8. Saint Roald, Quietly

Public civic life at Troll increasingly organizes around the airfield's rising economic importance,
leaving less and less room for the older Norwegian-heritage observances. **But** a smaller group of
older residents never actually stops keeping St. Roald's veneration alive, just more privately than
before. **Therefore** a visitor expecting Troll's public identity to be its whole identity is
surprised to find a quieter, older tradition still very much alive in individual households and small
gatherings. **But** that private persistence never translates back into public prominence.
**Therefore** Troll ends up carrying two genuinely real identities at once, one loud and public, one
quiet and private, neither one fully replacing the other.

## 9. Freya's Ledger

The newly-chartered Airfield Authority needs someone to run day-to-day truck dispatch between the
depot, the runway, and the city's own trucking companies. **But** every shipping company operating at
Troll wants priority scheduling, and the Authority's charter doesn't specify how disputes between
equally legitimate commercial claims should be resolved. **Therefore** dispatch coordinator Freya
Nakamura-Olsen ends up making those calls herself, shipment by shipment, with no fixed formula to
fall back on. **But** that kind of discretionary power invites real pressure from every direction,
each company wanting to be her priority. **Therefore** she becomes, without ever seeking the role, one
of the few people in Troll whose daily judgment calls actually shape how the city's whole freight
economy runs.

## 10. Better Made, Not Bigger

Troll's freight-hub economy generates real, above-average spending power for a working-class city.
**But** that prosperity never turns into ornate or extravagant display — Troll's whole civic
character stays plainly working-class regardless of what residents can afford. **Therefore** the
extra money shows up instead in quality: better-made goods, better-made tools, and a glitch-coolant
culture that stays limited-selection and strong-effect like its poorer neighbors' but is simply
better crafted throughout. **But** visitors used to reading prosperity as visible excess sometimes
miss it entirely. **Therefore** Troll's wealth stays legible mainly to residents themselves — a quiet
difference in craftsmanship rather than anything a stranger would immediately notice.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#2** is the most load-bearing of the ten — the Airfield Authority it establishes becomes the
connective tissue for #6 and #9, giving Troll's economic rise an actual governing structure rather
than leaving "the airfield matters" as an abstract fact. **#4 and #5** work as a natural pair, tracing
how Troll's freight capacity and fuel security both had to be deliberately built up rather than simply
inherited — the two investments that made the city's later economic centrality possible at all.
