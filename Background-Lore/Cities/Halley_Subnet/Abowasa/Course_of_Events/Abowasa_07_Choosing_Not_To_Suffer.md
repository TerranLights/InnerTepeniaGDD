## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

A real resource shortage hits Abowasa — the specific kind of scarcity that, elsewhere in Tepenia,
plausibly splits a community along national lines, each side quietly deciding the other has been taking
more than its fair share. Abowasa faces the same fork every one of those other cities faced.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Abowasa doesn't split. Instead, the two communities solve the shortage jointly, and fast, without
anyone needing to lose outright — but that outcome was never inevitable; it required both sides to
actually cooperate under real pressure, not just coexist comfortably the way they had been. The
resolution becomes local legend almost immediately, the story residents reach for whenever they explain
why Abowasa's own history reads as things working out rather than things being endured. But that same
legend lands very differently outside the city. Neighboring subnet cities carry their own harder-won
survival stories — real hardship, real loss, real scars — and against that backdrop, Abowasa's smooth,
untroubled version of a crisis starts to feel, to outside visitors, faintly suspicious, as if the story
must be smoothed over or self-flattering rather than simply true.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between local pride and outside
skepticism that shouldn't resolve with either side being simply right. A's instinct to tell the story
proudly is legitimate — it really did happen, and minimizing a genuine achievement out of politeness
has its own cost. B's discomfort is just as legitimate — coming from a city that paid in blood or ruin
for its own survival, hearing a neighbor's story of clean, cooperative success can land as tone-deaf
even when nobody involved meant it that way, and B's skepticism isn't cynicism so much as a reasonable
response to real disparity in what "survival" has cost each of them.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an Abowasa resident
or informal storyteller, proud of the shortage-crisis legend and genuinely inclined to tell it as
evidence of what the city is. Character B is plausibly a visitor, transplant, or trade contact from a
harder-hit Halley subnet neighbor — someone whose own city's history gives them every reason to hear
Abowasa's story as either naive or quietly self-congratulatory, whatever A actually intends by it.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The exchange doesn't end in either side conceding the other was right — it ends in A noticing,
uncomfortably, exactly how the story landed on B, and realizing that being technically accurate isn't
the same as being considerate of who's listening. Afterward, a real habit takes hold across the city:
Abowasa quietly learns to undersell its own good fortune around visitors from harder-hit places,
softening or shortening the shortage-crisis legend rather than telling it in full whenever the audience
has clearly paid more for their own survival than Abowasa ever had to.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The self-deprecating habit hardens into something closer to formal civic storytelling practice.
Whatever official or semi-official retellings of Abowasa's own history exist — for visitors, in
schools, in any context where outsiders are present — get told in a deliberately minimized register.
The city's own private self-understanding (real, quiet pride in having chosen cooperation and made it
work) and its external self-presentation (modest almost to the point of self-erasure) start to diverge.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Because the external story is systematically undersold, outsiders — including, eventually, Federation
administrators making decisions well outside Abowasa's own borders — consistently underestimate the
city's actual civic competence and resilience. Abowasa periodically gets passed over for
responsibilities, funding, or trust that its genuine track record would easily justify, purely because
it has built an entire culture around never claiming credit for having earned them.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Abowasa carries a real, proven record of cooperative crisis-resolution that it has trained itself never
to advertise. Internally, the city knows exactly what it's capable of; externally, it reads to the rest
of the Federation as smaller and less capable than it actually is — a quiet, self-inflicted blind spot
in how Tepenia as a whole understands one of its own most functional small cities.
