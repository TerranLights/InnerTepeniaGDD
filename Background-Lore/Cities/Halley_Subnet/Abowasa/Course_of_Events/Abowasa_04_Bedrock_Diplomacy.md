## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Abowasa sits on solid mainland bedrock, a meaningfully different foundation than its nearest Halley
subnet neighbors. Halley itself runs on a deliberately relocatable, ski-mounted design built for a site
with no fixed ground at all; Neumayer manages its own unstable ice-shelf footing through elaborate
hydraulic-leg engineering. Both are proud, functioning, genuinely impressive solutions to a problem
Abowasa has simply never had.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

Neither Halley's nor Neumayer's engineering actually solves their underlying instability — both only
compensate for it, continuously, and both know it. When one of them — Neumayer, whose hydraulic legs
are built to manage exactly this kind of failure and are now failing to fully manage it — hits a
foundation crisis its own engineers can't fully resolve on their own terms, Abowasa's quiet, largely
unadvertised stability research becomes the answer nobody at Neumayer thought to ask for until they
genuinely needed it. But accepting that answer means something uncomfortable: admitting that a small,
inward-facing city a fraction of Neumayer's size has been sitting on knowledge Neumayer's own engineers
don't have.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between institutional pride under
real pressure and cautious outside expertise trying not to overstep. This isn't a simple
humility-versus-arrogance story — A's reluctance is a legitimate institutional instinct (a city
doesn't want its own engineers publicly shown up by a much smaller neighbor, especially mid-crisis, when
authority and public confidence matter most), and B's caution is just as legitimate (offering unsolicited
expertise to a bigger, prouder neighbor risks looking presumptuous, and getting the terms of the
relationship wrong the first time could foreclose it permanently).

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly Neumayer's own chief
structural engineer or a senior administrator, forced by the crisis to consider outside help their own
institutional culture never previously entertained. Character B is plausibly an Abowasa geologist or
stability researcher — someone who has quietly believed the joint institute's bedrock work (see #3) was
regionally relevant for years without ever having the standing or the occasion to prove it, and who now
has exactly one real chance to get the offer right.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

The crisis outlasts A's resistance before it outlasts B's patience. Once it's clear the instability
isn't going to resolve on its own, Neumayer formally brings Abowasa's researchers in, and their
stabilization approach works well enough that a single emergency visit obviously isn't sufficient —
unstable ice needs continuous monitoring, not a one-time fix. Both sides negotiate an actual ongoing
consulting arrangement rather than a favor quietly forgotten once the crisis passes: Abowasa exporting
knowledge instead of goods, for the first time, on a standing basis.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Abowasa's own researchers start regularly rotating out to Neumayer's site for fieldwork, and — once the
precedent exists — to Halley's as well. The city's Technical/Scientific sector, previously a purely
domestic research identity, takes on a genuinely regional, semi-itinerant character: a meaningful share
of Abowasa's own scientists now spend real stretches of time away from the city they're supposedly
studying on behalf of.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Researchers who are regularly away doing fieldwork inevitably bring more than their own findings back
with them — they come home carrying observations, techniques, and even informal personnel exchanges
from Halley's relocatable-structure engineering and Neumayer's hydraulic systems. Abowasa's own
scientific culture stops being a pure exporter of bedrock expertise and becomes a genuine importer and
synthesizer of subnet-wide engineering practice too — quietly cosmopolitan, in exactly the one domain
where the rest of the city still reads as small and inward-facing.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Abowasa now runs a standing consulting relationship with Neumayer, and a newer one with Halley, built
on real, tested expertise rather than goodwill. Its research institute has a rotating, outward-facing
character its founders never anticipated, functioning as both the subnet's answer for stable-ground
knowledge and an unlikely conduit bringing its neighbors' own hard-won engineering practice back home.
