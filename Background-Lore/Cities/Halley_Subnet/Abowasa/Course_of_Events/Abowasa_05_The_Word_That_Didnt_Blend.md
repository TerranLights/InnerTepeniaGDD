## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

As daily hybridization deepens across the city, Finnish specifically starts slipping. Fewer children
grow up speaking it at home; more mixed households simply default to whichever language is easiest
when both sides of a marriage, a friendship, or a workplace are in the room, and that easiest language
is rarely Finnish.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

The generation actually watching this happen recognizes what's disappearing well before it's gone
completely — grandparents who notice their grandchildren answering Finnish questions in Swedish or in
the blended household tongue, without the children even registering the switch as a choice. A
deliberate, unglamorous effort to keep Finnish alive as an actually-spoken household language starts
taking hold in a handful of families, driven less by policy than by simple insistence at the dinner
table. But that insistence only ever survives inside the specific families willing to carry it — it
never becomes a citywide expectation, and the households that don't share the same instinct feel no
particular pressure to start.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between two people who both love
their children and disagree about what that love actually requires — not a fight between someone who
cares about heritage and someone who doesn't. A's insistence on Finnish-only at home is a genuine act
of care, trying to hand down something real before it's gone. B's resistance is just as genuine: in a
household that's already bilingual or mixed, strict insistence can isolate a child rather than enrich
them, and B may be raising kids in exactly that kind of household, weighing practical ease against
symbolic duty and reasonably landing somewhere different than A does.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly an older parent or
grandparent from a Finnish-identified household, personally alarmed at how fast the language is
slipping within their own family. Character B is plausibly a younger parent — very possibly from one
of the intermarried, hyphenated-surname households established in #1 — for whom strict Finnish
insistence at home feels impractical or even unkind to children who are, by birth, equally Swedish and
equally Abowasan.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither fully persuades the other, and the disagreement never resolves into a citywide mandate — B's
practical objection holds for most households, and daily language at the kitchen table keeps drifting
the way it already was. But A's insistence does succeed at something smaller and more durable: a
public, civic observance, Turku Remembrance, gets formally established to honor the Aboa/Turku naming
connection and everything it stands for — not a requirement that everyone speak Finnish, but a
guarantee that the city itself will publicly honor and support the households that choose to.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because Turku Remembrance formalizes an opt-in practice rather than a mandate, it creates a
recognizable, respected role within the city: specific "language keeper" households known for actually
raising children bilingual, sought out by other families who want their own kids to pick up some
Finnish, or who need help translating an old record or a grandparent's letter.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

Once "language keeper" is a recognized role rather than just an accident of ancestry, people from
households with no Finnish heritage at all start actively seeking it out — asking to be informally
taught, occasionally even semi-adopted into a language-keeper family's practice. Within a few
generations, "who speaks Finnish at home" stops being a question of bloodline and becomes a question
of chosen commitment, the role fully decoupled from the ancestry that originally defined it.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Abowasa ends up genuinely bilingual at the civic level — Turku Remembrance publicly honoring Finnish
every year without fail — and unevenly bilingual at the kitchen-table level, permanently. The
"language keeper" role that grew out of that split has become its own small, respected civic identity,
open to anyone willing to take it up rather than reserved for a shrinking founding lineage.
