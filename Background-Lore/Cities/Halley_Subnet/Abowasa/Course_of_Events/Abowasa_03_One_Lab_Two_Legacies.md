## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Even after daily household life has thoroughly blended (see #1), the inherited FINNARP and SWEDARP
research programs stay administratively separate — two proud institutions, each carrying its own
national scientific lineage, operating side by side inside a city that no longer keeps anything else
that neatly divided.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A city this small cannot actually support two competing research institutions drawing on the same
tiny pool of funding and the same small handful of researchers, and the strain shows fast: duplicated
equipment nobody can afford to duplicate, grant applications competing against each other for the same
limited pool, promising researchers pulled between two under-resourced programs instead of one properly
resourced one. The pressure forces the obvious answer — merge the two programs into a single joint
institute, the first real institution, as opposed to household or neighborhood custom, that the city
will have ever built jointly. But agreeing that a merger has to happen is the easy part. Merging means
someone has to decide whose leadership structure survives, whose naming convention the new institute
carries, and whose research methods take precedence over the other's — and neither program is willing
to simply be absorbed into the other.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a negotiation, not a fight, between two senior
researchers each defending their own institution's continuity — not out of vanity, but because each
genuinely believes their program's particular methods and training lineage are worth preserving, and
each has real professional standing riding on how the merger resolves. The trap to avoid is making
either program's methods simply "correct" and the other "wrong" — Finnish and Swedish scientific
traditions arriving in Tepenia would plausibly differ in emphasis and convention, not competence, and
the conflict should read as two legitimate scientific cultures negotiating coexistence, not a contest
with an obviously right answer.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly the senior-most
FINNARP-descended researcher, whose authority and identity are bound up in a specific inherited
methodology; Character B is the SWEDARP-descended counterpart, holding an equivalent stake in a
different one. Both are plausibly the kind of figure the placeholder "founding-era joint coordinator"
from `Full_Extrapolation` §VI would have had to work through a generation earlier — people whose
professional legacy is now genuinely on the table, not just their institution's paperwork.

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Neither side's structure wins outright. The merged institute adopts a dual-directorship rather than a
single chain of command, a hybrid name that credits both lineages, and — crucially — splits its
methodology not by nationality but by research discipline, so that geological work runs on whichever
tradition's methods are actually strongest for that specific problem, regardless of which program
originated them. That specific compromise, reached under real pressure and genuinely satisfying neither
side completely, becomes the working template for how every later joint decision at Abowasa gets
negotiated — the household-level habit of blending proven, for the first time, at institutional scale.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because the merger produced something structurally new rather than one program simply absorbing the
other, the institute itself becomes an object of interest beyond its actual research output —
administrators from neighboring cities start visiting specifically to study how Abowasa pulled off a
genuine cross-national institutional merger, treating the dual-directorship model as a case study in
its own right, separate from anything the institute actually discovers about bedrock.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

As other cities' administrators keep coming specifically to study the merger model, junior researchers
trained inside the institute start getting recruited elsewhere in the subnet not just for their
geological expertise but specifically for their firsthand experience running — and surviving — a
genuine dual-national team. "Trained at Abowasa's joint institute" becomes its own quiet credential,
outlasting the founding generation who actually negotiated the original compromise.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

The merged institute stands as Abowasa's first real joint institution and the reusable template for
every joint civic decision that follows it — but it has also, almost by accident, turned the city into
a minor regional exporter of institutional know-how as well as scientific research, with its alumni
carrying a specific, portable kind of credibility that has nothing to do with geology at all.
