## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

For years, Abowasa's only shared civic occasions are each founding nation's own imported holidays,
celebrated separately, politely attended by the other side out of neighborliness rather than genuine
belonging.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

A generation raised entirely in Abowasa — belonging fully to neither founding nation's own imported
tradition — starts wanting a civic occasion that's actually theirs: not Finnish, not Swedish, just
Abowasan.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a confrontation between a member of Abowasa's
first fully native-born generation, pushing for a new, genuinely shared civic holiday, and an
older, first-generation resident loyal to their own founding nation's already-meaningful imported
tradition, who reads "something new" as implicitly "something that replaces what we brought with us."
The tension should be generational and emotional, not procedural — this is about what each generation
feels ownership over, not a scheduling dispute.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a young,
native-born Abowasan organizing support for the new observance — someone whose entire frame of
reference is the joint city, not either founding nation individually. Character B is plausibly an
older resident from either founding community, genuinely attached to their own nation's holiday, whose
resistance comes from a real sense of loss rather than simple stubbornness. Either nationality works for
B; the important thing is that their attachment to the old tradition is portrayed as sincere, not
small-minded.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

Deciding the new holiday's actual form forces both communities to agree, together, on what's genuinely
worth celebrating about the joint settlement itself — producing **The Joint Founding**, the first date
on Abowasa's calendar that belongs to the city rather than to either half of it.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

Because The Joint Founding has to be designed collaboratively from nothing, rather than borrowed whole
from either founding nation, its specific traditions become the first piece of Abowasa's civic culture
that neither founding community can claim sole authorship of.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

That authorship-neutral quality makes The Joint Founding the template later generations instinctively
reach for whenever a new shared civic practice needs inventing — rather than each new institution
defaulting to a Finnish or Swedish model purely out of habit, the way everything before it had.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Abowasa now has a proven, working example of how to build genuinely joint civic culture from nothing —
a precedent its own two founding communities can point back to, and actually reuse, whenever the
question of "whose way do we do this" comes up again.
