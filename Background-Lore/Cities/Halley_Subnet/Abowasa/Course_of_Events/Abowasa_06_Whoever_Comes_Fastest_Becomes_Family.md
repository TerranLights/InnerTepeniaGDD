## State of Affairs 1
###### The city as it stands before this cycle's turning point.

Abowasa's small scale means newcomers get folded into full community standing unusually fast —
everyone already knows everyone, so there's no long probationary period for a stranger to sit through
the way there might be in a larger city. It's a small, mostly unremarked kindness, extended mainly to
occasional visitors passing through the subnet.

---

## Trigger
###### What disrupts the above baseline.

As Tepenia's Interwar population grows and word spreads across the subnet that Abowasa is, simply, the
easiest place around to actually become a local rather than just visit, the same fast-absorption habit
that once just made travelers feel welcome starts pulling in genuine new settlers from well beyond the
two founding nations — people drawn by nothing more dramatic than how quickly they'd belong somewhere.
But doing this once is charming; doing it repeatedly, at real scale, starts to change who "Abowasa"
even means, straining an informal, personal absorption process that was never built for sustained
inflow, only for the occasional guest.

**Design notes — what kind of conflict this needs:** a disagreement between someone who sees fast
absorption as Abowasa's defining virtue, worth protecting and even expanding, and someone who fears
that virtue, left unchecked, will quietly erase the very "two peoples" story that made it meaningful in
the first place. Avoid framing this as generosity versus xenophobia — B isn't arguing against
newcomers; B is arguing that an identity built on two specific founding nations doesn't survive being
diluted into a generic multinational one without someone, somewhere, actively working to keep the
original story alive alongside the growth.

**Design notes — what kind of character(s) this needs:** Character A is plausibly a recently arrived,
non-Finnish/Swedish immigrant who has personally been embraced quickly and now advocates loudly for
keeping that door open exactly as wide as it already is. Character B is plausibly a founding-lineage
household head — Finnish, Swedish, or one of the hyphenated households from #1 — who isn't opposed to
growth but worries that nobody is doing the work of making sure the founding story survives it.

---

## Culmination
###### The conflict's actual outcome — specific and concrete.

No formal gatekeeping policy ever gets adopted — A's practical argument holds, and the fast-absorption
habit keeps running exactly as it always has. But B's underlying worry gets addressed sideways rather
than through restriction: the community starts actively over-investing in its specific founding
symbols — the merged kitchen, the two protected languages, the observances already taking shape in #1
and #5 — precisely because the surrounding population is diversifying too fast to trust to hold onto
that story by default anymore. The dual-national character quietly becomes a multi-national one, almost
without anyone formally deciding it should.

---

## 2nd-Order Change
###### The direct, immediate structural consequence of *this specific outcome*.

The old everyone-knows-everyone absorption process, now handling real sustained inflow rather than the
occasional visitor, starts to buckle under its own informality and needs actual structure for the first
time — specific households begin taking on a semi-recognized role as sponsors, informally responsible
for walking a given newcomer through the city's particular customs rather than leaving it to chance
encounters.

---

## 3rd-Order Change
###### The downstream consequence of the 2nd-order change — caused by *it*, not by the original conflict.

The sponsor-household role gets disproportionately claimed by the city's mixed-heritage,
hyphenated-surname households — the families who already, by simple virtue of what they are, embody
how belonging works in Abowasa better than anyone else could explain it. The founding Finnish/Swedish
story, instead of dissolving under the weight of a growing multinational population, ends up literally
embodied in the specific people doing the work of welcoming everyone else in.

---

## State of Affairs 2
###### The new baseline, feeding the next cycle.

Abowasa is now genuinely multinational in practice, its old two-nation founding fact preserved less
through any policy than through the living example of its own mixed-heritage families, who have become
the city's informal ambassadors to everyone arriving after them. The city keeps growing outward while
still pointing, deliberately, back at where it started.
