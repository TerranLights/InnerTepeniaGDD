# Abowasa — Candidate Courses of Events

**Scope note (revised):** all ten chains below are set strictly *before* the Long Night War, during
the Second Interwar Period when Tepenia is a full, functioning country — no war damage, no
post-war/present-day material. 10 candidate But/Therefore chains built from Abowasa's Megasheet (dual
Finnish/Swedish founding, "Two Peoples, One Small Place," household-scale intimacy, bedrock
stability). Post-settlement only; nothing here is canon.

---

## 1. Two Kitchens Into One

Finnish and Swedish exiles start out trying to keep separate households, 200 meters apart, the way
the two real stations always had been. **But** a community this small can't actually support two of
everything — two smiths, two cooks, two craft traditions — without both running thin. **Therefore**
daily material culture blends fast, out of plain necessity. **But** language and civic memory turn
out to be the one thing both communities refuse to fully surrender. **Therefore** Abowasa settles
into its lasting shape: a hybrid kitchen and a divided tongue, on purpose.

## 2. Season Becomes Permanent

Both inherited stations were built for year-round occupation, but neither nation ever staffed them
through winter — a budget choice, never a structural one. **But** exile leaves nobody a season to go
home to. **Therefore** the first winter gets staffed anyway, not as new tradition but as the only
option left. **But** doing it once means doing it every year after. **Therefore** "seasonal outpost"
quietly becomes "permanent home" without anyone ever formally deciding it should.

## 3. One Lab, Two Legacies

Even after daily household life blends (#1), the inherited FINNARP and SWEDARP research programs stay
administratively separate, each proud of its own national scientific lineage. **But** a city this
small can't actually support two competing research institutions drawing on the same tiny pool of
funding and researchers. **Therefore** the two programs merge into a single joint institute — the
first real institution, not just household or neighborhood custom, that the city builds jointly.
**But** merging means picking whose leadership structure, whose naming convention, and whose methods
take precedence. **Therefore** the merged institute becomes the working template for how every later
joint decision at Abowasa gets negotiated — the household-level habit of blending proven, for the
first time, at institutional scale.

## 4. Bedrock Diplomacy

Abowasa sits on solid mainland bedrock; Halley and Neumayer, its nearest subnet neighbors, sit on
moving ice shelves they've each built elaborate engineering solutions to manage. **But** neither of
those solutions actually solves the underlying instability — it only compensates for it. **Therefore**
when one of them hits a foundation crisis its own engineers can't fully resolve, Abowasa's stability
research becomes the answer nobody thought to ask for until they needed it. **But** accepting help
means admitting a small, quiet city knows something the bigger neighbor doesn't. **Therefore** a real,
ongoing consulting relationship forms — Abowasa exporting knowledge instead of goods.

## 5. The Word That Didn't Blend

As daily hybridization deepens, Finnish specifically starts slipping — fewer children speaking it at
home, more households defaulting to whichever language is easiest in mixed company. **But** the
generation watching it happen recognizes what's disappearing before it's gone entirely. **Therefore**
a deliberate, unglamorous effort to keep Finnish alive as a spoken household language takes hold —
Turku Remembrance formalizing what had been informal insistence. **But** it survives only as one
family's practice among many, never a citywide mandate. **Therefore** Abowasa ends up genuinely
bilingual at the civic level and unevenly bilingual at the kitchen-table level, permanently.

## 6. Whoever Comes Fastest Becomes Family

Abowasa's small scale means newcomers get folded into full community standing unusually fast — everyone
already knows everyone, so there's no long probationary period for a stranger to sit through.
**But** as Tepenia's Interwar population grows and word spreads across the subnet that Abowasa is the
easiest place around to actually become a local rather than just visit. **Therefore** the same
fast-absorption habit that once just made visitors feel at home starts pulling in genuine new
settlers from beyond the two founding nations, drawn by nothing more dramatic than how quickly they'd
belong. **But** doing this repeatedly changes who "Abowasa" even means. **Therefore** the city's
founding dual-national character quietly becomes a multi-national one over the following generations,
almost without anyone deciding to expand it.

## 7. Choosing Not to Suffer

At a real fork — a resource shortage that could plausibly have split the city along Finnish/Swedish
lines the way scarcity divides other Tepenian communities along national lines — Abowasa instead
solves it jointly, fast, without anyone needing to lose. **But** that outcome wasn't inevitable; it
required both communities to actually cooperate under pressure, not just coexist under comfort.
**Therefore** the choice becomes local legend, the moment residents point to when explaining why
Abowasa's story reads as things working out rather than things being endured. **But** neighboring
cities' harder-won survival stories make Abowasa's own smooth history feel, to outsiders, slightly
suspicious. **Therefore** Abowasa quietly learns to undersell its own good fortune around visitors
from harder-hit places.

## 8. The Coordinator Nobody Named

In the earliest post-arrival years, before anyone thought of "Abowasa" as one place, the day-to-day
business of coordinating two adjacent, separately-run stations still has to get done — shared
supply runs, shared weather reporting, shared emergencies. **But** neither nation's own chain of
command actually covers cooperation with the other. **Therefore** an unofficial, practical
coordinator role emerges — whoever's willing to walk between the two and make decisions nobody
formally authorized them to make. **But** that role is never institutionalized or credited.
**Therefore** the position that effectively founded Abowasa's whole cooperative character belongs, in
the record, to nobody in particular.

## 9. One Roof, Two Flags

A specific structure — a supply shed, a comms mast, a shared well — ends up sitting close enough to
both stations' original boundaries that neither can plausibly claim it alone. **But** early
administrative habits (each community still keeping its own separate rules) mean a dispute over who
maintains it, who uses it first, who pays for its repair. **Therefore** the two communities negotiate
a genuinely joint-management arrangement for that one structure, the first formal thing they ever
agreed to run together. **But** that small precedent turns out to be reusable. **Therefore** joint
management, tested once on a shed, becomes the default model for everything the city builds after.

## 10. Two Names, One Calendar

For years, Abowasa's only shared civic occasions are each nation's own imported holidays, celebrated
separately, politely attended by the other side out of neighborliness rather than belonging.
**But** a generation raised entirely in Abowasa, belonging fully to neither import, starts wanting
something that's actually theirs. **Therefore** a new, deliberately shared observance gets proposed —
not Finnish, not Swedish, just Abowasan — marking the joint settlement itself rather than either
founding nation. **But** deciding its form means both communities have to agree on what's actually
worth celebrating together. **Therefore** the resulting holiday, The Joint Founding, becomes the one
date on Abowasa's calendar that belongs to the city rather than to either half of it.

---

## Worth Your Attention

**#3** and **#4** are the most load-bearing — they turn Abowasa's established Technical/Scientific
research strength into actual causal events (an internal institutional merger, an external consulting
relationship) rather than a static profile line. **#1** and **#5** work as a matched pair: the same
underlying "hybridize daily life, protect symbolic identity" split, told once from the winning side
(daily culture) and once from the contested side (language).
