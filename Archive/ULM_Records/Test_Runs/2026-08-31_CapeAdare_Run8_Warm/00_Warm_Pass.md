# 1. ⭐ The shape flips — and `02` §4.0 predicted exactly this

**`02` §4.0 is binding here: the shape is a property of the admitted input set, not of the location — and where
two input sets exist, run it twice, because the disagreement is worth more than either reading.**

| | Cold pass (Run 7) | Warm pass (Run 8) |
|---|---|---|
| **Admitted inputs** | `Specs` minus two excluded sections; census; founding text | everything |
| **STANDING COST quadrant** | **thin** — one entry (rookery coexistence), from G2 only | **the largest quadrant by far** |
| **Shape** | **BALANCED, leaning "one absence"** | **COST-DOMINANT** |

**What filled it, and why the cold pass could not see it.** `Local_Cultures` §15 assigns **technical/scientific
20% — heritage site preservation** and **education 15% — historical/civic memory-keeping**. §17 makes archival
record-keeping **the robot population's respected specialization**. §24's Arcanet presence is archive-weighted.
§25 exports heritage-preservation expertise as a trained skill.

**Put together: roughly 35% of Cape Adare's stated economy is the maintenance of one 1899 wooden hut and the
documentation of it.**

> ## ⛔ CORRECTED, SAME DAY — that 35% was a canon defect, and this pass amplified it instead of catching it
>
> **Developer's correction:** *"one-third of the entire city's economy is genuinely unreasonable… Cape Adare
> cannot be a city that's entirely based around maintaining a tent."*
>
> **They are right, and the arithmetic is damning:** 35% of 1,050,051 is **~367,500 people** maintaining one
> small wooden hut — about **36,000×** the real-world conservation effort at the actual site.
>
> **⚠ Refined by a second developer correction, which reversed part of the first fix (M-60):** the sector
> *sizes* were never the problem — **a narrow object had colonized a broad sector.** "Heritage" properly means
> *stewardship of the past into the future*: libraries, civic and municipal archives, conservation of the
> city's own older building stock, records administration. **At 20%+15% that is entirely ordinary for a city of
> a million; pointed at one hut it is absurd.** `Local_Cultures` §15 now keeps its percentages and widens its
> definitions, with hut-specific conservation as a **sub-1%, high-prestige specialization** inside them.
>
> ### ⚠ What this does to §1's headline claim — stated plainly rather than quietly amended
>
> **The shape flip is real but substantially overstated as written above.** With heritage at ~3% rather than
> 35%, the STANDING COST quadrant fills **meaningfully but not dominantly**. **The honest revised reading is
> that the warm shape sits between the two** — a genuine ongoing preservation obligation the cold pass could
> not see, but **not enough to carry a cost-dominant classification on its own.**
>
> **And the methodological failure is mine, not the cold pass's.** Run 7 never saw the number. **Run 8 saw it,
> treated it as authoritative input, and built its headline finding on top of it — without running Gate 11
> against it.** `04` Gate 11 asks *"would a person actually do this, at this cost, priced in this location's
> physical conditions"*, and one division would have answered no. **A warm pass inherits its sources'
> defects unless it actively tests them, and this one didn't.** Recorded as M-59.
>
> ### What survives the correction, and is better for it
>
> **§2.1's finding survives intact and improves.** A small, dedicated, high-prestige archival institution
> keeping centuries of records is **more** poignant than a third of the city doing it — and it makes the
> accidental-disaster-recovery reading sharper, since the survival of the record then depends on a specific,
> nameable, comparatively fragile institution rather than on sheer mass employment.

**This is the second recorded instance of the §4.0 phenomenon** *(after Zhongshan Runs 3/4, which produced
opposite shapes on the same city in one week)*. **What makes this instance more useful:** Zhongshan's flip came
from *quarantining known institutions*. Cape Adare's came from **canon that existed and was simply unreachable
from the attribute tier** — which is the far commoner situation, and means **any cold pass on a location with a
rich culture file should expect its cost quadrant to read artificially thin.**

**Recorded to the observations log as M-58.**

# 2. Synthesis — four findings no single source contains

**The warm pass's real justification. Each of these requires holding several sources at once.**

## 2.1 ⭐ The city accidentally built its own disaster-recovery system, out of reverence

**The sources, separately:**
- `Local_Cultures` §5b names the fault line: *"what happens to that identity if the hut itself is lost? This
  question was alive in Cape Adare's culture long before the Long Night War made it literal."*
- `Full_Extrapolation` §I answers it: the structure did not survive, **but centuries of documentation plausibly
  did** — *"the structure is gone; the record of exactly what it was, and how it stood, very likely isn't."*
- §15/§17/§24/§25 establish the documentation apparatus: a dedicated institution *(~2–4% of the economy, per
  the same-day correction — not the third originally claimed)*, the robots' specialization, the Arcanet's
  weighting, the city's export.

**Together, and stated by none of them:** **the apparatus that saved Cape Adare's identity was not built to
save it.** It was built out of reverence — centuries of people measuring, photographing, and archiving a hut
because it mattered, not because they were hedging against its loss. **The city spent its entire history
constructing the exact instrument that would let it survive its own named worst fear, without ever framing it
that way.**

**Why this is the pass's standout:** it converts §5b's fault line from a tragedy into something structurally
stranger and better — **a civic anxiety that was already, unknowingly, answered.** And it gives the robot
population's archival specialization (§17) retroactive weight it does not have on its own: they were the ones
holding the record.

**Player-facing, per `Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md` §0b:** a player who reaches the ruins finds no hut —
and finds the archive. **The discovery is not "it's gone," it's "they kept everything."**

## 2.2 The city already had the answer to the tension the cold pass found

**Run 7's standout finding** (Phase 2, and genuinely absent from the withheld material): the community carrying
the founding memory — New Zealand, flagged *earliest founding wave* at 3.19% — is not the community holding the
demographic weight (USA 22.26%, China 19.85%, neither founding-wave flagged). Run 7 derived a latent civic
tension from this.

**`Local_Cultures` §6a, which Run 7 could not see:** *"Precedence is earned by residence and reverence, not
ancestry."*

**Both-are-true, and the synthesis is sharper than either:** the demographic mismatch is **real**, and the city
**already carries an explicit civic doctrine that neutralizes it.** Precedence is not heritable, so New
Zealand's earliness confers nothing transferable — which is *why* the tension Run 7 correctly identified never
became a live grievance. **The cold pass found a real fault line; the withheld material shows the city built
the load-bearing beam across it before anyone noticed the crack.**

## 2.3 The Yalta pick pays off twice, and the Mega-Init only banked once

`Cape_Adare_Mega_Init.md` reads Yalta as (a) a resort that became communal, and (b) a small town hosting a
world-changing meeting.

**It misses the sharper parallel, which `Full_Extrapolation` §VIII sets up:** Yalta's 1945 conference was sited
there partly for **neutrality** — ground that belonged to the meeting rather than to any one party. **Cape
Adare's own §5 identity is precisely that: *"inherited equally by everyone who arrived because none of them
could claim it was theirs to begin with."*** **The founding character and the gathering-host role are the same
property, not two separate echoes** — a place owned by nobody is the natural site for a meeting nobody should
own. That makes §VIII's proposed self-governance gathering **structurally inevitable rather than merely
plausible.**

## 2.4 The core/outer model resolves the "vast but sparse" claim into physical fact

`Local_Cultures` §9 and the Vision Notes assert Cape Adare is *"geographically large but low-density — a city
that looks big on a map but feels like a small town on the ground."* **Asserted, never explained.**

**Today's acquired attributes explain it:** the ice-free cape is **2.94 km²** — the smallest documented downtown
core in Tepenia — while the peninsula behind it runs **74 km**, high and ice-covered. **Under the core/outer
model, Cape Adare is a tiny dense core with an enormous outer area**, which is exactly what "looks big on a map,
feels like a small town" describes from the inside. **A vision-session impression now has a measured physical
mechanism** — `Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md`'s Retroactive Mechanism technique, applied successfully.

# 3. What this pass cannot claim

- **It is not a cold test, and its convergences prove nothing about the methodology.** Every agreement here is
  between a reader and material that reader has read.
- **§2.1, §2.3 and §2.4 are syntheses across existing sources**, not new derivations from constraint. Their
  value is real but it is *editorial* — noticing what several documents jointly imply.
- **Only §2.2 inherits a genuinely cold-derived component** (Run 7's mismatch finding), and even there the
  resolution comes from withheld material.
- **The shape flip (§1) is the one finding here with methodological weight**, because it is a measurement of
  the input set rather than a claim about Cape Adare.
