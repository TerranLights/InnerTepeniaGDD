# Sinheung — Phase 7: Order

## 7a — Work and economy

**Division of labor, per Phase 2/4:** a small, nationally-visible chamber-manufacturing trade; a much larger
general fabrication/logistics workforce (the actual majority pattern); a non-industrial administrative/service
layer. **What it sends outward:** chamber units nationally (the headline export) plus general finished goods.

**The non-thematic export, per `03` Phase 7a's requirement** (something ordinary, emotionally neutral, unrelated
to the headline function, proving the economy is real rather than allegorical): **dimension stone / aggregate,
quarried from the Larsemann Hills' own exposed bedrock.** This follows directly and only from Phase 1's G2
profile — Sinheung sits on one of Antarctica's rare genuinely ice-free oases, meaning it is one of a small
number of Tepenian sites where actual quarriable rock is physically accessible at all. **Unrelated to
fabrication/chambers, ordinary, and grounded in a fact this pass already established rather than invented for
this purpose.** A resident elsewhere in Tepenia could plausibly own a small piece of dressed Larsemann Hills
stone (a doorstep, a paving block, ballast) with no idea it came from the same city that built their robot.

## 7b — Governance and decision

**The most productive question is not who holds power but what is unadministrable.** Per Phase 4's land-
scarcity mundane-concern finding and Phase 5's Zhongshan-tri-junction dependency: **the precise, current
right-of-way priority across the shared oasis transit routes is structurally unadministrable.** Three cities
occupying one small landmass, each with its own economic rhythm (Phase 4's freight-schedule organizing
principle), means any formal, fixed allocation of road/junction priority would have to be renegotiated
constantly as each city's own freight volume shifts — and nothing in admissible canon suggests such a formal
allocation exists. **This is the edge case nobody wrote**, per `03` Phase 7b's own diagnostic question, and it
follows directly from Phase 1's arithmetic land-scarcity deficit rather than being invented fresh.

**Prohibitions:** none established in admissible canon. **Null, recorded with reason:** genuinely absent, not
merely unexplored — nothing in Sinheung's Tier-1 attribute material implies a specific chosen policy of
exclusion.

## 7c — Knowledge and transmission

**How does someone learn to do the thing this place does?** Chained from Phase 5d's membership-conversion
finding (having a piece of one's own work accepted into the fabrication/freight chain without correction): the
same mechanism plausibly governs skill transmission generally, not only newcomer-to-resident conversion.
**Apprenticeship here is output-proven, not credential-proven** — directly downstream of Phase 1's G4 founding
disposition (a claim proven through demonstrated output, not through paper legitimacy, which is itself ironic
given the founding claim *itself* was paper-secured). **What becomes of those who cannot learn it?** Per Phase
6's asymmetric-record-keeping finding (successes recorded, failures under-recorded): the plausible, non-villain
shape is **quiet lateral reassignment** rather than dismissal or public failure — someone who cannot clear the
"accepted without correction" bar drifts into peripheral, less-output-visible roles (administration, service,
the non-thematic-export quarry work from §7a) rather than being formally failed out. **No author, no policy,
no cruelty required** — exactly the `00d` shape: everyone involved is acting reasonably, and the harm (a
permanent, invisible demotion in visible standing) is a byproduct nobody chose.

## 7d — Private life, minorities, and counterculture

**Check canon for an existing counterculture first:** none found in admissible material — expected, since
Sinheung's culture-pass files (where a counterculture would most likely already be recorded) are quarantined.

**Derive from the specific pressure:** what does Sinheung require of everyone, and who cannot or will not give
it? Per Phase 4's central civic finding — a quiet, near-universal reflex toward demonstrable output, even in
contexts where nobody is watching — **the demand is continuous self-proof.** Running `03` Phase 7d's own
required check: ***is the contradiction between groups here, or inside each person?***

**Finding: inside each person, not between factions.** Phase 4 already established the "prove it" reflex as
a widely-shared, mostly-unnoticed disposition rather than an externally imposed rule with visible enforcers —
there is no institution demanding it and therefore no population positioned to refuse it as a group.
**Consequence, stated per `03` Phase 7d's own guidance:** Sinheung's core pressure **cannot be reformed from
within and cannot be opposed from within either, because there is nobody to organize.** A resident who feels
the "prove it" pressure and resents it has no counterpart faction to join — the contradiction is
distributed, not organized, which is a genuinely different (and rarer) shape than most locations' countercultures
produce.

**Weaker, secondary candidate — checked and kept distinct:** Phase 4's "off-rhythm" population (workers whose
schedules don't track the Mirny freight cycle) is a real, distinguishable minority, but it is a **structural**
population, not a value-based counterculture — they are out of sync with the ambient rhythm, not refusing,
performing what the majority cannot, or over-literally applying a stated rule (per `03` Phase 7d's three
counterculture shapes). **Correctly excluded from the counterculture finding above** — flagged separately as a
Phase 9/10 population-texture note, not conflated with it.
