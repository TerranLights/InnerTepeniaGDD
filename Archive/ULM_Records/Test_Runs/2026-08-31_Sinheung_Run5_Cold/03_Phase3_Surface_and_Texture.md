# Sinheung — Phase 3: Surface & Texture

## A. Formalize first

Admissible canon carries almost no explicit sensory material for Sinheung — Specs is climate/founding/economy-
focused, not descriptive. **This phase is genuinely underspecified in existing material**, which is itself the
correct null-with-reason: nothing to formalize, proceed to generation grounded in Phase 1's physical/founding
facts and G7 research.

## G7 research — Progress Station, the direct real-world basis

**Concrete facts found:** Progress Station has a stark seasonal population swing — **summer population 50,
winter population 25**, a 2:1 ratio. It has "a modern wintering complex" with residential units, **a sauna, a
gym**, and medical facilities. An adjacent airfield ("Zenit," 3,000m runway) handles large aircraft. From 1998–
2001 it took over logistics operations **transferred from Mirny Station**, becoming the main support base for
Vostok.

**Fusion, not transcription:** Sinheung's population (888,292, Band 4 Urban) is five orders of magnitude larger
than the real station's few dozen residents — the sauna/gym/wintering-complex facts don't scale up literally.
**What does scale is the *ratio* and the *function*:** a station built around extreme seasonal population
swing, and a station whose core role was logistics relay rather than self-sufficient settlement. **Both of
these are already independently true of Sinheung** (Mirny raw-material supply line; presumably some seasonal
variance given the polar night) — this is the "formalize, don't invent" case in G7 form: the real precedent
didn't hand Sinheung anything new, it corroborated something the in-fiction economy already implies without
ever having traced it to source. **Recorded as corroboration, not derivation** — flagged per `05` §6.1's
provenance discipline, since this is exactly the kind of thing that could look like independent research
support for a claim that was actually just restated.

## B. The four sensory sub-fields, answered separately

**Sound.** The dominant register is **industrial, not residential** — heavy fabrication machinery, chamber-
line equipment cycling, freight moving raw material in from the Mirny/Zhongshan direction. Underneath it,
something Sinheung's physical constraint (G2) makes specifically audible in a way most Tepenian cities are not:
**the freeze-thaw cycle is loud.** A site that actually crosses 0°C repeatedly (unlike a permanently-frozen
site) means structural creak, meltwater runoff, and ice movement are recurring, dated, near-daily sounds rather
than a single seasonal event. **Axis: this is a place where the ground itself is audible**, which most
Tepenian Antarctic settlements, built on stable permanent ice or bedrock without a freeze-thaw line, are not.

**Smell.** Heavy industrial fabrication first (metal, ozone from electrical/chamber processes — direct
expression of the Electricity symbol's "animation" register, per Phase 1's G1). Underneath: **thawed ground
smells**, distinctively, in a way permanently-frozen settlements never encounter — wet stone, meltwater algae
in the oasis lakes, an organic note that is completely absent from most of Tepenian Antarctica and would read
as *wrong* to a visitor from an ice-sheet city.

**Feel.** Underfoot: exposed bedrock and gravel, not the ice/snow surface most Tepenian residents' feet know —
this is one of the only genuinely ice-free surfaces most residents of other cities will ever have walked on.
**Air:** the "milder microclimate" (mean −10°C) is cold enough to still be dangerous but warm enough that the
body's calibration for "how cold is this really" is wrong in both directions depending on where a person is
from — someone from a colder Tepenian city may underdress here; someone unfamiliar with Antarctica at all will
still find it lethal.

**First impressions.** ⚠ Per `00b`'s general-population discipline, checked explicitly: the "first impression"
most likely to be reached for is the industrial one (the Chamber Works, the freight lines) — **but per the
Phase 8C failure precedent already recorded in this methodology (Gate 2's music catch), the most vivid material
is not automatically the general one.** The corrected-shape answer: an arriving visitor's actual first
impression is more likely to be **the density itself** — three cities' worth of built environment compressed
into a shared, genuinely small ice-free footprint (Phase 1's density flag), which is a spatial experience no
amount of industrial detail alone conveys. **General answer stated first, industrial vividness held as the
explicitly-scoped second layer**, per `00b`'s corrected-shape rule.

## C. Public/institutional split

**Street-level register:** industrial, freight-heavy, audibly/olfactorily thaw-marked as above.
**Institutional register (inside the Chamber Works specifically):** by G3's own profile, this is a facility
built to match an externally-designed spec (Neumayer's Mark IV) — the interior register is likely to feel
**imported and precise** relative to the exterior city's inherited-and-adapted register. **Flagged as a
finding, not yet confirmed:** if true, walking from Sinheung's ordinary industrial streets into the Chamber
Works itself should feel like crossing into a different building culture — clean, standardized, schematic-
matched — a literal spatial expression of the G3 deficit (the design authority sits elsewhere, and the
building's own interior shows it).

## D. The seam — founding era vs. crisis era

**Two real seams, not one, and this is the differentiating structure.** Most locations in this project have a
single founding-era/crisis-era seam. Sinheung's admissible material implies **three layers**: (1) the original
Russian-built Progress Station physical plant (pre-Korean, inherited whole), (2) the Korean founding-era
adaptation of that plant into a fabrication economy, and (3) war-damage repair, since the city's current status
is explicitly "damaged, partially operational." **A visible seam should exist between all three** — Russian-
built structural bones, Korean-era fabrication retrofit, and visible war-damage patchwork — which is a more
legible, layered built environment than a typical single-founding-era Tepenian city. This directly expresses
Phase 1's G4 finding (an inherited, not built, foundation): **the city's own walls testify to a claim that was
never fully "made" by the people living inside them, only inherited and then repaired.**

## E. Seasonal variance

**At the polar night's worst (~60 days, per Specs' climate table):** the freeze-thaw sounds/smells (B, above)
go silent — this is the one time of year the ground itself is not audible, since nothing is thawing. **The
industrial register becomes dominant by default**, not by contrast, since it is the only register still
active. This is a genuine seasonal-identity shift: for roughly two months, Sinheung becomes texturally
indistinguishable from a permanently-frozen Tepenian industrial city, losing the one sensory signature (the
audible/smellable thaw) that differentiates it the rest of the year.

## F. Retroactive Mechanism

**What is established as simply true here that nothing explains?** Per Specs: the "milder microclimate"
(mean −10°C, warmer than the surrounding ice sheet) is stated as a bare climatic fact with no mechanism given
beyond "ice-free terrain microclimate." **Candidate physical cause, consistent with G2's own established
facts:** exposed dark bedrock in a Southern Hemisphere summer with genuine daylight (unlike ice, which
reflects) absorbs and re-radiates heat, which is a real, physically grounded explanation (the same mechanism
real Antarctic oases like the Larsemann Hills and Schirmacher Oasis are actually known for) rather than an
invented one. **Second-order consequence, free:** if bedrock absorption is the mechanism, the effect should be
strongest exactly where the ground is most exposed and weakest under any snow cover — meaning Sinheung's
warmest micro-pockets are wherever building density has kept the ground bare longest, which ties the city's
warmest spots to its most built-up, most industrial areas. **This gives Phase 4/10 a concrete, derivable urban
heat-pattern**, not an arbitrary one.
