# Sinheung — Phase 1: Constraint & Capability (the spine)

**Four generators run separately below, per `02` §5.1 step 3 — comparison happens only after all four are
complete, so the first read cannot contaminate the others.**

---

## G2 — Physical & environmental constraint

**What it gives freely:** ice-free ground in a continent that is almost entirely ice — meltwater lakes, exposed
bedrock, a harbor-adjacent maritime position on Prydz Bay. A genuinely milder microclimate than the surrounding
ice sheet (mean annual −10°C is warm by Tepenian Antarctic standards). Light: ~61 days of midnight sun.

**What it withholds absolutely:** true isolation from the ice sheet's worst extremes, but not isolation from
cold itself — nothing here is temperate. ~60 days of true polar night. Space: if the density check above is
even roughly right, this is one of the most spatially constrained population concentrations in the entire
project, sharing a 34km² oasis with two full-scale sibling cities.

**What it demands continuously:** heat, structural maintenance against the freeze-thaw cycle a "milder"
microclimate actually makes worse (more days crossing the 0°C line than a permanently frozen site), and —
distinctively — **spatial negotiation with two immediately adjacent cities for a genuinely scarce, non-
renewable resource: ice-free ground itself.** Land is not simply cold-limited here, as at most Tepenian
settlements; it is arithmetically scarce.

**What it permits but punishes:** expansion. Unlike almost every other Tepenian settlement, where "more space"
is a matter of pushing further into an effectively infinite ice sheet, Sinheung's growth is bounded by two
neighbors occupying the same small landmass. Going outside the oasis proper means leaving the one warm-relative,
ice-free advantage the site has at all.

### G2 four-quadrant profile

| Quadrant | Content |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | Ice-free ground; milder microclimate; maritime access via Prydz Bay; genuine daylight for a third of the year |
| **DEFICIT** | Physical land. Not "cold prevents X" (the universal Tepenian deficit) but a specific, arithmetic land scarcity unique among Tepenian cities of this population scale |
| **STANDING COST** | Continuous freeze-thaw structural maintenance (worse than a permanently-frozen site, because the thaw line is actually crossed repeatedly); continuous informal spatial negotiation with Zhongshan and Shirayuki over shared oasis ground |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | Building upward/inward rather than outward is permitted but structurally expensive at Sinheung's stated industrial-fabrication economy (heavy machinery favors horizontal footprint) |

**Shape:** leaning toward **cost-dominant** — the standing cost (perpetual freeze-thaw maintenance plus land
negotiation) is large relative to the other three cells, though not yet confirmed until compared against G4.

## G3 — Function & purpose

**What the place is for, on its own terms:** heavy industrial fabrication — raw material trucked in from Mirny
via the Zhongshan tri-junction, finished into goods. Specifically and nationally significant: Sinheung is one of
only two cities in Tepenia (with Byrd) that manufactures fabrication-synthesis chambers — the actual apparatus
that creates robots, built to an externally-designed schematic (Neumayer's Mark IV).

**What the parent (Mirny subnet / the Federation) needs from it:** a functioning national chamber-manufacturing
node. This is explicitly stated as hiding "behind Sinheung's otherwise modest political profile" — i.e., the
Federation's actual dependency on this city is disproportionate to Sinheung's own visible political weight.

**Where the two diverge — this is the generator's real yield:** Sinheung's own self-conception (per Specs'
"Character & Culture" section — treated cautiously as light scene-setting rather than a deep culture-pass
conclusion, since it is contiguous with clean attribute material and states no personality triple) is oriented
around the Jeju-do claim and the cluster's three-way Korean/Chinese/Japanese daily negotiation. **The Federation's
actual need from Sinheung is narrower and more structural: keep the chamber line running.** A city that thinks
of itself primarily as "the Korean corner of the Larsemann Hills cluster" is, from the Federation's actual
dependency structure, "one of two places robots can physically be made." **Those are not in tension exactly —
but the second one is invisible to the first**, which is the G3 divergence the methodology asks for.

### G3 four-quadrant profile

| Quadrant | Content |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | Nationally load-bearing manufacturing capacity (chamber production) that most of Tepenia depends on without knowing where it comes from |
| **DEFICIT** | Engineering origination — Sinheung builds *to* Neumayer's schematic; the credit and the design authority sit elsewhere. Sinheung can execute at scale but cannot originate the thing it is most known for producing |
| **STANDING COST** | Keeping fabrication equipment matched to an externally-updated spec — a standing cost paid specifically because the design authority is not local (this ties to G2's freeze-thaw cost: two independent maintenance burdens, one physical, one technical-administrative) |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | Being the visible, credited face of "where robots come from" nationally while the actual engineering credit is known (at least institutionally) to belong to Neumayer — permitted, and probably a little resented, but never contested outright |

**Shape:** **STRENGTH and STANDING COST both populated heavily; DEFICIT genuinely load-bearing (not filler).**
Reads structurally similar to G2's cost-dominant lean, but from an entirely independent fact (function, not
physical geography) — first agreement to note, not yet confirmed as grounding until the full comparison table.

## G4 — Founding condition

**Who founded it, why, under what constraint:** Korean exiles, via the Jeju-do International Court of Diplomacy
— the same instrument that allocated Shirayuki to Japan and confirmed Zhongshan's claim to China. Critically:
**this was a claim secured by treaty, before a single Korean exile had set foot on the site**, not a claim grown
organically the way most Tepenian cities' founding stories work (station inheritance, informal settlement, a
population simply staying where they landed).

**With what:** inherited, fully-built Russian polar research infrastructure (Progress Station, AARI) — deep
institutional roots, genuinely advanced physical plant, none of it built by the founding population itself.

**Without what — the generator's real yield:** **without any organic claim of their own.** Every other
Larsemann Hills founding at least has physical proximity or prior operator history as an informal claim
(Zhongshan literally sits on Zhongshan Station, a Chinese-operated site under its own name); Sinheung's Korean
population inherited a *Russian*-built, Russian-named, Russian-operated station with **zero** prior Korean
presence of any kind. The claim is entirely paper — an outcome of a negotiating table the founding population
never sat at, secured for them by diplomats before they existed as a population at all.

### G4 four-quadrant profile

| Quadrant | Content |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | A large, sophisticated, already-built research and industrial base inherited intact — Sinheung did not have to build its own foundation from nothing |
| **DEFICIT** | No organic, lived claim to the physical site itself — no founding story of discovery, hardship-earned-ownership, or prior presence. The claim is legally airtight and experientially thin |
| **STANDING COST** | Continuously demonstrating that a paper claim was worth securing — this is explicit in Sinheung's own admissible material: the founding population "start[ed] to genuinely" need to prove the claim through output before there was anything concrete to point to (Course-of-Events material is inadmissible as a *source*, but Specs' own Character & Culture section independently states the city "negotiated its identity... daily" against its cluster neighbors — corroborating, not derivative, since it is attribute-adjacent scene-setting, not a narrative conclusion) |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | Being visibly, publicly "the Korean city" in a way that must be performed and re-asserted, rather than simply being true by lived history the way Zhongshan's Chinese identity can rest on physical/nominal continuity with the original station |

**Shape:** **DEFICIT and STANDING COST both populated; STRENGTH populated; GRUDGING TOLERANCE the weakest cell.**
Third independent generator landing on cost/deficit weight.

## G1 — Assigned symbolic substrate (Uranus + Electricity)

**Read from the registered files, not from the assignment table's rationale column (`05` §6.1c) and not from
either symbol's traditional/intuitive meaning (`02` §6.0).**

- **Uranus — "Anomaly."** An unintentional outsider; a condition of existence, not a chosen role — "less of a
  role and more of a condition of its very existence." Positive: an identity built *after* a defining event,
  reorientation as the new baseline rather than damage waiting to be corrected; cold with a real cause that
  isn't the obvious one (distance). Negative: a permanent tilt inherited from something that happened once, long
  ago, and was never anyone's choice; sideways treated as normal simply because it has been sideways long
  enough.
- **Electricity — "Animation."** Positive: the literal spark that turns inert matter into something conscious
  and active; connection and communication; power and capability; rapid, decisive transformation; the single
  current that unifies a whole system end to end. Negative: instability (a surge and a total blackout are two
  faces of the same fragility); total dependency — a system completely helpless the instant its power source is
  removed; a direct echo of the Amundsen Tower's own collapse story.

**Pairing relation (`02` §6.3):** **Tensioned, and specifically Ironic in one direction.** Uranus's positive
pole is about a *permanent, stabilized* reorientation — an anomaly that has settled into being simply how things
are, "sideways treated as normal... long enough." Electricity's negative pole is the opposite temperament
entirely: total, sudden, structural fragility, collapse the instant the current is withdrawn. **The irony:** the
one thing that makes Sinheung's post-defining-event identity feel permanent and settled (Uranus, positive) is
powered by the one thing in the pairing that is inherently NOT stable and could fail all at once (Electricity,
negative) — and Electricity's positive pole is *literally the city's own headline industry* (chamber
manufacture — "the spark that turns inert matter into something conscious and active" is not a metaphor here,
it is Sinheung's actual export). **So the pairing reads: a place whose settled, normalized sense of its own
permanence rests on the one faculty it possesses that is structurally the least permanent.**

### G1 four-quadrant profile

| Quadrant | Content |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | Animation/creation-capacity (the chamber industry, read symbolically as well as literally); a settled, post-event identity that no longer experiences its own origin as an open wound |
| **DEFICIT** | No inherent stability faculty — Electricity's negative pole supplies no counterweight to its own instability; the pairing has nothing that says "and this part cannot collapse" |
| **STANDING COST** | Maintaining the appearance/experience of permanence (Uranus's positive pole) against a substrate that is, by its own symbolic negative pole, one surge or one blackout from total collapse |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | Total dependency on the single current/system that animates it — permitted because there is no alternative, expensive because there is no alternative |

**Shape:** **STANDING COST populated heavily again — fourth generator, same lean.** Flagged per the frame file:
this generator's grounding is weaker (provenance caution on the pairing choice), so this agreement is recorded
but weighted less than the G2/G3/G4 agreement below.

## Reading the shape (`02` §4)

**Three of four independent generators (G2, G3, G4) — and a fourth, weaker one (G1) — agree that STANDING COST
is the largest quadrant.** Per `02` §4.0, this must be reported with its input set: **this reading holds on
Tier-1 attribute generators alone (physical constraint, stated function, stated founding condition), without
any admitted culture-pass conclusion, and without treating the cluster's Local_Cultures/Full_Extrapolation
material as input at all.** This is a stronger-than-usual cost-dominant reading precisely because it was NOT
built from institutional particulars (per §4.0 rule 2's suspicion test) — it comes from geography, stated
economic function, and stated founding mechanism, three genuinely independent fact classes.

**Shape: COST-DOMINANT**, on G2/G3/G4/G1 (weighted; G1 lower confidence). The matching question: *is this place
doing anything other than continuing to exist?*

**First-pass answer, to be tested through Phases 2–9:** yes, more than most cost-dominant locations — Sinheung
is not merely surviving, it is nationally load-bearing (chamber manufacture). **But every one of its four
standing costs is a cost of *proving* something** — that the claim was worth securing (G4), that the equipment
still matches spec (G3), that the freeze-thaw structural loss is contained (G2), that the permanence is real and
not one blackout from failing (G1). **The tentative synthesis: Sinheung is not merely paying to continue
existing — it is paying, continuously and on four independent fronts, to continue being *believed*, by itself
and others, to have deserved to exist in the first place.** This is the strongest candidate for Sinheung's
central finding so far, and per `00_RUNBOOK.md` Step 5's strongest-finding check, it will be re-examined at that
step for whether it improperly rests on any provisional/unwritten-parent assumption — at this point it does
not; all four legs are Tier-1, present, admissible inputs.

## Deficit addresses (`02` §4.1) — run before research, per Step 2

- **G2's land deficit:** address = **Diffuse**, tending toward **In the parent** — no single named counterparty
  holds "the missing land"; it does not exist to be acquired from anyone. Where the address could plausibly be
  "In the parent" (the Mirny subnet or Federation *could* theoretically resettle population elsewhere), no
  evidence exists that Sinheung's population perceives this as an option — flagging for the "has anyone here
  ever had a reason to formulate this as a problem?" check per `02` §4.1's diffuse-address note. **Provisional:
  Diffuse**, pending Phase 2/4 evidence.
- **G3's engineering-origination deficit:** address = **Single address — Neumayer.** This is the *best* case per
  `02` §4.1 (a grievance is a relationship that can eventually be acted on) — a named, specific counterparty
  holding exactly what Sinheung lacks.
- **G4's no-organic-claim deficit:** address = **In its own past**, and specifically the harder sub-case: the
  witnesses (people who could compare "a claim grown organically" against Sinheung's paper claim) are not
  Sinheung's own population — they are the *other* Larsemann Hills founding populations, who are neighbors, not
  departed emigrants. This is neither the standard "in its own past" case (evidence departed) nor the standard
  diffuse case (no counterparty) — **it is address = In its own past, but the evidence is present and living
  next door, not absent.** This is a variant `02` §4.1 does not explicitly name — flagged as a methodology
  observation below.
- **G1's no-stability-counterweight deficit:** address = **Diffuse** — no named counterparty holds "stability
  the pairing lacks"; it is a structural absence in the symbolic substrate itself, not a relationship.

**Count of addresses across all four: 1 single, 2 diffuse, 1 "in-its-own-past-but-living-nearby" (novel).** Per
`02` §4.1, a count above one address type is not itself suspicious here (these are four different deficits, not
one deficit counted four times) — but the *G3 single-address* case, being the strongest and most actionable, is
flagged as the primary Phase 5/7 relational thread to develop (Sinheung ↔ Neumayer).

## Methodology observation — a deficit-address variant `02` §4.1 does not name

**Recorded for the shared observations file.** `02` §4.1's "in its own past" address assumes the witnesses who
could name the deficit have departed (the Shirayuki Run 1 worked case: emigration removed the people who could
compare). Sinheung's G4 deficit is structurally different: **the comparison population (founders with an
organic, lived claim) never left — they are the immediate neighbors, Zhongshan and Shirayuki, living within a
few hundred meters to 8km.** This is a third case, distinct from both "in its own past" (evidence departed) and
"diffuse" (no witness ever existed): call it **"in a neighbor's present"** — the deficit is nameable, the
comparison is constantly and physically available, but naming it requires looking sideways at a peer rather than
backward at one's own history or forward at an absent authority. This is likely to be the sharpest possible
version of an "in its own past" deficit, precisely because the unfavorable comparison is not abstract or
historical but a five-minute walk away, every day, permanently. **Proposed addition to `02` §4.1's table** —
flagged, not yet added to the rule file per the reciprocal-obligation convention, pending confirmation this
pattern recurs on a second location.
