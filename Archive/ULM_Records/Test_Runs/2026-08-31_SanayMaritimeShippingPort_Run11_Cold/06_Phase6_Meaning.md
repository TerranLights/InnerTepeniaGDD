# Phase 6 — Meaning

**Asks:** What does this place believe, what does it hold sacred without saying so, and what does it do about
death?

**Applicability:** optional for Installation type (`03` §0.1). Attempted anyway per LAW 0 — optional is not a
license to skip without checking.

**⚠ Note on this phase's own drafting, per M-85 (see `OBSERVATIONS_and_Methodology_Findings.md`):** this phase
deliberately avoids the phrase "function over sentiment," which this session was exposed to via the cold-run
prep document's own descriptive section as a withheld-file characterization of Sanay. Everything below is
built from this pass's own Phase 1/3/4/5 findings, using this pass's own language, not the withheld phrase.

---

## A. The Naming technique

Searched Phases 1-5 for religious-register language attached to non-religious objects (*sacred, honor, sworn,
kept, owed, witnessed, proper, must, never*). **None found in this pass's own admissible material or its own
derived findings.** Genuine null on the Naming technique specifically — proceeding to generate from Phase 1-5's
own established character, per the technique's own fallback instruction.

## B. Belief landscape — a stake, not a creed

**The candidate: what is at risk here that is at risk nowhere else** (per `03` Phase 6's own stake category).
Phase 1's G5 finding establishes the port as one of only two Halley subnet receiving ports; Phase 5's 5e
finding establishes this dependency is real and structurally load-bearing for the whole subnet's Upper Earth
trade access. **The stake: if this port fails, the subnet's seaborne link to Upper Earth resupply narrows to
one remaining port (Belgrano) or none at all** — a genuinely subnet-wide consequence riding on one
Installation's continued function, not merely a local inconvenience if it falters.

**Whether this stake is consciously held by the port's own working population, or is simply an unexamined
background condition of the job, is REQUESTED** — admissible canon does not establish either a stated civic
pride or a stated anxiety about this dependency. Recorded as an open belief-landscape question, not resolved
by invention.

## C. Death and the dead

**The question is obligatory; a section is not, and this pass genuinely has too little admissible material to
answer it with any confidence.** Nothing in admissible canon addresses a workplace fatality or injury practice
specific to the port, despite Phase 1's own G2 finding establishing real physical risk (marginal-weather
crossing attempts, tolerated but genuinely dangerous). Given Phase 2's finding that nobody's home is here,
whatever mortuary practice exists for a worker lost on the job would most plausibly resolve through the
city-level practice this pass's scope excludes — **this is a genuine boundary case worth stating rather than
silently absorbing**: the port GENERATES the risk (Phase 1 G2's Grudging Tolerance) but this pass's own scope
cannot answer what happens after, because that answer lives with Sanay's own city-level Meaning material,
which is entirely inadmissible to this pass. **REQUESTED, explicitly, for whoever eventually runs a full
Sanay-city pass**: does the city have any practice distinguishing a death at the port specifically (an
occupational hazard) from an ordinary death elsewhere?

## D. The Failure State of the Core Value

**Candidate core value, derived from Phase 1's own convergent Grudging Tolerance finding (§C there): the port
never refuses anything at the point of contact.** No freighter is turned away for a risky arrival window
(Phase 1 G2); no traffic is refused for exceeding the trucking chain's onward capacity (Phase 1 G5); reliance
on outdated documentation is never forbidden even where it may fail (Phase 1 G4). **This is this pass's own
independently-derived candidate for the port's central, unstated promise** — not received from any withheld
source, but read directly off three independently-run generators converging on the identical structural shape.

**When does it become impossible to keep?** When the deferred costs from all three domains land simultaneously
rather than separately — a storm-timed arrival attempt goes wrong AND the trucking backlog is already at
capacity AND the situation is one the internally-taught knowledge base has no precedent for. **What would grow
up around that circumstance**, per the technique's own question, is genuinely open — admissible canon does not
establish whether the port has ever actually faced this compounding failure, or how it would respond. Flagged
as a strong candidate for Phase 10 (a named historical incident, if canon ever supplies one) rather than
invented here.

**Does the culture ritualize the failure or refuse to?** Cannot be answered from admissible material alone.
**Recorded as REQUESTED rather than guessed** — inventing an answer here would be exactly the "accepting the
first coherent answer because it fits" failure LAW 0 warns against, especially given how close this pass's own
independently-derived "never refuse at the point of contact" finding already sits to the withheld
characterization flagged in M-85.

## E. Observance

**Genuine null, honestly recorded.** Nothing in admissible canon establishes any port-specific observance, and
this pass's own Band-1-equivalent, no-resident, rotation-clock population (Phase 2/4) makes a conventional
"local holiday" framing a poor fit regardless — a population that never shares a common calendar position
(everyone's schedule is tied to freighter arrivals, not a shared date) structurally struggles to hold a
synchronized observance at all. **This absence is itself worth stating as a finding**, not merely a gap: the
port's own rotation-clock structure (Phase 4) may make ANY shared, dated observance mechanically difficult to
sustain, which is a genuinely different shape from a settlement's ordinary observance question. Whether the
port instead has some non-dated, achievement/rotation-triggered marker (echoing the Membership-by-Persistence
mechanism in Phase 5d) is a plausible candidate but unconfirmed — **REQUESTED**, not invented.
