# Phase 9 — Populations

**Asks:** What is life here like for each distinct kind of person present, and how do those kinds relate?

**Type note applied**: per `03` §0.1's own Installation guidance, the population split here is genuinely more
about staff/rotational status than about kind (human/robot) — confirmed below, not merely assumed.

---

## A. Populations present and the lens

**Human and robot**, per the setting's own two-category baseline (of the project's four representation
categories, only this one is in general use, per standing canon). **The port has no independent census of its
own** (Phase 2 A) — both populations are drawn proportionally from Sanay's own city-wide demographic mix
(Germany Primary; robot figures applying the same national-origin proportions as human population, per the
admissible census methodology note), inherited as a Determined property from the parent, not locally
originated.

**Lens declared**: national-origin anchoring, inherited wholesale from Sanay (the outer-city standard lens),
is available but — per §C below — is NOT the axis that turns out to matter most for describing life at the
port specifically.

## B. Per-population culture

**Nothing port-specific to add to the setting's own baseline human-robot relations for either population
considered separately.** Per Gate I's own inheritance-classification discipline (`04`), this pass does not
invent a local variant of a Determined-level fact — Sanay's own city-wide human-robot composition and the
setting's own established egalitarian baseline apply here unmodified, inherited rather than originated. **This
is correctly recorded as a DETERMINED, surface-tagged inheritance**, not a gap.

## C. Inter-population relations — the stronger move

**Per `03` Phase 9 C's own instruction: is there a category here that matters more than kind?** Yes, and it is
this pass's own strongest Phase 9 finding. **The port reorganizes its working population along the
veteran/new axis established in Phase 5d** (survived a full rotation cycle, including its bad stretch, vs.
not) — a distinction that cuts directly across both human/robot kind and national origin, because Phase 1's
G4 finding (self-taught, peer-transmitted operating knowledge, no external institution to draw on) makes
who-taught-you-the-job the load-bearing relationship, not where-you-or-your-nation-of-origin-came-from. **A
new worker's actual daily relationship to the port's own culture is shaped far more by how many rotations
they have survived than by their national-origin tier or their kind** — the port simply has no admissible
mechanism (unlike a full city) for national-origin culture to organize around, since it never had its own
separate arrival wave (Phase 2 C) to carry one in.

**This does not erase the human/robot distinction — it subordinates it for this location's own specific
purposes.** Both a human and a robot worker experience the identical veteran/new distinction, for the
identical structural reason (Phase 1 G4 applies to the port's own operating knowledge regardless of who is
learning it), which is itself worth stating: **the port's defining scarcity (no external institution) is kind-
blind by construction.**

## E. Swap test

**Would the veteran/new reorganization axis survive unchanged if swapped onto Belgrano** (the nearest
comparable — the Halley subnet's other receiving port, per Phase 5a)? **Partially inconclusive, honestly
recorded rather than claimed clean.** Belgrano plausibly shares a similar rotation-clock structure (both
receive the same seasonal South African freighter traffic), which would predict a similar reorganization —
but this pass has no admissible material establishing Belgrano's own founding condition. **The specific
MECHANISM behind Sanay's own veteran/new axis is Phase 1's G4 finding** — no living institution survived the
SANAE-era handoff chain, only documentation — and if Belgrano's own founding condition differs (a different
institutional history, a different handoff pattern), the axis might not swap cleanly even if the surface
rotation-clock structure does. **Recorded as a genuine limitation of this swap test, not resolved by
assumption** — REQUESTED: Belgrano's own founding condition, which this pass does not have grounds to state.

## F. ⚠ Amendment from the Zodiac Lens (Phase 10 §B2), folded in at Step 5 per `03` §0.4

**Added after Phase 10's Zodiac Lens run and its cross-sign synthesis** (`11_Zodiac_Lens.md`) — recorded as a
revision here, not silently merged, per the standing rule that a person-shaped Zodiac Lens result amends
Phase 9 rather than feeding it as an input.

**The veteran/new axis (§C above) is sharper than originally stated.** Seven independent Zodiac Lens signs
(Taurus, Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Scorpio, Aquarius) converged on a two-part institutional shape
this session's own Phase 1 generators did not derive: the port runs on exactly one written authority (an
unrevised inherited manual) and exactly one living authority (peer-taught workaround knowledge), with no
mechanism anywhere reconciling the two. **This means the veteran/new distinction established in §C is not
merely a social status marker — it is functional authority that exists NOWHERE in any official structure.** A
veteran is not "someone who has learned the rules better than a newcomer"; they are the sole living repository
of knowledge the port's own written record does not and cannot contain, because nothing has ever formalized
the gap between the two. This is a genuinely deeper account of why the veteran/new axis outweighs kind/origin
(§C) than this pass's own Phase 1/2 reasoning alone supported — it is not simply that operating knowledge is
peer-taught; it is that peer-taught knowledge is structurally the port's ONLY corrective to its own frozen,
unrevisable written authority.

**The schedule-protecting counterculture (§C, cross-referenced from Phase 7d) is confirmed genuinely dual.**
Five independent Zodiac Lens signs (Aries, Cancer, Scorpio, Capricorn, Pisces) each found real, distinct
resonance in this population from different registers — individualist self-possession, rejection of "any
nest," earned-loyalty peer protection, an unmet core need for protected unproductive time, and a non-punitive
informal standing economy. **This corroborates, rather than resolves, Phase 7d's own open question** (is the
tension a population or internal to each person?) — the five-sign spread supports both readings simultaneously,
which is itself the finding: this population is real enough to independently attract five separate
characterizations, not thin enough to collapse into one.

**Dual-tag for this amendment:** Genuinely emergent, deep — neither claim was available to any single
generator run in Phase 1; both required the convergence itself to become visible.
