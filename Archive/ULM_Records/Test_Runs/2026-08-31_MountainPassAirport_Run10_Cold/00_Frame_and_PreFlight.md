# Mountain Pass Airport — Frame Declaration and Pre-Flight

**Run 10. Cold. TYPE-DIVERSITY PHASE — Installation.** First test of the Installation type under this
methodology. Chosen per `RESUME_HERE.md` §2's candidate table, at the developer's direction, after a
scoping discussion in which Mountain Pass Airport was picked over Belgrano Airfield and Machu Picchu
Airport specifically **because** it has no prior Settlement identity to contaminate an Installation-type
read — the other two airports are infrastructure grafted onto an already-established city's culture.

**Session note, recorded before Phase 0 because it changed the run's own shape:** the initial scoping
conversation framed Mountain Pass as a "genuinely thin" pick, on the assumption that its only available
state was the dark, unstaffed post-war ruin. Re-reading `01_Frame_Typology_and_Inheritance.md` §4.1 (THE
DEFAULT FRAME IS NEUTRAL) before declaring the frame corrected this: absent a specific reason otherwise,
a pass defaults to the pre-war Second Interwar Period baseline. Applied here, Mountain Pass Airport was
not always dark — it was an **active, staffed joint venture** manufacturing Cradle chambers and running
a functioning airstrip, right up until Amundsen Tower's destruction cut its power. The neutral-frame
pass is therefore Band 1 (a small crew), Status Living, with real founding, function, and network-position
material — thinner than a city, considerably richer than the abandoned-outpost framing that motivated the
original pick. **This is itself a finding about the frame law's practical stakes, not just a bookkeeping
correction** — see the observations log.

## Frame Declaration

**Location:** Mountain Pass Airport (a.k.a. "the Mountain Pass outpost," "Mountain Pass" — the developer
has flagged wanting a proper name for the industrial-outpost side of this facility eventually; "Mountain
Pass Airport" is the only name currently in canon, naming the airstrip, and is used throughout this pass
for the whole site since no separate name exists).

**Type:** **Installation** (primary) + no modifiers in this pass's own frame. *(The `ruined`/`dark`
modifier belongs to the post-war state, which is explicitly out of frame here — see Temporal frame,
below.)* Purpose-built for a function (chamber manufacturing + airstrip); controlling institution (a
joint Vostok-Kunlun venture, not a single city or a national authority); staffed rather than settled.
Matches `01` §1.1's defining property exactly.

**Population band:** **Band 1 — Intimate, ~1–30.** *(PROVISIONAL, revised during Phase 1 research — see
Input Contract below; no canon figure exists.)* **Seasonal model, not a flat figure**, per the real-world
comparable researched in Phase 1 (the real-world Concordia Station, Dome C — a bi-national, extreme-
altitude joint venture with a documented seasonal crew swing; see the research log): a small skeleton
**closure-season crew (~4–8)** maintaining the chambers already in progress and the airstrip's minimum
viable state through the route's seasonal closure, and a larger **open-season crew (~15–25)** during the
months Hwy 37 is passable, when both cities can rotate staff through and shipments move. This is a
genuine research-changed finding (Gate 7), not the pass's own invention — the flat 8–20 estimate in an
earlier draft of this declaration is superseded by this seasonal model.

**Extent band:** **Band 0–1**, matching the population band. A single site — one manufacturing structure,
one airstrip, minimal ancillary building. No divergence between population and extent bands to flag; this
is the ordinary case, not the exception `01` §2 warns to watch for. *(Gate 11's population-over-area check
is run formally at Step 7 once a plausible site footprint is established — see that gate.)*

**Status:** **Seasonal / rotational**, per `01` §3 — the obligatory question this status carries (*which
population is the subject, and what does the handover feel like?*) is answered directly by the seasonal
crew-size model above: the closure-season skeleton crew and the open-season full crew are genuinely two
different populations with two different relationships to the place, and the handover between them —
timed against Hwy 37's own seasonal closure — is treated as load-bearing material throughout this pass,
not a footnote. Revised from an initial flat "Living" declaration once Phase 1's research grounded the
seasonal model; still provisional in its exact headcounts, per the Input Contract below, but no longer
provisional in kind.

**Temporal frame:** **Second Interwar Period (2564–2812 GDD), specifically its later span** — after the
Cradle network and Amundsen Tower's continent-wide regulated grid both exist and are mature, established
infrastructure (not raw and new), and **before** the Long Night War (2812) and Amundsen Tower's
destruction. Per `01` §4.1, this is the default neutral baseline, not a deliberate choice requiring
special justification — but it is also the *only* frame in which Mountain Pass Airport is a going
concern at all, which makes the default unusually load-bearing here specifically.

**Epistemic horizon:** the people here know Vostok, Kunlun, the Mirny subnet, the Cradle network, and
Amundsen Tower's regulated grid as ordinary, mature, working infrastructure — the Tower is simply "how
the grid works," not a marvel or a fear. They have no knowledge of the Long Night War, Tower's
destruction, or their own outpost's eventual permanent dark fate. **Nothing about impermanence, doom, or
"this won't last" may leak into this pass** — the outpost's own residents have no reason to think of
themselves as temporary. *(A genuine post-war examination — the dark, standing-but-inactive Mountain
Pass, the Calethina-chamber discoverable thread — is legitimate future work, built ON TOP of this pass,
never folded into it, per `01` §4 rule 2.)*

**Parent:** **No single parent.** Mountain Pass Airport is co-administered by **Vostok and Kunlun jointly**
— a structurally unusual position, closer to a shared subsidiary than a child location. It sits inside the
**Mirny Subnet** (both founding cities' own subnet), and physically on **Hwy 37 (the Mountain Cut
Throughway)**, between Kunlun and Vostok. **Provisional assumption, stated explicitly per `01` §5.2:** this
pass assumes the Mirny Subnet supplies the outpost's Determined properties (currency, calendar, language
family, the subnet's own Arcanet affiliation) exactly as it would for either founding city, and that
neither Vostok nor Kunlun individually outranks the other in matters the joint venture itself decides.
Where the two founding cities' own established character disagrees, that disagreement is itself Phase 1
material (see the generator-conflict section), not a tie to break here.

**Children:** None.

**Sibling set:** **None.** Mountain Pass Airport is the only joint-venture industrial waypoint of its
kind in Tepenia — no other location shares its specific combination of dual-city ownership, Installation
type, and non-city status. Per `01` §5.3a, this is the ordinary case, not a degraded one. **Substitutes
used, per the ranked list:**
1. **Own earlier states** — weak. The outpost's founding *is* close to its only state in this frame; there
   is no long pre-founding history to differentiate against. Not the primary substitute here, unusually.
2. **Nearest analogous location at a different scale** — **Byrd's Chamber Works**, the only other
   documented Cradle-manufacturing physical site with an actual described facility (a reinforced section
   of Byrd's own underground plant complex). Used throughout Phase 1 and Phase 7.
3. **Real-world comparables** — run per `Real-World_Basis_Extrapolation_Method.md`; see the research log.
4. **The generator-conflict method** (`02` §5) — the primary substitute for this pass, since two
   independent founding populations (Vostok's, Kunlun's) supply a naturally occurring conflict-generator
   without needing an external sibling at all.

**Written:** ALONE (default).

**Configuration:** **EXCEPTIONAL**, in four distinct ways, each named because each is load-bearing for a
specific set of findings:
1. **Dual-city founding and administration**, rather than a single founding population. Findings that
   depend on this: the composition read (Phase 2), the governance read (Phase 7b), and the capability
   profile's own conflict-resolution (Phase 1).
2. **No prior culture-pass conclusions exist for this location at all** — not even a stub, `Local_Culture`
   file, census entry, or symbol assignment. This is exceptional in the *opposite* direction from every
   prior contamination concern this methodology has recorded: there is nothing to quarantine, because
   nothing has ever been written. Genuinely useful for a cold-pass test, and stated so it is not mistaken
   for an oversight in this pass's own admissibility work.
3. **The founding populations are asymmetric on a specific, checkable axis**: Kunlun's Census I population
   is **0 humans / 123,449 robots** — entirely robot. Vostok's is mixed (129,617 humans / 259,644 robots,
   Census I). A jointly-staffed outpost drawing from both cities inherits this asymmetry directly, and it
   is treated as load-bearing for Phase 2 and Phase 9.
4. **Small, highly technical population doing precision work**, not survival or extraction labor — this
   changes which real-world comparables are appropriate (a research/technical outpost, not a mining camp)
   and is stated explicitly so Phase 1's research step reaches for the right comparison class.

**Provisional assumptions about the parent (Mirny Subnet / joint Vostok-Kunlun administration):**
1. The Mirny Subnet supplies Determined properties (currency, calendar, language family) identically to
   how it does for Vostok and Kunlun themselves — no subnet-level override specific to this outpost exists
   in canon, and none is invented here.
2. Vostok and Kunlun hold genuinely joint, non-hierarchical authority over the outpost's own internal
   governance — canon states the venture was "jointly established and staffed" by both cities' engineers,
   with no city named as senior partner. This pass treats that as a real 50/50 structure rather than
   resolving it toward either city, and flags the tension this creates in Phase 7b.

**Generators available:** all eight (`02` §2). **Generators selected: G2 (physical & environmental
constraint), G3 (function & purpose), G4 (founding condition), G5 (network position), G8 (demographic
composition)** — five selected, more than the required three, chosen for genuine independence: G2 and G5
are both near-universal and independent of the founding story; G3 restates the founding purpose in a
different register but is kept because G3's "what does the parent need" half diverges sharply from G4's
"why was it built" half here (see Phase 1); G4 and G8 are independent because the founding *reason*
(fabrication capacity) and the founding *population* (who actually came) are not the same fact for a
dual-city venture. **G1 (symbolic substrate) and G7 (real-world inspiration) are not selected as primary
spine generators** — G1 because no symbol system has ever been assigned to this location and inventing
one is out of scope for a first cold pass (recorded as a REQUESTED item, not silently skipped); G7 because
research is run in support of the deficits Phase 1 actually names, per Step 2's "do not research yet"
rule, not as an independent profile generator. **G6 (defining event)** is explicitly **not** run as a
spine generator in this frame — the Long Night War and Tower's destruction are the outpost's defining
*post-war* event and are out of frame per the neutral-frame law; within this frame's own boundary, the
outpost has no defining event yet, which is itself recorded rather than papered over.

**Reserved decisions this pass must not foreclose:**
- **A proper name for the outpost itself**, distinct from "Mountain Pass Airport" (which names the
  airstrip). Already flagged as developer-desired in existing canon; this pass uses a working designation
  and does not settle one.
- **Proper names of any named individual.** Phase 10 people-entries stay role/archetype placeholders.
- **Whether the outpost's staffing model is genuinely rotational or permanent** — treated as provisional
  throughout, per the Status declaration above, and named as a REQUESTED item.
- **Belgrano's and Rothera's own still-open Cradle-manufacturer candidacy** — unrelated to this pass, not
  touched.
