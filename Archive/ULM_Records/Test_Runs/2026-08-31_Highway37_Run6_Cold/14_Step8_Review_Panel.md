# Step 8 — The Review Panel

**Run 2026-08-31**, per `00f_Review_Panel.md`. Six Flat Archetypes cast for Highway 37, plus the mandatory
Passer-Through and Neighbor, the Lover faculty's standing question, and relevant Life Arcs and Shadows.

---

## Panel A — the Flat Archetypes

**The Child** — *cannot be cast at all.* No child has ever existed anywhere along this corridor: Mountain Pass
Airport's population is entirely adult, assigned, posted staff (Phase 2 §A), and the corridor itself has no
resident population of any kind. **This is a strong finding in its own right, per `00f` §7's own precedent
(the Frostlands' uncastable Neighbor)** — the absence of a standpoint confirms, from a completely different
angle, the same Band-0/Band-1 reading Phase 0 already established. **Recorded as a result, not substituted
around.**

**The Lover** (cast as: two crew members, both posted, in a relationship) — *asks: could I build a life with
someone here, and where would we actually go?* **Objection, addition:** privacy is genuinely scarce in a small,
shared, enclosed common space (Phase 3 §D), and — sharper than the space question — **nobody's actual home is
here.** Every relationship formed at Mountain Pass Airport is bounded by the same rotation clock that bounds
everything else (Phase 4 §B). → **`unmet`.** Per the panel's own governing test (would satisfying this make the
location more like a settled place?) — yes, and a Corridor's whole identity (Phase 1's cost-dominant shape,
Phase 11's Pisces reading) depends on *not* being a place anyone builds a permanent life around. **The refusal
is written as characterization**: this is not a place for building a life, and the corridor does not pretend
otherwise.

**The Parent** (cast as: a posted worker with a dependent back in Vostok or Kunlun) — *asks: who has my child
during a crisis?* **Objection, addition, but out of this pass's own scope:** the answer lives entirely in
Vostok's or Kunlun's own childcare arrangements, neither of which this pass researched (they belong to those
cities' own passes, not this one). → **`noted`** — real, but explicitly deferred, not this location's own
material to resolve.

**The Ruler** (cast as: whichever city's logistics office currently has responsibility for a given shipment) —
*asks: where does it come from, who settles up?* **Objection, inconsistency — but already covered, not new:**
this is exactly the unadministrable governance gap Phase 7b already found from a completely different
direction (the diffuse-parent generator reading). → **`accepted`, folded into the existing Phase 7b finding**
rather than producing a standalone one, per `00f` §8's own note that this is an equally good outcome.

**The Elder** (cast as: a long-serving robot crew member who has completed many more rotations than the
rotation-clock norm would suggest, given established robot longevity) — *asks: was that always true?* **A
genuine addition, not previously derived:** per this project's established robot-longevity canon, **a robot
crew member could plausibly hold institutional memory across dozens of rotations in a way no human posting
ever could** — an Elder figure the rotation-clock system itself does not anticipate, since the whole mechanism
(Phase 5 §5d) assumes turnover. → **`accepted`.** New finding: **the rotation-clock system has a quiet
exception built into it by robot longevity alone**, worth carrying forward as a REQUESTED item for whoever
eventually writes named characters for this outpost.

**The Mentor** (cast as: whoever is currently pairing with a first-rotation crew member) — *asks: how does
someone learn this, and what becomes of the ones who can't?* **Already covered directly**, Phase 7c. →
**`accepted`, folded into the existing finding.**

## Panel B — Life Arcs, pulled in selectively, silences recorded

**The Maiden** (leaving the parental home) — **silent.** No young person has ever separated here (per the
uncastable Child, above); the passage this arc asks about cannot occur at this location at all.

**The King** (sacrificing personal power for the realm) — **silent.** Per Phase 7d's own finding (no organized
population, nobody to hold or cede power over), there is no "realm" here for anyone to sacrifice power over.

**Both silences are consistent with each other and with the pass's own established character** — not scattered
gaps, but the predictable shape of a genuinely Band 0-1, rotation-clocked, ungoverned location.

---

## Panel C/D — Shadows

**The Denying "Innocent" One** (cast against the power-dependency shadow, Phase 5 §5e / Zodiac Lens Scorpio) —
*asks: what does this place refuse to know that it already knows?* **Verdict: not guilty, with the conditions
for guilt stated.** Vostok's and Kunlun's own engineering staffs are not in denial about the Tower-grid
dependency — they actively monitor and manage it (Phase 4 §B). **It would become guilty only if they knew
Concordia or the wider Federation depended on their silence and chose not to correct it** — no evidence
supports that; the more honest reading (already reached independently by the Zodiac Lens's contradiction check,
file 11) is mutual non-communication rather than active concealment on either side.

---

## The Lover faculty's question, run every time

*Is this place alive, and could anyone love it?* **Answered honestly, not flattered:** the corridor itself is
not lovable on its own terms — cold, textureless, unwitnessed (Phase 3 §C, Panel A's Child finding). **But what
people build inside it might be**: the Vostok–Kunlun correspondence custom, the one-person-deep robot-coffee
practice, "Waypoint" played in a convoy cab. **The honest answer is that the corridor is not loved; small,
specific things people do within it might be** — a real, unflattering, and characteristically correct answer
for a Band 0-1 Corridor, not the same answer a settled district would give.

---

## The Passer-Through

**Cast as a convoy driver crossing once, never returning.** **What they see:** exactly what is there — the
climb, the empty plateau, the one warm interior at Mountain Pass, the corridor's own plain functional purpose.
**No hidden menace is discovered, and none should be** — the corridor's real vulnerability (the power
dependency) is not the kind of thing a single crossing would ever have reason to notice, consistent with `00d`'s
own requirement that a shadow be genuinely unnoticed rather than dramatically hidden. **PASS** on the
discoverability test — the surface is true, nothing is oversold, nothing sinister lurks in plain sight.

## The Neighbor

**Cast as a traveler who uses Hwy 22 (the Transcontinental Highway) instead** — biased by design. **Objection:**
Hwy 37 is the unglamorous, lesser road, carrying no prestige. **Already covered**, Phase 5 §5a's own
quiet-friction finding. → **`accepted`, folded into the existing finding**, not standalone.

---

## Four-corner check

**Did the objections come from genuinely different directions?** The Child's silence, the Lover's `unmet`, the
Ruler's `accepted`-but-covered, the Elder's genuinely new addition, and the Denying Innocent One's not-guilty
verdict **all approach the location from different standpoints and land on different outcomes** — no single
objection is wearing three hats here. **Confirmed as a genuine four-corner (in practice, six-plus-corner) run,**
not a collapsed single lens.
