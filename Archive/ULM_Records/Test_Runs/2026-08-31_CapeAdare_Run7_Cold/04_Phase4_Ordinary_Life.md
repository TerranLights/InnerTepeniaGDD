# Phase 4 — Ordinary Life

**Named axis: "arrival is everyone's business, not a department's."** Mandatory, promoted early per `03`'s own
instruction. **Headline function, stated in one sentence: Cape Adare is the Ross Sea's first-landfall gateway
and a historic heritage site.** Writing away from it, per Phase 4's core move — and per the self-origination
constraint (`00`'s exclusion of the Character & Culture section), every finding below is independently derived
from admissible facts, not restated from that section's own claims about pace or community character.

---

## A. Routines

**Movement itself is routed, not incidental.** Per Phase 3 §C's boardwalk-corridor finding, an ordinary
resident's commute — to work, to a neighbor, anywhere — runs through fixed, marked, rookery-avoiding paths as a
basic fact of getting around, not a tourist experience. **This is genuinely different from most Tepenian
cities' ordinary movement**, where the built environment doesn't have to negotiate with a quarter-million-plus
resident wildlife population.

**Storm-watching as background competence, not a special skill.** Given Phase 1's first-hit-by-weather finding,
an ordinary resident plausibly tracks incoming systems the way someone elsewhere tracks traffic — not out of
civic duty, simply because the city is exposed enough that everyone develops the habit.

## B. Mundane concerns

**Whose custom is today's default is a live, ongoing question, not a settled fact.** Per Phase 1's own DEFICIT
finding (no dominant national bloc) and Phase 2's arrival-order mismatch, Cape Adare has no single majority
culture setting the ambient default the way most cities do. **The mundane, unglamorous cost of this**: small,
constant, low-stakes negotiation — which holiday gets the bigger civic acknowledgment this year, whose food is
"the" food at a shared gathering — plays out at the level of ordinary daily interaction rather than being
settled once and repeated. **Not dramatic, not a conflict** — simply a genuine, ongoing texture this city's own
demographic shape produces that a majority-anchored city doesn't have.

## C. Personal struggles

**Working a gateway economy means an uneven, arrival-dependent rhythm.** Per the admissible (if thin) Economy &
Industry section, Cape Adare's economic base is maritime trade and the logistics of arrival and departure —
work here is plausibly tied to shipping and sea-ice conditions (Phase 1's unpredictable-transition finding),
producing real stretches of feast-or-famine activity rather than a steady, evenly-distributed workload.

## D. Escapism / downtime

**Watching the horizon is already a form of downtime, not only a duty.** Given Cape Adare's own first-landfall,
first-storm-warning position (Phase 1 §D), a plausible, low-cost communal pastime is simply **tracking and
informally wagering on what arrives next** — the next ship, the next storm system, whichever comes first.
**This is derived directly from the corridor's own network-position finding, not imported generically** — a
gateway city's own idle hours plausibly orient toward the one thing its position uniquely gives it early sight
of.

## E. Would this be someone's entire day, forever?

**No, and the variance is real**: a maritime-logistics worker's day, a rookery-corridor engineer's or
maintainer's day, and a day spent in the ongoing cross-cultural negotiation of §B are all genuinely different
days, not one archetype repeated. **Differentiation axis restated: nothing here has a single default resident,
because nothing here has a single default culture to produce one.**
