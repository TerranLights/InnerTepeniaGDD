# Phase 7 — Order

**Named axis: "nobody is vested with settling who was really first."** Mandatory.

---

## 7a — Work and economy

**Division of labor**: maritime logistics (admissible Economy & Industry section) as the headline. **The
non-thematic export, required by the phase**: guano — an ordinary, unglamorous, high-volume byproduct of a
rookery this size, historically a genuine commercial resource in the real world at far smaller colonies than
Cape Adare's. **Entirely unrelated to the heritage/gateway headline**, proving the economy is more than
allegory, per the phase's own requirement.

## 7b — Governance and decision

**What is unadministrable, per the phase's own most productive question**: per Phase 1's "no dominant anchor"
deficit and Phase 6 §D's failure-state finding, **nobody is vested with authority to arbitrate a dispute over
founding precedence** should one ever arise — there is no dominant cultural authority positioned to rule on
"who was really first," and no admissible canon establishes any body that could. **A genuine, load-bearing gap
rather than an invented crisis**: the condition for this gap to matter (Phase 2's arrival-order mismatch) is
real; whether it has ever actually mattered is not established, and this pass does not assert that it has.

## 7c — Knowledge and transmission

**How someone learns the boardwalk-corridor system (Phase 3 §C)**: plausibly universal basic knowledge, learned
in childhood the way a street-crossing rule is learned elsewhere, not a specialized trade skill — Cape Adare's
population is stable and resident (Phase 5 §5d), not rotation-clocked, so this is lived-in knowledge rather
than onboarding material. **What becomes of someone who cannot navigate it independently**: per the project's
standing non-malice discipline, the plausible answer is an ordinary accommodation (assisted or guided routes),
not exclusion — **not established in admissible canon, but consistent with every other non-punitive finding
this pass has produced, and offered as the most consistent reading rather than invented freely.**

## 7d — Private life, minorities, and counterculture

**Check canon first**: no counterculture is established in admissible material. **Derive from the specific
pressure**: per Phase 4 §B, Cape Adare requires ongoing participation in cross-cultural negotiation — nobody
gets to simply assume their own heritage is the unmarked default, because no heritage numerically dominates
enough to be one. **Who cannot or will not give it?** A population that maintains a strong, deliberately
single-heritage household or community rather than participating in the city's broader, constantly-negotiated
blend — **not a refusal of the city's demands in general, but a refusal of this one specific demand
(participate in the negotiation) that the city's own demographic shape makes unusually pointed here.** **Kept
sympathetic, not criminal, per the phase's own instruction**: these are people for whom the negotiation itself
is exhausting or unwanted, not people opposed to anyone else's presence.

**Sanction proportionality, checked**: nothing in this pass's admissible material suggests any penalty attaches
to this choice at all — it reads as a quiet personal/communal preference, not a violation of anything
enforceable, and this pass does not invent a sanction where the evidence doesn't support one existing.
