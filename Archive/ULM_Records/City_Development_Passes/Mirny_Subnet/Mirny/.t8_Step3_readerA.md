## 1 · The seven `[V]` targets Step 2 handed forward

### T1 — Where drift settles in the lee under katabatic flow

**Came back (primary: Tabler, *Snow Fence Guide*, SHRP-W/FR-91-106, text extracted locally):**
- Deposition happens where the wind is slowed. There is a *"Recirculation zone: A region of recirculating wind formed immediately downwind of the slip-face, or any solid barrier."*
- Drifts grow to an equilibrium and then stop: *"When a fence has filled to capacity, the drift is said to have reached equilibrium. At this stage, the fence has no more effect on blowing snow."* UWyo summary of the same work: drifts *"reach a distinctive shape, and a size that does not grow. Blizzards pass without losing snow."*
- For a 50 %-porous fence: maximum depth 1 to 1.2 × fence height (H) near 6H downwind; final length 30 to 35H; upwind drift out to 15H, about 0.5H deep.
- *"Snow fences too close to the road can increase the amount of snow on the road!"* The minimum setback is 35H.
- *"End effect: The lack of snow deposition near the ends of a snow fence caused by wind scouring."*
- *"the angle can vary by as much as 25° without affecting performance."*
- *"Most blowing snow travels within 6 ft. (1.8 m) of the surface."* From Table 1, the share of transport (within the lowest 5 m) that moves in the lowest 0.5 m is **88.5 % at 10 m/s, 51.9 % at 20 m/s, and 33.8 % at 30 m/s.**
- *"Solid fences do not collect snow efficiently."* A search summary (S23) says a solid barrier's downwind drift reaches 10H and that the upwind side fills first. **The 10H figure is not in the Tabler text: unverified.**
- Norilsk "Potapov shields" (sever-press; gw2ru): angled wooden barriers so that *"the wind emerges from underneath them with such force that it would sweep the snow off a road."* Three types (snow-transferring, snow-retaining, directional). Before building one, you *"study the blizzard"*: snow properties, wind direction and variability, and the amount transported. The angle is computed per road section.
- Mirny's wind: *"restricted to the southeast quadrant"* (science-start, tool translation). *"The easterly-south-easterly winds predominate"* (AARI, via search summary S4).

**What follows:**
- **1st order.** A drift is set by the height of its obstacle and the direction of the wind, not by any one storm. It grows to a fixed size and then stops growing.
- **2nd order. The drift "regenerates" (Step 2, G2 Standing Cost) because it is cleared.** Left alone, a lee drift reaches equilibrium, and every later blizzard passes over it. Cleared, it refills. The standing cost is therefore a choice made again after every storm: clear the lee and pay forever, or leave it and lose the lee as usable ground.
- **3rd order. The wind splits into a half that can be written down and a half that cannot.** With the wind held to one quadrant (±25° is tolerated), each structure's drift footprint can be predicted in advance from its height. That is exactly the kind of knowledge a record holds well. Onset, visibility and whether it is safe to walk now cannot be written that way (T2). This sharpens Step 2's conflict *"Authority lives in writing; danger lives in the wind"*: **the written record is competent about where the snow will lie and incompetent about when the air will close.**
- **`[V]` "Out of the wind is under the snow" — CONFIRMED for solid obstacles.** Shelter and deposition are the same place: the lee's recirculation zone. The bare ground is at the scoured ends and corners, which are in the wind. In a storm, a path is either bare and exposed or sheltered and deep.
- **The same wind is two regimes at two heights.** At Mirny's mean wind (Step 2: 12.0 m/s; AARI: 11.2 m/s), most moving snow is below the knee (drifting snow, under 2 m). Only at storm speeds does a large share rise into the face (blowing snow, over 2 m). The drifting-versus-blowing split maps onto Mirny's ordinary day versus its storm.
- The lee can be engineered to scour rather than fill (Potapov), but only after the specific site's wind has been studied, and it needs a steady direction, which Mirny has. **This is a possibility handed forward, not a Mirny fact.**

**Holes (not inferred):** Mirny-specific drift rates. Building-scale drift at the real station is site history and withheld (GPS). The solid-barrier 10H figure.

### T2 — Clear-sky whiteout: how deep is the blowing-snow layer, and can it be seen coming?

**Came back:**
- Blowing snow starts at a *"threshold velocity [of] about 5 to 8 m s−1"*, depending on *"age, temperature, density, sphericity, and cohesiveness"* of the surface snow (Palm et al., IntechOpen). **Mirny's mean wind is above that threshold.**
- *"the average height of the layers is about 120 m, but they can range from just a few meters to over 400 m in depth"* (IntechOpen). The CALIPSO range is 30–1000 m (search summaries S13/S14).
- *"Blowing snow is prevalent in all months except November through February, though in these summer months, it still occurs at a reduced frequency"* (IntechOpen).
- Satellite detection works only where the lidar reaches the ground, i.e. with no overlying cloud (IntechOpen). **Blowing snow is observed under clear skies by construction.**
- Katabatic jump: where the flow meets the break of slope at the coast it undergoes a hydraulic jump, forming *"100-1000 m high, highly localized 'walls' of snow in the air in an otherwise cloud-free sky"* that extend more than 400 km along the coast. **This comes from two concordant search summaries of an EGU 2021 abstract. Both fetches failed, so it is not verified at the source.**
- Piteraq (East Greenland; WHOI Oceanus, verbatim): *"The sky suddenly becomes clear—indicating that the wind has shifted direction and is now coming from the mountains and the vast Greenland Ice Sheet beyond."*
- Katabatic onset warms the surface: *"The onset of strong drainage flows is commonly accompanied by a surface temperature increase … onset and termination … typically abrupt, elevated air temperatures remain for days afterward."* The mechanism is adiabatic warming plus mixing that brings inversion air down (S18 summary). In the Vihma et al. 2011 model, *"for slope heights of 1500 m and more, temperature increased down the slope"* (S20 summary; paper 403). The ice surface south of Mirny *"elevates to a height of 1.5 km at a distance of 100 km"* (AARI via S4).
- There are two whiteouts (Wikipedia, *Whiteout*). One is light blocked by *"falling snow, wind-blown spin-drift … or localised fog."* The other is **flat light**, from light *"scattered and diffused … received from multiple directions."* A first-person account of a flat-light whiteout (*Vokrug Sveta*, "Белая тьма"; location unstated) ends when the wind rises: *"Rescuing wind dispersed the milky haze"* (tool translation).

**What follows:**
- **1st order. The clear sky is not a lie. It is an accurate signal that air is coming off the ice, and that air lifts snow.** The same goes for the mild thermometer: onset warms the surface. Both cues Step 2 lists under Grudging Tolerance as "ordinary signals" are, physically, **honest precursors of the wind. They are misread, not false.**
- **2nd order. The deficit moves; it does not shrink.** Step 2 says *"The sky lies here … and so does the air."* The research says the sky and the air tell the truth in a language nobody at the founding had learned. "No inherited judgment of the wind" survives intact and gets sharper: **the missing thing is a reading, not a cue.** (This is a tension for Step 2's owner, §9-1. Step 3 does not rewrite the spine.)
- **3rd order. Mirny has two whiteouts with opposite relations to the wind.** Under a clear sky the wind makes the whiteout. Under overcast in calm air the wind cures it. The same cue, "the wind is rising", means danger in one and rescue in the other. **A written rule cannot carry this** ("wind rising, go inside" is wrong half the time). Only a reader who can tell the two skies apart can.
- **Depth.** A layer averaging ~120 m is shallow against the sky and deep against buildings. Height gives a view of the layer arriving, not a way out of it, because the danger is visibility at eye level (2 m), not the top of the layer.
- **Can it be seen coming? Partly.** (a) The sky clears first (the piteraq sign; same physics). (b) The surface warms. (c) Where a katabatic jump forms, it is a visible wall in a clear sky. **No source found says whether a jump forms at Mirny's coast, or what an approaching surge looks like from the city.** Upwind observers meet it first, which is the input to UI-1.

**Holes:** Mirny-specific onset cues. Mirny-specific blowing-snow frequency (the 70–75 % winter figures are for other longitudes). Whether onset warming is measured at Mirny.

### T3 — Winter-solstice day length at 66°33′S

**Came back:**
- The station is at *"66°33′11″S 93°00′35″E"* (Wikipedia). The Antarctic Circle *"currently runs 66°33′51.1″ south of the Equator"* and is *"drifting southwards at a speed of about 14.5 m (48 ft) per year"* (Wikipedia). **The site is about 40″ (~1.2 km) north of the circle, and the circle is moving away from it.**
- sunrise-sunset.org API, 66.553°S 93.010°E, 21 June 2026 (raw JSON): sunrise 04:34:50 UTC, sunset 07:04:37 UTC, `day_length` 8987 s (**2 h 29 m 47 s**). Civil twilight 02:51:41–08:47:45 UTC (~5 h 56 m). Nautical twilight 01:31:35–10:07:52 UTC.
- **My computation** (NOAA declination series, daily, 2026). With standard horizon h0 = −0.833°: **solstice minimum 2.15 h · June mean 2.59 h · May mean 5.52 h · July mean 4.0 h · continuous sun 8 Dec → 5 Jan = 29 days.** With h0 = −1.1°: minimum 2.48 h, June mean 2.87 h, continuous sun 6 Dec → 7 Jan (33 days).
- At local noon on the solstice, the geometric altitude of the sun's center is about **+0.01°**. The midwinter day exists only because of refraction and the size of the solar disk.
- Novaya Zemlya effect (Wikipedia): under long inversion paths, refraction can raise the apparent sun by up to ~5°.
- Gaisma (−66.55°) lists June as 24-hour darkness. That is geometrically impossible north of the circle. **Not used.**

**What follows:**
- Step 2's *"~29 days of continuous light, Dec 8 → Jan 5"* is **confirmed exactly** under standard refraction. *"No polar night"* is **confirmed.** *"June still averages 2.4 h"* is **not reproduced**: I compute a 2.59 h mean and a 2.15 h minimum. The figure sits inside the ±0.3 h refraction band, so this is flagged, not contested (§9-4).
- **Refining "The calendar tells the truth; the hour lies":** the calendar tells the truth about how much light the season will bring. **At midwinter, the exact length of the day is set by the air.** Ordinary variation in refraction moves it by about 0.3 h, and an inversion can move it further. Whether the wind (which destroys inversions, T2) shortens the midwinter day is **not inferred** (OT-15).
- Derived geometry, to be earned or cut later: at midwinter the sun grazes the northern horizon over the sea ice, and the wind comes from the south or southeast off the plateau. **Facing the midwinter sun puts the wind at your back.**

### T4 — Katabatic lulls by season and hour

**Came back:**

**What follows:**

### T5 — The sea-ice window and wind closures

**Came back:**
- First signs of ice early March. Stable ice formation begins, on average, in the second ten days of March *"due to the storm autumn winds"* (AARI via S24). It takes about a month from the start of stable formation to established fast ice (S25).

- **Break-up *"between 27 January and 9 March (средняя дата 13 февраля)"*** (science-start; also S25 summary). Average 13 February.
- Thickness 160–200 cm along the coast (S25 summary only; **unverified at source**).

- The katabatic dies 8–10 km offshore (Repina).

**What follows. The unstated half of "ice from March to October" was asked here, as M-158 requires:**
- **November to February is not open sea at the shore.** The fast ice outlasts the calendar summer and breaks up around 13 February on average. **Open water at the shore runs, on average, from mid-February to the middle of March: about four to five weeks.** In a late year (break-up 9 March) it is roughly a week.
- **The wind plays two roles at sea.** In autumn, storm winds delay freeze-up and so lengthen the open-water window. In season, gales close ship work.
- **The harbor's readiness is an ice-road's readiness.** In December and January the ship stands at a fast-ice edge that has been tens of kilometers wide, and cargo crosses ice whose bearing capacity must be checked. **The harbor watch's work in that season is reading ice, which is another tacit-judgment job.**

### T6 — Whether storms affect relay equipment

**Came back:**
- *"Static interference on radio antennas prompted early investigations of charge separation in blizzards"* (Schmidt, Schmidt & Dent 1999, *Boundary-Layer Meteorology*, text extracted locally). *"Field mill readings 4 cm above the surface in a moderate blizzard showed electric field strength as high as +30 kV m−1."* *"During blizzards, the atmospheric electric field reverses direction and its magnitude increases."* Particle charge shows *"strong positive correlations … with wind speed and particle flux"* (Schmidt 1992, as cited there).
- Precipitation static from blowing snow *"can raise the noise power at least 50 db above atmospheric and man-made levels and can cause loss of such radio data 10–30% of the time in winter and spring months,"* at eight fixed frequencies from 51 kc/s to 20 Mc/s (Herman 1965, doi:10.1029/AR004p0221; S33 summary). **That was measured at an inland ice-sheet station: the mechanism transfers, the percentage does not.**
- Triboelectric RF noise: onset wind speed *"typically O(10 m/s)"*; *"an apparent preference for discharges from above-surface structures"*; spectra at the low end of 100–200 MHz (arXiv:2103.06079).
- Rime needs supercooled droplets from fog, low cloud or coastal weather (S32 summary). **Katabatic air is dry, so icing belongs to the moist cyclonic wind, not the katabatic one** (inference from mechanism; no Mirny rime data found).
- Mirny sits at corrected geomagnetic latitude **~77.2–77.3°S** (S35 summary: IAGA code MIR), which is the **polar cap**. A polar cap absorption event *"results from the ionisation of the D-layer of the polar ionosphere by high energy protons,"* *"causes a HF radio blackout for trans polar circuits and can last several days,"* and begins *"few minutes to several hours"* after the flare (Australian Bureau of Meteorology Space Weather Services). Mean durations of ~8 h (moderate) and 1.6 days (severe) come from the S36 summary only; the source returned 403 (**unverified**).

**What follows (conditional: the relay's in-world technology is not in my admitted set, so this assumes it has exposed radiating structures):**
- **1st order. Three attacks with three signatures.** (a) Static noise rises with wind speed and blowing-snow flux, so it is **worst exactly when the ground storm is worst.** (b) Wind loading and icing, and icing comes with the moist cyclonic wind. (c) Geomagnetic blackout, **which has no connection to local weather: it can arrive under a calm clear sky** and last from hours to days.
- **2nd order. "Signal, not body" (Step 2 conflict): dependence and degradation peak together.** The relay is noisiest at the moment the city most needs it to decide when people move.
- **3rd order. The inverse of the whiteout.** Whiteout is the eye failing under a clear sky. A geomagnetic blackout is **the explicit medium failing under perfect weather**, and no wind watch can see it coming. Mirny's two doubled strengths (Step 2 §5: *"the record, the relay"*) each have a failure that arrives in good conditions.

## 2 · 3.1 Research the deficit: who has judgment of the wind?

The spine's deficit: **no inherited judgment of the wind** (Step 2 §4 SPINE row; §6 #5). Three real cultures that have it. The contrast writes the finding.

**A. East Greenland: the piteraq** (*"that which attacks you"* in East Greenlandic; northtrotter).
- The cue is the clear sky (WHOI, verbatim, T2). *"Village dogs start barking furiously."* The town's answer is a public switch: *"an official warning system that sounds an alarm when a piteraq is forecast and completely shuts down the town until the piteraq subsides"* (WHOI), issued 24 hours ahead (northtrotter). Residents should *"be prepared to stay home for several days"* (ESKP). There are 1–3 severe events and dozens of weaker ones a year, mostly in autumn and winter (Wikipedia).
- **Contrast:** *"piteraqs are well known to Greenlanders, but they have not been well studied by scientists"* (WHOI). **That is Mirny's inverse: Mirny has the study (the record) and not the knowing.** The missing reading is short, one sentence about a clearing sky. That is exactly the kind of knowledge that goes unwritten because everyone local already has it, and so it never reaches a record.

**B. Igloolik (Inuit): the ground as compass.**
- Four named winds, of which Uangnaq (WNW) and Nigiq (ESE) prevail. Uqalurait are tongue-shaped drifts that *"always point in the direction of Uangnaq"*. In low visibility: *"all you have to do is kick around the fresh snow to get at the uqaluraq form below."* Qimugjuk are lee ridges behind obstacles, aligned with the same wind (Anijaarniq, elders' testimony).
- Aporta's GPS finding (popsci excerpt): *"It was younger hunters who tended to rely the most on GPS"*; GPS tracks run *"straight as an arrow"*; the connection to the surroundings becomes *"shallow"*. An elder's snow-reading kept a rescue from following GPS *"straight into a dangerous landscape and the floe edge."* The holders of the knowledge *"were in their seventies or eighties."*
- **Contrast:** the wind that blinds also writes its direction on the ground, and a culture with judgment reads it with the feet when the eyes fail (→ UI-3). **Igloolik's relay-trust (GPS) erodes a skill that exists. Mirny's relay-trust sits where no skill ever existed** (G4: no living teacher). **Igloolik is erosion; Mirny is absence.**

**C. The Adriatic: the bora** (Wikipedia).
- *"light bora"* comes *"in the presence of anticyclone clear skies"*, and *"dark bora"* with *"cyclone clouds … with rain or snow."* *"Chains and ropes are occasionally stretched along the sidewalks in downtown Trieste."*
- **Contrast:** a people living with a downslope wind name its kinds by the sky that comes with them. Mirny's own sources give **at least three kinds**: the clear katabatic, the cloudy cyclonic, and the combined one, which is the worst (AARI: strongest *"when the vectors of katabatic and barometric winds coincide"*). **The record holds wind speed. A vocabulary would hold wind kinds.** Mirny has the first and not the second.

## 3 · 3.2 Substitute institutions: places that publish a threshold in place of an individual's judgment

**A. Norilsk: Storm HQ and *aktirovka*** (goarctic; school pages; tool translations).
- Storm HQ (*штаб «Шторм»*, mid-1980s) is inter-agency: traffic police, city administration, road services, rescuers. Its thresholds are wind **> 20 m/s**, visibility **< 50 m**, and *"weather severity"* = **T − 2V** (each m/s counts as two degrees of frost). Roads close and buses run in convoy. Stranded people shelter in the city Palace of Culture with *"water and tea"*. Ropes along walls appear in historical accounts. **Meanwhile the plant's working group runs "five teams on 12-hour shifts" for "maintaining uninterrupted plant operations."**
- *Aktirovka* is a weather day formalized by an act. Attendance may be skipped, but *"the final decision on attendance is made by parents."* It is announced by the city's automated phone line "007" at **06:30 and 11:30**. *"During storm warnings, apply the maximum value of the chart."* ***"Complaints about discrepancies between announced and actual weather conditions are not reviewed."***
- **What it substitutes:** individual judgment becomes one number, at a named hour, from one voice. The individual keeps the last word but has **no recourse against the number.** This is Step 2's *"authority lives in writing"*, formalized: the announced figure governs even when the street disagrees.
- **Contrast with Mirny's conflict, "people must stop; the relay must not":** Norilsk's answer is **a roster.** The city stops and a designated crew does not. The substitute for "stopping" is a split in the population.
- Derived: on Norilsk's index, **Mirny's average conditions score −35.2** (−11.2 °C − 2 × 12.0 m/s, using the READER mean and the spec mean wind as Step 2 cites them). The index is linear, so the annual mean of T − 2V equals exactly that. Earn or cut later.

**B. Yakutsk: a table built for calm cold** (ysia.ru).
- Readings from two stations (*"Gimein area"* and *"Airport"*) at **06:00**, taking the lowest. Tiers: grades 1–5 cancel at −45 calm, −44 at 1 m/s, −43 at 2 m/s, −42 at 3–5 m/s. Grades 1–8: −48 → −45. All grades: −50 → −47.
- **Contrast:** the wind column ends at 5 m/s, which is where Mirny's mean wind is barely half reached. **The form cannot be transplanted.** A Mirny threshold would have to be a wind table with temperature as the minor term. Kept for later: *"lowest of two instruments governs"* (the worst reading wins).

**C. Potapov shields (Norilsk, 1944–45)** (T1). An engineered structure that encodes one site's wind and turns a lee into a scour. It substitutes a built object for the judgment of drift, and it works only where the wind is directionally steady, a condition Mirny meets.

**D. Chongqing, 1938–43: a graded forward-observer warning.**
- *"hundreds of people served as air-defense spotters"* relayed by *"smoke signals … hand-crank sirens, flag signals … and telephone"*. Red lanterns hung on Pipa Mountain (370 m) went from *"2 … quickly … 3"* to show the alert level. Balls were hung *"on tall buildings across the urban area"*, and green meant all clear. Tracking departures gave *"approximately 4 hours warning"* (sina blog; 12371.gov.cn; tool translations). **The mapping "one ball = far post, two balls = near post" appears only in the S58 summary (unverified).**
- The fog season, October to May, was protection: *"Heavy fog locked the city, making it disadvantageous for Japanese bombing raids"* (whb.cn). The clear months were the *"running to shelters"* season (S58 summary).
- **Contrast:** a whole city lived with the ordinary value of weather inverted (**clear = danger**, fog = safety), and the reading was carried every day by a public visible signal at a high point. Proposal, not finding: **an inverted sky-reading can be held by a population through a public signal rather than through inheritance.** That matters for Mirny's Act 1.

## 4 · Borrowed Form (Step 2 §9): which stock carried a way of passing on unwritten skill or reading weather

*Coverage 3 of 14 (§0.2). Researched, not recalled. Divergence operator applied: time · separation · local environmental setting · local struggles · local goals · local sensibilities.*

### China: *Genglubu* (更路簿), the Hainan fishermen's route books
**Research:**
- Kurz, *A History of Route Books (genglubu) since 1974*, text extracted locally and quoting the 1974 account: *"This book does not have the name of an author, and it cannot have been written by one person alone. It appears that these navigational compass books … are not too dissimilar and are all unique. The earliest drafts written by people were consequently being copied and were continually revised and amended."* It calls the books *"the product of the collective accumulated experiences of many people."* A keeper confirmed the distances *"According to his more than fifty years of navigational practice."*
- Kurz, on oral transmission: *"The oral transmission of the texts implies that the probably illiterate fishermen memorized the route books. Only when they had access to basic schooling and writing, were they able to put the memorized texts onto paper."* A boy *"at a young age was taken by the old fishermen from the hamlet out to sea."*
- Scholars call it a *"heavenly book"* (天书) that ordinary readers find very hard to understand (Jiemian). The youngest master captain in Tanmen is 78 (Jiemian). Only nine old captains survive in Tanmen (chinanews 2016).
- **Revisited pick (LAW 0-R), and it changed the finding:** Captain Su keeps *"三本《更路簿》，分别是祖传的风帆《更路簿》和他编写的两本机船《更路簿》"*, one inherited sailing-era book and **two he wrote himself for motor ships** (chinanews 2016).

**Mechanism:** the record is written **by practitioners**, from memory, and late. Every keeper amends his copy, so the copies diverge, and **the divergence is the knowledge.** A book's authority comes from a living practitioner's decades of use. **When the vessel changes, practitioners write new books and keep the old one.**

**Divergence operator:**
- **Time and separation:** Mirny's record precedes its practitioners. Knowledge runs record → people, the reverse of the Hainan direction, and none of the three stocks wrote it.
- **Setting:** wind, drift and ice rather than reefs and bearings. The record's unit would be something like drift direction, lull hour or day length, not a *geng* of distance.
- **Struggle:** the record's authority against the blame placed on injured people.
- **What transfers:** only the **amended copy**, a record that becomes living when the people who survived the wind write in it.
- **What must not transfer:** the sea-route content, the dialect, and the sovereignty frame (withheld: political, not culture).

**The finding:** Step 2 offers two moves, keeping the record (a cost with no failure signal) and departing from it (the person owns the failure). This form offers **a third: amend it.** The survivor's departure becomes the next reader's instruction, and blame moves from the person to the copy.

### Russia: the Pomor *lotsiya*, and wind names that migrate
**Research:**
- *"Pomor lotsias, or navigation books—folk guidance for seafaring."* They were *"inherited within Pomor families and supplemented over time with new observations."* They recorded about 400 place names, landmarks and anchorages, and **dangerous areas *"где уходу нет"* (where no shelter exists from weather).** They were small leather-bound notebooks of 24 pages. The Dvinin copy passed from grandfather to father to grandson, **who carried it when he moved in 1909** (ar.culture.ru).
- *Shelonik* (dic.academic / ruwiki): the *"southwest wind blowing from the mouth of the Shelon River"* across Lake Ilmen (Novgorod). It is used by Pomors and Kola and White Sea navigators. *"On the Kolyma, it denotes a southwest gale preceding marine storms. On Lake Baikal, the same term refers to 'южный или юго-восточный ветер' [a south or southeast wind]."*
- The Pomor rule that one wind is named differently when it blows from the sea (*побережник* / *морянка*) and from the land (*горний*) appears only in the S40 summary. The dictionary PDF failed on a TLS error and the kolamap extraction was incoherent (**unverified**).

**Mechanism:** (1) a family-held written record, supplemented each generation, whose specific category is **places with no escape.** (2) **A wind name carried from home and reattached to whichever local wind fills the same role. The name keeps its role and changes its direction.**

**Divergence operator:** no Russian home wind is a clear-sky wind off an ice sheet that blinds. The *shelonik* form predicts that exiles' names would reattach **by role, not by compass**: "the wind before the storm" goes to Mirny's cyclonic wind, and a land-wind name goes to the katabatic. If the land-versus-sea naming rule verifies, it fits Mirny closely: **off the ice** (dry, clear, lifts snow) against **off the sea** (moist, falling snow, icing). In Act 2 the name survives and its origin is forgotten. **No specific Russian word is proposed; composition names the stock, and the name that survives is Mirny's to decide.**

**The finding:** the *lotsiya*'s category, *"where there is no escape"*, is exactly the list Mirny's record needs and cannot produce without practitioners.

### Australia: surf lifesaving
**Research:**
- Rip signs: *"Deeper and/or darker water," "Fewer breaking waves," "Sandy colored water extending beyond the surf zone," "Debris or seaweed," "Significant water movement"* (SLS NSW).
- *"The rip channels often look like the calmest or safest place to swim to inexperienced observers."* Only **22 %** identified the rip on site. Of those who identified rips in both photographs, **only 34 % could identify the actual rip current at the beach.** Self-rated swimming competence did not predict ability (Pitman et al. 2021, *NHESS*). **The study site, Muriwai, is in New Zealand. The mechanism is physical; the Australian element here is the institution, not this measurement.**
- *"Lifesavers will put flags up on the beach each day. The position of the flags may change throughout the day as the conditions change"* (healthdirect, Australian government). Nippers is the junior program for ages 5–13, where volunteers teach surf awareness on the beach itself (S42 summary).

**Mechanism:** (1) **the hazard looks like safety.** (2) **Knowledge from a picture transfers poorly to the live scene (34 %).** (3) The institution does not try to make everyone a reader. **Trained readers publish a movable sign that is reset through the day**, and children are trained early, on the real ground, by volunteers.

**Divergence operator:** Mirny's equivalent of the calm-looking rip is the clear sky and the mild hour. The equivalent of the flags is **a public, movable mark of where it is safe now, set by people reading live.** What must not transfer: beach, swimming, flag colors and the recreational frame.

**The finding:** **the 34 % figure is the one direct measurement of Step 2's deficit 7** (*"the record … can't name what it left out"*). **A record teaches the picture, not the place.**

## 8 · 3.6 The Unrecognized Instrument

*Procedure limit as §0.1. Each candidate: already present? → which deficit it answers → research test → cost and shadow.*

### UI-1. Quarry crews upwind plus the relay: a warning line waiting to be connected (Step 2 §9)
- **Already present:** crews go south, upwind, every working day (Step 2: L71, L174). The subnet's signal passes through Mirny (L184).
- **Research test:** the wind comes from upslope. Onset is typically abrupt (T2). Blowing snow starts at 5–8 m/s, so upwind observers meet the wind and its snow first. Real precedents for converting distant sightings into a public visible sign exist: Chongqing's graded forward posts with lanterns on high points, and Nizhny Tagil's lookout with bell and lights. **Lead time is distance divided by front speed.** No katabatic-surge propagation speed was found (OT-10). The gust-front analog (5–15 m/s; S16 summary, general meteorology, not katabatic) gives **about 1–3 minutes of warning per kilometer of separation.** The quarry distance is not in my admitted set.
- **What it answers:** deficit 5, not by giving everyone judgment but by **relocating judgment to the people most exposed.** It also turns Step 2's conflict *"the south sends the stone and the wind"* into an asset: **exposure becomes the instrument.**
- **Cost and shadow:** the city's safety is paid for by the crews Step 2 says already *"pay the wind's price at home for the stone."* Their standing rises, which inverts Step 2's *"the person best at surviving the wind has the least standing in the record"*. But their errors become public: a false call stops the city and a missed call injures it. Norilsk shows the terms a public signal carries (*"complaints … not reviewed"*).
- **Physics sub-finding (conditional on radio in the relay):** the relay's own static rises with wind speed and blowing-snow flux, and discharges prefer above-surface structures (T6). **An exposed antenna upwind would register the arriving storm as noise before the city does.** The channel degrades exactly when it carries the warning, so the line would have to be read through noise. **At Mirny, "the signal went bad" could itself be the message.**

### UI-2. The harbor watch's live wind calls (Step 2 §9)
- **Already present:** the harbor watch (L143; Step 2: *"harbor readiness inside a window the wind can shut"*).
- **Research test:** the katabatic dies 8–10 km offshore, so the watch stands at the boundary between wind-ruled and calmer water. In December and January the work crosses fast ice up to tens of kilometers wide, beginning to break up from late January, and ice-bearing is surveyed *"for the applied task of ship unloading"* (T5). **The watch is the one post in the city whose job is daily live judgment of wind and ice.**
- **What it answers:** deficit 7 (no living teacher). **It is the one place where judgment is practiced**, and so the only candidate teaching route. The analogs are Nippers (children on the real ground with volunteers) and the *Genglubu* boy taken out to sea by the old fishermen.
- **Cost:** the season is short (December to March). The watch's judgment is seasonal, and in winter its calls are about ice, not ships.

### UI-3 (surfaced by the research, not named by Step 2). The drift forms underfoot
- **Already present:** the wind is held to one quadrant (science-start; AARI). Sastrugi form *"along ridges parallel to the prevailing wind; they are steep on the windward side and sloping to the leeward side"* (Wikipedia, *Sastrugi*). Lee ridges behind obstacles align with the wind (Anijaarniq, *qimugjuk*). Each building's drift footprint is fixed by its height and the steady wind (T1).
- **What it answers:** the clear-sky whiteout (deficit 5). When the eyes fail, a directionally steady wind has written a compass into the surface that the feet can read (*"kick around the fresh snow to get at the uqaluraq form below"*). The city's own buildings write fixed-orientation lee ridges as well.
- **Why unrecognized:** Mirny carries drift as a Standing Cost (*"Drift that regenerates"*; *"every structure makes a new lee, and a new drift"*). **The same drift is an instrument.** And because drift geometry is the writable half of the wind (T1), **this is the one piece of wind-judgment the record could actually hold.**
- **Not yet passed:** no directional-constancy figure for Mirny (OT-3, TOOL-DEATH plus the search cap).
