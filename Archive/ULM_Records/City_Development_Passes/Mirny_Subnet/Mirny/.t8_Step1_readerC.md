## 2. The findings that fire, in detail

### A1. The ring, and who gets the sheltered side of it (L168)

> "Mirny is dense with buildings, arranged in a scattered, concentric formation two or three layers deep — a
> heavily fortified, smooth-shaped outer ring built to shed the katabatic wind rather than catch it, sheltering
> huge industrial yards inside, machinery running more often than not; residential space sits inside these same
> inner buildings, alongside the industrial yards, wherever the wind isn't as much of an issue, rather than in a
> separate residential layer." (L168)

The ring divides exposed from sheltered, and the file writes only the sheltered side. It describes residential
placement as a fact of geography, with no one doing the placing: residences "sit" wherever the wind is less of
an issue. It does not say who decides.

The gate's questions go unanswered:
- Who is on the exposed side? The file calls the wind "the permanent condition of outdoor life" (L162), and it
  names outdoor work: the ring itself, quarries "to the south," and raw materials "trucked in" (L168). Whose
  work or residence falls where the wind *is* an issue, and on what terms, is not written.
- Is that placement durable? Not written.
- Is there a route from an exposed placement to a sheltered one? Not written.

G10 L52 names this pattern: *"a membership mechanism with no author has no appeal process either."* Here the
allocation has no author written, and no appeal is written either.

### A2. The supply elevation (L176)

The file writes Mirny's gain in full: "the near-exclusive top-tier industrial/quarrying hub ... elevating
Mirny's industrial identity well beyond a subnet-local construction engine" (L176). It also writes that one
quarry base serves two claims: "Mirny's own quarries supply both the eastern-highway construction materials
already established above AND this new, nationally significant chamber-manufacturing supply chain" (L176; the
first claim is set out at L174).

The file does not write what happens when this mechanism decides against someone:
- When output cannot serve both claims, which is served, and who decides?
- The file's own qualifier "near-exclusive" leaves room for another source but names none. What does the
  receiving end do if Mirny's supply stops or is withheld?
- Can the elevation be lost, and how?

The flow itself (out of Mirny, to a named recipient) is **relation** and is legal as written.

### A3. Hub status and relay failure (L184, L192, L208)

L184 does write the against side: "if it fails, the whole Mirny subnet loses contact with itself." What it does
not write is how long such a failure lasts or how contact is restored. No repair or restoration is written for
the Second Interwar.

Post-war, the only route back is a placeholder, "Relay Technician Zoya Marchenko ... the current administrator
keeping the damaged subnet relay hardware functional post-war" (L192). That conflicts with L208: "whether the
subnet relay hardware is operational — is TBD." The inherited file therefore asserts one route back and leaves it
open at the same time.

### A4. The Split Brain (L198)

The against side is written in full and at absolute scope: "the Split Brain severed every inter-subnet Arcanet
connection without exception" (L198). No route back (any restoration of the link) is written. The file puts the
road in its place, "What actually connects them is physical, not informational" (L198, L200). That answers
*what connects*, not *whether the severance can be undone*. This is a national-level mechanism; whether national
canon writes a route back cannot be determined from the admitted set (D5).

### A5. The exile (L150, L152)

The file writes the exiles only as founders: "Russian exiles built on the genuinely substantial, functional
station that remained, and developed their own scientific and operational culture from there, independently"
(L150). The exile itself is the decision against them. Its terms, its durability and any route back are not
written in this file, and the mechanism that imposed it is not in the admitted set (C3).

### A6. War damage and the displaced (L202, L144, L222). Post-war layer.

The file records the displaced only as a fact: "Mirny refugees in Concordia exist" (L202). It explains them by
the ties that brought them, not by whether they can go back. Durability and route back are not written.

The one physical route is written from the other end. The only direction the file names for people on Hwy 110
is outbound from Concordia: "Concordia's most direct physical supply/evacuation route to an external city — a
logistics asset" (L144). The open question about the road asks "whether the highway link survives war damage,
since that's the actual mechanism connecting the two cities" (L222). It does not ask about return.

The file does ask whether the road is passable, but only as a supply and strategy question, never as the
displaced's way home. This matches G10 L39: *"A "no route back" nobody has perceived as a problem is a textbook
shadow."* The finding sits outside the default Second Interwar frame. It is recorded rather than dropped (D1).
