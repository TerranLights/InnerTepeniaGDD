## 2. Four-quadrant profiles, run separately (`02` §5.1 step 3)

### 2.1 G2: physical and environmental constraint

**Governing figures:**
- **Location:** 66°33'S, 93°01'E, on the Antarctic Circle (Spec L3, L8, L53).
- **Temperature:** mean −11.2 °C (READER L8). READER L14's monthly means average to −11.19.
- **Wind:**
  - mean 12.0 m/s (Spec L82); averages 10–14 m/s (Spec L71)
  - sustained storms above 40 m/s (Spec L71)
  - katabatic, from the S and SE, off the plateau (Spec L71)
- **Precipitation:**
  - 527.1 mm falls
  - about 200 mm lands and stays
  - about 327 mm is lost to sublimation and wind transport, for roughly 38% retention (Spec L72, L77–L79)
- **Light:**
  - no polar night (Spec L85)
  - midnight sun from about Dec 8 to Jan 5, which is 29 days inclusive (Spec L86)

**STRENGTH: what it gives freely**

- **A sunrise every day of the year, except in the midsummer weeks when the sun doesn't set.** `[W]` (Spec L55, L85, L86.) Even June averages 2.4 hours of daylight (Spec L112, computed per L126). July averages 3.8 and May 5.6 (Spec L111, L113).
  - *First order:* winter is a season of short days, not a season of night.
  - *Second order:* the only break in the daily sunrise–sunset rhythm comes in midsummer, not midwinter.
  - *Third order:* the nightless weeks coincide with everything else the year gives:
    - They are the warmest: Jan mean −2.0, Dec −2.7 (READER L14).
    - They are the driest: Jan 14.8 mm, Dec 23.7 mm (Spec L107, L118).
    - They come before the winds build. February's note reads "Days shortening; katabatics intensifying" (Spec L108).
    - They fall inside the ice-free half of the sea-ice cycle: ice "beginning to form" in March and "breaking up" in October (Spec L109, L116).

    **The season of greatest outdoor and sea opportunity is also the season with no night.** Mirny's hardest-working weeks would be its sleepless weeks, and any rest taken then comes out of the only open window.
- **Rock.** `[W]` "The coast here is rocky and exposed." (Spec L57.) It gives solid ground to build heavy on and stone to build with. `[F]` It is also quarried: raw material is taken "from the south (toward the continental interior/South Pole direction)" (Spec L174).
- **The wind removes most of the snow for free.** `[W]` 327 of 527 mm (62%) never stays (Spec L77–L79). Up to a third evaporates before it lands, "and most of the remainder is entrained and carried past" (Spec L82, admitted characters). The city carries a snow load of about 200 mm, not 527.
- **Survivable temperatures.** `[W]` "the cold is NOT what defines this city" (Spec L82, admitted characters). December and January mean highs are above freezing, at +0.4 and +1.1 (Spec L118, L107). The record low is −36.5 °C (Spec L70). Ordinary protection meets the temperature. The danger is dynamic, not static.
- **A seasonal sea.** `[W]` "maritime access is possible during the navigable window but not year-round" (Spec L57).

**DEFICIT: what it withholds absolutely**

- **Calm air.** `[W]` The wind is "the dominant environmental feature of this city" (Spec L71). "Even at relatively moderate temperatures, sustained katabatic events make outdoor exposure dangerous" (Spec L139).
  - *First order:* the air outdoors is almost never still.
  - *Second order:* whether anyone can go out depends on the state of the wind, not on the clock or the thermometer.
  - *Third order:* every outdoor commitment is provisional by nature, because it is made against a forecast. Outdoor life here runs on "wind permitting."
- **A trustworthy sky.** `[W]` "Whiteout here routinely occurs under a clear sky, because the snow is *lifted*, not falling" (Spec L82; bold markup dropped). "Visibility collapses quickly in major events" (Spec L134).
  - *First order:* visibility can fail without any cloud.
  - *Second order:* the ordinary cue (a clear sky means safe) is false. The real cue is at ground level, in the direction the wind comes from: south and southeast, upslope (Spec L71).
  - *Third order:* the city has a danger side and an opportunity side. The inland horizon to the south is where the hazard starts. The sea to the north is where supply and summer are. The same southern direction comes back under G5 (§3.3 C2).
- **A year-round sea.** `[W]` "the Davis Sea freezes seasonally" (Spec L133). Ice forms around March and breaks up around October (Spec L109, L116).
- **Night, for 29 days.** `[W]` (Spec L86.) This matters mainly through the midsummer convergence described under STRENGTH.

**STANDING COST: what it demands continuously**

- **Wind-proofing and sealing.** `[W]` The structure must hold against a 12.0 m/s mean and storms above 40 m/s (Spec L71, L82). This is a *category* of task that starts again with every wind event (`02` L378–379).
- **Drift.** `[W]` The snow that stays arrives moved, not fallen: "entrained and carried past" (Spec L82). It settles wherever the wind drops, so clearing starts again after every wind event.
  - Drift should peak in midwinter, when the heaviest precipitation (Jun 70.3 mm and Jul 71.7 mm, Spec L112–L113) meets the "strong katabatic season" (May–Aug; Spec L111, L113, L114).
  - *Provenance note:* this entry sits next to an excluded span (Spec L82, chars 423–500). It was re-derived only from L79 and from L82's admitted characters 1–68 and 166–422 (see §8, S4).
- **Heat whose cost follows the wind.** `[W]` "effective wind-chill during katabatic events can drive apparent temperatures far below ambient" (Spec L142, admitted characters). The heating load tracks the forecast, not the thermometer.
- **Someone always watching the wind.** `[W]` Blizzards "can develop rapidly" (Spec L134), and outdoor activity is dangerous "regardless of cold protection" (Spec L131). *What fails first if this lapses for a week* (`02` L383): someone is caught outside when a blizzard builds.
- **A winter with no midpoint.** `[W]` The May–Sep monthly means all sit between −15.4 and −16.4 °C, a spread of 1.0 °C across five months (READER L14). The coldest month (Aug, −16.4) is only 1.0° colder than June (−15.4). Winter arrives in May and holds level. There is no low point to get past, so the cost has to be carried for five months as one block.

**GRUDGING TOLERANCE: what it permits but punishes**

- **Trusting the thermometer.** `[W]` January averages −2.0 with highs of +1.1 (READER L14, Spec L107). Yet outdoor activity in strong katabatic conditions "is dangerous regardless of cold protection" (Spec L131). Nobody forbids going out on a mild day. The place is simply shaped so that reading the wrong gauge is expensive.
  - *Who pays:* whoever hasn't learned the wind yet: newcomers, the young, anyone in a hurry.
  - *Is it perceived as a cost* (`02` L404–405)? It is read as that person's own carelessness, which is the stronger answer.
  - *Third order:* the people who could make the danger legible are the experienced ones, and they no longer need it made legible. So it never is. The harm has no author and can't be seen from inside, which is `02` §3.2's shadow, in this city's own terms.
- **Trusting a clear sky.** `[W]` (Spec L82.) The same structure, with a different gauge.
- **Trusting the calendar.** `[W]` September's note reads "Spring; katabatics moderating" (Spec L115). Its record low, −36.3, is within 0.2° of August's −36.5, the all-time record (Spec L114–L115, L70). The calendar says the worst is over before the record agrees.
- **Summer thaw.** `[W]` December and January highs are above freezing, and January has "brief rain possible" (Spec L107). Rain falling on frozen surfaces glazes them. *(The glazing step is general physics, not researched. It is a Step 3 check.)*

**G2 shape on its own:**
- All four quadrants are filled, and STANDING COST is heavy.
- GRUDGING TOLERANCE is the most internally consistent quadrant. Every entry in it has the same form: **trusting an ordinary signal.**

### 2.2 G4: founding condition

**Inputs:**
- "**Settled:** Post-Falkland Treaty." (Spec L150)
- "No institutional knowledge from any one operator, Soviet/Russian or otherwise, survived that chain of handoffs — but preserved journals, logs, and orientation manuals left behind across the centuries gave the exiles a real documentary starting point." (Spec L150)
- The founders "built on the genuinely substantial, functional station that remained" (Spec L150, admitted characters).
- The founders were exiles from Russia, China and Australia (DR-9).

The operators' identity is excluded. It is not used here (GPS law).

**STRENGTH**

- **A working plant at founding.** `[W]` The founders didn't have to build their first shelter. Their first winter was spent inside walls that already worked.
- **A written record centuries deep:** journals, logs and orientation manuals. `[W]`
  - *First order:* the founding generation's first teacher was a text.
  - *Second order:* no founder had done this before. The record's absent authors therefore held more practical authority than any living founder did.
  - *Third order:* three founding stocks (DR-9) shared one thing at the start, and it wasn't an origin. It was the record.
  - *Open, and not answerable from admitted material:* what language or languages the record is in. That would decide who read it first, and so who held founding authority.

**DEFICIT**

- **No living institution.** `[W]` Nobody at the founding had been taught by someone who had done this before.
  - A record carries procedure: thresholds, steps and schedules.
  - It can't carry embodied timing: when to turn back, or what a gust that is about to become a storm feels like.
  - **Characteristic failure** (`02` L364): a procedure followed correctly into a situation it never anticipated.
  - *Provenance note:* this sits next to an excluded span (Spec L150, chars 613–701). It was derived only from the admitted words "No institutional knowledge … survived" and "a real documentary starting point" (see §8, S4).
- **No shared origin.** `[W]` Three stocks founded the city (DR-9). *This depends on the same ruling G8 uses.*
- **No choice.** `[W]` The founders were exiles. Attachment to the place wasn't given at founding. It had to be built.

**STANDING COST**

- **Keeping up someone else's plant.** `[W]` The founders maintained a station they hadn't designed, working from its documents. Every repair was also an act of interpretation.
- **Keeping the record.** `[W]` The founding generation learned that people don't survive handoffs but documents do. So whatever it learned had to be written down, or it would die with the people who knew it. This is Necessity Before Meaning (`02` L214–216). The constraint is that only what is written survives. It is moralized afterward into a duty to write things down.
  - *What fails first if this lapses for a week:* **nothing.** Keeping the record is the only standing cost here that gives no failure signal, so it is the one most likely to lapse unnoticed. The city's best protection against losing knowledge is also its least protected cost. *(Provisional.)*

**GRUDGING TOLERANCE**

- **Knowing without writing it down.** `[W]` Knowledge held only in the body is never forbidden. But in a founding culture where authority comes from the record, it carries no standing, and it dies with the person who holds it. The loss is read as that person's failure to document it. *(Provisional. This becomes the spine conflict in §3.3, C1.)*

**G4 shape on its own:** balanced, with moderate weight in every quadrant.

**DEFICIT**

- **No redundancy.** `[F]` The subnet's contact with itself rests on one installation (Spec L184).
- **Upstream asymmetry.** `[F]` The stone leaves as stone. The chambers and highways are built somewhere else. No return flow is recorded.
- **Stability it doesn't own.** `[F]` In the Tower era, grid regulation came from a structure Mirny didn't own, and the grid was designed on the assumption it would be there (Energy L102–L103). *What regulated the grid before the Tower is not stated in the admitted material.*
- **Winter by land only.** `[W]` Once the sea freezes, supply depends on Hwy 110.

**STANDING COST**

- **Relay uptime for the whole subnet.** `[F]` Mirny pays for it, and the whole subnet feels it. *What fails first if this lapses for a week:* the subnet's contact with itself. It is noticed first somewhere other than Mirny.
- **Continuous stone.** `[F]` Mirny is "near-exclusive" (Spec L176) and nationally mandated (DoI L275), so there is no substitute supplier and no pause.
- **The ring itself.** `[F]` An "outer wind-fortified ring" (Spec L174) is a permanent structure under a permanent wind. Its upkeep never ends.
- **Watching the harbor.** `[F]` Wind can close it "even within the navigable window" (Spec L143).

**GRUDGING TOLERANCE**

- **Downtime.** `[F]` Relay maintenance or a quarry pause has to happen, and nobody forbids it. But every place downstream pays for it. So maintenance is scheduled around other places' tolerance, and gets deferred.
  - *Third order:* the hub's reliability is borrowed from its own future.

**G5 shape on its own:**
- **On F,** strength and standing cost are both heavy. **Every strength F adds comes with a standing obligation:**
  - relay → uptime
  - stone → mandate
  - ring → ring upkeep
  - harbor → harbor watch

### 3.3 Conflicts and the both-are-true test (`02` §5.3)

**C1: G2 against G4, on grudging tolerance. This is the spine conflict.**

- **The two readings:**
  - **G2:** the wind punishes anyone who trusts the ordinary, readable signal: the gauge, the sky, the calendar.
  - **G4:** the founding culture gives no standing to anyone who trusts an unwritten, embodied signal.

  So a person in Mirny is punished outdoors for trusting what can be read, and discounted indoors for trusting what can't.
- **The single property behind both:** Mirny's founding passed down *procedure* (the record) into a place where staying alive depends on *judgment* (the wind). **Authority lives in writing, and danger lives in the wind.**
- **Different objects:** one is standing indoors (the record's authority). The other is survival outdoors (what the body reads). **Both stand.**
- ***And what does that cause?*** Three things, in order:
  1. The resident best equipped to survive the wind has the least standing in the record, and the reverse is also true.
  2. So the city's written safety knowledge and its real survival skill drift apart.
  3. Each injury is put down to the injured person's carelessness, since the record had the right thresholds. The gap between the two is never framed as a problem, because each side is blind to what the other knows.

**C2: G2 against G5, on the south.**

- **G2:** south and southeast, upslope toward the plateau, is where the wind comes from (Spec L71, L131).
- **G5:** the south is where the stone comes from (Spec L174).
- **One direction, two objects:** the air is the danger, and the ground is the wealth. **Both stand.**
- ***And what does that cause?*** Processing is sheltered "inside the city's outer wind-fortified ring" (Spec L174), but extraction happens to the south, toward the wind's source.
  - **The work the nation values most would be the most wind-exposed work in the city.**
  - The quarry worker pays both of Mirny's standing costs at once: the wind's and the network's.

**C3: G2 against G8, on leaving. Left OPEN.**

- **G2:** the place is survivable. The cold doesn't define it, and the light never fails.
- **G8:** a quarter of the population left anyway.
- **Candidate property:** the environment isn't deadly, but it is relentlessly expensive (see G2's STANDING COST), so people leave because of the upkeep rather than the danger.
- **Why it stays open:** testing that needs the destination or the pull factor, and the admitted material supplies neither. Per `02` L613–615 it is **flagged open, not decided.**

**C4: G3's internal split (its own terms against the parent's need).** Resolved by object and by scale in §2.5. **Both stand.**

**Provisional cross-note: G8 × G2 × G5.**

- Energy L42 gives species-level energy profiles: robots "need stable, high-density siligel-compatible power; humans need consistent low-level heat."
- *That line is written about a different city's post-war grid.* Only the species-level profiles are used here. No condition of that city is carried over.
- **In Mirny:**
  - The wind attacks the human need locally and daily, through wind-driven heat loss (Spec L142).
  - In the Tower era, the robot need was underwritten by a distant regulator that Mirny didn't own (Energy §11).
  - So each half of a near-even population kept its vulnerability in a different place. The humans' was local and felt. The robots' was distant and unfelt.
- *This is provisional.* It depends on the Tower's date, which is not in this reader's file set (§8, S7), and nothing is built on it.

## 4. Shape: the double reading (`02` §4.0; RB L2546–2558)

The two readings produce **different shapes.** §4.0 predicts this: the shape is a property of the input set. So the three steps (question, instrument, consequence) were run on each reading.

- **Question** (`02` L453): *what does the balance cost?*
- **Instrument:** G2 × G4, the wind against the record.
- **Consequence:** nothing here is extreme. The cold is moderate, the light never fails, and the snow blows away. So the price is paid in **misjudgment**: sudden losses to one person at a time, outdoors, on days that looked mild, blamed on the person.

**Reading F.**
- **Inputs:** the full admitted set. It adds Spec L143, L174, L176, L182 and L184, DoI L275, and Energy §11.
- **Fill:** F adds four strengths: the relay, upstream stone, the sheltered core and the harbor. **Each comes with a standing obligation:** uptime, the mandate, ring upkeep and the harbor watch. The Tower-era regulation adds a deficit that has no local cost at all. STANDING COST becomes the largest quadrant.
- **Shape:** **COST-DOMINANT.**
- **Question** (`02` L458): *is this place doing anything other than continuing to exist?*
- **Instrument:** G5 × G3, the flows and the mandate.
- **Consequence:** **yes. It keeps other places going.** Its exports are highway construction material, "infrastructure-maintenance machinery" (Spec L174), and stone for the chambers robots are made in. **Mirny's product is continuity.** Its identity is upkeep at two scales: keeping itself up against the wind, and keeping the network up by supplying it.

**How the two readings diverge** (`02` L479–482; at least four axes, tense included):

| Axis | Reading W (attribute canon) | Reading F (full admitted) |
|---|---|---|
| Shape and question | Balanced: *what does the balance cost?* | Cost-dominant: *is it doing anything but continuing?* |
| Whom the cost serves | the city itself | other places: the subnet, the eastern highways, Sinheung |
| Who pays | everyone, undivided. W has no trades to assign it to | named trades: the relay crew, quarry workers, the harbor watch |
| **Tense: where and when the loss happens** | **now, here, outdoors, to one person, on a day that looked mild** | **later and elsewhere** (highway wear, a chamber shortfall), **or at once but far away** (the subnet going silent) |
| Failure signal | loud and local: an injury, a storm | felt first by the places that benefit, not by Mirny |
| Who can see the cost (`02` L380–382) | invisible to those who pay it, because everyone pays | visible to those who pay it, invisible to those who benefit |
| Typical deficit address | nowhere at all, or diffuse | in the parent, or a single address |

**Synthesis (what neither reading shows alone).** Mirny carries **two standing costs that are opposite in visibility and in tense:**
- **The wind cost** is paid now, by everyone, and named by no one.
- **The network cost** is paid by named specialists, and noticed only by distant beneficiaries when it lapses.

**The two meet at the quarry (C2).** There, extraction on the upwind side pays the first cost in order to serve the second.

The shape reported here belongs to the declared input sets in §0, per §4.0.
