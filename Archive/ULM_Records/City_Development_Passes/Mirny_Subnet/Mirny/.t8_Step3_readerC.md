## 2 · The `[V]` physics, verified

### 2.1 Where drift settles in the lee

- **Snow settles where the wind slows.** Deposition happens in the recirculation zone *"formed immediately downwind of the slip-face, or any solid barrier"* (Tabler, *Snow Fence Guide*, SHRP-W/FR-91-106, glossary). *"The fetch is measured from the upwind side of the snow fence to the next upwind obstacle"*, and obstacles include *"ice pressure ridges, and open water"* (same, p. 17).
- **The geometry:**
  - A porous (≈50 %) barrier builds an equilibrium **downwind drift of 30–35 H**, maximum depth about 1.2 H. It also builds an **upwind drift of about 12 H, 0.5 H deep**, holding *"about 15 % as much snow as the downwind drift"*.
  - A solid, deep barrier *"will … accumulate snow on the upwind side of the barrier first, and deposition on the downwind side will be restricted to within 5H"* (Tabler pp. 22, 47).
  - **Storage capacity scales with height to the power 2.2** (p. 59).
  - **"End effect"** is the wind scouring around the ends of a barrier.
- **Most of the moving snow is low, and it climbs as the wind rises** (Tabler Table 1, share of transport in the lowest 5 m that lies below 0.5 m):

  | Wind speed | Share below 0.5 m |
  |---|---|
  | 10 m/s | **88.5 %** |
  | 20 m/s | **51.9 %** |
  | 30 m/s | **33.8 %** |

  *"Most blowing snow travels within 6 ft. (1.8 m) of the surface."*
- **Antarctic station practice** (CFD and wind-tunnel studies): *"acceleration of flow under buildings … blows away the snow from the building to the leeward."* Leeward of connecting passages at one station the drifts ran *"approximately 60m long, 3m high."* Elevated buildings scour underneath, with an optimum floor height of *"about 44 % of the height of the building."* (Search summaries of ScienceDirect/Cambridge abstracts, snippet-level only.)
- ⚠ **Scope, not contrast (M-158):** Tabler's numbers are from the Wyoming high plains. His 6 km upper limit on fetch rests on sublimation under those conditions, so it is **not** transferred to Mirny. The *shapes* (recirculation zone, upwind drift, end scour, H^2.2 storage) are aerodynamics and do transfer. The *lengths* are orders of magnitude only.
- **What this does to the spine:** it supports `[V]` *"snow arrives carried and settles wherever the wind is interrupted"*, *"every structure makes a new lee, and a new drift"*, and *"Out of the wind is under the snow"*. It adds three things:
  1. A solid building drifts on its **windward** face as well as its lee.
  2. The drift a building holds grows faster than the building (H^2.2), so **the taller the shelter, the disproportionately larger the drift it makes**.
  3. **The ends of a structure scour while its lee buries.** The walkable ground next to a building is at its corners, not behind it.

### 2.2 Clear-sky whiteout: the layer, and whether it can be seen coming

- **The classes, as ground observers draw them:**
  - **WMO:** *drifting snow* stays below eye level (1.8 m); *"neither vertical visibility, nor horizontal visibility at eye level … is noticeably diminished."* *Blowing snow* reaches eye level or higher; *"horizontal visibility at eye level is generally very poor"* (WMO Cloud Atlas).
  - **Russian meteorological classes:**
    - **позёмок (pozyomok):** transport in a 0.5–2 m layer; horizontal visibility at 2 m stays ≥ 10 km.
    - **низовая метель (nizovaya metel', ground blizzard):** a layer "several meters" high; horizontal visibility usually 1–9 km, sometimes a few hundred meters, **"vertical visibility is quite good, so that the state of the sky can be determined."**
    - **общая метель (general blizzard):** the sky state can no longer be determined.

    (ru.wikipedia *Метель*, via search summary.) More than 90 % of pozyomok snow moves below 20 cm, and it *"is possible even in clear weather"* (Наука и жизнь, *Снежная круговерть*).
  - **Chinese classes:**
    - **低吹雪:** below 2 m.
    - **高吹雪:** above 2 m, horizontal visibility < 10 km.
    - **暴风雪:** ≥ 17.2 m/s, visibility < 1 km.

    Folk names: **白毛风** ("white-hair wind"; *"the gale lifts the ground snow like strands of white hair"*) and **大烟炮儿** in the Northeast (CMA; Baidu).
- ⭐ **The clear-sky whiteout has an exact definition in two founding stocks' meteorology.** It is the state where **looking up shows the sky and looking level shows nothing**: the Russian *nizovaya metel'*, with vertical visibility good and horizontal visibility falling.
- **Depth of the layer (satellite lidar):**
  - Layers run from the 30 m detection minimum up to about 1000 m, *"with an average depth of 120 m for all blowing snow events."*
  - *"In East Antarctica, blowing snow averages about 150 m in depth and can reach heights of 500 m or more."*
  - *"typical blowing-snow layers are 200 m along the coastal katabatic wind regions."*

  (Palm et al. 2011 and follow-ups, via search summaries. Note that CALIPSO sees only layers ≥ 30 m and only without cloud.)
- **Onset threshold versus Mirny's ordinary wind:**
  - An eclipse-planning bulletin for this coast: *"Even under clear skies, blizzard conditions can develop if winds are too strong — anything above 35 km/h brings poor visibility and blowing snow"* (NASA 2003 eclipse bulletin, §2). 35 km/h ≈ 9.7 m/s.
  - The site's own mean is **11.2 m/s** (AARI) or **11.3 m/s** (Turner et al. 2009, snippet), with **204 days a year above 15 m/s** (AARI).
  - ⭐ **So at Mirny, blowing snow is the ordinary day's condition, not the storm's, wherever loose snow lies upwind.** The spine's retention figure (~38 % of precipitation stays) means snow is regularly available to move.
- **Can it be seen coming?**
  1. **At the coast, sometimes, from downstream.** Where katabatic flow meets the flatter coast it can undergo a hydraulic jump. The result is *"100–1000 m high, highly localized 'walls' of snow in the air in an otherwise cloud-free sky"*, with updrafts > 5 m/s (Vignon et al. 2020, JAS 77(4); EGU abstracts).
     - The jump's position responds to downstream conditions: *"An increase in the downstream inversion-layer depth results in a retreat of the jump"* (Yu & Cai idealized simulations, snippet).
     - Rotors under the trapped waves produced a record gust at the case-study site.
     - ⚠ **This is general physics documented on other coasts.** Nothing I found shows that Mirny's coast forms jumps regularly. The site record says only that the katabatic *"completely decreases at a distance of 10–15 km from the slope foot"* (AARI). That is a transition zone; a jump there is possible, not demonstrated.
  2. **Onset, from upwind: NOT established.** Three reframed queries (S51–S53) found only two things:
     - katabatic fronts propagate *"relatively slow[ly] … compared to its wind speed"*;
     - a documented **"wind skip"**: *"down-valley stations can be affected before up-valley ones."*

     No source quantifies the onset sequence along a uniform coastal slope. **Dead end at the SOURCES**, logged §9.5.
  3. **The strongest storms need no local onset at all.** *"The largest wind speeds are observed in the cyclone rear at the coincident vectors of the catabatic and baric winds"* (AARI). *"Around the coast of East Antarctica the significant majority of strong wind events … are associated with enhancement of the downslope katabatic flow by the broadscale synoptic circulation"* (Turner et al. 2009, abstract). ⭐ **The worst wind arrives behind a passing cyclone, i.e. as the sky clears.**
  4. **The other whiteout is a separate mechanism.** Russian **белая мгла** ("white haze") is flat light under thin overcast, with shadows and horizon gone. It needs no wind. The spine named only the blowing-snow kind. At Mirny the overcast kind competes with a mean cloud cover of **6.4 tenths** in November (NASA bulletin).

- ⭐ **New: the June sun is above the horizon every day but almost never recorded as sunshine.** Mean June sunshine is **1.7 hours for the whole month** (en.wikipedia climate table), against December's 354.2. At the solstice the sun's apparent altitude peaks at roughly half a degree, due north.
  - The Haswell Islands lie north of the station. Haswell Island is 93 m high (ASPA 127) and 2.5 km from the station (snippet) or about 3 km off Mabus Point, which is *"just north of Mirny station"* (AADC gazetteer). From low coastal ground, an obstruction of about a degree on the northern horizon can therefore hide the midwinter sun.
  - ⚠ **Not asserted.** The azimuth and height geometry needs a map (open thread OT-3). Stated as the constraint only: **the midwinter sun belongs to whoever has an open northern skyline.**
- ⛔ **Rejected:** a search summary claimed that *"at your specified latitude … you would experience a polar night"*, and another that Mirny sits *"just south of the Antarctic Circle."* Both are wrong. Mirny (66°33′11″S) lies just north of the circle, and with refraction the sun rises on every day of the year. This is exactly the M-141 error shape (a plausible polar-night claim).

### 2.4 Katabatic lulls by season and hour

- **Summer and transition seasons** (AARI, snippet): *"the catabatic flows arise or intensify in the evening reaching their maximum around 5–8 h local time attenuating by 15–18 h and then increasing again."*
  - Consistent: *"Such winds are more frequent in the evening and overnight"* (NASA bulletin).
  - General: *"only weak or nonexistent katabatic flow expected from midmorning through late afternoon during summer."*
- **Winter** (AARI): *"the catabatic wind speeds increase and their attenuation or increase depend on the change of the pressure gradient with approaching cyclone."* **There is no diurnal lull.** Across the East Antarctic coast, katabatic speeds peak in **late winter (Aug–Sep)**.
- **Calm is about 3 %**: *"calm weather and weak winds comprise only about 3% of all cases observed"* (AARI). A continuous katabatic storm of **220 hours** is on record (AARI). Storms last *"from several hours to several weeks."*
- **Direction:**
  - AARI: *"easterly-south-easterly winds predominate."*
  - Turner (snippet): prevailing from the **southeast**.
  - NASA bulletin: winter downslope flow *"typically close to southeast"*, summer *"east of southeast."*
- ⭐ **What this changes:** the spine's "the calendar tells the truth; the hour lies" becomes **"the hour tells the truth in the warm half and nothing in the cold half."** In the lit months the clock carries the lull, roughly midday to late afternoon, because the sun no longer sets and no longer marks it. In winter neither sun nor clock carries anything; the signal is a pressure change.

Near Mirny the fast ice is pinned for most of the year. The pack beyond it is what moves.

### 2.6 Storms and the relay

- **Precipitation static from blowing snow.** Herman 1965 (Antarctic Research Series 4), abstract via search summary, seen twice (S27, S28), same source:
  - *"can raise the noise power at least 50 db above atmospheric and man-made levels and can cause loss of such radio data 10–30% of the time in winter and spring months."*
  - Eight fixed frequencies from **51 kc/s to 20 Mc/s**.
  - ⚠ **Scope:** LF–HF radio at one interior site. It says nothing about other bands.
- **Mechanism** (Schmidt, Schmidt & Dent 1999, full text read):
  - *"Static interference on radio antennas prompted early investigations of charge separation in blizzards."*
  - Field strength *"as high as +30 kV m⁻¹"* 4 cm above the surface in a moderate blizzard.
  - *"Particle charging increased by a factor of four with a twofold increase in jet velocity."*
  - The field *"decreases rapidly with height above the surface."*
- **Icing.** A combination of *"high winds (over 60 knots) unseasonably high temperatures and heavy, slushy snow"* coated an antenna array in rime. When the temperature dropped again the rime *"solidified and stuck fast"*, and a supporting cable *"had snapped"* (a station blog; physics only).
  - Mirny's December–January highs are above freezing (spine; en.wiki table: Jan mean max 1.1 °C), and one rainstorm is on record in December (ru.wiki).
  - **The warm-wind-then-freeze sequence is physically available at this site in the warm months.**
- ⭐ **What this changes:** the spine's conflict *"Signal, not body … the signal decides when people move"* gets a physical coupling. **The relay's noisiest hours are the city's blowing-snow hours**, so the signal is least reliable exactly when it decides the most. ⚠ The in-world relay technology is unspecified, and the dB figure is not transferred.

### 2.7 The ground between the coast and the quarries

- **Rock.** *"Mirnyy Station is situated on outcrops of Precambrian charnockitic granite, crystalline schist, and gneiss"* (Int. Geology Review 1959 abstract, via summary; the full text returned 403). The offshore islets *"are similar to the ancient Antarctic shield."* The ASPA plan places the station on *"coastal nunataks."* Mabus Point is *"An ice covered point marked by prominent rock outcrops, just north of Mirny station"* (AADC). Inland rock appears only as *"isolated nunataks"* (geochronology summaries).
- **Ice.**
  - Surface elevation near the station rises *"from 37 m in the northern part to 71 m in the southwest."*
  - *"glacier flow velocity varies from approximately 6 to 80 m/year."*
  - Visual inspection found 10 crevasses 10–20 cm wide in the runway area, and GPR found others under *"snow-firn bridging … at least 1.5 m."*
  - *"The marginal zone of the Antarctic ice sheet is rich with crevasses."*

  (2016/17 runway study; Popov 2020, via summaries.)
- **Wind on the slope.** The strongest drainage flows sit at the steep coastal terminus. On one surveyed slope (general physics), speed *"increases from the plateau to reach a maximum"* on the lower slope, then decreases toward the coast as the slope changes (paraphrased, to keep the source's British spelling out of this file). Mirny sits *"at the ice slope foot"* (AARI).
- ⭐ **What this changes:**
  1. **Upslope work sits in stronger flow than the city.** This reinforces the spine's conflict that *"the work the nation values most is the most wind-exposed."*
  2. **If the quarries are on or across the slope, the route to them is moving, crevassed ice whose bridges hide the hazard.** The route itself changes year to year.
  3. ⚠ **Direction.** The spine says the quarries lie **south** and **upwind**. The sources put the prevailing wind at **ESE (AARI) / SE (Turner, NASA winter) / east of SE (NASA summer)**. A quarry due south is 20–70° off the prevailing axis. Both lie in the southern, plateau-side sector, but **"upwind" holds only loosely** until the quarry bearing is fixed. Flagged for the pass, not resolved (§6).
  4. ⚠ Whether real bedrock exists where the in-world quarries sit is **not answerable from research**. Real rock south of the coast is sparse nunataks under a moving ice margin. Open thread OT-5.

## 4 · Borrowed Form: the three named founding stocks (researched, not recalled; divergence operator applied)

### 4.2 The amendable practitioners' book (Russian North, Hainan)

- **Pomor лоция (Arkhangelsk regional museum text, via culture.ru, verified by direct text check):**
  - *"Лоции передавались в семьях поморов по наследству и с годами дополнялись новыми наблюдениями"* ("the lotsii passed down in Pomor families by inheritance and over the years were supplemented with new observations").
  - The Dvinin book went **grandfather → son → grandson** and carries *"приписки и пометы"* (marginal additions and notes) on four leaves.
  - Its content is keyed to **shelter, not forecast**: places where one can *"отстоять"* (ride it out) *when "заводиться противняк"* (a contrary wind starts to brew) or *"несхожие ветры падут"* (unsuitable winds fall), and warnings of places where *"уходу нет"* (there is no escape from the weather).
  - A scholarly source (Шумилов/Савинов 2007, kolamap) records ownership passing father → son, **witnessed by an apprentice** (*"Свидетельствую ученик Савватей Гудов"*). It also says an **oral seafaring tradition** preceded the written book. It **warns that much literature on Pomor seafaring idealizes, and in some cases falsifies, it.**
- **Hainan 更路簿 / 水路簿 (Kurz, "A History of Route Books since 1974", full text read):**
  - *"the probably illiterate fishermen memorized the route books. Only when they had access to basic schooling and writing, were they able to put the memorized texts onto paper."*
  - The texts were *"continually revised and amended"*, *"the product of the collective accumulated experiences of many people."*
  - One owner's father annotated his copy with seasonal conditions, wind direction, currents and cloud color.
  - ⚠ Kurz is critical of the books' use as sovereignty evidence. **Only the form is used here.**
- **What the contrast writes:** in both forms **the book and the memory are one object, carried by practitioners who write into it.** Mirny has the inverse: **a book whose writers are gone, kept by people who did not write it** (spine G4). The borrowed form would do two things Mirny's record cannot yet do: **put the record back into heads**, and **let the reader write the page.**
- **Divergence:**
  - The Pomor book maps fixed shelter along a fixed coast. **Mirny's shelter moves**: every structure makes a lee and a drift (§2.1), so a shelter book would go stale with each building season.
  - The Pomor book was amended over generations; **Mirny's would need amending per build.**
  - The authority to amend it is the open question the spine's conflict already names: *"The person best at surviving the wind has the least standing in the record."*

### 7.1 Candidate A: quarry crews upwind + the subnet's relay = a warning line

1. **Present:** yes. The quarries are in the south (`M` L174). The relay passes the subnet's signal (`M` L184).
2. **Would it work?**
   - **As a lead-time warning: NOT supported.** Katabatic onset along a slope is not shown to reach upslope sites first. "Wind skip" is documented, and fronts propagate slowly relative to wind speed (§2.2). The strongest storms are synoptic enhancements that arrive with a regional pressure change and **as the sky clears** (AARI; Turner). **Dead end at the SOURCES** (S51–S53).
   - **As something else, it IS supported:**
     - The flow is **stronger on the lower slope than at the coast** (§2.7), so crews upslope feel a storm **at full intensity while the city still reads moderate.**
     - The blowing-snow layer is **30–1000 m deep, typically about 200 m** on katabatic coasts (§2.2), and in a ground blizzard **the sky stays readable while horizontal visibility collapses.** A crew inside the layer can report **the one datum the city's sky cannot give: how far one can see at head height, out there, now.**
   - **Its channel is weakest when the message matters.** Blowing snow degrades radio (≥ 50 dB noise; 10–30 % data loss, winter/spring, LF–HF, §2.6). Chongqing's answer was redundancy across **independent channels**.
   - **Verdict: CONFIRMED, as an intensity and visibility report from inside the layer, not an early warning.** It needs a second, non-radio channel to be trusted in the storms that count.
3. **Why unrecognized, from inside the place:**
   - The spine's §6 #8 already says it: asking admits the hub can't hold alone, so the hub under-reports.
   - Research adds a sharper reason from Chongqing. **Once a warning line exists, a missed warning has an owner.** Mirny currently speaks the clear-sky injury **as blame of the injured**, which has no counterparty.
   - **Connecting the line would move blame from the injured person to the line's keepers.** That is the relay crews and quarry crews, the city's most exposed and "most valued" workers.
   - ⭐ **Recognizing the instrument would create a grievance with an address in a city whose losses so far have none** (spine: "ten of eleven produce no grievance").
4. **Nth order:**
   - **(1)** A report-from-the-layer habit gives the crews standing in the city's decisions about movement.
   - **(2)** That standing collides with *"authority lives in writing"*, because the crews' report is live and unwritten.
   - **(3)** The first fatal miss after the line exists becomes Mirny's first **addressed** grievance. It is the Chongqing mechanism: the social contract gets renegotiated around who owed the warning.

### 7.2 Candidate B: the harbor watch's live wind calls

1. **Present:** yes (`M` L143; spine G2 standing cost, G5).
2. **Would it work?**
   - **Physically, the harbor sits where the katabatic decays** (10–15 km seaward of the slope foot, AARI), the zone in which a katabatic jump's **visible wall of snow** can form under a clear sky (general physics). The harbor is the one place in the city **positioned downstream of the wind's edge**, where the edge can be *seen* rather than stood in.
   - Its working window (open water, mean Feb 3 → Apr 6) falls in the transition season, when the diurnal katabatic cycle exists. **Its calls can be keyed to an hour.**
   - The SLSA form shows how such calls reach the public: **a daily marker set by a named reader.**
   - ⚠ **Conditional.** Jumps at Mirny's coast are not demonstrated (§2.2). And for about ten months the harbor faces fast ice, not water.
   - **Verdict: CONFIRMED CONDITIONALLY.** The watch is the city's natural *reader of the edge*. Whether the edge is visible at this coast is OT-2.
3. **Why unrecognized:** the watch's calls are made **for ships**, and the harbor is closed about ten months a year. Its judgment is scoped to a season and a purpose. **The city does not think of it as a wind authority because, most of the year, there is nothing for it to call.**
4. **Nth order:**
   - **(1)** In winter the frozen sea is a road that reaches *out of the katabatic* (§2.5).
   - **(2)** The people who know that ground best in every month are the harbor watch.
   - **(3)** **The watch's off-season knowledge, "how far out the katabatic dies today", is the only map of Mirny's one escape from its own wind.** It is unrecorded because it was never the watch's job.

### 9.7 Divergences from source (3.4: stated, not implied)

- **Trieste:** "clear" bora means visibility. **Mirny:** a clear katabatic means sky visible and ground blind.

- **Pomor / Hainan books:** fixed coasts, amended over generations. **Mirny:** moving shelter, amended per build; the writers are gone and the readers inherit.
- **SLSA flags:** a visual marker on a beach. **Mirny:** the visual marker fails in the hazard and touch is the in-storm channel (not yet earned).
- **Chongqing:** an enemy whose departure can be watched from hundreds of kilometers away. **Mirny:** a wind whose onset gives no demonstrated lead. Only the multi-channel principle and the grievance mechanism transfer.
- **Fogbank / Tagil:** in one a single undocumented factor, in the other a lost family secret. **Mirny:** a whole plant whose silences reveal themselves one failure at a time.
