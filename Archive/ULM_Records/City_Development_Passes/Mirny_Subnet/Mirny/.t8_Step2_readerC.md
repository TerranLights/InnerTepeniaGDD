**The four questions, answered (`02` L174–179):**

**STRENGTH: what it gives freely**
- **G2-S1. The sun rises every day of the year.** There is no polar night (`M` L85[1–27]; L55[578–985]; L97)
  **[G]**. June, the darkest month, still averages **2.4 h** of daylight (`M` L112[1–66]) **[G]**.
- **G2-S2. ~29 days of continuous light**, **Dec 8 → Jan 5** (`M` L86[1–71]) **[G]**. They fall in the warmest
  months: Dec −2.7 °C and Jan −2.0 °C mean (`RD` L14) **[G]**.
- **G2-S3. The wind clears most of what falls.** About **527 mm** falls, **~200 mm** stays and **~327 mm** is

  of the remainder is entrained and carried past" (`M` L82[166–422]) **[G]**. Wherever the wind runs
  unobstructed, the surface clears itself.
- **G2-S4. Ambient temperatures the spec itself calls "relatively moderate"** (`M` L139[103–270]) **[G]**. The

**DEFICIT: what it withholds absolutely**
- **G2-D1. Calm.** Katabatic flow comes "from the south and southeast (off the East Antarctic plateau)" (`M`
  L71[1–94]). Mean wind is **12.0 m/s** (`M` L82), averages run 10–14 m/s, and sustained storms beyond **40 m/s**

  (`M` L57[188–371]) **[G]**.
- **G2-D2. Honest surface cues.** "Whiteout here routinely occurs under a clear sky, because the snow is lifted"
  (`M` L82[166–422]) **[G]**. Wind-chill "can drive apparent temperatures far below ambient" (`M` L142[103–198])
  **[G]**. Outdoor exposure is dangerous "even at relatively moderate temperatures" (`M` L139) and "regardless of
  cold protection" (`M` L131[126–366]) **[G]**. **The two readings a person makes by instinct, looking up and
  feeling the air, both mislead here.**
- **G2-D3. A year-round sea.** The Davis Sea freezes seasonally (`M` L133). Sea ice begins to form in March and
  breaks up in October (`M` L109, L116 notes). Inside the window, "katabatic wind events can close the harbor"
  (`M` L143), and "ice conditions can change quickly" (`M` L133) **[G]**.
- **G2-D4. Darkness, for ~29 days at midsummer** (`M` L86[1–71]) **[G]**.
- **G2-D5. A felt turn of winter.** The monthly means for May through September are −15.8, −15.4, −16.3, −16.4
  and −15.9 °C: **five months within 1.0 °C of each other** (`RD` L14) **[G]**. September's record low
  (**−36.3**, `M` L115) sits 0.2 °C from the all-time record (**−36.5**, August, `M` L114, L70) **[G]**. **Until
  October (−13.4), temperature gives no sign that winter is ending.**

**STANDING COST: what it demands continuously**
- **G2-C1. Drift work that regenerates.** Most snow is carried past (`M` L82), so what stays settles wherever the
  wind is interrupted **[V: lee-side deposition]**. Every structure makes its own drift, and clearing is never
  finished while the wind blows. The work peaks in the windy half of the year: **319.3 of the 527.1 mm (60.6%)
  falls May–Sep** (`M` L111–115) **[G]**, the months noted "strong katabatic season", "intense katabatic winds"
  and "katabatics moderating" (`M` L111, L113[82–107], L115) **[G]**.
- **G2-C2. Wind-rating of everything that goes outside.** Protection has to be keyed to wind, not to cold (`M`
  L131[126–366]; L142[103–198]) **[G]**.
- **G2-C3. A continuous reading of the wind.** The sky cannot be read (`M` L82), and "blizzard conditions can
  develop rapidly; visibility collapses quickly" (`M` L134) **[G]**. So the decision to go out depends on a
  reading someone is taking at all times **[D]**. The admitted set names no watch institution.
- **G2-C4. Heat: constant, but moderate.** The annual mean is −11.2 °C, with a winter plateau near −16 °C (`RD`
  L14) **[G]**.
- **G2-C5. Readiness at the harbor inside a window the wind can shut** (`M` L143) **[G]**. Cargo moves in the gaps
  between wind events, not by the calendar **[D]**.

**GRUDGING TOLERANCE: what it permits but punishes**
- **G2-T1. Going out on your own reading of a clear, mild day.** Nobody forbids it. The clear-sky whiteout (`M`
  L82) and wind-chill (`M` L142) punish it **[G]**. **The typical casualty is the confident person on a
  fair-looking day, and the cost reads as that person's misjudgment** **[D]**.
- **G2-T2. Building.** Every added structure makes a new lee **[V]**. The snow it catches settles on its
  downwind side, which is north and northwest given flow from the south and southeast (`M` L71[1–94]) **[G + V]**.
  **One structure's shelter becomes the drift on its downwind side.** Nobody forbids building. The cost lands
  downwind and reads as weather **[D]**. **This matches the `02` §3.2 shadow structure: authorless, unenforced,
  invisible from inside.**
- **G2-T3. Working through the lit month.** The ~29 days of continuous light (`M` L86) fall in the season when
  outside work is possible: "conditions improving" in November (`M` L117), "katabatics moderating" in September
  (`M` L115), "sea ice breaking up" in October (`M` L116) **[G]**. **No dusk ends the day in the one season that
  permits outdoor work** **[D]**.
- **G2-T4. Planning on the window's calendar length.** It is permitted, and punished by closures inside the window
  (`M` L143) **[G]**.

**Three orders (`02` L184–187):**
- **a. The sky lies.** (1) Whiteout happens under a clear sky, because the snow is lifted (`M` L82). (2) The most
  basic weather reading, looking up, fails, so going out requires a *reading*, not a glance. (3) **Whether the
  day is safe is not an individual's call to make by eye. It belongs to whoever holds the instrument. In Mirny,
  fair weather is a claim someone else has to confirm.** **[D]**
- **b. The moment lies; the season does not.** (1) There is no polar night; day length shrinks to 2.4 h in June
  and grows back, and never fails (`M` L112–115). (2) Winter temperature is flat for five months (`RD` L14), so
  temperature cannot mark the season's turn, but daylight can: Jun 2.4 → Jul 3.8 → Aug 7.6 → Sep 11.2 h (`M`
  L112–115). (3) **Day length is the one surface cue that never misleads. Mirny can trust its calendar and cannot
  trust its hour.** **[D]** Physically, the sun can be visible overhead while ground-level visibility is gone
  **[V: the depth of the blowing-snow layer]**. **Looking up tells the truth about the season; looking across
  lies about the moment.**
- **c. Shelter means snow.** (1) The wind carries most snow past (`M` L82). (2) Snow settles where the wind stops
  **[V]**. (3) **Every calm place is also where snow piles up: being out of the wind and being snowed in are the
  same location. Choosing where to be is choosing between exposure and digging.** **[D + V]**
- **d. The lit month.** (1) Open water, milder wind and continuous light all arrive in the same season (`M`
  L86, L115–118). (2) Outdoor and harbor time is set by wind lulls, not by daylight or the clock (`M` L143). (3)
  **The busiest weeks have no natural stopping point, so rest in that month has to be declared rather than
  prompted.** **[D]**

first authors, not as a constituency.**

running is made of people's broken shifts. **Third order [D]:** the founding loss happened in "that chain of
handoffs" (`M` L150[362–612]), where no one's institutional knowledge survived. **The handoff is Mirny's most
practiced act and the place where its founding loss happened.** Every shift change is a small version of that
loss.
