# A · The Naming technique — run first, before generating anything

The spine requires this before any belief is generated: search the location's existing canon for
religious-register language attached to non-religious objects, and only generate from scratch if the search
comes back empty. It did not come back empty.

**`Specs/Zhongshan.md`, three places, all about ordinary infrastructure and paperwork:**

> L144 — *"Zhongshan's claim to its own site was **never left to chance**."*
> L150 — *"Zhongshan was **not** a small city squeezed into borrowed infrastructure… built up and maintained
> **continuously** rather than left to decay… one that in this particular city's case **never actually left**."*
> §15 *(via `16` §18)* — *"the research heritage is **continuous from the founding station**."*

None of those objects needs that language. A land title does not require *never left to chance*. An
inherited facility does not require *never actually left*. The register is doing work the subject matter
does not ask for, which is exactly what the technique looks for.

## The proposition that would have to be true

Stated as one sentence a resident would agree with:

> **Nothing here ever had to start over.**

That is this city's unnamed load-bearing thing, and it is not a creed. Per the spine's own instruction, where
a place has no sited faith the phase should expect a stake, a compact, a debt, or a bare standard of worth,
and write it as what it is rather than promoting it into a religion. **This is a standard of worth.** What
makes a thing count here is that it was never interrupted.

It is also, unusually, verifiable. Phase 2 established this city as one of only two named exceptions in
`No_National_Stereotypes.md` — roughly 481 years of continuous habitation by the same origin stock before
the founding, so the founding population did not arrive at an empty place. The belief is not a story the
city tells about itself. It is a fact the city happens to have.

## The portability counter-check

The spine requires it: if the name would work equally well for a sibling, it has been written too abstractly.
**Continuity as a *fact* is nearly unique here** — the exception is named in canon and there is one other.
And the register quoted above is in this city's own spec, not inherited from a national file. It passes.

## The Ice-Cold Buddhism negative is physical, not just administrative

The faith's premise is superconductor-as-nirvana: a robot reaching resistance-free flow as something like
liberation, which the research grounds in *"exact combinations of extreme cold and precise order."*

This city's coldest temperature ever recorded is **−33.8 °C** (May, NOAA NCEI station `AYM00089573`).
`Robot_Cold_Physiology.md` L82 puts the point where cold becomes mechanically consequential for a robot at
**~−40 °C**, where greases *"exhibit properties similar to a solid, with catastrophic results."*

**The all-time record here falls about six degrees short of the temperature at which cold starts to matter to
a robot at all.** This is a coastal oasis, not a plateau interior. The faith built on extreme cold has its
sacred sites where the cold is extreme, and this is not one of those places — on the instrument, not by
assignment.

## What is deliberately not done here

The Unmeasured denomination is described as *"scientist-priests"* whose Watchers *"plausibly hold a real day
job at an observatory or research station,"* and the file notes that Tepenia's existing scientific
infrastructure is a natural point of contact for the religion. This city is the technical and scientific
city — three-quarters of a 35% sector mandated, the developer's own *"science and technology is fundamentally
Zhongshan's identity."*

**That is a tempting fit and this pass declines it.** The Watchers' stronghold is confirmed as Kunlun. The
research-institution overlap is a general mechanism that applies wherever research happens, not a siting
claim, so it supports *"adherents work here, unremarkably"* and supports nothing further. Assigning a
denomination because the roster had a slot and this city had none is the exact error the NO FORCED FIT law
names, and inventing a bespoke faith to fill the phase is the same error in the other direction. **The slot is
left open explicitly: no faith is sited here, which is not the same as no faith being possible here.**

Likewise refused: the vibration reading. This city's environment is defined by a katabatic wind felt as
vibration by a workforce that is two-thirds robot, and Cymatics Reverence venerates vibration. The
portability check kills it — katabatic wind is a feature of most of this coastline, so the argument would
work about equally well for many cities and is therefore written too abstractly to be about this one.

# D · The failure state of the core value

**The central promise, from A: nothing here ever had to start over.**

**The circumstance under which it becomes impossible to keep is annual, and canon names it precisely.** This
city's harbor is CONSTRUCTED tier — the one port class in the country defined by exactly this:

> `Ports.md` §2 — running cost **"paid every year, forever,"** killed by **"a year nobody pays it."**
> §1.1 — *"an Antarctic port is not a place. It is a recurring act of construction — built partly from the sea
> it serves, and expected to fail."*

> ## The city whose standard of worth is that nothing was ever interrupted holds its only access to the sea on the one thing in the country that must be rebuilt from nothing every year or it stops existing.

That is not a tension this pass constructed. Both halves are canon: the continuous-habitation exception in
`No_National_Stereotypes.md`, and the tier assignment in `Ports.md` §5x.

## Does the culture ritualize the failure, or refuse to?

The spine notes the refusal is often the stronger half, and here the sources point at refusal.

`Ports.md` §5x describes what a CONSTRUCTED port produces in a city: *"a trade and a calendar. Somebody
rebuilds it, on a schedule, and that is **a job, a skill and a season**."* A job, a skill and a season — not a
rite, not an observance, not a commemoration. The source frames it as work and only work.

**A city whose worth-standard is continuity would have no use for a ceremony marking the one act that
demonstrates discontinuity.** The rebuild is done, thoroughly and on time, by people whose year is built
around it (Phase 4 found the harbor crew's year runs November to March, inverted against everyone else's),
and nothing is said about it. Phase 5 found the same act doing quiet social work — having worked a rebuild is
what converts a visitor into a member — which is precisely the shape of a thing that matters enormously and
is never announced.

# E · Observance

## 1 · Independence Day — June 21, and where it lands here

Canon, national: the Falkland Treaty anniversary, which is also the Antarctic winter solstice —
*"the darkest day of the year as the day a nation of exiles chose to call itself a nation."*

Step 3's research finding `F-6` arrived at the same date from the opposite direction: **the one observance
real Antarctic stations share across every nation is Midwinter Day** — the shared thing is a date, not an
origin. The national holiday and the real-world tradition are the same day.

**What this city's own data adds:** June here is **full polar night, 0 hours of daylight**, and the **wettest
month of the year at 18.7 mm** against January's 5.2. The spec's own climate row calls it *"a slight
midwinter plateau."* And the harbor's window is November to March, so **June 21 falls dead center of the shut
season** — the national day arrives when the port is closed and the crew whose whole year is the rebuild has
nothing to do.

`National_Holidays.md` leaves this legitimately open in its own words: *"Does every city observe Independence
Day identically, or does the Hut Point remembrance ritual stay unique to Scott/Fort McMurdo while other cities
mark the day differently?"* This pass answers only the local half — **here it is a holiday that lands on the
idle half of the year, in the dark, in the rain** — and leaves the national question where the file put it.

## 2 · The opening of the window — derived, and flagged as derived

The port opens once a year and berthing order *"decides whose cargo lands this year and whose waits a full
year. That is not a scheduling matter; it is an economy."* An event with that weight, recurring annually at a
fixed season with everyone watching, is the natural site of an observance.

⚠ **Marked as derived, not canon.** `Ports.md`'s surrounding argument about what the berthing queue eventually
forced is `⚠ PROPOSAL` in its own file and Phase 5 declined to adopt it. The window itself, its months, and
the cost of losing a place in it are canon; a *custom* attached to the opening is this pass's inference and
should be confirmed before anything downstream leans on it.

## 3 · The small and unserious one

The spine requires at least one, on the grounds that an ordinary, slightly silly, genuinely enjoyed local
holiday does more for a place's livability than a third memorial day.

`National_Holidays.md` Category 2 — the "persisted aesthetic" holidays, robots celebrating human historical
aesthetic epochs — is national, explicitly not tied to any founding nation, and carries its own open
question: *"what does an actual observance look like? None of the five aesthetic holidays have concrete
practices attached yet."*

This city has the material to answer that locally rather than nationally. §15 gives it *"precision
manufacturing, with the craft ethic applying to industrial output as much as to art,"* in a city whose
workforce is roughly two-thirds robot. **A persisted-aesthetic holiday in a precision-manufacturing city
plausibly takes the form of making things in the period style** — not performing an era, but building in it,
badly and on purpose and for fun, by people whose ordinary work is tolerances. It is unserious, it is local,
and it does not require a single new institution.

⚠ Same flag: the national file's Category 2 observance question is open by canon's own admission, so this is
offered as this city's local answer, not as a national one.

# Differentiation axis

**Nothing here ever started over — and the harbor has to, every year.**

*One-sentence test.* Delete every other city's name: *"This place's standard of worth is that it was never
interrupted, and its only access to the sea has to be rebuilt from nothing every single year."* Survives,
names nobody, and is not portable — it requires both the continuous-habitation exception and a
CONSTRUCTED-tier port, and the number of places holding both is very small.

Checked against `04`: this is a standard of worth, not a creed, and it has not been promoted into one.
