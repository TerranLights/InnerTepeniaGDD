# 5a · Peers

## What is shared

Three things, and all three with the same two cities: the port at Nella Fjord, the Tri-Cities Airport, and the
junction where Hwy 4, Hwy 22 and Hwy 110 meet.

One correction to how the first write framed this. `Airports.md` distinguishes hosting an airport from being
served by one, and it maintains a separate "Served, Not Host" category for cities with air access they do not
own. Zhongshan is in the host column, not that one. The same holds at the fjord: this city has a shore. It is a
party to these arrangements, not a guest at them.

## The core move — what does each side refuse to develop, and who supplies it instead

Canon answers this directly, and the answer is not symmetrical.

This city does not develop maritime trade, and that is a denial rather than a gap. §15 states *"Zhongshan is
not a trade hub,"* and §18 makes the distinction explicit: *"Silence invites inference; denial forecloses it."*
Its marine sector is fishing, and `04` §3 does not list it among the national fish providers. What it develops
instead is technical and scientific work — three-quarters of that 35% sector is mandated, the largest single
mandate the city carries.

Sinheung develops chamber manufacture, one of only two such sites in the country. Its raw materials arrive
*"by truck from Mirny via the Hwy 110/Hwy 4 tri-junction at Zhongshan,"* and the developer ruled in 2026-09-02
that the finished chambers travel out the same way: *"there's enough highway access connecting at the
Tri-Cities… that shipping robot-synthesis/manufacture chambers via the ocean is not necessary."*

So the mechanism is: **this city supplies passage, and its neighbor supplies goods.** Hwy 110's northwestern
terminus is Zhongshan. The road that carries a neighbor's raw materials in and its manufactured output back
out is the one highway named for this city.

## The quiet friction, which is the nastier one

The loud thing here would be the port margin — two cities facing each other across the same water with
dominion settled by 450 meters. That is real and it is 5e's material.

The quieter fact is worse. **The junction employs nobody.** §18 rules it in terms: *"The three-highway junction
is NOT mandated. A crossroads is not automatically an employer."* The most conspicuous geographic asset this
city has — the point where the transcontinental highway ends and the regional network begins — confers no
economic standing on it at all, and the freight crossing it belongs to somebody else's industry.

Nobody is fighting about this. There is nothing to fight about, which is exactly what makes it the quiet one.

## Both directions

The outward half: this city is a crossing that national freight uses, and a source of technical people. §15's
own export clause reads *"Zhongshan produces engineers and researchers who end up across Tepenia."* §18 draws
the conclusion — *"Zhongshan's national contribution is PEOPLE, not cargo."*

The inward half is the one the first write omitted, and it is checkable rather than atmospheric.
`City_Cross_Subnet_Relationships.md` is the country's dedicated map of how cities relate to one another. Part 1
catalogs infrastructure; Part 2 catalogs established relationships. **Zhongshan appears in Part 1, as a highway
endpoint. It appears in Part 2 zero times.** Part 5 counts the Mirny subnet as fully covered on the strength of
the Part 1 appearance.

`Airports.md` adds the one case where a relationship was on offer. The Mountain Pass outpost was *"an act of
solidarity from the Tri-Cities,"* and the file traces the kinship city by city: Zhongshan's tie runs to Kunlun,
Shirayuki's to Vostok, Sinheung's to both. Sinheung *"had both reasons to push, and its neighbors were not
equally invested."*

So the inward answer is not that nobody thinks about this place. It is narrower: **the country's own relationship
map records this city as a location rather than as a party**, and the one regional venture that could have
changed that was, in canon's own words, not equally its own.

| Era | The claim |

| **~2688** | De facto amalgamation. Canon's cause is sprawl — *"physical growth… closes the remaining gaps between them."* The shore is still legally this city's, and still carries no dominion |

# 5c · The parent

## What the Federation requires

The technical and scientific export, mandated at three-quarters of the 35% sector — 148,786 workers, the city's
only mandate. And passage: the junction carries a neighbor's inbound raw materials and its outbound national
product, under a 2026-09-02 ruling that settled the whole cluster's logistics on the road network this city
terminates.

## What it supplies, and where the two disagree

The disagreement is exact, and canon states both halves of it. **The Federation routes a neighbor's national
output across this city's junction, and rules that the junction employs nobody here.** Required to host the
crossing; given no standing for hosting it.

Behind that sits the structural silence, `Falkland Treaty` Article III.1: settlements *"may, at their own
initiative, associate into regional groupings for the resolution of local questions, the coordination of shared
infrastructure, and the maintenance of order among themselves… Nothing in this Article shall be read to define
what authority sits above such regional groupings."*

The article that requires shared infrastructure to be coordinated regionally is the same article that declines
to name who stands above the regional grouping. This is not a gap the city fell into; it is one the country was
founded with.

## What this place believes about the parent, and whether it is accurate

The first write deferred this to Phase 6. That deferral had no license — the phase's failure-mode clause covers
skipping 5c when the parent is *unwritten*, and this parent is written.

The relevant fact is that **the treaty's silence is published.** It is in the founding document, not in a
practice, an omission, or a filing somebody would have to discover. Whatever else this city holds about the
Federation, it cannot hold the expectation that a national authority will settle a berthing dispute at Nella
Fjord — that was foreclosed in writing before the city existed.

So the accuracy answer is yes, and that is the finding worth having. The usual shape of this sub-question is a
place wrong about its parent in some revealing way. Here the expectation is correct, and correctness is not
comfort: there is no misunderstanding to clear up and no appeal that has not already been answered in advance.
What the parent supplies is certainty, and nothing else.

The felt version — whether that reads as abandonment, as independence, or as nothing much at all — is Phase 6's
and Phase 9's, after the read-last culture material. What is answered here is the structural half the spine asks
for.

# 5d · The edge and the crossing

## What "outsider" means here

The spine requires this definition before the experience question. The three cities share the airport and the
port, so arrival from inside the cluster crosses no working boundary. The membrane is not at the city limit.

But the cluster is not the boundary either, because the real gate is temporal. **The port's window is roughly
four months, November to March.** Outside it there is no arrival by sea at all. An outsider here is defined by
when they can get in as much as by where they are from.

## Arrival types, enumerated

| Route | Terms |
|---|---|
| **By sea, Nov–Mar only** | Berthing order set by whoever commands the port, which is not this city |
| **Hwy 22, transcontinental** | From Byrd via the South Pole. The eastern terminus of the Hwy 1 → Hwy 22 thread, which `City_Cross_Subnet_Relationships.md` calls *"the single most structurally important corridor in the whole network"* — Palmer subnet's only overland exit and Byrd's only link to the country. Not hitchhiking-valid |
| **Hwy 110, regional** | Zhongshan ↔ Concordia, via Davis, Mirny and Casey. Hitchhiking-valid its whole length |
| **By air** | Tri-Cities Airport, domestic only. No international gateway here |

Two things follow that the first write missed. The corridor carrying two subnets' entire overland access to the
rest of the country **ends here** — it does not pass through. And of the two overland routes, the long one
requires means while the domestic one does not: under the node-based hitchhiking ruling, someone with nothing
can reach this city from Concordia by waiting inside warm stopping places and talking to whoever stopped. The
transcontinental route offers no such thing.

## What converts a visitor into a member

The spine wants a mechanism rather than a ceremony, derived from the local economy, with no announcement.

`Ports.md` §5x supplies the local economy's distinguishing feature directly: *"A CONSTRUCTED port implies a
trade and a calendar. Somebody rebuilds it, on a schedule, and that is a job, a skill and a season nobody in a
rock-harbor city has ever heard of."* This is a CONSTRUCTED-tier port — paid every year, forever, killed by a
year nobody pays it.

**So the mechanism is having worked a rebuild.** The harbor is remade before each window opens; the work is
annual, scheduled, and shared across all three cities. A visitor is someone who has not yet been here for one.
Membership takes a year to acquire because the qualifying event happens once a year, nobody marks the
threshold, and the labor it admits you to is the cluster's rather than this city's — which is consistent with
the boundary the definition task found.

# 5e · Dependency

| Needs | From whom | Named? |
|---|---|---|
| A berth inside the four-month window | Whoever holds dominion at Nella Fjord — Sinheung, by 450 m | Named to the meter. `Ports.md` measures it: 0.71 km against this city's 1.16 km |
| The port itself, rebuilt | Shared labor across three cities, annually, permanently | Named — it is the tier definition |
| A body to decide berthing order when it is contested | Nobody | Named as absent, in the founding treaty |

`Ports.md` states the consequence plainly: *"berthing order decides whose cargo lands this year and whose waits
a full year. That is not a scheduling matter; it is an economy."*

That sharpens the deficit this pass has been tracking since `02_Spine.md` into something concrete. The
arbitration question at this location is not abstract. It is **who berths first**, the cost of losing is a full
year rather than a delay, and the question recurs every single season.

And `Ports.md` gives the shape of the answer over time: the interval between de facto amalgamation and legal
unification is roughly ninety years, and *"a port that must be rebuilt every season generates a dispute every
year… ninety arguments, each individually survivable, none of them settling anything."*

Two things to keep straight about that. **The three amalgamation stages and their dates are canon**
(`Tri-Cities_Region.md`, 2026-07-07). **The claim that the port is what forced stage 3 is marked `⚠ PROPOSAL`
in `Ports.md`'s own file, and that file explicitly says a culture pass on the cluster should decide whether to
adopt it.** This pass does not adopt it. Nothing above depends on it: the window, the shared tenure, the
dominion margin and the annual recurrence are all canon or developer ruling on their own.

What this city needs from elsewhere, then, is not goods. It sits on a food-term-100% oasis, it is a GROWER by
`16` §18's own baseline, and nothing in canon has it waiting on a shipment to survive. **What it needs is a
decision**, and that is the one thing nobody is appointed to make.

# Differentiation axis

**Present at everything, deciding nothing.**

This city hosts an airport rather than being served by one. It holds a shore on the fjord and can see the
opposite bank. It sits where the transcontinental highway ends and where a neighbor's national output crosses.
Its own highway runs to the only fully functioning city in the country. Every one of those is a real asset,
and canon assigns the decision attached to each one somewhere else: dominion to the neighbor by 450 meters,
the junction ruled not an employer, the national contribution a stream of people who leave.

*One-sentence test.* Delete every other city's name: *"This place hosts the end of the country's longest road,
its own shore, and a shared airport, and holds decision-making authority over none of them."* Survives, names
nobody, and is not true of a hub, an isolated location, or a place that merely lacks infrastructure.

Checked against `04`: not "isolated," not "central," not "contested." The axis is the gap between presence and
authority.
