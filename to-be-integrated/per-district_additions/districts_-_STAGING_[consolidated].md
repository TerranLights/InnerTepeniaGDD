# Districts — Consolidated Staging File

**What this is:** a staging area, not yet committed to canon. Consolidates the original hand-written draft
(`districts_-_short_details_[initial_drafting].txt`), the Grok-assisted expansion
(`districts_-_short_details_[Grok-suggested].txt`), and a full new pass of Additions and Social Cohesion
Mechanics developed 2026-07-29, district by district, grounded in `District_Canon_Reference.md`. Nothing here
is final — this is the working set to review before promoting individual pieces into the real canon files
(most likely `District_Canon_Reference.md` itself, or each district's own deeper reference files).

**Two corrections applied during this pass, not yet reflected in the original `.txt` files:**
- **Capricorn's Grok-suggested Additions had a literal unresolved placeholder** — "towering merit-based
  plazas with [[undetermined]]." Resolved here using the district's own already-established canon: the
  plazas feature Capricorn's real status-lighting system (higher-ranked buildings get more exterior light,
  "you can read the hierarchy from the street") — no invention needed, just naming what's already established.
- **A new lore fact surfaced 2026-07-29, worth flagging for later:** the player character does not begin the
  game in the Central Hub — Calethina's Lab sits at the Cancer/Taurus/Capricorn three-district corner. The
  game eventually ends **in, or more precisely *under*, the Hub.** This ruled out a proposed "Calethina's Lab
  Observation Deck" Hub addition (removed below) and should inform Hub-related quest/ending design later.

## Cancer — The Sanctuary (Nurturing/Protective)

- Personality: the city's emotional pressure valve — has absorbed everyone else's trauma for two centuries and never been asked if it's okay
- What you'd find: curved, home-like architecture with no straight lines, hydroponic soil and warm broth smells, elaborate memorial traditions
- Who lives there: caregivers, nurses, grief counselors, refugee families

**Being a real city:**
- the moment it takes care of its most vulnerable, unconditionally
- A true city is proven not by what it builds, but by who it refuses to let fall through the cracks.

**Additions:**
- major hospitals
- public (and private) parks
- quiet "listening gardens" with sound-dampening canopies and free grief-counselor kiosks
- communal broth kitchens that never close
- **Threshold Waiting Rooms** — small neighborhood spaces for people who've just received hard news to decompress before going home to family; not clinical, just quiet and warm
- **Second Chair Workshops** — a specialized craft trade dedicated to building/maintaining the memorial chairs and objects Cancer families keep for the deceased
- **Paired Creches** — neighborhood nurseries where newly-activated robots and human infants are cared for side by side
- **Communal Warm-Soak Houses** — literal shared warm-water bathing spaces, extending the district's warmth-as-care sensory identity into a vulnerable, non-hierarchical ritual
- **The Quiet Register** — a nonverbal doorway-token system signaling "I need help but can't ask directly right now"

**Small offices for educational training:**
- first aid
- trauma-informed caregiving & emotional first response
- basic hydroponics & soil-healing for domestic growers

**Social cohesion spaces/mechanisms:**
- open-air memorial circles where residents leave voice-notes or physical tokens for the city's collective grief
- rotating "care shifts" where any resident can sign up to sit with strangers for a few hours
- **Habit-Keeping Evenings** — families/companions "keeping" a deceased person's routines alive share what they've been doing, sometimes inviting neighbors to join a piece of it
- **Lullaby Nights** — recognized lullaby-masters perform publicly and regularly for the whole neighborhood, not just privately at bedtime
- **Open Door Hour** — a scheduled window where households signal they're open to drop-in visits, aimed at isolated residents
- **Caregiver Respite Circles** — a deliberate correction to Cancer's own blind spot (burned-out caregivers quietly reassigned with no acknowledgment); peer-facilitated circles for caregivers to receive care themselves
- **Broth Nights** — potluck gatherings built around cooking a lost person's favorite dish together
