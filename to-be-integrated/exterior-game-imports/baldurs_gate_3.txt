"Reactive Plating Pulse" (placeholder, not developer-chosen) — from Blade Ward
  Diegetic: brief nanoweave/reactive-plating surge routed through the jack-in link.
  Effect: -50% incoming Bludgeoning/Piercing/Slashing for 2 turns (self or one ally in jack-in range).
  Cost: 2 AP. Cooldown: 3 turns.
  Note: cleanest 1:1 conversion in this batch — BG3's own numbers (half damage, 2 turns) carry over
  almost untouched, only the delivery method changed (spell → jack-in pulse).

"Rapport Spike" (placeholder, not developer-chosen) — from Friends
  Diegetic: short-range neural-flattery/pheromone-compound pulse, Enchantment's diegetic default.
  Effect: target NPC's disposition improves for the duration (non-hostile targets only); on
  expiry OR if the target later realizes they were manipulated, real downside — reputation hit,
  possible hostility, matches BG3's own "-10 approval when it wears off" + Tactician+ "considered
  a crime" framing almost exactly.
  Cost: 2 AP. Cooldown: 3 turns. Non-combat-lean use case (dialogue/social encounters).
  Open question: should this actually be a Speech/Deception skill-gated perk instead of a flat
  quickhack, given how narrative-facing it is? Flagging for the actual conversion pass, not
  resolved here.

"Overwatch Assist" (placeholder, not developer-chosen) — from Guidance
  Diegetic: Bridge Unit coaching/analysis pulse, live-feeding pattern-recognition to the target.
  Effect: lowers one specific skill check's effective threshold for the target by a flat amount
  for the duration — same logic Skills.md already uses for "companion presence extends the
  detection floor," just temporary and jack-in-delivered instead of passive.
  Cost: 2 AP. Cooldown: 2 turns. Repeatable per BG3's own "repeatable" note.

"Micro-Drone Deploy" (placeholder, not developer-chosen) — from Mage Hand
  Diegetic: Conjuration's drone-deployment default — a small remote manipulator drone launched
  through the jack-in link.
  Effect: deploys a Tiny drone (low HP, no combat relevance) that can grab/shove/throw small
  objects at range without the player closing distance. Can't target characters/items directly
  (matches BG3's own restriction).
  Cost: 2 AP to deploy. Cooldown: long (BG3's own "1/Short Rest" pace — translate to something
  like 1/scene or a multi-turn cooldown well past combat length; exact number TBD).
  Note: mostly a utility/puzzle tool, not a combat quickhack — low priority for a first
  implementation pass but cheap to build once the drone-deployment tech exists for other entries
  (see Conjure Minor Elemental, Grasping Vine, etc. in the Level 4 triage — this could share tech).

"Holo-Decoy" (placeholder, not developer-chosen) — from Minor Illusion
  Diegetic: holographic decoy/noise-maker projector — Illusion's diegetic default, one of the
  framework's own named examples.
  Effect: projects a small illusory disturbance that compels nearby enemies/NPCs to investigate/
  turn toward it (out of combat: draws attention away from the player; in combat: same, weaker
  version). Castable while hidden; doesn't break the player's own stealth. Can't target
  characters/items.
  Cost: 2 AP. Cooldown: 3 turns.
  Note: strong overlap with existing Stealth/Sneak-adjacent gameplay — worth checking whether
  this duplicates something already in the stealth/detection system once that's designed (see
  TODO.md's "Sneaking and line-of-sight" backlog item — currently undesigned, so no conflict yet).

"Adaptive Shielding Pulse" (placeholder, not developer-chosen) — from Resistance (cantrip)
  Diegetic: Abjuration's diegetic default, same family as Reactive Plating Pulse above but tuned
  for a single chosen damage/status category rather than the fixed physical trio.
  Effect: raises the target's resistance threshold against one chosen damage type (per
  Damage_Types.md) or status-effect category for the duration.
  Cost: 2 AP. Cooldown: 3 turns.
  Note: pairs naturally with Reactive Plating Pulse above — consider whether these should actually
  be two ranks of the same quickhack (fixed physical resistance vs. flexible single-type choice)
  rather than two separate quickhacks. Flagging, not deciding here.

"Subvocal Modulator" (placeholder, not developer-chosen) — from Thaumaturgy
  Diegetic: Transmutation's chem-stim/cyberware lens — a subvocal resonance booster.

  Cost: 2 AP. Cooldown: 3 turns.
  Note: same open question as Rapport Spike above — narrative-facing enough that a skill-gated

  playtesting/design decide.

"Targeting Lock" (placeholder, not developer-chosen) — from True Strike
  Diegetic: smart-scope calibration / Bridge Unit target-lock pulse.
  Effect: boosts the player's next ranged attack's hit-chance or aimed-shot precision — direct
  hit on Skills.md's one legitimate combat-RNG context. Consumed after the first attack made
  while active (matches BG3's own "consumed after first attack" rule).
  Cost: 2 AP. Cooldown: 2 turns.
  Note: probably the single most mechanically load-bearing quickhack in this batch, since it's
  the only one that touches the game's actual RNG — worth extra playtesting attention once this
  reaches real implementation.

"Psy-Audio Harassment" (placeholder, not developer-chosen) — from Vicious Mockery
  Diegetic: targeted infrasound/psy-audio pulse, or a jack-in insult-injection if delivered
  directly rather than at range — Enchantment/Psychic's diegetic default.
  Effect: minor Psychic/Neural damage (Damage_Types.md) plus, on a failed flat Nerve/Humanity
  resistance threshold (Section 3's saving-throw substitution), degrades the target's next
  attack accuracy for 1 turn.
  Cost: 2 AP. Cooldown: 2 turns.
  Note: the one quickhack in this batch that deals damage — worth double-checking it doesn't
  functionally duplicate a Signal Weapon basic attack once that weapon category is fleshed out
  (Hacking_and_Traceability_System.md references Signal Weapons but doesn't yet have concrete
  numbers to compare against).
