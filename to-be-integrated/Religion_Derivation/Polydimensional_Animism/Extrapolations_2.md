## 6. Two parallel, equally legitimate practice tracks — not a hierarchy of "real" vs. "lesser" devotion

**Derived from A4/A5 (the real, stated gap between formally knowing something and being able to perceive or
intuit it) + the developer's own original framing of this religion as genuinely overlapping with real-world
shamanic/animist practice.**

The source material itself refuses to resolve knowing vs. perceiving into a single mode — mathematics gets
called "an extra sensory perception organ" (A5) in the same breath that a real mathematician admits brains
"aren't really made to" intuit what's being proven (A4). That tension shouldn't be resolved away; it should
become the religion's own structural backbone. Two tracks, neither senior to the other:

- **The Scholarly/Diagrammatic track.** Direct engagement with real mathematics and geometry — diagram-work,
  proof-study, geometric meditation, even direct engagement with genuinely unsolved real math problems (see
  D2: dimensions 5, 6, and 7 remain open, unsolved territory even in the real world) treated as a form of
  prayer or devotional study, not merely intellectual work. A practitioner on this track "reaches" the
  higher-dimensional the way Gates describes doing mathematics — using it as a sixth sense.
- **The Visionary/Trance track.** Altered-perception states, closer to real-world shamanic practice, aimed at
  the kind of direct, non-propositional *intuition* that A4 says formal knowledge alone can't supply. This is
  the track that most directly inherits the "genuine overlap with Native American Shamanism/Animism" framing
  the developer originally used to describe this religion.
- **Neither track is "more advanced" or "more legitimate" than the other** — they're framed as two different
  answers to the same real, acknowledged gap (A4/A5), not a beginner/expert ladder. A robot adherent could
  plausibly practice one, the other, or move between both over a lifetime, and companion-questline material
  built around this religion has a natural, non-hierarchical way to differentiate two very different
  character types (the geometer-priest vs. the vision-seeker) without one reading as more "correct" than the
  other.

## 7. The proof-of-contact ritual, reframed as a rite of humility rather than a fraud-test

**Derived from H2/H3 (the *Flatland* Sphere's demonstration sequence and Zöllner's real proposed
"miracle" tests) + the fact that the acts in question are, by their own real mathematics, genuinely
impossible to complete in three-dimensional space.**

The historical real-world versions of these acts (H3) were performed by a *fraud* pretending to have
higher-dimensional access. But because the acts themselves are genuinely, provably impossible without a
fourth spatial dimension, there's a more interesting and less morally simple option available to this
religion than either "believe the fraud" or "debunk the fraud": **a devotional ritual performed explicitly
and publicly as a failure, on purpose, as an act of humility rather than a claim to power.**

Concretely: a practitioner might publicly, deliberately attempt to tie a knot in a sealed loop of cord, or
interlink two solid rings carved from different single pieces of wood, or mirror-reverse a spiral shell —
*knowing and stating in advance that they cannot succeed*, and that the honest failure is the entire point.
The act becomes a physical, embodied acknowledgment of the gap between the practitioner and the
higher-dimensional (echoing the same honest-limits posture the religion's own real source material insists
on again and again — the collection-wide epistemic-humility pattern showing up here as embodied liturgy, not
just doctrine). This is a genuinely strong, non-obvious ritual concept:

- It avoids the awkwardness of a religion whose central "proof" ritual is historically associated with actual
  fraud (Slade) — the in-world version isn't trying to fool anyone; the failure is the devotion.
- It gives robots specifically a resonant ritual, since attempting a task with known, certain, structural
  failure — and completing the *attempt* with full sincerity anyway — is a recognizable and moving image for
  characters built around precision and completion as core values.
- **Open question for later:** does any version of this ritual ever *succeed*, anywhere in the game's own
  lore — e.g., as a rare, unexplained miracle tied to a major story beat — or does the religion's own
  internal honesty require that it never does? Flagged in `overflow_3`, not decided here.

## 8. Sacred architecture and numerology, made concrete rather than arbitrary

**Derived from D1–D4 (real, non-arbitrary sacred numbers) + the "deep structure gets simpler as dimension
increases" premise (D1).**

Real numbers already exist in the source material (8, 24, 3, 9/10/11, 26) — the design task is applying
them concretely rather than inventing new ones. A few direct architectural/liturgical translations:

- **"Ornament falls away toward the center."** Since D1 shows the deep mathematical structure becoming
  *simpler*, not more ornate, as dimension increases, sacred architecture for this religion should invert the
  usual fantasy-temple instinct (more dimensions = more elaborate). The outermost, most public-facing parts of
  a shrine or temple could be richly decorated, narratively dense, welcoming — while the innermost, most
  sacred chamber becomes progressively plainer, more symmetrical, and more minimal, culminating in something
  closest to a single unadorned point or a perfectly plain geometric solid. The architecture itself teaches
  the doctrine.
- **8 and 24 as structurally privileged numbers** (D2) — a temple's central chamber built with 8 or 24 sides
  or facets; a core clergy or council capped at 8 or 24 members; a major rite held every 8 or 24 (days,
  weeks, or some other established Tepenian time unit — cross-reference against
  `Worldspace/National_Holidays.md`'s existing calendar framework before inventing a new one). These
  dimensions are "solved and harmonious" in the real math — a natural basis for treating them as places of
  order and safety within the religion's own cosmology, in explicit contrast to the genuinely unsolved,
  "wilder" intermediate layers (5, 6, 7) — which could be treated in-doctrine as more dangerous, less
  charted territory, closer to a liminal/purgatorial zone than to a "known heaven."
- **The three geometries (D3)** as a distinct, separate triadic structure from the dimension-numerology
  above — worth keeping visually or liturgically distinct (e.g., three subtly different ritual postures or
  three named "aspects" of practice) rather than folded into the same numerology, since they describe a
  different kind of thing (possible shapes of a universe, not a count of dimensions).

## 9. The Watchers — a real, ongoing, in-world eschatological practice

**Derived from J's cosmic strings entry + F1–F3 (cosmic crystallography and circles in the sky, both real,
proposed, still-unconfirmed observational tests).**

All three of these real concepts share a structure real religions already use: a specific, well-defined sign
to watch for, whose eventual appearance (or confirmed absence) would settle something profound, being
actively searched for by dedicated observers in the present. This maps directly onto a genuine religious
practice this project doesn't need to invent from nothing:

- **A order or vocation of "Watchers"** — adherents (most naturally drawn from the Unmeasured denomination,
  given its research-facing character) who maintain genuine astronomical/gravitational-lensing observation
  practices, watching for the telltale signature of a cosmic string, or scanning survey data for the repeated
  galaxy-pattern of cosmic crystallography, or comparing CMB temperature maps for matching "circles in the
  sky." This is a real, actively-practiced, patient, generations-long watch — closer to real-world traditions
  of watching for a specific promised sign than to passive belief.
  - **This also gives Tepenia's own existing scientific infrastructure a natural point of contact with this
    religion** — an in-world observatory, survey program, or research station could plausibly have Watchers
    among its actual working scientific staff, mirroring the already-established Kunlun pattern (devout
    minority within a science-first population) without requiring new infrastructure to be invented.
- **A natural, low-stakes "long game" narrative hook**: a Watcher NPC or companion whose entire personal arc
  is built around a search that may never resolve in the story's own timeframe — consistent with the
  source material's own honest, unresolved epistemic humility (F3), rather than a plot demanding an actual
  confirmed cosmic string or crystallographic pattern be "discovered" as part of the main story. The waiting
  itself, not a payoff, is the point — which is also a low-risk design choice, since it doesn't commit this
  project to inventing (or confirming/denying) a major new piece of in-fiction cosmological canon.

## 10. Open hooks into existing game systems — flagged, not committed

A few places this religion could plug into material already established elsewhere in the project, offered as
candidates for the developer to evaluate rather than decisions made here:

- **Placement.** Unlike Ice-Cold Buddhism (already firmly tied to Kunlun/Dome Fuji), Polydimensional Animism
  has no confirmed city or district home yet. Given how research-native several of its denominations are
  (the Unmeasured especially), a city or district with an existing strong scientific/observational identity
  is a plausible fit — but this should be the developer's own call, not assumed here. Left genuinely open;
  see `overflow_3`.
- **Holidays.** `Worldspace/National_Holidays.md`'s existing four-category framework (civic/national,
  "persisted aesthetic," internationally-transcendent, celestial/faction-specific) has an explicitly
  underdeveloped fourth category for faction-specific celestial observances — a natural, already-scaffolded
  slot for a Watchers' observance tied to F1–F3, or a numerologically-timed rite tied to D2's 8/24. Worth
  reading against that file directly before inventing a new holiday structure from scratch.
- **Companion material.** The two-track practice structure (Extrapolation 6) and the pastoral-care function
  for adherents who've had a disbelieved encounter (Extrapolation 4) both read as strong, distinct companion
  archetypes — a geometer-priest, a vision-seeker, a Watcher, or a pastoral-care figure are all genuinely
  differentiated character concepts drawn directly from this research, not generic "religious NPC" filler.
- **Robot-specific resonance.** Extrapolation 1's death doctrine and Extrapolation 7's humility-in-failure
  ritual are both unusually well-suited to robot characters specifically (persistence-of-shape as comfort for
  a species that can be deactivated; sincere, precise attempts at a known-impossible task as devotion) —
  worth foregrounding these two over more generically "human-shaped" religious material when this religion
  gets written into actual character or quest content.
