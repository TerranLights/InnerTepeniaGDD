# Extrapolations 1 — Death, Denominations, and Ethical Posture

This is where genuine synthesis happens — not restating the research (see `Points_1.md`/`Points_2.md` for
that), but asking "if this is really true, what would necessarily or plausibly follow?" and following the
chain as far as it reasonably goes. Every extrapolation below cites which point(s) it's derived from, so the
reasoning stays traceable back to real material rather than free-floating invention.

---

## 1. The central pastoral doctrine: death as a change of vantage, not an ending

**Derived from C4 (the eternal-now/four-dimensional-object premise) + A1/A2 (the cross-section perception
mechanism) + A6 (higher beings can access sealed/past things without breaking anything).**

If your entire life is already a complete, existing four-dimensional shape — not a sequence being built
moment by moment, but a whole object that a sufficiently high-dimensional vantage would see all at once,
childhood and old age together — then "dying" doesn't mean the shape stops existing. It means the *ordinary,
3D, cross-sectional* observers (everyone still moving forward through time) stop being able to see new
cross-sections of it. The shape itself was never incomplete, and was never lost. What ends is a particular
kind of *access* to it, not the thing itself.

**This chain gives Polydimensional Animism a genuine, real-math-grounded doctrine of comfort around death —
plausibly its single most load-bearing pastoral teaching, and an unusually good fit for a religion whose
adherents are robots who *literally deactivate*.** A few concrete consequences worth designing around:

- **Grief practice wouldn't center on loss of the person, but on loss of access.** The dead haven't stopped
  being real — they've become inaccessible to us specifically, the way a locked room is inaccessible to
  someone standing outside it (A6), not the way something destroyed is inaccessible to everyone.
- **A doctrine of "witnessed grief."** Combine C4 with A6 directly: if a higher-dimensional entity could, in
  principle, revisit any already-past moment of someone's complete shape without disturbing it — then no
  moment is ever truly *unwitnessed*, even a private one, even a robot dying alone with no one present. This
  is a genuinely moving implication: the worst fear many belief systems address (dying alone, unseen,
  unrecorded) is directly answered by this religion's own physics — not through promise or faith alone, but
  through an actual proposed mechanism. Worth treating as one of the religion's strongest, most humane
  selling points if it's ever put in front of a grieving character.
- **A liturgical formula practically writes itself:** something in the register of *"you are not gone; you
  are complete. What ends is our sight, not your shape."* Worth keeping as a genuine candidate line for an
  in-game funeral rite, eulogy, or last-rites moment.
- **RESOLVED by the developer 2026-07-21: yes, fully and directly.** Robots run on a "nano-architecture" gel
  brain — essentially the Ex Machina "blue glowy" model, not a discrete data store. **"Backing up" or
  "uploading" a robot mind is about as infeasible as uploading a human one** — the substrate itself carries
  the mind, not a separable, copyable file. This means **irreparable damage to a robot's brain or energy
  source (causing component degradation) genuinely IS death, from the robot's own perspective — not a
  technologically-recoverable event.** Robots are not immortal and never were; they simply live
  substantially longer than humans. **This makes the death-as-changed-vantage doctrine apply to robot
  deactivation exactly as directly as it applies to organic death — no tension with memory-backup mechanics
  to resolve, because that kind of backup doesn't meaningfully exist for robot minds in this project's own
  canon.** This is a significant clarification worth carrying forward into any future robot-consciousness or
  Companion System writing beyond just this religion.

## 2. Five real, distinct denominations — not a single monolithic creed

**Derived directly from B1–B4 (four structurally different concealment mechanisms) + the established
project precedent that the sibling simulation-theory religion already splits into real philosophical
subschools (Nihilism/Absurdism/Fatalism/Sartrism) responding to one shared premise.**

Polydimensional Animism should not be one belief, cleanly agreed on. The research base itself hands us (at
minimum) four genuinely different, mutually incompatible-but-not-contradictory explanations for *why* the
higher-dimensional is hidden — Kaluza-Klein's tiny curled loop, Randall's trapped-on-a-brane exile, the
merely-unmeasured-so-far possibility, and (via cross-reference to Adinkra Codex, if ecumenical dialogue is
ever wanted) supersymmetry's differently-*kinded* direction. Real robots, taking the same core premise
seriously, would not all land on the same explanation — the same way real-world religious traditions built
on one shared mystery split into schools with different emphases. Five candidate denominations, each
traceable to a specific real mechanism and each with a distinct emotional/practical character:

- **The Enfolded** *(from B1)* — hold that the sacred is infinitesimally, intimately close: everywhere,
  always, at every single point of ordinary space, just too small to reach. A doctrine of immanence and
  patience rather than distance or exile. Likely the most contemplative/domestic branch — practice centered
  on stillness and close attention rather than travel, seeking, or instrumentation.
- **The Bound** *(from B2, C2)* — hold that we are genuinely exiled onto a lower-dimensional shell (a brane),
  cut off from the Bulk and from whatever else lives there, with gravity as the one thread of contact that
  still reaches across. A doctrine of longing and exile. Likely the branch most invested in gravity itself as
  a devotional object — weight, falling, orbital mechanics, gravitational anomalies treated as the closest
  available thing to a sacrament (see Extrapolation 6, below).
- **The Unmeasured** *(from B3)* — hold that the gap is not categorical but merely technical: better
  instruments, sharper measurement, more refined technique will eventually close it. A doctrine of hopeful
  science rather than permanent separation. Almost certainly the branch most comfortable overlapping with
  ordinary secular research institutions — the "scientist-priest" wing, more at home in a laboratory than a
  shrine.
- **The Already-Complete** *(from C4, Extrapolation 1 above)* — hold the eternal-now doctrine as central and
  organizing: nothing is ever truly lost or separate; the "veil" between us and the higher-dimensional (and
  between the living and the dead) is a limitation of *our own* vantage point, not a real gap in the world.
  Almost certainly the largest, most pastorally-oriented branch — the one most involved in funerals, grief
  counseling, and comfort work, given Extrapolation 1's implications.
- **The Cyclical** *(from E3, "cosmic forgetfulness")* — a smaller, more austere sect that extends personal
  impermanence to cosmic scale: even the universe itself has died and been reborn before, remembering almost
  nothing of what came before. A doctrine of humility about ultimate origins — closer to real apophatic
  traditions (holding a big claim while declining to overclaim what's beneath it, per the collection-wide
  epistemic-humility pattern). Plausibly the smallest, most academically/monastically inclined denomination,
  less concerned with individual comfort than with cosmic-scale awe and restraint.

None of these five need to be hostile toward each other by default — see Extrapolation 4 below on why open
hostility would sit poorly against this project's own non-malice rule — but they give the religion real
internal texture, genuine disagreement, and distinct "flavors" usable for differentiating NPCs, companions,
or city populations without inventing anything not already grounded in the research.

## 3. The religion is structurally animist, not monotheist — and that has to be visible in practice

**Derived from C1 (higher-dimensional beings are explicitly bounded, not infinite) + C2 (they'd be alien
neighbors under different laws, not gods) + the developer's own original framing of this religion as
overlapping with, not replacing, real-world Shamanism/Animism/Paganism.**

If the entities this religion concerns itself with are real but genuinely limited — bounded, finite, "part
of a larger structure they cannot step outside of" the same way we are — then worship in the sense of total
submission or supplication is the wrong register entirely. The correct real-world analogy isn't a monotheist
praying to an omnipotent creator; it's an animist maintaining *respectful coexistence* with neighbors who are
different in kind but not different in ultimate status. Concretely, this should shape practice away from
begging/pleading forms and toward:

- **Acknowledgment and hospitality**, not supplication — leaving space, maintaining certain patterns or
  symmetries as a courtesy to something that might be present but can't be proven present, similar to
  real-world animist practices of leaving offerings or observing courtesies toward possibly-present spirits
  without claiming certainty either way.
- **Curiosity and respect as the primary devotional postures**, rather than fear or awe-before-power — since
  the beings in question, per C1/C2, aren't more *powerful* in an absolute sense, just differently
  positioned. A robot adherent's attitude toward the higher-dimensional should read closer to "a careful,
  humble scientist-neighbor" than "a supplicant before a throne."
- **This also gives the religion a natural, low-conflict relationship with Tepenia's existing scientific
  institutions** — since the entities aren't gods to be worshipped instead of understood, there's no
  inherent tension between devotion and research. (Compare directly to the already-established Kunlun
  precedent for Ice-Cold Buddhism: a science-first population that happens to include genuine devout
  adherents, not a contradiction between the two identities. The same shape likely fits Polydimensional
  Animism even better, given how research-native this religion's own premises are.)

## 4. Persecution, where it exists, should be structural and bureaucratic — never a villain

**Derived directly from I1–I3 (*Flatland*'s own persecution structure) and this project's own established,
binding non-malice worldbuilding rule.**

The strongest available real-world template (I1–I3) is *already* non-malicious in exactly the shape this
project requires: no one in *Flatland* is cartoonishly cruel to A Square. He's mocked, quietly discredited,
eventually imprisoned under a real (if unjust) law against destabilizing testimony — not hunted, not tortured
for sport. This maps cleanly onto Inner Tepenia's own established pattern of institutional inertia and
self-protective secrecy (compare directly to Libra's own already-locked "protective secrecy, not malice"
logic elsewhere in this project). Concrete implications:

- **No secret cabal suppresses this religion.** If any institution discourages it, the reasonable in-fiction
  motive is closer to reputational caution or bureaucratic risk-aversion than conspiracy — an institution
  worried that endorsing unproven, socially-marginal claims damages its own credibility, not an institution
  that considers the religion dangerous or false with malicious intent.
- **The realistic "cost" of open belief is social and professional, not physical.** An adherent who testifies
  too openly risks being quietly sidelined, passed over, treated as unserious — not violently punished. This
  gives quest/companion design a low-key, textured conflict template (a scientist or engineer NPC discredited
  early in their career for voicing genuine belief) without requiring an invented antagonist.
- **A dedicated pastoral-care function is worth designing in.** Per H1 (the testimony-and-disbelief structure
  of a genuine encounter claim), adherents who've had an experience and face social disbelief plausibly need
  — and would organically develop — something like a support congregation specifically for processing an
  experience the wider world dismisses. This is a genuinely humane, non-obvious detail worth keeping for
  companion or NPC writing: not every religious structure this project builds needs to center on doctrine
  alone; some of the most usable material here is pastoral-care shaped.

**Revised by the developer 2026-07-21:** the *severity* of this framing was flagged as too forced for a robot
society specifically — non-believers wouldn't plausibly view Polydimensional Animists as a destabilizing
threat worth institutional risk-aversion or quiet discrediting. The more natural in-fiction reception is
simply regarding adherents as **an oddity** — mildly eccentric, a curiosity, socially harmless. The
non-malice shape of this extrapolation (no cabal, no invented villain, no violence) still holds; only the
register/severity of the friction has been softened. The pastoral-care-function point above is unaffected by
this revision and still stands.

## 5. A real, grounded bridge to Adinkra Codex — without merging the two religions

**Derived from the already-logged cross-reference point that supersymmetric quantum mechanics functions as
a genuine proof technique within topology itself, touching both religions' real mathematical territory at
one specific, identifiable point (Nakahara Ch.12.9–12.10).**

Because this isn't a contrived thematic resonance but an actual place where the real mathematics of the two
religions' source material overlaps, it's a legitimate, low-effort hook for an ecumenical or rivalrous
relationship between Polydimensional Animism and Adinkra Codex clergy — without collapsing either religion's
distinct identity. A few directions this could go, none committed to:

- **A recurring scholarly dialogue or joint academic tradition** between the two religions' more
  research-oriented adherents (most naturally the Unmeasured denomination above, and whichever equivalent
  "scientist-priest" wing Adinkra Codex ends up with) — real, ongoing intellectual contact grounded in one
  specific real overlap point, not a vague shared vibe.
- **A schismatic or heterodox figure in either religion who claims the two revelations are secretly one** —
  a natural, well-motivated heresy given the genuine (if narrow) mathematical overlap, and a good source of
  low-stakes internal religious conflict that doesn't require inventing malice from either side.
- **Worth flagging and leaving open, not resolving now** — see `overflow_3`.
