# Long Vigil Endings

## Trigger Condition

**See `Game-Mechanics/Core-Mechanics/Fragmentation_Matrix.md` for the full underlying Bond/Grief grid** — The
Long Vigil is the bottom-right cell of that two-axis system (Grief Range 3 + Bond Range 3 simultaneously),
not a standalone mechanic invented just for these endings, exactly as Wild Child is the bottom-right cell of
the Fame/Infamy grid.

These endings are available when the player holds **The Long Vigil** status — total devotion and total
unresolved grief, simultaneously — with multiple districts of Concordia. The Long Vigil is not mixed
feeling in the sense of some districts loving the current self while others miss the old one. It is a
single district holding both at once, because the player has both fully become someone that district trusts
completely *and* left behind someone that district never finished mourning.

The Long Vigil is not ambivalence. Both feelings are correct in what they are responding to.

**The Long Vigil disqualifies the player from:**
- Any ending requiring a purely "Whole" or unchanged identity history (The Long Vigil requires having
  re-specced enough, with enough history, to be genuinely grieved)
- District endings that require uncomplicated Devoted status (The Long Vigil is not uncomplicated — it
  carries real, permanent grief alongside the devotion)
- Any resolution premised on "the past self is gone and nobody misses them" — by definition, someone does,
  intensely, permanently

**Long Vigil endings are tiered by count:**
- 3–4 districts at The Long Vigil → The Kept Record
- 5–7 districts at The Long Vigil → The Living Memorial
- 8–12 districts at The Long Vigil → Elegy for the Living
- All 13 districts at The Long Vigil → The Perpetual Wake

A player who meets a higher threshold also technically meets all lower thresholds. The game should route to
the highest qualifying ending.

**Why this is naturally rarer than Wild Child:** per the Fragmentation Matrix's own district calibration,
only Taurus and Cancer read as genuinely high-Grief-capable by default — most others are utility- or
curiosity-driven with little institutional mourning capacity. Reaching even the lowest tier requires the
player to have built deep, specific history that a district genuinely loved *before* re-speccing away from
whatever earned that devotion. This can't be farmed the way reputation can; it requires having actually
mattered, in a specific configuration, for its loss to register institutionally.

---

## Design Philosophy

Every other ending category built on a reputation or fragmentation state asks either *what does the city
think of you* (Reputation Matrix) or *what did you do to yourself* (Identity Fragmentation Endings). The
Long Vigil asks a third thing: *what does it mean that the people and institutions around you hold both a
complete embrace of who you are and a complete, unclosed grief for who you were, at the same time, forever?*

Wild Child's paradox is moral — hero and villain, both true. The Long Vigil's paradox is temporal — presence
and loss, both true, about the same person, held by people who are looking directly at that person while
they hold it. The city is not confused about which one is real. It has simply concluded that both are, and
built no framework for a citizen who is both loved now and mourned always.

The Long Vigil endings should feel unlike Wild Child's endings, even though they share a chassis. Wild Child
is about exceeding a system's capacity to judge. The Long Vigil is about exceeding a system's capacity to
let go — while the person in question is standing right there, alive, present, asking nothing of anyone.

## LV-1. The Kept Record
*The Long Vigil with 3–4 districts*

Three or four districts have each, independently, made the same quiet administrative choice: rather than
overwrite the record of who the player was when the player changed, they kept it — filed alongside, not
instead of, the record of who the player is now. Nobody voted on this. Nobody announced a policy. Each
district's own record-keeping apparatus simply declined to treat the earlier self as superseded.

**World Trigger**
A Libra administrative document — the same civic-identity-record type that triggers Identity Fragmentation's
own IF-1 — but where IF-1's document reads as internally contradictory, this one reads as internally
*reconciled*. Two dated entries, side by side, both stamped valid, neither marked superseded: one describing
who the player was, written with the same formal, respectful weight a closure record would carry, one
describing who the player is now, written as an ordinary active citizen entry. The document does not resolve
the two into one continuous story. It simply keeps both, on purpose. The player reads it and understands: an
institution decided, without being asked, not to let go of someone it lost.

**The End**
The player completes an action that specifically requires standing in both records at once — something a
district will only release, grant, or honor for someone it has verified as *both* the person it once trusted
completely *and* the person it trusts completely now. Not a workaround, not a loophole: a real institutional
act of faith kept across a change most systems would have treated as a discontinuity. The moment the district
performs that act — releasing something, honoring something, formally recognizing something — on the strength
of both records simultaneously, is the ending.

**Main Negative Effect on Concordia**
The district's choice to keep both records rather than overwrite becomes precedent — an administrative
practice of never fully closing a citizen's history now quietly applies to everyone in that district, not
just the player. The record-keeping apparatus that made an exception out of care for one specific person is
now carrying a slowly growing archive of every citizen's abandoned former selves, with no policy for when (or
whether) any of them are ever allowed to close.

**Minor Negative Effects**
- The other districts that do *not* hold The Long Vigil learn of the practice and treat it as either
  sentimental inefficiency or a genuine model worth adopting — a live disagreement across district lines
  about whether keeping unclosed records is compassion or a governance flaw
- The specific administrator or record-keeper who first declined to overwrite the player's earlier entry is
  now personally associated with the decision, for better or worse, and inherits scrutiny for every future
  case that tests the same precedent
- Companions or NPCs who knew the earlier self and see the kept record react to seeing it formalized — for
  some, relief that it wasn't erased; for others, discomfort that grief has been filed rather than felt
- The player's own administrative identity is now permanently dual-dated in at least one district's systems
  — a small, permanent, official acknowledgment that they have been more than one person, kept rather than
  reconciled

## LV-2. The Living Memorial
*The Long Vigil with 5–7 districts*

Five or more districts hold The Long Vigil. What was quiet record-keeping in the first tier has become
something more visible: informal memorial practice, sincerely observed, dedicated to a version of the player
who is not dead — who is, in fact, standing nearby, fully alive, fully present, fully a different person now.

**World Trigger**
The player encounters an actual small memorial in one of the Long Vigil districts — flowers, a plaque, a
remembrance object, placed by someone (or several people) who knew the earlier self. It is not vandalism. It
is not mockery. It is sincerely maintained, the way any real memorial is maintained, for someone who is, by
every measure that matters to the person tending it, gone. The player is looking at part of their own wake,
being held while they are alive to see it.

**The End**
The player visits the memorial and chooses to let it stand — acknowledging it, perhaps adding something to
it, without correcting it, dismantling it, or insisting that "that person" is actually still here in some
unbroken sense. An act of letting the mourning exist on its own terms, while remaining fully present and
engaged as who they are now. The moment of that acknowledgment, witnessed or unwitnessed, is the ending.

**Main Negative Effect on Concordia**
The living-memorial practice does not stay contained to the player. Word spreads of what it looked like and
why it mattered to whoever built it, and the practice becomes a recognized, if unusual, civic form — mourning
someone who is still alive becomes a real, repeatable thing people in these districts now know how to do,
available to anyone else whose transformation is significant enough to earn it. Concordia has gained a new
cultural practice, and it exists because of what the player's own history made visible.

**Minor Negative Effects**
- Some companions or NPCs who knew the earlier self find the memorial practice a genuine comfort; others find
  it unbearable — a live disagreement within the same community about whether this is a healthy way to grieve
  or a refusal to actually grieve at all
- The memorial's caretaker (whoever built and maintains it) forms a specific, ongoing relationship to the
  practice that outlasts any single conversation with the player — this is now part of who they are, not a
  one-time gesture
- Other districts without a Long Vigil condition regard the practice with curiosity or unease, unsure whether
  it represents a healthy new civic tradition or the erosion of what mourning is supposed to mean
- The player, now aware the memorial exists and persists, carries the knowledge that some part of how they
  are remembered is permanently and sincerely divorced from who they currently are — an odd, specific kind of
  loneliness available to very few people

## LV-3. Elegy for the Living
*The Long Vigil with 8–12 districts*

Eight or more districts hold The Long Vigil. The city's own history-making apparatus — Cancer's oral
tradition, Scorpio's communal archive, Libra's administrative footnotes, Gemini's Truth Markets — has begun
producing something new: formal, competing elegies for who the player was, running in permanent parallel
with an ongoing, living account of who they are. The player is alive. Still physically present. Still making
choices. None of this has stopped entire institutions from writing their eulogy in the present tense.

**World Trigger**
A terminal in the Janbogo Subnet Nexus, the same infrastructure that produces Wild Child's own competing
data streams — but running an "In Memoriam" feed for the earlier self alongside a "Current Citizen Record"
feed for the present one, updating simultaneously, neither retracted, neither treated as an error. The system
that is supposed to converge on a single truth about a person has instead concluded that both truths need to
keep running, indefinitely, side by side. The player finds themselves reading their own eulogy, written with
real care, about someone who is not dead, while their own current record updates in the adjacent column.

**The End**
The player makes a single unambiguous choice not to correct, merge, or retract either feed — letting the
elegy and the living record run in permanent parallel, witnessed. Unlike Wild Child's silence (which is a
refusal to intervene in a battle over judgment), this silence is a form of consent: the player allows
themselves to be mourned and to be present at the same time, on purpose, without resolving the tension for
the institutions doing both.

**Main Negative Effect on Concordia**
Concordia's historical apparatus has permanently gained the capacity — and the practiced habit — of writing
elegies for the living. This changes how the city will process every future citizen whose transformation is
significant enough to warrant it, for better or worse. Grief, in Concordia, is no longer something that
requires a death to justify — a philosophically loaded precedent that some future citizen, robot or human,
will eventually need mourned on their behalf while very much still alive, whether or not that's what they
would have wanted.

**Minor Negative Effects**
- Cancer's oral tradition and Scorpio's archive now hold fully realized, emotionally serious elegiac accounts
  of a person who is still walking around Concordia — a genuinely strange category of historical record that
  didn't exist before and will need to be reckoned with by anyone using either archive going forward
- Gemini's Truth Markets, whose entire premise is convergence toward a single verified account, now carry a
  permanent, acknowledged exception to that premise — a crack in the Markets' own claim to resolve all
  competing accounts eventually
- The elegies' authors — whoever within each district actually wrote them — become minor, semi-public figures
  associated specifically with mourning someone who is still alive, a strange kind of authorship with no real
  precedent
- Future historians examining this period will have to explain, without a clean answer, why formal elegiac
  material exists for a citizen who did not die during the period the elegy claims to cover

## LV-4. The Perpetual Wake
*The Long Vigil with all 13 districts*

Every district in Concordia simultaneously holds total devotion to who the player is and total, unresolved
grief for who they were. No institution in the city has a category for this. Every district's own systems —
administrative, historical, communal — have independently reached the same conclusion: the player currently
standing among them is both the person they trust completely and the site of a loss they have never stopped
mourning, and this is not a contradiction anyone has been able to resolve, correct, or wait out. It has simply
been true, continuously, for as long as anyone can now remember.

**World Trigger**
The Hub's central administrative registry — the same system whose permanent error state anchors Wild Child's
own WC-4 — but here, the anomaly isn't an unfilable record. It's a record that filed successfully in a state
the system was never designed to produce: a **closure entry** (the format normally used to mark a citizen as
deceased, their file formally concluded) filed *alongside* a fully active current-citizen entry for the same
identity, both permanently valid, neither superseding the other. The registry does not know how to reconcile
a closed file and an open one belonging to the same person, because under every rule it was built with, that
should be impossible. It has simply accepted both, because both are true, and it has no mechanism to reject
either.

A player who accesses this dual-active record — through whatever route the near-full-city Long Vigil
condition opens, paralleling how Wild Child opens Hub access no ordinary citizen relationship would grant —
finds their own file open in two states at once. The closure entry is written the way any closure entry is
written: complete, respectful, final in tone. The active entry beside it is written the way any living
citizen's entry is written: ongoing, current, unfinished. Both are correct. That specific dual-state record,
in that specific terminal, is the trigger.

**The End**
The player opens an input field in their own dual-active record and writes a statement — not addressed, as
Wild Child's WC-4 statement is, to "what I consider myself to be," but to something else: an acknowledgment
of what was lost, and of who is still standing here to say so. The system accepts it and files it as a
permanent, dual-timestamped entry, attached to both the closure record and the active one, resolving nothing
and overwriting nothing. The ending registers at the moment the terminal closes. The city will carry that
dual-active record, and the player's own words inside it, indefinitely.

**Main Negative Effect on Concordia**
The existence of a citizen whose file is simultaneously and permanently closed and open demonstrates that
Concordia's most basic distinction — between someone who is gone and someone who is here — is not as stable
as the city's governance assumed. Libra must decide whether this dual-active status could ever be permitted
for anyone else, which means deciding, for the first time in the city's administrative history, whether
grief and presence are allowed to be simultaneously, officially true for a living person. Whichever way the
decision goes, it will be applied to people who are not the player, in circumstances the player cannot
anticipate and will not be present to answer for.

**Minor Negative Effects**
- The dual-active registry entry becomes a permanent administrative curiosity — referenced, studied, and
  eventually cited as precedent by people arguing very different things about what it should mean for the
  city's future record-keeping
- Every district's simultaneous total devotion and total unresolved grief toward the same individual
  demonstrates that the Bond/Grief system, like the Fame/Infamy system before it, can be saturated in both
  directions at once — a fact Concordia's institutions did not previously have to plan around and now do
- The city's oral, archival, and administrative traditions each now contain a fully realized account of
  mourning a person who never died — a category of grief with no precedent, and no guarantee it stays rare
  once the city knows it's possible
- Whatever framework Libra ultimately builds to handle the dual-active record sets a precedent that will
  eventually be tested by someone whose situation looks nothing like the player's, in ways neither Libra nor
  the player could have designed for

## Implementation Notes

- Long Vigil endings are **tiered and exclusive**, exactly like Wild Child: a player qualifying for LV-4 does
  not also access LV-1 through LV-3. The game routes to the highest qualifying ending.
- The Long Vigil condition is **rarer than Wild Child by design** — see the Fragmentation Matrix's own
  calibration work: most districts default toward low institutional Grief capacity, so reaching even LV-1
  requires the player to have built and then left behind something a district genuinely, deeply valued.
- **LV-4's trigger requires Hub access** during a specific window, structurally parallel to WC-4's own
  access condition — the near-full-city Long Vigil state should organically open access no ordinary
  citizen relationship would, rather than requiring a separate quest to explain it.
- These endings do not "resolve" The Long Vigil condition, the same way Wild Child's endings don't resolve
  Wild Child. They are not closure and not a correction of the paradox. They are endings that let the city
  keep holding both things at once, permanently, with the player's own knowledge and consent.
- **Distinct from companion-level Long Vigil content:** these four endings are the district/faction-scale,
  citywide expression of The Long Vigil. A companion individually reaching The Long Vigil with the player
  unlocks a separate, personal Long-Vigil-only questline pathline (see `Fragmentation_Matrix.md` and
  `Companion_System.md`'s Personal Questline Design Rule) — the two systems share a name and a trigger shape
  but are not the same content, the same way Wild Child's citywide endings and its companion-questline route
  recommendation are related but distinct.
- As with all secret endings: these are complete conclusions, not punishments. A player who reaches LV-4 has
  built something no other player built, sustained across enough districts and enough history that eight or
  more institutions independently arrived at the same impossible, permanent truth about them.
