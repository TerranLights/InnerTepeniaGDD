# District Under-Questlines

**What this is:** the actual output of `District_Under_Questline_Design_Method.md`, run district by district. Companion document to `District_Main_Questlines.md` — that file holds each district's one required capstone quest; this file holds everything else naturally discoverable within a district, non-main, plural, never load-bearing for any ending condition. Unlike the main questline method, candidates here are **not narrowed down** — every candidate that passes the method's four tests (anchored to a starting point, non-conflicting, characteristically consistent, actually emergent) is kept as real content. A district can run any number of these in parallel. Floor of 5 per district, ideally 15–20; none of the districts below are necessarily finished at whatever count they currently sit at.

Each candidate's **consequence-test** applies the method's own default rule: whether the district can handle the results of its own established civic belief, not whether that belief is fair, true, or correct.

## Cancer

**Civic belief (Concordia Section I):** *"a city becomes real the moment it takes care of its most vulnerable, unconditionally... proven not by what it builds, but by who it refuses to let fall through the cracks."*

**Status: 18 of 15–20 target. Effectively complete.**

### 1. The Denied Crisis
**Starting point:** a specific Cancer care facility, mid-crisis, visibly under-resourced.
**Chain:** the player investigates why Libra hasn't responded, and finds a paper trail that's identical whether the cause is ordinary bureaucratic failure or Libra's own structural incentive to keep Cancer's suffering visible-but-unresolved (its "face" that makes Libra's fairness feel concrete). No evidence can ever distinguish the two.
**Fork:** publicly accuse Libra (explosive, unprovable); quietly fix the immediate crisis (safe, changes nothing structural); or pursue the slower, harder path aimed at the Libra-Cancer relationship itself.
**Consequence-test:** Cancer's belief says it cares for the vulnerable unconditionally — this asks whether that's still true when the thing standing between Cancer and adequate resources might be baked into the city's own politics, not fixable by trying harder.
**Source:** `Final_Megasheet_Data_Processing/Questlines/in-district_questlines/01_Cancer.md`, Thread 1; `District_Megasheets/01_Cancer/Cancer_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md`, Finding 1.

### 2. What the Ledger Doesn't Say
**Starting point:** the Zhongshan-descended caregiver (`Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section V) — a specific, named resident who practices her home culture's non-prying norm inside the Mother's Circuit.
**Chain:** incidental conversation reveals she's carrying a fragment of exactly the frontier knowledge Sagittarius' Memory Keepers are racing to preserve before it's lost forever.
**Fork:** alert Sagittarius (helps close a real continental preservation crisis, risks re-traumatizing her); respect her wish to leave the past behind; or find a third way — plausibly via the senior Gemini data archaeologist Notable Figure already flagged for exactly this cross-district connection (`Concordia_Ultra_Megasheet/Concordia_Full_Extrapolation.md`).
**Consequence-test:** Cancer's whole ethos is *not asking* — this is the first moment where not-asking has a real, external cost to someone besides the person being protected.
**Source:** in-district Questlines, Thread 2; Cross-Reference Finding 2; Throughway 3.

### 3. The Circuit House
**Starting point:** the Circuit House (`Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section VI) — an already-named location housing the Mother's Circuit's aggregated monitoring data.
**Chain:** something routine leads the player inside, where they find the monitoring capability is far more centralized than publicly acknowledged, plausibly under a specific figure or small group's effective control.
**Fork:** expose it publicly (real institutional fracture risk, given Cancer's already-strained capacity); use it quietly for leverage; or work slowly to rebalance the four factions without a public reckoning.
**Consequence-test:** the Trauma-Bearers specifically need this same surveillance apparatus to prevent another Overcrowding Decision — meaning even a "successful" fix risks causing the exact thing Cancer fears most. Directly engages Cancer's single most sensitive wound.
**Source:** in-district Questlines, Thread 3; Cross-Reference Finding 3.

### 4. The Number They Didn't Argue
**Starting point:** paperwork inside a Cancer care facility — a specific, still-existing exemption request from c. 2818, the one the automatic compliance formula flagged before anyone reviewed it.
**Chain:** the player learns the request was always valid — Libra's own formula simply never got to it in time — and that this wasn't the first time: two earlier, half-remembered incidents elsewhere in the city (an overwhelmed administrator, stale compliance data) already showed the same flaw, years before it landed on Cancer specifically.
**Fork:** press Libra for the specific, honest acknowledgment now possible ("your exemption was valid, we never got to it"); use the pattern of prior incidents to argue this is systemic, not a one-off; or let it stay a private, quiet grievance.
**Consequence-test:** Cancer never stopped caring for its grieving while waiting on the paperwork — this asks what it costs the district to have been right the whole time and unable to prove it until now.
**Source:** `Deep_Dives/01b_Cancer_Rationing_of_Grief_Alternatives.md`, confirmed mechanism.

### 5. The Archivist's Silence
**Starting point:** a specific, findable record gap — evidence that Cancer's own archives were deliberately edited following the Overcrowding Decision, and a trail leading toward whoever did it, now most likely long dead or very old.
**Chain:** the player has to decide whether to actually identify this person (a real name behind an anonymous, community-protecting act) or let the silence stand.
**Fork:** name them, and force Cancer to reckon with a specific person's choice rather than an abstract "the district decided"; leave it unnamed and let the silence do what it was built to do; or use the discovery as leverage in a different Cancer conflict entirely.
**Consequence-test:** Cancer's memorial culture is built on naming and remembering — this is the one deliberate exception, and the belief has to survive contact with its own founding cover-up.
**Source:** `Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section VII, Notable Figures — "a founding-era archivist."

### 6. The Green Ledger
**Starting point:** the Green Ledger itself (`Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section I) — the unofficial, never-written-down accounting of who tends what plantlife, as a form of care-debt labor for people recovering or convalescing.
**Chain:** the player encounters a dispute over the Ledger — someone who believes they're owed a debt nobody else remembers, in a system that was never written down specifically so it wouldn't feel transactional.
**Fork:** try to formalize a piece of it to resolve the dispute (undermines the whole point of an unwritten system); resolve it informally, the Cancer way, at the risk of it happening again; or let the disputants work it out without intervening.
**Consequence-test:** an unconditional, informal care economy has no enforcement mechanism by design — this tests whether that's sustainable at the scale of an actual grievance, not just a comforting ideal.
**Source:** `Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section I.

### 7. The Ones We Almost Turned Away
**Starting point:** a specific elderly resident — a survivor of the actual Overcrowding Decision triage, still living quietly in Cancer, present at memorial events but never speaking about what happened to them directly.
**Chain:** the player learns concrete, personal detail of what triage actually looked like from the inside, distinct from the archivist's institutional cover-up (candidate 5) — a genuine living memorial the district doesn't officially have.
**Fork:** encourage them to speak publicly (healing for some, reopening the wound for others in the district); keep it private, as it's stayed for decades; or push for an official memorial acknowledgment despite the destroyed records.
**Consequence-test:** Cancer memorializes everything except its own worst moment — this tests whether "no one left behind" extends even retrospectively, to people who were nearly left behind and survived anyway.
**Source:** `District_Canon_Reference.md`, the Overcrowding Decision of c. 2813.

### 8. Someone Else's Fear
**Starting point:** an old, unopened Taurus emergency-stockpile item that somehow ends up in Cancer — donated, or simply found.
**Chain:** tracing its origin surfaces the confirmed non-malice history between the two districts — Taurus hoarded out of genuine crisis-panic uncertainty while Cancer's own shelters went without, and neither district ever actually discussed it, just quietly carried two centuries of unreconciled fear pointed in different directions.
**Fork:** broker an actual conversation between representatives of both districts; let one specific relationship (a Taurus-Cancer bonded pair or friendship) carry the reconciliation privately; or expose the asymmetry publicly and force a citywide reckoning.
**Consequence-test:** Cancer's belief is about not turning away the vulnerable — this asks what Cancer owes the version of itself that went without, 250 years ago, and never got an apology for it.
**Source:** `Cross_District_Non_Malice_Audit.md`, "Taurus — 'The Stockpiling Culture's Dark Side' — reworked."

### 9. What the Circuit Was For
**Starting point:** an old founding-era charter for the original Mother's Circuit, found in a Trauma-Bearers' memorial space — its stated, benevolent original purpose, long since drifted from.
**Chain:** comparing the charter's plain language to what the Circuit actually does today reveals how far the drift has gone, without any single moment of betrayal — exactly the "nobody knows when the line was crossed" pattern already established.
**Fork:** use the old charter to argue for reform or rollback; accept the drift as necessary evolution nobody could have stopped; or quietly note the gap and do nothing.
**Consequence-test:** Cancer's own founding intentions erode under scale the same way everyone else's do — this is the district's own version of its "visible virtue, hidden cost" wound, aimed at itself instead of at Libra.
**Source:** Cross-Reference Finding 3; `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s Mother's Circuit entry.

### 10. The Shift Nobody Wanted
**Starting point:** a specific overnight or holiday intake shift at Cancer, chronically understaffed because nobody wants it.
**Chain:** the player is present for a real, hard case — more arrivals than the facility can immediately absorb — and watches the actual, practical friction between the Unlimited's "everyone gets in" philosophy and the Triage Realists' "some choices are unavoidable" philosophy resolve in practice, which differs from either faction's official rhetoric.
**Fork:** side with immediate, unconditional admission (straining resources further); side with realistic capacity limits (someone is turned away, however briefly); or find a genuinely situational third option unique to this case.
**Consequence-test:** the belief says "unconditional" — this is the most literal, ground-level version of that word actually being tested on one full-capacity night.
**Source:** `Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section VI, the Unlimited/Triage Realists' lack of fixed sites.

### 11. A Debt Only She Remembers
**Starting point:** the Zhongshan-descended caregiver (Full Extrapolation Section V) again, in a moment unrelated to candidate 2 — asked to share something personal in a Cancer group setting, and visibly declining.
**Chain:** the player learns more about why she stays in Cancer despite the friction (a bonded partner's family, or a posting that became permanent) and what it costs her daily to practice care inside a system that assumes openness is inherently good.
**Fork:** help carve out a recognized "quiet" role for her within Cancer's care system; encourage her toward a place that fits her culture better; or do nothing and let her keep managing the tension privately, as she already has been.
**Consequence-test:** unconditional care cuts both ways — can Cancer's own model unconditionally accept a caregiver who doesn't practice care the Cancer way?
**Source:** `Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section V.

### 12. The Recovering Gardener Who Won't Stop
**Starting point:** a specific resident who has been tending far more of the Green Ledger's plantlife than their fair share, for far longer than "recovering" would normally justify, and refuses to stop or hand off duties.
**Chain:** the player discovers the labor may be a way of avoiding unresolved trauma rather than processing it — Cancer's own "everyone contributes what they can" ethos has no mechanism for saying "you've contributed enough, please rest."
**Fork:** gently intervene and encourage rest; respect their autonomy and let them keep working; or involve the Trauma-Bearers to address it institutionally.
**Consequence-test:** unconditional acceptance of contribution, taken to its logical extreme, might actually prevent someone from healing — a direct test of the belief's own blind spot.
**Source:** `Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section I, the Green Ledger.

### 13. The Stranger With Nowhere Else
**Starting point:** a genuine outsider arrives in Cancer — someone the rest of the city actively distrusts or considers dangerous, not merely unlucky (plausibly connected to another district's own recently-confirmed conflict, e.g. a figure fleeing consequences tied to Aries' Political Weapon myth or Pisces' Recalibration Underground).
**Chain:** Cancer's own founding logic holds there was never an "us" with prior claim to measure an "outsider" against — this tests whether that logic actually extends to someone the rest of the city considers an active threat, not just fallen-through-the-cracks sympathetic.
**Fork:** accept them fully per Cancer's own stated principles, at real risk to Cancer's relationships with other districts; quietly vet or investigate them first, a compromise that already violates "unconditional"; or turn them away — the first documented exception to "no one left behind," with real narrative weight.
**Consequence-test:** the single most direct test of the belief in this whole set.
**Source:** `Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section III.

### 14. The Corner
**Starting point:** the physical Cancer/Taurus/Capricorn three-way corner where Calethina's Lab actually sits — a newly-activated robot's first days, geographically nearest to Cancer of the three.
**Chain:** Cancer's care ethos was built for people who lost everything and have nowhere else to turn — this person never had anything to lose in the first place. No biography, no trauma exactly, just newness itself.
**Fork:** fully integrate them into Cancer's care system despite them not fitting the usual profile; refer them elsewhere (Taurus, Capricorn, back to the Lab); or let a specific caregiver informally take on the open question of what this person actually needs, inventing a new category of care as they go.
**Consequence-test:** "no one left behind" was built for people with a past — this tests whether the belief extends to someone with no past to have been left behind from.
**Source:** `District_Canon_Reference.md`, Calethina's Lab's Cancer/Taurus/Capricorn location.

### 15. Two Kinds of Pain
**Starting point:** a specific traumatized new arrival whose needs don't fit neatly into either Cancer's ongoing-nurture model or Scorpio's confrontation-based transformation work — caregivers from the two districts actually disagree about which approach this one person needs.
**Chain:** the player becomes involved in mediating, or choosing, between Cancer's and Scorpio's genuinely competing philosophies of how to help this specific person.
**Fork:** side with Cancer's approach; side with Scorpio's approach; or find a combined approach unique to this one case.
**Consequence-test:** Cancer's belief says unconditional care — but what if real care sometimes requires the confrontation Cancer's own model explicitly avoids? Tests whether "unconditional" secretly means "only Cancer's specific kind."
**Source:** cross-district, Scorpio's established transformation-through-confrontation identity vs. Cancer's own care model.

### 16. The Formula's Shadow
**Starting point:** after candidate 4 (The Number They Didn't Argue), a specific figure — plausibly the same senior Gemini data archaeologist already flagged for cross-district connections — notices the exact shape of Cancer's own compliance-formula flaw recurring in fragments of Aries', Libra's, and Pisces' own separate histories, documents never before compared side by side.
**Chain:** piecing together whether this is coincidence or something deeper connecting all four districts.
**Fork:** pursue the full cross-district investigation (a genuine seed for the larger Continuity and Stability Act reveal once the Act itself is drafted, per `TODO.md`'s own flagged note); document the pattern privately without publishing it; or dismiss it as coincidence and move on.
**Consequence-test:** less about Cancer's own belief directly, more about whether a district that was quietly wronged by an old flaw wants to know how many others share the same wound. **Explicitly flagged as an early seed for the future Act-based Under-Questline** — revisit and expand once the Act's actual Articles exist.
**Source:** `Deep_Dives/01b_Cancer_Rationing_of_Grief_Alternatives.md`; `Continuity_and_Stability_Act_Requirements.md`.

### 17. The Trauma-Bearers' Room
**Starting point:** the specific, quieter memorial space (`Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section VI) reserved for the Trauma-Bearers to process guilt, distinct from Cancer's public memorial walls.
**Chain:** someone violates its unspoken sanctity — using it for an unrelated purpose, or a visitor from outside Cancer wandering in without understanding what it's for.
**Fork:** protect the space's privacy fiercely, even at real social cost; open it up as a broader resource given Cancer's own overcrowding strain; or use the intrusion as a genuine occasion to examine what "processing guilt" versus "public grief" actually means for the district.
**Consequence-test:** unconditional care usually means open access — this is the one space in Cancer explicitly not built on open access, testing whether an unconditional-care district can also hold something back for itself.
**Source:** `Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section VI.

### 18. What Grows Back
**Starting point:** after some loss (a damaged biome section, a death, an institutional failure), a specific part of Cancer's plantlife needs replanting — and per the Green Ledger's own logic, tending it is valued care-labor, making the replanting itself symbolically loaded.
**Chain:** multiple community members want the job for different reasons — grief processing, genuine skill, a need for meaningful work — with no formal way to decide, since the whole system was built to never be formalized.
**Fork:** let a specific person do it based on greatest need; base it on skill and quality instead; or turn it into a genuine community event with many hands.
**Consequence-test:** every other candidate in this set tests who gets to *receive* Cancer's unconditional care — this is the one that tests who gets to *give* it.
**Source:** `Cancer_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section I, the Green Ledger.

## Taurus

**Civic belief (Concordia Section I):** *"a city becomes real through accumulated, unbroken domestic continuity... a true city is one where families can simply stay, generation after generation, without ever needing to flee it."*

**Status: 15 of 15–20 target. Floor reached.** Taurus doesn't have a dedicated non-malice alternatives file the way Cancer/Leo/Capricorn/Pisces do — both its flagged items (the Insulation Schism, the Stockpiling Culture's Dark Side) passed cleanly or were reworked directly in `District_Canon_Reference.md`.

**Chain:** the player uncovers concrete evidence that Aries' worker retention and Leo's own sense of mattering both depend directly on Taurus' continued stability — evidence a sharp-eyed Taurus figure could act on if they realized it.
**Fork:** reveal it to that Taurus figure, letting the district negotiate from real strength for the first time; keep the discovery for the player's own future use; or quietly warn Aries and Leo first.
**Consequence-test:** Taurus' belief is about staying, not power — this tests what happens when unbroken continuity turns out to be leverage nobody intended it to be.
**Source:** in-district Questlines Thread 1; Cross-Reference Finding 1; Throughway 3.

### 2. Two Kinds of Almost
**Starting point:** a specific Unverified resident and a specific Coldsider resident, whose situations the player is the first outside observer to recognize as structurally identical.

**Fork:** help the two populations recognize their shared position and organize together; assist each separately through existing, narrower channels; or bring the parallel to Taurus' own community leadership directly.

### 3. The Senior Archivist's Quiet Work
**Starting point:** the senior Registry archivist (Full Extrapolation Section IV), encountered doing ordinary, unglamorous daily work restoring Unverified status case by case.

### 4. The Organizer Who Isn't Official
**Starting point:** the Coldsider community organizer (Full Extrapolation Section IV) — a recognized but never-legitimized voice.
**Chain:** a specific resource or warmth grievance needs the organizer's advocacy to resolve, but Taurus' dominant culture won't formally recognize them as a stakeholder in any negotiation.
**Fork:** push for the organizer to get an actual seat at the table; work through them informally, as things have always been done; or bypass them entirely, undermining their hard-won unofficial position.
**Consequence-test:** continuity for whom? Tests whether Taurus' stability is actually secure for the population it's never officially acknowledged.
**Source:** `Taurus_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section IV.

### 5. Someone Else's Fear, the Other Side
**Starting point:** a Taurus household elder who still, quietly, over-stockpiles supplies decades after the Long Night crisis — a habit that never stopped.
**Chain:** the player asks why, and slowly uncovers the elder's own personal memory of genuine crisis-panic uncertainty, and the quiet discomfort of an old fear that's never actually been processed even though it's long since known to have worked out.
**Fork:** help the elder finally let go of the habit; respect it as a harmless private ritual and leave it be; or connect the elder directly with a Cancer counterpart, mirroring Cancer's own "Someone Else's Fear" candidate, for a genuine two-sided reconciliation.
**Consequence-test:** domestic continuity has a dark mirror — sometimes "staying the same" means never actually processing an old fear rather than genuinely resolving it.
**Source:** `District_Canon_Reference.md`, the Stockpiling Culture's Dark Side. **Cross-reference:** intended as a companion piece to Cancer candidate 8.

### 6. The Warm Side Aid Point
**Starting point:** one of the informal Warm Side Aid Points already established in canon, where warm-side households quietly funnel resources to cold-side neighbors.
**Chain:** the player discovers this quiet network exists specifically to soften the Insulation Schism's effects without ever officially naming it as an injustice.
**Fork:** encourage expanding and formalizing the aid points (helps more people, forces acknowledgment Taurus culture has always avoided); leave it exactly as is, quiet and limited; or use its existence as proof Taurus already secretly knows the Schism is unfair.
**Consequence-test:** does maintaining continuity require never naming an injustice, only quietly softening its edges forever?
**Source:** `District_Canon_Reference.md`, "Warm Side" Aid Points.

### 7. A Newcomer Who Won't Leave
**Starting point:** a specific stabilized Cancer refugee or hardy Sagittarius settler trying to actually put down permanent roots in Taurus.
**Chain:** the player watches or assists as this person tries to become a "known face" — per Full Extrapolation Section II, warmth switches on close to fully once someone is vouched for, but getting there is slow.
**Fork:** help accelerate their acceptance through a shortcut (a vouch, a favor); let them earn it slowly and organically, the real way; or discover this particular newcomer may never actually be accepted, revealing a real limit to Taurus' stated hospitality logic.
**Consequence-test:** "families can simply stay, generation after generation" — but what about a family just starting that chain for the first time? Does the belief actually welcome new roots, or only protect old ones?
**Source:** `District_Canon_Reference.md`, Ongoing Influx from Cancer and Frontier Districts; `Taurus_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section II.

### 8. What the Registry Actually Says

## Leo

**Civic belief (Concordia Section I):** *"a city becomes real through shared culture and undeniable collective experience... spectacles that prove its people, human and robot alike, are fully alive and fully seen."*

**Status: 15 of 15–20 target. Floor reached.** Note: Leo's own `Leo_Full_Extrapolation.md` predates the confirmed Star War mechanism and still frames things in old "Grand Performance Faction won a war" language — the candidates below are built from the actual confirmed version (Twin Founding + Allocation Formula + Untimely Loss, see `Deep_Dives/03b_Leo_Star_War_Alternatives.md`) rather than that stale framing.

### 1. The Peak That Doesn't Answer
**Starting point:** a rising Leo performer (a natural companion or major recurring NPC), chasing the internal recognition hierarchy's peak.
**Chain:** clean external validation from Aries or Cancer — Leo's two most load-bearing relationships — has never once arrived for anyone in Leo's history, because both districts bundle their genuine need for Leo with open criticism of it.
**Fork:** help the performer climb the internal hierarchy to its peak, exactly what Leo's culture considers success; help them seek genuine validation outside Leo's system entirely; or help them recognize the substitution for what it is and find meaning independent of the hierarchy — the hardest, least-rewarded path.
**Consequence-test:** Leo's belief is proving people "fully seen" — this tests what happens when the district's own two closest relationships structurally can't ever fully see it back.
**Source:** in-district Questlines Thread 1; Cross-Reference Finding 1.

### 2. What Virgo Already Knows
