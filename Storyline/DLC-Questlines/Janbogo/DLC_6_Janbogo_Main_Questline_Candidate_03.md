# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #3: "How Loud It Actually Was"

**What this is:** a third prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around Denison's own established
verbal-pride tradition and its diaspora legacy — material distinct from both Janbogo's witness-burden
identity (Candidate #1) and Fort McMurdo/Scott's twin-city relationship (Candidate #2). **Not canon, not
chosen, not the only candidate** — the 3rd of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear
Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities (Denison, as ruins)** — the diaspora community whose dispute
drives this chain lives in Concordia, outside the subnet proper, referenced the same way Sanay's Concordia
diaspora was referenced in Halley subnet's own material without making Concordia itself a subnet city.

**Deliberately avoids Denison's own Cradle-chamber manufacturing legacy** — real, established, and already
connected to Halley subnet's Neumayer engineering lineage (`Janbogo_Cross_City_Throughways.md`, Throughway
3), but any quest content built around recovering or restoring that capacity would run directly into the
project's own binding Cradle Precedent (`Design_Principles.md` §IV — Cradle/player-origin material is
forbidden from ever becoming quest content, marked or unmarked). This chain uses Denison's other established
legacy instead: its verbal tradition, not its fabrication history.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Denison's own Full Extrapolation, Section I** — the resolved Enneagram tension: Denison's architecture
  is genuinely withdrawn, fused inward, aerodynamically smooth, invisible in its own severity from outside;
  its loud, proud verbal tradition of reciting precise wind statistics exists specifically to compensate for
  what the architecture itself can't show an outsider.
- **Denison's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 3** — since the city itself is gone, the verbal
  tradition — not the architecture — turned out to be the part of Denison's identity that could actually
  survive its destruction; Denison-descended Concordia residents are recognizable by "a habit of citing
  precise wind statistics in conversation," the literal, ongoing continuation of the one part of Denison
  that was never dependent on the physical city continuing to exist.
- **Denison's own Full Extrapolation, Section IV** — Denison's destruction traced to a genuinely strategic,
  infrastructure-disruption motive (a highway chokepoint on Hwy 183), distinct from Zukelli's punitive,
  message-sending fate — this chain doesn't touch or reinterpret that established cause.
- **Explicitly excluded:** the Cradle-chamber manufacturing legacy (see above); any claim about whether
  Zukelli's own destruction-message logic applies to Denison, which the subnet's own material already treats
  as a separate, distinct motive.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A Denison diaspora community organization in Concordia asks the player to travel to the ruins
and recover whatever historical meteorological records might have survived — wind-speed logs, environmental
monitoring data, the kind of precise figures the city's own founding engineers once tracked. The reason is
practical and a little delicate: descendants across different family lines have started reciting slightly
different versions of "the numbers," and a quiet dispute has formed over whose figures are the real,
original ones. It looks like a straightforward archival retrieval.

**BUT** — Denison's own architecture — withdrawn, fused, aerodynamically smooth, built to survive conditions
severe enough that the design itself never needed to announce them (Section I) — makes the ruins as
difficult and hazardous to search now as the city's own founding engineers intended them to be against the
weather, just with none of the oral tradition that once made the place legible left standing to guide a
visitor. Finding anything usable means a genuine, dangerous expedition into structure built to resist exactly
this kind of intrusion, not a simple records search.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually navigate the ruins' own hazards to locate whatever archival
material has survived — a real expedition, with real risk, before any dispute can even be addressed with
facts instead of family pride.

**BUT** — What the player actually recovers doesn't settle the dispute the way anyone hoped. The verified,
original historical figures are consistently more modest than at least some of what's recited in Concordia
today — the tradition, as oral traditions do, has drifted upward across generations, each family's own
retelling growing a little more extreme than the last. This isn't a minor correction. Denison's whole
surviving identity, per its own established legacy, was never the architecture — it was the telling, the
one part of the city that never depended on its own survival (Finding 3). If the telling itself turns out to
rest on numbers nobody actually recorded, the diaspora's one unbroken continuity with the destroyed city is
built on something less solid than anyone believed.

**THEREFORE** — The player's task shifts from "settle a family dispute with facts" to "decide what those
facts are actually for."

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Reporting the true, more modest figures
honors the exact value — precision, exactness, pride specifically in getting the numbers right — that the
tradition has always claimed to represent, but destabilizes the pride built on decades of inflated retelling,
forcing a diaspora community to reckon with the fact that their one unbroken link to a destroyed home has
been drifting away from the truth the whole time it was keeping that home alive in memory. Preserving the
community's own inherited, embellished figures as authoritative protects continuity and cohesion, and
respects how much weight this tradition has actually carried for people with nothing else physical left of
where they came from — but means the "precision" at the center of Denison's whole surviving identity is, in
practice, a comfortable fiction the community would rather keep than confront.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
choice in what gets reported back: the verified, accurate figures, honoring precision at the direct cost of
the community's settled pride and cohesion; or the community's own inherited numbers, left uncorrected,
preserving continuity and comfort at the cost of the exact value the tradition has always claimed as its own
reason for existing. Either way, something about Denison's one surviving legacy — the telling itself — comes
out of this changed, whether that change is honest correction or a quiet, deliberate choice not to know.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "retrieve some records"
into "the ruins are genuinely hazardous, and there's no oral tradition left standing to guide the search";
the second BUT turns "the records were found" into "the records contradict the very tradition they were
meant to verify"; the third BUT turns "report the findings" into "reporting them honestly and protecting the
community's own pride are now in direct, unavoidable conflict." Deleting any one Complication changes what
the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Denison's own most emotionally resonant established fact — that
its verbal tradition, not its architecture, is what actually survived the city's destruction (Cross-Reference
Finding 3) — and tests whether that surviving legacy can withstand the same scrutiny it claims to represent.
It's also the first Janbogo subnet candidate whose stakes live entirely in a diaspora community rather than
a currently-inhabited city, and the first whose central tension isn't between two parties who disagree, but
inside a single tradition that may not be able to survive being fact-checked by the very standard it was
built around.
