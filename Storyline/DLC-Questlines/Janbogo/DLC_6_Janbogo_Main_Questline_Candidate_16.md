# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #16: "Whichever One You'd Actually Call Real"

**What this is:** a sixteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around a cultural-authenticity
dilemma between an origin city and its own famous export. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.**
Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 4 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Janbogo, Fort McMurdo, Scott, Dumont d'Urville), per this
round's own "at least 4" standard. Concordia is referenced only as the already-established location Majyao
Bisyugota's establishment relocated to — not counted as a subnet city used.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Janbogo Mega-Init, "What It Feels Like"** — the established fact that Majyao Bisyugota's
  establishment — Janbogo's most nationally famous teahouse — has already relocated to Concordia's Aquarius
  district, meaning the original Janbogo location is no longer the nationally recognized site it once was;
  this chain assumes it either closed or passed to a successor keeping a quieter, local version of the
  tradition alive in its actual originating context.
- **Janbogo Mega-Init, real-world inspiration research** — the Ottoman/Turkish coffeehouse and New Orleans
  jazz parallels, establishing that the underlying communal-space tradition became genuinely city-wide and
  origin-agnostic, distinct from any single practitioner's own fame.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Majyao Bisyugota's own current whereabouts, motives for relocating,
  or relationship with Janbogo today — this chain treats the relocation itself as settled background fact,
  not material to develop further.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With the teahouse tradition's reputation now most strongly associated with Majyao Bisyugota's
own Concordia branch, other cities interested in establishing their own version don't know which model to
actually learn from — Concordia's famous, transplanted branch, or whatever continues, more quietly, at
Janbogo itself. The player is asked to help other cities figure out where to actually look — a
straightforward, if delicate, cultural-consultation task.

**BUT** — Whoever currently keeps Janbogo's own successor teahouse going, if it survived the war at all, has
a real, felt claim to authenticity — the tradition's actual originating context, the specific cold, the
shielded halls, the community it grew out of, still only exists there. But Majyao's own Concordia branch
carries the actual name and reputation that made the tradition nationally famous in the first place, and most
outside observers would naturally look there first, not to Janbogo's own quieter, less-known version.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to travel to Fort McMurdo, Scott, and Dumont d'Urville — the cities actually
interested in establishing their own version — consulting each about what they'd genuinely want to model
their own attempt on, before recommending either reference point.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once Janbogo's own current keeper is consulted directly: treating Concordia's
branch as the authoritative model, just because of its fame, would mean accepting that the tradition's "real"
home is now wherever its most famous practitioner happens to be, rather than where it actually developed —
quietly erasing Janbogo's own claim to being the origin. But insisting other cities model themselves on
Janbogo's own quieter version instead risks sending them toward something genuinely less proven and less
recognizable than what most people have actually heard of.

**THEREFORE** — What began as pointing cities toward a reference model becomes a real question about what
"the real version" of a tradition actually means once its most famous practitioner and its actual birthplace
are no longer the same place.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Recommending Concordia's famous branch gives
expanding cities real credibility and a proven, recognizable model to build from — but entrenches the idea
that fame, not origin, decides where a tradition actually lives, sidelining Janbogo's own claim and whoever
still keeps its quieter version going. Recommending Janbogo's own continuing version honors the tradition's
actual originating context and gives its current keeper real, deserved recognition — but leaves expanding
cities working from a far less proven, less recognized model than simply copying what already carries
national fame.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which version becomes the reference model other cities actually learn from: Concordia's famous
branch, offering proven credibility at the cost of Janbogo's own claim to origin; or Janbogo's own quieter
continuation, honoring that origin at the cost of a less recognizable, less proven model for cities trying
to build something real of their own.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "find the real version"
into "authenticity and fame have genuinely split into two different places"; the second BUT turns "consult
the interested cities" into "Janbogo's own keeper has a real stake in not simply ceding the question to
fame"; the third BUT turns "pick a model" into "there is no choice that doesn't cost either origin or
credibility." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none
reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes an already-established, easy-to-overlook fact — that Janbogo's
own most famous teahouse isn't in Janbogo anymore — and builds an entire dilemma directly out of the gap that
fact leaves behind, rather than assuming the tradition's fame and its home never separated. It's also the
first candidate in this run built around a quiet, ongoing consequence of a purely voluntary relocation,
rather than the war, giving DLC 6 a dilemma rooted in an entirely different kind of change.
