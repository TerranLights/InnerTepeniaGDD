# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #18: "Enough to Know, Not Enough to Rebuild"

**What this is:** an eighteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around a heritage-archive
recovery and reconstruction dilemma. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear
Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 4 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Cape Adare, Fort McMurdo, Janbogo, Dumont d'Urville), per this
round's own "at least 4" standard.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Cape Adare Full Extrapolation, Section I** — the established proposal that Borchgrevink's original 1899
  hut, though physically destroyed in the war, likely had its own heritage documentation (measurements,
  photographs, replica plans, a dedicated archive) evacuated or backed up elsewhere beforehand, given Cape
  Adare's own established "always historically conscious" civic character — a real loss, but not a total
  erasure.
- **Janbogo's own established hub/relay function and Fort McMurdo's own established capital-era
  record-keeping role** (Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3 and Throughway 2), used here to justify both cities
  as plausible waypoints or custodians for evacuated cultural materials.
- **Dumont d'Urville's own established survival, downtown core intact** (Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 1),
  used here to place it as another functioning subnet city capable of having received and stored evacuated
  materials.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about who evacuated the archive, when, or why one particular
  city over another — this chain treats the trail itself as genuinely uncertain, something the player's own
  investigation has to establish.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Borchgrevink's hut destroyed but its own heritage archive plausibly evacuated before the
war, a search begins to locate and recover it. The player is asked to help track down where the archive
actually ended up — a straightforward, if uncertain, recovery task.

**BUT** — The archive's trail is genuinely scattered. Tracking it down means checking Fort McMurdo's own
historical record-keeping institutions, Janbogo's own hub function as a plausible transit point for evacuated
materials, and Dumont d'Urville's own surviving civic infrastructure — with no single confirmed destination
to start from.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to investigate all three cities in turn, piecing together the actual
evacuation trail before the archive can be recovered at all.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the archive is finally found: it's real, but incomplete — enough survives
to definitively document what the hut was and how it stood, satisfying the historical record, but not enough
to support a fully accurate physical reconstruction without genuine guesswork filling real gaps.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a recovery mission becomes a real choice about what to actually do with an
archive that proves the hut existed without fully proving what it looked like in every detail.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Rebuilding a replica using the incomplete
archive gives Cape Adare's own historically-conscious culture back a real, physical, visitable monument to
the actual place its whole civic identity rests on — but necessarily presents some invented, unverified
detail as historical fact, a real compromise of the truth for the sake of having something to stand in front
of. Preserving the site as a documented ruin, with the archive displayed honestly as incomplete, keeps full
historical integrity intact — but leaves a city whose entire identity is built on being "the place where it
all began" without any physical structure to actually visit, a real loss for a culture this specifically
invested in physical historical connection.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether the hut actually gets rebuilt: reconstruct it, giving Cape Adare a real monument at the cost
of presenting some invented detail as fact; or leave the site as an honestly incomplete ruin, protecting the
truth at the cost of the physical connection Cape Adare's own culture values most.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "recover the archive"
into "the trail runs through three cities with no confirmed destination"; the second BUT turns "recover it"
into "what's recovered proves the past without fully documenting it"; the third BUT turns "rebuild or don't"
into "there is no option that doesn't cost either historical accuracy or a physical monument this culture
specifically needs." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end,
so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Cape Adare's own established Section I proposal — already
flagged as resolving "the most emotionally significant open question in Tepenian heritage" without forcing an
all-or-nothing answer — and pushes one step further, asking what happens when a real but incomplete
archive forces exactly the all-or-nothing choice its own source material was originally built to avoid. It's
also the first candidate in this run to treat a physical monument's own authenticity, rather than a policy,
a relationship, or a resource, as the actual object in dispute.
