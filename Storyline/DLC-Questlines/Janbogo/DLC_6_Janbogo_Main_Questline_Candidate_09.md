# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #9: "Where the Wires Actually Go Now"

**What this is:** a ninth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around Janbogo's own established
Arcanet-nexus relocation history — untouched material distinct from the witness-burden identity Candidate #1
already used. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 9th of the "at least 10" the method
calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities (Janbogo)** — Concordia is referenced through already-established
fact (it currently hosts the subnet's own Arcanet nexus hardware) rather than developed further, consistent
with the subnet's own vision-pass note that Concordia was "deliberately excluded... develop the rest of the
country first."

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Janbogo's own Full Extrapolation, Section IV** — the subnet's Arcanet nexus sits in Concordia, not
  Janbogo, because of a wartime emergency relocation: the nexus hardware was moved to protect it from further
  damage once Janbogo's own "damaged, partially operational" status became clear, prioritizing the
  subnet-wide communications function over keeping the hardware in its historically appropriate hub city — a
  pragmatic wartime decision, not a peacetime political arrangement.
- **Janbogo's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 1** — Janbogo's own unique value combined the Terra
  Nova Bay polynya's year-round maritime access with its established witness/relay role, making it, by two
  independent measures, the coast's one true connective node — the nexus's absence from its own historically
  appropriate home is, on that reading, a genuine displacement, not a minor administrative footnote.
- **Janbogo's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 2** — Janbogo's real civic trait is a repeatable process
  of absorbing whatever reaches it and re-originating it as simply its own; this chain tests whether that
  same civic character can also absorb a real, permanent loss without it curdling into grievance.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any new development of Concordia's own internal Arcanet infrastructure or
  politics beyond the single established fact that the nexus relocated there — this chain treats Concordia's
  side of the dispute at the level of plausible institutional reality (technicians, dependent systems,
  reasonable caution), not as new city-scale lore.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Decades after the war, a push emerges within Janbogo — part civic pride, part practical
argument — to relocate the subnet's Arcanet nexus hardware back to Janbogo, its historically appropriate
hub, now that the wartime emergency that justified moving it to Concordia has long since passed. The player
is asked to help investigate and advocate for the relocation — a straightforward matter, on its face, of
restoring a temporary wartime measure to its proper, permanent arrangement.

**BUT** — Concordia's own authorities and technicians push back, and not merely out of institutional
turf-protection. In the generations since the relocation, Concordia has built real, dependent infrastructure
and operational habits around the nexus's physical presence there — a live, subnet-wide communications
system is not something relocated lightly, especially post-war, when redundancy and system resilience are
already thin. Their caution is genuine and defensible, not merely self-interested.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually investigate whether relocation is technically safe and
politically achievable, brokering between Janbogo's own historical claim and Concordia's own legitimate,
practical concerns rather than simply picking a side on principle.

**BUT** — The investigation surfaces a harder truth than either side expected. Across the decades, the nexus
hardware has been incrementally modified and integrated with Concordia's own local systems in ways that make
a clean "return it to Janbogo" no longer a simple restoration of the original arrangement — it would require
genuine re-engineering, at real risk to reliability, to move something that has quietly, gradually become
load-bearing infrastructure exactly where it currently sits. Nobody ever formally decided the wartime
relocation should become permanent. It simply became true, the same way Neumayer's own uncredited engineering
legacy became true in Halley subnet, one unremarked year at a time.

**THEREFORE** — What began as "restore Janbogo's rightful function" becomes a genuine question of whether
that function is still restorable at all, or whether Janbogo's own claim to it is now more historical and
symbolic than practically recoverable without real, felt cost to the whole subnet's own communications
reliability.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Pushing the relocation through anyway
restores a genuine piece of civic justice to Janbogo — its own historically appropriate function returned to
where its established value (Cross-Reference Finding 1) actually says it belongs — but gambles real,
subnet-wide communications reliability on a technically difficult re-engineering effort that Concordia's own
technicians have real, defensible reasons to fear. Accepting the wartime relocation as the new permanent
reality is the safer, more technically sound choice — but means formally ceding a piece of what once defined
Janbogo, one more thing folded into the same absorb-and-move-on civic character (Finding 2) that has already
had to metabolize so much loss this subnet's own history has documented.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which cost gets accepted: push the relocation through, restoring Janbogo's historic function at real
technical risk to the whole subnet's communications reliability; or formally cede the claim, protecting
system reliability at the cost of one more piece of Janbogo's own historical identity quietly, permanently
folded into "simply how things are now" — the same civic habit that makes Janbogo distinctively itself
tested, once more, against something it may not actually be able to get back.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "restore the nexus to
its rightful home" into "Concordia has real, defensible reasons the relocation shouldn't simply be reversed";
the second BUT turns "negotiate a safe relocation" into "the thing being negotiated over has already quietly
stopped being cleanly separable from where it currently sits"; the third BUT turns "decide whether to move
it" into "there is no version of the decision that doesn't cost either subnet-wide reliability or a piece of
Janbogo's own historical claim." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing
at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes a single, previously undeveloped sentence of established fact —
that the nexus sits in Concordia "for pragmatic wartime reasons, not a peacetime political arrangement" —
and asks the natural follow-up question that fact invites but the original document never pursued: does
anyone, decades later, actually still want to reverse it, and can they. It's also the first Janbogo subnet
candidate to test the city's own celebrated absorb-and-move-on civic character (Cross-Reference Finding 2)
against a loss that's structural and institutional rather than emotional — not a memory to process, but a
literal function the city may have permanently lost the practical ability to reclaim, regardless of how
strong its historical claim to it remains.
