# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #2: "Whoever Calls It First"

**What this is:** a second prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around Fort McMurdo and Scott's
established twin-city relationship rather than Janbogo's own witness-burden identity (Candidate #1). **Not
canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 2nd of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the
Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 2 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Fort McMurdo, Scott).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Janbogo/Ross Subnet Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — Fort McMurdo and Scott's relationship
  tracks Fort McMurdo's own two-phase rise to formal capital status; the rivalry is offset by a genuine,
  concrete point of cooperation (the shared Erebus volcanic monitoring program, split by specialization);
  and their shared Hut Point Independence Day remembrance tradition plausibly absorbed a second, unspoken
  layer of grief and mutual dependence once the war forced their emergency infrastructure to genuinely
  merge.
- **Scott's own Full Extrapolation, Section II** — Scott's technical/research economy specializes in
  precision equipment calibration and environmental/geological monitoring, including a share of the ongoing
  Erebus monitoring program, complementing rather than competing with Fort McMurdo's own heavy-industry
  focus.
- **Scott's own Full Extrapolation, Section III** — the two cities' surviving sections remain in active
  contact post-war; shared emergency infrastructure and physical proximity meant cooperation intensified out
  of pure necessity, though the war stripped away much of the friendly rivalry's earlier luxury.
- **Scott's own Full Extrapolation, Section IV** — Hut Point Discovery Hut survived the war intact,
  protected by its distance from Fort McMurdo's more heavily targeted industrial infrastructure; the
  Independence Day remembrance tradition continues post-war, carrying even more weight given what both
  cities have lost since it began.
- **Scott's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 2** — Erebus monitoring as the one concrete, functioning
  example of the two cities' "genuinely collaborative and genuinely competitive" relationship: Fort McMurdo
  handling heavier geological/seismic monitoring given its industrial infrastructure, Scott handling
  precision instrumentation given its own established technical niche.
- **Scott's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 1** — Scott's own established "somewhat allergic to being
  perceived as Fort McMurdo's smaller sibling" trait, with a real historical arc tracking Fort McMurdo's own
  two-phase capital timeline.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim that this chain resolves the rivalry itself, or permanently settles
  Erebus's own long-term volcanic risk profile — this stays at the scale of one detected anomaly and one
  test of the two cities' existing cooperation under real pressure, not a general statement about Erebus.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Scott's precision instruments pick up an anomaly in the shared Erebus monitoring data — subtle,
ambiguous, the kind of signal that needs cross-verification against Fort McMurdo's own heavier
geological/seismic readings before anyone can say whether it's a real emerging threat or an instrument
quirk. The player is sent to help the two cities' monitoring teams coordinate a joint verification — a
straightforward scientific handshake, in principle.

**BUT** — The two cities' established "genuinely collaborative and genuinely competitive" relationship
colors how each wants to handle an unconfirmed signal. Fort McMurdo, whose whole civic gravity rests on
quiet, operational indispensability rather than dramatic posturing, doesn't want to raise a costly, city
-disrupting alarm over what might be nothing — a false alarm would cost it exactly the kind of quiet
credibility its "presence, not population" identity depends on. Scott, still carrying its own established
allergy to being read as Fort McMurdo's smaller sibling, worries that deferring entirely to Fort McMurdo's
caution means that if the signal turns out to be real, the fact that Scott's own precision instruments found
it first quietly disappears into Fort McMurdo's institutional authority the way it always has.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to broker an actual joint verification and response protocol under real time
pressure — not just get both cities to compare notes, but settle who has final say, how any finding gets
reported, and what threshold of caution is enough to trigger a real response.

**BUT** — While the two cities negotiate process and credit, the signal itself doesn't resolve into a clean
false alarm or full catastrophe. It's real, but modest — a minor venting event, not a major eruption — and
it's headed directly toward Hut Point, threatening the Discovery Hut that survived the war intact and the
grounds where both cities' own Independence Day remembrance has, since the war, come to carry a second,
unspoken layer of shared grief and mutual dependence (Finding 3). The emergency response the player helped
broker beforehand is about to be tested for real, not hypothetically.

**THEREFORE** — Whether Hut Point and its remembrance tradition survive this specific event depends directly
on whether the credit-and-authority tension between the two cities actually got resolved during the
verification process, or is still quietly festering when the real event arrives.

**BUT** — The reversal: there's no version of the joint protocol that costs nothing. A genuinely well
-integrated response — clear roles, shared credit, real mutual trust — protects Hut Point and handles the
event efficiently, but requires each city to give up something it specifically didn't want to: Fort McMurdo
ceding some unilateral authority over how the finding gets called and reported, Scott accepting real, acute
-crisis subordination to Fort McMurdo's heavier response capacity despite having found the signal first. A
protocol that never actually resolved the credit question still functions well enough to prevent outright
disaster — the two cities' infrastructure is too genuinely merged for it not to — but leaves Hut Point
damaged, or the observance itself disrupted, and leaves the underlying rivalry more bitter than the shared
ritual's own quiet, war-deepened meaning (Finding 3) can easily absorb.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is shaped entirely by
how well the player actually brokered the process before the crisis arrived, not by anything decided in the
moment of the event itself: a well-integrated protocol saves the site and the tradition intact, at the cost
of both cities genuinely conceding something long-contested about their own relationship; a protocol that
papered over the credit question instead still prevents catastrophe, thanks to infrastructure the war itself
already forced to merge, but at the cost of Hut Point's own physical or ceremonial integrity, and of leaving
the rivalry exactly as unresolved as the player found it — or worse.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "verify a signal" into
"verifying it means navigating a live rivalry over caution and credit"; the second BUT turns "negotiate the
protocol" into "the negotiation is happening in real time against an actual, arriving event, not an abstract
hypothetical"; the third BUT turns "respond to the eruption" into "how well the response goes was already
decided by whether the rivalry got resolved beforehand, and neither resolution is free." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes the one concrete, already-established point of Fort McMurdo/
Scott cooperation (the shared Erebus monitoring program, Cross-Reference Finding 2) and gives it a genuine,
live stress test rather than leaving it as background civic texture, while routing the actual climax through
Hut Point — a site the project's own cross-referencing work already flagged as carrying new, unspoken
post-war weight (Finding 3) — so the crisis lands on the one place in the subnet where the two cities'
history, rivalry, and cooperation are already most concentrated. It's also the first Janbogo subnet
candidate whose climax is a live physical-danger event rather than a memory, credit, or identity question
resolved through negotiation alone — though the negotiation still determines the outcome, keeping it
consistent with the method's own emphasis on consequences tracing back to earlier choices rather than being
decided by the crisis itself.
