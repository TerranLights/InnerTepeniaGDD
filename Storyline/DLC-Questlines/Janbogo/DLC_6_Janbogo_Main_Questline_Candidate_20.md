# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #20: "Whatever Scale Actually Costs"

**What this is:** a twentieth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around forming a new joint
manufacturing venture and the consolidation-versus-dispersal trade-off it creates. **Not canon, not chosen,
not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 4 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Fort McMurdo, Scott, Janbogo, Dumont d'Urville), per this
round's own "at least 4" standard.

- **Janbogo Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 1** — Fort McMurdo's own established survival logic: a genuinely

- **Janbogo Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 3** — Denison's own historical role as a fabrication

- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about the present-day Cradle network's own active manufacturing sites or

  general fact motivating a new, unrelated venture.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Denison's own historical manufacturing capacity permanently lost, an initiative emerges to
build new, distributed manufacturing capability elsewhere in the subnet — pooling Fort McMurdo's own
dispersed industrial footprint and Scott's own precision-instrumentation expertise. The player is asked to
help organize this new joint venture — a straightforward, if ambitious, coalition-formation task.

**BUT** — Fort McMurdo's own dispersed industrial footprint is exactly what let it survive the war in the
first place. Consolidating some of that capacity into a more efficient, centralized facility — necessary for
genuine manufacturing scale — would recreate exactly the kind of concentrated, single-point vulnerability
that made Denison a worthwhile target to begin with.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to negotiate how much consolidation is actually acceptable, consulting Fort
McMurdo and Scott directly, and traveling to Janbogo for logistics support and Dumont d'Urville as another
city with a genuine stake in restored manufacturing capacity.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the numbers are actually worked out: Scott's own precision-instrumentation
capacity, while genuinely valuable, is too small on its own to meaningfully replace what Denison once
provided. Real scale requires exactly the consolidation that reintroduces strategic vulnerability; staying
safely dispersed means the venture can only ever be a partial, symbolic gesture, not a genuine restoration.

**THEREFORE** — What began as organizing a joint venture becomes a real choice between pursuing genuine,
meaningful scale at the cost of real risk, or staying safe at the cost of the venture actually mattering.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Pursuing genuine consolidated scale gives the
subnet real, meaningful manufacturing capacity back, actually closing the gap Denison's destruction left —
but recreates a concentrated, valuable target with the same vulnerability profile that made Denison worth
striking. Staying safely dispersed avoids recreating that vulnerability and protects Fort McMurdo's own
proven survival strategy — but means the new venture never amounts to more than a symbolic gesture, leaving
the subnet's real manufacturing gap effectively unaddressed.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether the new venture actually consolidates for real scale or stays safely dispersed: consolidate,
closing the real gap at the cost of recreating genuine strategic risk; or stay dispersed, avoiding that risk
at the cost of the venture never becoming more than symbolic.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "pool capacity" into
"pooling it recreates the exact vulnerability that got Denison destroyed"; the second BUT turns "stay
dispersed" into "staying dispersed means the venture can never actually close the real gap"; the third BUT
turns "consolidate or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either real strategic risk or the
venture's own actual purpose." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at
the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Fort McMurdo's own established survival logic — dispersal as
protection, already confirmed as the specific reason it lived through the war — and asks what that same
logic costs the moment the subnet actually needs concentrated capability again, rather than treating
dispersal as an unambiguous virtue. It's also the first candidate in this run whose central risk is
self-inflicted and forward-looking — a danger the subnet would be choosing to recreate, not one imposed on it
by war or accident.
