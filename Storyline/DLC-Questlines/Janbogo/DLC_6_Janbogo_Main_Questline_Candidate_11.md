# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #11: "The Same Math, Asked Nicely"

**What this is:** an eleventh prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet — the first candidate in a new run
specifically built to link through **at least 5 of the subnet's 7 cities**, the same standing that Palmer,
Halley, and Mirny subnets' own Candidates #11–#20 were built under. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Janbogo, Fort McMurdo, Scott, Dumont d'Urville, Cape Adare) —
five of the subnet's seven, leaving out only Zukelli and Denison, whose own destructions this chain treats as
settled historical fact rather than active material.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Janbogo Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 1, and Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 4** — the subnet's own
  complete, confirmed war-status logic: survival correlated with being "worth more alive" (Janbogo's witness
  role, Fort McMurdo's dispersed capital function) or simple irrelevance (Scott's domestic unimportance,
  Dumont d'Urville's core surviving its own disrupted junction), while destruction correlated with punitive
  intent (Zukelli) or pure infrastructure value with no offsetting worth (Denison).
- **Janbogo Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3 and Pattern 4** — Scott's and Dumont d'Urville's own established,
  self-contained civic character that doesn't need strategic weight to feel complete, and Cape Adare's own
  established firstness-pride identity, used here to ground why these cities' own communities would have a
  real, felt stake in this question.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Zukelli's or Denison's own specific destruction details beyond
  what Pattern 1 already establishes — this chain treats their fates as the historical fact the peacetime
  framework question is reacting to, not new material to develop.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With reconstruction funds genuinely limited, subnet leadership proposes a peacetime investment
framework openly modeled on the same logic that determined the war's own toll — prioritizing whichever cities
and functions are demonstrably "worth more" to the subnet's continued operation, the identical calculus
Pattern 1 already confirms explains every city's wartime fate. The player is asked to help apply this
framework to actual investment decisions — a straightforward, if consequential, resource-prioritization task.

**BUT** — Applying "worth more alive" logic to peacetime investment naturally favors the cities it already
favored during the war — Janbogo's witness and hub function, Fort McMurdo's dispersed capital role — while
continuing to treat Scott and Dumont d'Urville as similarly low-priority for investment, even though their own
quiet, self-contained character is exactly why their own residents value them, not a deficiency needing
strategic justification to fix.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to decide, city by city, whether "worth more alive" is actually the right
framework for peacetime decisions at all, rather than applying it automatically because it already proved
descriptively accurate once.

**BUT** — The reversal deepens once Cape Adare's own community weighs in: continuing to use the same
evaluative framework that once decided who got destroyed feels genuinely wrong to them, regardless of what
outcome it produces now. Applying "worth more alive" logic to living communities in peacetime risks quietly
normalizing the exact cold, purely instrumental thinking about cities that made the war's own targeting logic
possible in the first place — treating a horror as a template rather than a warning.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a resource-allocation task becomes a real question about whether to adopt a
more humane, needs-based framework instead, even at a real cost in efficiency, or to keep using the
framework that's already proven to work by the coldest possible measure.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Continuing with the "worth more alive"
strategic framework efficiently directs limited resources toward the functions that demonstrably matter most
to the subnet's own continued operation — but perpetuates, in name and method, the exact calculus that once
decided who lived and who was destroyed, a continuity many communities, Cape Adare's especially, would rather
not carry into peacetime. Adopting a needs-based alternative breaks that continuity and honors Scott's and
Dumont d'Urville's own right to matter without strategic justification — but risks under-resourcing Janbogo's
and Fort McMurdo's own genuinely load-bearing functions relative to what a purely strategic allocation would
have delivered.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which framework actually governs the subnet's investment going forward: the proven "worth more alive"
logic, delivering real efficiency at the cost of carrying wartime calculus into peacetime; or a needs-based
alternative, breaking that continuity at the cost of real inefficiency in how the subnet's own resources
actually get used.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "prioritize investment"
into "the framework already treats quiet, self-contained cities as low-priority, the same way it did during
the war"; the second BUT turns "apply it anyway" into "using it at all risks normalizing the exact logic that
made the war's own targeting possible"; the third BUT turns "keep it or replace it" into "there is no option
that doesn't cost either real efficiency or carrying wartime calculus forward into peacetime." Deleting any
one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And
Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Pattern 1 and Throughway 4's own confirmed, complete
explanatory framework for the subnet's war-status — already flagged as this Ultra-Megasheet's most
structurally significant finding — and asks whether a framework that accurately explained a wartime atrocity
should be allowed to keep functioning as a peacetime governing principle just because it's efficient. It's
also the first Janbogo/Ross subnet candidate to give Cape Adare's own community real moral standing in a
present-day questline, rather than treating its destruction purely as a historical fact relayed through
Janbogo, as Pattern 5 otherwise frames it.
