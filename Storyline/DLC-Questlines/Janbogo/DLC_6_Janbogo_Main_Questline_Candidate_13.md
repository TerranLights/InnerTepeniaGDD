# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #13: "The Road That Still Won't Go Straight"

**What this is:** a thirteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around a physical-route
repair decision along a single established highway. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses
the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Janbogo, Zukelli, Cape Adare, Denison, Dumont d'Urville) — all
five sit directly on or immediately beside the same established highway.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **`Highways.md`, Hwy 183 (the "Janbogo Highway")** — the established, literal route: Concordia → (junction
  near Janbogo) → past Janbogo/Zukelli via connecting roads → past Cape Adare via a connecting road → Denison
  → Dumont d'Urville, with Janbogo, Zukelli, and Cape Adare reached by spur access rather than as main-line
  stops.
- **Janbogo Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 3** — Denison's own established position as a genuine Hwy 183
  chokepoint, and its separate historical role as a fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturer for the
  Cradle infrastructure, a role explicitly described as historical and fully separate from the present-day,
  smaller network — used here only for the general fact that real industrial wreckage remains at the site,
  not for any connection to the player character's own origin.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim connecting Denison's ruined manufacturing equipment to the present-day
  Cradle network's own active sites, or to the player character's own origin — this chain treats the wreckage
  strictly as a historical industrial site with its own standalone significance.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Denison destroyed, the direct stretch of Hwy 183 through its own former chokepoint remains
blocked, forcing every journey between Concordia and Dumont d'Urville onto slow, informal workarounds. The
player is asked to travel the route and help organize a permanent fix — a straightforward, if significant,
infrastructure-repair task.

**BUT** — Two real options emerge. Clearing and rebuilding directly through Denison's own ruins would
restore the shortest, most efficient path — but means excavating a site that was deliberately targeted and
destroyed, including the remains of its own former industrial manufacturing equipment, raising real,
practical hazards and a genuine question about disturbing a site with memorial weight. A considerably longer
inland bypass avoids Denison's ruins entirely — but adds real, permanent time and distance to every future
journey along this route.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to travel the corridor in full — past Janbogo, Zukelli's ruins, and Cape
Adare's ruins — to actually assess ground conditions at Denison and consult nearby communities before either
option can be chosen.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the assessment team reaches Denison itself: the ruins hold real, physical
remnants of the city's own former industrial manufacturing capacity — not anything usable as-is, but enough
intact wreckage to confirm this site once represented genuine, now-lost capability the country could still
use more of today. Clearing the ruins for a direct rebuild means formally removing or dispersing this
wreckage, foreclosing whatever future study of exactly what was lost here might otherwise be possible.

**THEREFORE** — What began as choosing between two engineering options becomes a real question about whether
efficient transit or preserving a historically significant industrial site actually matters more.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Clearing Denison's ruins for a direct rebuild
restores the shortest route between Concordia and Dumont d'Urville, benefiting every future traveler and
shipment indefinitely, and may recover genuinely useful salvaged material — but destroys or disperses a
historically significant industrial site, closing off any future study of what was actually lost there.
Preserving the ruins and committing to the longer bypass keeps that record intact for whoever might want to
study it later — but permanently burdens every future journey between Concordia and Dumont d'Urville with
real, ongoing extra time and distance, for the sake of a historical value most travelers will never directly
benefit from.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which option the subnet actually commits to: clear the ruins and rebuild directly, restoring an
efficient route at the cost of a historically significant site; or preserve the ruins and accept the longer
bypass, protecting that history at the cost of a real, permanent burden on ordinary future travel.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "repair the highway"
into "the efficient fix and the low-impact fix are two entirely different routes"; the second BUT turns
"assess the ruins" into "the ruins themselves hold something worth preserving, not just rubble to clear"; the
third BUT turns "pick a route" into "there is no route that doesn't cost either ongoing travel efficiency or
a historically significant site." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing
at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Denison's own established dual-motive destruction — a highway
chokepoint and a manufacturing site, struck for reasons having nothing to do with symbolism or punishment —
and asks what the physical aftermath of that specific kind of loss still costs the subnet today, years later,
in the most literal possible sense: a road that still doesn't go where it used to. It's also the only
candidate in this run to put all three of the subnet's ruined cities (Zukelli, Cape Adare, Denison) on the
same physical journey at once, simply because the existing highway map already puts them there.
