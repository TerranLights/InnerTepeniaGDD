# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #15: "Whoever Still Needs the Vigil Kept"

**What this is:** a fifteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around a civic
-grief-processing initiative shaped by a genuine internal split and a diaspora's own competing need. **Not
canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Janbogo, Zukelli, Cape Adare, Fort McMurdo, Scott).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Janbogo Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 5** — the established, cumulative nature of Janbogo's own witness
  burden: having served, twice, as the relay point for a neighboring city's destruction (Zukelli, then Cape
  Adare), and the established internal "vigil-vs-moving-on" split this has produced within Janbogo's own
  population.
- **Janbogo Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 1** — the specific, established chain confirming this witness
  role as a repeatable civic function assigned by the subnet's own geography and highway network, not a
  one-time consequence of Zukelli's fate alone.
- **The Zukelli Native mechanic** (established project-wide) — used here to justify that Zukelli's own
  destroyed population persists as a real, present-day descendant community with a genuine stake in how its
  former home is remembered.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about which faction within Janbogo currently holds more influence, or
  which specific outcome the diaspora communities themselves would prefer — this chain treats both sides of
  the dispute as genuinely, currently unresolved.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Janbogo's own population still carrying the accumulated weight of having witnessed two
neighboring cities' destruction, a proposal emerges to formally organize civic support around this shared
grief — drawing on Fort McMurdo's and Scott's own experience processing collective loss for comparative
guidance. The player is asked to help design and organize this support — a straightforward, if sensitive,
civic-recovery task.

**BUT** — Janbogo's own population is already genuinely split on how to process this. One group wants formal
commemoration that keeps the witness role visibly honored, continuing the vigil. Another wants to move past
it entirely, refusing to let their whole civic identity be defined by having watched neighbors die. Any single
program design takes a side in an argument the city hasn't actually resolved.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to consult both sides within Janbogo, and travel to Fort McMurdo and Scott for
comparative perspective on how their own communities processed collective loss, before any program can take
shape.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once Zukelli's and Cape Adare's own diaspora communities are consulted directly:
for them, Janbogo's ongoing vigil is the one living site where their destroyed home's memory stays honored
and real, since they have nowhere else that serves this function at all. Asking Janbogo to set that vigil down
for its own population's sake would mean asking Janbogo to keep it going indefinitely instead — for the
benefit of communities that no longer physically live there.

**THEREFORE** — What began as designing a support program becomes a real question about whose need should
actually take precedence: the diaspora's need for a living site of memory, or Janbogo's own population's need
to heal and define itself on its own terms.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Continuing Janbogo's own vigil honors the
diaspora's real, felt need for their lost home to be witnessed somewhere ongoing — but asks Janbogo's own
population to indefinitely carry an emotional burden for communities that no longer live there, at real cost
to their own capacity to heal and become something other than "the city that watches others die." Supporting
Janbogo's own desire to move on honors its population's legitimate need to heal — but leaves Zukelli's and
Cape Adare's own diaspora without the one living site that has served as their destroyed homes' actual
witness, a real loss for communities that have already lost everything else.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which direction the support program actually takes: continue the vigil, honoring the diaspora's need
at the cost of Janbogo's own capacity to heal; or help Janbogo move on, honoring its own population's need at
the cost of the diaspora's last living connection to their lost home.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "organize support" into
"Janbogo itself is already split on what kind of support it even wants"; the second BUT turns "consult both
sides" into "an entirely different set of stakeholders, the diaspora, has a real, competing need of their
own"; the third BUT turns "keep the vigil or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either
Janbogo's own capacity to heal or the diaspora's last living connection to what they lost." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Pattern 5's own flagged "most emotionally significant
throughline" — Janbogo's cumulative witness burden — and gives it an actual present-day stake, rather than
leaving it as historical characterization: whether that burden should keep being carried at all, and for
whose sake. It's also the first candidate in this run to put a destroyed city's own diaspora in direct,
substantive tension with the living city that has been carrying its memory, rather than treating the diaspora
purely as a source of historical testimony.
