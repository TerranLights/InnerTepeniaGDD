# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #10: "The Man Who Said Yes"

**What this is:** a tenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, completing this subnet's own minimum
count of ten distinct candidates the same way Candidate #10 did for both Mirny and Halley subnets. Built
around Councilman Renzo Adorni's own explicitly-flagged, unresolved legacy — untouched by Candidate #1, which
used Zukelli's ruins for a different purpose entirely. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** —
the 10th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 2 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Zukelli, as legacy/memory; Janbogo, home to the Zukelli
diaspora this chain's debate actually plays out within).

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Zukelli's own Full Extrapolation, Section VII** — Councilman Renzo Adorni, the local figure most
  associated, in the record surviving through the Janbogo diaspora, with the decision to shelter the AWOL
  Upper Earth defectors; whether he's remembered as a symbol of solidarity worth honoring or a cautionary
  tale about the cost of protecting the wrong person is explicitly named as the open question this document
  proposes he should concretely embody, rather than leaving the debate abstract.
- **Zukelli's own Full Extrapolation, Section III** — no advance warning was possible; the strike was
  calibrated for absolute, total certainty, meaning Janbogo's own survivors never had a moment where
  intervention was possible. This chain doesn't revisit Janbogo's own culpability question (already resolved
  there) — it stays on Zukelli's own internal decision, made before the strike, not Janbogo's response to it.
- **Zukelli's own Full Extrapolation, Section IV** — Janbogo's own split relationship to the ruins: a real
  minority who keep an informal vigil, and a larger majority who've made quiet peace with not looking,
  neither considering the other simply wrong — the tension itself, not a single settled civic answer, is
  named directly as "the actual living legacy."
- **Zukelli's own Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the Zukelli diaspora within Janbogo: a modest, real
  population, integrated into Janbogo's own civic fabric rather than forming a separate quarter, carrying a
  specific, direct trauma — not losing a city by report, but having personally watched it happen.
- **`TODO.md`'s Census finding on Zukelli** — the city died near its absolute peak population, not in
  decline, meaning any accounting of Renzo's decision has to reckon with the scale of what was actually lost:
  a full, thriving city, not a fading one.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about whether the defectors themselves were right to flee, or any
  invented detail about what Renzo specifically knew or was warned about beyond what Section VII already
  proposes as open — this chain treats the historical facts as genuinely contested, not quietly settled in
  one direction by new invented evidence.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Members of the Zukelli diaspora within Janbogo, alongside the informal vigil-keepers who cross
to the waterfront on personal anniversaries, propose finally, formally settling how Councilman Renzo Adorni
should be remembered — a monument, a plaque, some official civic designation — rather than leaving the
question as an unresolved private argument that's simmered, unofficially, for generations. The player is
asked to help gather the actual historical record and facilitate the decision.

**BUT** — Researching the record properly surfaces genuine, substantial support for both readings of Renzo's
choice, not a clean answer waiting to be uncovered. Real testimony supports the solidarity reading: sheltering
the AWOL defectors was consistent with the same founding principle behind several of Tepenia's own defector
-founded cities, a courageous act of protecting people who'd chosen exile over complicity. Equally real
testimony supports the harder reading: Renzo made that choice for the whole city, at a scale and with a
finality none of the roughly one million residents who ended up paying for it with their lives — a city
destroyed near its own absolute peak, not in decline — ever consented to risk.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually help the community reach some kind of decision, weighing testimony
and record from both readings rather than simply reporting back an inconclusive finding.

**BUT** — Whichever way the memorial leans, it stops being a description of history and starts being a
statement about present-day Tepenian values. Formally honoring Renzo as an unambiguous hero risks quietly
endorsing the idea that any individual or small council may unilaterally risk an entire city's destruction
for a principle, however worthy — a genuinely uncomfortable precedent to enshrine, given what actually
happened here. Formally framing him as a cautionary, tragic figure risks undermining the exact solidarity
principle several of Tepenia's own founding stories are built on, at the one moment in the nation's history
where that principle's real cost is fully, undeniably visible rather than comfortably abstract.

**THEREFORE** — The player's actual choice isn't which reading is more historically accurate — the record
genuinely doesn't settle that — it's which cost the community is willing to accept by finally choosing one.

**BUT** — The reversal is that Zukelli's own established legacy, per Section IV, was never a single settled
answer in the first place — the ongoing, unresolved tension between vigil-keepers and everyone else is
already named as "the actual living legacy." A formal, official verdict, in either direction, doesn't just
pick a side — it ends a genuinely honest, ongoing disagreement that the community's own established relationship
to Zukelli has been built around for generations, replacing lived ambiguity with an official answer that
some real portion of the community will experience as a loss regardless of which way it goes.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which official verdict Janbogo actually reaches: memorialize Renzo as a moral hero, affirming
solidarity as an absolute value even at catastrophic cost, at the cost of the felt dignity of everyone who
never consented to pay that cost; or memorialize him as a cautionary, tragic figure, honoring the scale of
what was actually lost, at the cost of the solidarity principle several of Tepenia's own founding stories
depend on being simple to hold onto. Either verdict trades the community's own honest, generations-long
ambiguity for an official answer — the closure some in the community have wanted all along, and the specific
loss others always knew that closure would cost them.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "research the record"
into "the record genuinely supports two opposed readings, not a hidden true one"; the second BUT turns "reach
a verdict" into "any verdict makes an implicit statement about present-day values, not just a historical
judgment"; the third BUT turns "settle the debate" into "settling it costs the community its own honest,
generations-long ambiguity, regardless of which way it's settled." Deleting any one Complication changes what
the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes the one figure the project's own material explicitly proposes
as the concrete center of an already-named "open question" — Zukelli's own Full Extrapolation naming Renzo
Adorni directly as the person a hero-or-cautionary-tale debate "would actually center on" — and gives that
debate real, playable stakes rather than leaving it as background lore texture. It completes this subnet's
own set of ten candidates on the same note Zukelli's own establishing document already struck: that the
community's real, lived relationship to this loss was never a single answer, and any attempt to finally give
it one is itself a meaningful, costly choice, not a neutral act of closure.
