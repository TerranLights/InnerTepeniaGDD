# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #6: "All That's Left to Carry It"

**What this is:** a sixth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built directly around a vulnerability
the subnet's own Full Extrapolation explicitly flagged as DLC-scale material but left undeveloped: Fort
McMurdo's founding culture surviving through institutional memory alone, with no lived daily-practice
equivalent to fall back on. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 6th of the "at least 10"
the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md`
§5a).

**Uses 1 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities (Fort McMurdo alone)** — its first solo candidate; it previously
appeared only alongside Scott in Candidate #2's Erebus crisis.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Janbogo/Ross Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section IV** — explicitly proposes this exact vulnerability as
  DLC-scale material: "Removing Fort McMurdo's capital-adjacent institutional memory (a hypothetical
  DLC-scale event, not proposed here as anything already planned) would leave considerably less behind to
  carry its founding identity forward than an equivalent loss would leave at Dumont d'Urville or Janbogo."
  Flagged as "a genuine structural asymmetry within the subnet, not a problem requiring resolution now" —
  this candidate is the resolution attempt that document deliberately declined to make.
- **Janbogo/Ross Subnet Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2** — the tested distinction between functional daily
  transmission (a founding culture surviving because ordinary civic life is still conducted in it) and
  ceremonial-only preservation (surviving only in names, holidays, and institutional memory, vulnerable to
  further dilution): Dumont d'Urville's French and Janbogo's absorption culture both read as functional;
  Fort McMurdo reads as ceremonial-leaning, resting on historical capital status and Saint-adjacent memory
  shared with Scott at Hut Point rather than on any everyday practice a new resident simply picks up.
- **`reference_national_capital_resolved` (established project fact)** — Fort McMurdo was the historical
  capital; the present day has no formal capital at all, meaning any capital-adjacent institutional
  designation Fort McMurdo still holds is already, administratively, vestigial rather than functionally
  descriptive of the nation's actual current governance.
- **Fort McMurdo's own established "presence-not-population" identity** — its civic gravity is explicitly
  operational, not political; it doesn't posture in Federation debates, it's just quietly indispensable —
  meaning any threat to its founding culture specifically has to come from an administrative or symbolic
  source, not a functional one, since its actual present-day operational role isn't in question.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim that this chain settles whether Fort McMurdo's founding culture
  "should" survive, or any invented new living tradition presented as a real, working solution — Pattern 2's
  own finding is that functional transmission emerged organically over generations at Dumont d'Urville and
  Janbogo; this chain treats manufacturing an equivalent on short notice as genuinely implausible, not a
  problem this quest can solve.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A modernization effort, administratively reasonable on its face, proposes formally retiring
Fort McMurdo's remaining historical-capital-adjacent designations and institutions — vestigial titles and
offices that haven't reflected the nation's actual governance since the country stopped having any formal
capital at all. The player is asked to help facilitate the change: streamline outdated administrative
structures, a straightforward bureaucratic cleanup with no obvious downside on paper.

**BUT** — A real segment of Fort McMurdo's own residents pushes back, and it isn't simple nostalgia. Per the
subnet's own established finding (Pattern 2), Fort McMurdo's founding culture persists only through this
exact institutional and ceremonial memory — unlike Dumont d'Urville's French or Janbogo's absorption
culture, there's no ordinary, everyday practice underneath it that any new resident picks up regardless of
origin. Retiring the vestigial designations doesn't just tidy up outdated paperwork; it removes the one
mechanism keeping Fort McMurdo's founding identity alive at all.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to investigate whether administrative modernization and cultural preservation
can actually coexist here — whether some new, living institutional form could carry the founding identity
forward without leaning on titles that no longer describe anything real about the nation's governance.

**BUT** — It turns out genuinely difficult to design a living daily practice to order. Dumont d'Urville's
French and Janbogo's absorption culture both emerged organically, over generations, through ordinary lived
necessity — not through anyone deciding to build them. Any replacement institution the player helps design
risks being just as ceremonial, just as vulnerable to the next round of administrative streamlining, as the
one it would replace. The underlying structural fragility Pattern 2 already identified can't actually be
engineered away within the scope of one policy decision — it can only be preserved as-is or accepted as a
real, foreseeable loss.

**THEREFORE** — The player's actual choice isn't "modernize or preserve, with a clever compromise available
somewhere in between" — the compromise the player was hoping to find doesn't exist. It's a direct choice
between two real outcomes, and no clever design solves it away.

**BUT** — The reversal is that neither outcome is a comfortable resolution. Keeping the vestigial
designations and institutions preserves Fort McMurdo's founding culture precisely because there is no better
mechanism currently available — but it means the city continues carrying administratively "dead weight,"
titles and offices that describe nothing real about the nation's actual governance, purely because letting
them go would let something else die with them. Retiring them achieves genuine administrative honesty about
the nation's actual, decades-old post-capital political reality — but starts Fort McMurdo's founding culture
on the same slow fade Zukelli's own founding culture already suffered through outright destruction (Cross
-City Patterns, Pattern 2's own "moot" verdict on Zukelli) — this time not through war, but through a
deliberate, reasonable-sounding administrative choice.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which cost Fort McMurdo actually accepts: preserve the vestigial capital-adjacent institutions,
keeping the founding culture's only life-support system running at the cost of genuine administrative
modernization and honesty; or retire them as originally proposed, achieving that modernization at the direct,
foreseeable cost of Fort McMurdo's founding identity losing the one thing keeping it alive, with nothing
organic available to replace it in time. Neither choice is presented as obviously correct, and unlike
Candidate #2's crisis-tested rivalry, nothing here forces the decision through an external emergency — it's
a calm, foreseeable, entirely administrative choice the city makes about its own future, with both costs
fully visible before anyone commits to either.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "retire vestigial
titles" into "the titles are the founding culture's only surviving support structure"; the second BUT turns
"design a living replacement" into "a living tradition can't actually be manufactured on demand, so no
replacement genuinely solves the underlying fragility"; the third BUT turns "preserve or modernize" into
"both options carry a real, foreseeable cost, and there's no version that avoids paying one of them."
Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a
disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes a vulnerability the project's own subnet-level synthesis
explicitly named and explicitly declined to develop — flagged word-for-word as "a hypothetical DLC-scale
event, not proposed here as anything already planned" — and builds exactly that event, using only facts the
subnet's own research had already assembled rather than inventing new material. It's also the first Janbogo
subnet candidate whose central tension is genuinely structural rather than emotional or interpersonal: the
problem isn't that anyone is wrong or acting in bad faith, it's that one specific city's founding culture
turns out to rest on a support structure with no organic backup, a fact nobody in the story can actually
change no matter how well the negotiation goes.
