# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #12: "Whichever Reading You Trust"

**What this is:** a twelfth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around a natural-hazard
emergency-response mechanism grounded in a genuine scientific disagreement. **Not canon, not chosen, not the
only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md`
§5a).

**Uses 5 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Fort McMurdo, Scott, Janbogo, Dumont d'Urville, Cape Adare).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Janbogo Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — the established, shared Erebus volcanic monitoring
  program, split by specialization between Fort McMurdo's heavier geological/seismic work and Scott's own
  precision-instrumentation niche.
- **Janbogo Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 1** — Janbogo's own established role as the subnet's message
  -relay point, via Hwy 183 and Terra Nova Bay's polynya maritime access, used here for its general
  hub-and-relay function, not its specific wartime witness history.
- **Janbogo Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3** — Cape Adare's own established coastal position near Janbogo,
  used here to place it plausibly within range of a genuine volcanic hazard's effects.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about Mount Erebus's own real-world eruption history or current
  activity level — this chain treats the monitoring program's own two specialized readings, and their
  disagreement, as the actual stake, not a specific volcanological outcome.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The Erebus monitoring program detects an alarming spike in volcanic activity. The player is
asked to help coordinate an emergency response — a straightforward, if urgent, disaster-preparedness task.

**BUT** — The program's own two specialized readings disagree. Fort McMurdo's heavier geological and seismic
data suggests a serious, imminent threat warranting immediate, disruptive evacuation. Scott's own precision
-instrumentation reading shows real activity, but not yet past the threshold for genuine eruption risk. Both
methods are legitimate on their own terms, and they point in different directions.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to travel to Janbogo, Dumont d'Urville, and Cape Adare to gather a fuller
picture — checking what an evacuation or warning would actually mean for each city's own position and
population — before any response can be decided.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the actual costs come into focus: ordering a full evacuation on Fort
McMurdo's more alarming reading would be hugely disruptive, displacing real populations and halting Fort
McMurdo's own dispersed capital functions, and might prove unnecessary if Scott's own more measured reading
turns out right. Waiting for greater certainty avoids that disruption — but risks catastrophe if the activity
escalates before any response is actually mounted.

**THEREFORE** — What began as coordinating a response becomes a real choice between acting now on the more
cautious data or waiting for clearer confirmation before disrupting anyone's life.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Acting immediately on the more alarming
assessment protects lives against a genuine possible catastrophe — but imposes real, immediate disruption on
Fort McMurdo and nearby cities that may, in hindsight, prove to have been unnecessary, straining trust in
future warnings if this one turns out to be a false alarm. Waiting for greater certainty avoids that
disruption and preserves the credibility of the warning system for real future emergencies — but risks a
genuine catastrophe if the activity does escalate before anyone acts.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which response the subnet actually takes: act now on the more cautious reading, protecting against
catastrophe at the cost of real, possibly unnecessary disruption; or wait for more certainty, avoiding that
disruption at the cost of real risk if the more alarming reading turns out to be right.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "respond to a hazard"
into "the subnet's own two expert readings disagree with each other"; the second BUT turns "gather more
information" into "acting on either reading carries a real, opposite kind of cost"; the third BUT turns "act
or wait" into "there is no option that doesn't risk either needless disruption or a real catastrophe."
Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a
disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that its central conflict comes from two legitimate, differently-specialized
scientific methods disagreeing in good faith, rather than from any city's flaw or hidden agenda, giving DLC 6
a dilemma with no antagonist at all. It also puts the subnet's own established Erebus monitoring partnership
under real, immediate pressure for the first time, testing whether a long-running cooperative program can
actually produce a single, usable answer when the stakes stop being routine.
