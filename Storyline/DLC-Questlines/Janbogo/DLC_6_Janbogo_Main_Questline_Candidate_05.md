# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #5: "Whatever It Actually Is"

**What this is:** a fifth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around Dumont d'Urville — the
one Janbogo/Ross subnet city no prior candidate has used. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate**
— the 5th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities (Dumont d'Urville alone)**.

**Deliberately does not resolve the mainland bridge's own established open question.** Dumont d'Urville's
own Full Extrapolation, Section III, is explicit: whether the crossing to the mainland is a genuine
permanent structure or the previously-established seasonal ice/boat crossing is "NOT yet resolved... not a
placeholder awaiting a quick answer," and the document deliberately declines to propose a resolution. This
chain follows that same instruction — it's written to work identically regardless of which the crossing
turns out to be, and treats the *ambiguity itself*, mirrored as a genuine, ongoing professional disagreement
between the city's own engineers, as part of the actual quest content rather than a gap the player or this
document should quietly settle.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Dumont d'Urville's own Full Extrapolation, Section II** — the city was hit relatively early in the Long
  Night War specifically because of its established status as a genuine two-highway junction (Hwy 2 and Hwy
  183 meeting directly here), a coordination node worth striking early to disrupt Tepenian subnet
  coordination before it could organize a response.
- **Dumont d'Urville's own Full Extrapolation, Section III** — the mainland bridge/crossing question,
  explicitly and deliberately left open by developer instruction; the full engineering brainstorm (flutter
  risk, pack ice pier loads, maintenance access, candidate solutions for each) already recorded in
  `Specs/Dumont_dUrville.md` stands as the complete state of the question.
- **The established downtown/mainland spatial stratification** — a dense, expensive, alive downtown core on
  Petrel Island proper, connected via the crossing to cheaper residential territory on the mainland side and,
  from there, the highway network — an established economic and geographic divide this chain treats as
  already-real rather than invented.
- **Janbogo/Ross Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Dumont d'Urville "carries the verdict of a core
  worth keeping — struck early for its junction value, but never finished, because its downtown was never
  the part that mattered strategically" — meaning the crossing itself, not the downtown core, absorbed the
  war's disruption.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about the crossing's fundamental engineering nature, permanent or
  seasonal — every beat below is written to hold under either answer, consistent with the developer's own
  standing instruction to leave the question open. Also excluded: any dependency on Pink Lucy or her own
  still-undeveloped personal questline — she left Dumont d'Urville before this chain's own present-day
  events and isn't required for this chain to function.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Post-war, the crossing connecting Petrel Island's downtown core to the mainland has never been
restored to full pre-war reliability — consistent with the subnet's own established pattern of the
downtown's core value surviving while its junction function absorbed the disruption. Mainland residents,
already on the cheaper, more residential side of the city's own established economic divide, have
degraded access to downtown's commercial and live-music economy, the actual source of much of the work
available to them. The player is asked to help get real, sustained repair work moving at last — a
straightforward infrastructure-restoration task.

**BUT** — Committing to a real fix means committing to an actual engineering approach, and the city's own
planners and engineers are genuinely divided over what the crossing fundamentally is and, therefore, what
"repairing" it even means — the same live technical uncertainty the project's own established material
leaves deliberately unresolved. A crossing built or reinforced as if it were permanent, load-bearing
infrastructure looks nothing like one built or reinforced as if it were an inherently seasonal, weather
-dependent link, and the city can't get resources moving without someone actually deciding which approach to
commit to.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to broker an actual decision between the city's own competing engineering
factions — not adjudicate the abstract technical question itself, but get enough of a working consensus that
real repair work can finally begin instead of remaining stuck in the same unresolved argument that's already
outlasted the post-war reconstruction period so far.

**BUT** — Whichever direction wins, it costs the mainland community something specific. A lighter, faster,
season-appropriate solution gets reliable access restored sooner and cheaper — but leaves mainland residents
exposed to unpredictable closures during exactly the seasons they most need steady access to downtown's
economy, the same precarity a seasonal crossing has always implied for whoever depends on it for work. A
heavier, more durable, permanent-style solution offers real long-term reliability — but costs far more,
diverting reconstruction resources that would otherwise go toward the mainland community's own housing and
infrastructure needs, taxing the very population it's meant to serve in order to build something meant to
serve them.

**THEREFORE** — What looked like a straightforward repair decision becomes a real trade-off between the
mainland community's own more urgent, near-term needs and its longer-term reliability — a choice about which
kind of hardship the same community is asked to absorb, not whether it absorbs one at all.

**BUT** — The reversal: there's no version of this that both the engineers and the community fully accept,
because the underlying uncertainty about what the crossing actually is was never something the player's
negotiation could resolve — it can only decide which unresolved risk the city is willing to build around.
Even a genuinely well-brokered compromise (a scaled, adaptive solution, say, sized to hedge against either
possibility) means Dumont d'Urville is deliberately building infrastructure it still doesn't fully understand
the nature of, an honest but unglamorous outcome next to either faction's own preferred, more confident
answer.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which real cost the city actually accepts: the faster, cheaper, season-hedged fix, restoring access
sooner at the price of lasting unpredictability the mainland community will keep living with; the slower,
costlier, more durable fix, buying long-term reliability at the direct expense of other mainland needs the
diverted resources would otherwise have gone to; or a genuinely hedged middle approach, honest about the
underlying uncertainty rather than resolving it, that satisfies neither engineering faction fully but asks
the least of any single group at once. None of the three outcomes actually answers what the crossing
fundamentally is — only how the city chooses to live with not knowing.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "repair the crossing"
into "repairing it requires resolving an engineering dispute the city itself has never settled"; the second
BUT turns "pick an approach" into "every approach costs the same community something specific, just a
different kind of cost"; the third BUT turns "choose the best option" into "there is no option that avoids
building around real uncertainty, only different ways of living with it." Deleting any one Complication
changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it turns an explicitly, deliberately unresolved design question — the
bridge's own fundamental nature — into genuine, load-bearing quest content without ever actually resolving
it, by locating the real drama in the city's own in-world uncertainty rather than in the player (or this
document) quietly picking an answer the developer has specifically asked to leave open. It's also the first
Janbogo subnet candidate whose central conflict is economic rather than about memory, credit, identity, or
physical danger — a straightforward, material question of which community absorbs which cost, made harder
specifically because the underlying technical facts needed to fully resolve it aren't actually available to
anyone in the story, developer included.
