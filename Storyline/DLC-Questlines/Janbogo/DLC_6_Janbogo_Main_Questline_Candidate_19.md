# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #19: "What's Already Living in the Empty Rooms"

**What this is:** a nineteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around a reconstruction
-versus-wildlife-coexistence dilemma. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear
Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 4 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Dumont d'Urville, Fort McMurdo, Janbogo, Cape Adare), per this
round's own "at least 4" standard.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Dumont d'Urville Mega-Init and Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the established fact that the
  Géologie Archipelago holds one of the largest Adélie breeding grounds in Antarctica, that penguins have
  colonized the city's own abandoned, post-war-damaged sections, and that the city's whole defining civic
  character is "negotiated coexistence" — accommodation, not resistance, toward the wind, the sea, and the
  penguins alike — plus the established robot naturalist-observation tradition as "the closest thing the city
  has to a shared contemplative practice."
- **Cape Adare's own established penguin-keeping tradition** (Full Extrapolation, Section VII, Rookery-Keeper
  Tomás Widjaja), used here only as a source of comparative experience in human-penguin coexistence, not as
  a claim that the two colonies are connected.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Dumont d'Urville's specific current population figures or exact
  housing shortfall — this chain treats the housing need as real and pressing without inventing a specific
  number.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — As Dumont d'Urville's post-war reconstruction gains momentum, planners want to reclaim the
city's own abandoned sections — the ones penguins have colonized since the war — to rebuild badly needed
housing. The player is asked to help plan the reconstruction — a straightforward, if consequential,
urban-renewal task.

**BUT** — Displacing the penguins that have colonized these spaces isn't simply clearing an inconvenience.
It cuts directly against the city's own established "negotiated coexistence" identity and its own naturalist
-observation tradition — the very thing that makes Dumont d'Urville what it is, rather than a problem to be
solved.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to consult the city's own community, including its naturalist-observation
practitioners, and travel to Fort McMurdo for reconstruction funding, Janbogo for construction and logistics
support, and Cape Adare for its own comparative experience managing a human-penguin relationship, before any
plan can move forward.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the actual stakes are weighed on both sides: the city's returning
population has a real, pressing need for this exact housing, not an abstract inconvenience. But the Adélie
colony here is confirmed as one of the largest in Antarctica, meaning any significant habitat loss within
city limits carries real, non-trivial ecological consequence, not a matter of a few displaced birds.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a reconstruction plan becomes a real choice between addressing genuine,
current housing need and protecting a large, ecologically significant wildlife population that has become
part of the city's own defining character.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Reclaiming the penguin-colonized sections for
housing addresses a genuine, pressing need for the city's own returning residents — but displaces part of one
of Antarctica's largest Adélie breeding grounds and undoes the very coexistence the city has, in a real
sense, chosen to be proud of having carried through the war intact. Preserving the habitat protects both the
colony and Dumont d'Urville's own defining civic character — but leaves real, returning residents without
adequate housing, forcing overcrowding or continued displacement elsewhere in the city for the sake of an
identity and a wildlife population, however genuinely valued.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether reconstruction actually reclaims the penguin-colonized sections or is redirected elsewhere:
reclaim them, meeting real housing need at the cost of the colony and the city's own coexistence identity; or
protect the habitat, preserving both at the cost of leaving real housing need unmet.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "reclaim the space" into
"reclaiming it means undoing the city's own defining coexistence identity"; the second BUT turns "consult the
stakeholders" into "both the housing need and the ecological stakes are genuinely significant, not one
trivial and one serious"; the third BUT turns "reclaim or protect" into "there is no option that doesn't cost
either real housing need or a large, established wildlife population and the city's own character." Deleting
any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And
Then.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Dumont d'Urville's own established "negotiated coexistence"
identity — already the city's defining civic trait, not an invented complication — and asks what that
coexistence actually costs once the city needs something concrete and pressing that the wildlife is
currently occupying. It's also the first candidate in this run to treat a wildlife population, rather than a
policy, a relationship, or a piece of infrastructure, as one of the two things genuinely at stake in the
player's own final choice.
