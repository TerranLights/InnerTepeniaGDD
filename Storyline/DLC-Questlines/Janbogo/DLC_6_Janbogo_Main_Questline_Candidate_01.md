# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #1: "A Message Meant for Someone Else"

**What this is:** a first prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate** — a worked demonstration of the method, covering exactly one of the "at least 10" possibilities
that method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md`
§5a).

**Unlike DLC 5 (Salagéa Aparast, confirmed protagonist) or DLC 1 (Kendra Heinrich, confirmed protagonist),
DLC 6 currently has no established protagonist or reserved companion** — `TODO.md` lists its "character and
storyline" as flatly TBD. This candidate is therefore necessarily protagonist-agnostic, not by the same
deliberate design choice as Halley subnet's candidates, but because there's nothing yet to design around or
route past.

**Builds directly on an already-established DLC 6 gameplay mechanic** — the stat-gated decode of Zukelli's
own resident-only navigation-marking system (10 Calculation, 10 Investigation, 6+ Engine, 6+ Nerve; see
`Specs/Zukelli.md`'s Gameplay Notes), confirmed by the developer as a concrete DLC 6 mechanic. This chain
gives that existing mechanic an actual quest reason to matter at main-questline scale, rather than treating
it as a standalone observation check.

**Uses 2 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Janbogo, Zukelli — the latter as an explorable ruin, not a
living city).

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Janbogo/Ross Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section I** — the subnet's own "survival was a verdict"
  identity: Janbogo "carries the verdict of ongoing use," spared specifically because a living witness was
  worth more than a dead one; Zukelli "carries the verdict of deterrence," destroyed not for what it had but
  for what its destruction needed to say to everyone else.
- **Janbogo/Ross Subnet Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 1** — Janbogo was spared specifically because its
  unique value as a living witness, capable of relaying Zukelli's destruction onward via Hwy 183 and Terra
  Nova Bay's polynya, made it worth more intact than destroyed; this establishes Janbogo's own internal
  "vigil-vs-moving-on" tension as the accumulated weight of at least two witnessed destructions (Zukelli,
  Cape Adare), not one.
- **Janbogo's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 2** — Janbogo's real distinguishing civic trait isn't
  any single cultural content but a repeatable process by which anything that reaches the city eventually
  becomes simply "Janbogo's own" — a genuinely distinctive absorb-and-renew throughline, in tension with the
  weight of sustained, backward-facing witness-bearing.
- **Janbogo's own Full Extrapolation, Section VII** — the Zukelli defectors were deliberately proposed as a
  small, mixed, multi-national group, strengthening the strike's own "message to all of Tepenia" universal
  logic rather than reading as retaliation against one nation specifically.
- **`TODO.md`'s Census finding on Zukelli** — Zukelli did not die half-empty: 72.5% human and 77.7% robot
  retention, near its absolute peak population when destroyed. The grief carries no softening qualifier —
  this is remembered as watching a full, thriving city burn, not a fading one.
- **Zukelli's own established resident-only navigation-marking system** (`Zukelli_Full_Extrapolation.md`,
  Section II) — organic color/pattern conventions on covered connections and heating-pipe conduits, developed
  by residents for their own daily use, not for outsiders or posterity.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim that this chain fully resolves Janbogo's own "vigil-vs-moving-on"
  tension, or that it settles whether the deterrent message "worked" (Full Extrapolation, Section VIII,
  already treated as functionally resolved) — this chain works at the scale of one specific act of memory
  -work, not the subnet's entire post-war psychology.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Zukelli's physical ruins are visibly degrading, and a Janbogo civic effort — call it a memorial
documentation project — wants to properly record what's left of the resident-only navigation-marking system
before it's gone for good: a genuine historical record of how Zukelli's people actually organized their own
daily lives, not just the fact of their deaths. The player, meeting the stat threshold the city's own
established mechanic requires, is asked to go decode and document as much of it as can still be read.

**BUT** — Not everyone in Janbogo wants this. The city's own real civic strength, per its established
absorb-and-renew character, comes from moving forward and remaking whatever reaches it rather than staying
anchored to old grief — and a prolonged, formal act of remembrance risks reinforcing exactly the
vigil-identity some residents feel Janbogo has already carried long enough, at the cost of the renewal
-oriented identity that's arguably its real distinguishing trait.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to build real support for the documentation effort across a genuinely divided
city before the work can even properly begin — not a simple permission-seeking errand, but an actual
negotiation between people who experience the same civic history in two different ways.

**BUT** — Once the player actually gets into the ruins and starts decoding the markings, what emerges isn't
simple, confirmatory grief-material. The patterns reveal signs of real, considered foresight — evacuation
routes, cache locations, systems that read less like the ordinary daily wayfinding of people going about
their lives and more like a population that had, on some level, prepared for exactly the kind of danger that
eventually found them. This doesn't fit the story either side of Janbogo's own divide has been telling
itself: Zukelli's residents weren't purely symbolic victims chosen to carry a message meant for everyone
else — some of them, plausibly, saw real risk coming and built quiet contingencies of their own, agency the
"verdict of deterrence" framing (Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section I) never had room for.

**THEREFORE** — What the player is holding now isn't the record the memorial project set out to make. It's
a genuinely more complicated, more humanizing picture of who Zukelli's people actually were — and a real
decision about what to do with it.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Publishing the full findings honors Zukelli's
residents as people who saw and prepared, not merely people something happened to — but it also unsettles
both sides of Janbogo's own existing divide: it complicates the vigil-keepers' settled grief (the population
they've mourned as pure, chosen victims turns out to have had its own agency and awareness), and it makes
"moving on" harder, not easier, for the renewal-minded residents, since a genuinely new, more specific truth
is a much harder thing to simply absorb and set down than an old, familiar grief already has been. Keeping
the findings quiet — specialist archival material only, not integrated into the city's own working civic
memory — avoids reopening either side's settled position, but leaves Zukelli's own residents exactly as
flattened by the "message" framing as they've been since the war, their real foresight and agency known to a
handful of researchers and nobody else.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
choice about what happens to the findings: integrate them fully into Janbogo's own civic memory — a truer,
more humanizing record of Zukelli, at the cost of genuinely destabilizing both sides of a civic tension the
city had, until now, at least learned to live inside; or preserve them quietly, respecting the fragile
equilibrium Janbogo's own vigil-vs-moving-on divide has settled into, at the cost of leaving Zukelli's people
remembered, permanently, as symbols in someone else's deterrent message rather than as the specific, aware,
full-at-their-peak population the census itself already insists they were.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "document the ruins"
into "the whole city isn't agreed this documentation should happen at all"; the second BUT turns "the decode
succeeded" into "what it found doesn't match the story either side of Janbogo already believes"; the third
BUT turns "share the findings" into "sharing them costs the exact equilibrium the city has learned to live
inside, and hiding them costs Zukelli's own people a truer memory." Deleting any one Complication changes
what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it gives an already-locked gameplay mechanic (the Zukelli decode check)
a genuine main-questline reason to matter, rather than leaving it as an isolated observation reward, and that
its climax complicates the subnet's own defining thematic frame — "survival was a verdict" — from the inside:
Zukelli's residents were assigned the verdict of deterrence by Upper Earth's own strike, but the decoded
markings suggest at least some of them were never simply waiting to be a message, they were people managing
real risk on their own terms, a fact Janbogo's own civic memory, on either side of its internal divide, has
had no way to know until the player's own work surfaces it. It's also the first Janbogo subnet candidate to
treat the census's own "died at its peak, not in decline" framing as something the memorial work can either
honor more fully or flatten further, depending entirely on the player's own choice.
