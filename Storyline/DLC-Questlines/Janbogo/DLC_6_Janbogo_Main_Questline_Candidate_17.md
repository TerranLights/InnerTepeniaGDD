# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #17: "Whatever the Record Already Decided"

**What this is:** a seventeenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around a historical
-legitimacy investigation into a settled civic narrative. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.**
Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 4 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Fort McMurdo, Scott, Janbogo, Dumont d'Urville), per this
round's own "at least 4" standard.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Janbogo Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — the established fact that Fort McMurdo's own capital
  timeline ran in two distinct stages: an earlier, informal, "arguably contestable" prominence, followed by a
  later, formal designation within the Second Interwar Period.
- **Scott's own established rivalry trait** (Throughway 2) — "somewhat allergic to being perceived as Fort
  McMurdo's smaller sibling," used here to justify Scott as a plausible historical counter-claimant during
  that earlier, informal period specifically, not the present day.
- **Janbogo's own established hub/relay function** (Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3 and 5), used here only for
  its general coordination role, not any specific claim about wartime witness duty.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about what Fort McMurdo's actual formal capital designation
  process consisted of, or any suggestion that its present-day, formal status is genuinely in question — this
  chain treats that later formalization as settled and undisputed.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A historical researcher revisits Fort McMurdo's own two-phase rise to capital status and finds
real ambiguity in the earlier, informal period — whether Fort McMurdo's own accepted claim to having been
Tepenia's effective seat of coordination during that time is accurate, or a story that solidified into fact
more through later retelling than genuine primacy at the time. The player is asked to help investigate the
historical record — a straightforward, if delicate, historical-research task.

**BUT** — The investigation turns up real evidence that another city — plausibly Scott, given its own
established rivalry, or Janbogo, given its own coordination role — held an equally plausible practical claim
to that same function during the informal period. Fort McMurdo's own accepted narrative may have quietly
overwritten a more genuinely contested, shared early history.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to travel to Scott, Janbogo, and Dumont d'Urville to gather each city's own
historical records and institutional memory, piecing together what the informal period actually looked like
before any single account can be trusted.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the fuller picture comes together: correcting the record to reflect a more
contested, shared early history would be more historically honest — but Fort McMurdo's own present-day civic
identity and pride are genuinely built on the simplified, accepted version of this story, one the whole
country has taken for granted for generations. Publicly complicating it risks real damage to that identity
for a correction with no bearing on Fort McMurdo's actual, undisputed formal status today.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a historical investigation becomes a real question about whether an honest,
complicated correction is worth unsettling a civic narrative that carries no current practical stakes at all.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Publishing the more accurate, complicated
account honors historical truth and gives real, overdue acknowledgment to whichever other city's genuine
claim has gone unrecognized — but risks destabilizing Fort McMurdo's own settled civic pride for a correction
with no bearing on its actual, current status. Leaving the simplified, accepted narrative alone preserves
that civic morale and avoids stirring up a dispute nobody currently needs resolved — but leaves a known
historical inaccuracy on the record indefinitely, and leaves whichever other city's own real claim
permanently unacknowledged.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether the corrected history actually gets published: publish it, honoring accuracy at the cost of
Fort McMurdo's own settled civic pride; or leave the record as it stands, preserving that pride at the cost of
a known inaccuracy and another city's unacknowledged claim.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "verify the history"
into "another city has a real, comparably plausible claim to the same period"; the second BUT turns "confirm
the correction" into "the correction has a real emotional cost with no practical governance stakes attached";
the third BUT turns "publish or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either historical accuracy
or Fort McMurdo's own settled civic pride." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually
choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Throughway 2's own established "two-phase, arguably
contestable" capital history — already flagged in the source material as an open ambiguity, not a settled
fact — and builds a dilemma directly from the gap between what's historically accurate and what a whole
country has simply taken for granted for generations. It's also the first candidate in this run whose stakes
are purely reputational and historical, with no material, safety, or resource consequence riding on the
outcome at all.
