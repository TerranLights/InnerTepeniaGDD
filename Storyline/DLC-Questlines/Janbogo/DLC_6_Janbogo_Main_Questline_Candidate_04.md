# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #4: "Built From What's Left"

**What this is:** a fourth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around Cape Adare's own
established heritage-preservation question — explicitly named, in the city's own Specs file, as "the most
emotionally significant open question in Tepenian heritage," and untouched by any prior candidate. **Not
canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 4th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the
Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 2 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities** (Cape Adare, as ruins and archive source; Janbogo, as the
proposed site for physical reconstruction, given its established role as the subnet's living hub and its
own civic character built around absorbing whatever reaches it).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Cape Adare's own Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Borchgrevink's original 1899 hut did not survive the
  strike that destroyed the city around it, but Cape Adare's own established "always historically conscious"
  character makes it plausible that centuries of careful documentation — measurements, photographs, replica
  plans, a dedicated heritage archive — existed well before the war and were evacuated or backed up
  elsewhere ahead of destruction. The structure is gone; the record of exactly what it was very likely isn't.
  A loss, but not a total erasure.
- **Cape Adare's own Full Extrapolation, Section VII** — Archivist Elena Manalo, credited with maintaining
  the heritage documentation of the hut across centuries, the figure behind the proposed surviving record.
- **Janbogo/Ross Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section III** — Cape Adare's own "firstness" civic identity,
  paralleled to Jamestown, Virginia: a small city's whole civic claim to significance resting entirely on
  having been first, independent of whatever it grew into or failed to grow into afterward.
- **Janbogo's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 2** — Janbogo's established civic trait: not any single
  cultural content, but a repeatable process by which anything that reaches the city eventually becomes
  simply "Janbogo's own" — a natural, earned reason a reconstruction project might plausibly be proposed
  there rather than left purely as an archival record with no physical home at all.
- **Janbogo/Ross Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Cape Adare "carries the verdict of a second
  message," its destruction folded into Janbogo's own accumulating witness burden, giving Janbogo a
  standing, established civic relationship to Cape Adare's loss specifically, not just a generic
  neighborly one.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about who specifically destroyed Cape Adare's motive beyond what's
  already established (a late-war, symbolic rather than strategic target, Full Extrapolation Section IV);
  this chain doesn't revisit that question, only what's done with what survived it.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A heritage effort, drawing on Cape Adare's own preserved archival record — Archivist Elena
Manalo's centuries of documentation, evacuated or backed up before the city's destruction — proposes using
those measurements, photographs, and replica plans to physically reconstruct Borchgrevink's original hut,
sited in Janbogo rather than at Cape Adare's own inaccessible ruins, giving the nation somewhere to actually
stand and feel connected to "the place where Antarctica's human story began." The player is asked to help
gather and verify what remains of the archival material scattered among descendants, backup sites, and
whatever's still recoverable from Cape Adare itself — a straightforward heritage-retrieval task.

**BUT** — A genuine civic debate erupts once the proposal becomes public, and it isn't a simple
procedural objection. Some feel a replica built somewhere safer and more visited cheapens the loss — turning
a real, symbolic-target tragedy into a comfortable facsimile that lets people forget the actual hut is
actually gone. Others feel a reconstruction built accurately from Elena Manalo's own surviving records is
exactly the kind of continuity Section I's own resolution already points toward — proof that the record's
survival, not just the structure's, is real and worth acting on, not merely worth filing away.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to build genuine support for the project across this divide before gathering
the actual archival material can even properly begin — not a permission formality, but a real disagreement
about what memory owes to loss.

**BUT** — Once the player actually assembles the surviving documentation properly, it turns out to be
incomplete in a specific, meaningful way: certain structural details of exactly how the original hut stood
were never fully recorded even in Elena Manalo's own careful archive, meaning any full reconstruction would
necessarily fill real gaps with informed approximation, not verified fact. What was supposed to be "the
record surviving where the structure didn't" turns out to be a partial record, and finishing the
reconstruction means deciding whether to present those filled-in gaps as historically faithful or to be
honest that parts of it are educated guesswork.

**THEREFORE** — The question the player is actually facing isn't "should this be built" anymore — that's
already been debated and largely settled — it's whether a reconstruction with real, undisclosed gaps in it is
actually a form of honoring the record, or a quieter kind of the same cheapening some residents feared from
the start.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Building it anyway, with the gaps openly
disclosed — clearly marking which elements are verified from Elena Manalo's own record and which are
informed reconstruction — gives the nation something real and physically present at the cost of a messier,
visibly imperfect monument, one that can never fully deliver the seamless "this is exactly what stood there"
experience the original proposal promised. Declining to build it at all preserves the integrity of what
actually survived — a genuine, if partial and physically absent, record — but leaves nothing anywhere a
citizen or player could stand in front of and feel the weight Cape Adare's own firstness identity has always
claimed for itself.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which outcome the project actually reaches: build the reconstruction with the gaps in the record
honestly marked, giving people something physical and present but visibly, admittedly incomplete; or decline
to build it, keeping Cape Adare's own surviving heritage exactly as real, exact, and physically absent as
Elena Manalo's own record actually left it. Neither path lets the nation have both a complete, seamless
monument and an honest one at the same time.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "gather the archive"
into "the whole project is genuinely contested before it can even start"; the second BUT turns "the archive
was assembled" into "the archive itself has real, meaningful gaps that change what 'reconstruction' actually
means"; the third BUT turns "finish the reconstruction" into "there is no version of finishing it that is
both complete and fully honest." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing
at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes a question the project's own Specs file explicitly names "the
most emotionally significant open question in Tepenian heritage" and gives it a concrete, playable stake —
not simply resolving whether the record survived (Section I already proposes it did, at least partially),
but testing what a nation actually does with a partial record once it decides that record matters enough to
act on. It's also the first Janbogo subnet candidate to route its climax through Janbogo's own established
"absorbs whatever reaches it" civic character (Cross-Reference Finding 2) as a genuine asset — the same
trait that let Chinese-French fashion and a Korean teahouse tradition both become simply "Janbogo's own" is
exactly what makes the city a plausible home for someone else's most sacred ruin, whether or not that
turns out to be the right call.
