# DLC 6 (Janbogo/Ross Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #7: "Not All Equally Kin"

**What this is:** a seventh prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 6's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Janbogo/Ross subnet, built around a genuine cross-subnet
contact the project's own material explicitly names as future DLC territory: the first real meeting between
Tepenia's three Korean-founded cities. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 7th of the
"at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Janbogo/Ross subnet's 7 cities (Janbogo) as host** — Sejong (Palmer subnet) and Sinheung (Mirny
subnet) appear as visiting delegations, referenced through facts already established in Janbogo's own Full
Extrapolation rather than developed further here; this candidate doesn't add new material about either city
beyond what that document already lays out.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Janbogo's own Full Extrapolation, Section V** — Tepenia's three Korean-founded cities are "kin, not
  siblings, and not all equally kin": Janbogo (Unified Korea) and Sejong (South Korea) share limited but
  real ceremonial contact; Sinheung reached its Korean-founded status through an entirely different
  mechanism (the Jeju-do diplomatic partition), sits at a far greater geographic and historical remove from
  both, and essentially has no established pre-Split-Brain contact with either. The explicitly named irony:
  Sinheung is, by current population, the most heavily and unambiguously Korean city in Tepenia (34.62%
  Primary), more so than Janbogo, which built and kept the deepest *institutional* Korean legacy (the
  teahouse tradition) despite its own Korean population diluting to Significant tier, and far more so than
  Sejong, where Korean identity survives only as founding-era naming heritage.
- **The same section's own explicit invitation** — "if these three communities ever do make genuine contact
  in future DLC content, that contact would surface a real asymmetry" — this candidate is that contact.
- **Janbogo's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 2** — Janbogo's real distinguishing civic trait isn't
  any single cultural content but a repeatable process of absorbing and re-originating whatever reaches it
  as simply "Janbogo's own" — a fact this chain treats as a genuine, live question about the teahouse
  tradition's own authenticity, not just a neutral civic compliment.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any new material about Sejong's or Sinheung's own internal culture beyond what
  Janbogo's own Full Extrapolation already states; this chain doesn't develop those two cities further, it
  only brings their already-established facts into direct, in-person contact with Janbogo's own.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A cultural exchange, unprecedented in living memory, brings small delegations from Sejong and
Sinheung to Janbogo specifically because of their shared Korean founding heritage — the first genuine,
in-person contact between all three communities. Janbogo's own established hospitality-and-synthesis civic
identity makes it a natural host. The player is asked to help organize and facilitate the visit — a
straightforward cultural-liaison task, expected to be a warm, kinship-affirming occasion.

**BUT** — Contact makes the established asymmetry immediately, uncomfortably visible in a way the abstract
fact never was. Sinheung's delegation, by far the most Korean city in Tepenia by population, has the least
developed institutional Korean culture to actually show for it — their civic identity centers on other
things entirely. Sejong's own Korean identity amounts to little more than founding-era naming heritage.
Janbogo's own delegation carries the deepest lived Korean tradition of the three, the teahouse and
everything built around it — despite being, by the numbers, the least Korean of the three going forward.
Each delegation arrived expecting some form of kinship or validation and instead encounters a version of
"being Korean-founded" that doesn't match what they brought or expected to find.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to help mediate a real, sometimes uncomfortable conversation between people
who expected simple kinship and found genuine mismatch instead — not a diplomatic formality, but an actual
reckoning with what these three communities' shared origin does and doesn't actually mean.

**BUT** — The friction cuts in a direction nobody on any side anticipated. Sinheung's delegates feel a real,
quiet exposure — being "most Korean" by the numbers and having comparatively little cultural depth to show
for it in front of the other two. But Janbogo's own residents develop a different, harder discomfort: is the
teahouse tradition genuinely Korean heritage, carried forward the way Dumont d'Urville's French or the
subnet's own established transmission patterns work — or is it another instance of Janbogo's own established
civic habit (Cross-Reference Finding 2) of absorbing whatever reaches it and simply making it "Janbogo's
own," in which case its claim to Korean authenticity is genuinely different in kind from Sinheung's lived
demographic reality, not just different in degree.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a cultural exchange becomes a genuine identity crisis running in both
directions at once — not one community feeling inadequate beside two secure ones, but all three finding
their own claim to "Korean-ness" is real, but real in a fundamentally different way than either of the
others.

**BUT** — The reversal: there's no framing of this that lets all three walk away with the comfortable
kinship narrative the exchange was meant to produce. An honest acknowledgment — that these are three
genuinely different relationships to the same distant origin, not three points on one shared scale —
respects each community's own real, distinct history, but leaves nobody with the simple, validating
"we are all Korean together" story they may have hoped this visit would confirm. A diplomatically smoother
framing — emphasizing shared origin, downplaying the differences — preserves warmth and the possibility of
continued contact going forward, but risks flattening each community's own specific, hard-won story into a
more convenient, shared one that isn't quite true for any of them.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how the exchange actually concludes: guide it toward honest acknowledgment of three genuinely
different kinds of claim to the same origin, at the cost of the comfortable unity narrative nobody quite gets
to keep; or guide it toward a warmer, smoother framing that protects the relationship and the possibility of
future contact, at the cost of leaving something real and specific about each community's own story
unspoken. Either way, this first contact settles the question Section V's own explicit invitation raised —
just not in a way that resolves it comfortably for everyone involved.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "host a friendly
exchange" into "the exchange immediately surfaces a real, uncomfortable asymmetry"; the second BUT turns
"mediate the discomfort" into "the discomfort runs in both directions, not just toward the community that
expected to feel inadequate"; the third BUT turns "conclude the visit" into "there is no conclusion that
gives all three communities the comfortable kinship story they arrived hoping for." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it directly answers an invitation the project's own material issued to
itself — Janbogo's Full Extrapolation explicitly proposing that genuine contact between these three
communities "would surface a real asymmetry" if it ever happened in future DLC content — using only facts
already on record rather than inventing new backstory for either visiting city. It's also the first Janbogo
subnet candidate to turn Janbogo's own celebrated absorb-and-re-originate civic trait (Cross-Reference
Finding 2), treated as an unambiguous strength in every prior candidate that's touched it, into a genuine,
uncomfortable question about authenticity — the same trait that makes Janbogo distinctively itself is exactly
what makes its own claim to Korean heritage vulnerable to the same scrutiny it's never previously had to face.
