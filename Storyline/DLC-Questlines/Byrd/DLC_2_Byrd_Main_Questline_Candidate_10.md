# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #10: "More Mouths Than the Growing Sections Were Built For"

**What this is:** a candidate built around ordinary demographic success straining Byrd's own self-
sufficiency systems, rather than an external disaster — no Cradle/chamber content. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Attributes #24, #42, #43, #54** — in-house food-growth infrastructure, ambient-cold storage, cultured
  protein, and water reservoirs — Byrd's own layered self-sufficiency architecture, all sized to a specific
  population at the time they were built.
- **Finding 21** — the five guild sectors interleave physically; expanding any one system's footprint isn't
  separable from the others' own territory.
- **Finding 25** — a visible old-core vs. newer-expansion structural distinction, the oldest sections
  concentrated nearest the original founding-era construction.
- **Finding 7** — specialized institutional and historical claim concentrating among founding-descendant
  families, in tension with the far larger, later-arrived population.
- **Finding 20** — Coober Pedy's own real precedent: Byrd's demographic tension resolves as genuine
  coexistence without full integration, not one unified, smoothed-over outcome.
- **`Byrd_Catalog.md`'s own Synthesis Notes** — self-sufficiency that began as necessity and became pride
  without much reflection, now facing a problem success itself created.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Generations after the systems were built, Byrd's own Growing Sections, water reservoirs, and
communal dining infrastructure are showing genuine strain — not crisis, just ordinary population growth
quietly outpacing what self-sufficiency architecture designed decades ago was ever sized for. The player is
brought in by guild leadership to help figure out what actually gets built or changed.

**BUT** — Real expansion isn't simple. Per Finding 21, the guild sectors already interleave physically —
there's no obvious empty footprint to just build into without encroaching on some other guild's own
established territory.

**THEREFORE** — The player investigates alternatives, and the most promising option turns out to be
reclaiming underused space in the old core (Finding 25) — sections dating to the earliest, founding-era
construction, structurally sound but no longer used at anything close to capacity.

**BUT** — Reclaiming that space runs directly into Finding 7's own established fault line. The old core is
disproportionately tied to founding-descendant families' own historical claim and institutional memory;
repurposing it for new growth reads, to some of them, as displacement and dilution, while the far larger
later-arrival majority sees straightforward, overdue practical necessity.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to broker this specific, concrete version of Byrd's own demographic tension —
not in the abstract, but over literal physical space and who has a right to decide what happens to it.

**BUT (reversal)** — Finding 20's own real-world grounding complicates the obvious "fair" answer. Coober
Pedy's precedent argues Byrd's actual stability has always rested on coexistence without full integration —
pushing for one clean, unified expansion plan that treats the whole city as a single undifferentiated
population might resolve the capacity problem efficiently, but risks the exact model that's kept two
distinct populations sharing Byrd without friction for generations.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: push for full, efficient integration and expansion,
solving the capacity crisis cleanly at the risk of the coexistence balance Finding 20 says has actually held
the city together; or negotiate a more limited, segmented solution that respects existing social boundaries
and founding-descendant claims, preserving that balance at the cost of leaving part of Byrd's own population
under-resourced going forward.

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns "build more capacity" into
"there's no free space to build into." The second BUT turns "reclaim the old core" into "that space carries
real, contested historical weight." The third BUT means even the efficient fix has a real social cost. No
Complication is deletable without losing the final choice.
