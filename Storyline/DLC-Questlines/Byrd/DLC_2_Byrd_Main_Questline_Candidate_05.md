# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #5: "A Diagnosis Worth Keeping"

**What this is:** the first replacement candidate written after Candidate #1 was ruled out by direct
developer instruction (the Cradle/chamber-manufacturing network must stay optional, unmarked side-content,
never a main-quest driver). This candidate is offered specifically as the new flagship option for giving
Maggie Aarden's established industrial/welding skills a genuine, direct mechanical stake — the same design
goal that made Candidate #1 attractive — without going anywhere near the Chamber Works. **Not canon, not
chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Course of Events chain #7** ("The Fleet That Grounded Itself") — Byrd's aviation fleet is permanently
  grounded not from lack of skill, but because a specific material or component the repair genuinely
  requires was never something Byrd could produce locally, and the supply chain that once provided it has
  quietly stopped existing.
- **Finding 14** — the Grounded Wings guild evolved into devoted archivists, keeping a complete, current,
  ready diagnosis of exactly what's missing — "custodians of a standing diagnosis," not resigned mechanics.
- **Finding 23** — this diagnosis has a physical dimension: the mothballed hangar complex (attribute #59) is
  kept in genuinely maintained, ready condition, reading as hope rather than neglect.
- **`Byrd_Community_Infrastructure.md`** — The Hangar Complex, listed as an Addition in its own right, and
  the Grounded Wings' own quietly-respected institutional character among the Social Cohesion Mechanisms.
- **`DLC_Overview.md`** — Maggie Aarden's established industrial/welding skill, and her permanent settlement
  in Byrd post-exile.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Word reaches Byrd, through the highway freight network or the Relay, that the specific material
the Grounded Wings' own decades-old diagnosis says the aviation fleet needs might genuinely be obtainable —
somewhere outside the city, for the first time since the fleet went permanently grounded. Maggie Aarden,
whose own welding/industrial expertise makes her the natural person to actually execute a real repair, is
drawn in immediately.

**BUT** — A lead isn't a component. The Grounded Wings' own diagnosis (Finding 14) is precise about *what*
is needed, but getting an actual, physical answer means the player has to go confirm and retrieve it — deep
into supply-chain, possibly cross-subnet territory Byrd itself has had no reliable contact with since the
fleet first went down.

**THEREFORE** — The player commits to the retrieval, leaning on Byrd's own established freight and highway
relationships (chain #6's Esperanza corridor, or further afield) to actually chase the lead down.

**BUT** — What they find complicates the diagnosis itself. The material exists, but not in the form or
quantity the Grounded Wings' own standing diagnosis assumed — meaning the fix isn't a simple restoration,
but a genuine engineering problem: adapting what's actually available to what the fleet actually needs,
which is exactly the kind of hands-on, high-stakes welding and fabrication work Maggie's own skills were
built for, but with no guarantee of success and real risk if it fails.

**THEREFORE** — The player and Maggie have to actually attempt the repair, working from an imperfect
solution rather than the clean fix the guild spent generations hoping for.

**BUT (reversal)** — Success isn't simply a happy ending. Restoring even limited flight capability changes
Byrd's whole calculus around the Isolation Crisis anchor and the still-unresolved highway question
(Candidate #3) — a working aircraft, even one, makes Byrd's total dependence on Hwy 1/22 no longer absolute,
which some of the city (the Grounded Wings especially) would celebrate, and others — invested in the guild
structure and resource allocation that grew up specifically because the fleet was gone — would see as
disruptive to hard-won, functioning arrangements.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — The player's role in how the repair is used, credited, and integrated into
Byrd's own economy — quietly, as one more tool among many, or as a genuine restoration of something the city
lost — shapes how this success actually lands, consistent with No Good Endings: even the version where the
fix works has a real, felt cost to how Byrd sees itself afterward.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns "a lead exists" into "the
lead still has to be chased down and confirmed." The second BUT turns "retrieve the part" into "the part
doesn't perfectly match what was diagnosed, so it's a real engineering problem, not a delivery." The third
BUT means even success reshapes the city in ways not everyone wants. No Complication is deletable without
losing the final choice's weight.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate does the specific narrative job Candidate #1 was built for — giving Maggie's established
welding/industrial skill a direct, hands-on, high-stakes mechanical stake — using material (chain #7,
Finding 14, Finding 23, the Hangar Complex) that has nothing to do with the Cradle network at all. It should
be treated as the strongest available replacement for "the anchor that gives Maggie something to actually
do with her hands," now that the Chamber Crisis option is off the table.
