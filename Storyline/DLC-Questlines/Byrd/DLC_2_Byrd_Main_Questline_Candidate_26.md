# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #26: "What Vostok Wants Back"

**What this is:** a cross-DLC candidate (Byrd/DLC 2 and Vostok/Mirny subnet, DLC 7) testing the actual terms of the Lake relationship for the first time — a genuine sovereignty question, not a resource crisis. No Cradle content. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Finding 55** — the Lake transplant framed as Vostok's own rare exception to "exported almost nothing else," scientific collaboration rather than trade — deliberately left open in that same Finding: "the exact timing of the transplant... and whether 'Charlene' was personally involved" were never pinned down.
- **`Vostok_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section VII** — the established Vostok-Kunlun infrastructure-sharing relationship as the closest existing precedent for what a Vostok-Byrd relationship might actually look like in practice.
- **`Specs/Vostok.md`** — Vostok's own research culture treats findings as belonging to a distributed, ongoing archive, not a one-time gift with no further interest attached.
- **Finding 53** — the Lake as Byrd's own deliberate, protected exception — something the city has come to treat as genuinely its own.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A message reaches Byrd via the Relay: Vostok's own research program wants something from the Lake in return — ongoing samples, data, or possibly a portion of the Byrd-adapted ecosystem returned to Vostok for direct study, since the population living beside it in Byrd, unlike Vostok's own researchers, isn't studying it scientifically at all, simply living with it.

**BUT** — Complying means allowing outside access to and extraction from what Byrd has treated, per Finding 53, as its own protected, deliberate exception — a real sovereignty question surfaces: is the Lake genuinely Byrd's now, or does Vostok retain some claim, given it made the transplant possible in the first place?

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually negotiate terms that were never formalized when the transplant happened — Finding 55 itself left this deliberately open.

**BUT** — Refusing outright risks real damage to the one meaningful relationship Byrd has with another city that isn't built on the Cradle network or highway freight — Finding 55's own established value. Both cities have legitimate, competing stakes in getting this right.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to find or broker a middle path that respects both Byrd's own ownership and Vostok's legitimate scientific interest in an ecosystem it made possible.

**BUT (reversal)** — Whatever agreement gets reached becomes the actual working template for how "collaboration, not trade" functions from here on — the first real test of whether that framing survives contact with an actual disagreement of interests, or quietly becomes something closer to Vostok holding ongoing rights over what's inside Byrd's own walls.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: formalize generous, ongoing access for Vostok's research, deepening the relationship at the cost of real autonomy over Byrd's own flagship landmark; or hold firm on full Byrd ownership, preserving complete sovereignty at the risk of souring or ending the relationship with the one city that made the Lake possible at all.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns a simple request into a genuine ownership question. The second BUT means refusal has a real cost too. The third BUT means the resolution sets precedent, not just settles one request. No Complication is deletable without losing the final choice's weight.

## Worth Your Attention

This is the first candidate in the whole pool to genuinely test what "collaboration, not trade" (Finding 55) means in practice rather than simply stating it — a natural companion to Candidate #25, which asks the same underlying question from the opposite direction (Byrd needing something from Vostok, rather than Vostok wanting something from Byrd). Together they'd make a strong two-part arc about what an ongoing relationship between two isolated, self-sufficient cities actually costs each side.
