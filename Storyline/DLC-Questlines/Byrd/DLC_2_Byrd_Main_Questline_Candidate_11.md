# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #11: "The Weight of the Reserve"

**What this is:** a power-crisis candidate reframed specifically around city-wide life support — heat, air,
water — rather than industrial or chamber output, so it carries no Cradle/chamber content at all. Distinct
from Candidate #6 (a structural/ground-safety crisis): this candidate's hazard is the power source itself
degrading, threatening survival directly rather than a specific section's structural integrity. **Not canon,
not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Attribute #10** — Byrd's independent, plausibly geothermal power generation.
- **Attribute #70** — a dedicated geothermal/volcanic-activity monitoring station, distinct from general
  structural/seismic monitoring (attribute #5).
- **Attribute #71** — an underground energy storage reserve (flywheel, battery, or thermal buffer),
  smoothing the geothermal source's own variability.
- **Attribute #37** — a single internal power distribution grid feeding heating (attribute #8), ventilation
  (attribute #1), and water production (attribute #11) alike — meaning a power problem is never contained to
  one system.
- **Finding 17** — Byrd's production intensity has never actually distinguished crisis from normal
  operation; the city has no established habit of treating "keep working" and "keep everyone alive" as
  separable priorities.
- **Finding 21** — the five guild sectors interleave physically and economically; no single guild can absorb
  a citywide problem alone.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The geothermal monitoring station (attribute #70) detects a genuine change in the power source's
own behavior — not the structural strain chain #11 already covers, but the source itself becoming less
stable. The player is drawn in through general engineering concern, expecting a contained technical problem.

**BUT** — It isn't contained. Attribute #37 means heating, ventilation, and water production all draw from
the same grid — if the underlying source keeps degrading faster than the Reserve (attribute #71) can buffer
it, the risk isn't reduced industrial output, it's city-wide life support for a sealed population with no
realistic evacuation option.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to help find a way to stabilize the situation — repairing or supplementing
the source, or managing consumption directly — with the clock now measured in survival, not economics.

**BUT** — Byrd's own total isolation closes off the obvious outside options. The aviation fleet has been
permanently grounded since chain #7; the highway's own real status is, per Candidate #3, genuinely uncertain.
Whatever gets done has to be done almost entirely in-house, requiring real cooperation across Finding 21's
own interleaved five-guild economy.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to broker a citywide rationing and prioritization plan — which sections and
which guild functions get power first while the source is stabilized.

**BUT (reversal)** — The "obvious" answer — life support before production, every time — cuts against
something Finding 17 already establishes as core to how Byrd understands itself: production has never been
treated as separable from survival here, not even under wartime pressure. Guild workers, especially in the
Fabricators' and Prospectors' own ranks, may genuinely resist halting work even at real personal risk,
because production has always *been* how this city defines collective survival, not a competing priority
against it.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: enforce strict, life-support-first rationing, guaranteeing
survival at the cost of forcing a halt that cuts directly against Byrd's own established self-understanding
and provokes real civic resistance; or grant guilds more autonomy to balance their own work against personal
risk during the crisis, respecting Byrd's own established values and identity at the cost of real danger if
that balance is misjudged under genuine uncertainty.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns "a technical anomaly" into
"a citywide survival risk." The second BUT turns "get outside help" into "there is no fast outside option."
The third BUT means even the correct-sounding answer conflicts with Byrd's own established identity. No
Complication is deletable without losing the final choice's weight.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate and Candidate #6 both draw on the ice-sheet/geothermal material this session's deep-dive
produced, but split cleanly on stakes — #6 is about a specific section's structural survival and cross-guild
resource cost, #11 is about the whole population's literal survival and Byrd's own relationship to the idea
of stopping work at all. Together they cover the two genuinely distinct hazard categories Findings 39 and 43
(air-handling cross-contamination, plumbing/structural cascade) already flag as real, separate risk classes
for this city — worth keeping both in the candidate pool rather than treating them as redundant.
