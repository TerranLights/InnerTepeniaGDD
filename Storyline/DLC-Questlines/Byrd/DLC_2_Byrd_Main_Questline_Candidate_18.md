# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #18: "More Roads Than Rastras"

**What this is:** a vehicle/fuel-fleet crisis candidate, mechanically distinct from Candidate #3 — that
candidate asks whether the *route* is passable at all; this one assumes the route is fine and asks whether
Byrd actually has enough working vehicles and fuel to use it at the scale freight demand requires. No Cradle
content — ground vehicles and fuel, not chamber manufacturing. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Course of Events chain #2** ("Belgrano's Gift, Byrd's Debt") — Byrd manufactures the Arrastradora/Rastra
  vehicle lineage domestically, the same vehicle class that found the city in the first place.
- **Attribute #44** — a dedicated ground-vehicle maintenance depot for the Arrastradora fleet and general
  highway freight vehicles.
- **Attribute #60** — a fuel depot for surface ground vehicles, distinct from the vehicle depot itself.
- **Finding 21** — the five guild sectors interleave physically and economically; competing priorities are
  the norm, not the exception.
- **Finding 13** (this document's own Candidate #13) — the same allocation-precedent mechanic, applied here
  to fuel and vehicle parts instead of scrap.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Dispatch reports the ground-vehicle fleet is degrading faster than it can be maintained —
fewer working Arrastradoras available for Hwy 1/22 freight runs than the schedule assumes. The player is
asked to help figure out why.

**BUT** — The fuel depot itself (attribute #60) shows the same strain — stockpile lower than expected,
raising the same kind of question Candidate #13 already surfaces for scrap: who's actually been drawing on
this, and why wasn't it flagged sooner.

**THEREFORE** — The player investigates the supply chain directly, and finds the vehicle depot's own
maintenance has been quietly deprioritized — not sabotage, just one guild's competing needs (Finding 21)
consistently winning out over fleet upkeep, cumulatively, until it became a real problem.

**BUT** — Fixing it means pulling resources from wherever they're currently going instead, and every
alternative destination has its own legitimate claim — genuinely competing priorities, not an easy call.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to negotiate a real reprioritization, weighing the freight fleet's own needs
against whatever else in the city currently has a stronger claim on the same limited labor and materials.

**BUT (reversal)** — Even a successful fix doesn't solve the actual root problem: Byrd's domestically-
manufactured vehicle fleet (chain #2's own established manufacturing legacy) was sized for a different era,
and current freight demand — especially if Hwy 1/22 traffic increases, per whichever way Candidate #3
resolves — plausibly exceeds what the fleet was ever built to sustain long-term. A one-time reallocation
patches the immediate shortage without addressing the underlying undersizing.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: commit to genuine, ongoing capacity expansion for vehicle
manufacturing and maintenance, solving the problem properly at real, sustained resource cost pulled from
elsewhere in the city; or accept a one-time patch, restoring current operations while leaving the same
undersizing to resurface — likely worse — the next time freight demand rises.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT widens "fewer vehicles" into "the
whole supply chain is under strain." The second BUT means the fix has a real opportunity cost. The third BUT
means even success is a patch, not a real solution. No Complication is deletable without losing the final
choice's weight.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate and Candidate #3 work well as a two-part arc: #3 asks whether Byrd's connection to the rest
of Tepenia exists at all; #18 asks whether Byrd can actually use it at scale even once that's settled. It
also gives chain #2's own established Arrastradora manufacturing legacy a present-day consequence it hasn't
had anywhere else in Byrd's material — the vehicle that found the city has a maintenance cost nobody had
previously accounted for.
