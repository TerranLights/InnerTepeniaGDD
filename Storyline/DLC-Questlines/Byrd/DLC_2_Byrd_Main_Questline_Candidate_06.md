# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #6: "More Than the Ground Can Hold"

**What this is:** a structural-crisis candidate built from the same ice-sheet-instability material as the
original Candidate #1, but deliberately reworked so the stakes are the city's own general safety and a
specific section's survival — never the Chamber Works or chamber output. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Course of Events chain #11** ("The Weight of All That Digging") — structural strain from centuries of
  excavation is an established, permanent, *actively managed* condition, not a solved, one-time crisis.
- **Finding 26** — the Prospectors' Circle's own ore-processing facility sits at the exact intersection of
  active excavation, fabrication-adjacent heat, and ice-boundary exposure — the single physical location in
  Byrd where three separately-established hazards meet at once.
- **Attribute #7** — condemned-zone protocols: a formal system for sealing off, monitoring, and occasionally
  reclaiming sections as instability risk changes.
- **Attribute #49** — a dedicated maintenance-access network, physically separate from public thoroughfares,
  used to inspect structural monitoring points before problems become visible elsewhere.
- **Finding 21** — the five guild sectors plausibly interleave physically rather than occupying clean,
  separate zones, meaning a localized problem's fix is never fully separable from the other guilds' work.
- **Finding 25** — the old-core vs. newer-expansion structural distinction; the oldest, most established
  sections sit nearest the original founding-era construction.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A structural event at the Circle's Own Ground — worse than the routine, actively-managed strain
chain #11 already establishes as normal — draws the player in through the Foundation Keepers and Prospectors'
Circle, who need outside help assessing whether the section can be saved.

**BUT** — Initial assessment is worse than hoped: this isn't the kind of strain the standing monitoring
practice was built to absorb. The section in question isn't peripheral — per Finding 25's own old-core
geography, it's close to some of Byrd's oldest, most load-bearing construction, meaning attribute #7's
condemned-zone protocol, normally a contained loss, would here mean sealing off something genuinely central
to the city.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to help determine whether the section can actually be shored up, working
directly with the Foundation Keepers on real engineering options rather than accepting the loss outright.

**BUT** — Per Finding 21, no fix here is self-contained. Shoring up one guild's own territory means diverting
labor, materials, and time from whichever other guilds' work physically interleaves with it — the crisis
isn't the Circle's alone to solve, and every other guild has a legitimate claim on the same limited
resources being asked for.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to broker a genuine citywide resource-allocation decision: which guilds give
up what, and how much, to fund the effort to save the section.

**BUT (reversal)** — The maintenance-access network (attribute #49) reveals an uncomfortable fact: the
warning signs were detectable earlier, through the very system built to catch exactly this, but weren't
flagged in time. This isn't purely a geological accident — it's partly an organizational failure, which
means the crisis also becomes a question of trust in the monitoring system itself, and who's actually
accountable for the gap.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — The player's choice: commit to the costly, cross-guild shoring effort —
preserving the section and confronting the systemic failure that let it get this bad, at real, shared
expense — or accept sealing the section per standard condemned-zone protocol, minimizing cost but
permanently losing whatever that specific piece of the old core actually held.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns "routine strain" into "a
genuinely load-bearing section at real risk." The second BUT turns "fix it" into "fixing it costs every
guild something." The third BUT adds accountability to cost — the crisis isn't just expensive, it's partly
the city's own fault. No Complication is deletable without losing the final choice's weight.

## Worth Your Attention

This is the direct, Cradle-free replacement for the structural-crisis material Candidate #1 also drew on
(chain #11, Finding 26) — same underlying hazard, but the stakes are reframed around general city safety and
cross-guild resource allocation rather than protecting chamber output specifically. It pairs naturally with
Candidate #4 (the founding-descendant/later-arrival majority tension), since a citywide resource decision is
exactly the kind of moment that fault line would surface in practice.
