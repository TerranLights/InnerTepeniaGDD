# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #15: "Whatever's Actually Fair"

**What this is:** a candidate built around the Commons Hall as the site of Byrd's own real, unsmoothed
diversity — a different register from Candidate #04's civic-power framing, focused on economic fairness in
daily commerce rather than who controls infrastructure. No Cradle content. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Finding 28** — the Commons Hall (attribute #67) is the single most reliable place in Byrd to actually
  observe the population's real, unsmoothed diversity firsthand, in contrast to more institutionally uniform
  spaces like the guildhalls or the civic archive.
- **Finding 20** — Coober Pedy's own real precedent: Byrd's demographic tension resolves as coexistence
  without full integration, distinct social threads sharing space without necessarily converging.
- **Attribute #68** — communal dining tied to guild shift schedules rather than a single citywide mealtime.
- **Finding 30** — the Dispatch guild's coordination office as the room where scheduling realities actually
  get set.
- **Finding 17** — Byrd's production rhythm has never distinguished crisis from normal operation, meaning
  guild schedules carry real operational weight, not arbitrary convenience.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A dispute breaks out at the Commons Hall — one population thread feels consistently shortchanged
in trade access and timing compared to others. The player is asked to mediate what looks, at first, like a
straightforward fairness complaint.

**BUT** — It isn't simple prejudice. Investigating traces the disparity to something structural: certain
guilds' own shift schedules (attribute #68) happen to line up with the Commons Hall's own busiest hours,
meaning whichever population cluster is more heavily represented in those guilds gets better access almost
by accident of scheduling, not deliberate unfairness from anyone involved.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to dig into the actual scheduling mechanics — working with the Dispatch
Floor (Finding 30) — to find exactly where the structural bias is actually coming from.

**BUT** — Fixing the schedule isn't free. Per Finding 17, Byrd's production rhythm has never treated guild
shift timing as arbitrary — rearranging it to fix Commons Hall access risks real operational cost to
whichever guild's own work depends on the current pattern.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to negotiate an actual compromise between fairness and operational
continuity, not simply demand the schedule change outright.

**BUT (reversal)** — Finding 20's own coexistence model complicates the obvious "equal treatment" fix.
Some population threads have their own genuinely valued rhythms and practices tied to their current
schedule, not just structural bad luck — full equalization risks reading as forcing one uniform system onto
populations who've maintained real, meaningful distinctiveness, the same tension Finding 20 already flags
as worth protecting rather than smoothing away.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: push full schedule equalization, resolving the economic
unfairness directly at the cost of imposing uniformity on populations who've maintained genuine, valued
distinctiveness; or negotiate targeted, partial fixes that preserve those distinct practices, at the cost of
leaving the underlying disparity only partially addressed.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns "unfairness" into
"structural, not deliberate." The second BUT means the fix has real operational cost. The third BUT means
even the "fair" fix has a real cultural cost. No Complication is deletable without losing the final choice.

interacting, exactly the register Finding 28 already flags the Commons Hall as best suited to dramatize.
Pairs naturally with Candidate #10 as two different angles on the same underlying coexistence tension.
