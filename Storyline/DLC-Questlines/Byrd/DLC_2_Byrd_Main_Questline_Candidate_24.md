# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #24: "What the Glass Can't Hold"

**What this is:** the flagship Lake-crisis candidate, built directly around the boundary wall's own established catastrophic-failure risk. No Cradle content. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Attribute #76** — dedicated structural monitoring specific to the Long Window's boundary wall, distinct from and more concentrated than Byrd's general structural monitoring.
- **Attribute #80** — a distinct, more severe hazard classification for boundary-wall failure than any other single risk currently on file: sudden, massive, immediate flooding.
- **Attribute #7** — condemned-zone protocols, established for ordinary structural risk, deliberately shown here not to translate cleanly.
- **Finding 53** — the Lake is the one deliberate exception to Byrd's own "reseal everything unusual" pattern, a genuine civic-values statement, not just infrastructure.
- **Finding 57** — the wall's failure risk raises the stakes ceiling on Byrd's existing structural-crisis material considerably.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Dedicated wall monitoring (attribute #76) detects genuine, developing structural stress on the Long Window. The player is brought in by the Foundation Keepers to help assess it.

**BUT** — This isn't a routine structural event. Attribute #80 already classifies boundary-wall failure as Byrd's single most catastrophic hazard type, and the standard condemned-zone response (attribute #7 — seal it off, wait, reassess) doesn't translate here: you can't simply seal off a residential and civic landmark the way you'd seal a tunnel section, and the city has never faced this specific kind of decision before.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to work with the Foundation Keepers and the ecosystem-maintenance staff (attribute #77) together to determine whether the wall can be reinforced without draining or destroying the lake itself.

**BUT** — Real reinforcement requires resources on the same scale as any other major structural crisis (Finding 21's interleaved economy), and invasive repair work risks damaging the very ecosystem the wall protects — this isn't just a cost dispute between guilds, it's a genuine conflict between structural safety and ecosystem preservation as competing values, not competing budgets.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to broker between these two goods directly, not simply negotiate who pays.

**BUT (reversal)** — Finding 53 sharpens the stakes further. If repair genuinely requires draining or reducing the lake — sacrificing part of the one thing Byrd chose to keep rather than reseal — doing so would be the first time necessity has actually overridden that original, deliberate exception. It raises a real question the city has never had to answer: was that choice permanent, or was it always going to be provisional the moment real cost arrived?

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: prioritize full structural safety, protecting lives at real, permanent cost to the lake's own scope or vibrancy; or prioritize preserving the lake as intact as possible, honoring the original choice at the cost of leaving genuine, ongoing danger unresolved.

---

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT means standard protocol doesn't apply. The second BUT turns a resource question into a values conflict. The third BUT tests whether Byrd's own founding choice about the Lake actually holds under real pressure. No Complication is deletable without losing the final choice's weight.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This is the most direct, highest-stakes possible use of the Lake material — the wall failing is the worst thing that can happen to Byrd's own flagship landmark, and Finding 53's own "deliberate exception" framing gives the resolution real thematic teeth: this isn't just "can we fix it," it's "does the choice that made Byrd different from every other resealed discovery in its own history actually survive contact with genuine cost."
