# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #25: "The Light Going Out"

**What this is:** a biological, not structural, Lake crisis — the bioluminescent ecosystem itself in decline, distinct from Candidate #24's wall-integrity threat. No Cradle content. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Attribute #75** — the bioluminescent lake ecosystem, transplanted and cultivated from Lake Vostok's own ancient biology, not engineered from scratch.
- **Attribute #77** — a small, specialized ecosystem-maintenance staff, a genuine complementary role to the Growing Sections' own growth-keeper specialists.
- **Finding 55** — the transplant as Vostok's own rare exception to "exported almost nothing else," framed as scientific collaboration.
- **Vostok's own established character** (`Vostok_Mega_Init.md`) — Thinking/Withdrawn/Positive Outlook, self-contained and inward-facing, work experienced as something lived rather than duty.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The bioluminescent ecosystem behind the Long Window begins dimming — a real, developing die-off, not cosmetic fading. The player is brought in by the small ecosystem-maintenance staff (attribute #77), who are the first to notice.

**BUT** — That staff, however dedicated, doesn't hold the deep genetics expertise to actually diagnose the cause. That knowledge genuinely belongs to Vostok's own research program, not to Byrd — the transplant was always downstream of expertise Byrd itself doesn't have.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to reach out to Vostok directly, seeking real expert help rather than attempting a Byrd-only fix.

**BUT** — Vostok's own established civic character is self-contained and inward-facing, organized entirely around its own research rather than outward engagement with the wider Federation. This isn't a transactional request Vostok is obligated to fill — genuinely earning their attention and priority requires making a real case, not simply asking.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to persuade Vostok's own research community that this specific problem is worth diverting their attention to.

**BUT (reversal)** — Even with full Vostok cooperation, there's no guarantee of a complete fix. The ecosystem represents 15–25 million years of accumulated evolutionary complexity transplanted into an entirely new, artificial environment — some die-off or permanent change may simply be unavoidable, meaning the player's efforts might only partially succeed, leaving the Lake dimmer and different going forward even in the best realistic outcome.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: push hard for a full Vostok intervention, giving the best possible chance at saving the ecosystem at real diplomatic cost and risk of overextending a still-fragile, newly-established relationship; or accept a more modest, Byrd-only stopgap, preserving the relationship's low-key character at the likely cost of a permanently diminished Lake.

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT means Byrd can't fix this alone. The second BUT means getting help isn't automatic. The third BUT means even success may be only partial. No Complication is deletable without losing the final choice's weight.

## Worth Your Attention

This is the candidate that most directly tests Finding 55's own "collaboration, not trade" framing under real pressure — the first time Byrd actually needs something back from Vostok rather than simply having received a gift, which is a meaningfully different dynamic than the one-way transplant established so far. Pairs naturally with Candidate #26 as two different versions of the same underlying question: what does Byrd actually owe, or get to ask of, the city that made the Lake possible.
