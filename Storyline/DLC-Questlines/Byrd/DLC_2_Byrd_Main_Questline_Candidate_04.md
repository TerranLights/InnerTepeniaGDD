# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #4: "Whoever Holds the Shaft"

**What this is:** a fourth prospective But/Therefore chain, built from `Byrd_Physical_Infrastructure_
Attributes.md`'s own extrapolation layer rather than directly from one of `DLC_Overview.md`'s three named
anchors — an internal political conflict emergent from this session's Findings, offered as a genuine
alternative or companion thread to the three developer-flagged options. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Finding 35** — access control at the Elevator Halls (attribute #47) gives whoever physically operates
  them a quiet, structurally real form of civic power, independent of Byrd's formal guild politics.
- **Finding 7** — specialized structural/wayfinding institutional knowledge plausibly concentrates
  disproportionately among founding-descendant families, not the far larger population who arrived later,
  as part of the labor migration waves drawn from across the Palmer, Halley, Janbogo, Mirny, and Mawson
  subnets.
- **Finding 44** — formal civic administration (attribute #26) and practical elevator-chokepoint control
  are two only-partially-overlapping loci of power, never formally reconciled or contested.
- **Finding 20** — Coober Pedy's own real precedent argues Byrd's founder/majority demographic tension
  resolves as genuine coexistence, not one side absorbing or replacing the other.
- **`Byrd_Community_Infrastructure.md`** — the Elevator Halls and the founding-descendant/later-arrival
  majority fault line, both flagged as the strongest available anchors for future content.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — An urgent situation demands the Elevator Halls run outside normal procedure — an emergency
shipment, an evacuation, something time-critical enough that the player goes straight to Byrd's formal civic
complex (attribute #26) expecting an authorization to be enough to get the shafts moving.

**BUT** — It isn't. Finding 44 turns out to be true in practice: the civic complex doesn't actually control
day-to-day elevator operations. That's practically held by whoever's staffed the shafts for generations —
plausibly concentrated, per Finding 7, among founding-descendant families whose claim runs through the
original expedition, not through the city's own formal government.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to go around the authorization they were told would work, and deal directly
with the people who actually run the elevators.

**BUT** — Getting there surfaces the real fault line underneath the delay. The operators, as
founding-descendants, carry their own institutional memory, priorities, and quiet skepticism of a formal
civic body that speaks for a population three-quarters of whom, per Finding 7, "chose nothing about Byrd
specifically at all." This isn't a bureaucratic holdup — it's Byrd's own unresolved founder/majority tension,
now standing directly between the player and the one thing they actually need.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to choose an approach: work within the informal system (earn the operators'
trust, understand their own logic and terms) or force the issue (bring the civic complex's formal authority
to bear on the elevators directly, for what's plausibly the first real test of it).

**BUT (reversal)** — Forcing it isn't clean. Finding 20's own Coober Pedy precedent argues Byrd's real
stability has always rested on coexistence without full integration, not one population formally asserting
authority over the other. Bringing accountable civic control to the city's single chokepoint could read,
correctly, as exactly that assertion — risking the quiet balance that's let two distinct populations share
Byrd without ever fully converging.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — The player's choice: align Byrd's actual practical power with its formal
government, gaining real accountability over the city's one true lever at the cost of disturbing a coexistence
that's held for generations without being forced; or work within the founding-descendants' own informal
system, resolving the immediate crisis while leaving Byrd's most consequential piece of infrastructure
permanently outside any accountability nobody currently living chose to grant it.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns "get authorization" into
"authorization doesn't actually work here." The second BUT turns "find the real operators" into "the real
operators are one side of an unresolved civic fault line." The third BUT means fixing the accountability gap
has a genuine social cost, not just a procedural one. Each Complication is load-bearing for the final choice.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate is the strongest available option for internal, non-external-crisis conflict — it doesn't
need an outside threat at all, only two already-established, still-live tensions (Finding 7's demographic
split, Finding 44's power gap) that this session's own deep-dive surfaced and that no prior Byrd document had
connected before. Worth pairing with Candidate #1 or #3 as a secondary or interleaved thread rather than
treated as fully standalone, since it's more character/faction-driven than crisis-driven.
