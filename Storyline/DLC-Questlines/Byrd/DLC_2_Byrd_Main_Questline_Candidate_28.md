# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #28: "Enough to See By"

**What this is:** a character-driven, medical-ethics candidate connecting the Lake back to Byrd's own established light-deprivation infrastructure — the only candidate in the pool that treats the Long Window as a medical question rather than a structural, biological, or civic one. No Cradle content. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Attributes #4, #15** — Vitamin D/UV-therapy provisioning and dedicated mental-health infrastructure, both established specifically for isolation and light-deprivation effects, built well before the Lake existed.
- **Finding 56** — the Long Window as Byrd's real emotional landmark, the only place in the city with light, color, and movement that isn't engineered.
- **Finding 5 / Finding 36** — Byrd's own established, uncredited medical and epidemiological competence, forced by total isolation.
- **`Byrd_Community_Infrastructure.md`'s own Social Cohesion Mechanisms** — residents already visit the Long Window informally for morale, a quiet, valued relief rather than a prescribed treatment.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A specific resident is struggling severely with isolation and light-deprivation effects despite Byrd's existing, established infrastructure (attributes #4, #15) — the standard treatment genuinely isn't working for them. Time near the Long Window is proposed, informally, as something that might help more directly.

**BUT** — The Long Window was never built as formal medical infrastructure. Attribute #15's mental-health provisioning and the Lake are two separate, never-connected systems — using it as actual prescribed treatment would be genuinely new, untested territory for Byrd's medical establishment.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to work with both the resident and the medical establishment to figure out whether, and how, to formally integrate the Lake into Byrd's own established care practice.

**BUT** — The Long Window is also Byrd's single busiest informal civic and emotional gathering space, per its own established Social Cohesion Mechanism — carving out dedicated therapeutic access or quiet-hours for medical use could conflict directly with the shared, communal role it already plays for everyone else.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to negotiate between one resident's genuine, serious medical need and the wider population's own established, informal relationship with the same shared space.

**BUT (reversal)** — This raises an uncomfortable question that reaches beyond one resident. If the Lake genuinely helps in a way engineered UV therapy (attribute #4) doesn't, what does that imply about whether Byrd's existing, purely engineered medical infrastructure was ever really enough on its own? Success here doesn't just help one person — it quietly indicts the adequacy of a system Byrd's medical establishment has real professional pride in, the same "uncredited competence" theme (Finding 5) now facing its own genuine limitation from the opposite angle.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: formalize dedicated Lake-based therapeutic access, helping this resident and likely others like them, at the cost of disrupting a widely shared communal space and implicitly questioning established medical practice; or find a less disruptive accommodation, preserving both the Long Window's communal character and the medical establishment's own institutional confidence, at the cost of possibly leaving this resident's specific need only partially addressed.

## Worth Your Attention

This is the only candidate that connects the Lake back to the specific, already-established medical/mental-health infrastructure that predates it (attributes #4, #15) rather than treating the Lake as a self-contained new system — closing a loop between Byrd's oldest deprivation and its newest answer to it, and giving Finding 5's "uncredited competence" theme a rare moment of genuine, honest limitation rather than quiet excellence.
