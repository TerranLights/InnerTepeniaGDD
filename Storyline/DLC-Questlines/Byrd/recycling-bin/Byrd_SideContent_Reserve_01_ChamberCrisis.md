# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #1: "The Weight the Ground Can't Take"

**What this is:** a first prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 2's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Byrd, extended with one new input category unique to this
subnet: `Byrd_Physical_Infrastructure_Attributes.md`, this city's own 72-attribute/52-Finding physical-
infrastructure deep-dive, treated the same way the method already treats Cross-Reference Synthesis Findings
(pulling the 4th-order-effect line from each relevant Finding). **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Byrd is a single-city subnet carrying an entire DLC alone** — unlike every other subnet's 4–8 cities, there
is no cast of neighboring cities to spread stakes and factions across. This candidate leans directly on the
five-guild internal faction structure (`Byrd_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section I) to supply that missing
internal complexity, and builds its central conflict out of `DLC_Overview.md`'s own developer-leaned anchor:
**the Chamber Crisis**, chosen specifically because it gives Maggie Aarden's established industrial/welding
skills a direct mechanical stake.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Byrd's national importance rests on one thing above all: the Chamber Works, one of only two
active fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturers left in Tepenia. Maggie Aarden, settled permanently in
Byrd, is drawn into a developing problem there through her own established industrial/welding expertise —
something in the Chamber Works' own output has started drifting outside normal tolerances.

**BUT** — The Reserve's own dedicated monitoring (attribute #70) reveals the cause isn't the fabrication
process itself: the underground energy buffer (attribute #71) that's supposed to smooth out the geothermal
source's own variability is failing to keep pace, and continuing at current output risks a failure serious
enough to threaten the Chamber Works' survival outright.

**THEREFORE** — The player and Maggie have to trace the actual cause upstream, past the Reserve itself, to
whatever's actually straining it.

**BUT** — The trail leads directly to the Prospectors' Circle's own ore-processing facility (Finding 26) —
recent extraction work, needed to keep Byrd's own interleaved five-guild economy running (Finding 21), is
plausibly what's aggravating chain #11's already-established structural strain at the one place in the city
where excavation, fabrication heat, and ice-boundary instability all meet at once. Curtailing it protects the
Chamber Works; continuing it risks losing the Chamber Works entirely.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to broker, or force, a decision between the Fabricators' Guild and the
Prospectors' Circle — two of Byrd's own five guilds, each with a legitimate claim, neither willing to simply
absorb the other's cost.

**BUT (reversal)** — Finding 17 closes off the easy answer: Byrd's chamber-manufacturing output has never
actually spiked or had slack built into it — it's been running at the same intensity, unbroken, since before
the war. There's no reserve capacity on either side to throttle back into. Whichever guild yields, something
Byrd has depended on for generations genuinely breaks, not just slows.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per this project's own No Good Endings law, the player's choice determines
which of Byrd's two established, load-bearing competencies survives fully intact: the Chamber Works' own
national role, keeping Byrd one of only two places in Tepenia that can still build a robot from nothing; or
the Prospectors' Circle's own economic sustainability, on which the rest of the city's interleaved economy
depends. Maggie's own stake — her craft, her adopted home, the guild she's most directly tied to — is
inseparable from whichever side the player takes.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE, no repeated conjunction in a row. The first BUT
turns "fix a fabrication defect" into "the power buffer itself is failing." The second BUT turns "find the
cause" into "the cause is another guild's own necessary work, not a mechanical fault." The third BUT closes
off any version where both guilds get to keep what they have. Deleting any one Complication removes the
specific two-guild conflict the Resolution depends on, so none of them is a disguised And Then.
