# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #17: "Nobody Planned This Far Out"

**What this is:** a search-and-rescue candidate built around Byrd's own unplanned, organic tunnel growth —
a genuinely different register from the disaster-response (#14), structural-crisis (#06), and growth-strain
(#10) candidates already written, though it touches related material. No Cradle content. **Not canon, not
chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Finding 18** — Derinkuyu's real-world genuinely planned underground civic logic is the inverse of Byrd's
  own organic, unplanned tunneling — Byrd's growth was never given the top-down coherence a planned city
  like Derinkuyu had.
- **Attribute #14** — the buried tunnel network's own wayfinding/signage system, already flagged as
  necessary specifically because centuries of organic, unplanned tunneling would otherwise be genuinely
  disorienting.
- **Attribute #49** — a dedicated maintenance-access network, physically separate from public thoroughfares,
  requiring specialist (Foundation Keepers') knowledge to navigate effectively.
- **Finding 25 / attribute #72** — a visible old-core vs. newer-expansion structural distinction; newer
  sections are, by definition, less historically tested than the old core.
- **Course of Events chain #11** — structural strain is an actively-managed but real, ongoing condition,
  more of a live concern in newer, less-tested construction.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A group of residents or workers goes missing in a section of newer expansion tunnel, after a
recent excavation disrupted the informal wayfinding cues people had been relying on. The player is asked to
help locate them.

**BUT** — Standard public wayfinding (attribute #14) doesn't reliably cover this newer, still-forming
section — consistent with Finding 18's own point that Byrd was never built with Derinkuyu's kind of
top-down planned coherence. The only real navigational asset is the maintenance-access network (attribute
#49), and it's a specialist system, not something the player can simply use without Foundation Keeper
expertise.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to work directly with the Foundation Keepers, relying on their own
specialist knowledge of the access network to actually locate the missing group.

**BUT** — Time pressure collides with real risk: newer expansion sections are, by Finding 25's own logic,
less structurally tested than the old core, and chain #11 already establishes structural strain as a live,
ongoing concern, not a solved problem. Moving fast to find the missing group means moving fast through
ground the rescue team can't fully vouch for.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to balance speed against caution — pushing hard enough to find people in
time, without triggering or walking the rescue team into the very kind of structural event chain #11
already establishes as a real, standing risk.

**BUT (reversal)** — Once resolved, the incident raises an uncomfortable question about Byrd's whole
approach to growth. Finding 18 already flags that Byrd was never planned the way Derinkuyu was — this
near-miss is the clearest possible argument for finally investing in formal wayfinding and structural
pre-testing for new expansion going forward, but that investment means either real resource cost or a
slower pace of expansion, directly cutting against the population-growth pressure Candidate #10 already
establishes as a live, separate concern.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: invest in formal wayfinding and structural pre-testing for
all future expansion, reducing the risk of a repeat incident at real, ongoing resource cost; or accept the
organic-growth status quo that's defined Byrd since its founding, preserving pace and resources at the cost
of leaving the same risk — possibly worse, next time — unaddressed.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT means standard tools can't solve
this. The second BUT means the rescue itself carries real risk. The third BUT means the fix for next time has
a genuine, ongoing cost. No Complication is deletable without losing the final choice's weight.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate gives Finding 18's own Derinkuyu-contrast a genuinely dramatized, player-facing consequence
rather than leaving it as background civic-identity flavor — Byrd being the *un*-planned inverse of its
closest real-world civic parallel stops being an abstract fact and becomes the actual reason people get lost
and need rescuing. Pairs naturally with Candidate #10 as a direct, felt consequence of unmanaged growth.
