# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #8: "The Relay Goes Quiet"

**What this is:** a candidate built around Byrd's own Arcanet connection — its physical-vs-digital isolation
mismatch — carrying no Cradle/chamber content. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Finding 24** — Byrd's Arcanet access (attribute #62, distinct from the purely internal wired backbone,
  attribute #46) means the city is informationally connected to the rest of Tepenia even though physically
  remote — "we know exactly what's out there, it just never comes here in person."
- **`Byrd_Community_Infrastructure.md`** — The Relay, listed as its own Addition, and the specific social
  cohesion mechanism built on it ("knowing... exactly what's happening in the rest of Tepenia while almost
  never actually seeing a visitor in person").
- **Finding 15** — the Norilsk precedent: Byrd's whole external relevance is pinned on whichever single
  connection actually survives.
- **Course of Events chain #7** — the aviation fleet's own permanent, unrepairable grounding, already
  Byrd's one confirmed lost connection.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The Relay begins failing — a technical fault, or the leading edge of some larger, national-scale
Arcanet disruption reaching Byrd specifically. The player is pulled in because Byrd's population, per Finding
24, has quietly come to depend on this connection for something the highways and the dead aviation route
never provided: knowing, in real time, what's actually happening in the rest of the country.

**BUT** — Fixing it isn't simple. The Relay's own specialized, national-network-dependent technology isn't
something Byrd's own in-house expertise — built around fabrication, extraction, and structural engineering —
can fully service alone, unlike the purely internal wired backbone (attribute #46) the city already
maintains itself.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to find an alternate path to a fix, whether through the fragile highway
network (echoing Candidate #3) or by tracking down whatever specific expertise or parts the repair actually
requires.

**BUT** — The stakes sharpen considerably once framed against Finding 15's own Norilsk logic. Byrd's physical
isolation has always been real, but softened by exactly the thing now failing — residents knowing, via the
Relay, precisely what they're missing rather than facing blank unfamiliarity. Losing it too would push Byrd
into a totally unprecedented isolation, worse than anything the city has faced since its own founding.

**THEREFORE** — This stops reading as routine infrastructure repair and becomes a race to prevent Byrd from
crossing into a kind of isolation it has never actually experienced.

**BUT (reversal)** — The clearest path to a fix requires accepting real help from outside Byrd — another
city, a faction, a national body — and that help won't be free. Taking it restores the Relay, but plausibly
opens Byrd to outside leverage or influence for the first time in its established, insular history.
Refusing it and solving the problem entirely in-house protects that independence, but the resulting fix is
likely partial, more fragile than what existed before.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: accept outside help, restoring full connectivity at the
cost of a dependency Byrd has never had to carry; or preserve full self-sufficiency, at the cost of a
weaker, less reliable Relay than the one the city is used to — and a first, real taste of just how much
worse total isolation would actually feel.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns "fix the Relay" into "Byrd
can't fully fix this alone." The second BUT raises what's actually at stake — total, unprecedented isolation,
not mere inconvenience. The third BUT means the fix itself has a cost regardless of path. No Complication is
deletable without losing the final choice's weight.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate is the only one in the current set built entirely around Finding 24 — a tension nothing in
Byrd's material had articulated before this session's extrapolation work. It gives the Isolation Crisis
anchor (`DLC_Overview.md`) a genuinely new axis beyond the highway/aviation question Candidate #3 already
covers: informational isolation, not just physical, worth treating as either a standalone candidate or a
second act layered onto Candidate #3's own resolution.
