# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #12: "What Esperanza Doesn't Send"

**What this is:** a candidate built entirely from Byrd's own already-established food-supply relationship
with Esperanza (Palmer subnet, DLC 3) — no new cross-DLC lore invented, no Cradle content. **Not canon, not
chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Course of Events chain #6** ("What Esperanza Sends South") — Byrd's food arrangement with Esperanza was
  always variable, tracking Esperanza's own seasonal capacity rather than a fixed quota, which is why Byrd
  built genuine domestic food-storage and buffer-stock capacity in the first place.
- **Attribute #13** — supplemental local food storage/buffer-stock discipline, confirmed canon, established
  as the root of Byrd's own general "reflexively applies to other critical goods" self-sufficiency instinct.
- **Attribute #24** — in-house food-growth infrastructure, sized to supplement, not replace, the Esperanza
  relationship.
- **Finding 45** — the food-infrastructure attributes this session produced directly fill a gap
  `Byrd_Catalog.md`'s own per-nation Food findings left open, giving Byrd's food culture real, usable
  texture for the first time.
- **Cross-DLC Bypass Design Law** (`But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §6) — a natural seam for this DLC
  to touch another subnet's own established material without requiring new invention there.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Hwy 1 traffic from Esperanza slows, then drops well below its usual seasonal variation — nothing
catastrophic reported, just persistently less than Byrd's own buffer-stock planning assumes. The player is
asked to find out why, and how long Byrd's own domestic stores (attribute #13) can actually absorb it.

**BUT** — The buffer-stock discipline was built for Esperanza's *known* seasonal swings (chain #6), not for
a shortfall outside that established pattern. Byrd's own domestic growing capacity (attribute #24) was
explicitly sized to supplement the relationship, not replace it — meaning the city's real cushion is
thinner than its own civic self-image ("we handle this ourselves") assumes.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually travel Hwy 1 to Esperanza to find out what's really happening,
rather than simply managing the shortfall from Byrd's own side.

**BUT** — Whatever's happening at Esperanza isn't Byrd's to fix — it's Esperanza's own crisis, on Esperanza's
own terms, and Byrd has no standing to demand a resolution that serves its own needs first. The player has to
navigate someone else's problem as an outsider whose own city's survival happens to depend on the outcome.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to find a way to actually help Esperanza, on Esperanza's own terms, in order
to get Byrd's supply line moving again — aid, not extraction.

**BUT (reversal)** — Even a successful resolution doesn't erase what the crisis revealed: Byrd's own
buffer-stock discipline, its proudest piece of self-sufficiency, was never actually built to survive a real
disruption to the one external relationship it still depends on. The city can restock, but the underlying
fragility — a "self-sufficient" city whose margin depends on someone else's good season — doesn't go away
just because this particular shortfall resolved.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings, the player's approach to helping Esperanza — and to how
honestly Byrd's own leadership faces what the crisis revealed about its own limits — shapes whether Byrd
comes out of this with a stronger, more honest relationship to its one real outside dependency, or simply
patches the shortfall and quietly goes back to believing its own buffer-stock discipline was enough all
along.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT reveals Byrd's own cushion is
thinner than assumed. The second BUT means the fix isn't in Byrd's control. The third BUT means even success
doesn't resolve the deeper fragility. No Complication is deletable without losing the final choice's weight.

## Worth Your Attention

This is the only candidate in the current set that sends the player physically into another DLC's own
established territory using only material Byrd's own lore already establishes (chain #6's known
variability) — no new Esperanza-specific crisis was invented to make this work. It's also the clearest
available vehicle for directly challenging Byrd's own "self-sufficiency" self-image, using Finding 45's own
food-culture material as its emotional core.
