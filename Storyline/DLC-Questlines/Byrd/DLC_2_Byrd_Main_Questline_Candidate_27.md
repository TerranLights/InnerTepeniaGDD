# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #27: "Before It Was Kept Open"

**What this is:** a myth-vs-history candidate, directly claiming the open question Finding 53 itself flagged and left unresolved — how and why the Lake became the one thing Byrd chose to keep rather than reseal. Distinct from Candidate #02's pre-exile mystery: this is recent, Tepenian-era civic history, not ancient Upper Earth material. No Cradle content. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Finding 53** — the Lake as the one deliberate exception to chain #9's own "reseal everything unusual" pattern; explicitly flags the discovery/decision story as worth a placeholder answer, not yet nailed down.
- **Finding 27** — the Reading Rooms and civic archive give a clean, honest "it isn't here" when something genuinely isn't documented, rather than deflecting — the same mechanism, applied here to a much more recent decision than the pre-exile material it originally covered.
- **Course of Events chain #9** — the established mechanism (brief documentation, take what's useful, reseal, move on) that this specific case is the one exception to.
- **Finding 20** — Coober Pedy's own real precedent: the honest reading of Byrd's civic character allows contested, imperfect history rather than requiring smoothed-over unity.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The player starts pulling at a question the Reading Rooms can't fully answer: exactly how and why the Lake became the one discovery in Byrd's history that got a flex-tolerant glass wall built around it instead of being resealed like everything else.

**BUT** — Unlike the pre-exile mystery (Candidate #02), this should have *more* institutional memory, not less — building the wall was a huge, deliberate, expensive engineering project, not a quiet reseal. Yet the actual reasoning and debate behind the original decision turns out to be surprisingly thin in the official record too — a different, more uncomfortable kind of gap.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to piece the real story together from scattered, informal sources — oral history, old engineering logs, whoever remains from the generation that actually made the call.

**BUT** — What emerges complicates the city's own current, comfortable mythology. The decision wasn't the clean, unanimous act of civic wisdom Finding 53's own "deliberate exception" framing implies — there was real, contested debate at the time, engineers and leaders who opposed the resource cost, and the "obviously right call" reading is partly a story the city told itself afterward, once things worked out.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to decide whether to surface this messier, more honest history, or let the cleaner, more inspiring version everyone currently believes stand undisturbed.

**BUT (reversal)** — The messier truth doesn't diminish what the Lake means today, but it does complicate Finding 53's own thematic reading — was this really Byrd choosing wonder over necessity as a matter of civic character, or a contested, nearly-not-made decision the city got lucky on, later smoothed into a cleaner story? Both readings are equally available now, and which one the player surfaces shapes how residents understand their own most treasured landmark going forward.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: reveal the contested, more honest history, respecting the truth at the cost of deflating something the city takes real, unreflective pride and comfort in; or let the cleaner civic myth stand, preserving morale and meaning at the cost of residents believing something not quite fully accurate about their own defining landmark.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT means this gap is more surprising than the pre-exile one, not less. The second BUT complicates the city's own comfortable self-image. The third BUT means neither honesty nor comfort is free. No Complication is deletable without losing the final choice's weight.

A strong, distinctive addition to the pool's overall register.
