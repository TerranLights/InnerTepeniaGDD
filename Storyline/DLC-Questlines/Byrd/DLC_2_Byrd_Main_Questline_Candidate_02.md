# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #2: "What the Old Maps Don't Say"

**What this is:** a second prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 2's main questline, built around
`DLC_Overview.md`'s second named anchor — **What's Actually Down There** — Byrd's unexplored full
underground scale and the unexplained origin of the old maps that led to its own discovery. **Not canon, not
chosen.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic.

---

## Inputs actually used

- **`Specs/Byrd.md` Founding section** — the founding expedition located Byrd Station using "old maps and
  transport manifests," with no confirmation the site was still accessible — whose maps, and how they came
  to exist, is never explained anywhere in Byrd's established material.
- **Course of Events chain #9** ("Deeper Than Anyone Remembers") — deep excavation occasionally surfaces
  genuine pre-exile Upper Earth remnants; crews document informally, take what's useful, reseal, and move
  on, with no centralized preservation effort ever built.
- **Finding 3** — this file's own correction: the deep layers carry no coherent marking or archival system
  at all, only scattered personal crew notes, which is precisely what keeps the deep layers reading as civic
  legend rather than confirmed fact across generations.
- **Finding 27** — Byrd's own public library and formal records archive (attributes #65–66) explicitly do
  not hold pre-exile information; a character searching there gets a clean, textured "it isn't written down
  here," not silence.
- **Finding 18** — Derinkuyu's own genuinely planned underground civic logic is the real-world inverse of
  Byrd's own organic, unplanned tunneling — Byrd was never built with the kind of top-down documentation a
  planned city might have left behind.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The player, working through Byrd's Reading Rooms, starts pulling at a question `Byrd_Mega_Init.md`
itself flags as still open: who actually made the maps that let the founding expedition find Byrd Station in
the first place, decades before Tepenia's exile community had any reason to know it existed?

**BUT** — The library and archive (Finding 27) turn up nothing — helpful, complete records of everything
else in Byrd's civic history, and a clean, explicit gap on this one specific question. Whatever answer
exists doesn't live in any institution the city actually built.

**THEREFORE** — The only real lead is chain #9's own established mechanism: descending into the deep,
uncentralized layers themselves, where pre-exile Upper Earth remnants occasionally surface, and tracking down
whichever old crews or families kept their own personal notes on what they found.

**BUT** — Chain #9 is explicit that these encounters are rare, and the standing practice — document briefly,
take what's useful, reseal, move on — is exactly what's kept this question open for generations. The
player's own investigation risks simply repeating that same pattern: finding *something*, then losing it to
the same undocumented, personal-notes-only habit that swallowed every previous discovery.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to decide whether to break the pattern outright — formally document and
preserve whatever gets found this time, building the very centralized archive chain #9 says Byrd never
built — or follow the same path every crew before them has taken.

**BUT (reversal)** — Neither choice is free. Formalizing the discovery changes something real about Byrd's
own civic identity — the deep layers stop being quiet legend (Finding 3's own established texture) and
become settled history, for better or worse. Resealing it again, the way it's always been done, means
whatever the maps' own origin actually implies about Upper Earth's pre-exile presence at this specific site
stays buried — possibly including something Byrd's own residents would genuinely want to know about their
own city's origin.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — The player's choice decides whether Byrd's oldest open question becomes
recorded, shared civic history, or remains exactly what it's always been: a story that surfaces rarely,
never fully centralized, carried forward in personal notes rather than public record.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns "ask the archive" into "the
archive has a specific, real gap." The second BUT turns "go looking" into "looking this way has already
failed for generations." The third BUT means preserving the answer costs something too, not just finding it.
No Complication can be deleted without collapsing the final choice.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate turns an explicitly-flagged open question (`Byrd_Mega_Init.md`'s own unresolved map origin)
into the DLC's second anchor without inventing new lore to answer it — the resolution isn't "here's what the
maps mean," it's "does Byrd choose to finally find out and remember it, or does the city stay what it's
always structurally been: a place whose deepest history is never fully known by anyone currently living."
That framing is itself drawn directly from Finding 3's own established mechanism, not added on top of it.
