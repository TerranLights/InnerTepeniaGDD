# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #3: "The Road No One Will Confirm"

**What this is:** a third prospective But/Therefore chain, built around `DLC_Overview.md`'s third named
anchor — **The Isolation Crisis** — Byrd's unresolved aviation-route/refuel-stop problem and its role as a
two-way freight hub, tested by a supply crisis. **Not canon, not chosen.**

**A note on the standing Highway/Isolation Contradiction** (`TODO.md`, "flag, don't fix" — developer's
explicit direction): this candidate does not resolve that contradiction. It treats the uncertainty itself as
in-world — official Dispatch records and lived resident experience genuinely disagree about whether Hwy 1/22
still carry confirmed traffic — and builds the quest around the player's own journey being the thing that
actually tests it, rather than the lore files quietly picking a side. This candidate should hold up
regardless of which way, if any, that contradiction is eventually resolved outside this document.

## Inputs actually used

- **Course of Events chains #5–7** — Hwy 1 (Esperanza) and Hwy 22 (east) as established freight corridors,
  and the aviation fleet's own permanent, unrepairable grounding — Byrd's only confirmed *lost* connection.
- **Finding 11** — Byrd's own founding-era decision to maintain both highways indefinitely, now a fixed,
  non-negotiable line item baked into the city's economy regardless of internal hardship.
- **Finding 15** — the Norilsk precedent: Byrd's whole external relevance is pinned on whichever single
  connection actually survives, not on total isolation.
- **`Byrd_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` Finding 4** — Hwy 22 runs directly through Amundsen Station's ruins,
  the one piece of infrastructure the whole project agrees was destroyed.
- **Finding 22** — the Arrastradora vehicle lineage Byrd itself manufactures ties directly to the still-open
  question of whether people, not just freight, ever traveled the highways personally.
- **Finding 34** — the cold-weather gear staging area's own sizing and stocking is the single most concrete
  physical evidence for or against that question.
- **Finding 30** — the Dispatch guild's coordination office as the one room where highway-condition
  information (or its absence) actually converges.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The player needs to move something genuinely critical in or out of Byrd — a person, a shipment,
a piece of information — and goes to the Dispatch Floor expecting a straightforward answer about the
highways. Instead, they get conflicting accounts: Dispatch's own records say Hwy 1/22 are maintained,
functioning freight routes; longtime residents insist nobody's actually confirmed a personal crossing in
longer than anyone can specifically remember.

**BUT** — Per Finding 15, this isn't an administrative inconvenience — Byrd's aviation route is already
confirmed, permanently dead (chain #7). If the highways' real status is genuinely uncertain too, Byrd's
entire connection to the rest of Tepenia is riding on something nobody in the city can currently verify.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually attempt the route themselves, using Finding 34's own logic as
their guide — checking what the cold-weather staging gear near the elevators is actually sized and stocked
for, since that's the clearest physical clue anyone's left about whether this trip has been made recently.

**BUT** — `Byrd_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` Finding 4 is unambiguous: Hwy 22 passes directly through
Amundsen Station's own ruins, infrastructure the whole project agrees was destroyed. Even a nominally
"maintained" route runs straight through a genuine, unresolved hazard — passage isn't a formality, whatever
Dispatch's own records assume.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to find or force passage through or around the ruins, and in doing so,
finally get a real, current answer to whether Byrd's claimed highway access still means anything.

**BUT (reversal)** — What they find doesn't resolve cleanly. Finding 22's own Arrastradora lineage suggests
some vehicle traffic genuinely could make the crossing — but Finding 11 already establishes that formally
confirming and maintaining the route at that standard would mean committing more of Byrd's own already
stretched-thin guild resources than the city currently spends assuming it's simply fine. The uncertainty
isn't an oversight; it's structurally self-perpetuating, because actually resolving it costs something real.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings, the player's own choice: push to definitively
re-establish and confirm the route, committing Byrd's limited resources to verifying and maintaining
something the city has been running on assumption for who knows how long; or accept, and even quietly
formalize, the uncertainty — leaving Byrd's one remaining outside connection exactly as unconfirmed as it's
always effectively been, but preserving resources the city genuinely can't spare elsewhere.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT raises the stakes from
"conflicting paperwork" to "Byrd's last connection might not actually work." The second BUT turns "drive the
route" into "the route runs through confirmed-destroyed infrastructure." The third BUT removes the clean
answer entirely — confirming the road costs exactly what Byrd doesn't have spare. No Complication is
deletable without losing the final choice.

## Worth Your Attention

This is the candidate that actually engages the standing Highway/Isolation Contradiction without violating
"flag, don't fix" — it never states which version of Byrd's lore is correct; it makes the *uncertainty
itself* the thing the player's own journey resolves or doesn't, which is a stronger, more specific version of
the Isolation Crisis anchor than a generic supply-shortage plot would be. Every fact used (the aviation
fleet's confirmed death, the Amundsen ruins, the Arrastradora lineage, the resource cost of certainty) was
already established before this candidate combined them.
