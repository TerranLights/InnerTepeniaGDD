# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #19: "Water Finds Its Own Way"

**What this is:** a plumbing/structural-cascade candidate with a human, residential-displacement register —
distinct from Candidate #06 (general structural safety) and #14 (industrial air-handling disaster), which
already cover the city's other established hazard classes. No Cradle content. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Finding 39** — sewage/plumbing integrity and ice-boundary structural monitoring aren't fully separable
  systems; a plumbing failure near the ice boundary (attributes #39–40) is simultaneously a contamination
  problem and a structural one.
- **Attribute #31** — deep-structure water pressure and plumbing engineering, distinct from typical shallow
  city plumbing.
- **Finding 20** — Coober Pedy's own precedent: Byrd's population coexists without full integration, meaning
  temporary housing isn't simply interchangeable space.
- **Finding 25 / attribute #72** — the old-core vs. newer-expansion structural distinction; older sections
  carry real historical weight beyond their practical housing function.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A plumbing failure in an old-core residential section causes flooding and real damage,
displacing a number of residents. The player is asked to help manage the response.

**BUT** — Finding 39 means this isn't purely a housing problem — the leak sits near the ice boundary,
feeding uncontrolled moisture into the very insulation zone attributes #39–40 exist to protect, turning a
sanitation failure into a live structural concern at the same time.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to coordinate two responses simultaneously: housing the displaced residents
and supporting the Foundation Keepers' own structural assessment, neither of which can simply wait on the
other.

**BUT** — Temporary housing capacity is already thin (echoing Candidate #10's own capacity strain), and per
Finding 20, displaced residents aren't simply interchangeable — different population threads have real,
distinct needs and preferences about where and how they're housed, not easily solved by "just find them
somewhere to sleep."

**THEREFORE** — The player has to make real, felt trade-offs about resource allocation for the displaced,
pulling capacity from guild operations that have their own legitimate claims on the same limited space and
labor.

**BUT (reversal)** — Once the structural fix is made, a genuine choice remains: rebuild the damaged section
in place, restoring what was lost — including whatever historical or personal significance the old-core
location held for the people who lived there — at real, ongoing structural risk the ice boundary already
flags as live; or relocate permanently to safer, newer-expansion territory, protecting residents going
forward at the cost of a real, permanent loss of place.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: rebuild in place, restoring what was lost at continued
structural risk; or relocate permanently, trading safety for a genuine, felt loss of home and history for
the residents who lived there.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns "a leak" into "a structural
emergency happening at the same time as a housing crisis." The second BUT means housing itself isn't a
simple logistics problem. The third BUT means even the fix has a genuine, felt cost either way. No
Complication is deletable without losing the final choice.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate gives Byrd's third established hazard class (plumbing/ice-boundary cascade, per Finding 39) a
genuinely human register — the stakes are displaced residents and lost history, not industrial output or
citywide survival, distinguishing it clearly from #06 and #14 despite sharing underlying physical-
infrastructure material.
