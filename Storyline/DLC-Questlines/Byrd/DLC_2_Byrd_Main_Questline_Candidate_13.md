# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #13: "Everyone's Scrap, Somebody's Problem"

**What this is:** a small-scale, economically-grounded candidate built from the solid-waste/consumer-goods
loop this session's deep-dive surfaced — deliberately kept away from chamber fabrication specifically. **Not
canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Finding 33** — solid-waste and scrap recycling plausibly closes a literal loop with Byrd's own in-house
  consumer-goods manufacturing (attribute #25), not two separate systems; the same self-sufficiency instinct
  that produces buffer-stock discipline (attribute #13) applies to scrap.
- **Attribute #52** — solid waste and industrial scrap handling/recycling infrastructure.
- **Finding 21** — the five guild sectors interleave physically and economically, rather than occupying
  clean, separable zones.
- **Finding 16** — the Jamshedpur precedent: extraction plausibly sited immediately adjacent to fabrication,
  minimizing the transport step between raw material and finished good.
- **`Byrd_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section I** — the five established guilds (Fabricators, Dispatch, Foundation
  Keepers, Prospectors' Circle, Grounded Wings), each with a legitimate, distinct claim on shared resources.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A genuine shortage of usable scrap material threatens Byrd's own in-house consumer-goods output
(attribute #25) — clothing, tools, household hardware, the everyday goods the Commons Hall actually
distributes. The player is asked to help figure out why the supply has tightened.

**BUT** — The scrap isn't disappearing — it's being claimed elsewhere first. Per Finding 21's own interleaved
economy, multiple guilds have a legitimate stake in the same recovered material, and nobody formally decided
whose claim comes first when the total supply can't cover everyone.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually trace where the scrap is going and broker a real allocation
among competing, equally legitimate guild claims.

**BUT** — Each claim is genuinely defensible on its own terms — the Prospectors' Circle wants it fed back
into extraction-adjacent equipment maintenance; Dispatch wants it for vehicle upkeep (attribute #44); the
Fabricators' own consumer-goods branch wants it for the everyday items residents actually depend on. There's
no neutral, obviously-correct answer, only genuinely different, equally real needs.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually choose a distribution formula, or negotiate one the guilds will
live with, rather than simply reporting the shortage back up the chain.

**BUT (reversal)** — Whatever formula gets set becomes precedent — the next shortage, in some other shared
resource this session's own attributes already establish Byrd runs on (water reservoirs, attribute #54; the
freight-hoist network, attribute #35), will plausibly be settled the same way, whether or not that's actually
the right call for a different resource under different pressure. The player isn't just solving today's
shortage — they're setting how Byrd resolves every future one like it.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings, the player's chosen allocation principle — strict
seniority among the guilds, proportional need, first-come, or some other rule — determines not just who gets
scrap this time, but the precedent the whole city will lean on the next time any shared, limited resource
runs short, for better or worse.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns "a shortage" into "a
genuine multi-guild claims conflict." The second BUT means there's no clean, obviously-fair answer. The third
BUT means the resolution outlives this specific shortage. No Complication is deletable without losing the
final choice's weight.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This is the smallest-scale, most quietly consequential candidate in the current set — no external threat,
no disaster, just an ordinary resource-allocation dispute that this session's own Finding 21 and Finding 33
make genuinely load-bearing. It's a strong candidate for an early-game or mid-game quest establishing Byrd's
five-guild politics before a larger crisis (Candidates #06, #11) tests them under real pressure.
