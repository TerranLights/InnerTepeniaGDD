# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #23: "What Waits on the Surface"

**What this is:** the only fully surface-set candidate in the current pool — every other candidate plays out
underground. Built around Finding 42's own smallest, least-populated Byrd specialty. No Cradle content.
**Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Finding 42** — surface operations (snow-clearing, surface lighting, gear staging) are plausibly the
  smallest, least-populated specialty in all of Byrd, sitting inside the Foundation Keepers' broader
  portfolio rather than justifying a guild of their own.
- **Attribute #17** — most residents have little routine reason to visit the surface city at all.
- **Attribute #32** — surface-level weather monitoring, a direct safety necessity given the extreme surface
  climate (−40°C winter, 108 days total polar night, 128 days total midnight sun).
- **Attributes #56–57** — surface snow-clearing/plow infrastructure and surface lighting for the polar-night
  period.
- **Attribute #33** — cold-weather surface-operations gear and staging areas.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Something goes wrong at the surface city — a structural failure at an elevator housing, or
someone stranded during a sudden shift in already-extreme conditions. The player is pulled directly into
Byrd's actual exterior environment, a setting almost no one in the city has real reason to experience.

**BUT** — The surface-operations crew, per Finding 42, is genuinely tiny — the smallest specialty in the
entire city — and what's happening exceeds what they can handle alone.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to recruit additional help from elsewhere in Byrd, but per attribute #17,
most residents have never had real reason to go to the surface at all, and have no practical experience
with the conditions waiting for them there.

**BUT** — Rushing untrained people to the surface is genuinely dangerous — attribute #32's own weather
monitoring exists precisely because conditions this severe can turn lethal fast. There's a real, live
tension between needing more hands and needing those hands to actually survive the environment.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to make real triage decisions: who goes up, how prepared they need to be
first, and how much delay that preparation costs against the urgency of the crisis itself.

**BUT (reversal)** — However it resolves, the incident exposes an uncomfortable structural assumption: Byrd
has always run on the belief that its tiny surface-operations crew is enough. This crisis just proved that
assumption wrong, at least once. Fixing it — training and maintaining a genuinely larger surface-capable
workforce — costs real, ongoing resources for a capability the city has never needed at this scale before;
not fixing it leaves Byrd exactly as under-prepared for the next incident as it was for this one.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: invest in permanently expanding surface-operations
training and capacity, reducing future risk at real, sustained cost; or treat this as a one-time emergency
and return to the tiny-specialist-crew status quo, preserving resources at the cost of the same
vulnerability resurfacing later.

---

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT means the existing crew can't
handle it alone. The second BUT means more help comes with real danger attached. The third BUT means even a
successful resolution leaves a structural choice about whether to actually fix the underlying gap. No
Complication is deletable without losing the final choice's weight.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This is the pool's clearest change of pace — every other candidate is set entirely within Byrd's underground
volume, and this is the only one that puts the player in the actual exterior Antarctic environment the rest
of the city has been sealed away from since its founding. Worth strong consideration purely for the
environmental variety it brings to a DLC that would otherwise be entirely subterranean.
