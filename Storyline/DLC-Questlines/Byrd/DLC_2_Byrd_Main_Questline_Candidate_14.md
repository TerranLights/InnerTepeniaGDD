# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #14: "The Air Between Them"

**What this is:** a fast-moving disaster-response candidate built around a hazard category this session's
deep-dive explicitly flagged as distinct from the structural/geothermal risks Candidates #06 and #11 already
cover — no Cradle content. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Finding 43** — Byrd's air-handling infrastructure is genuinely three separate systems (life-support
  ventilation, attribute #1; industrial dust filtration, attribute #38; exhaust venting, attribute #41), not
  one unified system — keeping them from cross-contaminating is itself a real, actively-managed hazard,
  flagged directly as "a fast-moving, city-wide hazard in a way a localized structural failure isn't."
- **Attribute #27** — fire-suppression and industrial-hazard containment systems.
- **Finding 21** — the five guild sectors interleave physically, meaning a failure in one guild's own
  territory is rarely contained to that guild alone.
- **Finding 13** (this document's own Candidate #13) — a prior resource-allocation precedent this candidate
  can optionally connect to, if run as a follow-up quest rather than standalone.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — An air-handling breach hits a section of Byrd — industrial byproduct bleeding into life-support
air, exactly the cross-contamination event Finding 43 already identifies as one of the city's most severe,
fast-moving hazard categories. The player is pulled in as an emergency responder alongside the Foundation
Keepers.

**BUT** — Containment (attribute #27) buys time but doesn't identify the actual source. Because three
genuinely separate systems are involved, figuring out *which one* failed — and why — determines the entire
shape of the response, and it isn't obvious from the crisis alone.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to investigate the source directly, under time pressure, while the affected
section stays sealed off.

**BUT** — The trail points to negligence, not accident — a specific guild's own maintenance or resourcing
decision (plausibly tied to an earlier resource-allocation call, if this follows Candidate #13) left a known
weak point unaddressed. This isn't a random failure; someone's choice made it more likely.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to manage both the physical crisis and the political fallout of what caused
it — including whether identifying the responsible party publicly helps the response or actively worsens it.

**BUT (reversal)** — Full transparency about the cause serves accountability, but risks real, lasting damage
to inter-guild trust — Finding 21's own interleaved economy depends on guilds trusting each other's
reliability precisely because their work physically overlaps. A quieter, managed resolution preserves that
functional cooperation in the short term, but leaves the actual root cause unaddressed, with a real,
concrete risk of recurrence.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: push for full public disclosure, holding the responsible
guild accountable at real cost to the trust Byrd's whole interleaved economy structurally depends on; or
manage the resolution quietly, preserving functional cooperation between guilds at the cost of leaving the
actual cause — and the risk of it happening again — unresolved.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT turns "contain the breach" into
"the source isn't obvious and matters enormously." The second BUT turns "find the cause" into "the cause is
someone's choice, not bad luck." The third BUT means neither transparency nor discretion is free. No
Complication is deletable without losing the final choice's weight.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This is the fastest-paced, most immediately dramatic candidate in the current set — a genuine emergency-
response quest rather than a slow-building political or economic one — while still resolving into the same
No Good Endings, guild-politics-driven shape as the rest. Explicitly designed to pair with Candidate #13 as a
two-part arc (an allocation decision, then its consequence), though it also stands alone with a generic
negligence cause if run independently.
