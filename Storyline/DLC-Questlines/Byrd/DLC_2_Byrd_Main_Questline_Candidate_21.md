# DLC 2 (Byrd) — Main Questline Candidate #21: "Neither Side of the Line"

**What this is:** a robot-specific version of Byrd's founding-descendant/redistributed-majority tension —
grounded in this project's own established robot culture rather than simply imported from the human
framework (Findings 7/20). No Cradle content. **Not canon, not chosen.**

---

## Inputs actually used

- **Finding 7** — specialized institutional knowledge concentrating among founding-descendant families,
  against a far larger population who arrived later, indirectly, via the Palmer City redistribution.
- **Finding 20** — Coober Pedy's real precedent: coexistence without full integration, not smoothed-over
  unity.
- **Attributes #19–20** — human/robot vertical residential stratification; robots concentrate in the deeper,
  less human-convenient levels.
- **Course of Events chain #3** — the founding expedition's crew was entirely robot, meaning Byrd's earliest
  human arrivals would have worked and lived closely alongside these same robots from the very beginning.
- **[[project_robot_biology]]** — established robot culture markers (coolant, robot coffee, siligel-adjacent
  communal practice) as the actual texture of robot social life, not an abstraction.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A proposal to renovate and modernize the deep-level, robot-concentrated zones (attribute #20)
comes before the city — updated charging and maintenance infrastructure, reworked communal spaces. The
player is asked to help build consensus.

**BUT** — Founding-era robots — centuries old, present since the original expedition (chain #3) — resist,
not from simple stubbornness but because these specific spaces carry real, lived personal history for them
in ways the numerically larger population of later-arrived robots (via the same redistribution that brought
most of Byrd's human population too) don't share.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually understand both robot groups' own perspectives on their own
terms — a genuinely robot-specific version of Finding 7's tension, built from this project's own established
robot culture rather than assumed to mirror the human situation.

**BUT** — The dispute isn't cleanly separable from the human population's own version of the same tension.
Founding-descendant human families plausibly have close personal and working ties to these same founding-era
robots, going back to the earliest days when humans and the founding crew lived and worked side by side —
meaning how the player handles the robot dispute has real, unavoidable ripple effects into the human
founding-descendant/majority tension too.

**THEREFORE** — The player's mediation approach shapes both disputes at once, whether or not that's the
intent.

**BUT (reversal)** — Neither resolution is clean. Modernizing serves the numeric robot majority and reflects
evolving robot culture and preference, but risks erasing something that genuinely mattered to Byrd's actual
founding generation — robot and human alike. Preserving the zones as they are honors that founding legacy,
but leaves the larger, later-arrived robot population's own needs unaddressed — the exact same shape as
Finding 7's human tension, now proven to recur independently in a population with a genuinely different
relationship to time and memory.

**THEREFORE (resolution)** — Per No Good Endings: modernize, serving the majority at real cost to founding-
era significance; or preserve, honoring that legacy at the cost of leaving the majority underserved — and
either way, the outcome quietly shapes how the wider city reads the parallel human tension too, since the
two are now visibly linked.

## Self-check

SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. The first BUT means the dispute isn't simple
resistance to change. The second BUT means it isn't contained to the robot population alone. The third BUT
means neither resolution is free, and both ripple outward. No Complication is deletable without losing the
final choice's weight.

## Worth Your Attention

This is the only candidate in the current set that treats Finding 7's tension as something that could
recur independently across a population with a genuinely different relationship to time, memory, and
lifespan, rather than assuming the human framework simply transfers. It also directly engages robot
consciousness and civic belonging — one of this project's own two creative north-star questions — at a
population level, complementing Candidate #09's single-figure treatment of the same broader theme.
