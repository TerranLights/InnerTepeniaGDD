# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #18: "The System That Wasn't Supposed to Work"

**What this is:** an eighteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around an eighth distinct
linking mechanism — a black-market smuggling investigation, rather than a physical route (#11), a functional
retrospective (#12), a supply coalition (#13), a comparative civic theme (#14), a child-welfare thread
(#15), an epidemiological trace (#16), or a representation-structure question (#17). **Not canon, not
chosen, not the only candidate** — an eighteenth possibility, beyond this subnet's own already-complete set
of ten. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Rothera, Marambio, Esperanza, Port Lockroy, Signy) — a source, two
transit/distribution points, an unwitting waypoint, and a desperate end customer.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Rothera's own established industrial production role** — a genuine source of materials that could
  plausibly be diverted from legitimate distribution before ever reaching an official channel.
- **Marambio's and Esperanza's own established shipping division of labor** — the subnet's actual
  distribution network, and therefore also its most obvious point of diversion for anything moving outside
  legitimate channels.
- **Port Lockroy's own small, quiet civic character** — a place where an unusual shipment or transaction
  could plausibly pass through with less scrutiny than a busier city would apply.
- **Signy's own established resource desperation** (Candidate #1's own grounding) — a city with genuine,
  ongoing need and, plausibly, the least patience for waiting on slow, formal channels to meet it.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim that this network connects to Candidate #13's own formal supply
  coalition, or Candidate #16's contamination event — this chain treats the black market as its own,
  separate response to the same general scarcity, not a consequence of either prior candidate's own
  specific resolution.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Reports surface of a black-market operation exploiting the subnet's own post-war scarcity —
diverting genuinely needed materials away from legitimate channels and reselling them at exploitative prices
to desperate communities. The player is asked to investigate and shut the network down — a straightforward
crime investigation, on its face.

**BUT** — Tracing the network means following its own distribution web across multiple cities, each playing
a different role: Rothera as a plausible source of diverted materials, Marambio and Esperanza as the
shipping points where legitimate cargo could be quietly skimmed, Port Lockroy as an unwitting, low-scrutiny
waypoint, and Signy as the network's own desperate, genuinely needy customer.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to investigate each city in turn, building a picture of the network city by
city rather than finding one central culprit to confront.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the network's actual organizers are identified: this isn't a purely
predatory criminal operation. At least part of it is ordinary people who turned to smuggling specifically
because legitimate channels — slow, bureaucratic, or simply insufficient to meet real, desperate need — were
failing communities like Signy in practice, whatever their intentions on paper. Shutting the network down
completely might remove the only reliable way some of the subnet's most vulnerable people were actually
getting adequate resources at all, however illegally or exploitatively.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a straightforward crime investigation becomes a genuine question about
whether an illegitimate system that's actually working is worth more, right now, than a legitimate one that
isn't.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Shutting the network down fully and enforcing
proper legitimate channels restores fairness, accountability, and process, and removes real exploitation from
the pricing — but potentially cuts off genuinely desperate communities from resources they were, however
imperfectly, actually receiving, at least until legitimate systems can be improved enough to match what the
black market was quietly delivering. Tolerating the network, or dismantling only its most exploitative
elements while preserving its actual distribution function, keeps resources flowing to people who need them
right now — but preserves a system operating outside any real accountability, and undermines the legitimate
institutions and coalitions the subnet is actually trying to build in its place.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how this actually resolves: full enforcement, restoring fairness and legitimacy at the cost of
cutting off resources some communities were genuinely, if illegally, depending on; or tolerance, or partial
dismantling, keeping resources flowing at the cost of preserving an unaccountable system and undercutting the
subnet's own legitimate rebuilding efforts.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "shut down a network"
into "the network spans five cities, each with a different role, not one culprit to confront"; the second BUT
turns "find the organizers" into "the organizers are, in part, people responding to real failures in the
legitimate system"; the third BUT turns "enforce or tolerate" into "there is no version of the choice that
doesn't cost either fairness and legitimacy or real, currently-flowing resources to people who need them."
Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a
disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Palmer subnet candidate structured as a genuine crime
investigation, giving DLC 3 gameplay variety distinct from the route-following, coalition-building,
policy-comparison, placement, and medical-mystery structures already built across this set. It's also the
first candidate whose central twist inverts the expected moral shape of a crime story entirely — the
"criminals" turn out to be filling a real gap the legitimate system has failed to close, making enforcement
itself, not just its absence, the thing with a genuine cost.
