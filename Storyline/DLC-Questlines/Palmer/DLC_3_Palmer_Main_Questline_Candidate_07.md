# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #7: "What the Room Was For"

**What this is:** a seventh prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around Palmer City's own established
role as the site of the Long Night War's inciting incident. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate** — the 7th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities (Palmer City, as ruins)**.

**Deliberately does not resolve whether Solenne Vaskarre is meetable in the present day.** That question is
explicitly flagged elsewhere (`Palmer_Subnet_Full_Extrapolation.md`, Section III) as an open fork the
developer hasn't settled. This chain works entirely from her already-established backstory and fate — relocated
to Concordia before the destruction — without requiring her to appear, the same approach several other
candidates in this set have taken toward other cities' own standing open questions.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Palmer City's own Full Extrapolation, Section I** — the tourism mechanism, formalized: Palmer City's
  economy runs on the same structural logic as 1920s Montreal's Prohibition tourism — Upper Earth's own
  restrictiveness around personhood and robot rights manufactures demand that Palmer City, having never
  carried that baggage, simply receives. An "open secret" nobody states officially.
- **Palmer City's own Full Extrapolation, Section III** — Solenne Vaskarre, working in Palmer City's
  entertainment economy (plausibly the Petrograd Room itself), was on shift, in a role the city's whole
  tourism economy depends on, when a senior Upper Earth diplomat denied her personhood and attacked her —
  "the same tourism economy that makes Palmer City who it is also produced the exact circumstance that got
  her killed by history, even though she was the one who survived the room." She was relocated to Concordia
  before the city's eventual destruction.
- **Palmer City's own Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the municipal holidays, including "Needle Down," a
  citywide, dusk-to-dawn live-music celebration of the city's jazz/rockabilly/electroswing tradition,
  modeled on Montreal's own modern jazz festival — a still-celebrated piece of the same civic identity that
  produced the tourism economy in Section I.
- **Palmer City's own Full Extrapolation, Section VI** — the ruins are genuinely accessible but uniquely
  hazardous: flooded lower levels, freeze-thaw structural decay, and fog that hides real structural danger
  rather than just limiting sightlines, distinct from any other Tepenian ruin site's cold-preserved,
  snow-stabilized wreckage.
- **Explicitly excluded:** Solenne's own present-day status, her specific nationality's diplomat
  counterpart's fate, and any invented detail about the exact physical site of the incident beyond what
  Section III already establishes (the Petrograd Room, "plausibly").

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A historical effort, tied to the national mythology Palmer City's founding still anchors,
wants the exact site and circumstances of the Long Night War's inciting incident properly documented before
the ruins' own ongoing structural decay makes that impossible. The player is asked to navigate Palmer City's
genuinely hazardous ruins — flooding, freeze-thaw collapse, fog hiding real danger — to recover whatever
physical record of that night still survives.

**BUT** — What the player actually recovers makes explicit something the national story has always left
conveniently vague. Solenne wasn't a private citizen who happened to cross paths with a visiting diplomat —
she was working, in the exact tourism-and-hospitality economy Palmer City is still celebrated for, when he
attacked her. The clean national account (an attack happened, she defended herself, a war began) has never
had to reckon with the fact that the same "open secret" economic structure the whole nation still marks with
Needle Down every year is the specific mechanism that put her in that room in the first place.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to decide how to characterize the finding for the institution that
commissioned the historical work, since the fuller picture complicates a story that currently unifies rather
than unsettles.

**BUT** — The stakes turn out to reach further than history alone. Palmer City's tourism-for-freedom
economy isn't just a fact about a destroyed city — it's a structure the nation still takes real pride in,
still celebrates, still implicitly holds up as proof of what Tepenia offers that Upper Earth can't. Telling
the fuller truth doesn't just complicate Solenne's own story; it complicates whether that same celebrated
structure — the one that made her vulnerable in the first place — deserves the uncomplicated pride it still
gets.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a preservation project becomes a real decision about whether the nation is
willing to see its own founding pride implicated in its own founding wound.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Presenting the full, complicated truth
honors Solenne's actual circumstance and is honest about the war's real origin — but destabilizes a clean,
unifying national story and casts a genuine, still-celebrated shadow over Needle Down and everything it
represents. Preserving the cleaner, heroic-but-incomplete version keeps national unity and untroubled
celebration intact — but leaves Solenne's own actual circumstance permanently obscured: honored as a hero,
never understood as someone the very economy everyone still celebrates helped put at risk.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
choice in what the historical record actually says: the full, complicated truth, honoring Solenne
completely at the cost of a national story's untroubled unity; or the cleaner account the nation already
believes, protecting that unity at the cost of ever really understanding the person the war is supposedly
about. Neither choice undoes what actually happened in that room — only how honestly the nation is willing
to remember it.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "document the site"
into "the documentation reveals an uncomfortable structural truth the national story has always avoided";
the second BUT turns "report the finding" into "the finding implicates something the nation still actively
celebrates, not just something in the past"; the third BUT turns "tell the truth or don't" into "there is no
version that doesn't cost either the nation's unity or Solenne's own full humanity." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And
Then.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes the single most load-bearing piece of lore in the entire
project — the Long Night War's own inciting incident — and asks whether the nation built on its aftermath
is willing to see its own founding pride implicated in its own founding wound, using only facts already
established in Palmer City's own Full Extrapolation rather than inventing new backstory. It's also the
first Palmer subnet candidate to route its stakes through Concordia's own present-day national mythology
(Needle Down) rather than staying contained to a single destroyed city's own local memory.
