# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #25: "Whatever Counted as Working"

**What this is:** a twenty-fifth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around evaluating an
interrupted coexistence experiment against genuinely conflicting standards of success. **Not canon, not
chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Sejong, Juan Carlos, Port Lockroy, Rothera, Signy).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Palmer Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — the established fact that Sejong's own dense, King
  George Island coexistence among nearly a dozen national communities produced "constant, informal,
  many-sided diplomatic coordination — a coexistence experiment whose success the Long Night War never let
  anyone finish judging."
- **Juan Carlos's own established comparison case** (same Throughway) — identical founding circumstances to
  Sejong's own, but a genuinely different outcome (a singular, isolated institution rather than a
  coexistence experiment), used here strictly for contrast, not for any tertulia-related content.
- **Signy's own established isolation** (Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 5), used here as a useful baseline: a
  city that never faced Sejong's own coexistence question at all.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Juan Carlos's own tertulia tradition, per this project's own
  standing deferral — Juan Carlos appears here only for its established isolation-versus-density comparison.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With peacetime allowing genuine reflection, a push emerges to formally evaluate whether Sejong's
own pre-war coexistence experiment — a dozen distinct national communities sharing one island — actually
succeeded, a question the war interrupted before anyone could judge it. The player is asked to help conduct
this assessment — a straightforward, if ambitious, evaluative task.

**BUT** — Genuinely evaluating "success" requires agreeing on what success would even mean, and Sejong's own
communities hold sincerely different standards — some judge it by whether real integration happened, others
by whether each community's own distinct identity survived proximity intact, others simply by the absence of
conflict. The evaluation itself surfaces disagreement about what the experiment was even trying to achieve.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to consult multiple communities within Sejong directly, and travel to Juan
Carlos, Port Lockroy, and Rothera for outside comparative context, to work out what a fair standard would
even look like.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once real testimony is gathered: the honest picture is genuinely mixed. By some
reasonable standards the experiment succeeded — no violent conflict, functional daily coexistence. By others
it clearly didn't — many communities maintained near-total separation, with minimal genuine blending,
essentially parallel societies rather than one integrated one. There is no single, honest verdict available.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a historical evaluation becomes a real question about which communities' own
definition of success the final assessment actually validates.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. A verdict emphasizing peaceful coexistence as
the true measure of success validates real, meaningful decades of non-violence and functional daily life
among a dozen distinct communities — but dismisses other communities' own sincere hope for genuine
integration, effectively declaring their own standard invalid. A verdict emphasizing genuine integration as
the true measure validates those communities' own real, unmet hope — but dismisses the substantial, genuinely
difficult accomplishment of decades of peaceful coexistence among a dozen different communities, treating it
as insufficient rather than valuable in its own right.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which standard the final assessment actually emphasizes: coexistence as success, honoring peace at
the cost of dismissing the integration-minded communities' own real hope; or integration as the true measure,
honoring that hope at the cost of undervaluing a genuine, hard-won peace.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "conduct an evaluation"
into "the communities themselves don't agree on what would count as success"; the second BUT turns "gather
testimony" into "the honest answer is genuinely mixed, not a hidden clear verdict"; the third BUT turns "pick
a standard" into "there is no standard that doesn't cost either one group's own hope or another group's own
real accomplishment." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end,
so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Throughway 2's own explicit framing — "a coexistence experiment
whose success the Long Night War never let anyone finish judging" — completely at its word, building the
entire chain around the fact that judging it now doesn't produce a clean answer, only a choice about which
of several sincere, competing standards gets to write the verdict. It's also the first Palmer subnet
candidate whose stakes are purely about how a shared history gets interpreted, with no material resource,
policy, or infrastructure at issue at all.
