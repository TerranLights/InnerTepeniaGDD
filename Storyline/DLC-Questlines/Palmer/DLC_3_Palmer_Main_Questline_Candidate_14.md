# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #14: "The Same Word, Five Different Meanings"

**What this is:** a fourteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around a fourth distinct
linking mechanism — the subnet's own genuinely varied expressions of human-robot partnership, rather than a
physical route (#11), a functional retrospective (#12), or a supply coalition (#13). **Not canon, not
chosen, not the only candidate** — a fourteenth possibility, beyond this subnet's own already-complete set
of ten. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Palmer City, Esperanza, Marambio, Rothera, Signy) — the five cities
whose own established human-robot relationships are each genuinely different in kind, not just degree. No
prior candidate in this set has grouped these five together for this specific comparison.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Palmer City's own established founding basis** — founded on a robot-partnership/ideology basis rather
  than any national identity; human-robot equality is a *founding conviction* here, not an emergent practice.
- **Esperanza's own established structure** — long-lived robot caregivers as the primary at-home caregivers
  for the city's children, a *functional, structural dependency*, not a symbolic commitment.
- **Marambio's own established dockworker culture** (already the basis of Candidate #5) — many human
  dockworkers in genuine, lived romantic partnerships with robot coworkers, a *lived, personal* relationship
  rather than an ideological or structural one.
- **Rothera's own established vault history** (already the basis of Candidate #4) — humans and robots
  sheltered together during the war's worst strikes, a relationship forged through *shared survival*, not
  founding principle, caregiving structure, or romance.
- **Signy's own established resource asymmetry** (already the basis of Candidate #1) — humans sustain fine
  on the Scotia Sea's marine resources; robots have no local siligel source at all, a relationship currently
  defined by *acute, one-sided deprivation*, not partnership of any kind.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any re-litigation of Candidates #1, #4, or #5's own specific climaxes — this
  chain treats each city's established relationship as a starting fact to be weighed against the others, not
  a question to resolve again individually.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Post-war resource scarcity forces a genuine, subnet-wide policy question: how should scarce
rebuilding materials and support be allocated between human and robot populations across Palmer subnet's
surviving and recovering cities. The player is asked to help develop a fair allocation framework — a
straightforward policy task, on its face.

**BUT** — A single, uniform policy doesn't actually fit any of the five cities equally, because "human
-robot partnership" means something structurally different in each one. Palmer City's own founding
conviction of equality, Esperanza's functional caregiving dependency, Marambio's lived romantic partnerships,
Rothera's survival-forged solidarity, and Signy's own acute, one-sided deprivation are five genuinely
different relationships, not five instances of the same one at different intensities.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to develop something more nuanced than one blanket standard, engaging each
city's own specific relationship and actual need individually rather than imposing a single framework
everywhere.

**BUT** — Tailoring the response city by city creates a real controversy of its own: treating Signy's own
acute crisis as more urgent than Palmer City's own more abstract, principle-based equality claim, for
instance, risks looking like some cities' robots are being valued or prioritized more than others, purely
based on which city they happen to live in — an uncomfortable hierarchy the subnet has never had to
confront directly, since these five relationships have simply always existed side by side without anyone
comparing them.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a resource-allocation task becomes a real question about whether fairness
means treating every city identically, or treating every city's own real, different relationship and need
as what it actually is.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. A genuinely tailored, city-specific approach
respects each place's own real, established relationship and addresses actual on-the-ground need — Signy's
own crisis gets treated with the urgency it specifically requires — but creates a visibly uneven standard
across the subnet that could read, uncomfortably, as valuing some robots' welfare more urgently than
others depending on simple geography. A single, uniform standard applied identically everywhere guarantees
visible, formal equality and avoids any appearance of favoritism — but ignores real differences in actual
need, potentially under-serving Signy's own acute crisis in the name of consistency, or imposing Palmer
City's own particular founding ideology onto cities that never adopted it as their own.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
recommendation for how the subnet actually allocates its own scarce resources: a tailored, city-specific
approach, honoring each place's own real relationship and need at the cost of a visibly uneven standard
across the subnet; or one uniform standard, guaranteeing formal consistency at the cost of failing to meet
each city's own actual, established need on its own terms.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "write one policy" into
"the same word means five structurally different things in five different cities"; the second BUT turns
"tailor the response" into "tailoring it creates a visible, uncomfortable hierarchy nobody's ever had to
justify before"; the third BUT turns "pick a standard" into "there is no standard that is both fair to real
difference and free of the appearance of favoritism." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player
is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Palmer subnet candidate to treat "human-robot
partnership" as a genuinely comparative subject across multiple cities, rather than a single city's own
established fact — revealing that five already-written civic cultures, none of which were designed with the
others in mind, produce five structurally distinct relationships when actually placed side by side. It's
also the first candidate in this set whose central tension is a policy-design trade-off between fairness
-as-sameness and fairness-as-recognition-of-real-difference, a genuinely different register from any
resource-priority, historical-truth, or infrastructure question this subnet's own candidates have raised so
far.
