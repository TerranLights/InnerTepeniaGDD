# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #19: "Whichever Currency Wins"

**What this is:** a nineteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around a ninth distinct
linking mechanism — designing a regional trade-standard/currency system, rather than a physical route
(#11), a functional retrospective (#12), a supply coalition (#13), a comparative civic theme (#14), a
child-welfare thread (#15), an epidemiological trace (#16), a representation-structure question (#17), or a
crime investigation (#18). **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — a nineteenth possibility,
beyond this subnet's own already-complete set of ten. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Rothera, Signy, Port Lockroy, Marambio, Esperanza).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Established, project-wide fact** — the national energy-backed currency fractured post-war into regional
  currency and a separate trade standard, per `project_national_currency_history` — a real, unresolved economic
  gap this chain treats as needing a genuine regional answer for Palmer subnet specifically.
- **Rothera's own established industrial output** — the subnet's most reliable, productive manufacturing
  base, a natural candidate for backing any new regional trade standard.
- **Signy's own established marine-resource self-sufficiency and siligel scarcity** — a genuinely different
  economic profile from Rothera's, with its own real but very differently-shaped resources and needs.
- **Marambio's and Esperanza's own established shipping/trade roles** — the historical trade relationships
  and routing knowledge that any new regional standard would need to actually move goods through, even with
  both cities themselves destroyed.
- **Port Lockroy's own small, modest economy** — Tepenia's second-smallest city, with the least leverage of
  any surviving Palmer subnet city to shape a new standard in its own favor.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about how this regional standard relates to Concordia's own national
  currency policy — this chain stays at Palmer subnet's own regional scale, not the national one.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With the national energy-backed currency fractured, Palmer subnet's own surviving economic
centers — Rothera's industry, Signy's marine resources, Port Lockroy's modest trade, and whatever's left of
Marambio's and Esperanza's own historical shipping relationships — need some kind of working regional
trade-standard to actually exchange goods and value with each other again. The player is asked to help
broker this system — a straightforward, if technical, economic-recovery task.

**BUT** — Each city's own economic profile is different enough that no common standard is neutral. Rothera's
manufactured goods, Signy's marine resources, and the former shipping routes' own leftover trade
relationships don't translate into one fair common measure easily, especially post-war, when the normal
price-discovery mechanisms that would sort this out organically simply don't exist anymore.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually negotiate a working valuation and exchange system among these
different economies, balancing real, structural differences rather than picking an arbitrary number.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once a real proposal takes shape: whichever specific resource gets chosen as the
new standard's actual backing isn't a neutral technical choice. Pegging the regional currency to Rothera's
manufactured output, for instance, gives Rothera outsized economic influence over the whole subnet's future
by design, not by accident — the currency question is, underneath, a question about which city becomes the
new center of economic gravity for everyone else.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a technical economic-recovery task becomes a real decision about whose
production gets to define value for the entire subnet going forward.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Backing the standard on Rothera's own
reliable manufacturing output creates a genuinely stable, workable system in a fragile post-war economy — but
permanently cements Rothera's own outsized economic power over the whole subnet's future, at real cost to
Signy's and Port Lockroy's own economic autonomy. Choosing a more distributed, multi-commodity standard that
doesn't favor any single city is more equitable in principle — but is technically more complex, harder to
maintain, and less immediately stable, a real risk in an economy already struggling with basic function.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which standard actually gets adopted: a Rothera-backed standard, trading long-term equity for
short-term stability and cementing one city's own outsized influence; or a distributed, multi-commodity
standard, trading immediate reliability for a fairer distribution of economic power that the fragile post-war
economy may or may not actually be able to sustain.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "design a currency" into
"no common standard is actually neutral between these different economies"; the second BUT turns "propose a
backing" into "the backing choice quietly decides who holds economic power over everyone else going
forward"; the third BUT turns "pick a standard" into "there is no standard that is both stable and
equitable at once, given what the post-war economy can actually sustain." Deleting any one Complication
changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Palmer subnet candidate built around a genuinely
economic mechanism — currency and trade-standard design — rather than logistics, governance, crime, or
culture, giving DLC 3 a mechanism no other candidate in this set has used. It's also the first candidate to
treat a seemingly technical, neutral-sounding decision (what backs a currency) as the actual site of real
political consequence, making the climax a choice about economic power distribution disguised as a
monetary-policy question.
