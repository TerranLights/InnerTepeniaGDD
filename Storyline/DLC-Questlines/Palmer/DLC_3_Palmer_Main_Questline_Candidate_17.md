# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #17: "A Vote for What's Already Gone"

**What this is:** a seventeenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around a seventh distinct
linking mechanism — the structure of representation itself, rather than a physical route (#11), a functional
retrospective (#12), a supply coalition (#13), a comparative civic theme (#14), a child-welfare thread (#15),
or an epidemiological trace (#16). **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — a seventeenth
possibility, beyond this subnet's own already-complete set of ten. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses at least 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Rothera, Port Lockroy, Signy — the three surviving cities —
plus Esperanza and Palmer City as the two destroyed cities whose scattered diaspora this chain centers on;
the framework itself, once built, naturally extends to Marambio's, Sejong's, and Juan Carlos's own diaspora
as well).

**Distinct from Candidate #10.** Candidate #10 asked a substantive policy question — should the subnet keep
serving as Tepenia's gateway. This chain asks a procedural one — who actually gets a voice in deciding
questions like that in the first place, now that five of the subnet's eight cities no longer physically
exist.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **The established fact that five of Palmer subnet's eight cities are destroyed** (Esperanza, Juan Carlos,
  Marambio, Palmer City, Sejong), with only Rothera, Port Lockroy, and Signy surviving in any populated form.
- **Each destroyed city's own established diaspora facts** — Esperanza's diaspora carried forward primarily
  by long-lived robot caregivers (Full Extrapolation, Section IV); Palmer City's own diaspora unusually
  scarce, given the Peninsula's distance from Concordia (per Solenne Vaskarre's own established rarity as an
  evacuee, Full Extrapolation Section III).
- **The general, project-wide established reality** of diaspora communities maintaining real, felt stakes in
  their destroyed home cities' own memory and future, referenced across this subnet's own prior candidates
  (#3's registry grief, #7's founding-myth question, #10's own subnet-wide reckoning).
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about Concordia's own district-level governance structure —
  this chain treats diaspora organization as informal and community-driven, not routed through any specific,
  developed Concordia institution.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With five of the subnet's eight cities destroyed, a genuine question arises about how decisions
affecting Palmer subnet's own future should actually get made — who has standing to weigh in, and how much
weight their voice should carry. The player is asked to help organize a fair representative structure,
drawing in both the three surviving cities' current populations and the scattered diaspora of the five that
are gone — a straightforward, if ambitious, governance-organizing task.

**BUT** — There's no established precedent for weighting a currently-populated surviving city's own voice
against a destroyed city's scattered, dispersed diaspora. Rothera, Port Lockroy, and Signy have real,
present populations living with the actual, ongoing consequences of whatever gets decided. Esperanza's and
Palmer City's own diaspora communities have real, felt stakes in their former home's memory and future, but
no longer live with the subnet's day-to-day reality at all.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to negotiate an actual representative framework, reaching out to both
surviving city leadership and diaspora communities — most of them now in Concordia — to work out how voice
and standing actually get allocated between the two.

**BUT** — A genuine, difficult tension surfaces once real negotiation begins. Some diaspora members argue a
destroyed city's own voice should carry extra, not equal, weight — their perspective represents something
that can never be refounded or replaced, a moral claim beyond simple headcount. Surviving city residents
argue, just as sincerely, that people no longer living with a decision's actual consequences shouldn't hold
equal sway over people who do, whatever the historical claim.

**THEREFORE** — What began as organizing a governance structure becomes a real argument about what
representation is actually for — honoring irreplaceable loss, or reflecting who actually bears the
consequences of what gets decided.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Granting diaspora communities meaningful,
weighted representation honors the destroyed cities' own unique, irreplaceable perspective and gives their
scattered populations real voice over decisions touching a place that was once their whole life — but hands
real power over ongoing, lived consequences to people who no longer live with them, a genuine democratic
tension. Weighting representation primarily by current physical presence and population ensures decisions
are made mainly by the people who actually bear their consequences — a defensible democratic principle — but
risks permanently silencing the destroyed cities' own claim to a future they helped build and lost, treating
their loss as though it no longer has any bearing on what the subnet becomes next.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how the framework actually gets built: weighted diaspora representation, honoring irreplaceable loss
at the cost of granting real power to people no longer living with the consequences; or presence-based
representation, honoring who actually bears those consequences at the cost of a silence the destroyed
cities' own scattered populations never chose for themselves.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "organize
representation" into "there's no precedent for weighting presence against irreplaceable loss"; the second
BUT turns "negotiate a framework" into "the negotiation surfaces two sincere, incompatible theories of what
representation is actually for"; the third BUT turns "pick a weighting" into "there is no weighting that
doesn't cost either real democratic legitimacy or a destroyed city's own claim to a say in what comes next."
Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a
disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Palmer subnet candidate whose stakes are purely
procedural — not a specific policy outcome, but the structure that would decide any future policy outcome at
all — giving it a genuinely different relationship to the rest of this set than any prior candidate,
including Candidate #10's own subnet-wide scale. It's also the first candidate whose central question could,
in principle, reshape how every other candidate in this set's own subnet-wide decisions (the gateway question,
the supply coalition, the resource-allocation policy) would actually get made, without this chain needing to
resolve any of those questions itself.
