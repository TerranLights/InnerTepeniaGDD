# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #13: "Whoever Keeps Paying For It"

**What this is:** a thirteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around a third distinct
linking mechanism — a real, functioning supply-chain coalition, rather than a physical route (Candidate #11)
or a retrospective lost-function analysis (Candidate #12). **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate**
— a thirteenth possibility, beyond this subnet's own already-complete set of ten. Uses the Linear Escalation
Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Signy, Rothera, Marambio, Esperanza, Port Lockroy) — the one city
that needs sustained help reaching it (Signy) and the four surviving cities whose own established functions
could actually form a real supply chain capable of reaching it. No city overlaps with Candidate #12's own
cast; Rothera, Marambio, and Esperanza each reappear from earlier single- or dual-city candidates, but this
specific five-city combination, and the coalition-building mechanism itself, is new.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Signy's own established facts** (already the basis of Candidate #1) — "isolation as both its identity and
  its cost"; no road or highway, the weakest Arcanet link in the Federation, dependent entirely on outside
  parties choosing to reach it.
- **Rothera's own established function** — the Palmer subnet's industrial center, producing "raw materials
  into finished infrastructure components used across the whole subnet, including Palmer City" — already
  under its own internal pressure (Candidate #4's own vault-conversion dilemma) for how its production
  capacity gets allocated.
- **Marambio's and Esperanza's own established shipping division of labor** — Marambio handling bulk,
  high-volume cargo, Esperanza handling smaller-scale, specific, documented shipments, an arrangement
  established for their own Weddell Sea corridor's needs, not originally oriented toward Signy at all.
- **Port Lockroy's own established postal/correspondence identity** — "moves words, not materials," the
  established coordination and communication counterpart to Rothera's own materials-moving role.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any re-litigation of Signy's own initial siligel crisis (Candidate #1's own
  climax) — this chain picks up afterward, addressing whether that crisis response becomes a genuine, lasting
  arrangement or stays a one-time exception.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — In the aftermath of Signy's own initial resupply crisis, Signy's community wants something more
durable than emergency aid: a real, lasting supply relationship that doesn't depend on crisis-driven
improvisation every time isolation and shortage collide again. Since Signy has no direct road, highway, or
reliable Arcanet link to anywhere, building this means actually assembling formal cooperation across the
entire chain that could realistically reach it — Rothera's production, Marambio's and Esperanza's own
shipping capacity, and Port Lockroy's own coordination function tying the logistics together. The player is
asked to help broker this multi-city arrangement — a straightforward "build lasting infrastructure" task.

**BUT** — None of these four cities' own established priorities already point toward Signy. Rothera's
production is already spoken for by its own post-war housing and industrial needs. Marambio's and Esperanza's
shipping division of labor was built for their own corridor, not for a fifth city genuinely marginal to
either. Port Lockroy's postal role has never had to coordinate logistics at this scale. Getting real,
sustained commitment from all four means real, separately-won buy-in, not one shared moment of goodwill.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to negotiate individually with each of the four contributing cities, building
the coalition piece by piece, since each has its own distinct reason to be reluctant or willing.

**BUT** — Once the coalition is actually built and running, a genuine complication surfaces: maintaining it
means each of the four contributing cities now permanently carries a small, ongoing cost it didn't have
before — a share of Rothera's production, a share of Marambio's and Esperanza's own shipping capacity, a
share of Port Lockroy's coordination bandwidth — all in service of the subnet's single smallest, most
isolated population. This raises a real, ongoing fairness question: should four more self-sufficient cities
permanently subsidize the one city that will likely always be the hardest and most expensive to serve, or
does Signy's own extreme vulnerability simply warrant that kind of permanent cross-subsidy regardless of the
continuing burden on everyone else?

**THEREFORE** — What began as building a supply chain becomes a real decision about whether to make that
chain permanent, or keep it something revisited and re-earned each time.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Formalizing a permanent, guaranteed
arrangement secures Signy's own long-term survival and real security, honoring the subnet's responsibility to
its most vulnerable member — but places an indefinite, ongoing burden on four cities that each now
permanently carry a cost primarily benefiting somewhere else. Keeping the arrangement flexible and
ad hoc, revisited rather than guaranteed, protects the four contributing cities' own long-term autonomy and
resource flexibility — but leaves Signy's own security genuinely precarious, dependent every time on whether
the same goodwill happens to hold up again.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which kind of chain gets built: a permanent, formalized commitment, guaranteeing Signy's future at
real, indefinite cost to four other cities; or a flexible, re-earned arrangement, protecting those four
cities' own autonomy at the cost of Signy's own security never actually being settled for good.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "build a supply chain"
into "none of the four contributing cities already have a reason to prioritize Signy"; the second BUT turns
"get everyone on board" into "maintaining the coalition means a permanent, ongoing cost for cities that
weren't previously carrying one"; the third BUT turns "decide how formal to make it" into "there is no
version of the arrangement that doesn't cost either Signy's own security or the other four cities' own
long-term autonomy." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end,
so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Palmer subnet candidate built around assembling a
coalition rather than following a route or reconstructing a lost function — the player's real work is a
series of separate, individually-won negotiations with four cities that each have their own distinct reason
to hesitate, rather than one unified decision point. It also gives Signy's own established isolation a
genuine structural consequence beyond the single crisis Candidate #1 already resolved: real, lasting support
for the subnet's most vulnerable city is possible, but never free, and someone always ends up paying for it
whether the arrangement is made permanent or not.
