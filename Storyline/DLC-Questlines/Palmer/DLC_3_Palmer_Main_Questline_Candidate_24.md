# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #24: "The Only One Who Was Actually There"

**What this is:** a twenty-fourth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around locating a living
witness to a destroyed city's history and the accuracy-versus-compassion dilemma that follows. **Not canon,
not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Palmer City, Rothera, Marambio, Sejong, Port Lockroy).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Palmer Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2** — the established proposal that "Solenne Vaskarre" *(placeholder
  name — not yet decided; see the developer's own note)*, given robot lifespans, may still be alive in the
  present-day game era nearly 250 years after Palmer City's destruction — "the single most consequential
  person Palmer City ever produced, potentially still carrying that history in person."
- **Rothera's own established shipping and record-keeping role** (Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 4), used here
  as a plausible holder of surviving documentary records relevant to cross-referencing her testimony.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about her own current whereabouts, activities, or exact
  testimony — this chain treats locating and interviewing her as the player's own open-ended task, not a
  predetermined outcome.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Leads suggest "Solenne Vaskarre" *(placeholder name)* — Palmer City's own most historically
consequential figure — may still be alive somewhere in the subnet. The player is asked to help locate and, if
she agrees, interview her to preserve a living account of Palmer City's own pre-destruction history — a
straightforward, if far-reaching, investigation.

**BUT** — Finding her raises a real complication: nearly 250 years of personal grief and memory mean her own
testimony may not be a simple, neutral record. It could be shaped by two and a half centuries of loss,
selective memory, or a specific personal narrative built to make an unbearable event survivable — valuable
as living history, but not necessarily accurate the way an untouched document would be.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to travel to Rothera, Marambio, Sejong, and Port Lockroy, cross-referencing
whatever surviving documentary fragments exist against her own account, to see where memory and record might
actually diverge.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once real discrepancies surface: confronting her directly, in the name of
historical accuracy, risks causing real, additional harm to someone who has already carried this loss alone
for two and a half centuries — someone who has arguably earned the right to remember it her own way. But
accepting her account without challenge risks enshrining real inaccuracies into the only living record
Tepenia will ever have of a destroyed city.

**THEREFORE** — What began as preserving a historical account becomes a real choice between historical
accuracy and an individual survivor's own hard-earned peace.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Pressing for accuracy and reconciling the
discrepancies gives Tepenia's own record of Palmer City real, verified integrity — but risks real emotional
harm to someone who has already survived an unbearable loss alone for far longer than any ordinary human
grief would ever have to last. Accepting her testimony without challenge protects her from that harm and
honors her own way of carrying the memory — but risks recording real inaccuracies into history, purely to
spare one person's feelings, however deeply earned that consideration is.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether to press her on the discrepancies or accept her account as given: press for accuracy,
protecting the historical record at the cost of real harm to her; or accept her account, protecting her at
the cost of a real, uncorrected inaccuracy in Palmer City's own remembered history.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "interview a witness"
into "the witness's own memory may not match the documented record"; the second BUT turns "check the record"
into "real discrepancies exist, and confronting them has a real emotional cost"; the third BUT turns "press
or accept" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either historical accuracy or a survivor's own
hard-earned peace." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so
none reads as a disguised And Then.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Pattern 2's own quietly remarkable proposal — a single living
person who might still carry Palmer City's own history in first-hand memory — and builds an entire dilemma
around the fact that living memory and documentary record are not the same kind of truth, rather than
treating her as a simple exposition source. It's also the first Palmer subnet candidate whose central
tension is between historical accuracy and one individual's own emotional survival, rather than a policy
question affecting a whole community.
