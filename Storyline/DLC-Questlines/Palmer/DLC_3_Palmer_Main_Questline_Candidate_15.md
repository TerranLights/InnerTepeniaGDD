# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #15: "What Esperanza Actually Raised"

**What this is:** a fifteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around a fifth distinct
linking mechanism — displaced-children placement across the subnet's destroyed and surviving cities, rather
than a physical route (#11), a functional retrospective (#12), a supply coalition (#13), or a comparative
civic theme (#14). **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — a fifteenth possibility, beyond this
subnet's own already-complete set of ten. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Esperanza, Palmer City, Sejong — the three destroyed source cities
whose own displaced children need placement; Rothera, Port Lockroy — the two surviving cities positioned to
receive them).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Esperanza's own established founding charter and identity** — chartered on the principle that the
  children of people who sacrificed everything for exile deserved to be looked after, with robots as the
  primary at-home caregivers; "being from Hope" as a living identity now carried forward by Esperanza's own
  long-lived robot caregivers rather than human bloodlines (Full Extrapolation, Section IV).
- **Palmer City's and Sejong's own established destruction and populations** — both fully destroyed, each
  with their own scattered, real diaspora and, plausibly, displaced children among the survivors of each
  city's own fall.
- **Rothera's own established civic character** — an industrial workshop city with a genuine, if
  function-first, community, its own underground vaults having sheltered people (human and robot) together
  through the war's worst strikes.
- **Port Lockroy's own established civic character** — a small, intimate, historically-conscious town,
  Tepenia's second-smallest city, where "real intimacy comes from simply being one of Tepenia's smallest
  cities" (Local Cultures, Section 6e).
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Signy's own capacity to receive displaced children — Signy's
  established resource crisis (Candidate #1) makes it a genuinely poor candidate for additional dependents
  right now, and this chain doesn't ask it to take on that role.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — In the aftermath of the war, displaced and orphaned children from three of the subnet's
destroyed cities — Esperanza, Palmer City, and Sejong — need homes and real, ongoing care. Esperanza's own
scattered diaspora, still carrying its founding values and its established expertise in robot-led caregiving,
naturally steps forward to help coordinate placement, even though Esperanza itself no longer exists as a
physical city. The player is asked to help place these children with willing, capable families and
communities across the subnet's surviving cities — Rothera and Port Lockroy — a straightforward, if
emotionally weighty, placement task.

**BUT** — Neither surviving city is an obvious fit. Rothera's own civic character is function-first and
industrial, genuinely capable of real community but never built with children's specific needs in mind. Port
Lockroy is small, historically self-absorbed, and intimate in a way that could be wonderful or claustrophobic
for a child arriving from somewhere completely different. The player can't simply place children wherever
there's room — real matching, city by city and child by child, has to account for actual fit, not just
availability.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to assess and match specific children and family groups to Rothera or Port
Lockroy based on genuine capacity and character, a real, careful process rather than a logistics exercise.

**BUT** — A deeper controversy surfaces once placement actually begins: should these children be raised
according to Esperanza's own specific caregiving philosophy and values — robots as primary caregivers, "being
from Hope" as identity — even in cities that never shared that particular history or approach, or should
Rothera and Port Lockroy each integrate the children fully according to their own local culture, letting
whatever was distinctly Esperanza (or Palmer City, or Sejong) about these children's own origin simply
become part of wherever they end up.

**THEREFORE** — What began as finding homes for displaced children becomes a real question about whose
values actually raise them, and what happens to a destroyed city's own civic identity once its children are
the only part of it still growing up.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Honoring Esperanza's own caregiving
philosophy across all placements preserves something real and precious about a destroyed city's own values,
carried forward through its most vulnerable survivors — but risks imposing a specific cultural framework on
communities and children where it may not naturally fit, potentially leaving these children perpetually
"not quite" local within whatever new community actually raises them. Letting Rothera and Port Lockroy
integrate the children fully into their own existing local culture gives them the best real chance at
belonging and an ordinary childhood — but means whatever was distinctly Esperanza, or Palmer City, or Sejong,
about where they came from simply dissolves, unremembered, within a single generation.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how placement actually proceeds: honor and actively preserve each child's own origin-city heritage
within their new home, at the cost of the exact belonging and normalcy the placement was meant to secure in
the first place; or let each receiving city fully absorb the children as its own, at the cost of the last
living thread connecting them to a city that no longer exists anywhere else.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "find homes" into
"neither receiving city is an obvious fit, and real matching takes real judgment"; the second BUT turns
"place the children" into "placing them raises a genuine question about whose values actually raise them";
the third BUT turns "preserve heritage or don't" into "there is no version of the choice that doesn't cost
either a child's own sense of belonging or the last living trace of where they came from." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Palmer subnet candidate whose stakes rest entirely on the
subnet's own most vulnerable population — displaced children from three destroyed cities — rather than on
adult civic, political, or infrastructural questions, giving DLC 3 a genuinely different emotional register
at its most consequential moments. It's also the first candidate to treat Esperanza's own established
founding charter — caring for the children of people who sacrificed everything — as something that has to be
practiced again, for real, in the present day, rather than remaining a fact about a city that no longer
exists to enact it.
