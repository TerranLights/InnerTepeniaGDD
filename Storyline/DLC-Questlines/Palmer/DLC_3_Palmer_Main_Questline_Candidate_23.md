# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #23: "Whatever the Records Can't Actually Prove"

**What this is:** a twenty-third prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around an unresolvable
historical-uncertainty dilemma threatening a community's own comfortable self-understanding. **Not canon, not
chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Port Lockroy, Palmer City, Rothera, Marambio, Signy).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Palmer Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 5** — the established, explicit proposal that Port Lockroy survived
  the Long Night War "through strategic irrelevance and probable misidentification with Palmer City's own
  larger strike zone," genuinely distinct from Rothera's deliberate, engineered decentralization and Signy's
  own raw isolation.
- **Palmer Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3** — Palmer City's own established destruction, used here to place
  it as the actual intended strike zone Port Lockroy may have been mistaken for.
- **Rothera's and Marambio's own established logistics and shipping roles** (Pattern 4), used here to justify
  their plausible custody of any surviving wartime records relevant to the investigation.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Marambio's paleontological history, or any specific claim resolving
  whether Port Lockroy's survival actually was a targeting error — this chain treats that question as
  genuinely, permanently unresolvable from the available evidence.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A historian's discovery raises a real possibility: Port Lockroy's survival through the war may
not reflect genuine strategic unimportance at all, but a targeting error — Upper Earth's forces possibly
mistook it for part of Palmer City's own larger strike zone. The player is asked to help investigate this
claim — a straightforward, if delicate, historical-research task.

**BUT** — Port Lockroy's own civic sense of security is quietly built, in part, on the comfortable assumption
that its survival reflects something true about its own safety. Confirming a targeting error, rather than
deliberate irrelevance, carries very different implications for how safe the city should actually consider
itself going forward.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to investigate carefully, consulting Port Lockroy's own community and
comparing surviving records held at Palmer City's own ruins, Rothera, and Marambio, and checking the
comparison against Signy's own genuinely confirmed survival mechanism for contrast.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the investigation reaches its limit: the surviving evidence is genuinely,
permanently ambiguous. The player, and Port Lockroy's own community, have to decide how to treat an
unresolvable question, not simply learn a hidden truth one way or the other.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a historical investigation becomes a real question about whether to publicly
acknowledge a genuine, unsettling uncertainty, or let a comforting assumption continue standing unchallenged.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Publicly acknowledging the genuine uncertainty
is honest and respects the community's own right to understand its real, unresolved risk profile — but
introduces real anxiety into a community that has, until now, felt genuinely safe, undermining a sense of
security with real, positive value in daily life. Letting the comfortable assumption stand unchallenged
preserves that sense of safety and peace of mind — but rests the community's own self-understanding on a
claim a rigorous accounting of its own history would have to flag as uncertain, not confirmed.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether the uncertain finding actually gets acknowledged: publish it, respecting the community's right
to the truth at the cost of real, ongoing anxiety; or leave the comfortable assumption unchallenged,
preserving peace of mind at the cost of resting on a claim that may not hold.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "verify a historical
claim" into "the claim threatens a comfortable assumption the community's own security depends on"; the
second BUT turns "investigate thoroughly" into "the investigation can't actually resolve the question either
way"; the third BUT turns "publish or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either honesty or the
community's own peace of mind." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at
the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Pattern 5's own explicit, hedged language — "probable
misidentification," never stated as confirmed fact — and builds a dilemma directly out of that
hedge itself, rather than resolving it one way or the other, making genuine unresolvability the actual
subject matter instead of a gap to be filled in. It's also the first Palmer subnet candidate whose central
stakes are a community's own peace of mind rather than any material resource, relationship, or policy.
