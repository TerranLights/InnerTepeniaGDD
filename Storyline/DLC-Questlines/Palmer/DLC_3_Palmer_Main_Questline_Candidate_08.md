# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #8: "Whoever Kept the List"

**What this is:** an eighth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around Juan Carlos's own established
archive/customs function — the last of Palmer subnet's 8 cities to anchor a candidate. **Not canon, not
chosen, not the only candidate** — the 8th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear
Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities (Juan Carlos, as ruins and archive)**.

**Deliberately avoids the tertulia** (flagged 2026-07-21 for possible full removal — see
`project_tertulia_flagged_for_removal` memory and `TODO.md`) **and doesn't duplicate "The Archivist's
Trail"** (the already-established DLC 1 cross-DLC bypass content built around one archivist's personal
backup notes). This chain uses a different part of Juan Carlos's own established function — the formal
archive's actual vetting records — material neither of those two already-spoken-for threads touches.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Juan Carlos's own Local Cultures, Section 21 (Political Character)** — "a quiet city with an outsized,
  largely invisible national responsibility": the original home of the Federation's immigration and customs
  records, directly linked to the Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority, tracking "anyone who had held a
  position in their home country's Upper Earth government" among the exile population itself.
- **Juan Carlos's own Full Extrapolation, Section II** — the formal archive and its Arcanet link were fully
  destroyed in the strike, consistent with denying any adversary a surviving record of the intelligence Juan
  Carlos held; what survived instead was one archivist's own off-network personal notes (already the basis
  of DLC 1's "Archivist's Trail" — explicitly not reused here).
- **Juan Carlos's own Local Cultures, Section 21** — Juan Carlos's civic character: "modest and inward
  -facing," never developing Sejong's own complex inter-community diplomatic apparatus — a quiet, coherent
  community with little established practice at the kind of open, contested negotiation this chain's climax
  requires.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about the tertulia as a civic institution or export; any content
  duplicating the specific discovery-trail already established for DLC 1's own bypass gift.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A historical-justice effort, working from whatever fragments of Juan Carlos's formal vetting
records survive in the ruins, wants to review how the city's own archive actually handled the exiles it was
built to watch — specifically, who among incoming Upper Earth government-linked arrivals was granted full
trust and citizenship, and who was flagged, restricted, or quietly denied full integration because of prior
political service. The player is asked to help recover what's left of these case files — a straightforward
historical-research task, seemingly a matter of records and dates.

**BUT** — What actually survives reveals a real, uncomfortable inconsistency. Some arrivals with genuinely
troubling pasts were quietly waved through — personal connections, simple bureaucratic error, or
plain-old favoritism — while others with far more marginal, arguably innocent involvement in an Upper Earth
government were harshly restricted, denied full standing in the very community that was supposed to be
their refuge. The clean "necessary security function" framing doesn't survive contact with the actual
records — this wasn't consistent vetting, it was uneven judgment applied unevenly, generations ago, by
people no longer around to explain themselves.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to decide what to do with the finding, since the researchers want to
identify specific individuals who were wrongly restricted and correct the record — a real act of historical
justice, if it happens.

**BUT** — Correcting these old case files means re-litigating decisions made by specific, named exile-era
officials — people whose own present-day descendants may take real, sincere pride in an ancestor's
"protective" service to a fragile young nation. Publicly correcting the record means naming those officials
as having made unjust, inconsistent calls, a genuine reputational cost landing on people who did nothing
themselves. Not correcting it means the specific individuals who were wrongly restricted — refugees denied
the exact refuge Juan Carlos's whole founding purpose was supposed to guarantee — stay exactly as
unacknowledged as they've always been, and whatever institutional stigma attached to their own descendants,
if any survives at all, stays unaddressed too.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a records-recovery task becomes a genuine act of historical justice with real,
present-day people on both sides of the ledger — the wrongly restricted and the officials who wronged them,
neither group able to speak for themselves anymore.

**BUT** — The reversal is that Juan Carlos's own established civic character (Section 21) offers no ready
model for handling this kind of open, contested reckoning — a quiet, coherent community that never
developed Sejong's own practice at inter-community negotiation has no established civic process for a fight
this genuinely public and this genuinely uncomfortable. Whatever resolution the player helps broker, the
community is doing something it has never had real practice at, for the first time, using an old city's
own leftover records as the occasion.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which way the reckoning goes: pursue full correction and public exoneration, giving genuine justice
to specific wronged individuals at the direct cost of specific named officials' own descendants' reputations
today; or let the historical record stand exactly as it's always stood, protecting those descendants and the
community's own settled peace with its history, at the cost of leaving real, specific, provable historical
injustice permanently uncorrected. Neither choice is free, and Juan Carlos's own quiet civic character — used
to being right, not used to being argued with — has to hold together through whichever one the player
chooses.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "recover old records"
into "the records reveal real, uneven injustice, not neutral bureaucratic history"; the second BUT turns
"correct the record" into "correcting it costs specific, present-day people who did nothing wrong
themselves"; the third BUT turns "decide the outcome" into "the community handling this has no established
practice at handling anything like it." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually
choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it finds new, previously-untouched dramatic material in Juan Carlos's
own established archive function without competing with either of the two threads already built from it —
the tertulia (flagged for possible removal) and "The Archivist's Trail" (already DLC 1's own established
content). It's also the first Palmer subnet candidate whose central injustice is bureaucratic and uneven
rather than a single dramatic act — the harm here was done through inconsistency and neglect, generations
ago, by people who genuinely believed they were protecting something, which makes it harder to resolve
cleanly than a single villain ever would.
