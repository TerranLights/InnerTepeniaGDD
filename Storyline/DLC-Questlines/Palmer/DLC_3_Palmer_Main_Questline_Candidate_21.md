# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #21: "The Same Distance That Kept It Safe"

**What this is:** a twenty-first prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around a communications
-infrastructure upgrade weighed against the isolation that kept a city safe. **Not canon, not chosen, not the
only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md`
§5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Signy, Rothera, Port Lockroy, Esperanza, Marambio).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Palmer Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 4** — the established, ironic double edge of Signy's own
  isolation: it survived the war specifically because it was too remote and marginal to be worth targeting,
  but that same isolation left it with the weakest Arcanet link in the Federation, reliant on old-fashioned
  radio — and post-war, that same weak link makes it hardest for Signy to actually ask for the help its
  robot population now needs.
- **Signy's own established siligel shortage** (Signy Mega-Init, "What It Feels Like"), used here as the
  genuine, ongoing humanitarian urgency driving the upgrade proposal.
- **Rothera's, Port Lockroy's, Esperanza's, and Marambio's own established, functioning roles in the
  subnet's shipping and infrastructure network**, used here only for the general fact of their capacity to
  help extend physical relay infrastructure, not for any fossil-related or other struck material.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Marambio's paleontological history — consistent with this
  project's own standing correction, Marambio appears here strictly as a functioning logistics and shipping
  city.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Signy's own robot population suffering real, ongoing harm from the siligel shortage, a plan
emerges to finally extend proper digital Arcanet infrastructure to Signy, ending its reliance on
old-fashioned radio and giving it a genuine, fast connection to the rest of the subnet. The player is asked
to help organize this infrastructure project — a straightforward, if overdue, technical-upgrade task.

**BUT** — Signy's own survival through the war traces specifically to being too remote and marginal to be
worth targeting. Extending real digital infrastructure means making Signy more visible and reachable in a way
that could, in principle, undo the very obscurity that kept it safe.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to consult Signy's own community about whether they actually want this
trade-off, and travel to Rothera and Port Lockroy to help plan and build the actual relay infrastructure that
would extend toward Signy.

**BUT** — The reversal deepens once the current crisis is weighed directly: Signy's robots are already
suffering real, ongoing harm because the weak link makes it too hard to coordinate the siligel shipments they
need. Delaying the upgrade to preserve obscurity leaves that harm unaddressed for longer than necessary.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a technical-upgrade decision becomes a real choice between solving an urgent,
present crisis and protecting a long-term security advantage that has already proven itself once.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Upgrading Signy's connection quickly and fully
solves the urgent siligel crisis and gives Signy a genuine, lasting connection to the rest of the subnet —
but permanently increases its visibility and reachability, undoing the specific obscurity that kept it safe
through the war, a real and lasting security cost. Holding off, or implementing only a limited, reversible
fix, protects that long-term security by preserving Signy's obscurity — but leaves its current population's
real, ongoing crisis unaddressed for longer than it needs to be.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in what actually gets built: the full upgrade, solving the present crisis at the cost of a lasting
security exposure; or a limited, reversible fix, protecting that security at the cost of prolonging real,
ongoing harm to Signy's own robot population.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "upgrade the
connection" into "the upgrade undoes the exact thing that kept Signy safe"; the second BUT turns "consult the
community" into "the current crisis is real and ongoing, not a hypothetical to weigh calmly"; the third BUT
turns "upgrade fully or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either lasting security or
prolonged, real harm." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end,
so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Throughway 4's own explicitly flagged irony — the trait that
kept Signy safe is the same trait now standing between it and rescue — and turns it into an actual, playable
choice rather than leaving it as descriptive commentary. It's also the first Palmer subnet candidate to treat
a city's own physical connectivity, rather than a resource, a policy, or a relationship, as the direct object
of the player's final decision.
