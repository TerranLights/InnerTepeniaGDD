# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #16: "Nobody Meant for This to Spread"

**What this is:** a sixteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around a sixth distinct
linking mechanism — an epidemiological trace along the subnet's own siligel supply chain, rather than a
physical route (#11), a functional retrospective (#12), a supply coalition (#13), a comparative civic theme
(#14), or a child-welfare/heritage thread (#15). **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — a
sixteenth possibility, beyond this subnet's own already-complete set of ten. Uses the Linear Escalation
Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Rothera, Marambio, Esperanza, Port Lockroy, Signy) — a production
node, two distribution nodes, and two consuming cities where symptoms first surface.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Rothera's own established industrial role** — the Palmer subnet's own manufacturing center, producing
  materials and components distributed across the whole subnet.
- **Marambio's and Esperanza's own established shipping division of labor** — Marambio handling bulk,
  high-volume cargo, Esperanza handling smaller-scale, specific shipments, together forming the subnet's
  actual distribution network.
- **Signy's own established siligel scarcity** (Candidate #1's own grounding) — a city already dependent on
  outside supply for its robot population's basic needs, with no local production of its own, making it a
  genuine consuming endpoint for whatever actually reaches it.
- **Port Lockroy's own small, intimate population** — Tepenia's second-smallest city, where any localized
  health event would be immediately, visibly significant given how few residents there are to begin with.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim that this contamination connects to Signy's own original siligel
  shortage (Candidate #1) — this is a distinct, later event: a supply-chain quality problem, not a supply
  -chain volume problem.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Reports surface of robots falling ill or malfunctioning after consuming siligel in multiple,
seemingly unconnected cities across the subnet — Port Lockroy and Signy among them. The player is asked to
trace the contamination back to its actual source before it spreads further or causes serious harm — a
straightforward, if urgent, investigative task.

**BUT** — The siligel supply chain crosses multiple cities and stages — production at Rothera, distribution
through Marambio's and Esperanza's own established shipping roles, consumption wherever it finally arrives —
and post-war, the normal quality-control and traceability systems that would exist in peacetime simply don't.
The investigation has to be built from scratch, city by city, with no existing paper trail to shortcut the
work.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to visit each stage of the chain in sequence, gathering evidence and ruling
each city's own potential role in or out, one link at a time.

**BUT** — Once the actual source is identified, the reversal lands: it isn't sabotage or negligence. Someone
at one stage of the chain — plausibly at Rothera's own production end — implemented a genuine, well
-intentioned ingredient or process substitution specifically to cope with real post-war material scarcity,
without realizing it carried this harmful effect. The person responsible acted in good faith, under real
duress, trying to keep the supply moving at all when the proper materials simply weren't available.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a contamination investigation becomes a real question about accountability:
whether to publicly identify and correct the responsible practice, or handle it quietly.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Publicly identifying and correcting the
specific substitution stops the contamination definitively and protects every robot depending on this supply
chain from the same harm in the future — but publicly, uncomfortably blames someone for a genuine,
resourceful attempt to help everyone cope with real scarcity, risking a chilling effect on exactly the kind
of improvisation keeping the wider subnet's own strained post-war systems functioning at all. Quietly fixing
the problem without public attribution protects that person from unfair blame for a good-faith mistake and
preserves the culture of resourcefulness scarcity has forced on everyone — but means the wider subnet never
learns why this actually happened, leaving real risk that someone else, somewhere else, makes the identical
well-intentioned mistake without ever knowing the danger.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how the finding gets handled: identify and correct the practice publicly, protecting the whole supply
chain going forward at the cost of a specific, real person's unfair public blame for trying to help; or fix
it quietly, protecting that person completely at the cost of leaving the rest of the subnet's own
improvised, scarcity-driven systems exactly as exposed to the same risk as they were before anyone knew to
look for it.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "trace a contamination"
into "there's no existing paper trail, the whole investigation has to be built from nothing"; the second BUT
turns "find the source" into "the source is a good-faith mistake born of real scarcity, not negligence or
malice"; the third BUT turns "correct it" into "there is no version of correcting it that doesn't cost either
a real person's unfair blame or the wider subnet's own awareness of a real, ongoing risk." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Palmer subnet candidate structured as a genuine
investigative mystery, tracing a problem backward through a supply chain rather than following a route,
reconstructing a lost function, building a coalition, comparing civic cultures, or placing displaced people.
It's also the first candidate whose antagonist, such as it is, isn't a villain or an institution but a single
person's own well-intentioned improvisation under real scarcity — making the actual climax about whether
accountability and safety are worth the cost of discouraging the exact resourcefulness the whole post-war
subnet currently depends on to survive.
