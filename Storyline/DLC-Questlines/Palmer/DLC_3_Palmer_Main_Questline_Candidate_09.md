# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #9: "Whoever Gets to Ask"

**What this is:** a ninth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around the subnet's own flagged
cross-DLC connection to Byrd. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 9th of the "at least
10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 2 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Rothera, living and present-day; Palmer City, referenced as
destroyed, off-screen) — Byrd (a different DLC's own single city, not yet developed) is referenced only
through what's already established about it, not developed further here.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Palmer Subnet Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 1** — Palmer City and Rothera both contributed
  historical records and maps that helped the Belgrano-led expedition locate Byrd Station; Rothera's own
  decentralized industrial model, or its surviving engineers' records, plausibly shaped Byrd's early
  industrial planning directly. Because Palmer City was thoroughly destroyed while Byrd was not, Byrd's own
  archives may now hold the only surviving primary-source material about pre-war Palmer City anywhere in
  Tepenia — making Byrd, in a real sense, a Palmer subnet creation twice over, and simultaneously the one
  place a player could stumble onto genuine documentary history about a completely different,
  already-destroyed DLC's own city.
- **Rothera's own Full Extrapolation, Section XI** — did Rothera's industrial model get exported elsewhere?
  Yes, tentatively, to Byrd — Rothera's own engineers, or their surviving records, plausibly assisted Byrd's
  early industrial planning, though this remains undeveloped and informal rather than institutionally
  documented on Rothera's own side.
- **Established, project-wide fact** — Palmer City evacuees in Concordia are unusually scarce, "the single
  most consequential person the city ever produced is also one of the very few who ever successfully left it"
  (Palmer City Full Extrapolation, Section III, re: Solenne Vaskarre) — meaning Palmer City's own diaspora
  has little standing, presence, or leverage anywhere in present-day Tepenia to reclaim its own lost history.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any new development of Byrd's own present-day culture, institutions, or attitude
  toward outside researchers beyond what's already established (a genuine industrial center with underground
  fabrication and "genuine community," per Throughway 1) — this chain treats Byrd's own response as a real,
  open question the player has to actually navigate, not a predetermined yes or no.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A researcher trying to recover any surviving primary-source material about destroyed Palmer
City — vanishingly rare anywhere in Tepenia, given how few evacuees ever reached Concordia — traces a real,
if half-forgotten, lead through Rothera: Rothera's own decentralized industrial model, or its engineers'
surviving records, plausibly shaped Byrd's early industrial planning, meaning Byrd's own archives might hold
genuine documentary history about Palmer City that exists nowhere else. The player is asked to help follow
the lead, starting with Rothera itself.

**BUT** — Rothera's own established civic character — function-first, never self-mythologizing, energy
going into building rather than remembering — means it never treated its own contribution to Byrd's founding
as something worth preserving as formal institutional memory. What Rothera actually holds is scattered,
informal, possibly already lost to individual families and old work logs rather than any archive built to
survive scrutiny. Simply confirming the connection is real work before anyone can even think about reaching
Byrd's own records with any confidence.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to reconstruct the Rothera-to-Byrd connection from fragments, piecing
together enough of a real, credible thread before there's anything solid enough to bring to Byrd at all.

**BUT** — Once the connection is genuinely established, a harder truth surfaces. Whatever Palmer City
material Byrd's own archives might hold sits inside a completely different subnet, inside an active,
functioning present-day city with its own priorities and its own genuine community — and Palmer City's own
descendants, scattered and vanishingly rare, have no real standing or leverage to simply claim access to
someone else's archive for their own city's sake. The question stops being "can this material be found" and
becomes "whose call is it whether anyone gets to look."

**THEREFORE** — What began as a research trail becomes a real question about ownership: does a destroyed
city's own scattered descendants have a claim on records another, unrelated city happens to hold, or is
that archive Byrd's own to control, regardless of what it might contain.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Pressing the claim on Palmer City's behalf —
insisting Byrd grant access, whatever the actual negotiation looks like — could restore genuine, otherwise
permanently lost memory to a destroyed city's own descendants, a real act of cultural recovery. But it also
treats Byrd's own archive as a resource that exists for someone else's history, not respecting that Byrd
has never asked to be anyone's inheritance and has its own community, its own priorities, and its own say
over its own records. Respecting Byrd's own autonomy and not pressing the claim honors that sovereignty
completely — but leaves Palmer City's own scattered descendants without the one substantive lead to their
lost heritage that might exist anywhere in the country.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how the request is actually made: press firmly for access on Palmer City's behalf, prioritizing
historical recovery for a destroyed city's own people at the cost of treating Byrd's own archive as
available to outside claims regardless of Byrd's own wishes; or defer entirely to whatever Byrd itself
decides, honoring its full ownership of its own history at the cost of leaving Palmer City's own last
possible lead unpursued. Neither choice resolves who a city's memory actually belongs to once that memory
ends up, by historical accident, inside someone else's walls.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "follow a research
lead" into "the lead itself has to be reconstructed from fragments before it's usable at all"; the second
BUT turns "confirm the connection" into "confirming it raises a real ownership question neither side has
ever had to answer"; the third BUT turns "get access" into "there is no version of asking that doesn't
either impose on Byrd or abandon Palmer City's own last lead." Deleting any one Complication changes what
the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes the single most significant item the subnet's own
Cross-City Throughways file flagged — a genuine, documented link reaching all the way into a different
DLC's own single city — and builds a full main-questline chain around the ownership question that link
quietly raises, without inventing any new content for Byrd itself. It's also the first Palmer subnet
candidate whose climax depends entirely on a city outside the subnet's own control, meaning the resolution
the player reaches is never fully in Palmer subnet's own hands to begin with — a genuinely different kind of
powerlessness than any of this set's other candidates have required.
