# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #4: "Somewhere to Go Again"

**What this is:** a fourth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around Rothera's own established
underground vault network — untouched material distinct from Signy's siligel crisis (Candidate #1), Sejong's
historical-interpretation dilemma (Candidate #2), and Esperanza's registry triage (Candidate #3). **Not
canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 4th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the
Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities (Rothera alone)**.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Rothera's own Full Extrapolation, Section III** — the underground vaults grew gradually over
  generations, feasible specifically because of Adelaide Island's mountainous terrain, and served a real,
  direct wartime function sheltering Rothera's population during the war's worst strikes — a second,
  human-scale layer of resilience alongside the city's industrial dispersal: "the people survived because
  they had somewhere to go."
- **Rothera's own Full Extrapolation, Section II** — Rothera manufactures vehicle and transit-infrastructure
  components distributed via Hwy 1 to every other Palmer subnet city, "literally building the network it's
  part of" — establishing real, subnet-wide pressure for Rothera to expand its own present-day manufacturing
  and housing capacity, a natural source of demand for repurposing underused space.
- **Rothera's own Full Extrapolation, Section I** — the runway remains only partially restored, since
  post-war reconstruction prioritized industrial output first, establishing precedent that Rothera's own
  civic planners already make real, felt trade-offs between competing post-war priorities rather than
  restoring everything at once.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about whether a comparable future crisis is actually likely — this
  chain treats the underlying uncertainty (will the vaults ever be needed as shelter again) as genuinely
  unresolvable in-story, not something the player or this document quietly settles one way or the other.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With subnet-wide demand for Rothera's manufactured goods growing and its own population swelling
with refugees from destroyed neighboring cities, Rothera's civic planners propose repurposing a substantial
portion of the underground vault network — previously reserved wartime shelter space — into permanent
housing and expanded manufacturing capacity. The player is asked to help survey the vaults and facilitate
the conversion — a straightforward capacity-expansion task, addressing a real, pressing present-day need.

**BUT** — A real, vocal segment of Rothera's own population resists, and it isn't simple nostalgia. The
vaults are the specific reason "the people survived because they had somewhere to go" during the war's
worst strikes, and the peace, however welcome, is recent and unproven. Giving up dedicated shelter capacity
for permanent, hard-to-reverse peacetime use is a real gamble on the city's own future safety, made by people
who remember exactly what having nowhere to go would have cost them.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually investigate how much of the vault network can be converted
without meaningfully compromising Rothera's own emergency-shelter capacity — weighing a real, present, and
urgent need against a real, if currently unrealized, future risk.

**BUT** — The investigation surfaces a genuinely hard truth rather than a clean technical answer. Because
the vaults grew gradually over generations rather than being built to one consistent specification, there's
no clean way to designate "this section is shelter, that section is expansion" with real confidence. Some
converted space could plausibly still function adequately in an emergency; some couldn't; and nobody can be
fully certain which is which without an actual crisis testing it — an organic, structural ambiguity native
to how the vaults were actually built, not a question anyone deliberately left open.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a capacity-survey task becomes a genuine wager: how much of Rothera's own
uncertain future safety is the city willing to convert into certain, immediate present-day benefit.

**BUT** — The reversal is that both outcomes carry real, foreseeable cost. Converting a substantial portion
of the vaults addresses genuine, current housing and industrial need — refugees who need homes now, subnet
-wide production the whole of Palmer subnet actually depends on — but gambles Rothera's own proven
emergency-preparedness on optimistic assumptions about which converted sections could still serve if needed,
assumptions nobody can actually verify short of living through another crisis. Preserving most of the vault
network's shelter readiness protects against a recurrence nobody can rule out — but leaves real, current
needs unmet for people who need space now, not hypothetically, someday, if the worst happens again.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how far the conversion actually goes: prioritize present-day housing and industrial capacity,
accepting genuine uncertainty about Rothera's own readiness for whatever comes next; or preserve the vault
network's proven shelter function at real, felt cost to people with urgent, current needs the city could
otherwise meet. Neither choice resolves the underlying uncertainty about whether the peace holds — it only
decides how much of Rothera's own hard-won security the city is willing to spend finding out.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "convert unused space"
into "the space isn't unused, it's the reason people survived the war"; the second BUT turns "find a safe
conversion plan" into "there's no way to know with confidence which converted sections would still work in
an emergency"; the third BUT turns "decide how much to convert" into "there is no version of the decision
that doesn't gamble something real, whichever way it goes." Deleting any one Complication changes what the
player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it turns Rothera's own genuinely moving established fact — that its
underground vaults gave people somewhere to go during the war's worst strikes — into a live, present-day
prudential dilemma rather than leaving it as settled backstory: having survived once because it had
somewhere to go, does Rothera bet on not needing that again. It's also the first Palmer subnet candidate
whose central tension is precautionary rather than distributive — not who gets a scarce resource first
(Candidates #1 and #3), but how much of a hard-won safety margin a city is willing to spend on certain,
present benefit against an uncertain, unprovable future risk.
