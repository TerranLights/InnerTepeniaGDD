# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #20: "The Story Isn't Finished Yet"

**What this is:** a twentieth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around a tenth distinct
linking mechanism — consolidating a single, subnet-wide historical archive — closing out this particular run
of "at least 5 cities" candidates (#11 through #20) the way each subnet's own Candidate #10 closed its
original ten-candidate set. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation
Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses at least 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Esperanza, Palmer City, Sejong — three destroyed cities
whose own history is genuinely contested; Rothera, Signy — two surviving cities whose own recent choices are
part of that same contested record) — and, by its own nature, naturally extends to draw on Marambio, Juan
Carlos, and Port Lockroy as well, since a subnet-wide archive has no honest reason to exclude any of them.

**A deliberate closing note.** This candidate is the only one in the "5-city" run built specifically to
reflect on the other nine rather than introduce a wholly separate stake — it treats the actual, unresolved
moral questions those nine candidates each raised (whose currency backing was fair, Candidate #19; whether
tolerating the black market was right, #18; how representation should be weighted, #17) as the very
material this chain's own archive would have to contend with.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **This subnet's own established devastation** — five of eight cities destroyed, three survivors, and a
  scattered diaspora, all already carrying genuinely different, sometimes conflicting relationships to what
  actually happened and what it meant, as established across this whole candidate set.
- **The genuinely unresolved nature of this project's own No Good Endings law, applied cumulatively** — none
  of Candidates #11 through #19 resolve into a clean, settled consensus; each ends in a real, live trade-off,
  meaning the subnet's own recent history contains multiple genuinely still-contested decisions, not a single
  agreed-upon narrative.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about which specific outcome any of Candidates #11 through #19 actually
  reached — this chain treats those outcomes as still genuinely open, whichever way any individual playthrough
  or design pass ultimately resolves them.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A genuine effort emerges to build a single, definitive Palmer subnet historical archive —
consolidating records, testimony, and artifacts from across the whole subnet, destroyed cities and survivors
alike, into one coherent account, both for the subnet's own scattered diaspora and for the rest of Tepenia to
properly understand what actually happened here. The player is asked to help gather contributions from
multiple communities — a straightforward, if large-scale, "build the definitive record" task.

**BUT** — Different communities hold genuinely different, sometimes conflicting accounts of the very same
recent events and choices — not ancient, settled history, but live, still-contested memory: whether the
subnet's own new currency standard was fair or quietly favored one city, whether tolerating a black market
that was actually delivering resources was right or wrong, whether diaspora representation was weighted
fairly against the survivors who actually live with the consequences. A single "definitive" account isn't
achievable without someone deciding whose version of a still-open argument gets to be the official one.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to navigate genuinely competing, current narratives from different
communities about their own shared, recent, unfinished history — not reconciling the past, but arbitrating
the present.

**BUT** — The reversal lands directly: whichever version becomes "the archive" doesn't just record history,
it settles it — for the rest of Tepenia's understanding, and for the subnet's own future generations. Genuine,
still-live disagreements the subnet's own communities haven't actually finished having yet would be closed
by the act of writing them down as though they were already resolved.

**THEREFORE** — What began as building a historical record becomes a real decision about whether the
subnet's own story is actually ready to be told as one story at all.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. A single, authoritative account gives the
subnet and the rest of Tepenia a clear, usable historical record — useful for outside understanding, and for
whatever closure the subnet's own communities might want — but risks prematurely settling genuinely
unresolved internal arguments, imposing a false consensus where real, sincere disagreement still exists. A
deliberately multi-vocal archive — presenting the real disagreements side by side rather than picking a
single account — is honest about the fact that the subnet's own story isn't finished being decided, but hands
the rest of Tepenia, and the subnet's own future generations, a messier, harder-to-use record of what
actually happened and what it meant.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which kind of archive actually gets built: one settled, authoritative account, at the cost of closing
arguments the subnet's own people haven't actually finished having; or a genuinely multi-vocal record, honest
about live disagreement, at the cost of leaving the rest of Tepenia, and the subnet's own future, without a
single clear answer to point to.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "gather the history"
into "the history is still actively contested, not settled"; the second BUT turns "write it down" into
"writing it down settles arguments that are still genuinely live"; the third BUT turns "pick a format" into
"there is no format that gives the subnet both a usable answer and an honest one at the same time." Deleting
any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised
And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the only one in this run of ten built to look back at the other
nine rather than stand entirely on its own — treating the genuinely unresolved trade-offs Candidates #11
through #19 each ended on as the actual, lived material a subnet-wide archive would have to contend with,
rather than settled facts waiting to be filed away. It closes this particular exercise the same way each
subnet's own Candidate #10 closed its original set: by asking what the whole collection of stories actually
adds up to, rather than adding one more story next to the rest.
