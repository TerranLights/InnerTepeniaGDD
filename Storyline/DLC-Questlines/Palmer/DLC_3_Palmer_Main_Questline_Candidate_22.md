# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #22: "Whatever Hope Used to Carry"

**What this is:** a twenty-second prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around filling a permanent gap
in the subnet's own logistics system left by a destroyed city. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Rothera, Port Lockroy, Marambio, Esperanza, Signy).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Palmer Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 4** — the established, previously-unstated four-part logistics
  division of labor across the subnet: Rothera moving materials, Port Lockroy moving words, Marambio moving
  bulk volume, and Esperanza moving specific, individually-tracked shipments.
- **Palmer Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3** — Esperanza's own established destruction as a morale strike,
  used here to establish that its own logistics function is genuinely gone, not merely disrupted.
- **Esperanza's own established founding charter** (Palmer Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 3) — raising
  its children to "push further into Antarctica, in the name of robots and humans hand-in-hand," used here to
  characterize the specific, individualized care behind its former logistics role, not simply its volume.
- **Signy's own established siligel shortage** (Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 4), used here to give a
  concrete, present-day stake in whether specific, individually-tracked shipments actually get handled
  reliably going forward.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Marambio's paleontological history — Marambio appears here only in
  its established shipping-and-logistics-hub capacity.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Esperanza destroyed, the subnet's once-complete four-part logistics system has quietly lost
one whole function — individually-tracked, higher-value cargo now has no dedicated handler, forcing
unreliable, ad hoc workarounds. The player is asked to help figure out which city should formally absorb this
lost function — a straightforward, if overdue, logistics-reorganization task.

**BUT** — Rothera, Port Lockroy, and Marambio each already have their own well-established, distinct
roles that don't naturally overlap with what Esperanza used to handle. Taking on this additional
responsibility would mean any of the three genuinely diversifying beyond its own established specialty, a
real operational shift, not simply picking up extra work.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to consult all three surviving logistics cities directly, and travel to Signy
to understand exactly what's actually at stake for a city that depends on specific, reliably-tracked
shipments reaching it at all.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the deeper issue surfaces: Esperanza's own "specific, documented shipments"
role wasn't just a logistics category — it reflected the same individualized care its own founding charter
was built around. None of the three surviving cities share that particular civic disposition. Whichever one
takes on the function would be performing a role it doesn't organically embody — the function could survive
mechanically while losing the specific quality that made it meaningful.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a straightforward reassignment becomes a real question about whether a
function can actually be replaced at all, once the city that gave it meaning is gone.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Formally assigning the function to whichever
city is the best practical fit restores a genuinely useful, missing logistics capability for the whole
subnet — but turns what was once a specific expression of Esperanza's own particular care into an ordinary
bureaucratic add-on for a city that doesn't organically share that disposition, a real loss of meaning even as
the function itself survives. Leaving the gap unfilled honestly acknowledges that nothing can truly replace
what Esperanza specifically was — but leaves the subnet's own logistics system permanently incomplete, with
real, ongoing costs for cities like Signy that need specific, individually-tracked shipments handled
reliably.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether the function actually gets formally reassigned or left unfilled: reassign it, restoring real
capability at the cost of turning a once-meaningful role into an ordinary bureaucratic task; or leave it
unfilled, honoring what can't truly be replaced at the cost of real, ongoing unreliability for cities that
depend on it.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "assign the role" into
"none of the three cities can take it on without genuinely diversifying beyond their own specialty"; the
second BUT turns "pick the best fit" into "the role was never just a logistics category, and no surviving
city shares what made it meaningful"; the third BUT turns "reassign or leave it" into "there is no option that
doesn't cost either real meaning or real reliability." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player
is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Pattern 4's own quietly significant discovery — a complete,
four-part logistics system nobody in the lore had ever named as a single system — and asks what happens once
one of its four parts is permanently gone, treating the resulting gap as an actual dramatic stake rather than
leaving it as an unstated absence. It's also the first Palmer subnet candidate to treat a destroyed city's own
lost function, rather than its lost population or archive, as the specific thing a later questline has to
reckon with.
