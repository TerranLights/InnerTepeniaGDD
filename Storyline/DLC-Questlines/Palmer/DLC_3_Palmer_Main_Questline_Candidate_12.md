# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #12: "Nobody Ever Compared Notes"

**What this is:** a twelfth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around a functional throughline rather
than physical geography this time — the five destroyed cities that together, unknowingly, constituted
Tepenia's actual working apparatus for contact with Upper Earth. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate** — a twelfth possibility, beyond this subnet's own already-complete set of ten (and beyond
Candidate #11's own physical-route chain). Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities** (Juan Carlos, Marambio, Esperanza, Palmer City, Sejong) — the five
destroyed cities whose own established functions, combined, formed the practical machinery of Tepenia's
relationship to Upper Earth: customs and vetting (Juan Carlos), receiving and shipping (Marambio),
trans-shipment (Esperanza), tourism and entertainment (Palmer City), and the lived, ongoing demonstration of
Tepenian multinational coexistence to any Upper Earth observer (Sejong). Only one city overlaps with
Candidate #11's own cast (three of that chain's five: Esperanza, Marambio, Palmer City); Port Lockroy and
Rothera are absent here, replaced by Juan Carlos and Sejong — a genuinely different combination built on
function, not physical route.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Juan Carlos's own established function** — the origin site of Tepenia's first bureaucratic archive,
  directly linked to the Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority, tracking and vetting arrivals (Local
  Cultures, Section 21; already used differently in Candidate #8).
- **Marambio's own established function** — the confirmed receiving node for South American shipping,
  Tepenia's Palmer-subnet Upper Earth trade partner (Local Cultures, Section 5, post-fossil-strike
  identity).
- **Esperanza's own established function** — a genuine trans-shipment role in the same Weddell Sea corridor,
  complementary to Marambio's own bulk-shipping identity (Marambio Full Extrapolation, Section VI).
- **Palmer City's own established function** — the tourism mechanism, formalized (Full Extrapolation,
  Section I): the "open secret" economy receiving Upper Earth visitors seeking freedoms harder to access at
  home.
- **Sejong's own established function** — a genuinely uncertain, ongoing experiment in whether nearly a
  dozen nations could share one small island in permanent working proximity, a living demonstration of
  Tepenian coexistence any Upper Earth observer could see directly (Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section I).
- **Explicitly excluded:** any duplication of "The Archivist's Trail" (Juan Carlos's own personal-notes
  survival, already DLC 1 content) or Sejong's own already-resolved interpretive dilemma (Candidate #2) — this
  chain treats each city's function at a structural level, not re-litigating either candidate's own specific
  climax.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A modern effort wants to properly document and understand Tepenia's own lost, pre-war apparatus
for contact with Upper Earth — not necessarily to rebuild it, but because so much practical, institutional
knowledge about how it actually worked died along with the five cities that each ran one piece of it. The
player is sent to gather what remains at each site — customs records at Juan Carlos, shipping logs at
Marambio, trans-shipment documentation at Esperanza, tourism-economy records at Palmer City, and the
coexistence experiment's own working history at Sejong — a straightforward, if far-reaching, research task.

**BUT** — Each site's own loss is different in kind, and no single method works twice. Juan Carlos's
formal records are destroyed, its surviving trace off-network and personal. Marambio's own receiving
infrastructure is gone, its causeway now a bridge to nowhere. Esperanza's records survive only in fragments.
Palmer City's ruins are uniquely hazardous, actively decaying rather than preserved. Sejong's own experiment
never actually concluded, its own success still genuinely contested. The player has to solve five entirely
different problems, not one repeated task five times.

**THEREFORE** — The player adapts a different approach at each site, treating the research as five distinct
puzzles that only make sense together once all five are actually assembled.

**BUT** — Once enough pieces are gathered, the reversal lands: the full picture reveals this was never a
coordinated system at all. Five separate cities each ran one function, largely unaware of exactly how
central their own piece was to the country's whole relationship with the outside world, and none of them
ever formally compared notes with each other about it. Tepenia's actual interface with Upper Earth worked
because five uncoordinated pieces happened to add up to something functional — not because anyone designed
it that way — and its total loss is, in the same sense, more structurally significant than anyone currently
understands, precisely because nobody understood how it worked while it was still standing.

**THEREFORE** — The question stops being "what did we lose" and becomes "what do we do with knowing exactly
how fragile and uncoordinated the whole thing actually was."

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Recommending a deliberately coordinated,
centralized replacement — consolidating these functions somewhere that can actually talk to itself this time
— builds something more genuinely robust and efficient than what existed before, but recreates exactly the
single-point-of-vulnerability risk that made concentrated, high-value functions targets across every subnet
this project has examined so far, this time by conscious design rather than accident. Recommending a
deliberately distributed replacement, spreading the same functions back across multiple sites the way they
accidentally were before, avoids creating one obvious target — but repeats the same lack of coordination and
mutual unawareness that meant nobody realized how significant or interdependent these five cities actually
were until all five were already gone.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
recommendation for how Tepenia rebuilds its own relationship with Upper Earth going forward: a coordinated,
centralized system, genuinely more functional and self-aware than the one that existed before, at the cost of
recreating a real, concentrated vulnerability; or a deliberately distributed system, echoing the old
apparatus's own accidental resilience through fragmentation, at the cost of repeating the exact
lack-of-coordination that left nobody understanding what was actually at stake until it was already lost.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "gather records" into
"each site demands a genuinely different method, not one repeated task"; the second BUT turns "assemble the
full picture" into "the full picture reveals an uncoordinated system nobody understood while it existed";
the third BUT turns "recommend a rebuild" into "there is no version of the recommendation that doesn't
recreate either a concentrated vulnerability or the original lack of coordination." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Palmer subnet candidate to link its five cities by
function rather than geography, and to conclude that the subnet's own devastating loss wasn't just about
what died at each individual site, but about a *system* nobody at the time even knew existed, made visible
only in hindsight, only after every piece of it was already gone. It's also the first candidate in this
whole project whose central choice is a genuine infrastructure-design trade-off — concentration versus
distribution — informed directly by the accumulated damage-pattern logic this project has built up across
every prior subnet, rather than a personal, civic, or historical-memory question.
