# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #5: "Nobody's Had to Name It Yet"

**What this is:** a fifth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around Marambio's own established
human-robot dockworker culture — untouched by Candidates #1–#4, and built entirely on material that
survived the 2026-07-21 fossil/paleontology strike (see `TODO.md`'s "RESOLVED — Marambio
Fossil/Paleontology Strike" and `Marambio_Identity_Rename_Options.md`). **Not canon, not chosen, not the
only candidate** — the 5th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain
schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities (Marambio alone)**.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Marambio's own Local Cultures, Section 16** — many human dockworkers are in relationships with robot
  coworkers, a lived, ordinary version of the project's established Bonded Lattice themes; given ordinary
  human lifespans against robots' much longer ones, a robot dockworker would very plausibly outlive a
  human partner — "a quiet fact of life this city's own culture has never had the reflective habit to
  name."
- **Marambio's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 2** — Marambio may function as an "unexamined
  Janbogo": the same founding-institutional-persistence pattern Janbogo celebrates openly, happening
  inside Marambio too, but never noticed or named, consistent with a city whose whole civic energy goes
  into function rather than self-reflection.
- **Marambio's own Local Cultures, Section 18** — no dominant formal religion, no established ceremonial
  vocabulary for personal life events; the closest shared value is a practical ethic of motion and
  connectivity.
- **Marambio's own Local Cultures, Section 20** — private, personal practice is where identity lives at
  Marambio; the public civic face is organized entirely around function, not around any part of personal
  or communal life.
- **Marambio's own Local Cultures, Section 6f** — the one genuine, established shared ritual the docks
  already have: smoke breaks together during quiet stretches between ship dockings, "a small, genuine
  ritual of shared rest inside an otherwise constantly-moving city" — the only existing civic vocabulary
  this chain has to build from.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim that this chain resolves Marambio's own flagged Post-Culture Identity
  rename question (still open, see `Marambio_Identity_Rename_Options.md`) — this chain doesn't depend on
  or reference whichever name is eventually chosen.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The player gets pulled into the life of a specific, long-partnered human-robot dockworker
couple: the human partner is aging, nearing the natural end of a full life, and wants one concrete thing
before the end — some real, lasting acknowledgment of the partnership, since Marambio's own culture has
never once had occasion to formally recognize anything like it. It looks like a small, personal favor:
help arrange something fitting.

**BUT** — Marambio's civic character has no vocabulary for this at all. No dominant religion, no
ceremonial holidays built around personal life, a public civic face organized entirely around function
(Section 20) with private life kept entirely private. The player can't borrow an existing ritual form —
there isn't one. Whatever gets arranged has to be built from nothing, or as close to nothing as any
Tepenian city's culture gets.

**THEREFORE** — The player works with the dockworker community itself to figure out what an honest
recognition would even look like, drawing on the one piece of real, shared civic vocabulary the docks
already have: the smoke-break ritual, the closest thing to communal life Marambio's own function-first
culture ever developed.

**BUT** — Word spreads, and the reversal lands: this couple isn't unique. A real, previously invisible
number of human-robot dockworker partnerships across the shipyard are living through, or have already
lived through, this same asymmetry — each handling it entirely alone, with no shared language, no
acknowledgment, and no reason to think anyone else understood. What looked like one small, personal
request turns out to be the first time this specific, widespread experience has ever surfaced at all.

**THEREFORE** — The question stops being "arrange one recognition" and becomes whether Marambio should
build something ongoing — real, lasting institutional space for an experience its own population has
apparently been carrying, unsupported, for generations.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Building that lasting space means
Marambio actually examining itself for the first time — becoming, in some small way, self-aware the way
Janbogo already is about its own founding persistence (Cross-Reference Finding 2) — genuinely helpful to
every future robot partner facing this same loss, but a real, permanent change to a civic character whose
entire established identity has always run on *not* doing that. Keeping the recognition small and
specific to this one couple honors Marambio's own established, unexamined, function-first character
exactly as it's always been — but leaves the next robot partner to go through the same loss exactly as
alone as everyone who came before them.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's
own role in how far this goes: help formalize a real, ongoing recognition — built out of the docks' own
smoke-break ritual rather than anything imported — giving future partners genuine support, at the cost of
permanently altering the one thing that's always defined Marambio, its refusal to examine itself; or keep
it small, private, and specific to the one couple who asked, honoring the city's own established
character faithfully, at the cost of leaving every future robot partner to carry this exact same loss
completely alone, the way everyone before them always has.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "arrange a small
favor" into "there's no existing form to build it from"; the second BUT turns "one couple's recognition"
into "an entire invisible community has been carrying this alone"; the third BUT turns "build something
lasting" into "doing so changes the one thing that's always defined this city, and not doing so leaves a
real, ongoing harm exactly as unaddressed as it's always been." Deleting any one Complication changes
what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Marambio's own established fact — a quiet asymmetry its
culture "has never had the reflective habit to name" — and turns the act of naming it into the climax
itself, rather than treating the asymmetry as background flavor. It's also the first Palmer subnet
candidate to test the specific tension the Cross-Reference Synthesis already flagged as unique to
Marambio: a founding character built entirely around function, never self-reflection, put under real
pressure to become, at least once, the second thing instead of the first.
