# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #1: "Untouched, Not Unaffected"

**What this is:** a first prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate** — a worked demonstration of the method, covering exactly one of the "at least 10" possibilities
that method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md`
§5a).

**Builds directly on an already-confirmed DLC 3 hook** — Signy's siligel shortage, explicitly named in its
own Full Extrapolation as "the actual DLC 3 questline hook, as the Specs file itself already suggests as a
strong candidate," the closest thing Palmer subnet has to Halley subnet's own Troll Airfield anchor.

**DLC 3's own character and storyline remain TBD** (`TODO.md`), the same undesigned-protagonist situation as
DLC 6 — this candidate is necessarily protagonist-agnostic.

**Uses 1 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities (Signy alone)** — the wider subnet's siligel distribution network is
referenced as established fact, not developed as new city-scale content.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Signy's own Full Extrapolation, Section IV** — the siligel shortage: real but not yet critical by the
  time a player could plausibly reach Signy, on a genuine countdown of weeks to a couple of months of reserve
  supply remaining — enough for real narrative urgency without demanding instant catastrophe. Signy's whole
  "untouched but not unaffected" identity given concrete, playable shape: a city that looks fine on arrival,
  until the player learns what's actually running out beneath the surface.
- **Palmer Subnet Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 4** — Signy survived specifically because it was too
  remote and marginal to be worth targeting, but that same isolation left it with the weakest Arcanet link in
  the Federation, reliant on old-fashioned radio. Post-war, that isolation now threatens the robot population
  specifically — siligel cannot be produced or improvised locally, and the human population's genuine marine
  self-sufficiency has no equivalent for robots. Any distress signal toward Concordia has to travel the same
  weak, slow link that made the city safe in the first place — the single most survivable trait in Palmer
  subnet is also the specific mechanism standing between Signy and the rescue it may eventually need.
- **Signy's own Full Extrapolation, Section VII** — reaching Concordia is still technically possible
  post-war, but severely degraded; "if Signy has something important to say to Concordia, it has to work for
  it."
- **Signy's own Full Extrapolation, Section VIII** — The Long Watch, an existing observance marking the
  arrival of the season's first supply ship, now carrying new urgency given the shortage.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any invented detail about the wider subnet's own siligel production capacity
  beyond what's already established as generally strained post-war; this chain treats the broader supply
  competition as a plausible, real-world-consistent pressure, not a fabricated antagonist.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Signy looks, on arrival, like the one Palmer subnet city the war genuinely spared — intact,
functioning, its research station confirmed fully operational. But its robot population is on a real,
ticking countdown: weeks to a couple of months of siligel reserve left, with no way to produce or improvise
more locally, and no equivalent to the marine self-sufficiency keeping the human population comfortably fed.
The player is asked to help get word out and secure a resupply — a straightforward rescue-logistics task.

**BUT** — The very trait that kept Signy safe is the trait working against it now. Its extreme isolation
left it with the weakest Arcanet link in the Federation, reliant on old-fashioned radio that can still,
technically, reach Concordia — just slowly, unreliably, at real effort. Simply getting the request heard in
time is itself a genuine achievement, not something the player can assume will happen on its own.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to physically carry the message, or find some faster alternate relay, rather
than trusting Signy's own degraded communications to get the word out fast enough against the actual
countdown.

**BUT** — Once contact is finally made and a resupply route is actually arranged, the real bottleneck turns
out not to be distance at all. Siligel supply chains across the wider subnet are themselves strained
post-war, and fulfilling Signy's specific need means diverting a limited, contested resource from somewhere
else. Signy's own defining trait — the isolation that kept it untouched while its neighbors burned — becomes
a real argument against prioritizing it: other, more visibly war-torn communities have an equally urgent, and
in some eyes more sympathetic, claim on the same limited stock, and a city that was simply lucky enough to
survive intact can look, to a stretched distribution authority, like the wrong place to send scarce supply
first.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a straightforward resupply errand becomes a real question of priority: does
Signy's population's genuine, non-negotiable need get treated the same as anyone else's, or does its own
untouched status quietly cost it its place in line.

**BUT** — The reversal is that both directions carry a real, felt cost. Prioritizing Signy's resupply
immediately honors an urgent, real need on its own terms, regardless of how the city fared in the war — but
risks a genuine, understandable resentment from communities that suffered direct destruction and might
reasonably feel a city "lucky enough" to survive intact shouldn't jump the queue ahead of them. Routing Signy
into the ordinary distribution order, behind more visibly damaged cities, respects a kind of
visible-suffering-first fairness and avoids the appearance of favoritism — but leaves Signy's robot
population racing an actual, ticking clock, real harm to real people who did nothing to deserve going last
simply because their own city happened to survive.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which cost the wider subnet actually accepts: push Signy's resupply through immediately, honoring
urgent need over visible suffering, at the cost of real resentment from communities who lost far more; or
let Signy wait its turn behind more obviously war-torn cities, honoring a fairness logic most people would
recognize as reasonable, at the direct cost of gambling with a population whose survival depends on not
running out the clock. Neither choice is presented as obviously correct — one privileges need, the other
privileges visible loss, and Palmer subnet doesn't have enough siligel to fully satisfy both principles at
once.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "send for help" into
"the very trait that saved this city makes asking for help genuinely hard"; the second BUT turns "the
message got through" into "getting help here means taking it from somewhere else that also needs it"; the
third BUT turns "decide who goes first" into "there is no priority rule that doesn't cost someone something
real." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads
as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes the confirmed, already-flagged DLC 3 hook and carries it to full
main-questline scale using the exact irony the subnet's own Cross-City Throughways already named — the same
trait that made Signy the safest city in the subnet is also what makes it hardest for Signy to ask for help,
and, once help is finally sought, the same untouched status becomes a live argument about whether it deserves
help first at all. It's also the first Palmer subnet candidate whose central conflict is a resource-priority
question with no villain on either side — everyone involved is acting on a reasonable, defensible principle,
and the tragedy, if there is one, belongs entirely to circumstance rather than anyone's bad choice.
