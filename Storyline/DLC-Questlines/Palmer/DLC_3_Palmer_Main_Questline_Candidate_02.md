# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #2: "Whatever Lesson Gets Told"

**What this is:** a second prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, built around Sejong's own established
coexistence-experiment question rather than Signy's siligel crisis (Candidate #1). **Not canon, not chosen,
not the only candidate** — the 2nd of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation
Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Palmer subnet's 8 cities (Sejong, as ruins and archival record)** — the surviving cities whose own
present-day governance decisions this chain's climax actually affects (plausibly Rothera or Port Lockroy,
both damaged-but-functional and already absorbing diaspora from destroyed neighbors) are referenced only as
the general, already-established post-war reality of Palmer subnet's surviving population growing more
mixed, not developed as new content here.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Palmer Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Sejong's own destruction proposed as a fourth Upper
  Earth strike doctrine distinct from Juan Carlos's security strike, Marambio's efficiency strike, and
  Esperanza's morale strike: an *ideological erasure strike*. Sejong's whole civic premise was a genuinely
  uncertain, ongoing experiment in whether nearly a dozen nations could share one small island in permanent
  working proximity — a successful, celebrated example would directly inconvenience Upper Earth's own
  narrative that Tepenia's exiles couldn't build anything real. Destroying Sejong before the experiment could
  be judged doesn't just remove a city; it removes the possibility of Sejong ever becoming proof of anything.
- **Sejong's own Full Extrapolation, Section III** — "Did the Coexistence Experiment Succeed? — Yes and No,
  Genuinely Both." Functionally, real practical coexistence was achieved (transitional rather than
  hard-walled boundary zones, shared infrastructure and logistics). Socially and emotionally, the
  established tension — multiple communities competing for the same favorable ground — never fully
  resolved. Explicitly named as giving DLC 3 "real dramatic material rather than a flatly settled answer."
- **Sejong's own Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the Neighbors' Accord, a holiday marking the practical
  success of the original land-use/resource-sharing agreement specifically, deliberately separate from any
  claim the underlying social tension was ever resolved — the city's own civic calendar already keeps these
  two truths formally distinct rather than merged into one story.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any invented detail about a specific named post-war resettlement institution
  elsewhere in Tepenia; the stakes here stay at the scale of Palmer subnet's own surviving cities' ordinary,
  already-established post-war reality of absorbing diaspora from destroyed neighbors, not a new invented
  program.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Palmer subnet's surviving cities absorbing displaced populations from their destroyed
neighbors, a research effort turns to Sejong's own ruins and surviving records to settle a genuinely useful,
practical question: did the coexistence experiment actually work, and if so, how — real, usable guidance for
governing communities that now have to manage exactly the kind of dense, multinational proximity Sejong once
did. The player is asked to help recover and assess what's left of the record.

**BUT** — What the player actually finds mirrors Section III's own established both/and complexity, not a
clean verdict. The record shows real, functional success — genuine shared infrastructure, a real, celebrated
accord — sitting directly alongside real, unresolved social competition that never went away. There's no
honest single answer to bring back. Whichever emphasis the player chooses to foreground in reporting the
findings will shape the actual lesson surviving Palmer subnet cities draw from Sejong's own story.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to decide how to characterize the findings for an audience that wants
something actionable, not an academic "it's genuinely both" — a real editorial choice about what the record
is "for," not just what it says.

**BUT** — The stakes turn out higher than a historical research question. The surviving cities are actually
going to use whatever characterization the player delivers to shape how they structure their own newly
diverse, multinational governance going forward. Emphasizing the "cooperation worked" reading builds trust
and organic buy-in for a lighter-touch approach — but risks repeating the exact unresolved competitive
tension that was just as real at Sejong, since that flaw gets minimized in the telling. Emphasizing the
"tension never resolved" reading protects against repeating that specific flaw — but risks justifying heavy,
top-down control that stifles the very organic, practical cooperation that made Sejong's own arrangement
work as far as it actually did.

**THEREFORE** — What began as an archival assessment becomes a real decision with living consequences: the
player's own characterization of a destroyed city's legacy is about to shape how a living one governs itself.

**BUT** — The reversal is that either choice repeats, in a smaller and better-intentioned way, the exact
harm Upper Earth's own original strike was designed to accomplish — Sejong's destruction erased the
possibility of the experiment ever being judged in full; now, generations later, telling only half of what
actually happened there — whichever half — erases the same fuller truth again, just through selective memory
instead of an ideological erasure strike. Sejong's own destroyed population's genuinely mixed experience,
both the real success and the real friction, gets flattened either way into whichever single lesson best
serves the living cities' present-day needs.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
choice in how the record actually gets used: deliver the more hopeful, cooperation-emphasizing account,
helping the living cities build trust and organic buy-in at the cost of repeating a real, documented flaw;
deliver the more cautionary, tension-emphasizing account, protecting against that same flaw at the cost of
over-correcting into control that undercuts genuine cooperation; or insist on delivering the full, genuinely
both/and truth exactly as found, honoring Sejong's own complicated legacy completely, at the cost of handing
the living cities a harder, less immediately actionable answer than the "usable lesson" they actually asked
for. Every option costs something, and none of them lets Sejong's own real, mixed history simply speak for
itself without someone deciding what to do with it.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "recover the record"
into "the record itself refuses to give a clean answer"; the second BUT turns "report the findings" into
"the report will actually shape how living cities govern themselves, not just how history remembers Sejong";
the third BUT turns "choose an emphasis" into "any single emphasis repeats, in miniature, the same erasure
the original strike was designed to accomplish." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is
actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that its climax makes literal and personal a thematic echo the subnet's own
material already set up but never carried through: Sejong was destroyed specifically to prevent its own
coexistence experiment from ever being judged, and this chain puts the player in the position of doing
exactly the judging Upper Earth's own strike was designed to make impossible — only to discover that judging
it honestly, in a form useful to anyone, may require the same kind of selective flattening the original
strike was trying to force in the first place. It's also the first Palmer subnet candidate whose central
tension is about the ethics of historical interpretation itself, rather than a resource, a rivalry, or a
crisis unfolding in the present tense.
