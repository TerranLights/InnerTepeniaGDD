# DLC 3 (Palmer Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #10: "Whatever the Gateway Was For"

**What this is:** a tenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 3's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Palmer subnet, completing this subnet's own minimum count
of ten distinct candidates the same way Candidate #10 did for Mirny, Halley, and Janbogo subnets. Built
around the subnet's own core identity thesis directly, rather than any single city's own material. **Not
canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 10th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the
Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses all 8 of Palmer subnet's cities** — the three survivors (Rothera, Port Lockroy, Signy) as living
present-day parties to the decision; the five destroyed cities (Palmer City, Esperanza, Marambio, Sejong,
Juan Carlos) referenced collectively, through their diaspora, as the weight bearing on it.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Palmer Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section II** — each city's own specific role within the subnet's
  gateway identity: Palmer City the face, Esperanza the promise, Juan Carlos the memory, Marambio the
  throughput, Port Lockroy the correspondence, Rothera the workshop, Sejong the experiment, Signy the edge.
  The subnet's whole established identity is *being the gateway* — Tepenia's actual point of contact with
  Upper Earth.
- **The subnet's own established casualty pattern** — five of eight Palmer subnet cities destroyed
  (Palmer City, Esperanza, Marambio, Sejong, Juan Carlos), the highest concentration of destruction
  documented in any subnet covered by this project so far, plausibly not coincidental to the subnet's own
  gateway visibility and significance to Upper Earth.
- **Signy's own Full Extrapolation, Section IV / Cross-City Throughway 4** — the subnet's own established
  fact that isolation from the gateway function is what let Signy survive untouched, while every gateway
  -facing city took the war's worst losses.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about which specific Upper Earth actor or motive drove the
  disproportionate destruction — this chain treats the pattern as established fact (documented across
  Candidates #1 through #9's own city-level material) without assigning new blame or mechanism.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Post-war, Palmer subnet's surviving institutions — Rothera, Port Lockroy, Signy, and
representatives of the scattered diaspora from the five destroyed cities — face a real decision about the
subnet's own future: rebuild and recommit fully to Tepenia's gateway role (tourism, customs, trade, the
whole established identity Section II lays out city by city), or deliberately recede from that exposed
position, given it plausibly cost this subnet more than any other region in the country. The player is
asked to help facilitate the planning process — a straightforward, if consequential, policy question.

**BUT** — The gateway identity isn't a policy that can simply be adjusted — it's woven into what every
surviving city actually is. Rothera's own manufacturing exists to supply the whole gateway network; Port
Lockroy's postal correspondence connects directly to Upper Earth tourism information flows; even Signy's own
established identity as "the edge" only means anything in relation to the gateway it sits at the edge of.
Receding from the role doesn't just change subnet policy — it asks each surviving city to abandon or
fundamentally redefine what it's always actually been for.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to help each surviving city reckon concretely with what receding, or
continuing, would specifically cost or preserve for them — not an abstract subnet-wide vote, but a real
negotiation city by city.

**BUT** — A genuine complication surfaces once the destroyed cities' own diaspora communities are actually
consulted: they're not united. Some feel the gateway role is precisely what got their home cities killed and
want nothing further to do with continuing it — a justified, protective resentment. Others feel abandoning
the gateway role now would mean their destroyed home's own purpose, and the loss it paid for that purpose,
meant nothing — that continuing the mission is the only way their lost city's own meaning survives at all.
This isn't survivors against diaspora. It's a real, sincere split running straight through the diaspora
itself.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a subnet-wide policy decision becomes a genuine reckoning with what the
subnet's own dead actually died for, and whether continuing their purpose honors them or simply repeats
the exact exposure that killed them.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Rebuilding and recommitting fully to the
gateway role honors what Palmer City, Esperanza, Marambio, Sejong, and Juan Carlos's own established
purposes actually meant, giving their loss ongoing significance — but genuinely risks repeating the same
catastrophic exposure, this time with a population that chose the risk with open eyes rather than simply
inheriting it. Deliberately receding from the gateway role protects the surviving population from ever
facing that exposure again — but quietly declares that being the gateway, the entire reason five cities
existed and died the way they did, is no longer something Tepenia wants to be at all.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which way Palmer subnet actually goes: rebuild the gateway fully, honoring the destroyed cities'
own purpose at real, informed risk to everyone who chooses to continue it; or recede from the role
deliberately, protecting the living at the cost of what amounts to a quiet abandonment of what five
destroyed cities' entire existence was actually for. Neither choice is presented as correct, and — fittingly,
for the candidate that closes this subnet's own set of ten — neither choice changes what already happened
to the cities that are gone. It only decides what the subnet chooses to be, going forward, because of it.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "adjust subnet policy"
into "the gateway identity is inseparable from what each surviving city actually is"; the second BUT turns
"consult the diaspora" into "the diaspora itself is sincerely split, not united against continuing"; the
third BUT turns "rebuild or recede" into "there is no version of the choice that doesn't either honor the
dead at risk to the living, or protect the living by abandoning what the dead died for." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And
Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first in this set to operate at full subnet scale, engaging
Palmer subnet's own established "gateway" identity thesis (Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section II) directly
rather than working through any single city's own local material — a deliberate structural echo of how
Mirny, Halley, and Janbogo subnets' own Candidate #10s each closed their sets on a note that reached back
to the whole subnet's defining idea rather than one more local story. It's also the only Palmer subnet
candidate whose stakes are genuinely retrospective and prospective at once: nothing the player does changes
what already happened to five destroyed cities, only what the three survivors, and everyone who still
carries the other five with them, decide those deaths were actually for.
