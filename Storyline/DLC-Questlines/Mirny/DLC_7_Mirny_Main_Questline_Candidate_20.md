# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #20: "Whatever the Council Inherits"

**What this is:** a twentieth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around a tenth distinct linking
mechanism — consolidating a single, subnet-wide historical archive — closing out this run of "at least 5
cities" candidates (#11 through #20) the same way Palmer subnet's own Candidate #20 and Halley subnet's own
Candidate #20 closed their matching runs. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear
Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Mirny, Zhongshan, Davis, Vostok, Kunlun — the five cities most
directly implicated across this run's own contested decisions) — and, by its own nature, naturally extends to
draw on Sinheung, Shirayuki, and Casey as well, since a subnet-wide archive has no honest reason to exclude
any of them.

**A deliberate closing note.** Like Palmer's and Halley's own Candidate #20, this chain is built specifically
to reflect on the other nine in this run rather than introduce a wholly separate stake — treating the
genuinely unresolved trade-offs Candidates #11 through #19 each ended on (which policy the vulnerability
assessment recommended, how Casey's survivors actually got resettled, whether either unification proposal
went forward, which route decision the subnet made, whether Davis's and its peers' credit went public, whether
the exchange program bound its participants, whose manufacturing standard won, which representation model the
new council adopted, whether information got restricted after the counter-intelligence finding) as the actual,
lived material a subnet-wide archive — and the new council Candidate #18 proposed — would inherit and have to
contend with.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **This run's own accumulated decisions** — nine prior candidates (#11–#19), each ending in a genuine,
  unresolved trade-off per this project's own No Good Endings law, rather than a settled consensus, meaning
  the subnet's own recent history already contains multiple live, contested outcomes by this point in the run.
- **Candidate #18's own proposed Mirny Subnet Council** — used here as the natural in-world body that would
  actually need this archive, since a permanent council making ongoing subnet-wide decisions needs some
  working record of what's already been decided and what's still genuinely contested.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about which specific outcome any of Candidates #11 through #19 actually
  reached — this chain treats those outcomes as still genuinely open, whichever way any individual playthrough
  or design pass ultimately resolves them.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Candidate #18's own proposed Mirny Subnet Council taking shape, a push emerges to build a
single, definitive subnet-wide historical archive — consolidating records and testimony from across the
subnet into one coherent account the new council can actually work from. The player is asked to help gather
contributions from multiple cities — a straightforward, if large-scale, "build the record" task.

**BUT** — Different cities hold genuinely different, still-contested accounts of the very same recent
events — not settled history, but live, ongoing argument: whether the vulnerability assessment's own
recommendation was right, whether Casey's survivors were resettled fairly, whether either unification
proposal should have gone forward, whether the route decision honored or abandoned Casey's memory, whether
Davis's and its peers' credit should have gone public, whether the exchange program's terms were fair,
whose manufacturing standard should have won, and whether the counter-intelligence finding justified
restricting the subnet's own ordinary openness. A single "definitive" account isn't achievable without
someone deciding whose version of a still-open argument becomes the official one.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to navigate genuinely competing, current narratives from different
communities about their own shared, recent, unfinished history — arbitrating the present, not simply
recording the past.

**BUT** — The reversal lands directly: whichever version becomes "the archive" doesn't just record history,
it settles it — for the new council's own future decisions, and for the subnet's own understanding of itself.
Genuine, still-live disagreements the subnet's own communities haven't actually finished having yet would be
closed by the act of writing them down as though they were already resolved.

**THEREFORE** — What began as building a historical record becomes a real decision about whether the
subnet's own story is actually ready to be told as one story at all — and, by extension, whether the new
council can fairly build on it.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. A single, authoritative account gives the
council and the subnet a clear, usable historical record — useful for actual governance and whatever closure
the subnet's own communities might want — but risks prematurely settling genuinely unresolved internal
arguments, imposing a false consensus where real, sincere disagreement still exists, right at the moment a
new representative body is supposed to be adjudicating exactly those disagreements fairly. A deliberately
multi-vocal archive — presenting the real disagreements side by side rather than picking a single account —
is honest about the fact that the subnet's own story isn't finished being decided, but hands the new council,
and the subnet's own future, a messier, harder-to-use record to actually govern from.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which kind of archive actually gets built: one settled, authoritative account, at the cost of closing
arguments the subnet's own people haven't actually finished having, right as a new council prepares to govern
on the strength of it; or a genuinely multi-vocal record, honest about live disagreement, at the cost of
leaving that same new council, and the subnet's own future, without a single clear record to build from.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "gather the history"
into "the history is still actively contested, not settled"; the second BUT turns "write it down" into
"writing it down settles arguments that are still genuinely live, right as a new council needs to adjudicate
them fairly"; the third BUT turns "pick a format" into "there is no format that gives the council both a
usable record and an honest one at the same time." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is
actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the only one in this run of ten built to look back at the other
nine rather than stand entirely on its own — and, distinctly from Palmer's and Halley's own closing
candidates, it ties that reflection directly to a specific in-world institution (Candidate #18's own proposed
council) rather than an abstract future audience, giving the archive question real, immediate governance
stakes rather than purely historical ones. It closes this run the same way Palmer's and Halley's own
Candidate #20 closed their matching sets, and the way each subnet's own Candidate #10 closed its original
ten-candidate set: by asking what the whole collection of stories actually adds up to, rather than adding one
more story next to the rest.
