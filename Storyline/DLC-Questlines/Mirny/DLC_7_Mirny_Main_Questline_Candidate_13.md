# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #13: "Not Every Union Looks the Same"

**What this is:** a thirteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around a third distinct linking
mechanism for this run — evaluating and facilitating proposed civic-unification efforts against an
established precedent, rather than Candidate #11's strategic vulnerability assessment or Candidate #12's
resettlement-policy design. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation
Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Zhongshan, Vostok, Kunlun, Mirny, Davis). **Explicitly does not
reference Mountain Pass, the Cradle chamber network, or the player character's own origin** — Vostok's and
Kunlun's own established partnership is used here only for its general, mutual-dependency and resource
-sharing facts, consistent with Candidate #11's own approach to the same relationship.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Mirny Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — the Tri-Cities' own established three-stage amalgamation
  history: legal separation, then de facto merger, then full legal unification, built gradually over a
  century and a half and driven by shared tri-junction infrastructure and two of the three cities' own shared
  Jeju-do founding mechanism.
- **Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 6** — Vostok's and Kunlun's own established "two loneliest outposts"
  relationship: a genuine, concrete, mutual resource-sharing infrastructure partnership, not merely emotional
  companionship.
- **Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2** — Davis's own established feeder/enabler role relative to Mirny's
  own subnet-hub industrial capacity, used here to characterize the Mirny-Davis relationship as a functional
  hierarchy rather than a peer partnership.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Mountain Pass or the Cradle chamber network — Vostok's and
  Kunlun's own partnership is treated here strictly as an isolation/resource-sharing relationship, not as the
  specific infrastructure Throughway 1 traces toward the player character's own origin.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Inspired by the Tri-Cities' own successful amalgamation, two separate proposals reach the
player: Vostok and Kunlun want to formalize their existing "two loneliest outposts" partnership into
something closer to genuine union, and Mirny and Davis are considering closer integration given their own
established feeder relationship. The player is asked to help evaluate and facilitate whichever proposals seem
workable, using the Tri-Cities' own history as the template — a straightforward, if delicate, diplomatic
-facilitation task.

**BUT** — The Tri-Cities' own successful union depended on conditions that don't automatically transfer.
Their amalgamation grew from physical proximity, shared vulnerable infrastructure, and two of the three
cities' own shared founding mechanism. Vostok's and Kunlun's entire safety, by contrast, comes from mutual
remoteness, not proximity — genuine unification could require exactly the kind of increased connection that
undermines the isolation keeping them both safe. Mirny and Davis, meanwhile, don't share a peer relationship
at all — theirs is a functional hierarchy, and formal unification could simply entrench that imbalance under
the appealing language of partnership.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually assess each proposal on its own terms, rather than assume the
Tri-Cities' own success story transfers automatically to either pair.

**BUT** — The reversal deepens once the assessment is underway: refusing to facilitate either proposal, on
the grounds that the precedent doesn't cleanly fit, risks denying these cities real agency to shape their own
future relationship on their own terms simply because their circumstances don't match a known success story.
Endorsing both proposals uncritically risks locking each pair into a structurally different, possibly worse
arrangement than the model they're trying to emulate, dressed in the same appealing language of closer union.

**THEREFORE** — What began as evaluating precedent becomes a real question about whether to trust these
cities' own aspirations toward each other, even where the model they're borrowing doesn't actually fit their
situation.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Facilitating both unification pushes honors
Vostok's, Kunlun's, Mirny's, and Davis's own genuine agency and aspiration toward closer cooperation — but
risks trading away Vostok's and Kunlun's own isolation-based safety, and formalizing Davis's own subordinate
position to Mirny under a name that implies something more equal than what it actually is. Discouraging or
slow-walking both proposals protects Vostok's and Kunlun's own safety and avoids entrenching Davis's own
position — but denies all four cities real agency to pursue a relationship each has genuinely expressed
interest in, on the theory that their own aspirations don't fit a template well enough to trust.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether and how these unification proposals actually proceed: facilitate them, honoring real civic
aspiration at the cost of real safety and equity risks; or discourage them, protecting against those risks at
the cost of denying these cities their own chosen direction.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "apply the Tri-Cities
template" into "the conditions that made it work don't actually exist for either new pair"; the second BUT
turns "refuse or endorse" into "refusing denies real agency, endorsing risks recreating the model's
downsides under a better name"; the third BUT turns "facilitate or discourage" into "there is no option that
doesn't cost either real safety and equity, or these cities' own chosen direction." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes the Tri-Cities' own amalgamation — already established as a
genuine, hard-won success story spanning a century and a half — and tests whether a real success story
travels, rather than assuming any similar-sounding proposal automatically deserves the same treatment. It's
also the first Mirny subnet candidate to put Davis's own established subordinate feeder role to direct
dramatic use, treating the unequal relationship Pattern 2 already documents as something a "closer union"
proposal could either fix or quietly make permanent, depending entirely on how the player handles it.
