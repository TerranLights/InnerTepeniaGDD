# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #4: "Whoever the Work Requires"

**What this is:** a fourth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around Kunlun's own previously-unused
subnet-level identity line. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 4th of the "at least
10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md`
§5a).

**Deliberately uses only 3 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Kunlun, Davis, Mirny). Overlaps with Candidate #1
in two of three cities (Kunlun, Mirny), but the actual dramatic engine, theme, and cast role are entirely
different — Mirny recurring as the subnet's own logistics/relay hub across multiple candidates is a
structural fact about the subnet, not padding.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

  Head-center identity in Tepenia — a city that became one enormous distributed instrument, re-resolved
  from a single nation into whoever the actual expertise required." Genuinely unused across Candidates
  1–3.
- **Kunlun's own Full Extrapolation, Section II** — the observatory's archive is one of the only facilities
  in Tepenia positioned to have tracked orbital debris from the Amundsen Tower's own collapse — a direct,
  still-untapped record of what actually happened when the Tower fell.
- **Kunlun's own Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Kunlun's founding human population's end was a
  *deliberate, evidence-based decision*, not a passive fade: chronic altitude sickness took a real,
  mounting biological toll, tracked by the city's own medical infrastructure, until the community itself
  (or a wider Federation safety authority) chose to phase out human habitation entirely. This is the direct
  root of the established "human presence forbidden, for humans' own safety" policy.
- **Kunlun's own Full Extrapolation, Section IV** — a real, established internal distinction: Kunlun's
  task-focused astronomers/comms specialists relate to the extreme environment purely functionally; its
  Ice-Cold Buddhism devotee population holds the same conditions with genuine reverence. Both readings are
  simultaneously true of the same population.
- **Davis's own Full Extrapolation, Section I and Section VI** — Davis's own genuinely distinct scientific
  specialty (paleoclimate reconstruction from layered lake-sediment cores — extracting coherent long-term
  signal from noisy accumulated records) and its own proposed placeholder figure, Naturalist Ratna Wirawan,
  already characterized as embodying the city's "enabler" ethos directly.
- **Mirny's own established relay function** (Local Cultures §23; Mirny Subnet Cross-Reference Synthesis)
  — the subnet-hub relay already routes all six other subnet cities' communications, at reduced,
  war-damaged capacity (also load-bearing in Candidate #1).
- **Explicitly excluded:** the Cradle chamber-manufacturing network (untouched — this chain concerns the
  Amundsen Tower's physical collapse/debris record, an entirely different, non-forbidden thread already
  extensively developed elsewhere, e.g. `Storyline/DLC_01_Echoes_of_Amundsen.md`) and any named connection
  to the Planetary Split Brain (this chain never claims the debris record resolves or feeds that mystery —
  it stands on its own as a piece of Amundsen Tower lore).

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Kunlun's observatory archive holds decades of continuous orbital debris tracking, including,
somewhere in its accumulated depth, a direct physical record of the Amundsen Tower's own collapse. But
Kunlun's own current population — astronomers and comms specialists, task-driven and precise — lacks the
specific analytical skill the raw, decades-deep, noisy dataset actually requires: not more astronomy, but
the ability to extract a coherent historical signal from long-accumulated, imperfect records.

**BUT** — The one person in the whole Mirny subnet whose own methodology is a genuine match for exactly
that skill isn't an astronomer, or even a Kunlun resident. It's Davis's own paleoclimatologist, credited
locally with reconstructing coherent climate history from layered, noisy lake-sediment cores — the same
underlying analytical discipline, just applied to a completely different medium. And she's human.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to travel to Davis, make the case for work entirely outside her own field,
and then broker her actual passage to Kunlun — where the city's own founding principle ("whoever the
actual expertise required") offers real, established precedent for the exception, but collides head-on
with the other lesson Kunlun's own founding taught it: the deliberate, evidence-based decision to end
human habitation there at all, after watching altitude sickness take a real, cumulative toll on its own
founding population.

**BUT** — This isn't a bureaucratic obstacle to negotiate past — it's a real, physical risk to a real
person, and Kunlun's own population is genuinely split on how to weigh it. Its task-focused astronomers
and comms specialists read the situation through pure evidence and need: the data justifies the risk, full
stop. Its devotional population — for whom the extreme environment itself carries real, reverent weight,
not just working conditions to schedule around — reads asking a human to risk exactly what already cost
Kunlun its own people as something closer to a violation of what the city's whole hard-won safety policy
was actually *for*.

**THEREFORE** — The practical solution everyone can actually live with routes through Mirny instead: rather
than the specialist making the dangerous ascent at all, Mirny's own subnet-hub relay — already routing
every other city's communications — gets repurposed to stream the raw debris data down to Davis, where she
can work at safe altitude, with Kunlun's own comms specialists relaying her findings back.

**BUT** — The remote solution isn't free either. Mirny's own relay hardware is already running at reduced,
war-damaged capacity, barely sufficient for ordinary subnet routine traffic. Sustaining genuinely
data-heavy, ongoing transmission for as long as the actual analysis takes means real, felt resource
competition returns — every other city in the subnet feels some cost of this decision, even though none of
them are the ones who nearly took the physical risk.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the climax finds a real, working
answer: the debris data does get analyzed, and the player earns something genuinely new about the Amundsen
Tower's own collapse. But the cost isn't nothing — Kunlun's own devotional population is left with a real,
unresolved discomfort about how close the city came to repeating its own founding tragedy for the sake of
data, and the Davis specialist herself, having done real, valued work entirely outside her own field, is
left to decide whether this was a one-time favor or the start of something she never actually asked for.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the obstacle rather than delaying it: first BUT turns
"find an analyst" into "the right analyst is human, not robot" — a categorical complication, not a skill
check; second BUT turns "get her there" into a genuine ethical split within Kunlun's own population about
repeating its own founding tragedy; third BUT turns the remote-work compromise from a clean fix into a
real, subnet-wide resource cost. Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually doing
afterward, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's strongest asset is that Kunlun's own founding history supplies both halves of its central
tension on its own — "whoever the expertise required" and "we already learned this lesson once, at real
cost" are both already-established facts about the *same* city, not an invented conflict layered on top.
The Davis connection (a transferable data-analysis skill, not an invented orbital specialist) and the Mirny
resolution (reusing the already-established, already-strained relay capacity from Candidate #1) both trace
directly to existing material rather than new invention. Distinct from Candidate #1 in theme (an ethical
test of a founding principle vs. a legitimacy/naming question) and in climax shape (a quiet, personal,
unresolved discomfort vs. a public, subnet-wide reckoning) despite sharing two of three cities.
