# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #18: "Whichever Voice Weighs More"

**What this is:** an eighteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around an eighth distinct
linking mechanism for this run — designing a permanent subnet-wide representative council's own composition,
rather than Candidate #11's strategic vulnerability assessment, Candidate #12's resettlement-policy design,
Candidate #13's unification-precedent evaluation, Candidate #14's physical-route crisis, Candidate #15's
credit-attribution/security campaign, Candidate #16's cultural-exchange dilemma, or Candidate #17's
coalition-standardization dispute. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear
Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Zhongshan, Kunlun, Vostok, Davis, Casey). **Distinct from Candidate
#12:** that chain designed a policy for integrating new, displaced arrivals into existing cities; this chain
designs an ongoing structure for how existing communities get a permanent political voice in future
subnet-wide decisions — a procedural question, not an integration policy.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 1** — the subnet's own near-complete taxonomy of demographic
  -formation mechanisms: Zhongshan's organic multi-generational immigration, Kunlun's deliberate curated
  draw, Vostok's demographic-redistribution accident (a population with zero ancestral connection to the
  founding legend), Davis's genuinely dispersed structure with no dominant community, and Casey's own "wide,
  mixed early wave" hybrid case.
- **This run's own accumulated precedent** — the fact that Candidates #11 through #17 each required some kind
  of subnet-wide negotiation or decision, used here as the in-world justification for why a permanent council,
  rather than repeated ad hoc negotiation, is now being proposed.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about which of this run's own prior candidates' outcomes the
  council would actually revisit or ratify — this chain treats the council's own founding structure as the
  entire stake, not a vehicle for resolving any specific prior dispute.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With subnet-wide coordination increasingly necessary, a push emerges to establish a permanent
Mirny Subnet Council — a formal representative body for future subnet-wide decisions, rather than relying on
fresh, ad hoc negotiation each time. The player is asked to help design its own composition and
representation structure — a straightforward, if consequential, governance-design task.

**BUT** — The subnet's own genuinely different founding mechanisms make a single representation principle
impossible to apply fairly. Zhongshan's organic community has real demographic weight to point to. Kunlun's
curated, credentialed population has a claim to representation by expertise and contribution rather than raw
numbers. Vostok's current population, having no ancestral connection to its own founding legend at all, has
a genuinely different relationship to speaking for the city's history than either of the other two. Davis has
no dominant community structure at all from which to select a single representative in the first place.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to negotiate a genuinely mixed representation model, since no single
principle — population, contribution, historical claim, or community structure — fits all of these cities'
actual situations.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the practical weight of the choice becomes clear: whatever hybrid formula
gets adopted will functionally determine which of the subnet's own competing interests prevail in every
future subnet-wide decision the council ever makes. Designing the council's own structure isn't a neutral
administrative choice — it's the single decision that quietly shapes the tilt of every decision that follows
it.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a governance-design task becomes a decision about the future itself, not just
about how a council gets seated.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. A representation model weighted toward
population and scale gives the subnet's largest communities the strongest voice, reflecting who's actually
most affected by most decisions — but risks permanently sidelining smaller, differently-structured cities like
Davis, Vostok, and Kunlun, whose real, sometimes disproportionate contributions wouldn't be reflected in their
political weight. A model weighted toward contribution and expertise gives technically vital cities a voice
proportional to what they actually provide — but risks concentrating real political power in a handful of
small, specialized cities over a subnet whose larger population bears the practical consequences of council
decisions without proportional say.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which representation model the new council actually adopts: population-weighted, reflecting who bears
the consequences at the cost of sidelining smaller, differently-structured cities; or contribution-weighted,
reflecting genuine expertise and value at the cost of concentrating real power away from the population the
council's decisions actually govern.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "design a council" into
"no single representation principle actually fits every city's own real situation"; the second BUT turns
"negotiate a hybrid formula" into "the formula itself will quietly tilt every future decision the council ever
makes"; the third BUT turns "weight by population or contribution" into "there is no weighting that doesn't
cost either fair representation for smaller cities or proportional say for the population most affected."
Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a
disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Pattern 1's own demographic-formation taxonomy — already used
once in Candidate #12 for a resettlement question — and applies it to a genuinely different problem, ongoing
political representation, showing the same underlying diversity of city types can generate two structurally
distinct dilemmas rather than one repeated one. It's also the first candidate in this run to make its own
climax about the shape of future decision-making itself, rather than a single resolved policy outcome — a
fittingly reflective note this close to the run's own eventual closing candidate.
