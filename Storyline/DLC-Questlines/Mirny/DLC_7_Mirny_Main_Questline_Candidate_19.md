# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #19: "Whatever Wasn't Meant to Be a Secret"

**What this is:** a nineteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around a ninth distinct linking
mechanism for this run — a counter-intelligence investigation into a wartime information leak, rather than
Candidate #11's strategic vulnerability assessment, Candidate #12's resettlement-policy design, Candidate
#13's unification-precedent evaluation, Candidate #14's physical-route crisis, Candidate #15's
credit-attribution/security campaign, Candidate #16's cultural-exchange dilemma, Candidate #17's
coalition-standardization dispute, or Candidate #18's governance-structure design. **Not canon, not chosen,
not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Sinheung, Mirny, Zhongshan, Shirayuki, Davis). **Explicitly does not
reference the Cradle chamber network's connection to the player character's own origin** — this chain uses
Sinheung's own established chamber-manufacturing role strictly as the thing Upper Earth's wartime targeting
already exposed, a separate question from Throughway 1's own off-limits material.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Sinheung Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 2** — the established, explicitly flagged finding that Upper
  Earth's own wartime intelligence about Sinheung's chamber-manufacturing significance was sharper than most
  Tepenians' own awareness of it, since the war's targeting of chamber production specifically means Upper
  Earth's war planners had to have known the city's real manufacturing role — a genuine asymmetry the source
  file itself proposes as "a possible thread for future war-era storytelling."
- **Mirny's own established doubled hub function** (Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3) — its role
  routing all subnet communications, used here as a natural node the investigation has to pass through when
  tracing how information could plausibly have moved.
- **Davis Mega-Init, Personality section** — the established detail of Davis's own quiet enjoyment of
  smuggled Upper Earth books, used here as a genuine, already-existing point of informational contact with
  Upper Earth, however small and non-malicious in its own original context.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim that this investigation connects to Mountain Pass, the Cradle network,
  or the player character's own origin — this chain stays entirely within Finding 2's own established scope.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A researcher uncovers unsettling evidence: Upper Earth's own wartime intelligence about
Sinheung's chamber-manufacturing significance was more precise than most Tepenians' own understanding of the
city's importance at the time. The player is asked to investigate how Upper Earth actually knew this, and
whether the same vulnerability might still exist today — a straightforward, if serious, counter-intelligence
task.

**BUT** — Tracing the leak backward means following how information could plausibly have moved at all —
through Mirny's own comms hub, which routes every subnet city's communications, and through the Zhongshan
cluster's own neighbors, Zhongshan and Shirayuki. There's no single obvious channel; the investigation has to
proceed city by city, mapping how ordinary information actually flows through this subnet.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to investigate each node in turn, building a real picture of the subnet's own
information pathways rather than chasing one suspect.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once a real source comes into focus, and it isn't a traitor or a spy. Davis's
own small, well-established habit of trading for smuggled Upper Earth books — an innocent, non-malicious point
of contact — turns out to be exactly the kind of ordinary channel through which enough incidental detail
could plausibly have reached Upper Earth to let a sufficiently attentive observer piece Sinheung's real
importance together, with no deliberate leak or betrayal involved at all. The vulnerability isn't a person;
it's the ordinary, everyday openness the subnet's own economic and cultural life depends on.

**THEREFORE** — What began as hunting for a leak becomes a real question about whether to restrict this kind
of ordinary information contact going forward, or accept it as a cost of the openness the subnet actually
needs to function.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Restricting and monitoring this kind of
routine contact protects strategically valuable cities like Sinheung from being profiled the same way again —
but undermines the same open information flow that Mirny's own comms hub and the subnet's own cultural and
economic life actually depend on, adding real friction and suspicion to ordinary daily contact. Leaving
information open preserves that functional openness and the trust it depends on — but leaves the exact
vulnerability that let Upper Earth profile Sinheung once already sitting there for anyone paying close enough
attention, with no guarantee it doesn't happen again.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether and how information restrictions actually get imposed: restrict it, protecting strategically
vital cities at the cost of real friction in the subnet's own ordinary openness; or leave it open, preserving
that openness at the cost of leaving the same vulnerability in place, unaddressed.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "find the leak" into
"there's no single channel, the whole subnet's own information pathways have to be mapped"; the second BUT
turns "find the source" into "the source is ordinary openness, not betrayal"; the third BUT turns "restrict
or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either real security or the openness the subnet actually
needs." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads
as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Sinheung's own Cross-Reference Synthesis at its explicit word —
that finding literally proposes itself as "a possible thread for future war-era storytelling" — and builds
that exact thread into a main questline candidate, rather than leaving it as an unused observation. It's also
the first Mirny subnet candidate whose central "vulnerability" is a completely blameless, sympathetic detail
(Davis's own quiet enjoyment of smuggled books) rather than any city's flaw or failure, making the dilemma
about ordinary openness itself rather than about anyone's mistake.
