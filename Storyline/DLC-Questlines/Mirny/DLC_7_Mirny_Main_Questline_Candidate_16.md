# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #16: "Wherever They Choose to Stay"

**What this is:** a sixteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around a sixth distinct linking
mechanism for this run — a subnet-wide cultural/educational exchange program and the talent-retention
question it creates, rather than Candidate #11's strategic vulnerability assessment, Candidate #12's
resettlement-policy design, Candidate #13's unification-precedent evaluation, Candidate #14's physical-route
crisis, or Candidate #15's credit-attribution/security campaign. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Shirayuki, Davis, Mirny, Vostok, Kunlun).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Shirayuki Mega-Init, "What It Feels Like" and "Who Lives Here, and Why"** — the established fact that
  people come to Shirayuki from across Tepenia specifically for its culture and soul, not academic ranking
  alone, and that it's "genuinely, uncomplicatedly easy to make friends here" — plus its own Post-Culture
  Identity theme, *"A Place Decided For You, Made Into a Place You'd Choose."*
  Shirayuki's own excellent schools, established in the same section.
- **Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2** — Davis's established feeder/enabler role and Mirny's own doubled
  hub function, used here to characterize what these cities' own practical, ongoing needs actually are.
- **Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 6** — Vostok's and Kunlun's own established remoteness and mutual
  -support relationship, used here only to place them as sending cities with a genuine, practical stake in
  where their own most promising people end up.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about altitude, health, or population-composition changes at Kunlun —
  this chain treats Kunlun strictly as a sending city with an ordinary practical interest in retaining talent,
  consistent with its own established robot-only, curated-specialist population.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A subnet-wide educational and artistic exchange program is proposed, sending promising young
people from Davis, Mirny, Vostok, and Kunlun to study and train in Shirayuki, drawing on its own established
reputation as a place people already travel to for its culture, not its rankings. The player is asked to
help organize the exchange — a straightforward, if ambitious, cultural-coordination task.

**BUT** — The program's own genuine appeal creates a real risk: Shirayuki is, by its own established
character, a place it's "genuinely, uncomplicatedly easy" to want to stay in. Talented young people sent from
Davis's feeder economy, Mirny's industrial function, or remote Vostok and Kunlun may simply choose to remain
once the exchange ends, draining their home cities of exactly the people those cities most need to sustain
themselves.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually negotiate real terms for the exchange — return expectations,
participation quotas, incentives to come back — to balance the genuine cultural benefit against the
brain-drain risk facing the sending cities.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the terms get specific: any binding return or service requirement runs
directly against the thing that makes Shirayuki's own culture what it is. People choosing to stay is a genuine
expression of its established civic identity — freely chosen belonging, not obligation — not a flaw to
engineer around. Imposing binding terms would import a transactional logic into a program whose whole point
is sharing that same freely-chosen spirit, contradicting the very culture it's meant to showcase.

**THEREFORE** — What began as negotiating exchange terms becomes a real question about whether to protect
the sending cities' own practical future with binding commitments, or preserve the voluntary, freely-chosen
character that is Shirayuki's own defining civic virtue.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Binding return or service commitments protect
Davis's, Mirny's, Vostok's, and Kunlun's own long-term talent base — but undermine the freely-chosen,
non-transactional spirit that makes Shirayuki's own culture genuinely what it is, turning a program meant to
share that spirit into something that contradicts it. A fully voluntary exchange preserves that authentic
spirit and gives every participant a genuine, uncoerced choice — but does nothing to prevent real brain drain
from cities that can't currently afford to lose their own most promising people.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether and how binding terms get attached to the exchange: impose them, protecting the sending
cities' own future at the cost of contradicting the freely-chosen spirit the program is meant to share; or
leave it voluntary, preserving that authentic spirit at the cost of real, ongoing talent loss for the cities
that sent their own best people in good faith.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "organize an exchange"
into "the program's own appeal creates a real risk of losing people permanently"; the second BUT turns
"negotiate return terms" into "binding terms directly contradict the culture the exchange is meant to share";
the third BUT turns "impose terms or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either the sending
cities' own future or the freely-chosen spirit the whole program depends on." Deleting any one Complication
changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that its central tension comes directly from Shirayuki's own established
civic virtue, not a flaw or a villain — the very quality that makes the city worth sending people to (freely
chosen belonging) is exactly what makes protecting the sending cities difficult, giving DLC 7 a dilemma with
no external antagonist at all. It also gives Shirayuki its first appearance in this five-city run, using its
own established arts-and-education identity as the actual mechanism rather than background color, and gives
Davis, Vostok, and Kunlun a shared practical stake — talent retention — distinct from the roles each played
in Candidates #11 through #15.
