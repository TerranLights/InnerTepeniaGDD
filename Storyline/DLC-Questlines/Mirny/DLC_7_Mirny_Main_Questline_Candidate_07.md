# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #7: "Making It Beautiful, Keeping It True"

**What this is:** a seventh prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, pairing two cities that have never appeared
together in any prior candidate. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 7th of the "at
least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 2 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Davis, Shirayuki) — the smallest cast of any candidate so far,
deliberately: this story doesn't need a third city to be complete, and forcing one in would dilute it.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Davis's own Local Cultures, §6(a)** — "Do the work well; let the work speak. Davis's cardinal value —
  visible self-promotion is mildly distasteful here, consistent with the founding namesake's own
  unglamorous competence."
- **Davis's own Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Davis's own genuinely distinct research niche: paleoclimate
  reconstruction from layered lake-sediment cores, methodologically distinct from Vostok's and Kunlun's
  ice-core work.
- **Davis's own Full Extrapolation, Section VII** — a real, established minority fault line: most residents
  genuinely don't want outside attention, but a minority quietly wish the Vestfold Hills' remarkable terrain
  drew more outside recognition — not the city's dominant mood, but real and present.
- **Shirayuki's own Local Cultures, §6(b) and §15** — a genuinely different, comfortable relationship to
  visibility: "research findings, artistic work, and educational institutions are genuinely public civic
  pride"; a real, defining 25% arts/music/fashion sector and a real 20% education sector, "a genuine civic
  strength."
- **Explicitly excluded:** framing this chain around "uncredited competence" as its stated thesis. The
  actual dramatic engine is a genuine methodological tension (scientific rigor vs. accessibility), not a
  quest about wanting credit — any resonance with that broader civic pattern stays a background echo, per
  the developer's own standing instruction that this theme should be something the player notices over
  multiple playthroughs, never a DLC's own stated premise.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Davis's own paleoclimate research, genuinely valuable and methodologically distinct, has never
had a real educational pipeline behind it — no dedicated path exists for training successors in its own
specific sediment-core methodology, in a city whose own cardinal value is "let the work speak" rather than
actively recruiting attention or students.

**BUT** — Key researchers are aging, and the specific expertise genuinely risks being lost within a
generation if nothing changes. Davis's own methodology needs real students, somewhere — and the nearest
city with genuine, established educational infrastructure and a real research-and-arts civic identity is
Shirayuki.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to broker a real, lasting connection between the two cities — not a one-off
favor, but something like a standing program bringing Shirayuki's own students to learn Davis's methodology
directly.

**BUT** — Shirayuki's own students and faculty, drawn broadly to research, education, and the arts, have no
pre-existing pull toward Davis's comparatively obscure specific niche — and Davis's own "let the work speak"
ethos actively works against making the work sound exciting or visible enough to draw genuine outside
interest, as opposed to filling an assigned program slot nobody actually wants to be in.

**THEREFORE** — Something genuinely creative has to happen to make real interest, not administrative
compliance: framing and presenting Davis's own sediment-core data through Shirayuki's own established
artistic sensibility — visualizing decades of layered climate history the way ice-core science itself can
be genuinely, legitimately beautiful when actually shown to people, not just tabulated.

**BUT** — This doesn't sit easily with everyone at Davis. Some senior researchers worry that packaging the
work for broader appeal risks distorting the actual patience and rigor the science depends on — that the
same quiet, unglamorous, undramatic character that makes Davis's science genuinely trustworthy might not
survive translation into something exciting enough to draw outside attention, even for a good reason.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution finds a real,
working answer — a genuine new pipeline of Shirayuki students actually engaging with Davis's methodology,
presented in a way that draws real interest without becoming false to what the work actually is — but it
comes at a real cost to Davis's own historic character: some residents welcome finally having a future for
work they'd assumed would simply end with them; others feel something true about the quiet, undramatic way
Davis has always done things has been genuinely, irreversibly given up for the sake of its own survival.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the obstacle: first BUT turns "no pipeline exists" into
"the expertise is actively, urgently at risk of being lost"; second BUT turns "connect the two cities" into
"connecting them administratively isn't enough, real interest has to be earned"; third BUT turns "found a
creative solution" into "the solution itself has a real cost to what Davis has always been." Deleting any
one Complication changes what the player is actually doing afterward, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's cast is the smallest of any so far — two cities, no Mirny, no logistics hub required —
and it's deliberately kept that way, since the actual dramatic engine (a genuine rigor-vs-accessibility
tension, not a resource dispute or a legitimacy question) doesn't need a third party to be complete. Its
strongest asset is that Davis's and Shirayuki's own established temperaments toward visibility are already,
independently, near-perfect opposites (§6(a) vs. §6(b) in their respective Local Cultures files) — this
chain's only real contribution is putting a genuine, felt reason for the two to need each other on top of
that existing contrast. Distinct from every prior candidate in cast size, in stakes (a research
methodology's own survival, not identity, legitimacy, logistics, or trust), and in never once routing
through Mirny — proof the subnet doesn't require its own hub city to produce a solid DLC-scale story.
