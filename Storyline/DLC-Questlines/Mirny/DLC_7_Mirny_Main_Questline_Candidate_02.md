# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #2: "Three Names, One Record"

**Status: held for reference, flagged 2026-07-21 — not currently usable as written.** The developer
correctly caught a real conflict: this chain explicitly frames the Long Record as "a real, legitimate
piece of the nationwide Planetary Split Brain puzzle," but the Planetary Split Brain is bindingly
established as an *unmarked* questline (`TODO.md` — "no quest marker, no XP payout, no tracker/journal
entry, no notifications," confirmed 2026-07-20). A DLC *main* questline is marked and tracked by
definition, so explicitly naming/confirming that connection inside it undercuts the Split Brain's own
design law. **The core of this chain — the three-cities cooperation test, the identity tension between
Zhongshan's "kept the name" stance and Sinheung's/Shirayuki's fresh self-naming, the tri-junction strike
forcing renegotiation — is still strong and worth keeping.** What needs revision, whenever this is
revisited: strip the explicit Split Brain framing (Zhongshan's "Long Record" can simply be the most
complete pre-war archive in the subnet, valuable for its own local reasons — cluster identity, its own
founding history — without the marked main quest ever stating or confirming it connects to the larger
Split Brain mystery; an attentive player is still free to *notice* that connection on their own, which is
exactly how the unmarked design is supposed to work).

**What this is:** a second prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, deliberately using different
source material from Candidate #1 (which centered on Vostok/Kunlun/Mirny). **Not canon, not chosen, not
the only candidate** — the 2nd of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain
schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a), same shape as Candidate #1.

**Deliberately uses only 3 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Zhongshan, Sinheung, Shirayuki — the Larsemann
Hills cluster) rather than forcing a wider cast. Per the developer's own direction: a solid questline
using fewer cities beats a padded one using all of them.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Mirny Subnet Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — the Larsemann Hills cluster's three cities
  (Zhongshan, Sinheung, Shirayuki) arrived at their present-day closeness via three different founding
  mechanisms, then a single shared wartime strike hit all three at their shared tri-junction
  infrastructure node (Hwy 4/110/22) simultaneously.
- **Sinheung's own Full Extrapolation, Section VI** — corrected and strengthened: all three Larsemann
  Hills cities were founded by the exact same three-way Jeju-do negotiation (Japan, Korea, the Sinian
  Federation) — a real diplomatic precedent between the three parties, agreed before any of them ever set
  foot on the ice, not organic neighborliness that happened to work out.
- **Zhongshan's own Full Extrapolation, Sections II–III** — Founding Elder Mèi Sun's own founding
  political stance, "we kept the name; we did not keep them" (Zhongshan deliberately kept its Chinese
  station name as a statement of continuity/distinctness); **"The Long Record,"** Zhongshan's own primary
  archive, established as "custodian of the most complete pre-Split-Brain history in the Mirny subnet."
- **Shirayuki's own Full Extrapolation, Section III** — the cluster's present-day warmth is the end state
  of a genuine multi-generational process (legal separation → de facto merger → full legal unification,
  per the Tri-Cities Amalgamation History), not something that existed from day one.
- **Mirny Subnet Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 3** — naming as a chosen, meaningful, repeatable
  civic act is uniquely concentrated in this subnet: Sinheung's own first-ever act of self-naming
  (2026-07-14) and Shirayuki's own freshly-completed naming (2026-07-08) both sit inside this exact
  cluster, alongside Zhongshan's own opposite choice — to keep an inherited name on purpose.
- **`Storyline/DLC_PSB_Framework.md`** — the already-established Planetary Split Brain main-story premise:
  the Long Night War's destruction of Amundsen Station severed all six subnets from each other, each now
  holding isolated, sometimes-conflicting historical records; the player assembles the true picture by
  reconciling them.
- **Explicitly excluded:** Sinheung's own chamber-manufacturing role and the wider Cradle network, per
  `Design_Principles.md` §IV. Sinheung's manufacturing identity is mentioned only as ordinary background
  color where the source material already states it (its "damaged but functional" status, its role in the
  local economy) — never as the chain's own causal engine or objective.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Word reaches the player of Zhongshan's "Long Record" — by established reputation, the most
complete surviving pre-Split-Brain archive anywhere in the Mirny subnet, and therefore a real, legitimate
piece of the nationwide Planetary Split Brain puzzle the player is already assembling elsewhere.

**BUT** — The Long Record turns out not to be purely Zhongshan's own city history. Because Zhongshan,
Sinheung, and Shirayuki were all founded by the same three-way Jeju-do negotiation, and the cluster's own
closeness only warmed into today's intimacy gradually across three generations of amalgamation (legal
separation → de facto merger → full legal unification), the Record's own threads are genuinely
intertwined with both neighboring cities' own founding and merger history — readable in full only with
context that exists piecemeal across all three cities, since full centralization never actually happened
even as the cluster politically unified.

**THEREFORE** — To extract anything genuinely useful for the larger Split Brain picture, the player has
to travel between and earn real standing with figures in all three cluster cities, not just Zhongshan —
each holding a different piece: Zhongshan's own archive, Sinheung's own still-forming sense of its
freshly self-named identity, Shirayuki's own quiet, layered reverence for its diplomatic origin.

**BUT** — The same tri-junction infrastructure node that took the cluster a century and a half to grow
into one shared thing is exactly what the Long Night War's own shared strike hit. Whatever pre-war
administrative or archival coordination once ran through that shared node is gone — the player isn't just
gathering scattered fragments, they're the first party in generations actually asking these three cities
to coordinate access to their own combined memory on purpose, rather than simply coexisting as neighbors
the way the post-war cluster has settled into.

**THEREFORE** — This becomes a real test of whether the cluster's hard-won unity can survive being asked
to actively cooperate under real stakes, not just quietly coexist — echoing, at civic scale, the same
three-way negotiation Japan, Korea, and the Sinian Federation once conducted at Jeju-do, except now
peer-to-peer between the cities themselves rather than top-down between nations.

**BUT** — Real tension surfaces once the request is made explicit: Zhongshan's own founding stance was a
deliberate assertion of continuity and distinctness ("we kept the name; we did not keep them"). Sinheung
just completed its own first-ever act of self-naming — a hard-won act of self-definition, still fresh.
Pooling three cities' separate institutional memories into one shared, cross-referenced record reads, to
some residents in each city, as exactly the kind of quiet erasure Zhongshan's own founding stance was
built to resist — while others see it as simply the next stage of the same amalgamation process that
already carried the cluster from legal separation to full legal unification.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the climax isn't a clean win: the
record can genuinely be pieced together, and does contribute a real, earned fragment to the larger
Planetary Split Brain puzzle — but the process forces all three cities to publicly answer a question their
post-war closeness had let them leave comfortably vague. Are Zhongshan, Sinheung, and Shirayuki three
distinct cities that simply cooperate closely, or is "the cluster" itself becoming something that now
supersedes each city's own separate identity? The DLC doesn't resolve this question — it makes it
impossible to keep avoiding.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the obstacle rather than delaying the same one: first
BUT widens the scope from one city's archive to three; second BUT removes the pre-war infrastructure that
would have made this easy, forcing the player into the role of active negotiator; third BUT turns a
logistics problem into a genuine identity/politics problem the cluster itself has to answer. Deleting any
one of the three Complications changes what the player is actually doing afterward, so none reads as a
disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's strongest asset is the same as Candidate #1's: almost nothing in it was invented. The
Jeju-do three-way founding, the amalgamation stages, Zhongshan's own "kept the name" stance, Sinheung's
and Shirayuki's own fresh naming acts, and the tri-junction's shared wartime strike were all already
sitting, separately, in established files — this chain's only real contribution is connecting them into
one causal line and tying that line into the already-established Planetary Split Brain main-story premise,
which no existing document currently does. Distinct from Candidate #1 in cast (Zhongshan/Sinheung/
Shirayuki vs. Vostok/Kunlun/Mirny), in theme (collective identity vs. individual civic legitimacy), and in
climax shape (a question about what a *cluster* is vs. a question about what a *hub* has earned) — worth
keeping both live as genuinely different directions for DLC 7, not near-duplicates of the same idea.
