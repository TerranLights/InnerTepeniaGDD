# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #17: "Whoever's Way Becomes Everyone's Way"

**What this is:** a seventeenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around a seventh distinct
linking mechanism for this run — forming a new joint manufacturing coalition and negotiating its own
production standard, rather than Candidate #11's strategic vulnerability assessment, Candidate #12's
resettlement-policy design, Candidate #13's unification-precedent evaluation, Candidate #14's physical-route
crisis, Candidate #15's credit-attribution/security campaign, or Candidate #16's cultural-exchange dilemma.
**Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Zhongshan, Sinheung, Davis, Mirny, Shirayuki). **Limits Sinheung's own
manufacturing material to its general economic and civic-character facts** — no reference to the Cradle
network or the player character's own origin, consistent with Candidates #11 and #15's own treatment of
adjacent material.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Post-war scarcity drives a push to form a joint manufacturing coalition, pooling Zhongshan's and
Sinheung's own manufacturing capacity, Davis's raw-material supply, Mirny's industrial and logistics hub
function, and Shirayuki's own design sensibility, aiming for efficient, standardized joint production of
urgently needed post-war goods. The player is asked to help broker the coalition's formation — a
straightforward, if ambitious, coalition-building task.

**BUT** — Real efficiency requires real standardization — common specs, shared processes, interoperable
parts. But Zhongshan's and Sinheung's own manufacturing excellence is rooted in each city's own distinct,
self-contained methods — one competency-driven, one forcefully assertive — and submitting to a shared
standard means giving up some of the very particular practice that makes each city's own output good in the
first place.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to negotiate how much standardization each city actually accepts, without
gutting the distinct expertise that makes their contribution worth having at all.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once a workable middle-ground standard actually takes shape: Zhongshan's and
Sinheung's own manufacturing approaches aren't just stylistically different — they're built on genuinely
different underlying tolerances and assumptions, meaning any real, working shared standard mathematically
favors one city's own existing tooling and methods more than the other's, however carefully it's negotiated.
Full symmetry isn't actually achievable given the two cities' own real technical differences.

**THEREFORE** — What began as negotiating a standard becomes a real question about which city's own approach
the coalition's shared standard actually ends up favoring, and whether the efficiency this delivers for Davis
and Mirny is worth the uneven cost it imposes on whichever of the two manufacturing cities comes out behind.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Adopting the shared standard delivers real
efficiency gains that genuinely benefit Davis's and Mirny's own stake in the coalition and the wider subnet's
urgent post-war need — but treats one city's own manufacturing culture as the baseline and the other's as the
deviation to correct, an uneven cost imposed on whichever city loses more of its own distinct practice.
Refusing to standardize preserves both Zhongshan's and Sinheung's own full autonomy and distinct methods
intact — but leaves the coalition's actual output far less efficient, undercutting the very reason Davis and
Mirny agreed to join in the first place.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether and how standardization proceeds: adopt a shared standard, delivering real efficiency at the
cost of an uneven burden on one manufacturing city's own practice; or preserve full autonomy for both,
protecting their distinct methods at the cost of the coalition's own practical effectiveness.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "form a coalition" into
"real efficiency requires giving up some of what makes each city's own work good"; the second BUT turns
"negotiate a middle ground" into "no truly symmetric middle ground actually exists, given the cities' real
technical differences"; the third BUT turns "standardize or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't
cost either real efficiency or an uneven burden on one city's own distinct practice." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it draws its central tension directly from the Larsemann Hills cluster's
own established, contrasting Enneagram reads — Zhongshan's competency and Sinheung's assertiveness — turning
a purely psychological contrast into a concrete, mechanical dilemma about whose manufacturing practice a
shared standard actually privileges. It's also the first Mirny subnet candidate in this run to treat
efficiency itself, rather than security, memory, or autonomy, as the value genuinely in tension with fairness,
giving DLC 7 a distinctly different flavor of trade-off than Candidates #11 through #16.
