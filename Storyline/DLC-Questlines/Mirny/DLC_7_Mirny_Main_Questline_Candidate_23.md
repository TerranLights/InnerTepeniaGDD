# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #23: "Whoever Gets to Read the Long Record"

**What this is:** a twenty-third prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around an archive-access policy
dilemma. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Zhongshan, Sinheung, Shirayuki, Davis, Mirny). **Explicitly does not
reference the Planetary Split Brain** — the Long Record is treated here strictly as the subnet's most
complete pre-war archive, valuable for its own local historical reasons, consistent with how this material
is meant to be used in a marked main questline.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Zhongshan Full Extrapolation, Section III** — the established Long Record, Zhongshan's own primary
  archive and "custodian of the most complete pre-Split-Brain history in the Mirny subnet."
- **Mirny Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — the Tri-Cities' own established three-stage amalgamation
  history (legal separation, then de facto merger, then full legal unification), used here only for the
  general fact that Zhongshan's own archive plausibly documents this shared process from its own institutional
  vantage point, not for any claim about which cluster city's account is more accurate.
- **Davis Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Davis's own established paleoclimate research specialty, used
  here to justify a genuine, legitimate outside research interest in comparative historical and
  environmental records.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about which cluster city's own perspective on the amalgamation is
  correct — this chain treats the risk of one-sided institutional framing as a real, structural possibility,
  not a settled judgment about Zhongshan's own account.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With outside researchers, including Davis's own paleoclimate scientists, increasingly interested
in studying the Long Record for legitimate historical and scientific purposes, the player is asked to help
negotiate broader access to Zhongshan's own archive — a straightforward, if sensitive, archive-access task.

**BUT** — Zhongshan's own community has real, mixed feelings about outside access. The Long Record isn't
just documentation — it's tied directly to the city's own civic identity and founding moral stance. Granting
broad access risks its contents being used or interpreted by outsiders in ways that could distort or
appropriate Zhongshan's own understanding of its own history, while restricting access denies real,
legitimate historical and scientific value to researchers elsewhere.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to negotiate specific access terms, consulting Zhongshan's own archivists and
the outside researchers, including those from Davis, about what's actually being requested and why, while
Sinheung and Shirayuki, the Long Record's own cluster neighbors, weigh in on how the archive concerns them
too.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the archive's own actual contents are reviewed for access purposes: parts
of the Long Record document the Tri-Cities' own shared amalgamation history specifically from Zhongshan's own
institutional vantage point. Sinheung and Shirayuki, having arrived at today's closely-entangled cluster
identity through a genuinely different founding mechanism of their own, have a real stake in whether that
one-sided framing gets exposed to wider scholarship as though it were simply "the record," rather than one
city's own account of a shared history.

**THEREFORE** — What began as an access-negotiation task becomes a real question about whether opening the
archive risks surfacing and spreading an institutionally one-sided account of a history all three cluster
cities actually share.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Granting broad access advances real historical
and scientific scholarship, giving researchers across the subnet genuine new knowledge — but risks
publicizing one-sided or incomplete framing of the shared amalgamation process, a real risk of reopening
tensions between communities that took a century and a half to actually settle into their current unity.
Restricting access protects against that risk and keeps Zhongshan in control of how its own archive gets
used — but denies legitimate outside researchers real, valuable knowledge, and leaves the subnet's own most
complete pre-war record effectively locked away.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether the Long Record's access actually gets opened or restricted: open it, advancing real
scholarship at the cost of a genuine risk to inter-cluster relations; or restrict it, protecting against that
risk at the cost of real, legitimate research being denied.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "grant access" into
"Zhongshan itself is genuinely divided about whether outside access is safe"; the second BUT turns "negotiate
terms" into "the archive's own contents risk exposing a one-sided account of a history three cities actually
share"; the third BUT turns "open or restrict" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either real
scholarship or real inter-cluster stability." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is
actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it uses the Long Record strictly as what it is on its own local terms —
Zhongshan's own primary archive and civic anchor — without ever naming or confirming any connection to the
nationwide Planetary Split Brain mystery, consistent with that mystery's own binding design law (unmarked,
untracked, discoverable only by an attentive player, never stated outright in a marked main questline). An
archive valuable for its own institutional reasons turns out to matter to more than just the city that keeps
it. It's also the first candidate in this run to treat an archive's own existence, rather than an event or a
resource, as the actual object the whole chain revolves around.
