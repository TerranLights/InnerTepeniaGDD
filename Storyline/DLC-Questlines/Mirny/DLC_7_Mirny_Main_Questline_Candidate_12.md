# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #12: "However You Happen to Land"

**What this is:** a twelfth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around a second distinct linking
mechanism for this run — designing a post-war resettlement policy across genuinely different civic
integration traditions, rather than Candidate #11's strategic vulnerability assessment. **Not canon, not
chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Casey, Zhongshan, Kunlun, Vostok, Davis).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 1** — the subnet's own near-complete taxonomy of demographic
  -formation mechanisms: Zhongshan's organic multi-generational immigration (a fully-formed founding culture
  later arrivals simply integrated into); Kunlun's deliberate, function-based curated draw (a re-resolved,
  19-nation population selected for specific credentials); Vostok's unresolved demographic gap (a later,
  unconnected population with zero ancestral claim to the founding legend, an ambient fact rather than a
  traceable event); and Davis's genuinely dispersed 13-nation Notable tier, with no single community
  concentrated enough to dominate.
- **Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2** — Casey's established destruction (a genuine dual highway
  chokepoint), used here as the source of this chain's own displaced population.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific figure for how many of Casey's survivors actually went where — this
  chain treats the resettlement's own scale as a live, working problem the player has to help organize, not a
  fixed, pre-determined outcome.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Casey destroyed, its surviving population has scattered into other Mirny subnet cities as
displaced persons in need of formal resettlement. The player is asked to help design a fair, workable
framework for integrating Casey's own displaced survivors across the subnet's receiving communities — a
straightforward, if significant, resettlement-policy task.

**BUT** — The cities actually receiving these survivors have fundamentally different, historically-earned
traditions for how someone becomes genuinely part of them. Zhongshan's own organic culture expects newcomers
to integrate gradually into an already-formed identity, with no formal admission process. Kunlun's own
curated-draw tradition expects newcomers to meet specific functional credentials to really belong. Vostok's
own unresolved demographic gap means its current population simply *is*, ambiently, without an origin story
to explain how it came to "belong," with no ancestral claim required at all. None of these traditions applies
cleanly to displaced war survivors arriving with none of the qualifications each city's own history assumes.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually negotiate a workable resettlement framework city by city,
reconciling or overriding each destination's own distinct integration tradition in turn.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once Davis enters the picture: its own genuinely dispersed, 13-nation
demographic character, with no dominant community to assimilate into, gives Casey's survivors neither a
clear existing culture to blend into (unlike Zhongshan) nor a clear standard to meet (unlike Kunlun) — meaning
the city that looks, on its face, the most naturally open turns out to be the hardest place to design a clear
resettlement policy for, precisely because it never had a clear integration tradition of its own to begin
with.

**THEREFORE** — What began as organizing a resettlement plan becomes a real question about whether a single,
consistent policy or each city's own distinct tradition should actually govern how Casey's survivors are
received.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. A uniform, subnet-wide resettlement policy
gives Casey's survivors consistent, predictable treatment no matter where they land — but forces every
receiving city to set aside its own distinct, historically-earned integration tradition in favor of something
imposed from outside. Letting each city apply its own tradition to its own share of displaced survivors
respects each destination's own established civic character, Davis's genuine openness included — but leaves
Casey's own survivors facing wildly inconsistent treatment depending entirely on which city they happened to
land in, through no fault or choice of their own.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which approach the resettlement framework actually takes: a uniform policy, giving survivors
consistent treatment at the cost of overriding each city's own earned tradition; or tradition-by-tradition
resettlement, honoring each city's own civic character at the cost of real, arbitrary inconsistency in how
survivors of the same disaster actually get treated.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "resettle the
survivors" into "each destination's own integration tradition assumes qualifications these survivors don't
have"; the second BUT turns "reconcile the traditions city by city" into "the seemingly most open city is
actually the hardest case, for having no tradition at all"; the third BUT turns "pick a framework" into
"there is no framework that doesn't cost either consistency for the survivors or respect for each city's own
earned character." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so
none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Pattern 1's own taxonomy — already flagged as a reference set
worth checking any future city's demographic-formation story against — and stress-tests it directly, asking
what happens when a taxonomy built to describe how cities historically formed has to actually accommodate new
people right now. It's also the first Mirny subnet candidate to give Casey, a destroyed city, a genuine
throughline into the present-day questline via its own scattered population, rather than leaving it as a
purely historical case study the way Candidate #11 and the subnet's own war-damage taxonomy otherwise treat
it.
