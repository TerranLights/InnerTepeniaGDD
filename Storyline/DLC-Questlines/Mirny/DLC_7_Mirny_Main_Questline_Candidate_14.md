# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #14: "Whichever Way Still Passes Through"

**What this is:** a fourteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around a fourth distinct linking
mechanism for this run — a physical-route repair crisis along a single established highway, rather than
Candidate #11's strategic vulnerability assessment, Candidate #12's resettlement-policy design, or Candidate
#13's unification-precedent evaluation. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear
Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Zhongshan, Davis, Mirny, Casey, Vostok) — four of these five lie
directly on the established route, with Vostok weighing in as a distant, principled voice.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **`Highways.md`, Hwy 110 (the "Coastal Cut Highway")** — the established, literal route: Zhongshan →
  Davis → Mirny → Casey → Concordia, the subnet's own main East Antarctic coastal route, confirmed
  hitchhiking-valid.
- **Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2 / Casey Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Casey's own established
  destruction as a genuine dual highway chokepoint, one strike severing two supply corridors — Hwy 2 and Hwy
  110 both — at once, meaning the route this chain repairs has been precariously patched together since the
  war rather than freshly damaged now.
- **Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2** — Vostok's own established extreme remoteness, used here to give
  it a natural, principled stake in a debate about whether inconvenient distance justifies writing a place out
  of the subnet's own everyday geography.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Hwy 2's own parallel status past Casey toward Dumont d'Urville —
  this chain treats Hwy 110's own coastal route as its sole concern, not the wider Janbogo/Ross subnet
  reroute already established elsewhere.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The precarious, patched-together stretch of Hwy 110 running through Casey's own war-ruined
chokepoint finally shows signs of imminent, complete failure. The player is asked to travel the route —
Zhongshan, Davis, Mirny, then Casey's own ruins — to assess the damage and help organize a permanent fix
before the subnet's main coastal route is severed for good — a straightforward, if urgent, infrastructure
-crisis task.

**BUT** — A full, permanent repair through Casey's own ruins would take real time and resources. A genuine
alternative exists: a considerably longer inland detour that works perfectly well, but routes traffic — and
the informal hitchhiking movement Hwy 110 is established as supporting — entirely away from Casey's own
location. Choosing it would complete, quietly and administratively, what the war's own strike started: the
last living connection to where Casey once stood, severed from the subnet's everyday traffic for good.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually consult the communities along the route — Zhongshan, Davis, and
Mirny — about which option they'd genuinely prefer, rather than simply picking the more efficient engineering
solution.

**BUT** — The reversal deepens once real consultation gets underway: Davis and Mirny, closest to the
affected stretch and most inconvenienced by any prolonged closure, increasingly favor the permanent inland
reroute, since it would be faster and safer going forward regardless of Casey. Zhongshan, more distant
communities, and Vostok — whose own established remoteness gives it real, felt standing on the question of
what inconvenient distance should and shouldn't justify — feel strongly that abandoning the route through
Casey's ruins would be a second, quieter erasure of a city already destroyed once.

**THEREFORE** — What began as choosing an engineering option becomes a real disagreement about whether
practical convenience or a connective obligation to a destroyed city should govern the subnet's own
infrastructure going forward.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Permanently rerouting inland away from Casey's
ruins gives Davis, Mirny, and the wider subnet a faster, safer, more reliable route indefinitely — but
deliberately erases the last everyday connection to Casey's own location, completing what the original strike
started. Committing to the difficult, costly repair through Casey's own ruins preserves that connection and
honors whatever memorial and practical value it still holds — but imposes real, ongoing cost and risk on
Davis's and Mirny's own daily traffic for a stretch of road serving a city that no longer exists to use it.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which option the subnet actually commits to: the inland reroute, delivering lasting practical benefit
at the cost of permanently erasing Casey's own last connection to the subnet's everyday geography; or the
costly repair through Casey's ruins, preserving that connection at the cost of real, ongoing burden on the
cities that actually use the road today.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "repair the highway"
into "the efficient fix and the connective fix are two different routes entirely"; the second BUT turns
"consult the communities" into "the communities closest to the cost and the communities most invested in the
memory genuinely disagree"; the third BUT turns "pick a route" into "there is no route that doesn't cost
either lasting practical benefit or Casey's own last connection to the subnet." Deleting any one Complication
changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Casey's own established "single strike, dual chokepoint"
destruction (already this subnet's cleanest war-damage finding) and asks what that original strike is still
quietly costing the subnet, generations of patchwork later, rather than treating the destruction as a closed
historical fact. It also gives Vostok a genuinely different kind of role than Candidates #11 or #13 — not as
a stakeholder in its own infrastructure or unification question, but as a principled outside voice whose own
lived experience of remoteness gives it real standing on whether distance should ever be allowed to erase a
place from the map.
