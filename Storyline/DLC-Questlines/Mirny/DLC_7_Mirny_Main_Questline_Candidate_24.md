# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #24: "Whoever's Order Gets Bumped"

**What this is:** a twenty-fourth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around an environmental-crisis
response competing against an existing manufacturing commitment. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Davis, Zhongshan, Mirny, Sinheung, Vostok).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Davis Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Davis's own varied, differently-salinated lake system and its
  genuinely valuable paleoclimate sediment-core research, used here as both a food-resource base and a
  scientifically irreplaceable dataset at risk.
- **Zhongshan Full Extrapolation, Section IV** — Zhongshan's own established precision-manufacturing
  capacity, ~25% of its Division of Industry, used here as the only source capable of producing specialized
  monitoring or filtration equipment on short notice.
- **Mirny's own established doubled hub function** (Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3), used here for its
  general logistics-coordination role.
- **Sinheung's own established post-war reconstruction needs**, used here only as a general, ordinary
  rebuilding priority, not connected to its chamber-manufacturing role.
- **Vostok's own established research legacy**, used here strictly for its general environmental-monitoring
  and research-methodology experience, not for any connection to Mountain Pass or the Cradle network.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any reference to the Cradle chamber network, Mountain Pass, or the player
  character's own origin.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Davis's own lake ecosystems show signs of a genuine ecological disruption, threatening both the
food resources the city depends on and the integrity of its own uniquely valuable paleoclimate sediment
records. The player is asked to investigate the cause and coordinate a response — a straightforward, if
urgent, environmental-crisis task.

**BUT** — Stopping the disruption requires specialized monitoring equipment that only Zhongshan's own
precision-manufacturing sector can build on short notice, delivered through Mirny's own logistics hub. But
Zhongshan's manufacturing capacity is already fully committed to an existing, previously promised order.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to travel to Zhongshan and consult Vostok's own research methodology to
understand exactly what equipment is actually needed, then negotiate whether and how Zhongshan's existing
commitment can be adjusted.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the existing order is identified: it's for Sinheung's own post-war
reconstruction materials, an ordinary but genuinely urgent rebuilding need, not an abstract inconvenience.
Helping Davis means directly delaying Sinheung's own recovery.

**THEREFORE** — What began as sourcing equipment becomes a real choice between two cities' own equally
legitimate, time-sensitive needs.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Diverting Zhongshan's capacity to Davis
addresses a genuine environmental crisis and protects irreplaceable scientific data before it's lost for
good — but delays Sinheung's own ongoing reconstruction, a real, felt hardship for a city already working to
rebuild. Maintaining Sinheung's original priority protects its own recovery timeline and honors a commitment
already made — but leaves Davis's ecological crisis, and a body of research data that can't be recovered once
lost, to keep deteriorating.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which priority actually gets served first: help Davis, protecting irreplaceable data and its food
supply at the cost of delaying Sinheung's own reconstruction; or hold to Sinheung's original priority,
honoring that commitment at the cost of a real, possibly permanent loss at Davis.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "source equipment" into
"the only source is already fully committed elsewhere"; the second BUT turns "negotiate a change" into "the
competing priority is just as legitimate and urgent as the one the player is trying to solve"; the third BUT
turns "help Davis or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either irreplaceable data or another
city's own recovery timeline." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at
the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that its dilemma comes from scarce production capacity itself, rather than
from disagreement, ideology, or hidden motive — Zhongshan isn't refusing to help, it simply doesn't have
enough capacity for both needs at once, making the conflict a genuine zero-sum resource problem rather than a
dispute to mediate. It's also the first candidate in this run to put a scientifically irreplaceable dataset
on one side of the scale against another city's own ordinary, lived reconstruction need, rather than weighing
two comparable kinds of value against each other.
