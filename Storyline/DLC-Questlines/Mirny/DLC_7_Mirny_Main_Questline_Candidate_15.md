# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #15: "Whatever Gets Written Down Gets Aimed At"

**What this is:** a fifteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around a fifth distinct linking
mechanism for this run — a public credit-attribution campaign for chronically undervalued infrastructure,
rather than Candidate #11's strategic vulnerability assessment, Candidate #12's resettlement-policy design,
Candidate #13's unification-precedent evaluation, or Candidate #14's physical-route crisis. **Not canon, not
chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Davis, Sinheung, Mirny, Zhongshan, Shirayuki). **Explicitly limits
Sinheung's chamber-manufacturing material to its general, established economic-and-civic-credit facts** — it
does not reference Mountain Pass, the Cradle network, or the player character's own origin, consistent with
Candidate #11's own treatment of adjacent material.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3** — the subnet's own confirmed instance of a project-wide civic
  theme: Davis's triple-reinforced "enabler" identity (namesake, real-world precedent, and current economy
  all converging independently), Sinheung's own "quietly load-bearing infrastructure... despite modest
  political profile" chamber-manufacturing role, and Mirny's own doubled hub function (communications and
  industrial together) — three separate instances of "does genuinely consequential work and receives none of
  the visible credit for it."
- **Mirny Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2** — the subnet's own established single-principle war-damage logic
  (infrastructure value and reachability alone determine targeting), used here to justify the genuine security
  tension that documenting exactly how valuable this infrastructure is would also create.
- **Mirny Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — the Zhongshan/Sinheung/Shirayuki cluster's own shared
  tri-junction infrastructure, used here to bring Zhongshan and Shirayuki into the campaign as Sinheung's own
  immediate neighbors and stakeholders.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any reference to Mountain Pass, the Cradle chamber-manufacturing network, or the
  player character's own origin — Sinheung's manufacturing role is treated here strictly as an economic and
  civic-recognition question, not the specific plot thread Throughway 1 traces elsewhere.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — An effort begins to formally document and publicly credit the subnet's own chronically
undervalued infrastructure work: Davis's real, essential enabler role, Sinheung's own quiet
chamber-manufacturing capacity, and Mirny's own doubled hub function. The player is asked to help gather
documentation and push for formal recognition — a straightforward, if far-reaching, credit-attribution
campaign.

**BUT** — Publicly documenting exactly how essential this infrastructure actually is does double duty: it's
also, necessarily, a precise, detailed roadmap of the subnet's own most strategically valuable, most
reachable targets — exactly the profile Pattern 2 already establishes as what determines who gets struck.
The same information that would finally earn these contributors their due credit is the same information that
would make their vulnerability legible to anyone looking for it.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to weigh, city by city, whether the case for public recognition is actually
worth the security exposure publicizing it would create.

**BUT** — The reversal deepens once the cost of staying quiet becomes concrete: without public recognition,
Davis in particular continues to be treated across the subnet as a minor, dependent feeder city rather than
the genuinely essential contributor Pattern 3 already confirms it is — a status that shapes real, material
things, from how much say Davis gets in subnet-wide decisions to how much investment its own development
actually receives.

**THEREFORE** — What began as weighing security against recognition becomes a real question about whether
security-through-obscurity is worth continuing to materially shortchange Davis, and to a lesser extent
Sinheung and Mirny, indefinitely.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Full public documentation finally gives
Davis, Sinheung, and Mirny the material recognition, investment, and voice their actual contributions
warrant — but hands any future adversary an accurate, detailed map of exactly which infrastructure matters
most, undoing whatever quiet security margin obscurity has provided until now. Keeping the full picture
undocumented preserves that margin — but leaves Davis in particular, materially shortchanged indefinitely,
treated as less important than it actually is, for the sake of a security benefit that's real but diffuse and
hard to point to.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether the campaign actually goes public in full, partially, or not at all: full documentation,
delivering real material recognition at the cost of a genuine security exposure; or continued quiet,
preserving that security margin at the cost of leaving Davis and its peers materially shortchanged for as long
as the silence holds.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "document the credit"
into "the documentation is also a targeting roadmap"; the second BUT turns "weigh security against
recognition" into "staying quiet has a real, ongoing material cost, not just an abstract security benefit";
the third BUT turns "go public or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either real security
margin or real material recognition these cities have earned." Deleting any one Complication changes what the
player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Pattern 3's own project-wide "does the work, gets no credit"
theme — already confirmed as recurring across three subnets — and gives it a genuinely different complication
than Halley subnet's own version of the same mechanism (Candidate #11, credit withheld by shared cultural
value): here, credit is withheld for a hard security reason directly traceable to this specific subnet's own
established war-damage logic (Pattern 2), making the trade-off concrete and checkable rather than a matter of
civic temperament. It also gives Davis its most materially consequential appearance in this run yet, treating
its own triple-reinforced enabler identity as something the subnet is actively, if unintentionally,
shortchanging in the present, not just a historical curiosity.
