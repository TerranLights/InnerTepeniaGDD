# DLC 7 (Mirny Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #6: "What the Numbers Don't Say"

**What this is:** a sixth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 7's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mirny subnet, built around a temperament/trust dynamic
rather than an identity question (Candidate #2's shape), a logistics crisis (#5), or an ethical exception
test (#4). **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 6th of the "at least 10" the method
calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 3 of Mirny subnet's 8 cities** (Sinheung, Zhongshan, Mirny) — deliberately re-pairs Sinheung and
Zhongshan (already both present in flagged Candidate #2) around a completely different engine, to test
whether the pairing itself has more than one story in it.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Sinheung's own Local Cultures, §6 (Social Contract & Unwritten Rules)** — items (d) and (e): Sinheung
  "performs its identity outward precisely because it has something to prove," with structurally low
  privacy/high mutual visibility, "the opposite of Zhongshan's non-prying insularity" — a real, established
  temperament fact, not invented for this chain.
- **Sinheung's own Local Cultures, §6(b)** — "Hospitality character: practical and generous rather than
  ceremonial; less patience for performance than Zhongshan's ritual warmth next door."
- **Zhongshan's own established "chaos that is actually structure" civic personality** (`District_Canon
  ` cross-reference via Mirny Subnet Cross-City Patterns) — genuine private confidence, no felt need to
  publicly justify itself.
- **Sinheung's own Local Cultures, §23** — explicitly flagged, unresolved: "Mirny (the city)... Genuine
  relationship TBD." This chain proposes one concrete answer to that open question.
- **Sinheung's own Local Cultures, §15** — heavy industrial fabrication (45%) dependent on raw materials
  trucked in from Mirny via the Hwy 110/Hwy 4 tri-junction at Zhongshan — a real, established supply
  relationship this chain's allocation dispute runs through.
- **Explicitly excluded:** the wider Cradle network as a directed quest objective. Sinheung's own local
  chamber-manufacturing recovery is referenced only as ordinary background economic fact (explicitly
  permitted per `Design_Principles.md` §IV — local, in-DLC-scale references are fine; only the *nationwide
  network itself* as a quest objective is forbidden), never as the chain's actual causal engine.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Post-war reconstruction materials, routed through Mirny's own industrial output, need dividing
between two real, competing local priorities: Zhongshan's precision-manufacturing recovery and Sinheung's
own local fabrication recovery. Resources are genuinely limited; some real trade-off has to be made, by
someone, using imperfect information.

**BUT** — Whatever the actual allocation ends up being, it's genuinely ambiguous — not obviously unfair,
but not obviously, provably fair either. Sinheung's own established civic temperament — a city that
"performs its identity outward precisely because it has something to prove," low on the kind of quiet
privacy Zhongshan's culture takes for granted — reads that ambiguity as evidence of being treated as an
afterthought, consistent with its own founding-by-diplomatic-imposition history and its constant, felt need
to defend a claim that still feels like it needs defending.

**THEREFORE** — Sinheung's own representatives escalate, publicly and directly (consistent with their own
established "low privacy, high mutual visibility" civic style), demanding redress — creating real friction
with Zhongshan, whose own established private, non-prying, quietly confident temperament means it doesn't
feel any need to publicly justify or contest anything, which Sinheung's own representatives could easily
misread as Zhongshan simply getting away with something specifically because it doesn't make noise.

**BUT** — When the player actually digs into the real allocation math, the numbers turn out to be
genuinely, defensibly fair — neither city is actually being shorted. The real problem isn't the resource
split at all; it's that the same ambiguous situation produces two structurally different reactions from two
cities with genuinely different relationships to trust and visibility, and nothing about the process itself
was ever built to be legible to a city that can't simply take quiet reassurance at face value.

**THEREFORE** — The player's actual task shifts from "resolve a resource dispute" (already effectively
true) to "make the allocation process itself concretely legible and provable," specifically because
Sinheung's own psychology genuinely requires that, not because anyone did anything wrong.

**BUT** — Building a more transparent, provable process — exactly what Sinheung genuinely needs to feel
secure — risks reading, to Zhongshan, as precisely the kind of performance and prying its own culture has
never needed and doesn't particularly want. A solution built around one cluster city's real psychological
needs isn't free for its neighbor.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution finds a genuinely
workable arrangement — Sinheung gets a real, concrete, provable process it can point to and trust; Zhongshan
quietly tolerates somewhat more visibility than it would ideally choose, for the cluster's own sake — but
neither city's underlying temperament actually changes. The next genuinely ambiguous decision could
plausibly reopen the exact same dynamic, and everyone involved knows it.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the obstacle: first BUT turns "divide resources fairly"
into "the ambiguity itself gets read as a slight by one city specifically"; second BUT turns "resolve the
dispute" into "the dispute was never really about the resources"; third BUT turns "build a transparent
process" into "the fix for one city's needs has a real cost for its neighbor." Deleting any one Complication
changes what the player is actually doing afterward, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate deliberately reuses two cities already present in the flagged Candidate #2 (Sinheung,
Zhongshan) to test whether that pairing has more than one real story in it — it does: Candidate #2 was
about collective identity and what a "cluster" is; this one is about trust, visibility, and psychological
security, a genuinely different register built from a different, previously-unused source fact (Sinheung's
own §6 temperament profile) rather than the founding/naming material Candidate #2 drew on. It also proposes
a concrete answer to an explicitly flagged open question (Sinheung's own "relationship to Mirny: TBD"),
which no other candidate has done. Worth noting the climax's own honesty: unlike a story that would resolve
the temperament gap itself, this one treats the gap as a permanent, real fact about these two cities that
good process can accommodate but not erase — consistent with the project's own preference for real,
continuing texture over tidy character-arc resolutions at the civic scale.
