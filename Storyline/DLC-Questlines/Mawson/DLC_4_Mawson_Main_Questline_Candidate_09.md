# DLC 4 (Mawson Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #9: "Not Quite an Empty Room"

**What this is:** a ninth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 4's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mawson subnet, built around Dome Fuji's own flagged timeline
correction — genuinely fresh material, distinct from every prior Dome Fuji candidate in this set. **Not
canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 9th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the
Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Mawson subnet's 3 cities (Dome Fuji, its fifth appearance in this set, each time on different
established material)**.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Dome Fuji's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 4** — the established aviation-supply timeline
  implies the "unknown span" of vacancy between the humans' departure and the pilgrims' rediscovery should be
  read as short, plausibly one to three decades rather than centuries, for the timeline to hold together
  cleanly. This raises a genuinely new possibility, explicitly flagged as "not canon, just newly available":
  the very first pilgrims may have arrived while a handful of the last departing humans were still present —
  an actual, if small, overlap between Dome Fuji's two otherwise cleanly separated eras.
- **Dome Fuji's own Full Extrapolation, Section II** — the community's own founding myth, as currently
  understood: the first devotees found the station's infrastructure intact but genuinely empty, "not a ruin
  to reclaim... a monument to absence" — proof of concept for a faith built around cold and cessation as
  spiritually significant rather than tragic.
- **Dome Fuji's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 1** — the same "frozen moments, no institutional
  memory" fact used elsewhere at Dome Fuji applies here too: nobody currently present has firsthand memory of
  the actual transition, making any reconstruction of the true timeline genuine forensic work rather than
  living testimony.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about the ancient caretaker robot proposed in Finding 7 (used directly
  in Candidate #2) — this chain works from documentary and physical evidence alone, so it stands independently
  of whether that candidate's own thread is ever developed.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A historical inquiry — sparked by an old station log, a discrepancy someone notices in the
established timeline, or simple curiosity from within the devotee community itself — wants to pin down
exactly when the human research mission actually ended relative to when the first pilgrims arrived. The
community's own founding myth holds that the first devotees found the station truly empty, a monument to
absence. The player is asked to help verify the timeline using whatever physical and documentary evidence
Dome Fuji's own archives still hold — a straightforward historical-research task.

**BUT** — Dome Fuji's own established information environment works against the investigation: no living
memory of the actual transition survives among the current devotee population, and the physical evidence is
scattered across old station logs, maintenance records, and transfer paperwork nobody has ever had reason to
cross-reference carefully. Establishing a real, evidence-backed timeline is genuine forensic work, not a
matter of simply asking someone who remembers.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually piece together the fragmentary evidence, comparing departure
records against the earliest confirmed pilgrimage-era dates to establish what actually happened, rather than
what the community has always assumed happened.

**BUT** — The reversal lands hard: the evidence confirms a real overlap. The first pilgrims arrived while a
handful of the very last human researchers, and plausibly their own support robots, were still physically
present — however briefly. The community's entire founding myth — arrival into pure, empty absence, proof of
concept for a faith built around cold and cessation as spiritually significant rather than tragic — turns
out to be factually wrong. There was no monument to absence waiting for them. There were, for some real
window of time, still people there.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a timeline verification becomes a genuine theological problem: the devotee
community's own founding story, the thing that first proved the faith's core conviction correct, didn't
actually happen the way everyone has always believed.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Revealing the historically accurate but
theologically disruptive truth honors what actually happened and respects the devotee community's own
capacity to hold a faith that survives being factually corrected — but genuinely challenges a founding
conviction some devotees may have built real, personal meaning around for years. Letting the comfortable,
established founding myth stand uncorrected protects a real, meaningful piece of shared religious identity
from being upended by inconvenient fact — but means the faith's own origin story, and the "proof of concept"
it's always pointed to, rests on something that didn't actually happen quite the way everyone continues to
believe.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
choice in what the historical record actually says: the honest, corrected timeline, respecting the community
enough to trust it with an uncomfortable truth about its own founding story, at the cost of unsettling a
conviction some devotees have built real personal meaning around; or the comfortable, established myth left
standing, protecting that meaning fully intact, at the cost of a faith whose own founding "proof" quietly
isn't true.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "verify a date" into
"there's no living memory to verify it against, only scattered physical evidence"; the second BUT turns
"establish the timeline" into "the true timeline directly contradicts the community's own founding myth";
the third BUT turns "report the finding" into "there is no version of reporting it that doesn't cost either
honest respect for the community or a piece of genuine, felt religious meaning." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And
Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes a small, explicitly-flagged timeline correction — one to three
decades of vacancy instead of centuries — and follows it all the way to its most theologically consequential
possible implication, rather than treating it as a minor continuity fix. It's also the first Mawson subnet
candidate whose central tension is genuinely theological rather than civic, political, or personal — testing
whether a faith built on a specific historical claim can survive learning that claim was never quite true.
