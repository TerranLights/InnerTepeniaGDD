# DLC 4 (Mawson Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #8: "The First One From There"

**What this is:** an eighth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 4's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mawson subnet, built around Dome Fuji's own explicitly
-flagged, still-open narrative gap: no native Concordian devotee exists yet. **Not canon, not chosen, not the
only candidate** — the 8th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain
schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Mawson subnet's 3 cities (Dome Fuji, its fourth appearance in this set, each time on different
established material)** — Concordia is referenced through its own already-established political character,
not developed further here.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Dome Fuji's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 10** — the established pilgrimage pattern drew Dome
  Fuji's devotee population from every Tepenian city *except* Concordia, simply because no Concordian ever
  happened to make the pilgrimage, not anything about Concordia's people. Nothing rules out an individual
  Concordian making the pilgrimage in the present day — such a devotee "would carry real, earned weight
  without requiring any invented lore to justify it," and real potential relevance to Concordia's own
  district-tension storylines: a citizen choosing transcendence over participation in "the true city" project.
- **Dome Fuji's own Local Cultures, Section 6a** — arrival is itself the credential; devotion is the only
  membership requirement, with origin explicitly treated as irrelevant to standing within the community.
- **Dome Fuji's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 9** — the devotee population has, in the overwhelming
  majority of cases, no lived experience of humans at all, let alone direct experience of Concordia
  specifically — any arrival carrying real, current knowledge of "the last major surviving city" would be
  a genuine informational and emotional event for the whole community.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any development of Concordia's own specific district politics or factions beyond
  the general, already-established fact that leaving Concordia to pursue something else can read as a real
  political statement within it — this chain treats that political fallout as background pressure, not new
  Concordia-district content.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A Concordian citizen — the first ever to attempt it — sets out to
make the pilgrimage to Dome Fuji. The player is asked to help them survive the genuinely hazardous interior
plateau crossing, a straightforward escort-and-support task for an unprecedented, but on its face personal,
journey.

**BUT** — Leaving Concordia for total isolation isn't a politically neutral choice within Concordia's own
current moment. Concordia is the one city everyone else is actively trying to rebuild around, "the true
city" project in progress, and a citizen choosing transcendence and withdrawal over participation reads, to
different parties back home, as a real statement — some hearing it as implicit critique of Concordia's own
direction, others fearing it as a precedent that could invite others to follow.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to manage not just the physical crossing but the political fallout building
behind them, protecting the pilgrim's own choice from becoming a symbol they never asked to carry.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the pilgrim actually reaches Dome Fuji and prepares to formally arrive.
Devotion is supposed to be the only credential — origin is supposed to dissolve on arrival, the same way it
does for every other devotee here. But being the very first person ever to arrive from Concordia
specifically makes this pilgrim an object of real, unavoidable curiosity within Dome Fuji's own community —
the one city almost nobody here has any current, lived information about at all. The exact anonymity they
may have come seeking is complicated by the fact that where they're from is, for the first time in Dome
Fuji's history, genuinely remarkable to the people already here.

**THEREFORE** — What began as helping someone complete a personal pilgrimage becomes a real question about
whether this person can actually get the dissolution of identity Dome Fuji is supposed to offer, or whether
being first from somewhere that matters this much makes that dissolution impossible for them specifically.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Letting the community mark and remember this
as a real, significant event — the first Concordian ever to arrive — gives devotees something genuinely
moving, consistent with how deeply isolated they otherwise are from any current knowledge of Tepenia's last
major city, but it means this pilgrim never actually gets the full anonymity that arrival is supposed to
grant everyone equally, a real exception carved into a faith that explicitly claims not to make them.
Insisting on being treated exactly like any other devotee, with no special notice of where they came from,
honors the "arrival is the only credential" ideal completely — but denies the wider community something
real and meaningful, and denies the historical fact of this moment its own actual weight.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how this pilgrim's arrival is actually handled: let it be marked and remembered as the significant
event it genuinely is, at the cost of the pilgrim's own full, promised anonymity; or insist on total,
unmarked equality with every other devotee, honoring the faith's own founding principle fully, at the cost
of a real, meaningful moment — for the whole community, not just this one person — going quietly unspoken.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "help someone make a
pilgrimage" into "the pilgrimage itself is a political event back home, whether the pilgrim wants that or
not"; the second BUT turns "arrive safely" into "arriving here doesn't erase identity the way it's supposed
to, for this specific person"; the third BUT turns "mark the moment or don't" into "there is no version that
doesn't cost either the pilgrim's own promised anonymity or the community's own genuine, earned occasion."
Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a
disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it directly answers an invitation the project's own material issued to
itself — Dome Fuji's own Cross-Reference Synthesis explicitly proposing that a first Concordian devotee
"would carry real, earned weight without requiring any invented lore to justify it" — using only facts
already established rather than inventing new backstory for Concordia. It's also the first Mawson subnet
candidate whose central tension tests the faith's own founding principle directly: that arrival dissolves
where you're from, tried against the one arrival where where-you're-from turns out to matter more than it
ever has before.
