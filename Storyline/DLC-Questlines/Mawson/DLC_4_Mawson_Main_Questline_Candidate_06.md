# DLC 4 (Mawson Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #6: "Not Everyone Wanted to Be Welcomed"

**What this is:** a sixth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 4's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mawson subnet, the first candidate to pair Mawson and Dome
Fuji directly. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 6th of the "at least 10" the method
calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 2 of Mawson subnet's 3 cities** (Mawson, Dome Fuji).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Mawson Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section I** and **Mawson's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding
  2** — Mawson's hospitality character and honeymoon-destination reputation were both deliberately,
  institutionally cultivated over decades — "genuinely easy to make friends here" is a real, earned civic
  trait, not incidental.
- **Dome Fuji's own Local Cultures, Section 6a** *(cited directly in its own Cross-Reference Synthesis,
  Finding 7)* — "a resident's presence at Dome Fuji is itself a credential; no one arrives here by accident,
  drift, or economic necessity" — arrival is treated as a serious, legitimate act, not something to be
  second-guessed by people back home.
- **Dome Fuji's own Full Extrapolation, Section II** — devotees arrive one at a time, drawn from
  contributing cities across Tepenia, each completing an individually significant pilgrimage before choosing
  to stay.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about which specific devotee or family this chain concerns beyond the
  general premise; no invented detail about Dome Fuji's own doctrine beyond what's already established.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A family in Mawson wants to reconnect with a relative who left, years ago, on pilgrimage to Dome
Fuji — a real, felt loss for a city whose whole civic identity runs on warmth and easy connection, now facing
someone who chose total isolation and a faith few in Mawson understand. The player is asked to help arrange
contact or deliver a message — a straightforward reconnection task, on its face.

**BUT** — Dome Fuji's own established character makes any well-meaning "checking in" or gentle attempt at
persuasion a real misstep. Arrival there is itself a credential, a serious and legitimate choice — not
something to be gently walked back by people who love the person who made it. Mawson's own instinctive,
deliberately cultivated warmth, its whole civic reflex to reach out and welcome back, is exactly the wrong
lens for understanding, let alone respecting, a choice built around total withdrawal from that same kind of
connection.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to help the family approach this in a way that actually respects the
pilgrim's choice rather than quietly working against it — genuinely difficult, since it runs against
everything Mawson's own culture instinctively wants to do.

**BUT** — When contact finally happens, the reversal lands: the pilgrim reveals that leaving wasn't only
about a religious calling found elsewhere. Part of it, plainly stated, was Mawson itself — specifically, the
constant, performed warmth and hospitality-as-civic-duty that felt exhausting and hollow to someone who
needed real solitude, not friendliness on schedule. Mawson's own most celebrated trait, genuine and valued by
most of its population, may have been part of exactly what this person needed to escape.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a reconnection errand becomes a real, uncomfortable question the family — and,
by extension, Mawson's own civic self-image — has never had to face: whether the city's own warmth is
actually a gift to everyone who lives there, or whether it costs some people something real that nobody's
ever had reason to notice before.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Accepting the pilgrim's own account honestly
means Mawson's own comfortable self-image — the warm, easy, hospitality-forward city everyone agrees it is —
has a real, uncomfortable exception it's never had to reckon with, and the family has to sit with the fact
that loving someone the way Mawson loves everyone may have been part of what drove them away. Rejecting or
deflecting the explanation, preferring to believe the departure was purely spiritual and had nothing to do
with Mawson itself, protects that comfortable self-image fully — but means the family, and the city, never
actually understand or make peace with the real reason this person left, and quietly dismisses the one
account of it that actually came from the person who lived it.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how the family receives what they're told: accept the pilgrim's account fully, honoring their own
stated truth at the cost of Mawson's own untroubled self-image; or hold onto the simpler, spiritual-calling
explanation, protecting that self-image intact at the cost of ever really reconciling with why this specific
person needed to leave. Either way, nothing brings the pilgrim back to Mawson, and nothing changes what
actually happened — only how honestly the people left behind are willing to understand it.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "arrange a reunion"
into "reaching out at all risks disrespecting a serious, legitimate choice"; the second BUT turns "make
respectful contact" into "the pilgrim's own reason for leaving implicates the very thing Mawson is proudest
of"; the third BUT turns "hear them out" into "accepting the truth costs the family's comfortable
understanding of home, and rejecting it costs ever actually understanding the person at all." Deleting any
one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And
Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Mawson subnet candidate to test whether the subnet's own
warmest, most celebrated civic trait — Mawson's deliberately cultivated hospitality — might also be a
genuine cost for at least some of the people who grew up inside it, using Dome Fuji's own established
"arrival as credential" character as the one place in Tepenia where such a person could plausibly go to
actually escape it. It's also the first candidate in this set to route its climax entirely through a single
family's own capacity to hear an uncomfortable truth, rather than through any infrastructure, resource, or
institutional question.
