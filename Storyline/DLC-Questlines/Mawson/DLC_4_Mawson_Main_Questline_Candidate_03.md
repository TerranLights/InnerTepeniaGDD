# DLC 4 (Mawson Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #3: "The Season Between Ice and Water"

**What this is:** a third prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 4's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mawson subnet, built around Sayowa's own established
seasonal ice-road mechanic and its cooperative (not contested) junction governance — genuinely different
material from Candidate #1's use of Sayowa. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 3rd of
the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Mawson subnet's 3 cities (Sayowa alone)**.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Sayowa's own Full Extrapolation, Section IV** — East Ongul Island's connection to the mainland changes
  character across the year: a seasonal ice road usable once winter sea ice thickens enough for vehicle
  traffic, combined with maritime/icebreaker access during the summer thaw when the ice road isn't viable —
  a real environmental detail flagged directly for its "storytelling potential for DLC 4."
- **Sayowa's own Full Extrapolation, Section III** — unlike Troll Airfield's genuinely fractured, contested
  control in the Halley subnet, the Sayowa Junction is maintained through an informal, cooperative
  arrangement among the parties who depend on it (Sayowa itself, Mawson, Concordia-route and Halley subnet
  interests) — nobody has fully claimed it because everyone who needs it keeps it running, a deliberately
  low-drama governance style distinct from Troll's open conflict.
- **Sayowa's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 2** — Sayowa's own survival traces to an attacker's
  calculated restraint, not resilience or age — a genuinely new damage-pattern category in this project's own
  accumulated framework, giving DLC 4's own infrastructure stakes a distinct texture from DLC 5's Troll.
- **Sayowa's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 3** — Sayowa's whole character, at every scale, runs on
  "small vessel, outsized cargo": modest population, disproportionately critical function, quiet governance
  that never makes a show of its own real significance.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim that the cooperative arrangement was ever previously tested by genuine,
  high-stakes disagreement — this chain is specifically the first time it is.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Something time-sensitive needs to cross between Sayowa and the mainland right at the seasonal
transition — the exact window when the winter ice road is no longer safe to trust but the summer
icebreaker route isn't yet reliably running. The player is asked to help manage the crossing during this
genuinely hazardous in-between period — a straightforward, if urgent, logistics-timing task.

**BUT** — Sayowa's own cooperative junction governance — informal, low-drama, built on everyone simply
needing the connection to keep working — was never built for a genuinely risky, time-pressured call like
this one. The arrangement handles routine operation well precisely because nobody has ever had to actually
decide anything difficult; it has no established process for resolving a real disagreement about whether to
risk a thinning ice road or wait for a safer, later window.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to broker an actual decision among the cooperating parties — Sayowa itself,
Mawson, and the Concordia-route and Halley subnet interests who all depend on this crossing — testing
whether their easy, unclaimed cooperation can survive a moment where their interests genuinely diverge.

**BUT** — The parties' stakes in timing turn out to be real and to conflict. Mawson's own interests —
predictable travel windows for the honeymooners and visitors its whole civic identity depends on — push
toward caution and waiting. Whatever urgent outside need created the time pressure in the first place pushes
toward risking the crossing now. And Sayowa itself, whose own people would bear the actual physical risk of
an unsafe crossing, has the most at stake in the outcome and, within a "cooperative" system built on nobody
making unilateral calls, the least individual leverage to simply refuse.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a logistics-timing problem becomes the cooperative arrangement's first real
test: whether it can actually make a hard call together, or whether "nobody needs to fully claim it" only
ever worked because nobody's interests had genuinely clashed before.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Risking the crossing now serves whatever
urgent outside need created the pressure and preserves the cooperative arrangement's own flexible,
unclaimed spirit — but puts real, physical risk on whoever actually attempts a transitional-ice crossing,
almost certainly Sayowa's own people, the party with the least say and the most exposure. Holding and
waiting for the safer window protects everyone's physical safety and respects the fact that Sayowa
disproportionately bears the risk in this arrangement — but fails whatever time-sensitive need prompted the
whole situation, and strains a cooperative system that has never had to tell an urgent outside party no
before.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which way the crossing goes: push it through now, meeting the urgent outside need and keeping the
cooperative arrangement's easy flexibility intact, at real physical risk to the party least able to refuse;
or hold for the safer window, protecting Sayowa's own people at the direct cost of whatever the delayed
crossing was actually needed for. Either way, the Sayowa Junction's own quiet, unclaimed governance style —
untested until now — comes out of this having actually had to decide something difficult, for the first
time.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "manage a timing issue"
into "the cooperative system has no way to actually resolve a real disagreement"; the second BUT turns
"get everyone to agree" into "the parties' interests genuinely conflict, and the party bearing the most risk
has the least say"; the third BUT turns "decide when to cross" into "there is no version of the decision
that doesn't put real risk or real failure on someone with a legitimate claim either way." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Sayowa's own established, deliberately low-drama governance
style — explicitly designed to read as distinct from Troll Airfield's open conflict — and finds the one
kind of pressure that style was never actually tested against: a genuine, time-boxed disagreement where
everyone's stakes are real and don't line up. It's also the first Mawson subnet candidate built around the
literal physical geography of the seasonal ice road, giving DLC 4 a concrete environmental hazard distinct
from anything established in the other subnets so far.
