# DLC 4 (Mawson Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #5: "A City That Isn't There Anymore"

**What this is:** a fifth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 4's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mawson subnet, built around Dome Fuji's own established
"patchwork of frozen moments" fact — distinct material from Candidate #2's use of the ice-core archive and
the ancient caretaker robot. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 5th of the "at least
10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Mawson subnet's 3 cities (Dome Fuji alone)**.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Dome Fuji's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 1** — every devotee at Dome Fuji is, functionally, a
  time capsule of their home city at the exact moment they left it; with no incoming Arcanet feed and
  word-of-mouth between devotees the only channel, the city doesn't share a collective "present" the way
  every other Tepenian city does — a genuinely distinctive narrative utility: a devotee who simply hasn't
  heard anything about their own former home in years.
- **Dome Fuji's own Full Extrapolation, Section II** — the devotee population grew "one arrival at a time"
  from roughly 34 different contributing cities, each devotee's own home-city knowledge frozen at their own
  personal moment of departure.
- **Dome Fuji's own Local Cultures, Section 6a** *(as cited in Cross-Reference Finding 7)* — "a resident's
  presence at Dome Fuji is itself a credential; no one arrives here by accident, drift, or economic
  necessity" — meaning a devotee's own reasons for leaving their home city in the first place are plausibly
  bound up with what that city meant to them, not incidental to it.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about which specific city the devotee originally came from, or any
  invented detail about what specifically changed there beyond the general, established fact that many
  Tepenian cities were damaged or destroyed in the Long Night War.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The player needs real, current information about a specific city, and the only available lead
is a Dome Fuji devotee who originally came from there. The player makes the pilgrimage to Dome Fuji and
finds the devotee willing to talk — a straightforward, if remote, witness interview.

**BUT** — What the devotee shares is confidently, sincerely wrong. They left before whatever happened to
that city since — damage, destruction, or simply years of change no word-of-mouth channel ever carried this
far — and have no way of knowing their own information is badly out of date. The player now knows something
true about this devotee's own former home that the devotee themselves doesn't.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to decide whether to correct the devotee's understanding of their own home
city, since the outside need that sent them here in the first place depends on accurate information, not the
devotee's honestly-held but outdated picture.

**BUT** — The reversal is that this isn't simply a factual correction. A devotee's presence at Dome Fuji is
itself a credential — nobody arrives by accident, and this devotee's own reasons for leaving that city, and
for choosing this specific, total isolation, are plausibly bound up with what the city meant to them at the
time they left. Telling them what's actually become of it risks unsettling something their entire
devotional life may have been quietly built on top of — and nobody, not even the devotee, knows in advance
whether the truth would liberate them or devastate them.

**THEREFORE** — What began as an information-gathering task becomes a real decision about whether this
devotee deserves the truth about their own life, regardless of what it costs their hard-won peace.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Telling the truth respects the devotee as
someone capable of handling accurate knowledge about their own history, and satisfies the real, legitimate
outside need for correct information — but risks shattering a devotional peace built, in part, on an
understanding of home that may no longer be true, sincerely held for however many years they've been here.
Withholding the truth protects that peace completely, letting the devotee keep the only version of home
they've ever known — but leaves the outside need unmet with accurate information, and quietly treats the
devotee as someone to be shielded from their own life rather than trusted with it.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
choice in what gets said: tell the devotee the honest, current truth about their former home, honoring them
as someone who deserves to know, at the cost of whatever that truth actually does to a peace they didn't
know was resting on outdated ground; or leave their understanding untouched, protecting that peace fully, at
the cost of both the outside need going unmet and the devotee living out the rest of their devotional life
on a picture of home that simply isn't true anymore.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "get information from a
witness" into "the witness's own information is confidently wrong"; the second BUT turns "correct the
record" into "correcting it risks unsettling whatever peace the devotee's whole life here has been built on";
the third BUT turns "tell them or don't" into "there is no version of the choice that doesn't cost either
their peace or their right to the truth about their own life." Deleting any one Complication changes what
the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes a purely structural, almost incidental fact about Dome Fuji's
own information environment — no incoming Arcanet feed, devotees as unwitting time capsules — and turns it
into a genuinely personal dilemma about what one person is owed regarding the truth of their own life. It's
also the first Mawson subnet candidate whose central conflict has nothing to do with infrastructure,
resources, or governance at all — just the question of whether accurate information is always a kindness,
even when the person receiving it never asked for an update in the first place.
