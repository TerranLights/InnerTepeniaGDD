# DLC 4 (Mawson Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #4: "The Last Happy Week"

**What this is:** a fourth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 4's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mawson subnet, built around Mawson's own established
honeymoon-destination economy — untouched by Candidate #1's own use of Mawson's severed hub function. **Not
canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 4th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the
Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Mawson subnet's 3 cities (Mawson alone)**.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Mawson Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Mawson "carries the hub's own quiet acceptance — a
  hospitality reputation built to serve people passing through on their way to build lives elsewhere."
- **Mawson's own Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the established honeymoon tradition: a customary visit to
  the Prince Charles Mountains overlook, timed to whichever seasonal extreme happens to fall during the
  stay, a one-to-two week visit that couples make before returning to settle wherever they'll actually live.
- **Mawson's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 2** — the honeymoon-destination reputation was
  deliberately, institutionally cultivated, a genuine civic priority, not an incidental byproduct of
  Mawson's demographics.
- **The subnet's own established war-damage reality, project-wide** — a large share of Tepenian cities are
  destroyed or severely damaged (established across every subnet candidate set produced so far), meaning any
  couple's planned settling destination has a genuine, non-trivial chance of no longer being what it was.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim that Mawson formally reorganizes its own civic function into a
  resettlement institution — this chain stays at the scale of specific couples and specific honest
  -disclosure choices, not a subnet-wide policy change.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The player is drawn into the stay of a specific honeymooning couple at Mawson, partway through
their customary one-to-two week visit, when it becomes clear their planned settling destination — the city
they intended to build their actual life in, once the honeymoon ends — has been seriously damaged or
destroyed since they last had reliable information about it. Mawson's own hospitality apparatus, built
entirely around facilitating a joyful transition toward an already-chosen life, has no established practice
for this. The player is asked to help the couple figure out what to do — seemingly a matter of finding them
better information.

**BUT** — Mawson's whole hospitality industry, and the honeymoon tradition itself, was built to serve
people already headed somewhere, not to help people decide where "somewhere" even is anymore. There's no
established resettlement guidance, no protocol, nothing in the Prince Charles Mountains overlook tradition or
anywhere else in Mawson's own established civic apparatus built for a couple whose actual next step is now
genuinely open.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to improvise real, substantive guidance — drawing on Mawson's own established
warmth and hospitality expertise, but applying it to a kind of need the city was never actually built to
meet.

**BUT** — Word spreads, and the reversal lands: this couple isn't unique. A real, growing number of
post-war honeymooning couples arrive at Mawson with no clear settling destination left at all, simply
because so much of the country has been damaged or destroyed. What looked like one couple's unlucky
circumstance turns out to be an emerging, structural reality Mawson's own civic identity has no framework
for.

**THEREFORE** — The immediate question narrows back down to something the player can actually act on: not
whether Mawson should become a resettlement institution, but whether Mawson's hospitality staff should start
giving couples the full, honest, unvarnished truth about their intended destination's real post-war
condition — right in the middle of what's supposed to be the most protected, joyful period of their lives.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Giving couples the full truth before they
leave helps them prepare and make genuinely informed choices about where to actually go — but shatters the
protected bubble the honeymoon tradition exists specifically to provide, injecting grim, often devastating
reality into what was supposed to be an uncomplicated beginning. Preserving that protected joy, and letting
couples discover the truth only once they actually arrive at their destination, honors Mawson's own
cherished civic function completely intact — but sends people off unprepared for what may be real,
significant loss waiting for them, having spent their last easy days believing something that's no longer
true.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how Mawson actually handles this: disclose the hard truth honestly, honoring the couple's right to
real information at the cost of the protected joy Mawson's whole hospitality tradition is built to provide;
or protect that joy fully, honoring what makes Mawson the place it's always been, at the cost of sending
people onward unprepared for what they'll actually find when they get there.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "find better
information" into "there's no established practice for this kind of need at all"; the second BUT turns
"help one couple" into "this is a growing, structural reality, not an isolated case"; the third BUT turns
"tell them the truth or don't" into "there is no version of the choice that doesn't cost either their
remaining happiness or their real preparedness." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is
actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Mawson's own warmest, most celebrated established trait — a
deliberately cultivated honeymoon-destination reputation, a place built for beginnings — and finds the one
kind of situation that trait was never designed to survive: a country damaged badly enough that "wherever
they'll actually live" is no longer a safe assumption. It's also the first Mawson subnet candidate whose
central tension is protecting joy versus telling the truth, rather than any resource, infrastructure, or
governance question, giving DLC 4 a genuinely different emotional register from Candidates #1 through #3.
