# DLC 4 (Mawson Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #1: "The Junction Everyone Leans On"

**What this is:** a first prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 4's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mawson subnet. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate** — a worked demonstration of the method, covering exactly one of the "at least 10" possibilities
that method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md`
§5a).

**Mawson subnet is genuinely small** — 3 cities (Mawson, Sayowa, Dome Fuji), not 7 or 8 like the other
subnets covered so far. Its own Ultra-Megasheet already names the subnet's defining trait explicitly:
"looking outward" — every city's most consequential relationships point outside the subnet entirely
(Dome Fuji to Kunlun/Mirny subnet, Sayowa's own cross-subnet highway bridging, a three-subnet aviation supply
chain). This candidate leans into that established character directly rather than inventing an internal
subnet-wide identity the material itself says doesn't exist.

**Uses 2 of Mawson subnet's 3 cities** (Mawson, Sayowa).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Mawson's own Full Extrapolation, Section I** — the Planetary Split Brain severed *inter-subnet* Arcanet
  links specifically, not intra-subnet ones. Mawson's hub function connecting the wider subnet to the rest
  of Tepenia took the brunt of the damage and is "damaged but partially operational"; its own connections to
  Sayowa and Dome Fuji plausibly survived in better shape.
- **Mawson's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 2** — Mawson's hospitality character and honeymoon
  -destination reputation were both deliberately, institutionally cultivated over decades, not incidental —
  a hub city that made connecting people to the rest of the country (honeymooners "visited before returning
  to settle in whatever city they'll actually live in") a genuine civic priority.
- **Sayowa's own established facts** (per Mawson Subnet Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2/3, and Sayowa's
  own Full Extrapolation) — Sayowa survived via an attacker's own calculated restraint, preserving a
  structurally critical junction specifically because destroying it fully would have cost the attacker
  access they still needed; Sayowa sits at the closest inter-subnet proximity in the Federation (Hwy 37 to
  Vostok/Kunlun/Concordia, Hwy 7-ext to Princess Elisabeth/Lazar in the Halley subnet) despite being one of
  the smallest Mawson subnet cities by population.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about repairing the Planetary Split Brain itself, or any invented new
  highway/Arcanet infrastructure beyond what's already established — this chain works entirely from Sayowa's
  own existing, confirmed cross-subnet road connections.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Mawson's whole civic identity depends on genuinely connecting people to the rest of Tepenia —
not just hospitality for its own sake, but the honeymoon-destination role specifically built around couples
passing through on their way to settle elsewhere. Post-war, the severed inter-subnet Arcanet link has
crippled that function at its root. The player is asked to help restore some real, working version of
Mawson's own connective role — not necessarily the literal Arcanet repair, but some functional equivalent
that lets the city be what it's always meant to be again.

**BUT** — The severed link is a Planetary-Split-Brain-scale problem, not something Mawson alone can fix, and
any workaround means depending on cooperation from cities and subnets that don't owe Mawson anything and
have their own severe post-war priorities. A "honeymoon town's" reconnection isn't going to rank high on
anyone else's own list unprompted.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually find and negotiate a working alternative, and the obvious real
lever is Sayowa — the one Mawson subnet city already sitting at the closest inter-subnet proximity in the
whole Federation, with real, existing highway connections reaching directly into Halley subnet.

**BUT** — Leaning on Sayowa for this puts new, uncompensated weight on a city that's already carrying more
than its own small population would suggest. Sayowa survived the war specifically because an attacker judged
the junction too valuable, alive, to destroy outright — meaning Sayowa is already the subnet's single most
leaned-on piece of infrastructure, and adding "restore Mawson's own connectivity" on top of that asks a small
city, which never chose this role for itself, to bear even more.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a straightforward reconnection project becomes a real question about how much
more weight Sayowa can reasonably be asked to carry on another city's behalf.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Routing Mawson's own reconnection through
Sayowa actually works — restores the honeymoon hub's core civic function, benefits from infrastructure that
already exists rather than requiring anything new — but places real, ongoing strain on a small city already
overextended relative to its size, with no guarantee Sayowa's own capacity, or its safety, holds up under yet
more reliance. Seeking a different, more equitable solution that doesn't lean on Sayowa protects it from
further strain — but is slower, costlier, and less certain, possibly leaving Mawson's own reconnection
incomplete for a long time, or indefinitely.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which path Mawson actually takes: route the reconnection through Sayowa, restoring Mawson's own
civic identity at real, ongoing cost to a small city that never asked to be the answer to someone else's
problem; or seek a harder, more equitable alternative, protecting Sayowa at the cost of leaving Mawson's own
core function — the thing that makes it Mawson — genuinely broken for longer, possibly for good.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "restore a
connection" into "the only real lever means asking something of a city that owes Mawson nothing"; the second
BUT turns "use Sayowa's junction" into "Sayowa is already the subnet's most leaned-on piece of
infrastructure, and this adds to that"; the third BUT turns "pick the fix" into "there is no fix that doesn't
cost either Mawson's own core identity or Sayowa's own limited capacity." Deleting any one Complication
changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Mawson subnet's own explicitly-named defining trait — every
city's most important relationships point outward, not inward — and builds the DLC's central conflict
directly out of that structural fact, rather than inventing an internal subnet rivalry the material itself
says doesn't really exist. It's also the first Mawson subnet candidate to put real, felt weight on the size
mismatch already implicit in Sayowa's own established profile: a city small enough to be one of the
subnet's least populous, carrying more real strategic weight than either of its larger neighbors, with no
say in how much more gets asked of it.
