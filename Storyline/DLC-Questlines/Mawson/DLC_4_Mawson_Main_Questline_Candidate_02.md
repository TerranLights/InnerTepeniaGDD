# DLC 4 (Mawson Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #2: "Nobody Left to Ask Except Him"

**What this is:** a second prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 4's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Mawson subnet, built directly on Dome Fuji's own
established ice-core archive — explicitly flagged in its own Cross-Reference Synthesis as "close to a
ready-made conflict premise." **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 2nd of the "at least
10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Mawson subnet's 3 cities (Dome Fuji alone)**.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Dome Fuji's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 3** — the ice core archive is one of Tepenia's most
  scientifically valuable, most untapped resources, held not by an institution but by individual devotees or,
  at most, an informal group of tenure-based stewards — explicitly named as a genuinely different quest
  flavor from Concordia's district-politics structure: personal persuasion, because there's no institution
  here to negotiate with.
- **Dome Fuji's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 7** — the strongest single proposal in that document:
  a support/maintenance robot from the original secular research era could plausibly still be functional
  today, having simply never departed rather than arrived through pilgrimage — the single oldest continuous
  resident perspective in the city, standing entirely outside the "arrival is the credential" system that
  defines everyone else's status.
- **Dome Fuji's own Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the archive survived the war intact; active scientific
  analysis has slowed to a fraction of its founding-era pace, tended by a small number of devotees with prior
  scientific training as a secondary, not primary, vocation.
- **Dome Fuji's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 9** — the devotee population has, in the overwhelming
  majority of cases, no lived experience of humans at all; any human arrival is a genuine novelty, not an
  ordinary visitor.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any confirmed connection to DLC 1's own Cross-DLC Bypass gift chain — Finding 3's
  own speculative link to Amundsen Tower research is noted as a real possibility, not committed to here, and
  this chain doesn't require it to be true.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A scientific interest elsewhere in Tepenia wants specific data from Dome Fuji's ice core
archive — genuinely valuable, centuries-old climate records nobody has actively analyzed in generations. The
player is sent to secure it, expecting a straightforward, if remote, retrieval task: reach the archive,
identify the right cores, bring back the data.

**BUT** — There's no institution to present credentials to. Dome Fuji's devotees don't gate access by
authorization; the whole faith runs on devotion as its only membership requirement, and an outside
scientific request simply doesn't register in that framework at all. The player can't produce a permit that
means anything here — they have to earn actual standing with people whose entire value system has nothing to
do with institutional legitimacy.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to genuinely engage the pilgrimage itself, in whatever partial form is
possible, to be taken seriously enough by any devotee to get real cooperation — not paperwork, but
something closer to earned respect.

**BUT** — Even once that trust is earned, it turns out not to be enough. The devotees maintaining the
Archive Vault tend it the way an old text is tended by people who believe it matters without necessarily
reading it every day — real, careful preservation, but the specific technical understanding the request
actually needs isn't something the current community fully has anymore. The one figure who might have it is
a maintenance robot from the original research era, centuries old, who never arrived through pilgrimage
because they never left at all — the single oldest continuous presence in the city, standing entirely
outside the community's own "arrival is the credential" logic, and the only individual at Dome Fuji for whom
humans are a personal memory rather than doctrine.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to find and engage this figure directly, someone who isn't a devotee, isn't
an outsider, and has no real stake in the status system everyone else at Dome Fuji operates by.

**BUT** — The reversal: this figure has real, complicated feelings about the request, earned across
centuries nobody else at Dome Fuji lived through. They watched the original scientific mission wither and
its population thin out generation by generation until the site emptied entirely; they then watched an
entirely different population arrive, for entirely different reasons, and slowly make the place their own.
An outside party now wanting to extract value from "their" archive again, for purposes that have nothing to
do with why either population ever actually lived here, isn't a neutral request to this figure — it's a
pattern they've already lived through once, ending in the place going quiet.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a data-retrieval task becomes a genuine negotiation with one individual's own
accumulated history, not a community's collective decision.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. The data itself is genuinely valuable, and
whatever real good it could do elsewhere in Tepenia is a real, legitimate stake, not an abstraction. But the
caretaker's own claim on the archive — earned by centuries of solitary stewardship no one else alive can
match — is equally real, and pushing past their reservations means overriding the wishes of the one person
who has protected this place longer than anyone, for reasons that have nothing to do with obstruction and
everything to do with having already watched this exact kind of outside interest end badly once before.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
choice in how the request is finally settled: honor the caretaker's own wishes fully, protecting Dome Fuji's
quiet autonomy and one individual's hard-earned claim to their own long stewardship, at the cost of leaving
genuinely valuable knowledge unused, wherever it might have helped; or press forward and secure the data
regardless of those reservations, delivering real value to whoever needs it, at the cost of overriding the
one person at Dome Fuji whose relationship to that archive predates everyone else's by centuries.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "get authorized access"
into "there's no authority here to grant it, only trust to earn"; the second BUT turns "earn the devotees'
trust" into "the devotees themselves don't have what the request actually needs"; the third BUT turns "ask
the caretaker" into "the caretaker has real, earned reasons to say no that have nothing to do with
obstruction." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none
reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it builds directly on the two strongest, most explicitly-flagged
proposals in Dome Fuji's own Cross-Reference Synthesis — the archive as a "ready-made conflict premise" and
the ancient caretaker robot as "the strongest single new idea in this document" — combining them into one
chain rather than leaving either as an isolated, unused observation. It's also the first Mawson subnet
candidate whose central relationship is with a single, centuries-old individual standing entirely outside
any community's own status system, giving DLC 4 a genuinely different register from the faction- and
city-level negotiations the design method's own worked examples default to.
