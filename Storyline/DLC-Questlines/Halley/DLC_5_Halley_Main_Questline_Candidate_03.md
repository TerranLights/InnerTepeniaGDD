# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #3: "What Still Runs Underneath"

**What this is:** a third prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around Princess Elisabeth's own
established ruin-and-reserve premise rather than Troll's contested control (Candidate #1) or Belgrano/
Neumayer's civic-memory asymmetry (Candidate #2). **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the
3rd of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Halley subnet's 8 cities (Princess Elisabeth alone)** — a solo candidate, the same pattern two
Mirny subnet candidates used for Kunlun and Vostok. Outside interest in the reserve, once it surfaces, is
kept deliberately unnamed/abstracted (word reaching other struggling settlements generally) rather than
pinned to a specific neighboring city not yet established as geographically adjacent.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Princess Elisabeth's own Full Extrapolation, Sections I & II** — the city's proposed buried energy
  -storage reserve (flywheels, battery banks, thermal storage, built underground against katabatic exposure)
  reconciled against `Specs/Princess_Elisabeth.md`'s already-finalized "no passive advantage at all": the
  reserve exists, but the war severed the access and control conduits linking it to the surface grid — a
  reserve that's intact and useless, not destroyed.
- **Princess Elisabeth's own Full Extrapolation, Section III** — the destruction wasn't broad or
  indiscriminate; it was a precision strike specifically against the integration points, distinct from
  Denison's environmental collapse or Zukelli's total-kill strike — an enemy that understood exactly what
  made the city work and severed exactly that.
- **Princess Elisabeth's own Full Extrapolation, Section VII** — the Founders' Turbine holiday: public
  maintenance and inspection of whatever zero-emissions infrastructure still functions among the ruins,
  observance treated as the act of upkeep itself, not a symbolic ceremony — genuine, felt civic pride in
  keeping the systems running.
- **Princess Elisabeth's own Full Extrapolation, Section VIII** — Straggling Survivor Joos Kaminari, a
  placeholder figure from the former underground maintenance crew, already proposed as the natural
  questline source with firsthand knowledge of the reserve's access points; Chief Engineer Margrethe
  Ollivier-Tanaka, credited across generations with keeping the zero-emissions systems balanced, work
  essential and barely known outside the engineering corps even before the war.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific neighboring city as the source of outside interest in the restored
  reserve (no Halley subnet city has an established, confirmed geographic-adjacency relationship to Princess
  Elisabeth in current material); and the Dome Fuji pilgrim-waypoint role (Section IV), since that's a
  separate, already-modest fact this chain doesn't need to touch.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Joos Kaminari, one of the last residents living among Princess Elisabeth's ruins, tells the
player what the small surviving population has always suspected but never been able to prove: the
underground reserve that used to back up the city's zero-emissions grid might still be down there, intact,
just disconnected. Reconnecting it looks like a straightforward restoration job — find the severed conduits,
repair them, give the last residents real power again.

**BUT** — The war didn't damage the integration points carelessly; it destroyed them with real precision
(Section III), and nobody left alive, not even the former maintenance crew's own survivors, has the
specialized parts or expertise to rebuild those exact conduits from what's on hand. Restoring access needs
real outside help — expertise or equivalent hardware the ruins' own tiny population can't supply from
inside itself.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to go out and bring back what's missing, which means bringing outside
attention to a city that has otherwise gone unnoticed and unvisited since the war.

**BUT** — Once the reserve is actually reconnected and proven to work, its true scale becomes impossible to
hide: this isn't a small backup system sized for a handful of survivors — it's a genuine, passive,
zero-emissions capacity built to power a whole city, sitting almost entirely unused by a population too
small to ever draw on more than a fraction of it. Word travels. The people and expertise the player brought
in to fix it start asking the obvious practical question: doesn't it make more sense to move what's
recoverable somewhere it would actually get used?

**THEREFORE** — What began as "help the last residents get their power back" becomes a live argument about
who a self-sufficient system that outlived its own city actually belongs to.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts both ways, and neither side is wrong. The last residents' claim isn't just
sentimental — Founders' Turbine (Section VII) already establishes that maintaining these exact systems,
not merely benefiting from them, is the one piece of civic identity the ruins' population has left; giving
that up isn't a fair trade for capacity they were never going to fully use anyway, on their own terms.
But the practical case is real too: a nearly-empty ruin sitting on subnet-scale energy capacity, while other
struggling settlements go without, is a genuine imbalance nobody chose and nobody can really justify
ignoring once it's known.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's
choice, not a fix to be discovered: keep the reserve entirely local, honoring the last residents' own hard
-won restoration and the Founders' Turbine identity intact, at the cost of a genuinely useful capacity
sitting mostly idle while others do without; or broker a real redistribution arrangement, sharing or
relocating recoverable capacity to serve more of the subnet, the practically fairer outcome, at the cost of
watching the population that just got its city's pride back lose it again, immediately, to somebody else's
greater need — the subnet's own established "uncredited load" identity landing, this time, on the city that
has already lost the most.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "repair a system" into
"the repair itself is beyond what's left of the city, so outside help is unavoidable"; the second BUT turns
"the repair succeeded" into "success reveals a capacity nobody the size of this population could ever
justify keeping to themselves"; the third BUT turns "decide who gets the power" into "both claims are
genuinely earned, and there is no version of this that doesn't cost somebody something real." Deleting any
one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And
Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes a proposal already flagged, in its own source document, as
"a concrete, ready-made questline mechanic" (Princess Elisabeth's own Full Extrapolation, Worth Your
Attention note) and carries it through to a genuine main-questline-scale ethical fork rather than stopping at
the restoration itself. It's also the first Halley subnet candidate built around a city with almost no
population left to speak for itself — raising the stakes of "who decides" in a way none of the subnet's
larger, more populous cities can replicate, and giving DLC 5 a genuinely different emotional register from
either Candidate #1's faction conflict or Candidate #2's credit dispute between two thriving cities.
