# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #11: "Whoever Actually Built It"

**What this is:** an eleventh prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet — the first candidate in a new run
specifically built to link through **at least 5 of the subnet's 8 cities**, the same standing that Palmer
subnet's own Candidates #11–#20 were built under. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the
Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Neumayer, Halley, Sanay, Troll, Belgrano), and naturally extends to a
sixth (Abowasa), since the pattern this chain investigates is established as running through all six.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2** — the subnet-wide "does the real work, someone else gets the
  name" pattern, running at four different scales: Halley itself (built the Sanay Arcanet relay nexus, took
  the subnet's own naming honor instead of technical control), Sanay (hosts that nexus and one of Tepenia's
  largest ports, without seeking comparable credit), and Neumayer alone, three separate times across its
  entire history (the original Rastra redesign, the Amundsen Tower's schematics, the current-generation
  fabrication-synthesis chamber design) — none of which carries Neumayer's name forward anywhere in the
  country.
- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 5** — the broader function-first, credit-averse civic register running
  across at least five of the subnet's eight cities, explicitly including Belgrano's institutional-habit
  "Boneyard Times" identity and Troll's quietly-higher-quality-rather-than-ostentatious goods, and framed in
  that same document as something close to "a mild civic failure" to visibly self-promote.
- **Halley Cross-City Throughway 2** — the specific, dated chain establishing Neumayer's three-fold uncredited
  legacy in full (the Rastra, the Tower, the fabrication-synthesis chamber), used here as the campaign's own
  central, best-documented case.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Byrd's own separate uncredited-debt problem (Throughway 3) — this
  chain stays inside Halley subnet's own internal credit question, not Byrd's cross-subnet one.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A campaign takes shape to formally document and publicly attribute Halley subnet's own long
history of uncredited work — starting with Neumayer's three-fold legacy (the Rastra, the Tower, the
fabrication-synthesis chamber) and extending to Halley's own relay-nexus construction, Sanay's port and
nexus hosting, and Troll's quiet reputation for quality over show. The player is asked to help gather
documentation and testimony across these cities to set the historical record straight — a straightforward,
if far-reaching, "give credit where due" task.

**BUT** — As the player gathers testimony, a genuine tension surfaces: many of the people and institutions
actually responsible for this work don't want the credit. This isn't modest false humility — per Pattern 5,
it's a sincerely-held civic value running across multiple cities, and several subjects experience the very
idea of public attribution as an outside imposition on a culture that has functioned specifically because it
doesn't operate that way.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually negotiate with reluctant subjects city by city, rather than simply
compile facts — the project becomes about whether the correction is wanted at all, not just whether it's
accurate.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the player reaches Neumayer's own current generation: the credit-averse
culture, however sincere, has a real, material cost. Younger engineers working in Neumayer's own
fabrication-design lineage today go unrecognized for genuinely original work in ways that quietly limit their
own standing and advancement, precisely because the institution's own culture treats claiming credit as
faintly improper — meaning the value isn't just abstract, it's actively costing specific people real
opportunity right now.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a historical-attribution project becomes a real question about whether the
subnet's own genuine cultural value of quiet competence is worth defending at the cost of people currently
being harmed by it.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Publishing full, public attribution — Neumayer
named for the Rastra, the Tower, and the chamber; Halley and Sanay properly credited for the nexus; Troll's
own quality work formally recognized — restores historical accuracy and gives real people, especially
Neumayer's own current engineers, deserved recognition and career standing. But it does so by overriding a
genuine, deeply-held civic value shared across multiple cities, imposing an outside standard of credit and
visibility onto a culture that has cohered, for generations, specifically by not working that way. Respecting
the subnet's own preference and leaving the record as it stands preserves that cultural integrity and honors
what these cities have actually chosen for themselves — but leaves the historical record incomplete, and
leaves real people, right now, uncredited for work that materially affects their own lives.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how the campaign actually concludes: full public attribution, restoring accuracy and helping real
people's standing at the cost of overriding a genuine, subnet-wide cultural value; or deference to that
value, preserving the subnet's own chosen culture at the cost of leaving real people uncredited and the
record incomplete.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "document credit" into
"the subjects themselves don't want it documented"; the second BUT turns "respect their wishes" into "their
own shared value is quietly costing real people real opportunity right now"; the third BUT turns "publish or
don't" into "there is no version of this that doesn't cost either real people's recognition or a genuine,
long-held cultural value shared across the whole subnet." Deleting any one Complication changes what the
player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that its linking mechanism — a public-credit-attribution campaign — is built
directly from this subnet's own single most distinctive finding (Pattern 2 and Pattern 5's shared "does the
work, forgoes the name" civic character), making it the first Halley subnet candidate whose central conflict
is the subnet's own defining trait turned into an actual dilemma, rather than background color. It also gives
Neumayer — already established as the subnet's most quietly consequential city, per the Cross-City Patterns
file's own closing note — its first candidate appearance built around its own legacy directly, rather than as
a name mentioned inside someone else's throughway.
