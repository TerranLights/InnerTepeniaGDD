# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #1: "Everything Downstream of the Fight"

**What this is:** a first prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate** — a worked demonstration of the method, covering exactly one of the "at least 10" possibilities
that method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md`
§5a).

**Builds directly on the already-established central-conflict anchor** — Troll Airfield's fractured control,
flagged in `TODO.md` as DLC 5's working central conflict since 2026-07-03, and the same anchor used as the
worked illustration in `But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §4. That illustration stops at a single side
-quest scale (help a faction, worsen Sanay's backlog, discover a national Rastra-parts stake). This candidate
formalizes and extends it to full main-questline scale, using the subnet's own Cross-City Throughways and
Full Extrapolation material that document went on to establish after §4 was written.

**Uses 4 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Sanay, Troll, Halley, Belgrano) — Neumayer is present as essential
backstory (the Rastra's true design origin) but stays offscreen, the way Kunlun functioned as backdrop-only
in one Mirny candidate.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Halley Subnet Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 1** — the established Sanay→Troll→Halley chain: Sanay
  is the subnet's actual supply-chain entry point; incoming freight splits at Sanay between direct Hwy 7
  delivery to nearby cities and onward trucking to Troll for airfield distribution to more remote
  destinations; Troll's control is fractured among factions holding the runway, the fuel depot, and trucking
  dispatch separately; Halley, having deliberately forgone its own docks and airstrip, depends entirely on
  overland resupply through Belgrano and Sanay and therefore has no influence over Troll's fight but no
  escape from its outcome either.
- **Troll's own Full Extrapolation, Section I** — the precise shape of the fracture: one faction holds the
  physical runway and control tower, another the fuel depot, another the trucking dispatch that actually
  moves freight to and from the field. No single group holds all of it.
- **Halley's own Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Halley's specific stake, distinct from Troll's or
  Belgrano's own: it determines nothing about who wins the fight, but whether Halley "eats this winter"
  depends entirely on the outcome.
- **Halley Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section III** — Troll's contested control has plausibly put Rastra
  shipments at risk too, alongside Cradle chamber freight: spare parts for the single most common vehicle in
  the country, manufactured at Belgrano's own mechanic/fabrication economy, pass through the same contested
  freight network as everything else.
- **Halley Subnet Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — Neumayer's founding-era engineers reverse
  -engineered Belgrano's original hauler into the template every Rastra in Tepenia descends from, a debt
  nobody outside Neumayer's own private archive knows about.
- **`TODO.md`'s flagged Halley permanent-loss path** — if nobody is left to maintain Halley's relocation
  program, the Brunt Ice Shelf's own calving process takes over and the city eventually falls into the sea,
  permanently. Explicitly a possible worst-case resolution, not a certainty.
- **Explicitly excluded:** the Cross-DLC Bypass gift already established for DLC 5 (the Archivist's Trail
  codekey, sourced from Sanay) — that chain is a separate, already-designed optional side path feeding DLC 1,
  not part of this main questline's own structure. Also excluded: naming a winning faction outright — per the
  method's own instruction, Troll's control question is meant to resolve through player choice, not be
  predetermined here.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Halley's relocation program needs a resupply shipment moved from Sanay overland to Halley — the
maintenance materials and propulsion-track parts that keep the city's still-mobile sections actually able to
keep relocating away from calving risk. It looks like a straightforward escort/logistics task, and by
Halley's own established position (it has no stake in who controls Troll), the player has no reason yet to
think this touches the airfield fight at all.

**BUT** — The shipment doesn't move. The trucking-dispatch faction contesting Troll's control has started
commandeering Hwy 7 capacity — trucks, drivers, fuel — to strengthen its own hand in the airfield fight,
and Halley's overland resupply is exactly the kind of freight caught in that squeeze. Halley's supply crisis,
which looked like its own problem, turns out to be a direct symptom of a fight happening somewhere else
entirely — precisely what Throughway 1 already establishes as the subnet's own defining shape.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to go to Troll itself. Fixing the shipment at the Sanay/Halley end is
impossible; the cause sits at the airfield, not the destination.

**BUT** — Troll's control is fractured three separate ways, and there's no clean fix. Freeing up trucking
capacity for Halley means backing the dispatch faction against the runway and fuel-depot factions — help
that necessarily worsens the other two factions' position, the same shape of cost the method's own §4
illustration already establishes for this exact anchor.

**THEREFORE** — The player commits to the dispatch faction, with less room to back out than the original
"deliver a shipment" framing implied.

**BUT** — The goalposts move. Backing the dispatch faction to free Halley's shipment turns out to also
determine something much larger: the same faction and the same trucking routes carry Belgrano's own Rastra
spare-parts shipments (Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section III) out through Troll to the rest of the country.
What the player thought was a single city's winter survival is actually a decision about whether the
national supply of parts for the country's single most common vehicle keeps moving at all — a stake tracing
all the way back to Neumayer's own uncredited, generations-old redesign of the original Rastra (Throughway
2), though nobody at Troll, Sanay, Halley, or Belgrano currently knows that lineage.

**THEREFORE** — The resolution is shaped by everything above, not just the original errand. Whichever
faction the player ultimately empowers at Troll determines two things at once: whether Halley's shipment
gets through this cycle (and, by extension, whether Halley's relocation program keeps functioning at all,
with the flagged permanent-loss path — the city eventually lost to the sea — sitting as the visible
worst-case shadow over every future cycle this same fight recurs), and whether Rastra spare parts keep
reaching the rest of Tepenia on schedule. A single delivery task has quietly become a decision whose reach
extends past the edge of the subnet, exactly the shape Section 4 of the But/Therefore method describes —
just carried to its full, main-questline-scale conclusion rather than stopping at the first "but."

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "deliver a shipment"
into "the real obstacle is a fight happening somewhere else entirely"; the second BUT turns "help the
dispatch faction" into "helping anyone here costs someone else something real"; the third BUT turns "resolve
Halley's shipment" into "this was never just about Halley — it's a decision with a nationwide reach." Deleting
any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised
And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it doesn't invent a new central conflict — it takes the anchor DLC 5
already has (Troll Airfield, tentative since 2026-07-03) and traces it all the way through the subnet's own
established Cross-City Throughways to a conclusion nobody had connected yet: the same fractured airfield
fight that decides Halley's winter also decides the health of the entire country's Rastra supply chain, a
debt whose true origin (Neumayer's own uncredited engineering lineage) never surfaces in the chain itself,
consistent with the subnet's own "uncredited load" identity (Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section I) holding
even at the climax. It also gives the flagged Halley permanent-loss path (the city falling into the sea) a
concrete, recurring mechanism to hang from — not a single quest-ending choice, but the visible shadow over
every future cycle of the same fight, which the player has just seen up close for the first time.
