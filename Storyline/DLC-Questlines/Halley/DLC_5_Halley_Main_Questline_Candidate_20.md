# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #20: "Whatever the Record Actually Says"

**What this is:** a twentieth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around a tenth distinct linking
mechanism — consolidating a single, subnet-wide historical archive — closing out this run of "at least 5
cities" candidates (#11 through #20) the same way Palmer subnet's own Candidate #20 closed its matching run,
and the way each subnet's own Candidate #10 closed its original ten-candidate set. **Not canon, not chosen,
not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Halley, Neumayer, Troll, Sanay, Belgrano — the five cities most
directly implicated across this run's own contested decisions) — and, by its own nature, naturally extends to
draw on Abowasa, Lazar, and Princess Elisabeth as well, since a subnet-wide archive has no honest reason to
exclude any of them.

**A deliberate closing note.** Like Palmer's own Candidate #20, this chain is built specifically to reflect
on the other nine in this run rather than introduce a wholly separate stake — treating the genuinely
unresolved trade-offs Candidates #11 through #19 each ended on (whose credit got attributed, which fund
allocation won out, which faction's vision governed Troll, which founding narrative got sung, how far the
trade gate opened, whether the substandard parts got tolerated, which route was taken, which priority scheme
woke the reserve, which governance model the coalition adopted) as the actual, lived material a subnet-wide
archive would have to contend with.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **This run's own accumulated decisions** — nine prior candidates (#11–#19), each ending in a genuine,
  unresolved trade-off per this project's own No Good Endings law, rather than a settled consensus, meaning
  the subnet's own recent history already contains multiple live, contested outcomes by this point in the run.
- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2 and 5** — the subnet's own established credit-averse civic
  character, already the direct subject of Candidate #11, used here as one specific, recurring live
  disagreement the archive would have to represent honestly or not at all.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about which specific outcome any of Candidates #11 through #19 actually
  reached — this chain treats those outcomes as still genuinely open, whichever way any individual playthrough
  or design pass ultimately resolves them.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A push emerges to build a single, definitive Halley subnet historical archive — consolidating
records and testimony from across the subnet into one coherent account of its own recent, consequential
decisions, both for the subnet's own communities and for the rest of Tepenia to understand what actually
happened here. The player is asked to help gather contributions from multiple cities — a straightforward, if
large-scale, "build the record" task.

**BUT** — Different cities hold genuinely different, still-contested accounts of the very same recent
events — not settled history, but live, ongoing argument: whether Troll's own governing vision was chosen
fairly, whether the reserve's reactivated priorities actually served the subnet, whether the manufacturing
coalition's governance model was just, whether crediting Neumayer's and Halley's own uncredited legacies ever
actually happened the way it should have. A single "definitive" account isn't achievable without someone
deciding whose version of a still-open argument becomes the official one.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to navigate genuinely competing, current narratives from different
communities about their own shared, recent, unfinished history — arbitrating the present, not simply
recording the past.

**BUT** — The reversal lands directly: whichever version becomes "the archive" doesn't just record history,
it settles it — for the rest of Tepenia's understanding, and for the subnet's own future. Genuine, still-live
disagreements the subnet's own communities haven't actually finished having yet would be closed by the act of
writing them down as though they were already resolved.

**THEREFORE** — What began as building a historical record becomes a real decision about whether the
subnet's own story is actually ready to be told as one story at all.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. A single, authoritative account gives the
subnet and the rest of Tepenia a clear, usable historical record — useful for outside understanding and
whatever closure the subnet's own communities might want — but risks prematurely settling genuinely
unresolved internal arguments, imposing a false consensus where real, sincere disagreement still exists. A
deliberately multi-vocal archive — presenting the real disagreements side by side rather than picking a
single account — is honest about the fact that the subnet's own story isn't finished being decided, but hands
the rest of Tepenia, and the subnet's own future, a messier, harder-to-use record of what actually happened
and what it meant.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which kind of archive actually gets built: one settled, authoritative account, at the cost of closing
arguments the subnet's own people haven't actually finished having; or a genuinely multi-vocal record, honest
about live disagreement, at the cost of leaving the rest of Tepenia, and the subnet's own future, without a
single clear answer to point to.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "gather the history"
into "the history is still actively contested, not settled"; the second BUT turns "write it down" into
"writing it down settles arguments that are still genuinely live"; the third BUT turns "pick a format" into
"there is no format that gives the subnet both a usable answer and an honest one at the same time." Deleting
any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised
And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the only one in this run of ten built to look back at the other
nine rather than stand entirely on its own — treating the genuinely unresolved trade-offs Candidates #11
through #19 each ended on as the actual, lived material a subnet-wide archive would have to contend with,
rather than settled facts waiting to be filed away. It closes this run the same way Palmer subnet's own
Candidate #20 closed its matching set, and the way each subnet's own Candidate #10 closed its original
ten-candidate set: by asking what the whole collection of stories actually adds up to, rather than adding one
more story next to the rest.
