# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #16: "The Parts That Aren't Supposed to Hold"

**What this is:** a sixteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around a sixth distinct linking
mechanism for this run — a mechanical-fraud/quality investigation, rather than Candidate #11's credit
-attribution campaign, Candidate #12's reconstruction-fund triage, Candidate #13's faction-mediation,
Candidate #14's commemorative-narrative reconciliation, or Candidate #15's trade-diplomacy negotiation.
**Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Belgrano, Sanay, Troll, Halley, Neumayer).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Belgrano Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the established garage/mechanic subculture specializing in
  Rastra-specific repair, and the proposed spare-parts fabrication and light manufacturing economy built
  directly on that expertise, producing replacement components that would otherwise have to be shipped in.
- **Halley Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 1 and 2** — Halley's own total dependency on overland resupply
  through Belgrano and Sanay, and Neumayer's own established position as the Rastra lineage's actual
  engineering origin and technical authority.
- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2 and 5** — Neumayer's own established meticulous internal
  record-keeping character, used here to justify its role as the technical body capable of actually assessing
  what's wrong with the parts, rather than simply confiscating them.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim connecting this candidate's substandard-parts problem to Candidate #12's
  own reconstruction-fund allocation — this chain treats the parts issue as a distinct, ongoing operational
  problem, not a downstream consequence of that fund's own resolution either way.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A rash of unusual vehicle breakdowns surfaces across the subnet — trucks failing along the
Belgrano-to-Halley overland route, machinery faltering at Sanay's trucking depots and Troll's own airfield
haulage. The player is asked to trace the cause and, if it's mechanical fraud, shut it down — a
straightforward, if urgent, investigation.

**BUT** — Tracing the failures back means following the actual parts-distribution network the vehicles run
on: Belgrano as the source of the specific substandard components, and Sanay's and Troll's own trucking and
haulage operations as the points where those parts actually get installed and put back into service. The
investigation has to proceed node by node along a real physical network, not toward one central culprit.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to investigate each city in turn — Belgrano, Sanay, Troll — building an
actual picture of how these components are getting made and where they end up.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once Neumayer's engineers formally assess the parts: this isn't profiteering.
Belgrano's own mechanics started producing cheaper substitute components, using inferior materials, because
genuine replacement parts and the shipping capacity to bring them in simply couldn't keep pace with real,
ongoing demand — and without these substandard parts, a significant portion of the fleet actually running
Halley's overland resupply route would already be grounded. The parts are measurably less safe, but they're
also, right now, the reason vehicles Halley depends on are still moving at all.

**THEREFORE** — What began as shutting down mechanical fraud becomes a real question about whether enforcing
safety is worth grounding vehicles a whole city's own resupply currently depends on.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Enforcing strict quality standards and halting
substandard part production protects long-term vehicle safety and removes a real, elevated risk from the
exact route Halley depends on most — but immediately shrinks the working fleet serving that route, worsening
an already precarious supply situation for a city with no alternative of its own. Allowing continued,
inspected use of the substandard parts keeps more vehicles running and Halley's resupply flowing at current
levels — but knowingly tolerates a real safety risk on the one route the subnet's most exposed city has no
backup for.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in how this actually gets settled: full enforcement, protecting long-term safety at the cost of
grounding part of the fleet Halley's resupply depends on right now; or tolerating inspected substandard use,
keeping the fleet running at the cost of a real, ongoing safety risk on Halley's own lifeline route.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "trace a breakdown" into
"the failures trace along an entire distribution network, not to one source"; the second BUT turns "find the
maker" into "the maker is responding to genuine scarcity, and the parts are currently load-bearing for
Halley's own resupply"; the third BUT turns "enforce or tolerate" into "there is no version of the choice
that doesn't cost either long-term safety or Halley's own current resupply capacity." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Halley subnet candidate structured as a genuine mechanical
-fraud investigation, giving DLC 5 gameplay variety distinct from the campaign, triage, mediation,
ceremony-planning, and diplomacy structures already built across this run. It also gives Belgrano its first
appearance in this five-city run as a source of a real problem rather than purely a supporting logistics
node, and gives Neumayer a fittingly technical role — the subnet's own established engineering authority
called in specifically to render a judgment its own meticulous, credit-averse character is already
established as suited for.
