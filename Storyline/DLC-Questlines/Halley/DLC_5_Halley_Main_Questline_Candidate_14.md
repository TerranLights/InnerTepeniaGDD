# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #14: "Whichever Version Gets Sung"

**What this is:** a fourteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around a fourth distinct
linking mechanism for this run — negotiating a subnet-wide founding commemoration's own official narrative,
rather than Candidate #11's credit-attribution campaign, Candidate #12's reconstruction-fund triage, or
Candidate #13's faction-mediation. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear
Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Lazar, Abowasa, Halley, Neumayer, Belgrano).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 4** — the subnet's own two clearest examples of founding plurality,
  in opposite directions: Lazar, never founded by one nation at all, two separate settlements
  (Novolazarevskaya, the unoccupied Maitri site) coalescing into Tepenia's single largest city with no clean
  founding-population story to dilute; and Abowasa, jointly founded by Finland and Sweden simultaneously at
  the subnet's smallest, most intimate civic scale — plurality expressed as household-level negotiation, not
  demographic mathematics.
- **The general pattern most other Halley subnet cities follow instead** (Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 4's own
  framing note) — one founding nation, later diluted to Notable tier by an eventual American-led majority —
  used here to place Halley, Neumayer, and Belgrano as the subnet's own typical-founding contrast cases,
  attending a ceremony built to celebrate the two cities that broke that pattern.
- **Lazar's own established scale** (Tepenia's single largest city, per `project_lazar_population_rebalancing`)
  and Halley's own established status as the subnet's namesake — used here to justify Halley as the natural
  host city for a subnet-wide commemorative event.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about the actual current demographic balance between
  Novolazarevskaya-descended and Maitri-site populations within Lazar — this chain treats that balance, and
  whether it still carries any felt asymmetry, as something the player's own investigation has to establish,
  not an assumed fact.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With a round-number anniversary of the subnet's own founding era approaching, a push emerges to
hold a formal, subnet-wide commemoration at Halley, centering Lazar's and Abowasa's own established founding
plurality — the subnet's uniquely matched pair of maximum-scale and minimum-scale examples — as its
celebratory throughline. The player is asked to help gather each city's own contribution and coordinate the
event — a straightforward, if ambitious, ceremony-planning task.

**BUT** — Once the player gathers actual community testimony in Lazar, rather than the official version,
real tension surfaces: the "coalescing" story isn't experienced as fully settled internally. Descendants
connected to the original, occupied Novolazarevskaya founding population feel their own settlement's
identity is being smoothed into an "equal merger" narrative with the once-unoccupied Maitri site — a framing
that doesn't match how unequal the two origins actually feel in lived memory.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually investigate this tension directly, talking to different
community factions within Lazar and cross-referencing against Abowasa's own genuinely equal, deliberately
joint founding as a contrast, to determine whether the subnet's celebratory "plural founding" framing is
honest or quietly papering something over.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the asymmetry is confirmed: correcting the commemoration's own framing to
reflect it would validate real, still-felt grievances among Novolazarevskaya-descended residents — but risks
reopening a civic tension that Lazar's own current, functioning unified identity has depended on not
re-litigating for generations. The "equal merger" story isn't just a nicer narrative; it's load-bearing for
Lazar's own present-day cohesion.

**THEREFORE** — What began as planning a ceremony becomes a real question about whether historical honesty
is worth the risk of reopening a civic wound a city has, by all present appearances, already moved past.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Presenting the honest, asymmetric account
validates real, felt history and avoids substituting a comfortable myth for what actually happened — but
risks destabilizing a civic peace Lazar's own present unity genuinely depends on staying settled, for no
guaranteed benefit beyond the truth itself. Presenting the comfortable "equal merger" narrative preserves
Lazar's own functional cohesion and gives the whole subnet a clean, unifying story to commemorate together —
but is, in a real sense, not what happened, and quietly overwrites the lived experience of part of Lazar's own
population for the sake of a better story.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which version becomes the subnet's official commemorative account: the honest, asymmetric version,
validating real grievance at the cost of reopening a settled civic tension; or the comfortable, unified
version, preserving Lazar's own present cohesion at the cost of overwriting part of its population's actual
lived history.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "gather contributions"
into "one of the two celebrated founding stories isn't actually settled the way the plan assumes"; the second
BUT turns "correct the record" into "correcting it risks reopening a tension the city's own present unity
depends on leaving closed"; the third BUT turns "pick a version" into "there is no version that doesn't cost
either the truth or a settled civic peace." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually
choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Pattern 4's own celebratory framing — Halley subnet holding both
of the project's clearest examples of founding plurality — and asks whether one of those two examples
actually earns the celebration it's been given, rather than treating the pattern as settled, positive fact.
It's also the first Halley subnet candidate whose central tension lives entirely inside a single city's own
population (Lazar), even though the questline itself is staged as a five-city, subnet-wide event — making the
external ceremony-planning frame a container for what is, underneath, an internal reckoning.
