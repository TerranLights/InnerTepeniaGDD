# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #5: "Two Moods, One City"

**What this is:** a fifth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, the first candidate built around Lazar —
the one Halley subnet city not yet used in Candidates #1–#4. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only
candidate** — the 5th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 2 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Lazar, Neumayer) — Neumayer's second appearance, after Candidate #2,
but in a completely different role: there, its quiet competence was a century-old historical credit dispute;
here, it's a present-day engineering collaborator, the same established civic character (Local Cultures §21,
§6) producing a different kind of consequence in a different context.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Lazar's own Full Extrapolation, Section IV** — the Old Core/New Expansion divide correlates directly
  with war damage: the old Novolazarevskaya core, built on continuously-maintained, deliberately robust
  research-station infrastructure, took proportionally less damage; the newer, faster-grown former-Maitri
  expansion took the worse damage — the district whose whole civic pride rests on "never having gone dark"
  is also, materially, the one that survived most intact.
- **Lazar's own Full Extrapolation, Section VII** — the developed dual-personality idea: Old Core carries a
  quieter, institutional, reserved character; New Expansion carries the bulk of the megacity's commercial
  density and faster, overflow-driven energy. Two adjacent moods within one functioning metropolis, not a
  tension the way old-core-vs-majority splits work in other cities.
- **Lazar's own Full Extrapolation, Section III** — Lazar's actual commercial-capital function: the
  convergence point of Hwy 7 and the Belgrano Highway Extension, the region's largest market, functioning as
  the Halley subnet's real distribution and logistics capital.
- **Lazar's own Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the established, frictionless relationship to Neumayer:
  complementary specialties, not competition. Neumayer relies on Lazar as its nearest genuine megacity
  market; Lazar's own engineering sector (20% of its industry, an inherited Novolazarevskaya research
  tradition) already has an ongoing professional relationship with Neumayer's precision-engineering culture.
- **Lazar's own Full Extrapolation, Section VIII** — Reconstruction Director Tomás Lindqvist-Adeyemi, a
  present-day figure overseeing Lazar's post-war recovery, proposed as a natural DLC 5 contact point given
  the sheer scale of what recovery at megacity size actually requires.
- **Neumayer's own Local Cultures, §6/§21** — rigor as civic virtue, precision over performance, a culture
  that channels its energy into its own instruments rather than outward projection — the same established
  character Candidate #2 already used, applied here to engineering collaboration rather than historical
  credit.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any Federation-wide or national reconstruction-funding mechanism — no such
  institution is established anywhere in current material, and inventing one wholesale for this chain would
  violate the method's own "actually emergent, not invented" requirement. This chain stays entirely at the
  scale of one city's own internal recovery and one existing inter-city relationship.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Reconstruction Director Tomás Lindqvist-Adeyemi asks the player to help fix a real, visible
imbalance in Lazar's own recovery: Old Core, structurally intact and disciplined, is sitting on functioning
infrastructure capacity it isn't using at full stretch, while New Expansion — the district that actually
drives the megacity's commercial economy, and by extension a real share of the whole subnet's own
distribution network — took the worse damage and is genuinely struggling. It looks like a straightforward
internal-reallocation job: convince Old Core's institutions to share what they're not using.

**BUT** — Old Core's own reserve isn't idle bureaucratic hoarding. The exact discipline and continuous
-maintenance culture that let it survive "never gone dark" (Section IV) is the same discipline producing its
current spare capacity — loosening that institutional rigor to free up resources for New Expansion risks
undermining the very resilience that makes Old Core a stable anchor for the rest of the city's recovery at
all. This is a real institutional logic, not stubbornness the player can simply talk past.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to find a different route into New Expansion's recovery, one that doesn't
require extracting anything from Old Core directly. The established, frictionless Lazar-Neumayer
relationship (Section V) is the obvious lever: Neumayer's own precision-engineering culture, already
professionally connected to Lazar's engineering sector, could plausibly help rebuild New Expansion's damaged
commercial infrastructure faster and more resiliently than New Expansion's own overflow-driven building
tradition ever managed on its own.

**THEREFORE** — The player brokers exactly that arrangement, bringing Neumayer's own engineers and standards
into New Expansion's reconstruction.

**BUT** — It works, and that's the reversal: rebuilt to Neumayer's own rigorous standard, New Expansion's
commercial infrastructure recovers faster and holds up better — but it also starts, visibly, to carry some
of Old Core's and Neumayer's own more measured, disciplined character, simply because that's the standard it
was rebuilt to. The fast, loud, overflow-driven commercial energy that made New Expansion New Expansion
(Section VII's own "two adjacent moods") is exactly what a more careful, precision-built infrastructure
doesn't naturally produce. The fix that saves the district's function threatens the specific character that
made it worth saving in the first place.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
influence over how the rebuild actually proceeds, not a clean success to report back: push for the full
Neumayer-standard rebuild (faster, more resilient, materially better for the whole megacity's economy and
every subnet city that depends on Lazar as a market and highway junction — but New Expansion's own loud,
fast, distinctive mood quietly homogenizes into something closer to Old Core's, and Lazar's established
"two adjacent moods" identity loses one of its two moods in the process); or deliberately keep the rebuild
messier and slower, true to New Expansion's own overflow-driven character even where that costs real
resilience (the district recovers on its own terms, distinctive energy intact, but genuinely more exposed to
whatever damages it next time, since it never adopted the standard that would have protected it). Neither
option is presented as correct — one trades identity for resilience, the other trades resilience for
identity, and Lazar's own dual-personality civic character can't have both increase at once.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "reallocate resources"
into "Old Core's reserve is load-bearing, not spare, so extraction isn't free"; the second BUT (structurally
a THEREFORE-THEREFORE run resolved by the reversal) turns "broker outside help" into "the help works, but it
changes what it touches"; the final BUT turns "New Expansion is rebuilt" into "rebuilding it well and keeping
it what it was are now in direct tension." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually
choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first to treat Lazar's own established "dual-personality" idea
(Section VII, explicitly flagged in that document as the developer's own previously undecided idea, "now
developed") as something that can be actively lost through the very process of recovering from the war,
rather than a static fact about the city's geography. It also reuses Neumayer — already established, via
Candidate #2, as a city whose quiet competence tends to produce complicated downstream consequences — in a
genuinely different register: not a century-old credit dispute this time, but a present-day rescue that
succeeds practically and costs something cultural, giving Neumayer's own established character a second,
distinct expression rather than repeating the first.
