# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #21: "The First Time It Ever Goes Dark"

**What this is:** a twenty-first prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around a civic-identity
-versus-engineering-risk dilemma. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear
Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Lazar, Neumayer, Sanay, Troll, Belgrano).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Lazar Full Extrapolation, Section IV and VI** — the established civic pride built on Lazar's own
  century-plus, unbroken "never gone dark" operational tradition, inherited from Novolazarevskaya's own
  continuously-maintained research-station infrastructure, and the Lazarus echo already noted as fitting
  local folklore.
- **Lazar Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the established, straightforwardly complementary relationship
  between Lazar and Neumayer, with Neumayer's own precision-engineering culture holding an ongoing
  professional relationship with Lazar's technical sector.
- **Lazar Full Extrapolation, Section III** — Lazar's own established status as the Halley subnet's actual
  commercial and logistics capital, the convergence point of Hwy 7 and the Belgrano Highway Extension, used
  here to justify real disruption risk to Sanay's, Troll's, and Belgrano's own trade and logistics if Lazar's
  grid or highway function is affected.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about the exact cause of the grid instability — this chain
  treats the engineering problem itself as a given, incidental fact, not material to develop further.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Ongoing reconstruction work uncovers a genuine instability in part of Lazar's own power grid,
requiring a planned, temporary shutdown to address safely — the first deliberate "going dark" in the city's
entire operational history. The player is asked to help plan and manage this — a straightforward, if
historic, engineering-safety task.

**BUT** — Lazar's own old Novolazarevskaya core, whose civic pride is explicitly built on never having gone
dark, has real, strong resistance to this. For many residents, even a brief, controlled shutdown means
losing something the community has valued as part of its own identity since founding, not a minor
inconvenience — and some push instead for a costlier, riskier workaround that would avoid any shutdown at
all.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to consult the old core's own community and reconstruction officials, and
travel to Neumayer for a second engineering opinion, and to Sanay, Troll, and Belgrano to assess how much
disruption either option would actually cause to the subnet's own trade and logistics running through Lazar.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once Neumayer's own engineers confirm the workaround is genuinely, technically
possible — but measurably riskier, carrying a real, quantifiable higher chance of a much larger, less
controlled failure later, and considerably more expensive to boot. This isn't a case of an obviously safer
choice being resisted for sentimental reasons; both paths are technically real options with real
consequences.

**THEREFORE** — What began as an engineering-safety decision becomes a real choice between the technically
safer option and a genuine attempt to preserve something the community values as core to its own identity.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Accepting the controlled shutdown is the
technically safer choice, minimizing real risk to the grid and to Lazar's own population — but ends,
permanently, a civic legacy the old core's own identity has been built around since its founding, something
no future event can undo once broken. Pursuing the costlier workaround preserves that legacy intact — but
knowingly accepts a real, quantifiable higher risk of a larger, less controlled failure at some future point,
gambling collective safety for a symbolic record.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether Lazar actually accepts the shutdown or pursues the workaround: accept it, minimizing risk at
the cost of ending an unbroken civic legacy; or preserve the record, honoring that legacy at the cost of a
real, accepted future risk.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "plan a shutdown" into
"the shutdown itself threatens something the community treats as core identity, not inconvenience"; the
second BUT turns "find a workaround" into "the workaround is real but genuinely riskier, not simply the safe
alternative in disguise"; the third BUT turns "shut down or work around" into "there is no option that
doesn't cost either an unbroken legacy or a real, accepted future risk." Deleting any one Complication
changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Lazar's own established "never gone dark" civic pride — already
flagged as a quietly delightful detail in its own source material — and turns it into an actual, high-stakes
decision rather than leaving it as flavor, asking what a community's own founding myth is actually worth
once a real, practical situation asks it to bend. It's also the first candidate to treat Lazar's own
established role as the subnet's commercial and logistics capital as a source of genuine stakes for other
cities, rather than simply as scale-setting background detail.
