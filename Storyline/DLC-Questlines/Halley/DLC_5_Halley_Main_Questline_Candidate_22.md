# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #22: "Whatever Moves Through Isn't Just Freight"

**What this is:** a twenty-second prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around a protected-freight
-protocol dilemma with real power-consolidation stakes. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.**
Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Troll, Halley, Sanay, Neumayer, Belgrano). **Explicitly does not
reference the player character's own origin** — this chain uses the Cradle network strictly as general
nationwide infrastructure whose shipments happen to pass through Troll, not as any specific chamber tied to
the player's own backstory.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Troll Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 3** — the established, explicitly flagged proposal that Troll's
  own freight network may occasionally carry actual fabrication-synthesis chambers, manufactured elsewhere and
  routed onward to other cities, meaning whoever controls freight-handling at Troll has real, if incidental,
  influence over the nationwide robot-creation supply chain.
- **Troll Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 1** — Halley's own established total dependency on how Troll's
  fractured control situation actually plays out, used here to justify Halley's own direct stake in this
  chain's outcome.
- **Sanay's own established role as the subnet's supply-chain entry point** (Sanay Full Extrapolation,
  Section II), used here to ground its stake in how any new freight protocol interacts with the existing
  system.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about which specific chamber travels through Troll or where it's
  ultimately headed, and any connection to the player character's own origin — this chain treats the general
  fact of chamber traffic, not any individual shipment, as the actual stake.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Troll's own freight network confirmed to occasionally carry fabrication-synthesis chambers
bound for other cities, a push emerges to establish a dedicated, protected handling protocol for this
uniquely important cargo, separate from Troll's own ordinary, unstable freight system. The player is asked
to help negotiate this protocol — a straightforward, if delicate, logistics-security task.

**BUT** — Troll's own competing factions each recognize what controlling this newly-elevated freight category
would actually mean — real prestige and leverage within the airfield's own fractured situation, since
everyone now understands these shipments touch population growth itself, not ordinary trade. None of them
wants to give up a direct claim to it.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to negotiate among Troll's own factions, and travel to Halley and Sanay to
understand how a dedicated protocol would need to interact with the wider freight system both cities already
depend on.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the shape of a workable protocol comes into focus: whichever faction, or
neutral authority, actually ends up running it gains disproportionate, lasting influence within Troll's own
power struggle — not from freight volume, but from the protocol's own symbolic and practical weight.
Establishing any dedicated protocol at all risks becoming the decisive factor that quietly settles Troll's
broader fractured control, without anyone having directly negotiated that outcome.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a narrow logistics-security fix becomes a real question about whether solving
it at all is worth the risk of deciding Troll's whole future by the back door.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Establishing a dedicated, protected protocol
gives this uniquely important cargo real, reliable protection from Troll's ordinary instability — but risks
becoming the lever that quietly resolves Troll's entire fractured power struggle, handing whoever runs it
influence nobody directly negotiated for. Leaving chamber shipments folded into ordinary freight avoids
creating that new, decisive lever — but exposes uniquely consequential cargo to the same ongoing instability
as everything else moving through Troll, with real, higher-order consequences if a shipment is delayed, lost,
or damaged.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether a dedicated protocol actually gets established: establish it, protecting the cargo at the
cost of risking Troll's own power struggle being settled unintentionally; or leave it folded into ordinary
freight, avoiding that risk at the cost of leaving critical cargo exposed to real, ongoing instability.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "establish a protocol"
into "every faction wants control of it for reasons beyond the freight itself"; the second BUT turns
"negotiate a workable version" into "any version at all risks quietly deciding Troll's whole future"; the
third BUT turns "establish it or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either critical cargo's own
safety or an unintended resolution to Troll's power struggle." Deleting any one Complication changes what the
player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Troll's own Cross-Reference Synthesis at its explicit word — a
finding flagged as "currently unexplored" and "worth flagging... if a future design pass wants to raise
[Troll's] stakes further" — and builds exactly that stakes-raising thread into an actual candidate, without
ever touching the player character's own specific origin. It's also the first candidate to treat a purely
logistical fix as potentially more politically consequential than the political conflict it was meant to
sidestep.
