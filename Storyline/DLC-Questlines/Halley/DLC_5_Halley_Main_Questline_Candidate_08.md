# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #8: "What the Manifest Doesn't Say"

**What this is:** an eighth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around Sanay's own established Trade
Yard and "Competence Without Commentary" civic character — material used only in passing, as part of
Candidate #1's logistics ensemble, and never centered on its own terms until now. **Not canon, not chosen,
not the only candidate** — the 8th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation
Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Halley subnet's 8 cities (Sanay alone)** — Sanay's first solo candidate, joining Princess
Elisabeth (#3), Troll (#6), and Belgrano (#7) in that pattern. The first Halley subnet candidate to center on
direct Upper Earth contact rather than purely internal Tepenian stakes.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Sanay's own Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the Trade Yard: a physically demarcated section of
  Sanay's port where incoming South African freighter crews dock, unload, and conduct business under
  defined, bounded protocols — practical rather than hostile, explicitly modeled on the real Canton System's
  own welcome-the-trade-but-bound-the-contact logic, distinct from Palmer City's more socially porous,
  tourism-facing Upper Earth relationship.
- **Sanay's own Full Extrapolation, Section VI** — Councilor Pieter Alsdorf, the figure most associated
  with Sanay's "Competence Without Commentary" civic stance: on record for actively declining to frame the
  city's own survival or success as exceptional, even when outsiders try to cast it that way.
- **Sanay's own Full Extrapolation, Section II** — Sanay's material stake in staying on good terms with
  Upper Earth trade at all: it's the subnet's actual supply-chain entry point, and its own function depends
  on incoming freighter shipments continuing without disruption.
- **Established Tepenian precedent for Upper Earth defection** — multiple Tepenian founding populations
  (Zhongshan's Sinian defectors chief among them) trace directly to individuals who chose exile from Upper
  Earth nations over remaining inside them; this chain treats a present-day, small-scale echo of that same
  choice as a live, ongoing possibility rather than something that only happened once, generations ago.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any resolution of the still-open, larger project question of *why* Upper Earth
  trades with Tepenia at all (flagged elsewhere as unresolved) — this chain stays at the scale of one
  individual's choice and one city's response to it, not a statement about the trade relationship's own
  underlying motives.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — During a routine Trade Yard exchange, a member of an incoming South African freighter crew
quietly approaches a Sanay official (or the player directly) with a request that falls entirely outside the
Trade Yard's own bounded protocols: they don't want to reboard. They want to stay in Tepenia. It looks, at
first, like a request the city's own careful systems should be able to handle quietly and humanely.

**BUT** — The Trade Yard's whole design exists specifically to prevent this kind of personal entanglement
between Upper Earth crews and Tepenian residents (Section V) — not out of hostility, but because the
bounded-contact system is precisely what's kept the trade relationship stable and low-risk on both sides.
A crew member simply not returning is exactly the outcome the boundary was built to avoid, and if it becomes
visible to Upper Earth authorities, it risks the whole arrangement Sanay's own function as the subnet's
supply-chain entry point (Section II) depends on.

**THEREFORE** — Handling this "quietly," the instinct Sanay's own civic character (Section VI) would
normally reach for without a second thought, turns out to require something much more deliberate than
simple discretion: an actual, constructed explanation for the crew member's absence that the departing
freighter's own captain and crew will accept without further investigation.

**BUT** — Constructing that explanation means Sanay's own celebrated "Competence Without Commentary" —
usually just a refusal to dramatize its own survival — has to be turned into something heavier: an actively
maintained falsehood, sustained indefinitely, resting on the same quiet, undramatic civic instinct that
otherwise reads as humility. And it doesn't stay contained to one case, either: if it works, and word of it
ever travels informally, other crew members in the future may see it as a viable path too, meaning each
quiet success makes the next attempt more likely, and each attempt risks the same exposure all over again.

**THEREFORE** — The player's actual task shifts from "help one person" to "decide whether Sanay is willing
to build and maintain a real, repeatable risk to its own supply-chain lifeline, on behalf of people the
Trade Yard's own bounded design was never meant to make room for."

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Helping this person stay, and quietly
accepting the precedent that creates, gives someone the exact choice generations of Tepenia's own founding
populations were once allowed to make — but it puts Sanay's own material stability, and the reliability of
the whole subnet's supply-chain entry point, on the line for a choice no city's Trade Yard protocol was ever
designed to accommodate. Following the bounded protocol strictly, and returning the crew member as required,
protects everything Sanay's own function depends on — but denies one person, in the present day, precisely
the kind of exile-and-belonging this country's entire population history is built from.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which way Sanay actually decides: help construct the cover story and let the crew member stay,
accepting a genuine, ongoing vulnerability folded quietly into the city's own celebrated undramatic
competence; or hold the boundary, returning the crew member to their ship, keeping Sanay's own trade
relationship and civic character exactly as bounded and legible as it has always been, at direct, personal
cost to someone who asked for the one thing Sanay's whole system exists to prevent itself from having to
decide about.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "grant a quiet request"
into "the Trade Yard's whole design exists to prevent exactly this"; the second BUT turns "handle it
discreetly" into "discretion here means active, ongoing deception with its own compounding risk"; the third
BUT turns "should this person stay" into "there is no version of the decision that doesn't cost Sanay
something real, whichever way it goes." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually
choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Halley subnet candidate to put real weight on direct,
individual-scale Upper Earth contact rather than treating Upper Earth purely as an offscreen trading partner
or historical origin point. It also gives Sanay's own quietest, least dramatic established trait —
"Competence Without Commentary" — a genuinely uncomfortable second meaning: the same instinct that makes the
city humble about its own survival is exactly the instinct that would let it carry out a real deception
without anyone, inside or outside the city, ever needing to make a show of it. Distinct from every prior
candidate in stakes-type: not a resource fight, a credit dispute, a triage call, or an identity-charter
question, but a single, deniable human choice with a real, compounding institutional risk attached to it.
