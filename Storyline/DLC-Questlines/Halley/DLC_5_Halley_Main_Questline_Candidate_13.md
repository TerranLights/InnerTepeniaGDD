# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #13: "Whoever Holds the Gate Decides Who Eats"

**What this is:** a thirteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around a third distinct linking
mechanism for this run — mediating a multi-faction conflict over shared critical infrastructure, rather than
Candidate #11's credit-attribution campaign or Candidate #12's reconstruction-fund triage. **Not canon, not
chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Troll, Sanay, Halley, Belgrano, Neumayer).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Halley Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 1** — the full, established supply chain: Sanay as the
  subnet's actual freight entry point, splitting goods either directly by Hwy 7 or onward to Troll's airfield
  for more remote destinations; Troll's own airfield control genuinely fractured among competing factions with
  no single authority; and Halley, having deliberately forgone its own docks and airstrip, depending entirely
  on overland resupply through Belgrano and Sanay — meaning Halley's own survival is already established as
  hostage to how Troll's fractured control actually plays out.
- **Halley Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — Neumayer's own founding-era redesign of Belgrano's crude
  first-generation hauler into the vehicle that became the template for every Rastra built across Tepenia
  since, used here to place Neumayer as the subnet's own natural stake-holder in whatever vehicles actually
  run the Belgrano-to-Halley overland resupply route this chain depends on.
- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 5** — Troll's own established character (quietly higher-quality goods
  rather than ostentation) and Sanay's own "Competence Without Commentary" identity, used here to ground the
  competing factions' own values as sincere civic positions rather than simple corruption.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about which faction currently holds the most territory or
  power at Troll — Throughway 1 establishes the fracture as a fact but not its current balance, so this chain
  treats the balance itself as something the player's mediation actually has to establish.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Troll Airfield's control fractured among competing factions and Halley's own survival
already hostage to how that fracture plays out, the player is asked to broker some kind of resolution — a
straightforward, if high-stakes, mediation task to restore stable, unified control over the airfield's
own freight priorities.

**BUT** — The fracture isn't simple corruption or a power vacuum; the factions hold genuinely different,
sincerely-held visions for what Troll's own control should actually be used for. One faction wants the
airfield leveraged to grow Troll's own trade and long-term prosperity, in keeping with the city's established
quiet-competence character. Another wants freight priority guaranteed first to Halley's and other dependent
cities' own basic subsistence needs, market conditions aside. A unified authority under either vision embeds
that faction's own values into how life-or-death goods actually get allocated subnet-wide.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually negotiate a substantive governance framework among these competing
visions, not just broker a ceasefire between factions.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the framework's real reach becomes clear: whichever vision wins doesn't
stay contained to Troll's own internal politics. It directly determines how reliably Halley — with no
alternative supply route of its own — actually gets fed and resourced, how much of Sanay's own distribution
labor gets valued, and how Neumayer's own Rastra-lineage vehicles get prioritized for the Belgrano-to-Halley
overland run that Halley's survival already depends on.

**THEREFORE** — What began as local mediation over an airfield becomes a decision that reshapes two other
cities' own daily survival odds and economic standing.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Locking in the subsistence-first vision
guarantees Halley a protected floor regardless of market conditions — a humane, reliable outcome for the
subnet's most exposed city — but suppresses the prosperity-driven faction's own legitimate claim to Troll's
resources, and with it, the long-term investment capacity that faction's vision would have generated for
everyone eventually. Locking in the prosperity-driven vision grows the whole subnet's long-term capacity and
honors Troll's own established economic character — but leaves Halley's basic survival exposed to market
-driven allocation with no protected floor, exactly the vulnerability Throughway 1 already establishes as
Halley's defining danger.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which framework the mediation actually settles on: a subsistence-first guarantee, protecting Halley's
survival at the cost of a legitimate faction's own claim and the subnet's longer-term growth; or a
prosperity-first framework, honoring Troll's own character and long-term capacity at the cost of leaving
Halley's own survival exposed to conditions outside its control.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "broker peace" into "the
factions hold sincerely different visions, not just competing greed"; the second BUT turns "pick a
governance framework" into "the framework's consequences reach directly into two other cities' own survival
and standing"; the third BUT turns "choose a vision" into "there is no vision that doesn't cost either a
legitimate faction's claim and the subnet's growth, or Halley's own protected survival." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Throughway 1's own already-established "Halley's survival is
hostage to Troll's fractured control" fact and turns it from background lore into the actual climax
mechanism, rather than leaving it as scene-setting — the player's mediation choice literally decides how that
existing dependency resolves. It also gives Neumayer a stake in a conflict its own name never appears in
directly within the source material, by following its established Rastra lineage forward into the specific
overland route this chain's stakes actually run on — a fresh throughway of its own, built the same way the
Ultra-Megasheet's own Throughways were.
