# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #25: "Whichever Ground Buys More Time"

**What this is:** a twenty-fifth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around a relocation-site
decision weighing stability against logistics position. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.**
Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Halley, Sanay, Belgrano, Troll, Abowasa).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 1** — Halley's own established "active, recency-favoring resilience"
  mechanism: continuously relocating, ski-mounted modules built on a drifting ice shelf.
- **Halley Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 1** — Halley's own total dependency on overland resupply
  through Belgrano and Sanay, and its own established relay-nexus infrastructure built at Sanay.
- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3** — Abowasa's own established geological and structural-stability
  research expertise, used here to assess relocation-site viability.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about the exact cause or timeline of the current ice-shelf
  destabilization — this chain treats the relocation need itself as a given, established fact.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Halley's own ice shelf showing signs of accelerated destabilization, the city needs to plan
its next major relocation — continuing its established practice — sooner and further than any previous move.
The player is asked to help survey options and organize the relocation — a straightforward, if significant,
logistics task.

**BUT** — Two genuinely viable sites exist. One sits on safer, more stable ice, but considerably further from
the overland routes Halley depends on and the relay-nexus infrastructure it built at Sanay. The other keeps
Halley well-positioned relative to these critical connections, but sits on comparatively less stable ground.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to consult Sanay, Belgrano, and Troll about which site would actually serve
the subnet's own logistics best, and travel to Abowasa for an honest assessment of the real difference in
risk between the two.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once Abowasa's own assessment comes back: the "safer" site isn't permanently
safe either — it simply buys more time before another relocation becomes necessary. The well-positioned
site's own added risk isn't theoretical — it carries a genuinely higher probability of a more disruptive,
less-controlled relocation being forced within a much shorter timeframe.

**THEREFORE** — What began as choosing a relocation site becomes a real choice between buying more time and
accepting a real, higher near-term risk of a worse, more sudden move later.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Choosing the safer, more remote site protects
Halley's own stability for longer and reduces the risk of a disruptive future relocation — but weakens its
position relative to the overland supply network Belgrano, Sanay, and Troll all depend on, adding real
distance and difficulty to routes already stretched thin. Choosing the well-positioned but less stable site
preserves Halley's own critical logistics function for the whole subnet — but accepts a real, higher
probability of being forced into a more sudden, less-controlled relocation sooner, a genuine future risk to
the city's own population.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which site Halley actually commits to: the safer, more remote site, buying stability at the cost of
the subnet's own logistics network; or the well-positioned, less stable site, preserving that network at the
cost of a real, higher future risk.

---

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "pick a site" into "the
safe option and the well-positioned option are genuinely different places"; the second BUT turns "get an
expert assessment" into "neither option is actually safe, only differently risky on different timelines";
the third BUT turns "choose stability or position" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either the
subnet's own logistics network or a real, higher future risk to Halley itself." Deleting any one Complication
changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Halley's own defining survival mechanism — continuous
relocation — and treats an ordinary instance of that mechanism as a genuine, high-stakes decision rather than
background lore, asking what happens when the city's own established coping strategy runs into a choice with
no comfortable answer. It's also the first candidate to make Halley's own relationship to the rest of the
subnet's logistics network, rather than any single neighbor's own crisis, the actual thing at stake.
