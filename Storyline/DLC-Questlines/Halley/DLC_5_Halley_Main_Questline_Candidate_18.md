# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #18: "Whichever Priority Wakes Up First"

**What this is:** an eighteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around an eighth distinct
linking mechanism for this run — a functional retrospective, reconstructing lost pre-war technical knowledge,
rather than Candidate #11's credit-attribution campaign, Candidate #12's reconstruction-fund triage,
Candidate #13's faction-mediation, Candidate #14's commemorative-narrative reconciliation, Candidate #15's
trade-diplomacy negotiation, Candidate #16's mechanical-fraud investigation, or Candidate #17's physical
-route crisis. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Princess Elisabeth, Neumayer, Abowasa, Halley, Lazar). **Distinct
from Candidate #12,** where reconnecting Princess Elisabeth's reserve was one of two allocation options inside
a resource-triage decision already assumed to be technically possible; here, the entire chain is about
whether reconnecting it is possible at all, and what reconnecting it would actually mean once done.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Princess Elisabeth Full Extrapolation, Sections I and II** — the established buried energy reserve,
  intact but cut off at its integration points rather than destroyed, and the established fact that access,
  not existence, was the actual failure.
- **Princess Elisabeth Full Extrapolation, Section VIII** — Straggling Survivor Joos Kaminari, a former
  underground maintenance crew member with firsthand knowledge of the reserve's own access points, proposed
  specifically as a natural questline source for reaching the buried systems.
- **Halley Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — Neumayer's own established position as the subnet's
  deepest engineering authority, spanning three separate historical eras of consequential, uncredited design
  work.
- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3** — Abowasa's own established geological and structural-stability
  research expertise, used here to assess whether the reserve's own underground tunnels and conduits, likely
  unmaintained since the war, are even safe to re-enter.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about the reserve's total energy capacity or how large a benefit
  reconnecting it would actually deliver — this chain treats the reconnection itself, and the system's own
  inherited behavior once reconnected, as the actual stakes, not a guaranteed windfall.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Princess Elisabeth's own buried energy reserve established as intact but disconnected, a
push emerges to actually reconnect it — but nobody currently knows how. The specific engineering knowledge of
how to safely restore the severed integration points died with the war. The player is asked to piece the lost
technical picture back together from surviving experts and records scattered across the subnet — a
straightforward, if demanding, retrospective-reconstruction task.

**BUT** — The reconstruction genuinely requires all three: Joos Kaminari's own firsthand knowledge of the
reserve's physical access points at Princess Elisabeth itself, Neumayer's own engineering authority to
actually interpret and restore the severed control systems, and Abowasa's own structural-stability expertise
to determine whether the unmaintained underground tunnels are even safe to re-enter at all. No single city
holds enough of the picture alone.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to travel between all three, assembling a single working reconnection plan
from three different, specialized, non-overlapping bodies of knowledge.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the plan comes together and Neumayer's engineers actually study the
recovered control schematics: the system doesn't just need physical reconnection — its original automated
control protocols include hard-coded priority-allocation rules for how the reserve's own energy gets
distributed once active, rules built around a pre-war hierarchy of destinations (some since abandoned, some
no longer remotely proportionate to present need) that has nothing to do with how Halley and Lazar actually
need this energy allocated today.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a technical-reconnection project becomes a real choice about whether to
activate the system exactly as it was originally engineered, or attempt to rewrite its inherited priorities
for the present day.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Activating the reserve exactly as originally
designed preserves the integrity of a delicate, barely-understood system nobody currently has the expertise
to safely redesign from scratch — but distributes a genuinely significant new energy source according to a
century-old hierarchy that no longer reflects who actually needs it, potentially shortchanging Halley's own
established dependency and Lazar's own scale. Attempting to rewrite the priority protocols for present-day
need serves the subnet fairly as it actually exists today — but risks corrupting or destabilizing a system
nobody fully understands, jeopardizing the entire reconnection on the strength of a modification nobody can
fully verify as safe.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which approach the reconnection actually takes: activate the system as originally built, preserving
its fragile integrity at the cost of an outdated, poorly-fitting distribution scheme; or rewrite its
priorities for the present, serving today's actual need at the cost of a real, unverifiable risk to the whole
reconnection succeeding at all.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "reconnect the reserve"
into "no single city holds enough knowledge to do it alone"; the second BUT turns "assemble the plan" into
"the recovered system carries inherited priorities nobody asked for and nobody currently needs"; the third BUT
turns "activate it" into "there is no version of activation that doesn't cost either a fair, present-day
distribution or the fragile system's own verified safety." Deleting any one Complication changes what the
player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes Princess Elisabeth's own established "access, not existence,
was the failure" premise (Full Extrapolation, Section II) — already flagged in that file as a ready-made
questline mechanic — and builds an entire five-city chain directly out of it, rather than treating the
reserve as a simple prize to be unlocked. It also gives Joos Kaminari, a previously unused placeholder Notable
Figure, an actual functional role in a main questline candidate for the first time in this project, and lets
Neumayer's and Abowasa's own established expertises combine on a problem neither could solve alone.
