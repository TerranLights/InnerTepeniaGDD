# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #12: "Whichever Fix Comes First"

**What this is:** a twelfth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around a second distinct linking
mechanism for this run — a subnet-wide reconstruction-fund allocation triage, rather than Candidate #11's
public-credit-attribution campaign. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear
Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 6 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Lazar, Halley, Princess Elisabeth, Abowasa, Sanay, Neumayer).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 1** — the subnet's own complete, four-category taxonomy of war
  damage, one city per category: Lazar (passive, age-favoring resilience), Halley (active, recency-favoring
  resilience), Princess Elisabeth (single-point-of-failure vulnerability, a buried energy reserve intact but
  disconnected), and Abowasa (function-targeted damage, research/technical infrastructure hit while
  residential fabric survived).
- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 3** — the established ground-stability triangle: Halley and Neumayer
  both living with unstable ground and no fixed foundation, Abowasa alone sitting on genuine mainland bedrock
  with real geological/structural-stability research expertise as its own established economic niche, and the
  file's own proposed (currently unstated) consulting relationship running from Abowasa to the other two.
- **Sanay's own established role** as a functioning, largely undamaged port and Arcanet nexus host (Halley
  Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 2), used here as the fund survey's own baseline case — a city with genuine,
  ongoing capacity that a triage process has to explicitly decide not to prioritize.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about Troll's or Belgrano's own damage profiles — this chain stays with
  the four cities Pattern 1 already establishes as the subnet's complete damage taxonomy, plus the two
  additional cities Pattern 3's stability relationship and Pattern 2's undamaged-baseline role bring in.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A subnet-wide reconstruction fund is being organized to rebuild war damage across Halley subnet,
and needs an actual allocation plan. The player is asked to survey the damage across the subnet's affected
cities and help design a fair prioritization order — a straightforward, if large-scale, resource-triage task.

**BUT** — The four damage patterns the survey turns up are fundamentally incompatible in kind, not just
degree: Lazar's older core needs comparatively little; Halley's own mobile-module damage is already partly
self-correcting; Princess Elisabeth's single disconnected reserve is a contained, technical problem; Abowasa's
function-targeted damage is a slow, specialized rebuild of research infrastructure that leaves daily life
mostly intact. There's no single fair metric — "worst damage," "most people affected," "cheapest to fix" —
that ranks these consistently against each other.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to build an actual comparative framework from scratch, traveling to each
city to weigh genuinely different kinds of harm against each other rather than a single shared scale.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once two real options crystallize. Reconnecting Princess Elisabeth's own intact,
buried energy reserve is a comparatively fast, contained fix that would deliver a large, immediate energy
dividend to the entire subnet at once. But funding it first means indefinitely delaying Abowasa's own slower,
specialized rebuild — the very research and technical infrastructure that Halley and Neumayer, per Pattern
3's own established stability triangle, actually depend on for the structural-safety expertise their own
unstable ground requires.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a straightforward damage survey becomes a real choice between a fast, large
-scale benefit to the whole subnet and a slower, narrower fix that two of the subnet's most physically
vulnerable cities specifically need.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Prioritizing Princess Elisabeth's reserve
delivers an immediate, subnet-wide energy dividend that helps every city, including the ones struggling
most — but leaves Abowasa's own specialized rebuild waiting indefinitely, and with it, the structural-
stability expertise Halley and Neumayer are already established as needing, leaving two of the subnet's most
physically vulnerable cities without the specific technical support their own unstable ground requires, for
however long the delay runs. Prioritizing Abowasa's own slower rebuild first protects that critical capacity
for the cities that most need it — but leaves the entire subnet without the large, immediate energy dividend
Princess Elisabeth's reserve would deliver, a real, felt cost to everyone, not just one city.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which allocation the fund actually adopts: Princess Elisabeth first, delivering a fast, subnet-wide
benefit at the cost of leaving Halley's and Neumayer's own structural vulnerability unaddressed longer; or
Abowasa first, protecting that specific vulnerable capacity at the cost of delaying a large, immediate benefit
the whole subnet would otherwise share.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "survey the damage" into
"the four damage types can't be ranked on one scale"; the second BUT turns "propose an order" into "the
fastest, biggest win for everyone directly trades against the slower fix two vulnerable cities specifically
depend on"; the third BUT turns "pick a priority" into "there is no order that doesn't cost either a
subnet-wide benefit or a vulnerable pair of cities' own specific safety margin." Deleting any one Complication
changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's built by weaving two of this subnet's own established
Cross-City Patterns together — the four-category damage taxonomy (Pattern 1) and the ground-stability triangle
(Pattern 3) — rather than drawing on either alone, so that its climax decision (which city gets funded first)
carries consequences for a relationship (Abowasa's stability expertise, Halley's and Neumayer's dependence on
it) established well outside the immediate damage-survey question. It also gives Sanay a meaningful, if
quieter, role as the fund's own undamaged baseline — the city whose established competence is precisely why
nobody suggests spending the fund on it, a small but pointed echo of Pattern 2's "does the work, gets no
attention" civic character running even into a purely logistical questline.
