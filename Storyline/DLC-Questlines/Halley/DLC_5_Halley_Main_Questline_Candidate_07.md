# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #7: "A Name Nobody Quite Chose"

**What this is:** a seventh prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around Belgrano's own established
Boneyard Times faction material — a live internal identity fault line no prior candidate has touched, since
Candidates #1 and #2 both used Belgrano only in an ensemble/logistics or historical-credit capacity. **Not
canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 7th of the "at least 10" the method calls for. Uses the
Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 1 of Halley subnet's 8 cities (Belgrano alone)** — Belgrano's first solo candidate, joining Princess
Elisabeth (#3) and Troll (#6) in that pattern.

**Confirmed independent of Salagéa Aparast** — she's a Belgrano native and this chain sits in her home city,
but per the developer's own standing note, DLC 5 must be completable without recruiting her, and this
chain's own resolution doesn't route through her personal questline or require her presence.

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **`City_Origin_Factions_PostWar_Refugee.md`, "The Boneyard Times — Belgrano Communities"** — Belgrano
  survived the war intact, then declined afterward; its remaining population inherited the founding
  discipline without the institution behind it. The community holds two live fault lines: those who
  remember peak Belgrano (grief for something lost) versus those born into the Boneyard Times (no such
  grief, since they never had the thing to lose); and "staying is loyalty" versus "staying is just what
  happened to you." Whether "Boneyarders" was ever fully embraced by the group it named, or stayed something
  applied to them from outside, is explicitly left open.
- **The same faction file's central, unresolved question** — whether a culture can be said to persist at
  all once the population sustaining it has collapsed by an order of magnitude.
- **`City_Origin_Factions_PostWar_Refugee.md`, "The Question of the Unbroken Name"** — Belgrano is named as
  "perhaps furthest along" of the four ruined/destroyed communities in negotiating when a person stops being
  "from" their origin city, precisely because its archival mission (Salagéa's own established civic
  tradition) gives the community forward-oriented purpose even while it stays backward-facing.
- **Belgrano's own Full Extrapolation, Section II** — Jefa de Mantenimiento Yolanda Petrescu, proposed as
  the figure who formalized the maintenance rotations keeping Belgrano's few genuinely load-bearing skills
  organized once institutional backing collapsed; whether she's remembered as founder or simply as whoever
  happened to be capable when it mattered is deliberately left open, a direct extension of the "chosen or
  just happened" question.
- **Belgrano's own Local Cultures, §6/§21** — function-before-comfort, institutional command-culture surviving
  without the institution itself, discipline as habit and inheritance rather than active organizational
  loyalty.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim that this chain resolves "The Question of the Unbroken Name" for
  Belgrano's community as a whole, or for the other three ruined/destroyed communities it's compared against
  (Zukelli, Palmer City, Casey) — this stays a Belgrano-specific, present-day, practical decision, not a
  philosophical settlement of the larger question.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — The maintenance rotations that keep Belgrano's Boneyard Times functioning — informally
organized, per Yolanda Petrescu's own legacy, since the institution that once backed them collapsed — have
reached a real inflection point: the people currently running them are aging, and there's no guarantee the
system survives the next generation on informal habit alone. A present-day organizer asks the player to help
build support for formalizing the rotations into an actual, chartered institution — membership, rules, a
name. It looks like a straightforward organizing task: gather buy-in, draft a structure, done.

**BUT** — Formalizing the rotations means formalizing an answer to a question Belgrano's own community has
deliberately left open for generations: whether "Boneyarders," and the "staying is loyalty" identity behind
it, is something to enshrine as a proud, chosen civic identity, or whether doing so forces a label onto
people — especially those born into the Boneyard Times with no memory of anything else — who never actually
chose it and may resent having it made official. The charter can't be written without taking a side on
exactly the fault line the faction file itself says has never been resolved.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually go negotiate this, generation by generation: the elders who
remember peak Belgrano and carry real grief for what was lost, and the younger residents who've never known
Belgrano as anything but what it currently is and carry no such grief, because to them there was never
anything to lose.

**BUT** — Neither side turns out to be simply wrong, and neither position survives contact with the other
cleanly. Some younger residents resent the older generation's habit of framing their own not-leaving as
loyalty, when for them it was never a choice at all — just the shape their whole life happened to take.
Some elders, in turn, worry that stripping the charter of any identity language at all — making it purely
functional, logistics with no meaning attached — risks the rotations becoming just another chore nobody
feels obligated to keep showing up for, once the discipline that's kept them running is treated as nothing
more than habit with nothing left to serve.

**THEREFORE** — What looked like drafting an organizational charter becomes the community's first real,
direct conversation about a question it has spent generations quietly declining to have.

**BUT** — The reversal: there's no version of the charter that resolves this instead of just choosing which
cost to accept. A charter that names and celebrates the Boneyarder identity secures the rotations' survival
by giving people a reason bigger than logistics to keep them running — but it also formally, permanently
names an identity some residents never agreed to carry, closer to being "erased twice" (the faction file's
own feared outcome for destroyed/ruined communities generally) if their own lived reality gets folded into
someone else's chosen meaning. A charter that stays deliberately neutral — organization without identity —
respects everyone's right not to be labeled, but risks leaving the whole system exactly as fragile as it
already is, propped up by nothing but the same aging habit that's already running out of people to carry it
forward.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
hand in which version of the charter Belgrano actually adopts — a named, identity-affirming institution
(functional continuity more likely secured, at the cost of formally canonizing an identity that was always,
deliberately, left unsettled); or a neutral, practical-only formalization (nobody's experience gets
overwritten by someone else's frame, at the real risk that logistics alone isn't enough to keep the rotations
alive once the specific people currently running them on habit are gone). Either way, Belgrano's own
long-standing, faction-file-confirmed ambivalence about "Boneyarders" gets resolved in the specific, practical
form of one document — not because the underlying question was ever actually answered, but because an
institution had to be built regardless of whether the answer was ready.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "draft a charter" into
"the charter can't avoid taking a side on an identity question the community has never resolved"; the second
BUT turns "negotiate between generations" into "both positions are genuinely earned, and neither one is
simply correct"; the third BUT turns "pick a version of the charter" into "every version secures something
by giving something else up, and there's no draft that avoids the trade." Deleting any one Complication
changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it takes a question the project's own faction material explicitly
labels unresolved — "whether 'Boneyarders' was ever fully embraced... is open" — and forces it into a
concrete, practical decision with a real functional stake (whether the maintenance rotations that keep
Belgrano's Boneyard Times running actually survive the current generation), rather than leaving it as
background lore color. It's also the first Halley subnet candidate whose climax is explicitly a piece of
in-world institution-building — the player doesn't just witness or arbitrate Belgrano's identity question,
they help write the actual document that answers it in practice, whether or not anyone involved is truly at
peace with the answer.
