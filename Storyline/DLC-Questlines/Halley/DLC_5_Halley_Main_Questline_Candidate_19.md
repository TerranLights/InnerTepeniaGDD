# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #19: "Whichever Vote Counts More"

**What this is:** a nineteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around a ninth distinct linking
mechanism for this run — forming a brand-new joint manufacturing coalition from scratch, rather than
Candidate #11's credit-attribution campaign, Candidate #12's reconstruction-fund triage (allocating an
existing fund), Candidate #13's faction-mediation (resolving an existing conflict), Candidate #14's
commemorative-narrative reconciliation, Candidate #15's trade-diplomacy negotiation, Candidate #16's
mechanical-fraud investigation, Candidate #17's physical-route crisis, or Candidate #18's functional
retrospective. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic
(`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Belgrano, Neumayer, Troll, Sanay, Lazar).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Belgrano Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the established spare-parts fabrication and light vehicle
  manufacturing economy built on Belgrano's own garage/mechanic subculture.
- **Halley Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — Neumayer's own established position as the subnet's
  deepest engineering-design authority.
- **Halley Cross-City Patterns, Pattern 5** — Troll's own established character (quietly higher-quality goods
  rather than ostentation), used here as the reputational asset that would make a joint venture's output
  genuinely desirable in trade.
- **Sanay Full Extrapolation, Sections I and II** — Sanay's established role as the subnet's actual
  supply-chain entry point and shipyard/logistics center, positioned here as the coalition's own raw-material
  import channel.
- **Lazar's own established scale** (Tepenia's largest city, per `project_lazar_population_rebalancing`),
  used here as the venture's proposed labor pool and largest internal market.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about what the coalition would actually manufacture beyond
  general post-war goods and components — this chain treats the venture's own governance structure, not its
  product line, as the actual stake.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With post-war scarcity straining every city's own capacity, an initiative emerges to formally
establish a joint Halley subnet manufacturing coalition — pooling Belgrano's parts fabrication, Neumayer's
engineering design, Troll's quality-goods reputation, Sanay's import logistics, and Lazar's own labor pool
into one coordinated venture. The player is asked to help broker the coalition's formation — a
straightforward, if ambitious, coalition-building task.

**BUT** — Forming genuine joint governance means agreeing how authority, decision-making, and profit actually
get shared among five cities of very different size and contribution. Lazar's sheer population gives it a
real claim to proportional influence; Neumayer's and Belgrano's specialized technical expertise is what
actually makes the venture function at all; Troll's own established reputation is what would make its output
desirable in trade in the first place. None of these claims reduces cleanly to the others.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually negotiate the coalition's own governance structure — voting
weight, decision rights, profit shares — among these genuinely different, non-comparable claims.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once real negotiation narrows to a specific choice: weighting governance by
population would hand strategic control of a highly technical operation to the city with the least
specialized stake in how it's actually run. Weighting governance by technical contribution would concentrate
real economic authority in a few smaller, specialized cities over a venture whose labor and market weight
come disproportionately from a much larger population that isn't among them.

**THEREFORE** — What began as coalition logistics becomes a real question about what actually justifies a
governing stake in a shared venture — how many people it serves, or how much of the actual work makes it run.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Population-weighted governance gives Lazar's
much larger population a fair, proportional voice in a venture that will employ and serve it most — but hands
strategic direction of a technically demanding operation to the city with the least specialized expertise in
running it, risking real operational missteps. Contribution-weighted governance protects the venture's
technical integrity by keeping strategic authority with the cities whose expertise and reputation actually
make it work — but concentrates real economic power over a shared venture in the hands of a few smaller
cities, at the expense of the much larger population whose labor and market the venture actually depends on.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which governance model the coalition actually adopts: population-weighted, giving Lazar a fair voice
at the cost of real operational risk; or contribution-weighted, protecting technical integrity at the cost of
concentrating power away from the population the venture most depends on and serves.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "form a coalition" into
"there's no single fair formula for how much say each city should get"; the second BUT turns "negotiate
governance" into "the real choice is between two specific, incompatible weighting principles"; the third BUT
turns "pick a weighting" into "there is no weighting that doesn't cost either operational competence or fair
representation for the population the venture depends on most." Deleting any one Complication changes what
the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Halley subnet candidate built around forming an entirely
new joint institution from nothing, rather than allocating an existing fund (#12), mediating an existing
conflict (#13), or negotiating terms for an existing relationship (#15) — the governance structure itself is
the thing being created, with no precedent to fall back on. It also gives Lazar its first appearance in this
run as a direct stakeholder in its own right rather than a downstream beneficiary of decisions made elsewhere,
using the same scale that made it useful as a triage and route consideration in Candidates #12 and #17 as an
actual seat at the table here instead.
