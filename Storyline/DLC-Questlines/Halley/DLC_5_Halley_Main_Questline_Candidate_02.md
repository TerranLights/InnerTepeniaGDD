# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #2: "The One Who Only Wrote It Down"

**What this is:** a second prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, deliberately avoiding the Troll Airfield
anchor used in Candidate #1 to test a genuinely different engine — a civic-memory/credit dispute rather than
a resource-control conflict. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 2nd of the "at least
10" the method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md`
§5a).

**Uses 2 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Belgrano, Neumayer) — the smallest cast so far, deliberately: this
story is about exactly two cities' asymmetric relationship to the same shared achievement, and a third city
would dilute rather than deepen it.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Halley Subnet Full Extrapolation, Section II** — the Rastra's own founding chain spans both cities:
  Belgrano's need and expedition, Neumayer's redesign. The proposed resolution treats both claims as
  genuinely earned and non-competing, but asymmetric — Belgrano remembers the Rastra as its own proudest
  achievement and talks about it; Neumayer, per its own established culture, would never publicly claim it
  at all, but keeps a complete private engineering record of the redesign.
- **Halley Subnet Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 2** — the precise mechanism: Neumayer's founding-era
  engineers reverse-engineered Belgrano's crude first hauler into the template every Rastra in the country
  descends from, a debt that "carries Neumayer's name forward nowhere in the country."
- **Belgrano's own Full Extrapolation, Section VI** — Expedition Day, an existing civic holiday
  commemorating the original Arrastradora's departure on the Byrd-founding crossing, explicitly named
  "Belgrano's proudest historical achievement and the clearest moment where its function-first culture
  produced something the rest of Tepenia still remembers."
- **Belgrano's own Local Cultures, §6/§21** — function-before-comfort, institutional command-structure
  civic identity; the Air Force ethic that never really stopped operating.
- **Neumayer's own Local Cultures, §6/§21** — rigor as civic virtue, precision over performance, quiet
  political energy channeled into defending the research/engineering mission rather than into Federation
  -wide projection; a city that "would rather be left to its instruments than court political influence."
- **Explicitly excluded:** the Amundsen Tower and Cradle chamber legacies (Throughway 2 also credits
  Neumayer's lineage with both) — this chain stays narrowly on the Rastra specifically, since pulling in the
  Tower's own loss (already Neumayer's most psychologically loaded relationship to Upper Earth, per §22)
  would overload a chain that works better small.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A national vehicle-manufacturing guild (or a Concordia historical institution — left flexible)
wants a definitive, documented origin story for the Rastra, the country's single most common vehicle, and
commissions the player to compile it. Belgrano is the obvious, willing starting point: Expedition Day
already exists, already tells this story, already gives the player exactly what looks like a complete answer.

**BUT** — The player discovers, cross-referencing engineering details against what Belgrano's own account
actually claims, that the vehicle described in Expedition Day's story and the vehicle that actually made the
Byrd crossing aren't quite the same machine. Something was substantially redesigned between Belgrano's
original hauler and the Arrastradora that succeeded — and Belgrano's own records don't explain what changed
or why it worked.

**THEREFORE** — The trail leads to Neumayer, where Throughway 2 already establishes the true redesign
happened. The player expects a quick correction: find the record, add the missing chapter, done.

**BUT** — Neumayer's own culture makes this harder than expected, in two separate ways. First, practically:
rigor is a civic virtue here, and an outsider asking to see engineering credit records gets met with the same
precision-and-verification standard Neumayer applies to everything, not casual cooperation. Second, and more
fundamentally: once the player does get access and confirms the full story, Neumayer's own political
character — quiet, uninterested in Federation-wide projection, content to be "left to its instruments" —
means the city has no interest in publicly claiming credit at all. The missing piece of history isn't
missing because it was lost. It was never going to be advertised, by design.

**THEREFORE** — The player now has the complete, true record of the Rastra's origin, and a real decision
about what to do with it — not a fact-finding problem anymore, but a question of whose account of a shared
achievement gets to stand.

**BUT** — The reversal: publishing the full story doesn't just correct history, it costs something specific
on each side. Amending Expedition Day's story publicly undercuts the one moment Belgrano's own civic
identity points to as proof its function-first culture produced something the whole country still remembers
— not a minor footnote to that city, but close to the center of it. Meanwhile, giving Neumayer the credit it
never asked for is not obviously a gift; it runs directly against the same quiet, instrument-focused
character that makes Neumayer itself as established as it is. Correcting the record and respecting both
cities' own established characters turn out to be in real tension with each other.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is a genuine choice,
not a discovery to report: leave Belgrano's public account standing as the guild's official record (true to
both cities' own established preferences, but leaving Neumayer's actual role buried — one more instance of
the subnet's own "uncredited load" identity, deliberately repeated rather than fixed); push for full public
credit to Neumayer (historically accurate and overdue, but unwanted by the city itself and destabilizing to
Belgrano's own civic pride); or thread the resolution the way the Subnet Full Extrapolation's own Section II
proposes — record the true, complete history somewhere real and checkable (a shared engineering
registry, a joint archive entry) without a national announcement that forces either city to perform a
version of pride or humility it doesn't actually want. None of the three is presented as correct; each costs
one of the two cities something the other doesn't have to pay.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "document an already
-known story" into "the known story doesn't match the actual engineering"; the second BUT turns "get the
missing record from Neumayer" into "Neumayer has the record but doesn't want it public, for reasons rooted
in its own established character, not obstruction"; the third BUT turns "publish the corrected history" into
"correcting it costs each city something different, and neither cost is fake." Deleting any one Complication
changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that its climax isn't a faction fight or a resource crisis at all — it's a
question of historical credit, made genuinely hard not by villainy or sabotage but by the fact that
Belgrano's and Neumayer's own already-established civic characters actually want different, incompatible
things done with the same true fact. That's the subnet's own "uncredited load" identity (Subnet Full
Extrapolation, Section I) examined at its most literal: a debt that's real, documentable, and confirmed, and
still nobody's obvious call to fix, because fixing it isn't free for anyone involved. Distinct from Candidate
#1 in engine, cast, and scale — no faction violence, no logistics crisis, just two cities' own established
values colliding over one shared machine.
