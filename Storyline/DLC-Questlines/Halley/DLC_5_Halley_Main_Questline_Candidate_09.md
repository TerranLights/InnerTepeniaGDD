# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #9: "The Route That Doesn't Need You Anymore"

**What this is:** a ninth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around a permanent infrastructure
-consolidation decision rather than a one-time disaster-triage event (Candidates #3 and #4) or a faction
fight (Candidate #1). **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate** — the 9th of the "at least 10" the
method calls for. Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md`
§5a).

**Uses 3 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Halley, Sanay, Belgrano) — the same three cities Candidate #1 uses
alongside Troll, but with Troll's own contested control deliberately absent from this chain's own stakes;
the conflict here is about the subnet's logistics architecture going forward, not who controls any single
asset.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Halley's own Full Extrapolation, Section VI** — the Weddell Sea itself remains seasonally navigable;
  what's actually degraded is the receiving infrastructure at Belgrano and Sanay, both damaged by the war.
  The sea was never Halley's real vulnerability; the ports it depends on are.
- **Sanay's own Full Extrapolation, Section II** — incoming South African freighter shipments arrive at
  both Sanay and Belgrano; from Sanay, goods split toward nearby cities via Hwy 7 or onward to Troll.
- **Sanay's own Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the Trade Yard: a defined, bounded, institutionally
  -backed zone for Upper Earth-facing trade, giving Sanay's own receiving infrastructure a procedural
  maturity Belgrano's more ad hoc post-war operation doesn't share.
- **`City_Origin_Factions_PostWar_Refugee.md`, "The Boneyard Times"** — Belgrano's post-war decline, its
  central unresolved question of whether a culture can persist once its population has collapsed by an
  order of magnitude, and its own mechanic/spare-parts economy (established in Belgrano's own Full
  Extrapolation, Section V) as one of the few things still giving the community real, functional standing.
- **`City_Origin_Factions_PostWar_Refugee.md`, "The Question of the Unbroken Name"** — the specific,
  named fear shared across Tepenia's ruined/destroyed communities of being "erased twice": once by the war,
  once by the gradual drift or exclusion that follows it.
- **Halley's own Full Extrapolation, Section I** — Halley's established position in the subnet's own
  logistics questions generally: it determines nothing about how supply infrastructure gets resolved, but
  depends completely on the outcome.
- **Explicitly excluded:** Troll's own airfield conflict (Candidate #1's anchor) — this chain's own
  consolidation question is upstream of Troll entirely, concerning which port Halley's overland resupply
  routes through in the first place, not what happens to goods once they reach the airfield.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — Halley's relocation planners, tired of gambling on two separately damaged, unpredictable ports
for their own resupply, propose a rational modernization: consolidate Halley's overland resupply routing
permanently through Sanay alone, whose Trade Yard and bedrock-founded infrastructure make it the more
reliable, better-institutionalized receiving point, rather than continuing to split dependency between Sanay
and Belgrano the way things stood before the war. The player is asked to help negotiate and implement the
change — a straightforward infrastructure upgrade, on paper.

**BUT** — Belgrano's own continued relevance as a real node in the subnet's supply chain is one of the few
things still propping up its fragile post-war survival. Its own mechanic and spare-parts economy — one of
the Boneyard Times' few genuinely load-bearing skills — depends on the city still being needed, still
visited, still worth the trip. Formally routing around it doesn't just streamline logistics; it risks being
the specific, final push that turns "declining but still functioning" into the exact "erased twice" outcome
Belgrano's own community has feared since the war ended.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to weigh going through with the full consolidation as planned against finding
some way to keep Belgrano meaningfully included in the subnet's actual supply routing, not just
symbolically remembered.

**BUT** — A middle path doesn't resolve the tension, it just relocates it. Keeping partial routing through
Belgrano means Belgrano's own degraded port infrastructure still needs real investment to handle even a
reduced share reliably — and every unit of scarce reconstruction material and skilled labor spent shoring up
Belgrano's continued relevance is a unit not spent making Sanay's own resupply chain, the one Halley and the
rest of the subnet actually depend on most, more resilient. The redundancy that would let both cities matter
is exactly what the region can't currently afford; keeping Belgrano relevant and making the wider system more
reliable pull against each other with the same finite resources.

**THEREFORE** — The decision the player is actually facing isn't "modernize or don't" — it's which specific
cost the subnet's own recovery is willing to absorb, and who absorbs it.

**BUT** — The reversal is that both outcomes are real losses, not one clean win against one avoidable one.
Full consolidation through Sanay gives Halley and the wider subnet a measurably more reliable resupply chain
going forward — and quietly accepts Belgrano's own continued decline as an acceptable cost of that
reliability, contributing directly to the exact fear its own community has held since the war. Preserving
Belgrano's routing role protects a specific, fragile, already-endangered community's own survival and
functional dignity — and leaves Halley's own resupply, and everyone downstream of it, meaningfully less
reliable than it could have been, indefinitely.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
hand in which cost the subnet actually accepts: consolidate fully through Sanay, buying real resilience for
Halley and the wider subnet at the cost of contributing directly to Belgrano's own continued, possibly
irreversible decline; or preserve Belgrano's place in the routing, honoring its community's own survival and
standing at the direct, ongoing cost of a less reliable resupply chain for Halley and everyone who depends on
it. Neither choice is presented as obviously correct, and unlike Candidates #3 and #4's disaster-triage
climaxes, this decision isn't forced by a single emergency — it's a deliberate, permanent restructuring
choice the subnet makes about its own future shape, with both outcomes fully foreseeable in advance.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "modernize the supply
route" into "modernizing it risks finishing off an already-fragile community"; the second BUT turns "keep
Belgrano included" into "inclusion has its own real cost, paid by the system Halley actually depends on
most"; the third BUT turns "pick the better option" into "both options are genuine, foreseeable losses, not
a right answer disguised as a hard one." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually
choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that its climax isn't a crisis forcing an unavoidable loss (Candidates #3 and
#4) or a fight over who controls a contested asset (Candidate #1) — it's a calm, foreseeable, entirely
avoidable-in-theory choice about the subnet's own future shape, made harder specifically because both
options are genuinely defensible and the cost of each is fully visible in advance rather than discovered
partway through. It's also the first candidate to put Belgrano's own established "erased twice" fear
(`City_Origin_Factions_PostWar_Refugee.md`) into direct, mechanical tension with another city's legitimate
need for a more reliable system, rather than treating that fear as background lore color the way Candidate
#7 treated the Boneyarders identity question as internal to Belgrano alone.
