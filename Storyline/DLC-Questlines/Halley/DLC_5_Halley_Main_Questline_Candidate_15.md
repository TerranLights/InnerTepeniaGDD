# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #15: "How Wide the Gate Opens"

**What this is:** a fifteenth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by running
`../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around a fifth distinct linking
mechanism for this run — negotiating the terms of an Upper Earth trade-protocol expansion, rather than
Candidate #11's credit-attribution campaign, Candidate #12's reconstruction-fund triage, Candidate #13's
faction-mediation, or Candidate #14's commemorative-narrative reconciliation. **Not canon, not chosen, not the
only candidate.** Uses the Linear Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Sanay, Belgrano, Troll, Halley, Lazar). Four of these five —
Sanay, Belgrano, Troll, Halley — also appear in Candidate #13's supply-chain mediation, because they're the
same established, physically real supply network (Throughway 1); this chain deliberately swaps in Lazar in
Neumayer's place, and stakes the questline on a diplomatic decision about Upper Earth contact terms rather
than an internal factional dispute over the same infrastructure.

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Sanay Full Extrapolation, Section V** — the established "Trade Yard" system: a specific, physically
  demarcated, bounded zone where South African freighter crews dock and conduct business under defined
  protocols, deliberately modeled on Guangzhou's own historical Canton System — welcoming the trade, profiting
  from it, but keeping contact clearly bounded rather than granting free rein, consistent with Tepenia's own
  established project-wide reasons to stay guarded around Upper Earth even while trading with it.
- **Sanay Full Extrapolation, Section II** — the established fact that South African freighter shipments
  arrive at both Sanay and Belgrano, and that from Sanay, goods split two ways: directly to nearby cities via
  Hwy 7, or onward to Troll's airfield for more remote destinations.
- **Halley Cross-City Throughways, Throughway 1** — Halley's own total dependency on overland resupply
  through Belgrano and Sanay, and Troll's own fractured airfield control, used here to establish that any
  change in trade volume through the ports has direct, material downstream consequences for Halley
  specifically.
- **Lazar's own established scale** (Tepenia's largest city, per `project_lazar_population_rebalancing`),
  used here as the most consequential of the "nearby cities" reached directly by Hwy 7 distribution, giving it
  a real, direct stake in any change to that route's volume.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any claim about why Tepenia remains guarded around Upper Earth contact beyond what
  Section V itself already establishes — this chain treats that guardedness as a given, existing policy
  position, not something needing its own justification.

## The Chain

**SETUP** — A South African delegation proposes expanding trade beyond the Trade Yard's current bounded
terms — more volume, broader access — a change that would meaningfully increase the flow of goods reaching
Sanay and Belgrano. The player is asked to help facilitate the negotiation — a straightforward, if
significant, diplomatic-facilitation task.

**BUT** — Expanding the terms isn't a decision Sanay and Belgrano can make alone. The increased freight would
flow through the same established distribution network — split via Hwy 7 to nearby cities like Lazar, or
onward to Troll's airfield for the rest of the subnet — meaning Troll's own fractured factions, Halley's
total dependency on this exact supply chain, and Lazar's own capacity to absorb a larger direct share are all
directly implicated by trade terms nominally being negotiated between two port cities and an Upper Earth
delegation.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to actually consult across the whole affected network — Troll, Halley, and
Lazar — rather than treat the negotiation as Sanay's and Belgrano's business alone.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once the deeper stakes become clear: expanding access delivers real, tangible
material benefit — more goods reaching every city downstream — but does so by loosening the deliberately
bounded, guarded protocol Section V establishes as existing for real, still-valid reasons. Wider access means
a wider opening for exactly the kind of outside influence or exploitation the bounded system was built to
prevent in the first place.

**THEREFORE** — What began as a trade-volume negotiation becomes a real choice between the subnet's own
established guardedness and a tangible material benefit that would measurably help every city downstream.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Expanding the trade terms delivers real,
felt benefit to Troll's airfield capacity, Halley's overland resupply, and Lazar's own direct share — but
loosens a deliberately guarded system for reasons that remain, per this project's own established Upper
Earth trade dynamics, still genuinely valid, not merely historical caution. Keeping the current bounded
protocol preserves that guardedness and the real risk-avoidance it was built for — but leaves real, tangible
material benefit on the table that could have measurably helped multiple struggling downstream cities right
now.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in which terms the negotiation actually settles on: expanded access, delivering real material benefit
downstream at the cost of loosening a guarded system for reasons that still genuinely apply; or the current
bounded protocol held firm, preserving that guardedness at the cost of real, felt benefit multiple cities
would otherwise have received.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "negotiate trade terms"
into "the terms affect three more cities than the two actually at the table"; the second BUT turns "consult
the network" into "the benefit itself is inseparable from loosening a protocol that exists for real, still
-valid reasons"; the third BUT turns "expand or hold" into "there is no version of the choice that doesn't
cost either real downstream benefit or a guardedness the subnet still genuinely needs." Deleting any one
Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none reads as a disguised And Then.

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that it's the first Halley subnet candidate to stake its climax on the
subnet's own external relationship with Upper Earth, rather than an entirely internal question, giving DLC 5
a mechanism this whole run hasn't used yet — foreign-trade diplomacy rather than internal
attribution, allocation, mediation, or narrative. It also deliberately reuses most of Candidate #13's own
supply-chain cast while swapping the actual decision at stake — proving the same physical network can support
genuinely different kinds of climaxes (an internal factional dispute in #13, an external diplomatic
trade-off here) without either chain feeling like a retread of the other.
