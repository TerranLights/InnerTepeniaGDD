# DLC 5 (Halley Subnet) — Main Questline Candidate #24: "However Big the Room Gets"

**What this is:** a twenty-fourth prospective But/Therefore chain for DLC 5's main questline, produced by
running `../DLC_Main_Questline_Design_Method.md` against Halley subnet, built around scaling an intimate
negotiation method up to megacity size. **Not canon, not chosen, not the only candidate.** Uses the Linear
Escalation Chain schematic (`../But_Therefore_Quest_Design_Method.md` §5a).

**Uses 5 of Halley subnet's 8 cities** (Abowasa, Lazar, Neumayer, Halley, Sanay).

---

## Inputs actually used (Step 1 of the design method)

- **Abowasa Mega-Init, One-Line Pitch** — the established civic character of Abowasa's own founding process,
  plurality expressed as household-level negotiation at Tepenia's smallest, most intimate civic scale, used
  here strictly as a general negotiation methodology, not as a claim about either founding nation's own
  character.
- **Lazar Full Extrapolation, Section VII** — the established "two adjacent moods" dual-personality divide
  between the old Novolazarevskaya core (institutional, reserved) and the former-Maitri commercial expansion
  (dense, faster-paced), explicitly framed as low-tension coexistence rather than active conflict.
- **Lazar Full Extrapolation, Section V** — Lazar's own established complementary relationship with
  Neumayer, used here to place Neumayer as a natural outside consultant on structural questions.
- **Lazar Full Extrapolation, Section III** — Lazar's own established status as the Halley subnet's actual
  commercial and logistics capital, used here to justify Halley's and Sanay's own stake in how its
  reconstruction proceeds.
- **Explicitly excluded:** any specific claim about which district's reconstruction priorities are actually
  more deserving — this chain treats the dispute itself as genuinely open, resolvable different ways.

---

## The Chain

**SETUP** — With Lazar's own old core and commercial expansion increasingly at odds over reconstruction
priorities, someone proposes adapting Abowasa's own established household-level negotiation model — normally
suited to an intimate, small-scale city — to mediate the dispute. The player is asked to help facilitate this
— a straightforward, if unusual, mediation-methodology task.

**BUT** — Abowasa's own method depends specifically on its city's small, intimate scale, where direct,
face-to-face negotiation is genuinely possible for most residents. Applying the same method to Lazar, a
genuine megacity of nearly 1.8 million, means the method's own core mechanism simply can't function the same
way — any adaptation requires real compromises, representative delegates instead of direct participation,
formal structures instead of informal conversation.

**THEREFORE** — The player has to consult Abowasa's own community about how their method actually works, and
travel to Lazar's own district representatives and Neumayer for outside structural input, before any adapted
version can be designed.

**BUT** — The reversal lands once a genuinely workable, scaled-up structure takes shape: it would require
Lazar's two districts to formally institutionalize their own divide, creating separate representative bodies
for each — a real risk of hardening what's currently an easy, low-tension coexistence into two official,
opposed camps with structured leverage against each other.

**THEREFORE** — What began as adapting a mediation method becomes a real question about whether resolving one
dispute fairly is worth the risk of manufacturing a permanent, formal rift that doesn't currently exist.

**BUT** — The reversal cuts against a clean answer either way. Adopting the scaled-up mediation structure
gives Lazar's reconstruction dispute a real, fair process, genuinely informed by Abowasa's own successful
principles — but risks turning Lazar's own easy dual character into something more formally divided and
adversarial. Handling the dispute through Lazar's own existing, informal channels avoids hardening that
divide and preserves the current coexistence intact — but leaves a real, pressing dispute to resolve without
any structured fairness, risking a poor outcome for one district or the other.

**THEREFORE** — Consistent with this project's own No Good Endings law, the resolution is the player's own
role in whether Lazar actually adopts the formal, adapted structure or resolves the dispute informally: adopt
it, securing fairness at the cost of risking a lasting, formal rift; or stay informal, protecting Lazar's own
easy character at the cost of a real dispute going unresolved by any fair process.

## Self-check (per the method's §7)

Reading only the conjunctions: SETUP → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE → BUT → THEREFORE. No repeated
conjunction in a row. Each BUT changes the shape of the problem: the first BUT turns "adapt the method" into
"the method's own core mechanism can't survive the scale-up unchanged"; the second BUT turns "design a
workable version" into "the workable version itself risks manufacturing a permanent divide"; the third BUT
turns "adopt it or don't" into "there is no option that doesn't cost either fairness or Lazar's own easy
coexistence." Deleting any one Complication changes what the player is actually choosing at the end, so none
reads as a disguised And Then.

---

## Worth Your Attention

This candidate's core asset is that its central tension comes from a genuine structural mismatch of scale,
not from any party acting in bad faith — Abowasa's method is sincerely offered and sincerely wanted, and the
problem is simply that what works at household size doesn't automatically survive being stretched across
nearly two million people. It's also the first candidate to treat Lazar's own established dual-personality
divide as something a well-meaning intervention could make worse, rather than as a fixed, stable
characteristic of the city.
