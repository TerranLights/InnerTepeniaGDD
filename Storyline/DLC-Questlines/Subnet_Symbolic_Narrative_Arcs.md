# Subnet Symbolic Narrative Arcs (Broad-Stroke Only)

**What this is:** a purely thematic, symbolic-layer companion to `Subnet_Symbolic_Associations.md`, written
because the actual DLC main questline for each subnet hasn't been chosen yet — that choice still needs a
proper read-through of the many candidate files already sitting in each subnet's own folder, likely a
week-or-two-long process on its own. In the meantime, this file takes each subnet's own Elemental and
Perfection-Itself association and asks what kind of story that *combination* naturally wants to tell — not a
plot, not specific beats tied to any actual candidate, just the broadest possible shape a story could take if
it genuinely honored both symbols at once, positive attributes and shadow sides alike. Treat every arc below
as a mood board, not an outline — the real main questline, whichever candidate eventually gets chosen, should
be judged on its own merits, not bent to fit this layer.

**Amundsen is excluded.** DLC 1's own story is already established (Kendra Heinrich, the Tower's collapse) —
this file only speaks to subnets whose main questline is still genuinely undecided.

---

## Palmer Subnet — Water + Generosity (Dana)

**Why these two resonate together:** Water's own virtue is wisdom; Generosity's own foundation is giving
without expecting anything back. Combined, they describe continuity that flows outward and keeps flowing
even after catastrophic loss — which is exactly what Palmer subnet's own established material already shows
(robots carrying identity forward, Palmer City's own gift of records letting Byrd exist at all). But their
shadows compound in a genuinely risky way: Water's fear plus Generosity's self-destructive excess describes a
subnet that could keep giving of itself past the point where it has anything left to give.

**Broad-stroke shape:**
1. **Opening** — confront how much has already been lost. Not a mystery to solve, just an honest reckoning
   with real, accumulated absence.
2. **Rising** — surface the ways the subnet has actually continued anyway, quietly, through giving rather
   than hoarding — and notice that nobody's ever properly added up how much has already been given away, or
   to whom.
3. **Turn** — something asks for more. A real, legitimate need shows up that can only be met by giving away
   something the subnet can't obviously spare.
4. **Climax** — the choice isn't whether to give — Water and Generosity both already answered that — it's
   whether to give *wisely* (Water's actual virtue) or simply give until there's nothing left (Generosity's
   own shadow, unchecked).
5. **Resolution** — whatever the eventual candidate's own specific ending is, the symbolic shape wants it to
   land on sustainable continuity: giving that flows on, rather than giving that drains dry.

## Halley Subnet — Earth + Discipline (Sila)

**Why these two resonate together:** Earth's virtue is trustworthiness; Discipline is exactly the sustained,
consistent conduct that earns trust over time. Together they describe a subnet that has become genuinely,
deeply reliable — the load everyone else quietly stands on. But Earth's own shadow (stagnation, an inability
to change) and Discipline's own shadow (rigidity, rule-following without understanding why) compound into the
same risk from two directions at once: a subnet so good at being dependable that it can no longer bend when
bending is actually required.

**Broad-stroke shape:**
1. **Opening** — establish just how reliable, disciplined, and load-bearing this subnet actually is — not as
   flattery, but as the specific, unglamorous thing that makes it valuable.
2. **Rising** — a real strain appears in that reliability. Something the subnet has always handled the same
   disciplined way no longer responds to being handled that way.
3. **Turn** — the old, disciplined, stable approach genuinely stops working, and the ground itself (literally
   or figuratively) starts to shift under a system built to never move.
4. **Climax** — choose between holding the discipline rigidly (safe, familiar, but visibly failing) or
   bending it into something new (risking the very stability that made the subnet trustworthy in the first
   place).
5. **Resolution** — the symbolic shape wants discipline reinterpreted as a living principle rather than a
   fixed rule — the subnet staying just as trustworthy, but proving it can actually adapt without breaking.

## Mirny Subnet — Fire + Wisdom (Prajna)

**Why these two resonate together:** Fire is assertive self-definition; Wisdom is the deeper, harder-won
understanding that mere assertion can't substitute for. Mirny subnet's whole civic energy already runs on
naming itself — Sinheung's, Shirayuki's, and Mirny's own flagged renames. Wisdom asks whether choosing a name
is the same thing as actually knowing oneself. Fire's shadow (arrogance, mistaking one's own light for the
only light) and Wisdom's shadow (intellectual understanding mistaken for the real thing) both describe the
same trap: declaring an identity instead of earning genuine self-knowledge.

**Broad-stroke shape:**
1. **Opening** — establish the subnet's confident, self-defining energy — the drive to choose and declare who
   it is, on its own terms.
2. **Rising** — something surfaces that the subnet's own confident self-story doesn't actually account for —
   a fact about itself it has never had reason to examine closely.
3. **Turn** — the comfortable option is to assert an answer anyway (Fire's own instinct); the harder option is
   to sit with genuine uncertainty long enough to actually understand what's true.
4. **Climax** — a real test of whether the subnet can tell the difference between confidently naming
   something and actually understanding it.
5. **Resolution** — the symbolic shape wants something closer to earned wisdom than a new label — self
   -knowledge that holds up under real scrutiny, not just a fresh, confident assertion.

## Janbogo/Ross Subnet — Metal + Patience (Kshanti)

**Why these two resonate together:** Metal's emotion is grief; Patience is the forbearance that lets grief be
carried without collapsing into despair — an almost exact match for Janbogo's own established cumulative
witness burden. But Metal's shadow (grief that can't move on, harsh unforgiving judgment) and Patience's own
shadow (passivity mistaken for peace, tolerating what should be resisted) point at the same underlying risk:
that the subnet's own long, patient vigil has quietly stopped being healing and started being a way of never
actually resolving anything.

**Broad-stroke shape:**
1. **Opening** — establish the weight of what's being patiently carried, and how long it's already been
   carried, without treating it as simply solved by naming it.
2. **Rising** — some new pressure makes the question of resolution unavoidable — the patient waiting can't
   simply continue indefinitely anymore.
3. **Turn** — a real verdict becomes possible for the first time — Metal's own cutting, decisive quality
   finally within reach after so much patient endurance.
4. **Climax** — choose between finally cutting through with a decisive verdict (closure, at the risk of
   reopening wounds the patient vigil had learned to live around) or continuing to wait (safe, but risking that
   patience has become avoidance).
5. **Resolution** — the symbolic shape wants a form of closure that actually honors the years of patient
   carrying, rather than a verdict that makes all that patience feel wasted or unnecessary.

## Mawson Subnet — Wood + Diligence (Virya)

**Why these two resonate together:** Wood grows outward; Diligence is the sustained effort that makes
outward growth possible in the first place. Together they match Mawson subnet's own established character
almost exactly — Sayowa's "small vessel, outsized cargo," Mawson's cultivated hospitality, Dome Fuji's
ongoing devotion. But Wood's shadow (overreach, an inability to ever be content, growth pursued at one's own
expense) and Diligence's shadow (burnout, effort without direction) combine into real risk: a subnet that
keeps reaching outward and working hard for everyone else, without ever pausing to ask what it needs for
itself.

**Broad-stroke shape:**
1. **Opening** — establish how much this subnet quietly does for its neighbors, and how little of that
   effort is actually noticed or credited by the subnets it serves.
2. **Rising** — the cost of that sustained effort becomes visible — the strain of always reaching outward,
   the sense of being taken for granted by exactly the neighbors it works hardest for.
3. **Turn** — a moment forces a real choice between continuing at the same pace (diligence without limit) or
   finally rooting down and asserting its own needs (Wood, for once, growing inward rather than outward).
4. **Climax** — the tension between what the subnet owes its neighbors and what it owes itself, with no
   comfortable way to fully satisfy both.
5. **Resolution** — the symbolic shape wants sustainable balance — outward growth that includes caring for
   its own roots, not effort spent without any limit at all.

## Byrd — Magnetism + Meditative Concentration (Dhyana)

**Why these two resonate together:** Magnetism is attraction and connection across distance; Meditative
Concentration is deep, focused, inward stillness. Byrd was pulled into existence by an expedition — an
outside force reaching toward it — and then turned inward to become something focused and self-contained.
Magnetism's own shadow (obsessive fixation, being pulled off one's own course) and Concentration's own shadow
(escapism, withdrawal mistaken for progress) both point at the same tension: the very connections that made
Byrd possible are also the thing its own inward focus is most at risk of quietly forgetting.

**Broad-stroke shape:**
1. **Opening** — establish Byrd's own deep, focused, self-contained character — a place that goes inward and
   downward rather than outward.
2. **Rising** — something surfaces the invisible debts that actually built Byrd — connections to other
   subnets it has never fully acknowledged or reckoned with.
3. **Turn** — tension between protecting its own hard-won inward focus (safe, familiar, but increasingly
   isolated) and reopening itself to the outside connections that made it possible in the first place.
4. **Climax** — a real test of whether depth and connection can coexist, or whether one has to be given up for
   the other.
5. **Resolution** — the symbolic shape wants genuine integration — depth that doesn't require total
   isolation, connections honored without Byrd losing its own center.

## Worth Your Attention

Read across all six, a pattern emerges that wasn't planned going in: every single arc's own climax is a
version of the same underlying question — *is the thing this subnet is best known for actually still serving
it, or has it quietly become the thing holding it back?* Water's generosity, Earth's discipline, Fire's
self-naming, Metal's patience, Wood's diligence, and Magnetism's inward focus are all, in their own subnet's
own established terms, genuine virtues that got this far specifically because they worked. The shared shape
across all six broad-stroke arcs is simply asking each subnet to find out whether its own defining strength
still holds up once it's actually put under real pressure — which, not coincidentally, is close to what this
project's own No Good Endings law already asks of every individual questline candidate, just scaled up to
the level of an entire subnet's own character.
