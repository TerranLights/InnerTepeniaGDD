## Overview of this range

The range begins in the middle of the chapter "The Ambivalence of the Fish Symbol," where Jung reads Leviathan and Behemoth, and the Egyptian pair Horus and Set, as a doubled expression of an incomplete God-image, and follows the symbolism of the north and the pole. It then covers three whole chapters: "The Fish in Alchemy" (the alchemists' "round fish" as a jellyfish, the burning starfish, the remora, the dragon's stone, the Bogomil/Cathar two fishes, and a modern patient's fish dream), "The Alchemical Interpretation of the Fish" (the magnet that catches the fish, the aqua doctrinae, Gerhard Dorn on self-knowledge, the stone and the unified adept), and "Background to the Psychology of Christian Alchemical Symbolism" (alchemy as Mater Alchimia, dogma as symbol, the broken bridge between dogma and experience, the fish as bridge between the historical Christ and the self). It ends with nearly all of "Gnostic Symbols of the Self" (the three magnetic agents in Hippolytus, the unconscious God, the Anthropos, the Naassene, Sethian and Monoimos doctrines, the point and the spark). The method throughout is amplification: a symbol is followed through ancient, patristic, Gnostic and alchemical texts, and its meaning is read as an expression of the archetype of the self and of the process of individuation. Jung repeatedly insists that psychology can establish only that symbols of wholeness coincide with the God-image, not that the image is God.

### Leviathan and Behemoth: the doubling of the God-image [pp.137-139, pars. 185-186]
- The monsters of tradition (Leviathan, Behemoth) have faded into mere attributes of death and the devil, and a fish replaces Leviathan in later symbolism [p.138].
- **The doubling.** This split corresponds to the **doubling of the shadow** met in dreams, where the two halves appear as different or even antagonistic figures. It happens when the conscious ego-personality does not contain all the contents it could contain; part of the personality stays split off and mixes with the normally unconscious shadow, and the two together form a double and often antagonistic personality [p.138].
- Applied to the myth: God's monstrous antagonist produces a double because the **God-image is incomplete** and does not contain everything it logically ought to contain [p.138].
- Leviathan is a fishlike, primitive, cold-blooded creature of the ocean depths. Behemoth is a warm-blooded quadruped (a bull, roaming the mountains in later tradition). Behemoth is related to Leviathan as higher to lower, like the winged and wingless dragon in alchemy. Winged beings are "volatile," vapors, gases, i.e., *pneuma* [p.138].
- Parallels: in Augustine Christ the fish is "drawn from the deep"; in II Esdras 13:2ff the "man" comes out of the sea like a wind, heralded by an eagle and a lion (theriomorphic symbols that terrify the prophet as Behemoth terrifies in Job). The fish drawn from the deep is the bait with which Leviathan is caught; it probably duplicates the great fish and stands for its **pneumatic aspect**. Leviathan, like the Ichthys, is eucharistic food [p.138].
- **Doubling as an act of realization.** Job 26:12: Yahweh smote Rahab "by his understanding" (*tebuna*), from *bin*, 'to separate, split, part asunder,' i.e., to **discriminate**, which Jung calls the essence of conscious realization. Rahab is cousin to Tiamat, whom Marduk split by filling her with Imhullu, the north wind. The motif of splitting is related to penetration and perforation in alchemy [pp.138-139].
- Leviathan and Behemoth thus represent **stages in the development of consciousness**: the fish changes, via the warm-blooded quadruped, into a human being; the human figure split off from the fish hints at God's incarnation. What was missing in the God-image was the **human element** [p.139].

### The fish in ancient cults [p.139, par. 186-187]
- Syrophoenician fish cult of Atargatis: temples with sacred fish pools no one might touch, and ritual fish meals. Cumont: this cult may have engendered Christian Ichthys symbolism. In Lycia the divine fish Orphos or Diorphos, son of Mithras and Cybele; variants of Semitic fish deities (Oannes, Dagon, Adonis called Ichthys by the Greeks); fish offerings to Tanit, Ea and Nina [p.139].
- Egypt: priests forbidden to eat fish (unclean as Typhon's sea); certain towns worshipped a fish; Plutarch reports a broiled fish eaten before the door on the ninth day of the first month; Doelger thinks this prepared the eucharistic fish [p.139].
- **Ambivalence of the fish:** unclean and an emblem of hatred on one side, an object of veneration on the other. A late Hellenistic sarcophagus painting shows a fish (an oxyrhynchus or barbel, one of the three most abominated fishes, said to have devoured Osiris's phallus) floating over a mummy in place of the soul-bird, so the fish is a soul symbol. Barbels were sacred to Typhon, "that part of the soul which is passionate, impulsive, irrational"; medieval allegory made fishes the damned [pp.139-140].

### Horus and Set: opposites that cooperate [pp.140-141, pars. 187-188]
- Set/Typhon is a god of death, destruction and the desert and the enemy of Osiris, but earlier a friend and helper of the dead. In the Pyramid Texts he and Heru-ur (older Horus) help Osiris climb to heaven (an iron-plate floor of heaven reached by a ladder); Heru-ur and Set are day and night. Paintings show their two heads on one body, which Jung reads as the identity of the opposites. Budge is quoted: they are Light and Darkness, Cosmos and Chaos, Life and Death, Good and Evil [pp.140-141].
- The pair represents the **latent opposites in Osiris**, as Behemoth and Leviathan do in Yahweh. The opposites must **work together** to help the one god reach the heavenly **quaternity**: the four sons of Horus (three animal heads, one human) dwell behind the thigh of Set in the northern sky (the Great Bear). Parallel: Ezekiel's cherubim (the evangelists' three animals and one angel), and the crystal plate and the throne with human form above it (Ez. 1:22, 26). Daniel 3:25's fourth figure in the furnace is cited as relevant [p.141].

### The north, the pole and the fish [pp.141-143, pars. 189-192]
- Arab tradition sees the region round the heavenly Pole as a fish (Qazvini). So the Pole, the region of Set and the sons of Horus, is "contained in the body of a fish." In Babylon Anu and Marduk have their seat at the north; the northern point of the ecliptic is the region of fire; Sabaeans and Mandaeans pray facing north; in the Mithraic liturgy Mithras holds the "golden shoulder of a young bull," the Bear constellation that turns the heavens; the god has endless fire attributes [pp.141-142].
- Why Ezekiel's vision of God comes from the north, the birthplace of all evil: **the coincidence of opposites is normal in a primitive conception of God**, who is not an object of reflection. At the level of conscious reflection it becomes a major problem we try to circumvent. That is why the **devil's position in Christian dogma is unsatisfactory**. Where there are such gaps in collective ideas ("the dominants of our conscious orientation") one can count on compensatory developments in the unconscious; these appear in alchemical speculation [p.142].
- The alchemists aimed at a more or less conscious restoration of the primitive God-image, hence paradoxes such as God's love glowing in hell-fire; Jakob Boehme, influenced by alchemy and Cabala, pictured good and bad as belonging to the same divine being [pp.142-143].
- The north in ancient thought is divided: seat of the highest gods and of the adversary; the direction of prayer; source of the evil *Aquilo* wind (the evil spirit); the navel of the world and hell. Bernard of Clairvaux to Lucifer: you strive toward the north; the higher you rush, the faster your setting. Footnote: the north wind was invoked in an alchemical account of coral hardening [p.143].

## Chapter X: The Fish in Alchemy

### 1. The Medusa [pp.144-155, pars. 193-212]
- **Sources.** Nostradamus, as a physician, would have known alchemy, which was practiced mainly by physicians. The *Turba philosophorum* (translated from Arabic into Latin in the 11th-12th century, the same sphere as the *Tabula smaragdina*, late Hellenistic ideas passed on through Harran Neoplatonists) and its appendices, especially the "Allegoriae super librum Turbae," are the **earliest source for alchemical fish symbolism**, before the 11th century and with no Christian origin. The fish became a symbol of the lapis (prima materia and end product; names: lapis philosophorum, elixir vitae, aurum nostrum, infans, puer, filius philosophorum, Hermaphroditus). The filius is a parallel to Christ; its father is God and its mother the Sapientia Dei or Mercurius as Virgo. **The filius/lapis means the self** [pp.144-145, pars. 193-194].
- **The recipe.** "There is in the sea a round fish, lacking bones and cortex, and having in itself a fatness, a wondrous virtue," cooked on a slow fire until fat and moisture vanish, then saturated with sea water until it begins to shine. The later "Aenigmata philosophorum" version has a "pisciculus" that "candescat." Both end ironically: when the *citrinitas* (yellowing, *xanthosis*) appears, "the collyrium (eyewash) of the philosophers" forms; washing the eyes with it lets them understand the secrets of philosophy [p.145, par. 195].
- **A jellyfish?** Jung argues the round fish is an invertebrate (no bones; *cortex* in medieval Latin means a shell), presumably a scyphomedusa. He describes the radial construction: divided into eight sections by four perradials and four interradials; tentacles with nematocysts that poison prey [pp.145-146, par. 196].
- **The shining.** The "round fish" starts to glow when warmed, as the heat in it becomes light. Jung links this to Pliny's *stella marina* ('star of the sea'), hot and burning, which consumes what it touches, mentioned together with the *pulmo marinus* whose rubbed stick becomes a torch. Caussin reads the "starfish" as the "inextinguishable power of true love"; Picinellus adds that it burns forever in the waters like the fire of the Holy Ghost (Pentecost's tongues), "divinae gratiae efficacitas" that ignites hearts drowned in a sea of sins; Song of Solomon 8:7 ("Many waters cannot quench love"); the fish is an emblem of religion and also the "hieroglyph of a lover's heart," divine or profane. Then Picinellus says it burns but gives no light, citing St. Basil on hell's fire, which burns without light, and the fish becomes *concupiscentia*, the "scintilla voluptatis" [pp.146-147, pars. 197-199].
- **Opposed readings of one symbol.** Jung notes how often medieval symbolists give diametrically opposed interpretations of one symbol without noticing the dangerous implication that **the unity of the symbol implies the identity of the opposites** [pp.147-148, par. 200].
- **God glows in the fire.** The "Gloria mundi" (anonymous alchemical treatise, printed in German 1620): the fire "in which God himself glows in divine love"; the unquenchable living fire; the fire of the Philosophers; fire that purifies as purgatory does; the Philosophers call it the fire of the Holy Ghost; it unites Mercurius with the sun into one inseparable thing; the fire unites body, spirit and soul as the Trinity is united; it is "fire and water at once" and God "mingles himself with himself in the living water" [pp.148-149]. Another treatise: water is the "hiding-place and dwelling-place of the whole treasure"; within it the "fire of Gehenna" holds the world-engine; it is kindled by the *primum mobile* and the stars [p.149, par. 201].
- **The unnatural fire.** It tortures bodies; it is the dragon that "burns furiously like hell-fire"; the life-spirit Phyton has an infernal aspect, and Abraham Eleazar calls Phyton a "god" [p.149, par. 202].
- **Four aspects of fire (Blaise de Vigenere):** intelligible (all light); heavenly (heat and light); elemental (light, heat and glow); infernal (burning without light, opposed to the intelligible). Jung links the **quaternity of fire** with Set and the sons of Horus and Ezekiel's northern fire. Footnote: quaternary symbols that appear spontaneously in dreams point to totality or the self; fire means passion, affects, desires, emotional driving forces, i.e., libido; so attributing a quaternary nature to fire amounts to saying **the self is the source of energy** [p.150, par. 203].
- **Steel and the magnet (Philalethes, "Introitus apertus").** The arcane substance is "chalybs" (steel), the "auri minera," the key of the Work, a secret infernal yet volatile fire, with the astrological sign of the star the Magi followed (a hidden nativity of the divine child). The steel is attracted by "our magnet," whose hidden center "with an archetic appetite turns towards the Pole," and the center "abounds in salt" (the *sal sapientiae*). In the Pole is the heart of Mercurius, "the true fire wherein its Lord has his rest"; whoever sails the great sea may steer by the North Star that "our Magnet" shows him. This points to the mystic journey (*peregrinatio*) to four quarters (the two Indies, East and West, plus the compass turn north), forming a cross or quaternity; the Pole is the source of the four directions and the hemispheric division, and the northern hemisphere resembles the round body of the medusa with its four (or multiples of four) radials, like a globe seen from the pole [pp.150-152, pars. 204-206].
- **A dream of a 20-year-old student** [p.152, pars. 207-208]. He was in confusion over a philosophical faculty that did not suit him and could not say what profession he wanted. *Dream:* he walks in a wood that grows lonelier until it becomes a primeval forest, dark under high trees; the path disappears; driven by expectation and curiosity he reaches a circular pool 10 to 12 feet across, a spring, clear but nearly black in shadow; in the middle floats a pearly organism about 18 inches across, faintly luminous: a jellyfish. He woke with violent emotion and decided to study science, and kept to it. Jung stresses the dreamer was under no suggestive psychological influence. The conclusion he drew was right but does not exhaust the symbol: it is an archetypal, "big" dream. The wood that darkens is entry into the unconscious; the round pool with the jellyfish is a **three-dimensional mandala, the self**, wholeness as the goal toward which the "archetic appetite" points, the magnetic north that orients the traveler on the "sea of the world." (A footnote points to *Memories, Dreams, Reflections*.)
- **Recapitulation.** The infernal fire is the **Deus absconditus** who dwells at the North Pole and reveals himself through magnetism; its synonym is Mercurius, whose heart is at the Pole and who guides men over the sea of the world. The idea that the world machinery is driven by an infernal fire at the Pole, that this is hell, and that hell is "a system of upper powers reflected in the lower" is a "shattering thought" with a certain grandeur (Meister Eckhart, returning to his true self, enters an abyss "deeper than hell itself"). Psychologically it is the **projection of an archetypal pattern of order, the mandala**, representing totality. The centering of the image on hell that is also God rests on the experience that **highest and lowest both come from the depths of the soul** and either wreck the frail vessel of consciousness or carry it safely to port with little help from us; the experience of this "center" is numinous in its own right [p.153, par. 209].
- **Picinellus on the tongue.** The burning-but-lightless "Stella maris" besides meaning Holy Ghost, love, grace and religion, also symbolizes man's tongue, speech and expressive powers, instinctive, unreflecting psychic activity (James 3:6: the tongue is a fire that defiles the whole body and sets on fire the "wheel of birth"). The evil "fish" coincides with our untamed propensities, a "small fire that sets a great forest ablaze" [pp.153-154, pars. 210-211].
- **The wheel of birth.** *trochos tes geneseos* (rota nativitatis): the wheel symbolizes the cycle of life, an idea akin to Buddhism. Since the *Timaeus* the soul has been called a sphere; as anima mundi it revolves with the world wheel whose hub is the Pole, where Mercurius's heart lies. The horoscope (*thema*, twelve houses cast at birth, first house the ascendent) is a wheel; Nigidius Figulus ('potter') was said to be named for the wheel of heaven turning like a potter's wheel. A horoscope maps the individual's basic character and is an equivalent of the individual psyche. Priscillian (d. 385) says Christ, "destroying the wheel of generation," overcame the day of our birth by the renewal of baptism. **"Setting fire to the wheel"** therefore means a catastrophic revolt of all original components of the psyche, a conflagration like panic or an uncontrollable and fatal outburst of emotion, made total by the central position of the "tongue," the diabolical element in every psyche. The Stella maris stands for the **fiery center in us from which creative or destructive influences come** [pp.154-155, par. 212].

### 2. The Fish [pp.155-162, pars. 213-224]
- **A real fish after all?** Theobald de Hoghelande (16th c.) understood the fish as real (the Turba's "fish-gall," and "a small round fish without bones or legs [cruribus]" in the Aenigmata, where the earlier text had the unintelligible "corticibus"). A round, transparent fish without "cortices" is described in the *Cyranides*: the "cinedian fish" of Syrian, Palestinian and Libyan coasts, six fingers long, a "pisciculus rotundus," with two stones in its head and another in the third vertebra of the tail (notochord), used as a love potion; the stone (also "opsianus," black obsidian-like; pertains to Saturn) is "twin or twofold": one opaque and black, the other black but shining like a mirror. It is the **dragon's stone** (*dracontius lapis*) [pp.155-156, par. 213].
- **The dragon's stone.** The only certain inference is that the animal is a vertebrate and a mythologem, since it contains the dragon's stone (Pliny's draconites; Ruland: obtained by cutting the head of a sleeping dragon; it becomes a gem only if a bit of the dragon's soul remains in it, the "hate of the monster as it feels itself dying"; white, a strong alexipharmic; found in water-snake heads) [pp.156-157, par. 214].
- **Saturn and the double nature.** The stone's double nature (black/white or black/shining) fits Saturn, in astrology the "star of the sun," alchemically black ("sol niger"), black outside like lead but white inside (the "shining white dove," "salt of the metals," lead of the air, in Degenhardus/Grasseus); lead can be turned to hyacinth and back (Vigenere); quicksilver comes from the "heart of Saturn" (Mylius); bright water from the plant Saturnia is "the bloom of the world" (Ripley); Hippolytus: Chronos is a "power of the color of water, and all-destructive." So the double nature of the cinedian stone could signify **the polarity and union of opposites** that gives the lapis its significance as a uniting symbol [pp.157-158, pars. 215-216].
- **Magic as compulsion.** The draconite is the "ligature of Aphrodite," i.e., love magic. Magic exercises a compulsion that prevails over the victim's conscious mind and will, an alien will stronger than the ego; the only psychologically verifiable analogue is the **compelling power of unconscious contents**, which show their affinity with the totality, the self, and its "karmic" functions (hereditary influences, ancestral vestiges, though the idea does not suggest as much as karma does to an Indian) [p.158, par. 216]. The principle of "outward uncomeliness" applies to the lead, the lapis and Christ alike (Ephrem: "his treasure is hidden and of small account, but when it is laid open, it is wonderful to look upon") [p.158].
- **The remora.** In a 17th-century anonymous French treatise ("Instructio de arbore solari" in *Theatrum chemicum* VI) the round fish becomes *Echeneis remora*, the sucking fish with a flat oval sucker on its head that holds ships. The text: the matter for the Philosophical Work is "that little fish the Echeneis," bloodless and spineless, shut up in the deep mid-region of the great universal sea; very small, alone, unique; impossible to catch for those who do not know where it lives; one must first know the "magnet of the wise," which draws the fish from the depth of "our sea"; it can hold back the proud vessels of the ocean, which is the **spirit of the world**; caught according to nature it becomes first water then earth, dissolves all fixed bodies, and purifies poisoned ones [pp.158-159, pars. 217-218].
- **Interpretation.** The ocean is the "spirit of the world," a **projection of the unconscious** (no apparatus could prove the world's animation; soul and spirit as psyche are themselves unconscious; if assumed "outside," they are projections). The alchemists' "our sea" is a symbol of the unconscious, as in dreams. The extremely small fish in the vast ocean that stops big ships is the **self as an unconscious content**, like a man alone on the sea of the world. It could not be caught without a "magnet of the wise" in the conscious subject: the **theoria**, a communicable teaching, the "cunning secret of the wise," the one solid possession from which the adept proceeds [pp.160-161, par. 219].
- **"Nature is not improved save through its own nature."** Bernardus Trevisanus found his way after fruitless laboratory work through Parmenides's sermons in the Turba; the lapis "has everything it needs" and nothing from outside should be added to the vessel [p.161, par. 220]. Jung's comment: it is unlikely the alchemists always knew what they wrote. Who has everything he needs? Even the loneliest meteor circles a distant sun; only **absolute totality** contains everything and needs nothing outside; "which of us can pull himself out of the bog by his own pigtail?" Even the anchorite depends on the ceaseless presence of God (Isaiah 33:14). Only absolute totality can renew itself out of itself [pp.161-162, par. 221].
- **The secret whispered.** The One and All, the Greatest in the guise of the Smallest, God himself in his everlasting fires, can be caught like a fish in the deep sea, "drawn from the deep" by a **eucharistic act of integration** (the Aztec *teoqualo*, 'God-eating') and incorporated into the human body [p.162, par. 222].
- **The remora and Caligula.** Pliny: the half-foot fish sucked fast to the rudder of Caligula's quinquereme and stopped it; Caligula was later murdered, so the fish was an omen; it played a similar trick on Mark Antony before Actium. For the alchemists the remora stands for the extremely small thing in the vastness of the unconscious charged with fateful significance: the **self, the atman, "smaller than small, greater than great"** [p.162, par. 223].
- **Helpful animals.** In Ripley, fish together with birds bring the stone, like the Oxyrhynchus sayings of Jesus (fowls of the air and fishes of the sea point the way to the kingdom of heaven). In Lambspringk's symbols the zodiacal fishes moving in opposite directions symbolize the arcane substance. Theriomorphism is a visualization of the unconscious self manifesting through "animal" impulses: some are known instincts, but most are feelings of certainty, beliefs, compulsions, idiosyncrasies and phobias that can run directly counter to biological instinct without being pathological. **Wholeness is perforce paradoxical** in its manifestations (two fishes in opposite directions; birds and fishes cooperating); the "kingdom of heaven" or conjectural "city" in the sayings likewise refers to the self [pp.162-163, par. 224].

### 3. The Fish Symbol of the Cathars (numeral garbled in source) [pp.163-170, pars. 225-236]
- **The Carcassonne document** (Benoist, 1691; Inquisition archives). The "revelation" to John, the beloved disciple: Satan, formerly ruling heaven's powers, wished to be like God and descended through air and water; beneath the surface of the earth he "found two fishes lying upon the waters, like oxen yoked for ploughing the whole earth from sunset to sunrise" at the command of the invisible Father; lower down he found hanging clouds, and set apart there his **"Osob," 'a kind of fire'**; the flames stopped his descent; back in heaven he announced he would set his throne on the clouds; he treated the angels like the unjust steward and was cast out with them; God allowed him a week of free action, during which Satan, using Genesis 1 as a model, created the world and man [pp.163-164, par. 225]. A similar belief is confessed by the Cathar John de Lugio; the idea that the devil created the world is common in sects; the alchemist Johannes de Rupescissa, probably one of the Poor Men of Lyons, is a possible link to alchemy [p.164, par. 226].
- **Osob.** The Old Bulgarian/Slavonic word *osob(a)* means 'individual, personality' in Russian, Polish and Czech: "his osob" is "that which is peculiar to him," which for the devil would be fire [pp.164-165, par. 227].
- **The two fishes as ruling powers.** Augustine reads the two fishes of the feeding of the five thousand as the kingly and priestly persons, united in Christ, so fishes were regarded as ruling powers. In the Cathar text they may represent two persons from before the creation: Satanael the elder son of God and Christ the younger; Epiphanius reports the Ebionites held a double sonship (Christ and the devil); the Bogomil doctrine of Satanael as demiurge arose among Paulicians and Euchites; the document is a Latin version of Euthymios Zigabenos's *Panoplia*, going back to the Bogomil bishop Basilius's confession of 1111 [pp.165-166, pars. 228-229].
- **Consciousness reading.** The fishes are found "in the beginning," over the dark face of the waters. With one fish it would be the pre-existent Logos; but two fishes joined by a yoke can refer only to the **zodiacal Fishes**, characterizing the ascendent at the world's birth. Cosmogonic myths are, at bottom, symbols for the coming of consciousness (Neumann is cited). The dawn state equals the unconscious, alchemical chaos or nigredo; the opus produces the albedo (illumination, broadening of consciousness). So the two fishes the devil found signify the **newly arisen world of consciousness** [p.166, par. 230].
- **The plough.** Oxen are the motive power of the plough, a symbol of mastery over the earth, wresting soil from the primal state. The fishes thus represent the **driving forces of the coming world of consciousness**, ruling and subduing the world astrologically through man and molding his consciousness. The ploughing begins in the west, as the alchemical work (Ripley) begins toward sunset and goes to midnight, ninety nights in the dark fire of purgatory, then east through many colors; it starts with descent into darkness (nigredo). The ploughing is "at the command of the Father," so God foresaw and intended the **enantiodromia that began in the year 1000**; the Platonic month of the Fishes is ruled by two principles, parallel like the oxen, one Christ and one Antichrist [pp.166-167, par. 231].
- **Conscious or unconscious reasoning?** Jung does not know whether this was ever argued consciously (a Talmudic prophecy for the year 530 suggests astrological reasoning, pars. 133ff.); it may be a product of the unconscious, which makes such "reflections" in dreams and fairy tales; around the 11th century Jewish astrologers began calculating the Messiah's birth in Pisces and Joachim of Flora expressed the sense that a new age had begun [p.167].
- **Synchronicity.** The text can hardly be earlier or much later than the 11th century, astrologically the middle of the Pisces aeon, when heresies sprang up everywhere and Christ's adversary, the second fish, appears as demiurge, a kind of Gnostic Renaissance (the Gnostic demiurge is an inferior being from whom evil comes; Irenaeus: the younger brother of Christ). The significant thing is the **synchronicity**, occurrence at a time fixed astrologically [p.168, par. 233].
- **Link to alchemy.** Jung has found no texts proving the Cathar fish was assimilated into alchemy; Lambspringk's symbol (late 16th century) is a revitalization of the archetype: two reversed fishes in "nostro mari" (the aqua permanens), "spiritus et anima," like the stag and unicorn, two lions, dog and wolf, two fighting birds, indicating the **double nature of Mercurius** [p.168, par. 234].
- **Historical sweep.** With the year 1000 a new world begins in a medley of movements (Bogomils, Cathari, Albigenses, Waldenses, Poor Men of Lyons, Free Spirit, Beguins, Beghards, Joachim's Holy Ghost movement), linked with the rise of alchemy, Protestantism, the Enlightenment and natural science, ending in "increasingly devilish developments" and the evaporation of Christianity under rationalism, intellectualism, materialism and "realism" [p.168, par. 235].
- **A modern fish dream series** [pp.169-170, par. 236]. A young woman with uncommonly vivid dreams, influenced by a materialistic father in an unhappy marriage, built an intense inner life early, replacing her parents with two garden trees; at six or seven she dreamed God promised her a golden fish; fish recurred. Before treatment: standing on the bank of the Limmat, a man throws in a gold coin, the water becomes transparent, a coral reef and many fishes, one with a silver belly and golden back. During treatment: at a broad river she throws her note pages into the water as giving something back, immediately has a fishing rod, fishes and sees only water, earth and rock; a big fish with silver belly and golden back bites; as she draws it to land the whole landscape comes alive (rock as primeval earth-foundation, grass, flowers, a forest, a gust of wind setting everything moving); behind her the voice of an older man, an authority figure known only from photographs and hearsay, says quietly: "The patient ones in the innermost realm are given the fish, the food of the deep." At this moment a circle ran round her, part of it touching the water... *(text continues in the next lines)*

- **Dream, continued** [pp.169-170, par. 236 to 238]. A circle ran round her, part touching the water; then the voice: "The brave ones in the second realm may be given victory, for there the battle is fought." A second circle touched the other bank; she saw far into a colorful landscape and the sun rose. The voice: the third and fourth realms come, similarly enlarged, out of the other two; "the fourth realm joins on to the first. It is the highest and the lowest at once, for the highest and the lowest come together. They are at bottom one." She woke with a roaring in her ears. (Footnote 103: the transparent water means attention, value (gold), is given to the unconscious; an offering to the genius of the fountain.)
- **Jung's commentary** [pp.170-171, pars. 237-238]. A "big" dream with the quality of something "thought," characteristic of the intuitive type; the dreamer knew some psychology but nothing of the historical fish symbol. The river bank is the threshold to the unconscious; fishing is an intuitive attempt to "catch" unconscious contents; silver and gold are alchemically feminine and masculine, the **hermaphroditic** fish, a *complexio oppositorum*, which also brings magical animation (the Ichthys as the food that bestows immortal life); the old man is the archetype of the **wise old man**; the fish is the "miraculous food," the eucharistic food of the initiates; the first circle touching the water is **partial integration** of the unconscious; the battle is the conflict of opposites (perhaps consciousness against the shadow); the second circle touches the "other bank" where the union of opposites occurs (in the Indian "quicksilver system" the arcane substance is *para-da*, 'leading to the other shore'; in the West, Mercurius); the fourth realm, stressed by the weighty pause, is the One that adds itself to the three and makes all four a unity. The circles form a **mandala**, the outermost circle paradoxically coinciding with the center, recalling the old image of God ("a circle whose center is everywhere and circumference nowhere"). The first coinciding with the fourth recalls the **axiom of Maria**: "One becomes two, two becomes three, and out of the third comes the One as the fourth."
- **Conclusion of the chapter.** The dream condenses the whole symbolism of the individuation process in a person ignorant of the literature. Such cases are not rare and demonstrate an **unconscious "knowledge" of the individuation process and its historical symbolism** [p.171, par. 238].

## Chapter XI: The Alchemical Interpretation of the Fish [pp.172-190, pars. 239-266]


### The attraction reversed [pp.172-173, par. 239]
- Jung returns to the problem the anonymous French author of the "Instructio de arbore solari" raised: **how the fish is caught**. The Echeneis attracts ships like a magnet attracts iron (tradition: it brings vessels to a standstill; footnote: Isidore's *echinus* is strictly a sea urchin, in the same radial class as starfish and jellyfish; a salted Echeneis let down into a mine was said to draw up gold; sal ammoniac is also called a "magnet").
- In the alchemical view the attraction no longer proceeds from the fish but from a **magnet that man possesses**. A later chapter will show the Gnostics said the same of their central figure (point, monad, son). The alchemists' innovation is to manipulate an instrument that exerts the Echeneis's power **on the Echeneis itself**, a parallel to the adept's claim to produce the *filius macrocosmi*, the equivalent of Christ, *Deo concedente*, through his art. The artifex or his instrument replaces the Echeneis as arcane substance [pp.172-173].

### The magnet of the wise, the prima materia, and magnesia [pp.173-177, pars. 240-245]
- The "magnet of the wise" can be **taught**; its secret content is the real arcanum of alchemy, the discovery or production of the prima materia. The doctrine ("theoria") is concretized as **Mercurius non vulgi**, philosophical mercury, as ambiguous as Hermes: sometimes a substance like quicksilver, sometimes a philosophy (Pernety: the matter of philosophical mercury has a "magnetic" virtue that draws the Sages' mercury from the rays of Sun and Moon) [p.173, par. 240].
- On the prima materia the adepts say very little. Jung's reading: no such material existed from which a lapis could be made; the names given it show it was not a definite substance but an **intuitive concept for an initial psychic situation**: water of life, cloud, heaven, shadow, sea, mother, moon, dragon, Venus, chaos, massa confusa, microcosmos [p.173].
- **Magnesia** (not the pharmacopoeia magnesium oxide; in Pliny and Dioscorides a magnetic iron-stone) is the whitened arcane substance, the "full moon" (Rosarium), the root-matter of the stone; Khunrath makes it synonymous with chaos and "Aes Hermetis," a Cosmic Ens, Three-in-One of body, spirit and soul, the true Subiectum Catholicon. Magnesia is feminine and the magnet masculine; magnesia carries "in its belly" the sal Armoniacum; in Greek alchemy it was the hermaphroditic transformative substance. Rosinus's recipe: take "this animate stone," sensitive to the presence and influence of magnesia and the magnet [pp.173-175, par. 241].
- **Jung's reading.** The recipe shows alchemy was not concerned with chemical processes: a substance that must be animate and sensitive points to a psychic function. The adept was occupied with one of the innumerable expressions for the **unconscious, the hidden part of the psyche that slipped into the unknown chemical compound by projection** and "bedevilled and befooled him in the guise of a hundred arcane substances" [p.175, par. 242].
- **The magnet as a living thing.** Chrysippus Fanianus: if Thales called the lodestone animate, why not salt, which penetrates, purges, contracts, expands? Dorn: the magnetic power is a spirit hidden within, not perceptible to the senses. Augustine recalls his first sight of the lodestone as a cold shiver (*vehementer inhorrui*); Alciati and Fallopius wonder at it, so intelligent people felt such an object was alive (like *lapis animatus*, *calx viva*). In the "Duodecim tractatus" the magnet is the symbol of the **aqua roris nostri**, the aqua permanens [pp.175-177, par. 243].
- **Doctrine and substance.** The "aqua doctrinae" (the doctrine itself) is contrasted with the "animate stone." Dorn: nature seeks a nature like itself; the axiom of Democritus: "Nature rejoices in nature; nature subdues nature; nature rules over nature." As magnes and magnesia form a pair, the *lapis animatus sive vegetabilis* is a **Rebis or hermaphrodite** born of the royal marriage. The two pairs form a **quaternio**, the fourfold basis of wholeness, and both pairs mean a *complexio oppositorum* or uniting symbol [pp.177, par. 244-245; note: in the text par. 245 begins at p.177].
- **Two categories of symbol** (Collesson, Benedictine prior, 17th c.: the dissolving substance is "the general soul of the world," drawn down from higher bodies, especially the rays of Sun and Moon; those who dissolve metals by natural and physical means know nothing of Mercurius): (1) symbols referring to the **extrapsychic chemical substance or its metaphysical equivalent** (serpens mercurialis, spiritus, anima mundi, veritas, sapientia); (2) symbols for the **preparations produced by the adept** (solvents: aqua, acetum, lac virginis) or their philosophical equivalent, the theoria or scientia, which, when "right," has miraculous effects on matter. The categories continually overlap [pp.178, par. 246-247].
- **Salt and truth.** Pernety: the alchemists' "magnet" is not the common magnet; its center holds a hidden salt prepared by the adept (a "menstruum for calcining philosophical gold") that forms their Mercury. "Salt" never means sodium chloride alone; the *sal sapientiae* is included. That it is both a product of art and already present in nature is resolved by taking it as **projection of a psychic content** [pp.178-179, par. 247]. Dorn's **Veritas** is likewise hidden in natural things and in the human body ("a certain metaphysical substance known to very few, which needeth no medicament, being itself an incorrupt medicament") and is a "moral" concept; the chemists' study is to liberate that unsensual truth from its fetters in things of sense. Collesson: the Philosophers knew by "divine inspiration" that this heavenly virtue is freed "not by its contrary but by its like," by peace rather than war [pp.179-180, par. 248].

### The doctrine as gift and the inner/outer opus [p.180, pars. 249-250]
- The doctrine, consciously acquired "through a kind of divine inspiration," is also the instrument that frees the object of the doctrine from its imprisonment in the body, since the symbol for the doctrine (the magnet) is the mysterious "truth" it speaks of. The treasure of doctrine and the secret in matter are **one thing**: owed to unconscious projection. **Dorn** is credited as first to see clearly the dilemma: the arcane substance is the same whether found within man or outside; the procedure takes place within and without; one who cannot free the "truth" in his own soul will not succeed with the physical opus, and one who can make the stone can do so only on the basis of right doctrine, through which he himself is transformed [p.180].

### Self-knowledge: Dorn on "quis" and "quid" [pp.180-185, pars. 250-256]
- Dorn: "Make yourself such as you wish the work you seek to be" (*Fac igitur ut talis evadas...*). The expectations put into the work must be applied to your own ego; experiment on ourselves is the best proof; "man's greatest treasure is to be found within man"; he who is not mentally blind will understand who and what he is inwardly and will know himself through outward things; "Learn from within thyself to know all that is in heaven and on earth," because heaven and the elements were once one and divinely separated [pp.180-181, par. 250].
- **Two kinds of self-knowledge.** Knowledge of the nature of the ego (convenient, confused with self-knowledge) is "a dog chasing its own tail"; **knowledge of the self as object** is a difficult, morally exacting study and is accused of selfishness and eccentricity. The alchemist had at least an indirect inkling that, as part of the whole, he held an image of the whole in himself, the "firmament" or "Olympus" (Paracelsus); this **interior microcosm** was the unwitting object of alchemical research. Today this is the **collective unconscious**, "objective" because it is identical in all individuals and therefore one; from this universal One each individual produces a subjective consciousness, the ego (Dorn's "formerly one," "separated by a divine act of creation") [pp.181-182, par. 251]. (Footnote: the idea reached full development 200 years later in Leibniz's monadology.)
- **Quis versus quid.** Dorn: "No one can know himself unless he knows what, and not who, he is, on what he depends, or whose he is, and to what end he was made." *Quis* is personal (the ego); *quid* is neuter, an object not even endowed with personality: not subjective ego-consciousness but **the psyche itself as the unknown, unprejudiced object** still to be investigated. This is the difference between knowledge of the ego and of the self. "What" is the neutral self, the objective fact of totality, on which the ego is causally dependent and toward which it is directed as a goal. Jung compares the opening of Ignatius Loyola's "Foundation" ("Man was created to praise, do reverence to, and serve God...") [pp.182-183, par. 252].
- **Causes and ends.** Man knows little of his psyche. Causal factors (Freud) and final factors (Adler) reside largely in unconscious processes and are unalterable and irreversible until they become objects of consciousness; correction comes only through conscious insight and moral determination, **which is why self-knowledge, so necessary, is feared**. Jung paraphrases the Foundation without theology: consciousness exists to (1) recognize its descent from a higher unity, (2) pay due regard to this source, (3) execute its commands intelligently and responsibly, (4) thereby give the psyche as a whole the optimum of life and development. The paraphrase is meant to be rationalistic, since the modern mind accepts the old language only if it "accords with reason," otherwise lip-service, affectation, forced belief, or indifference result [p.183, pars. 253-254].
- **Talents and evil.** The final factors are the talents entrusted to servants (Luke 19). Only an infantile person pretends evil is not everywhere at work, and the more unconscious he is the more "the devil drives him." Because of this inner connection with the dark side the mass man commits appalling crimes without thinking. Only **ruthless self-knowledge on the widest scale**, which sees good and evil in perspective and weighs motives, offers a guarantee that the end result will not be too bad [p.184, par. 255].

### Morienus, Rosinus: the stone and the person [pp.184-187, pars. 256-261]
- The idea of the crucial role of self-knowledge is older than Dorn; **Morienus Romanus** wrote on the rim of the Hermetic vessel: "All those who have all things with them have no need of outside aid." It is a moral matter. God made the world of four unequal elements and set man between them as the "greater ornament": "This thing is extracted from thee, for thou art its ore." The procedure "cannot be performed with hands"; it is a "human attitude" (*dispositio hominum*); the transformation is brought about by the **coniunctio**, the essence of the work [p.184-185, par. 256].
- **Rosinus ad Sarratantam** cites the Magus Philosophus: "This stone is below thee, as to obedience; above thee, as to dominion; therefore from thee, as to knowledge; about thee, as to equals." Jung: the stone has a psychic relationship to man: the adept expects obedience but the stone exercises dominion over him; it springs from man as knowledge and is found among those of like mind. This is the **paradoxical situation of the self**: smallest of the small, easily overlooked, needing to be perceived, protected, "built up" by the conscious mind, yet in experience long there, older than the ego, the secret *spiritus rector* of fate. The self does not become conscious by itself but is taught, if at all, by a **tradition of knowing** (the purusha/atman teaching). Since individuation is impossible without relationship to one's environment, the self is found among those of like mind. The self is an archetype that expresses a situation **within which the ego is contained**; like every archetype it cannot be localized in an individual ego-consciousness but acts like a circumambient atmosphere without definite limits in space or time (hence the **synchronistic phenomena** associated with activated archetypes) [p.185-186, par. 257].
- **Rosinus parallel:** the stone is "fixed more in thee," created of God; "thou art its ore"; it is extracted by division (*divisio, separatio*), and remains inseparably in thee by knowledge, in the Mercurius of the wise; "without thee it cannot be fulfilled, and without it canst thou not live, and so the end looks to the beginning, and contrariwise." The stone is implanted in man by God, the laborant is its prima materia. Read psychologically, this is **realization of an unconscious content**: fixation in the Mercurius of the wise (Nous) corresponds to traditional Hermetic knowledge, through which the self, as an unconscious content, is made conscious and "fixed" [pp.186-187, pars. 258-259].
- **Apperceptive concepts.** Without conscious concepts apperception is impossible. Many neurotic disturbances arise from contents constellated in the unconscious that cannot be assimilated for lack of apperceptive concepts to "grasp" them. This is why telling **children fairy tales and legends and inculcating religious ideas (dogmas) into adults** matters: they are instrumental symbols with whose help unconscious contents can be canalized into consciousness, interpreted and integrated. Otherwise their energy flows into ordinary conscious contents and intensifies them to pathological proportions: groundless phobias, obsessions, crazes, hypochondriacal ideas, intellectual perversions camouflaged in social, religious or political garb [p.187, par. 259].
- **Apocatastasis.** The old master saw the opus as an **apocatastasis**, restoring an initial state in an eschatological one. This is what happens in individuation, whether as Christian transformation ("Except ye become as little children"), Zen satori ("show me your original face"), or a psychological developmental process in which the original propensity to wholeness becomes a conscious happening [p.187, par. 260].
- **Where the center is.** For the alchemist the "center," the self, does not lie in the ego but outside it, "in us" yet not "in our mind," in what we unconsciously are, the *quid*. Today one distinguishes a **personal unconscious** (the shadow) and an **impersonal unconscious** (the archetypal symbol of the self). Lacking a theory of knowledge, the alchemist had to exteriorize his archetype and lodge it in matter, while feeling the center was paradoxically in man and outside [p.187, par. 261].

### Dorn on the unity of the adept and the stone [pp.188-190, pars. 262-266]
- Dorn: the incorrupt medicament can be found nowhere but in heaven, which pervades all elements with invisible rays meeting at the center of the earth and generates all creatures; "No man can generate in himself, but in that which is like him, which is from the same [heaven]" [p.188, par. 262].
- Jung's gloss: one can produce only in an object akin to one's own center, which comes from the same fountainhead, God. Separation into heaven, elements, man was necessary only for generation; everything separated must be reunited to restore the original unity. "Thou wilt never make from others the One which thou seekest, except first there be made one thing of thyself." **The union of opposites in the stone is possible only when the adept has become One himself.** The stone is the equivalent of individuation: a projection of the unified self. But the stone is also a **transcendent unity**; the self, though it can become a symbolic content of consciousness, is as a supraordinate totality necessarily transcendental too [p.188, pars. 263-264].
- Dorn: "Transmute yourselves from dead stones into living philosophical stones!" (cf. I Peter 2:4f). He lacked the concept of an unconscious existence that would have let him express the **identity of the subjective psychic center and the objective alchemical center**. He explained the magnetic attraction between the imagined symbol (theoria) and the "center" hidden in matter, the earth's interior or the North Pole, as the identity of two extremes; both theoria and arcanum are called **Veritas**, which "shines" in us but is not of us: it is "to be sought not in us, but in the image of God which is in us" [pp.188-189, par. 264].
- Dorn thus equates the transcendent center in man with the **God-image**, which explains why alchemical symbols for wholeness apply both to the arcanum in man and to the Deity (mercury and sulphur, fire and water referring to God, Christ, and the Holy Ghost). He gives being to this truth alone: "nothing exists beside that One" [p.189, par. 265].
- Dorn is presented as probably the first to sum up all alchemical symbols and state the motive of alchemy, more lucid than Jakob Boehme yet unknown to historians of philosophy. Jung's claim: alchemy is "the mother of the essential substance as well as the concreteness of modern scientific thinking," not scholasticism, which supplied discipline and training of the intellect [pp.189-190, par. 266].

## Chapter XII: Background to the Psychology of Christian Alchemical Symbolism [pp.191-201, pars. 267-286]

### Alchemy, Gnosticism and the split between faith and knowledge [pp.191-192, pars. 267-269]
- "Mater Alchimia" could name a whole epoch: beginning roughly with Christianity, it gave birth in the 16th and 17th centuries to the age of science, then sank unrecognized. Its real beginnings are the **Gnostic systems**, which Hippolytus rightly regarded as philosophical; with Greek philosophy, Near and Middle Eastern mythologies, Christian dogmatics and Jewish cabalism they attempted a unitary view of the world in which physical and mystical aspects played equal parts. Had this succeeded, we would not have today "two parallel world-views neither of which knows or wishes to know anything about the other." Until Kepler there were creditable attempts to interpret Nature on the basis of Christian dogma [p.191, par. 267].
- These attempts failed for lack of knowledge of natural processes; in the 18th century the rift between faith and knowledge arose: **faith lacked experience and science missed out the soul**. Science believed in absolute objectivity and overlooked that the vehicle and begetter of all knowledge is the psyche, treated as epiphenomenon; scientists use "a photographic apparatus" of whose nature they knew nothing. Only recently, especially in microphysics, has the psyche's objective reality entered calculations (the psyche's collision with physics) [pp.191-192, par. 268].
- For the Gnostics and alchemists the psyche existed as a source of knowledge. Contemporary science (apart from the psychology of the unconscious) knows only the outside; faith knows only the inside in the Christian form given since Paul and John. Faith, like science with its traditional objectivity, is absolute; hence they cannot agree [p.192, par. 269].

### Dogma as symbol; Eastern substitutes; the thin Christian layer [pp.192-195, pars. 270-274]
- Christian doctrine is a **highly differentiated symbol expressing the transcendent psychic**, the God-image and its properties; the Creed is a *symbolum*. It covers inner experience but not Nature in any recognizable form; hence subsidiary currents in every period sought to explore Nature also from the inside (alchemy) [p.192, par. 270].
- Dogma, like mythology, expresses the quintessence of inner experience and the operative principles of the objective psyche (collective unconscious), but in language alien to modern thinking. "Dogma" now sounds like rigid prejudice; real discussion of dogma had nearly ceased until the recent papal declarations; a sign the symbol is fading, **a dangerous development for psychic health**, as no other symbol expresses the unconscious so well. People look for exotic substitutes (India). That hope is delusory: Indian symbols formulate the unconscious as well as Christian ones, but each exemplifies **its own spiritual past**; "we can learn a lot from Indian thought, it can never express the past that is stored up within us." Our premise is Christianity, covering eleven to nineteen centuries of Western life, preceded by a longer polytheism and polydemonism; in parts of Europe it goes back only five hundred years; the last witch was burnt in Europe the year Jung's grandfather was born; barbarism has broken out again in the twentieth century. The wall separating us from pagan times is thin [pp.192-193, par. 271-272, nominal par. numbers].
- Germanic peoples accepted Christian monotheism often "at the sword's point of the Roman legions"; spread among barbarians necessitated a certain **inflexibility of dogma** (Islam similarly); in India the symbol developed organically; Buddhism was reassimilated into Hinduism within a millennium, with the Buddha as an avatar of Vishnu next to Christ, Matsya (the fish), Kurma, Vamana [pp.193-194, par. 272].
- Anyone who takes over Eastern forms of thought uproots himself; they are bloodless intellectual concepts that strike no chord in our inmost being. "We are rooted in Christian soil," though the foundation is thin and paganism returned in altered guise in much of Europe with slavery as its characteristic economic pattern [p.194, par. 273].
- Alchemy's pagan currents beneath the Christian surface continued in natural science, leading to nineteenth-century materialism and twentieth-century "realism." Christianity is "a helpless bystander"; the Church "pastures her sheep on the ruins of Europe"; her message works if combined with understanding of the present, but she wraps it in "sacrosanct words hallowed by age." Jung's Paul argument: Paul spoke the language of the Athenian market-place; he would not have succeeded using Minoan-age myths [p.194, par. 274].
- Present-day man is harder to convince than apostolic-era people: virgin birth of hero and demigod, the redeeming God-man, divine kings, the "Hermes ter unus" were believable philosophical truths and the base on which the Trinity could be built; sacred springs and chemical changes made consecrated water and transmutation plausible; "every schoolboy knows more about Nature than all the volumes of Pliny" [p.195, par. 274]. If Paul were alive he would have to accommodate his language to the intellectual faculties of modern Londoners in Hyde Park [p.195].

### Dogma, faith and the broken bridge [pp.195-199, pars. 275-282]
- Christian preaching ("kerygmatics") speaks in images that are hoary with age and deceptively familiar yet far from modern conscious understanding; they reach at most the unconscious, and only if the speaker's whole soul is in his work; usually the effect stays at most in feeling [pp.195-196, par. 275].
- **The bridge from dogma to the inner experience of the individual has broken down.** Dogma is "believed," hypostatized (as Protestants hypostatize the Bible, "anything can be authorized out of the Bible"), no longer formulating or expressing anything; faith has itself become the experience. Paul could appeal to the Damascus road; ancient and medieval believers were supported by the *consensus omnium*; all that has changed in the last three hundred years [p.196, par. 276]. (Footnote: Father Victor White points to Aquinas's *Veritas prima*, invisible and unknown, as what underlies belief. Footnote: dogma remains legitimate, since most believers need only hold a tenet true, and for them dogma remains a "magnet.")
- The danger: new wine bursting old bottles; what is not understood being thrown into the lumber-room, as at the Reformation, when Protestantism discarded nearly all the ritual that every religion needs and relies on *sola fides*. What remains of the symbolum? The person of Jesus is the obscurest item for a biographer; if only God is left, what of the Incarnation? Jung recommends the papal dictum "Sit, ut est, aut non sit" for the Creed [pp.196-197, par. 277].
- **Why a psychologist insists on dogma.** For the same reason the alchemist insisted on his "theoria": his doctrine was the quintessence of the symbolism of unconscious processes, as dogmas are the distillation of sacred history. To understand dogma one must consider the myths of the Near and Middle East underlying Christianity, then all mythology as expression of a universal disposition in man, the **collective unconscious**, inferable only from individual phenomenology; the **archetypes** are "unconscious organizers of our ideas." The motive force producing them cannot be distinguished from **instinct**: "no justification for visualizing the archetype as anything other than the image of instinct in man" [p.197, par. 278].
- No "nothing but" reduction: reducing human morphology to a four-legged saurian does not nullify the human form; the overriding question is not evolution's origin but its goal; yet an organism cut off from its roots perishes, so **anamnesis of origins is a matter of life and death** [pp.197-198, par. 279].
- **Myths and fairy tales** express unconscious processes; retelling them brings the processes alive and re-establishes the connection between conscious and unconscious. Separation of the two halves is **dissociation of the personality, the root of all neuroses** ("the conscious goes to the right and the unconscious to the left"). Opposites never unite at their own level (*tertium non datur*): a **supraordinate "third"** is required, and the **symbol** derives from both sides, reconciling their conceptual polarity by its form and their emotional polarity by its numinosity [p.198, par. 280].
- The ancients compared the symbol to water (tao: valley spirit, winding river). The Church's symbolum is the *aqua doctrinae*, matching alchemy's divine water with its double aspect in Mercurius; the healing properties of tao, baptismal water, the elixir point to the therapeutic character of the mythological background. Physician-alchemists held their arcanum healed mind as well as body. At the base of every neurosis is a **moral problem of opposites that cannot be solved rationally**, only by a supraordinate third, a symbol expressing both sides: Dorn's *Veritas*, Paracelsus's *theoria*. Alchemists and physicians continued the Gnostics' work (most Gnostics were "not so much heretics as theologians") and the Fathers': like a snake changing its skin, **the old myth needs to be clothed anew in every renewed age** or it loses therapeutic effect [pp.198-199, par. 281].
- The integration of the unconscious must be solved along lines traced by history, via a new assimilation of the traditional myth, presupposing continuity. The present tendency to destroy tradition or render it unconscious can interrupt development for centuries (the Marxist Utopia has done so); a purely scientific and technological education can cause spiritual regression and more dissociation; "with hygiene and prosperity alone a man is still far from health." **Loss of roots and lack of tradition neuroticize the masses and prepare them for collective hysteria**, calling for "collective therapy," abolition of liberty and terrorization; states where rationalistic materialism holds sway tend to become lunatic asylums rather than prisons [p.199, par. 282].

### Closing of the chapter: the Christ figure and the fish as bridge [pp.199-201, pars. 283-286]
- There had to be an **affinity ("magnet!")** between the Redeemer figure and unconscious contents for the human mind to perceive the light in Christ; the link is the **archetype of the God-man**, which became historical reality in Christ and, eternally present, reigns over the soul as supraordinate totality, the self. The God-man is "Lord of the spirits" and "Lord over the (evil) spirits," an essential meaning of Kyrios [pp.199-200, par. 283].
- The noncanonical fish symbol leads into a psychic matrix where archetypes become living and "disclose their hidden nucleus by perpetually circumambulating round it." The lapis that signifies God become man or man become God "has a thousand names"; it is not Christ but his **parallel in the subjective realm**. Alchemy gives a concrete idea of what Christ means in subjective experience; the same could be shown in a modern individual, as in *Psychology and Alchemy* Part II, but needs volumes of biographical data [p.200, par. 284].
- **Conclusion:** the fish is a spontaneous assimilation of the Christ figure by the unconscious (patristic allegory of Leviathan caught with the Cross as hook and the Crucified as bait; "de profundo levatus"). The fish image came out of the unconscious as equivalent of the historical Christ; "Ichthys" names what came up from the depths. **The fish symbol is the bridge between the historical Christ and the psychic nature of man, where the archetype of the Redeemer dwells**; Christ became an inner experience, the "Christ within." Alchemical fish symbolism leads to the lapis (salvator, servator, *deus terrenus*), psychologically to the **self**. In as much or as little as the fish is Christ does the self mean God. The self is a new realization of the divine Son in conceptual rather than theriomorphic form, a distinct **increase in conscious development** over the "mute and unconscious fish" [pp.200-201, pars. 285-286].

## Chapter XIII: Gnostic Symbols of the Self [pp.202-239, pars. 287-346]

### Cognition as recognition; the magnet in Hippolytus [pp.202-207, pars. 287-295]
- Since all cognition is akin to recognition, the developmental process Jung describes was anticipated and prefigured at the beginning of our era in **Gnosticism**, a product of cultural assimilation, useful for defining contents constellated by prophecies of the Redeemer, his historical appearance, or by the **synchronicity of the archetype** [p.202, par. 287]. (Footnote: Rhine's ESP experiments show intense emotional fascination is accompanied by phenomena explainable only by psychic relativity of time, space and causality; archetypes, being numinous, arouse such fascination; synchronistic phenomena are meaningful coincidences of causally unrelated facts.)
- In Hippolytus's *Elenchos* the attraction between magnet and iron appears three times (by Jung's count).
  1. **Naassenes** [pp.202-203, pars. 288-289]: the four rivers of Paradise correspond to eye, ear, smell, mouth; the mouth (prayers out, food in) is the fourth river, the Euphrates. "The fourth always makes a triad into a totality." This is the "water above the firmament," the living water of John 4:10; to it every nature comes to choose its own substances, "more [certainly] than iron to the Heracleian stone" (magnet). The water has the property of the *aqua doctrinae* and perfects every nature in its individuality, giving man a kind of **magnetic power to attract and integrate what belongs to him**, a parallel to the alchemical view: the doctrine is the magnet making integration possible.
  2. **Peratae** [pp.203-204, pars. 290-291]: no one is saved without the Son, who is the serpent: he brought the signs of the Father down and carries them back again "as naphtha draws fire," "more than the Heracleian stone draws iron"; the "perfect race of men" is drawn out of the world by the Serpent (cf. John 3:14). Here the attraction proceeds from the **Son symbolized by the serpent**, who draws from man the parts of divine origin (the "signs of the Father") back to their heavenly birthplace. **The serpent is an equivalent of the fish**: the Redeemer was interpreted as a fish because he arose from unknown depths and as a serpent because he came mysteriously from darkness. Fishes and snakes are favorite symbols for psychic happenings that suddenly dart out of the unconscious with frightening or redeeming effect, often expressed as helpful animals. The Gnostics favored the serpent as old symbol of the good genius loci (Agathodaimon) and their beloved Nous.
  3. **Sethians** [pp.204-205, par. 292]: a theory of "composition and mixture": a ray of light from above mingles with dark waters below as a minute spark; at the individual's death and at his figurative death (mystical experience) the two **unmix**: *divisio* and *separatio* in alchemical terms, with Mercurius appearing with the dividing sword (Matt. 10:34); what was mixed returns to its "own place" "like iron to the magnet"; the spark hastens to the Logos "more quickly than iron flies to the magnet." Dorn's "invisible rays of heaven" and the arcane substance as "shadow" of the ray are alchemical analogues.
- **Theriomorphic symbols and the level of consciousness** [p.204, par. 291]: animal symbols express the psychic level of the content, as far from human consciousness as an animal psyche; warm- or cold-blooded vertebrates or invertebrates indicate **the degree of unconsciousness**, and contents can produce symptoms matching physiological functions (cerebrospinal and sympathetic nervous systems). The Sethians compared the "Father" with the cerebrum and the "Son" with cerebellum and spinal cord. The snake symbolizes cold-blooded, inhuman contents (abstract intellectual as well as concretely animal), "the extra-human quality in man."
- **The three kinds of magnetic agent** [pp.205-206, par. 293]:

| Agent | Nature | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Water | inanimate, passive; drawn from the well, handled by human hands | the visible doctrine, *aqua doctrinae* or Logos, communicated by word and ritual |
| 2. Serpent | animate, autonomous; appears spontaneously and surprisingly; fascinating, fixed stare, cold blood, stranger to man | fear of everything inhuman and awe of the sublime; lowest (devil) and highest (son of God, Logos, Nous, Agathodaimon); like the fish personifies the dark and unfathomable (deep, forest, night, cave); the "Son" has the form of a snake, not the reverse |
| 3. Logos | a philosophical idea, an abstraction of the bodily, personal son of God, and the dynamic power of thoughts and words | a formulated, articulated content and product of consciousness; unlike the water it is an autonomous personality |

  A primitive saying "snake" means an experience of something extrahuman; the snake is not an allegory [p.206].
- The three symbols describe the unknowable essence of the incarnate God and are highly hypostatized (real water in ritual; the Ophites' eucharist with a live snake, like the Aesculapian snake; the "fish" meant something in itself, acquiring meaning in primitive Christianity with no written support, whereas the serpent refers to an authentic logion) [p.206, par. 294]. All three are **phenomena of assimilation** of a numinous nature with a degree of autonomy; had they not appeared, the annunciation of the Christ figure would have been ineffective: they are the **prototypes of the Christ-figure that slumbered in man's unconscious**, magnetically attracted by his historical appearance. Eckhart uses the same symbolism (lodestone and needles) for Adam's relation to Creator and creatures [p.207, par. 295].

### The new center and the Anthropos [pp.207-208, pars. 296-297]
- This magnetic process **revolutionizes the ego-oriented psyche** by setting up another goal or center against the ego, named by many symbols (fish, serpent, center of the sea-hawk, point, monad, cross, paradise). The myth of the **ignorant demiurge who thought himself highest** illustrates the ego's perplexity when it can no longer hide that a supraordinate authority has dethroned it. The "thousand names" of the lapis correspond to the Gnostic names for the **Anthropos**: the greater, more comprehensive Man, the indescribable whole of conscious and unconscious processes. This objective whole, the antithesis of the subjective ego-psyche, is what Jung calls **the self**, corresponding exactly to the Anthropos idea [p.207, par. 296].
- In therapy, supplementing the inadequate conscious attitude with unconscious contents aims at a **wider personality whose center of gravity need not coincide with the ego**, and which may even thwart ego tendencies as insight increases. Like a magnet, the new center attracts the "signs of the Father," everything belonging to the original, unalterable character of the individual ground-plan, older than the ego, acting toward it as the "blessed, nonexistent God" of the Basilidians acted toward the archon of the Ogdoad, the demiurge (and as the demiurge's son toward his father: the son knows the message from above and can tell his father he is not the highest God) [pp.207-208, par. 297].
- **The paradox of the self in individuation:** on one hand the self appears a priori in the products of the unconscious in circle and quaternity symbols, possibly in earliest childhood dreams; on the other hand only patient work on unconscious contents and the resultant synthesis of conscious and unconscious data lead to a "totality" that again uses circle and quaternity symbols; in this phase the original childhood dreams are remembered and understood. Alchemists expressed this paradox with the **uroboros** [p.208, par. 297].

### The unconscious God and the history of the God-image [pp.208-212, pars. 298-305]
- **Gnostics knew of an unconscious.** Epiphanius cites a Valentinian letter: "In the beginning the Autopator contained in himself everything that is, in a state of unconsciousness (*agnosia*)" (pointed out by G. Quispel). The "Father" in Hippolytus is *anennoetos* and *anousios*, "neither male nor female": not only unconscious and without being but *nirdvandva*, without opposites and qualities, hence unknowable, which describes the state of the unconscious. The Valentinian Autopator is more positive: the ageless Aeon, eternally young, male and female, containing everything, contained by nothing; with him *Ennoia*, which is the latent possibility of consciousness, since differentiation of consciousness comes only with the later syzygies and tetrads (processes of conjunction and composition) [pp.208-209, par. 298].
- **Paul's *agnoia*.** Acts 17:30: God overlooked ("despising") the times of ignorance. Gnostic tradition: the highest God, seeing the miserable unconscious creatures the demiurge had made (unable even to walk upright), began the redemption. *Metanoia* (change of mind) took on the moral character of repentance (Vulgate *poenitentiam agere*); the "sin" to be repented is *agnoia*, unconsciousness (pseudo-Clement's "sin of unconsciousness" for the man born blind). Per the Gnostics even the God is without consciousness; in line with the Christian view that God changed from the God of wrath to the God of Love (Caussin). Riwkah Scharf: Satan's figure in the Old Testament changes with Yahweh's, so the God-image was differentiated even within the Old Testament. The Rig-Veda (X, 129) likewise portrays the world-creating Deity as not conscious ("whether he made it or not, he alone... knows, or does not know") [pp.209-210, par. 299-300]. A footnote cites Hermetica's krater filled with Nous in which those who strive after consciousness can baptize themselves.
- **Eckhart** [pp.210-211, par. 301]: the Godhead has no predicable qualities except unity and being; it "is becoming"; it is an absolute coincidence of opposites ("of forms formless; of becoming becomingless; of beings beingless; of things thingless"). Jung: union of opposites is equivalent to unconsciousness, since consciousness presupposes subject/object differentiation; where there is no "other," consciousness ceases. Only the Father, "welling" out of the Godhead, "notices himself," becomes "beknown to himself," confronts himself as a Person; the Son is the Father's thought of his own being. The man who lives in God is likewise unconscious: in "The Poor in Spirit," he stands "empty of his own knowledge as he was when he was not anything." Love God "as he is: a not-God, a not-spirit, a not-person, a not-image; as a sheer, pure, clear One... let us sink eternally, from nothing to nothing."
- Eckhart knew without discursive knowledge the primordial mystical experience of India and the Gnostics, "the finest flower on the tree of the 'Free Spirit'"; his writings lay buried for six hundred years ("his time was not yet come") [p.212, par. 302]. [note: the text reads "beginning of the eleventh century" for the Free Spirit; this is the source's wording.]
- **God-image and consciousness.** These statements express transformations of the God-image that **run parallel with changes in human consciousness**, without being able to say which causes which. "The God-image is not something invented, it is an experience that comes upon man spontaneously." The unconscious God-image can alter the state of consciousness, and conversely. This says nothing verifiable about the "prime truth," the unknown God; psychologically, the idea of the unconscious God (*agnosia*, *anennoetos theos*) identifies the Deity with the **numinosity of the unconscious** (so too the atman/purusha philosophy and Eckhart) [p.212, par. 303].

### What psychology can and cannot say [pp.212-214, pars. 304-305]
- Psychology must refrain from metaphysical judgments. It can only establish the presence of pictorial symbols, whose interpretation is not fixed beforehand; that they have the character of "wholeness" and presumably mean wholeness; they are "uniting" symbols representing the conjunction of one or two pairs of opposites, giving a **dyad or quaternion**. They arise from the **collision of conscious and unconscious** and the resulting confusion ("chaos" or "nigredo"), empirically restlessness and disorientation; the circle and quaternity then appear as a **compensating principle of order** showing the union of warring opposites as already accomplished, easing the way to a quieter state ("salvation") [pp.212-213, par. 304]. (Footnote: a scientist's conscience does not allow asserting what cannot be proved; "What he says, is" is God's prerogative.)
- Symbols of wholeness use images that in all religions express the universal "Ground," the Deity: the circle; the cross and quaternity in all forms (Ezekiel's vision, Rex gloriae with four evangelists, Gnostic Barbelo "God in four," Kolorbas "all four"); duality (tao, hermaphrodite, father-mother); the human form (child, son, anthropos) and the individual personality (Christ, Buddha) [p.213].
- That the goal is called "God" shows its **numinous** character; naive minds make no distinction between God and the experienced image. Example: Romanesque pictures of the Son of Man with three angels with animal heads and one with a human head: psychologically the dilemma of one differentiated and three undifferentiated functions; traditionally it means the second Person of the Godhead in four-fold aspect. Psychology can only note that the same symbols, including the one-and-three dilemma, appear spontaneously and refer to the psychic totality of the individual, indicating an archetype whose derivative seems to be the **quaternity of functions** orienting consciousness. Since the totality exceeds consciousness indefinitely it includes the unconscious and hence all archetypes; archetypes are complementary equivalents of the "outside world" and therefore have a **"cosmic" character**, explaining their numinosity and "godlikeness" [pp.213-214, par. 305].

### Gnostic symbols of the universal "Ground" [pp.214-219, pars. 306-313]
- Psychology takes the "Ground" as an image of the unconscious background and begetter of consciousness. Chief image: the **demiurge**. Each of the many Gnostic symbols is another aspect of one transcendental idea [p.214, par. 306].
- Irenaeus: **Sophia** represents the world of the **Ogdoad** (double quaternity); as a dove she descended into the water and begot **Saturn**, identical with Yahweh (the "other sun," sol niger of alchemy, here the "primus Anthropus"), who made the first man, who could only crawl like a worm. Naassene demiurge Esaldaios, "a fiery god, the fourth by number," against the Trinity of Father, Mother, Son; the highest Father is the Archanthropos, the higher Adam; Sophia sometimes replaces the Protanthropos; the Ebionites identified Adam with Christ; in Theodor Bar-Kuni the original man is the five elements (4+1); the dragon of the Acts of Thomas says "I am the son of him that hurt and smote the four brethren which stood upright" [pp.214-215, par. 307]. (Long footnote: Prunicos/Barbelo; Helen of Simon Magus as soul held captive by lower powers, a forerunner of the alchemical "soul in fetters"; Sophia Achamoth's fall into chaos in Pistis Sophia; Saviour compounded of four.)
- The quaternity coalesces with the demiurge or Anthropos, "victim of his own creative act," caught in Physis's embrace on descending. The anima mundi or Original Man latent in matter expresses a **transconscious center** that, from its quaternary and round character, is a symbol of wholeness (perhaps conscious + unconscious), though historically always used as God-image. Psychology "can only establish that the symbolism of psychic wholeness coincides with the God-image, but it can never prove that the God-image is God himself, or that the self takes the place of God" [pp.215-216, par. 308].
- **Egyptian Heb-Sed** (Colin Campbell's description): the king ascends a four-sided pavilion with four staircases, takes a throne, turns to the four cardinal points, sometimes makes offerings to himself, the climax of deification. All kingship is rooted in this psychology: for the anonymous individual every king carries the **symbol of the self**, his insignia show him as the cosmic Anthropos (homo maximus, Swedenborg) [p.216, pars. 309-310].
- **Naassene point** (Hippolytus): the "undivided point," the "grain of mustard seed" that grows into the kingdom of God; present in the body, known only to the *pneumatikoi* (spiritual) as opposed to the *psychikoi* and *hylikoi*; the "utterance of God" and the "matrix of the Aeons, Powers, Intelligences, Gods, Angels... of the Years, Moons, Days, Hours"; "being nothing and consisting of nothing" it becomes "a certain magnitude incomprehensible by thought." Hippolytus complains that they bundle everything together and call him "polymorphous Attis," "shepherd of white stars" (synonyms: Adonis, Osiris, Adam, Korybas, Pan, Bacchus) [pp.216-217, par. 310].
- **Naas the serpent** is the Naassenes' central deity, the "moist substance" (Thales), "contains within itself, like the horn of the one-horned bull, the beauty of all things," pervades everything like the water out of Eden dividing into four sources; Eden is the brain; three rivers are sensory functions (sight, hearing, smell), the fourth, the Euphrates (mouth), as the **fourth function** has a double significance (bodily nourishment, and "gladdens, feeds, forms the spiritual perfect man"). "The fourth" is special, ambivalent, daimonion (cf. Daniel 3:24f, the fourth in the furnace) [p.217, par. 311].
- The Euphrates water is the "water above the firmament," living water, with magnetic properties, that from which olive yields oil and grape wine; Hippolytus: "that man is without honor in the world," the **perfect man**, *rhema theou* (the Word). The water, or "this Christ," is a **panspermia**, a matrix of all possibilities from which the *pneumatikos* chooses "his Osob" (his idiosyncrasy; note: a link to the Bogomil word in the fish chapter) that "flies to him more quickly than iron to the magnet." The spiritual men attain their proper nature by entering through the "true door," Jesus Makarios (blessed), obtaining knowledge of their wholeness: the images of the "strait gate" and John 14:6 blend. This is an **integration process characteristic of psychological individuation**; water coalesces with Christ and Christ with the inner man, which Jung calls a psychologically correct formulation: Christ as Word is the living water and the symbol of the inner complete man, the self [pp.217-218, par. 312; the Osob link is in note 49].
- For the Naassenes the universal Ground is the **Original Man, Adam**; knowledge of him is "the beginning of perfection and the bridge to knowledge of God" ("knowledge of God is perfect wholeness"). He is male/female; "father and mother" come from him; he has three parts: rational (*noeron*), psychic, earthly (*choikon*), which "came down together into one man, Jesus," so Jesus is related to the Original Man (Christ as second Adam); his soul is "of three parts and (yet) one." Examples: the Cabiri and Oannes (whose suffering soul let the "figure of the great, most beautiful and perfect man, humbled to a slave" suffer); "the kingdom of heaven which is to be sought within man," even "in children of seven years" (Hippocrates: a boy at seven is half a father). The Naassenes place "the procreative nature of the Whole in the procreative seed," not merely a sexual theory since human procreative power is a special instance of the Whole's procreative nature; this is the "hidden and mystical Logos," likened to the phallus of Osiris ("Osiris is water"). The seed is the cause of all things without partaking of their nature: "I become what I will, and I am what I am"; he who moves all is himself unmoved; he alone is good (Matt. 19:17). Another synonym is the ithyphallic Hermes Kyllenios: Hermes is the Logos, interpreter and fashioner of what has been, is and will be, worshipped as phallus because he has "an urge from below upwards" [pp.218-220, par. 313].

### The vision of Mary and the symbolic reading of obscene dream-imagery [pp.220-225, pars. 314-323]
- Christ himself was drawn into sexual symbolism: **Interrogationes maiores Mariae** (via Epiphanius): Christ took Mary to a mountain, produced a woman from his side and had intercourse with her, partaking of the flowing semen to show "that this was to be done, that we might have life." Mary fell to the ground; Christ replied, "Wherefore do you doubt me, O you of little faith?" (John 3:12; John 6:54). The symbolism offended third and fourth century Christians, especially when read concretistically by certain sects [p.220, par. 314].
- Jung's reading: possibly based on a visionary experience of the kind occurring in treatment today; an image of a **psychologem** that cannot be formulated rationally and uses a concrete symbol, as dream does during the *abaissement du niveau mental* in sleep. Such "shocking" surprises in dreams should always be taken **"as-if,"** even when clothed in sensual imagery stopping at no scurrility; they do not really mean offense, "as if they were stammering in their efforts to express the elusive meaning that grips the dreamer's attention." (Footnote: Jung disagrees with Freud's idea that dreams disguise for "moral" reasons, since they often do the opposite; he inclines to the alchemical view of Mercurius, the unconscious Nous, as a trickster; he rejects the Freudian method that dismisses the manifest content as a facade.) The context (John 3:12) shows the image is symbolic, not concretistic: "a mystery" not because it is hidden but because its meaning is still hidden from consciousness [pp.220-221, par. 315].
- **Interpretation by Jung's heuristic**, step 1: the **mountain** is ascent, the mystical, spiritual ascent to the place of revelation. Step 2: the Christ figure in Christian Gnosticism is God as Archanthropos (Original Man = Adam), epitome of man, "Man and the Son of Man," the inner man reached through self-knowledge, "the kingdom of heaven within..." [pp.221-222].
- **Step 2, continued** [pp.221-222, par. 318]: as the Anthropos he corresponds to the empirically most important archetype and, as judge of the living and the dead and king of glory, to the real organizing principle of the unconscious: the **quaternity, or squared circle, of the self**. Jung says this does no violence to the text, since mandala structures have the meaning and function of a center of the unconscious personality. The quaternity of Christ is exemplified by the cross, the *rex gloriae*, and Christ as the year. (Footnote on the Freudian method: the Parthenon's remarkable feature is not that it is stone built to gratify Athenian ambition, but that it *is* the Parthenon.)
- **Step 3** [p.222, pars. 319-320]: producing the woman from his side makes Christ the **second Adam** and plays the Creator of Genesis; Adam was held in various traditions to be male/female before Eve, so Christ here shows his **androgyny** drastically (three interpretations of Christ are contaminated together here, which is characteristic of all unconscious image-formation). The Original Man is usually hermaphroditic (in the Vedic tradition he produces his own feminine half and unites with her); in Christian allegory the woman from Christ's side is the Church as Bride of the Lamb. **The splitting of the Original Man into husband and wife expresses an act of nascent consciousness: it gives birth to a pair of opposites and thereby makes consciousness possible.**
- **Compensation reading.** Unconscious processes compensate a definite conscious situation, so the splitting compensates a conscious state of unity: the Anthropos, Origen's "Vir Unus," then at the forefront of religious interest. If the recipient (Mary) was a woman, what she missed in Christ's pure, deified masculinity was the counterbalancing femininity: "I am both, man and woman." This survives in the Catholic notion of Christ's androgyny ("Virgo de Virgine," a *sententia communis*); medieval iconography sometimes shows Christ with breasts (Song of Solomon 1:1). **Mechthild of Magdeburg** has a parallel vision: she is on a "rocky mountain" where the Virgin awaits the divine child, which she embraces and kisses three times; the mountain is the "spiritualis habitus"; the Son is the *medulla*, innermost core of the Father's heart, "strengthening, healing and most sweet," and "the innermost [core] of the soul is that sweetest thing," so the medulla equals the Father's heart, the Son, and the inner man; psychologically "that sweetest thing" is the **self, indistinguishable from the God-image** [pp.222-223, par. 320-321].
- **Difference between the two visions** [pp.223-224, par. 321]: the antique one has Eve born from Adam on the spiritual level of the second Adam (the feminine pneuma or second Eve, the soul, as Christ's daughter; the soul as Church); Mechthild's continues the sacred myth: the daughter-bride becomes a mother and **bears the Father as the Son**. The Son's quaternary character is emphasized (a "fourfold voice," four kinds of pulse in his heart, four rays from his face; the quaternity refers to the four gospels). Eckhart: "God is born from the soul"; in Angelus Silesius God and the self coincide absolutely. The procreative power no longer proceeds from God; **God is born from the soul**: the myth of the young dying god has taken psychological form, a sign of further assimilation and conscious realization.
- **Step 4: the hieros gamos on the mountain** [pp.224-225, pars. 322-323]. The hermaphrodite in old alchemical pictures likes elevated places; the alchemical coniunctio is incestuous (brother/sister or mother/son in changed times; the Egyptian Amen as Ka-mutef, "husband of his mother," and Mut as "mother of her father and daughter of her son"). **Self-copulation** recurs in descriptions of the world creator (God splits into masculine and feminine halves; the Indian Prajapati; the Heliopolitan creation text: "I had union with my clenched hand, I joined myself in an embrace with my shadow, I poured seed into my mouth"). In the vision world creation gives way to **spiritual renewal**: nothing visible is born; the taking of seed means nourishing life, "that we may live"; the "pouring out" (apporhoia) refers to a *logos spermatikos*, in gospel language living water "springing up into eternal life." The drastic naturalism points back to archaic forms of religion and forward to an early crude observation of Nature assimilating the archetype of man; this attempt continued to Kepler, who saw the Trinity underlying the structure of the universe (Jung credits W. Pauli's lecture) [p.225].

### Hippolytus's Naassene list continued: Hermes, the stone, the marriage quaternio [pp.226-229, pars. 324-331]
- **Hermes** is a conjurer (*psychagogos*), guide (*psychopompos*) and begetter of souls; souls "brought down from the blessed Man on high, the archman Adamas, into the form of clay" to serve the demiurge **Esaldaios, "a fiery god, the fourth by number"** (equivalent to Ialdabaoth, "child of chaos," also Saturn): the "fourth" is the devil, opposed to the Trinity; Goethe's "strange son of chaos" fits [p.226, par. 325].
- With his golden wand Hermes puts the dead to sleep and wakes sleepers (Ephesians 5:14). As the alchemists made Christ the cornerstone their *lapis philosophorum*, the Naassenes make it their Protanthropos, the "inner man," a stone fallen from Adamas; alchemists say the stone is "cut from the mountain without hands" (Daniel 2); the Naassenes say the same of the inner man brought into "the form of oblivion" (*lethe*), in Epiphanius's version begotten "without human seed," "a small stone that becomes a great mountain." Jung's footnote: the inner man is buried in the somatic man, the "soul in fetters"; **Lethe corresponds to the modern concept of the unconscious** [pp.226-227, par. 326].
- The Archanthropos is the Logos whom souls follow "twittering" like bats following Hermes in the nekyia, to Oceanus, "the doors of Helios and the land of dreams"; Hermes is Oceanus, begetter of gods and men, ebbing and flowing; men born from the ebb, gods from the flow ("I have said, you are gods": Psalm 82). Identity of God and man is explicit in Scripture and Naassene teaching [p.227, par. 327].
- **The Naassene triad:** the blessed nature of the Man on high (Adamas); the mortal nature of the lower man; the "kingless race begotten from above," including Mariam (the sought-for one), Jothor (Jethro, the great wise one), Sephora (Zipporah, the seer) and Moses "whose generation was not in Egypt" (Egypt = the body). The four form a **marriage quaternio** of the classic type, with husband and wife on one diagonal, sister and brother on the other; synonyms: mother/father, queen/king, the unknown woman/the distant lover, **anima/animus** [pp.227-228, par. 328]. (Footnote: the cross-cousin marriage of primitive societies corresponds on a primitive level.)
- Application: Moses is the husband, Sephora the wife, Mariam the sister, Jothor the wise old man (father-animus if a woman's quaternio; "the great wise one" suggests a man's). The brother-sister incest relation is lacking; Miriam has some mother significance for Moses and, as a prophetess, a "magical" quality (stricken "white as snow" with leprosy when Moses took the Ethiopian wife), making her not unsuited for the anima; the Shulamite says "I am black, but comely"; in the *Chymical Wedding* the royal bride is the concubine of the Moorish king; Ethiopians in alchemy synonymize the *caput corvi* and nigredo [p.228, par. 329].
- **Triad names** (possibly onomatopoetic, from Isaiah 28:10): Kaulakau (the higher Adam), Saulasau (lower mortal man), Zeesar (the "upwards-flowing Jordan," which Jesus made flow upstream: the rising flood, begetter of gods). This is "the human hermaphrodite in all creatures," called by the ignorant Geryon of the threefold body and by the Greeks the celestial horn of the moon. The quaternio equals Zeesar, the hermaphrodite, Geryon, the moon's horn, and the cosmogonic Logos (John 1) and the "generation of perfect men" (*teleioi anthropoi*) [pp.228-229, par. 330].
- The Logos/quaternity is "the cup from which the king, drinking, draws his omens" (Genesis 44:5), the beaker of Anacreon, the wine of Cana ("kingdom of heaven within us like the wine in the cup"), the ithyphallic gods of Samothrace and Kyllenian Hermes, both the Original Man and the reborn spiritual man, "consubstantial" with him. Hence Christ's saying to eat his flesh and drink his blood: he knew each disciple's individual nature and the need "to come to his own special nature" [p.229, par. 331].

### Korybas, the *teleios*, the Paracelsian Mumia [pp.229-232, pars. 332-336]
- **Korybas** descends from the crown of the head and unformed brain like the Euphrates from Eden and permeates all things; his image exists unrecognized "in earthly form"; the god dwelling in the flood. Corybants were thought to be the original men; the name denotes an anonymous member of a collective (Curetes, Cabiri, Dactyls); here the single Kyllenian Hermes whom "the Thracians call Korybas"; Jung suggests contamination with the phallic Korybas of the Dionysus legend [pp.229-230, par. 332].
- **"Perfect" man (teleios).** From the center of the perfect man flows the ocean where the god dwells; he is the "true door" through which the perfect man must pass to be reborn. Why would the perfect need rebirth? Jung: the perfect man is not perfected beyond improvement; compare Paul (Phil. 3:12 "not that I am already perfect," v. 15 "as many as are perfect"); *teleios* amounts to *pneumatikos* ('spiritual') with no specific degree; applied to a man in need of rebirth it can mean at most "whole" or "complete." Alchemists: "Perfectum non perficitur." (Footnote glosses: *teleios* = complete, having reached the destined goal; mature; initiated; one who has received Nous and has gnosis; Weiss: "the consciousness of imperfection and the will to progress is the sign of perfection.") [pp.230-231, par. 333].
- The father of the perfectus is the higher man or Protanthropos, "not clearly formed," "without qualities," called **Papa (Attis)** by the Phrygians; a bringer of peace who quells "the war of the elements" in the human body (medieval alchemy's filius philosophorum "makes peace between enemies or the elements"); he is also called *nekys* (corpse), buried in the body like a mummy in a tomb [p.231, par. 334].
- **Paracelsus's Mumia.** *De vita longa*: life is "a kind of embalmed mummy" preserving the mortal body; the body lives from the Mumia, through which the "peregrinus microcosmus" (wandering microcosm) rules; synonyms Adech, Archeus, Protothoma, Ides, Idechtrum, Protoplast, "the door whence all created things have come" (cf. the "true door"); the Mumia is born with the body and sustains it, though less than the "supercelestial Mumia," which corresponds to the Naassene higher Adam; in the Ides "there is but One Man... the Protoplast." The Mumia holds the powers of herbs, trees, water, metals, stones. It corresponds to the **Original Man forming the microcosm in mortal man** and sharing the macrocosm's powers. Compare the cabalistic **Metatron**: in the Zohar the Messiah is the "central column" of the Sephiroth, "created and constituted to be his image and likeness," including all gradations and binding them in the center [pp.231-232, par. 335].
- **The gate of heaven.** The dead man rises by passing through the "door of heaven": Jacob's gate of heaven on his way to Mesopotamia, which the Naassenes read as the stream of the great ocean flowing from the midst of the perfect man. The stream is the Oceanus flood-tide that generates gods; John 7:38 ("rivers of living water" from the belly) draws on a nonbiblical source. Origen: whoever drinks becomes a fountain springing up to eternal life; the "higher" water is the *aqua doctrinae*, rivers from Christ's belly, versus the "lower" water, *aqua abyssi*, where dwell the Prince of this world and the dragon; Christ is the river pouring into the world through the four gospels like the rivers of Paradise. Jung cites these ecclesiastical allegories to show how saturated Gnostic symbolism is with the Church's language; for Origen the "spiritual inner man" corresponds cosmically to the first-created heaven (Homily I on Genesis). The examples illustrate **how closely the religious teachings of the first two centuries were connected with psychic facts** [pp.232-233, par. 336 and following].

### Aipolos/Proteus, Eleusis, and the lower path [pp.234-235, pars. 338-339]
- The Original Man in his latent state (*acharaktēristos*, 'unformed') is called **Aipolos**, not for goat-herding but as *aei polōn*, "ever turning," the **Pole** that turns the cosmos, recalling alchemical Mercurius at the North Pole. The Naassenes also name him **Proteus** (Odyssey: the Old Man of the Sea who tells the truth, knows the sea's depths) [p.234, par. 338].
- **Proteus as a personification of the unconscious** (with trickster traits): hard to catch ("he might see me first"), must be seized quickly and held to make him speak; though sea-dwelling he comes ashore at noon to sleep among his **seals, warm-blooded**, i.e., contents capable of becoming conscious that appear spontaneously in the light world of consciousness; he teaches the hero the way home "over the fish-giving sea" (a **psychopomp**), "does not let himself be rolled," spins and changes shape like a revolving image that cannot be grasped; what he says is infallible. Hence the Naassenes: "knowledge of the complete man is deep indeed and hard to comprehend" [pp.234-235].
- **Eleusis.** Proteus is likened to the green ear of corn in the Eleusinian mysteries ("The Mistress has borne the divine boy, Brimo has borne Brimos!"). The "lower" correspondence is Persephone's dark path to "the grove of adored Aphrodite, who rouses the sickness of love": men should keep to this lower path to be initiated into the "great and heavenly" mysteries, "the gate of heaven" and "the house of God," destined only for the spiritual men, who must put off their garments and become bridegrooms "robbed of their virility by the virgin spirit" (Rev. 14:4). Footnote 132: prototypes are the emasculated Attis and Eleusis priests made impotent with hemlock [p.235, par. 339].
- **The Indian temple pundit anecdote** (footnote 131, Turukalukundram, southern India): a Tantrist pundit explained that temples were covered with obscene sculptures to remind ordinary people of their sexuality and **dharma**: those who tread the spiritual path without preparation would be carried off by Yama, the god of death; only after fulfilling dharma can one go the spiritual way; only those qualified by karma can ignore the injunction; the two seductresses at the temple entrance lure people to their dharma; the temple represents the whole world, so most sculptures are erotic; the lingam stands in the *garbha griha*, "house of the womb" [p.235].

### The indivisible point, the Monad, the spark [pp.236-239, pars. 340-346]
- Among the objective symbols of the self is the Naassene **indivisible point** (*atomos*), which accords with the **Monad and Son of Man in Monoimos**. Hippolytus on Monoimos: there is a Man like Oceanus, the All, source of the universe, unbegotten, incorruptible, everlasting; there is a Son of the Man, begotten and capable of suffering, whose birth is outside time. The Man is "a single Monad, uncompounded and indivisible, yet compounded and divisible; loving and at peace with all things yet warring with all things and at war with itself in all things; unlike and like itself," like a musical harmony; "its own mother, its own father." The emblem of the perfect Man is the **iota, the jot or tittle**: "many-faced, thousand-eyed and thousand-named." The Son of the Man is the one jot flowing from on high, filling all things, containing in himself everything in the Man. Jung: a Gnostic paraphrase of John 1 and a meaningful exposition of the psychological self (footnote: the relation of the iota to the self is that of the Hebrew Yod to the lapis in the cabala; Adam is the small hook at the top of the Yod) [p.236, par. 340]. This paradoxical Monad describes the psychological nature of the self as conceived by a second-century thinker under the influence of the Christian message [p.237, par. 341].
- **Plotinus** (*Enneads* VI, 9, 8): self-knowledge shows that the soul's natural movement is not straight but **circular around a center**, the principle from which she proceeds; divinity consists in being attached to the center; one who withdraws from it has remained un-unified, or is a brute. Jung: the point is the center of a circle created by the soul's circumambulation, "the center of all things," a God-image, still underlying the **mandala symbols in modern dreams** [p.237, pars. 342-343].
- **The spark (*spinther*, *scintilla*).** Common among the Gnostics, corresponding to Eckhart's "little spark of the soul" (*scintilla vitae*); in Saturninus; Heraclitus's soul as "spark of stellar essence"; in the Sethians the "very small spark" of light in thrall in the dark waters below; Simon Magus: a very small spark in semen and milk "increases and becomes a power boundless and immutable." Footnotes: the *spinther* is the higher element some humans carry from the world of light, letting them rise above the world of the Seven; alchemical echoes (the King swimming in the sea crying for rescue, Maier; *Aurora consurgens*); a modern vision (Wickes) of the Lonely One hanging in void who draws all solitude to himself until a spark is born in the dark, rises, and becomes a star; Zoroaster drawing down sparks from a star that scorched him [pp.237-238, par. 344].
- **The point in alchemy** [pp.238-239, par. 345]: the arcane substance (Maier: "purity or homogeneity of the essence"); the *punctum solis* in the egg yolk that grows into a chick (Turba; Ruska reads "saliens," 'springing,' for "solis," which Jung doubts); Khunrath's Sapientia as "salt-point"; gold in Maier; the scholiast of the *Tractatus aureus*: the midpoint, *circulus exiguus* and "mediator," reconciling hostile elements and "by persistent rotation" changing the square into a circle like itself; Dorn: the *punctum vix intelligibile*, starting point of creation; John Dee: all things originated from the point and monad (God both center and circumference); Mylius: the bird of Hermes; the *Novum lumen*: spirit and fire, the life of the arcane substance, like the spark. Essentially the same as the Gnostic conception.
- **Closing of the chapter** [p.239, par. 346]: Christ was assimilated to symbols that also meant the kingdom of God (mustard seed, hidden treasure, pearl of great price); he and his kingdom have the same meaning. Objections to this "dissolution of Christ's personality" overlook that it represents an **assimilation and integration of Christ into the human psyche**, visible in the growth of personality and development of consciousness. These attainments are now "gravely threatened in our antichristian age," not only by sociopolitical delusional systems but above all by **rationalistic hybris tearing consciousness from its transcendent roots and holding before it immanent goals** [p.239]. (Footnote: a theologian is cited: "Jesus is still in the making.")
