# 1 · ⭐⭐ PERMAFROST — **ground that is rock until it is soup**

> *(Ch. 7, Glaciers — verbatim)*
> *"**Frozen permafrost is nearly as hard as rock. It can be built upon with confidence. However, if warmed, it
> becomes soupy with a lot of melted water and is not safe to use as a construction base.**"*
>
> *"Permafrost is the layer of ground that remains frozen solid year-round. The upper section of this layer is
> called the **permafrost table**, growing and shrinking with seasonal temperature changes."*
>
> *"Because the melting of frozen ground produces unstable surfaces, understanding permafrost is important to
> civil engineering and architecture in cold regions."*
>
> *"Soil located below the permafrost layer is **not** frozen, but heated from below by the geothermal energy of
> the Earth's mantle and core."*

| What this establishes | |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Load-bearing capacity is a function of TEMPERATURE, not of material** | The same ground is a foundation or a liquid depending on its thermal state |
| ⭐ **The failure is a PHASE CHANGE, not a gradual weakening** | It holds, it holds, it holds — and then it does not. **There is no degraded middle state to notice** |
| **The frozen layer is a SLAB between two unfrozen zones** | A seasonally-thawing active layer above; geothermally-warmed ground below |
| **The permafrost table MOVES seasonally** | The boundary is not fixed |

## ⭐⭐ THE ENGINEERING RESPONSE, AND IT IS A STANDING OBLIGATION

> *"When the Trans-Alaska oil pipeline was built, the underground sections **had to be refrigerated** in order to
> avoid thawing the permafrost. Pipeline supports in the elevated sections are made so that **heat is released
> and doesn't affect the permafrost**."*

> ### ***The structure is a heat source, and the ground is what it threatens. Building at all creates a permanent, continuous obligation to manage your own waste heat — one that did not exist before you built.***

**❓ QUESTIONS THIS RAISES:** *what does a settlement owe its own ground, forever, simply for being warm? Who
pays that, and is it visible as a cost to anyone — or does it read as ordinary maintenance?*

## ⭐ AND THE TECHNICAL TERM FOR THE SITUATION

> *"**Periglacial** areas are those bits of land that border a glacier area but are not covered by ice. These are
> cold… places, where soil and rock exist as permafrost and **only the top few centimeters warm enough to thaw
> during the summer months.** Plant life has a hard time gaining much of a toehold… It is made mostly of mosses
> and grasses."*

⭐ **"Periglacial" is the registered term for ice-free ground bordering ice** — and the active layer it describes
is **centimeters**, not meters.

# 2 · ⭐⭐⭐ FROST WEDGING — **and the cross-source synthesis neither book states alone**

> *(Ch. 8, Weathering — verbatim)*
> *"**Frost wedging** is a form of physical weathering… caused by the repeated freeze-thaw cycle of water in
> extreme climates. Freeze and thaw weathering is common when temperatures **drop below freezing at night and
> rise during the day**."*
>
> *"When it rains, rainwater collects **in the joints of rocks**. As the air cools… the water inside the joints
> freezes. As water freezes into ice, **it expands**."*
>
> *"**Frost wedging happens when rock is pushed apart by the alternate freezing and thawing of water in
> cracks.**"*
>
> *"Frost action is best seen in **wet** climates with many freezing and thawing cycles (arctic tundra, mountain
> peaks). Frost **splits rocks into blocks** by joint block separation and also **wears away the edges of blocks
> grain by grain, rounding the surfaces**."*

> ## ⭐⭐⭐ THE SYNTHESIS — **two sources, one mechanism, stated by neither**
> **`Groundwater_Geophysics_Extraction.md` §1 establishes**, from Kirsch: *in hard rock, groundwater lives **in
> the joints and fissures** — the rock body itself is "nearly impermeable material."*
> **This book establishes:** *frost wedging works **in the joints of rocks**, pushing them apart, splitting rock
> into blocks and rounding the edges grain by grain.*
>
> ### ***THE FRACTURE NETWORK IS BOTH THE ONLY WATER STORE AND THE THING BEING DESTROYED. The reservoir enlarges itself by breaking its own container.***
>
> ⭐ **Neither volume says this. It exists only in the pair** — which is `LAW 0-R`'s multi-pick convergence
> working exactly as it is specified to.
