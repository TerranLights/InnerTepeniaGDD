# 1 · ⭐⭐ WHERE WATER LIVES IN HARD ROCK — **in the cracks, not in the rock**

> **§12.1.1, Definition** *(verbatim)*:
> *"Groundwater can be found in pore spaces of unconsolidated and consolidated sedimentary rocks and weathering
> layers, **in joints and fissures of hard rock, in fault zones, and in karst caves.** Aquifers with water
> reservoir stored in pore spaces are called pore aquifers or porous aquifers. Similar conditions for
> geophysical exploration are in aquifers connected to joints and fissures of rocks, **e.g., originated by
> cooling of igneous rocks.** So, also this type of aquifer will be treated as pore aquifer, while **fault zones
> and karst caves, where the water reservoir is embedded in nearly impermeable material**, are treated in Chap.
> 13 'fracture zones and caves'."*

**What this establishes, physically:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **In crystalline/igneous rock, the reservoir is the FRACTURE NETWORK** — joints, fissures, fault zones | The rock body itself is **"nearly impermeable material"** |
| ⭐ **Cooling joints in igneous rock are explicitly named as a reservoir type** | A granite's capacity to hold water is a property of **how it cracked**, not of what it is made of |
| **A weathering layer above hard rock is a separate, shallower reservoir** | §2, Fig. 2.6: *"Seismic structure of the weathering zone in a hard rock area"* — a real, mapped, distinct zone |

> ### ⚠ AND A PROPERTY THAT MATTERS FOR ANYONE TRYING TO FIND IT
> *"Typical seismic velocities… more than 3000 m/s for hard rock. **Seismic velocities for fractured hardrock,
> however, can be similar to seismic velocities of saturated sand.**"*
> ⭐ ***Fractured rock and a genuine aquifer look alike from outside.*** **Distinguishing "there is water here"
> from "there is broken rock here" takes more than one method.**

**❓ QUESTIONS THIS RAISES FOR A LATER PHASE — not answered here:** *if a settlement's ground stores water only
in fractures, what does it have to know about its own rock that a settlement on sediment never has to learn?
And what happens to that knowledge when the fracture network is invisible from the surface?*

# 2 · ⭐⭐ THE COASTAL FRESHWATER LENS — **fresh water floats on salt, and the interface moves**

> **§14.2, Origin of saltwater intrusions** *(verbatim)*:
> *"When aquifers in coastal areas have hydraulic contact with seawater, seawater intrusions occur due to
> **higher density of saltwater**. In the aquifer, **freshwater is underlain by saltwater** leading to a more or
> less well defined **salt/freshwater interface**. Freshwater reservoirs on islands are lens[-shaped]…"*

**The book gives the governing relation** *(Ghyben-Herzberg form, §14.2)* — the interface depth follows from the
density contrast and the height of the water table above sea level:

```
ρ_salt · g · z   =   ρ_fresh · g · (z + h)
```

**And it distinguishes two states, in its own figures:**

| Figure | |
|---|---|
| **Fig. 14.1** | *"Freshwater lens on a small island"* |
| ⭐ **Fig. 14.2** | *"**Balanced** (top) and **disturbed** (below) salt/freshwater interface"* |

> ### ⭐ THE MECHANISM, STATED PLAINLY
> **A coastal freshwater body is not a pool. It is a LENS held in place by a density balance** — and that
> balance is **disturbable**. **The book's own framing is that saline groundwater is a water-QUALITY problem**,
> *"especially under arid climatic conditions"* **(§14.1)** — which is the climate class the extraction was run
> against.

**❓ QUESTIONS THIS RAISES:** *a lens has no visible edge and no gauge. What tells a community that its
interface has moved — and does it find out before or after the water changes?*
