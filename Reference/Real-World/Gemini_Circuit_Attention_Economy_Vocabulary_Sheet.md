## 1. Attention Economy — core apparatus

| Term | Real-world meaning | Candidate Circuit usage |
|---|---|---|
| **Attention Economy 1.0** | Print/broadcast-era mass conversion of attention into revenue (Tim Wu's term) | A plausible "old economy" baseline the Circuit's current system superseded |
| **Attention Economy 2.0** (Hershock's coinage) | Users are drafted into double duty as both consumers *and* producers of behavioral training data; described as a **Klein bottle** — wishing, granting the wish, and generating the next wish fused into one loop, a **"perpetual desire machine"** | The Circuit's core economic logic candidate — an information district whose residents/workers are simultaneously customers of and raw material for its own systems |
| **Iconic function** | Connected devices compared directly to religious icons — attract/transmit attention into an inaccessible computational "beyond"; efficacy "directly proportional to the depth and duration of the attention we lavish" on them | Flavor for how ordinary Circuit residents might relate to their own infrastructure — reverent, habitual engagement with devices/terminals as a mundane-sacred act |
| **Network effect** | Unlike hierarchies (value falls with distance from center), network value *increases* with size — structural bias toward monopoly, "winner takes all" via power-law "bounty without spread" | Candidate structural explanation for why the Circuit, as an information hub, would concentrate economic power rather than distribute it |
| **Epistemological power vs. ontological power** (Zwick & Knott) | Knowing/predicting people vs. manufacturing/producing them — "data mining doesn't just learn about people, it makes people" | A sharp two-tier distinction for Circuit factions/corporations: some merely surveil and predict, others actively shape behavior — a useful axis for differentiating rival Circuit institutions |
| **Surveillance capitalism** (Zuboff) / **expository society** (Harcourt) | Surveillance "woven into the very fabric of our pleasures and fantasies" — "the technologies that end up facilitating our surveillance are the very technologies we crave" | Tonal register for how surveillance in the Circuit could be genuinely desired/craved by residents, not just imposed |
| **Illustrative order-of-magnitude math** | ~30 billion attention-hours/day traded globally ≈ $54 trillion/year notional value (vs. ~$90 trillion real 2019 global GDP) | Useful only as a *reasoning template* for scaling a Circuit-wide attention economy's notional value relative to Concordia's real economy — not a number to import literally |

## 2. Karmic/algorithmic vocabulary — reusable for Circuit institutions and their victims/beneficiaries

All of these are Hershock's own coinages, real published terminology, directly reusable without alteration:

- **Digital karma** — algorithms functioning as arbiters of experienced reality; the recursive process by
  which a person's/robot's own expressed values reshape what they're subsequently offered
- **Karmic cul-de-sac / karmic isolation** — a friction-free, self-reinforcing informational loop offering
  "no moral resistance whatsoever" — explicitly *not* like imprisonment (no felt deprivation), "lusciously
  seductive." Strong candidate for describing a specific Circuit neighborhood, service, or subculture that
  has algorithmically walled itself into total comfort
- **Karmic cloning** — sophisticated predictive-analytics simulation of a person's intentional presence,
  built from their data trail
- **Karmic gene editing** — precisely-timed, targeted behavior modification via content
- **"Big Others"** and **colonization of consciousness** — vocabulary for large-scale institutional/state
  actors appropriating attention at population scale; usable for a Circuit-controlling faction or corporation

Cross-reference: the same terms are also proposed as robot-specific *personal* theology in
`Reference/Real-World/Ice-Cold_Buddhism_Research/09_Vocabulary_Sheet.md` §4. The two uses are compatible —
one is individual/religious, this one is institutional/economic — and can coexist in the same setting without
contradiction.

## 3. Buddhist reframing of poverty — an alternative to resource-scarcity economics

**Directly reusable for any district's economic-underclass material, not Circuit-specific**, but flagged here
because the Circuit's information-economy framing makes it an unusually natural fit. Poverty redefined as
**"blockage-riddled interdependencies: the proliferation of duḥkha-generating impediments to relating
freely"** — not lack of income/resources, but "an affliction of broken or distorted interactions among 'the
wealthy' and 'the poor,'" relational rather than substance-based: **"the relational indignity of being forced
into a position of having nothing to offer to others that they would value."**

**Candidate application:** a Circuit underclass could be genuinely well-provisioned materially (universal
basic income, free bandwidth/access) while still experiencing real poverty in this relational sense — cut off
from any position to *give* rather than merely receive. A UBI-style Circuit welfare system could be written
as visibly, deliberately incomplete on exactly this axis, without needing to invent a new critique from
scratch.

## 4. Analytical tools — variety vs. diversity, relational equity

**Flagged in the source extraction as directly applicable to this project's own Cross-District-
Differentiation discipline — worth using as an actual audit lens, not just narrative flavor.**

- **Variety** — a purely quantitative measure of bare coexistence (a zoo: species merely coexist, externally/
  contingently related, dependent on outside inputs for survival)
- **Diversity** — a qualitative index of the *depth of interdependence* present (a savanna ecosystem: species
  internally/constitutively related, mutually dependent). **"Diversity is an index of ecosystem
  intelligence."**
- Applied to institutions: **"ethical plurality"** (many systems merely coexisting) is not the same as
  **"ethical diversity"** (systems actively differentiating *for* each other)

**Candidate audit question for the Circuit specifically:** does the district's information economy produce
genuine diversity (distinct communities/factions/services meaningfully depending on and shaping each other)
or merely variety (many superficially different services that are functionally interchangeable, all drawing
on the same underlying data/attention extraction)? A real risk for an information-district economy is that it
*looks* varied (many apps, many feeds, many providers) while being diversity-poor underneath — a sharp,
specific failure mode worth writing into the district rather than avoiding.

**Companion tool — relational equity** (from the education chapter, §10 of the extraction), a four-register
taxonomy that could double as an economic/institutional audit lens for the Circuit or any district:
**access equity** (who can participate at all), **operational equity** (day-to-day fairness within a system),
**structural equity** (resource distribution, formal/informal mobility limits), **contributory equity** (how
benefits extend beyond the immediate participants). Reframed as "a direction, not a destination" — useful for
writing an institution that is *improving* on this axis without being written as solved.

## 5. Governance/policy vocabulary — candidate in-world proposals or debates

Real proposed responses to attention-economy harms, from the extraction's data-governance chapter (§9) — all
explicitly named there as different **upāya** (skillful means), none sufficient alone. Usable as positions
real Circuit factions, politicians, or activist groups could actually hold and argue for/against each other:

- **Connectivity strikes** (collective, political, mass action — "data quarantine" denying algorithmic
  targeting data at a critical moment) vs. **connectivity fasting** (individual, wellness-framed, apolitical)
- **Data as public good/commons** vs. **data as private property** — with a real complication worth keeping:
  bigger commons are harder to govern collectively and their richest resources still require capital to
  reach, so "commons" framing doesn't automatically fix access inequality
- **Attention/data cap-and-trade or excise tax** — modeled on carbon schemes or sin taxes, with the
  structural nuance that data's harms occur at *extraction* (not downstream use, unlike carbon's combustion) —
  "less like mining and burning fossil fuels than cutting down and burning forests"

## 6. Caveats — what not to import uncritically

- **The book's own illustrations are real-world and dated** (Cambridge Analytica, GDPR, COVID-era EdTech) —
  useful for mechanism, not as Tepenian history to reference directly
- **No worked game mechanics here.** This is real-world policy argument, not a system for adjudicating
  in-world disputes — it supplies vocabulary and structure, not numbers, formulas, or institutional detail
  below the level of "here is a proposed policy"
- **Do not let this material converge the Circuit with Aquarius (The Labs) or Pisces (The Markets).** All
  three could plausibly touch "economy" or "information," but per this project's Cross-District-
  Differentiation discipline, each needs its own distinct answer — check that table before writing any of
  this into an actual district file
