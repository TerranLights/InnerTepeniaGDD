## 1. LEADS (grouped by purpose)

### A. Classic theory (verify-first items you asked about)

**A1. Obrdlik 1942, the origin of the term "gallows humor"** [VERIFIED-META]
- Obrdlik, A. J. (1942). "Gallows Humor"—A Sociological Phenomenon. *American Journal of Sociology*, 47(5), 709-716. DOI 10.1086/219002. https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/219002 (page returned 403 to me; record confirmed via Crossref + OpenAlex).
- Type: theory / sociological essay, based on Czech experience under Nazi occupation (not soldiers per se; oppressed civilians).
- Gives a writer: the founding claim that gallows humor arises in "precarious or dangerous" situations and is "an index of strength or morale." It has BOTH a positive effect (morale) AND a negative one: it is also aimed at others and works to disintegrate the target. That double edge (solidarity inward, corrosion outward) is directly usable for a character who jokes at the enemy and at the in-group's expense.
- Access: paywalled (UChicago/JSTOR). Abstract free.

**A2. Freud 1928 (written 1927), "Humor"** [VERIFIED-OPEN]
- Freud, S. (1928). Humor (trans. J. Riviere). *International Journal of Psycho-Analysis*, 9(1), 1-6. Read at https://www.freudedition.net/en/werke/humour/druckschrift
- Type: theory (primary text).
- Gives a writer: the canonical gallows example (the condemned man led out on a Monday: "Well, this is a good beginning to the week") and the claim that humor is "the triumph of narcissism, the ego's victorious assertion of its own invulnerability. It refuses to be hurt by the arrows of reality." Freud's mechanism: the sufferer shifts accent from the ego to a "super-ego" that treats the ego's troubles as trivial. Good for writing a character who jokes about their own wound or death from a position of detachment, and for the distinction between humor aimed at oneself versus at others.
- Access: free full text (freudedition.net).
- Companion: Freud, S. (1905). *Jokes and Their Relation to the Unconscious* (Standard Edition vol. 8). [VERIFIED-META: library catalog records only.] Source of the "tendentious joke" (hostile/obscene joke) concept: jokes as disguised aggression that bypass inhibition. Book; library/purchase.

**A3. Vaillant's defense-mechanism work (humor as a "mature" defense)** [VERIFIED-META]
- Vaillant, G. E. (1971). Theoretical hierarchy of adaptive ego mechanisms: a 30-year follow-up of 30 men selected for psychological health. *Archives of General Psychiatry*, 24(2), 107-118. DOI 10.1001/archpsyc.1971.01750080011003. (Crossref confirmed; search result page confirmed the topic.)
- Vaillant, G. E. (1977). *Adaptation to Life*. Boston: Little, Brown. (Kirkus review page opened: release Oct. 27, 1977; reports men from warm homes developed "mature mechanisms (suppression, humor)," those from difficult homes "immature mechanisms (fantasy, projection).")
- Vaillant, G. E. (2000). Adaptive mental mechanisms: Their role in a positive psychology. *American Psychologist*, 55(1), 89-98. DOI 10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.89 (Crossref confirmed).
- Type: theory / longitudinal clinical study (Grant Study of Harvard men, many of them WWII combat veterans).
- Gives a writer: the clinical framing that humor is on the same shelf as suppression, anticipation, altruism and sublimation: a defense that works because it keeps the painful thing in view while sparing the person its full affect. Useful for contrasting a character's "mature" humor with immature defenses (projection, acting out) and for the idea that the same man can use both.
- Access: 1971 and 2000 papers paywalled (abstracts free); book purchase / library.

**A4. Rod Martin's humor-styles work** [VERIFIED-META]
- Martin, R. A., Puhlik-Doris, P., Larsen, G., Gray, J., & Weir, K. (2003). Individual differences in uses of humor and their relation to psychological well-being: Development of the Humor Styles Questionnaire. *Journal of Research in Personality*, 37(1), 48-75. DOI 10.1016/S0092-6566(02)00534-2 (Crossref confirmed).
- Martin, R. A. (2007). *The Psychology of Humor: An Integrative Approach.* Burlington, MA: Elsevier Academic Press; and Martin, R. A., & Ford, T. E. (2018), 2nd ed., Academic Press (publisher and library pages confirmed via search results).
- Type: empirical (scale development) + textbook.
- Gives a writer: the four styles (affiliative, self-enhancing = adaptive; aggressive, self-defeating = maladaptive). A veteran character can be written as high on self-enhancing ("I can find the joke even now") and affiliative within the unit, while sliding into self-defeating when alone. Caveat from the military literature below: the HSQ was not built for gallows humor, and several authors note the "aggressive" style can still serve coping.
- Access: article paywalled; textbook purchase.

**A5. Kuiper 2012, humor as a resiliency process, with an explicit adaptive/maladaptive model** [VERIFIED-OPEN, read]
- Kuiper, N. A. (2012). Humor and resiliency: Towards a process model of coping and growth. *Europe's Journal of Psychology*, 8(3), 475-491. DOI 10.5964/ejop.v8i3.464. https://ejop.psychopen.eu/index.php/ejop/article/download/464/464.pdf
- Type: review / theory.
- Gives a writer: a clean statement of what gallows humor does in emergency work ("to relieve tension, vent their feelings, and cope," detach/distance the self, foster group cohesion "to ensure that personnel can act effectively") AND of its documented failure modes, which he lifts from case reports: "inappropriately masking emotions or pain, or alienating others by using cynical humor." Also cites the Finnish police longitudinal study in which officers high in humor were at greater cardiovascular/smoking risk (Kerkkänen, Kuiper & Martin, 2004), i.e., a funny, well-liked person can still be paying a physical cost.
- Access: free full text (CC BY).

**A6. Benign-violation theory** [VERIFIED-META]
- McGraw, A. P., & Warren, C. (2010). Benign violations: Making immoral behavior funny. *Psychological Science*, 21(8), 1141-1149. DOI 10.1177/0956797610376073 (Crossref + APS press release page in search results).
- Type: empirical + theory.
- Gives a writer: a working rule for when a dark joke lands vs. offends. Humor needs a violation that is simultaneously "benign," made so by (a) an alternative norm that makes it acceptable, (b) weak commitment to the violated norm, or (c) psychological distance. Explains why the same joke about a dead man is funny in the squad and monstrous to a stranger (or to the dead man's brother): distance and commitment differ. 
- Access: paywalled; popular summary free.

**A7. Joking cultures and in-group boundary** [VERIFIED-META]
- Fine, G. A., & De Soucey, M. (2005). Joking cultures: Humor themes as social regulation in group life. *Humor*, 18(1), 1-22. DOI 10.1515/humr.2005.18.1.1 (Crossref/OpenAlex abstract read).
- Type: theory.
- Gives a writer: "Every interacting social group develops... a joking culture: a set of humorous references that are known to members." Explains recurring in-jokes, nicknames and callbacks as a group's memory, and why a newcomer can't just join in. Johais and Molendijk (below) build their military analyses on this.
- Access: paywalled.

**A8. Humor and status** [VERIFIED-OPEN, PDF header; full text open on Wharton site]
- Bitterly, T. B., Brooks, A. W., & Schweitzer, M. E. (2017). Risky business: When humor increases and decreases status. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 112(3), 431-455. DOI 10.1037/pspi0000079. https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/2016-54537-001.pdf
- Type: empirical (8 experiments).
- Gives a writer: successful humor signals confidence and competence and raises status; an inappropriate attempt signals confidence but low competence and costs status. Useful for the "who is allowed to say that" problem (the new guy who makes the dead-comrade joke before earning it).
- Access: free PDF.

### B. Military-specific: ethnography, sociology, anthropology (best fit for the writing)

**B1. Molendijk 2025, "murky humor" in military culture** [VERIFIED-OPEN, read in full]
- Molendijk, T. (2025). Humor in dirty work with high-impact ambiguities and paradoxes: The looking glass of military jokes and laughter. *Public Anthropologist*, 7(1), 1-28. DOI 10.1163/25891715-bja10066. Open PDF via https://hdl.handle.net/2066/317974 (Radboud repository, CC BY 4.0).
- Type: anthropological/theoretical synthesis from soldier accounts (Dutch, US, Israeli, Danish, Iraqi, German material).
- Gives a writer: the single best theoretical treatment of WHAT military humor is like. Three recurring styles: black humor, self-deprecation (collective "us idiots"), and understatement. Specific documented patterns: soldiers' "dehumanization" jokes are aimed mainly at the SOLDIERS THEMSELVES, not the enemy; laughter at something genuinely comic and awful (a quoted soldier laughing at a man blown in half, then ashamed of it, "it's totally misplaced... but it just looked genuinely funny"); drinking from a prosthetic leg as a ritual. Most useful for limits: when the soldier no longer recognizes the paradox (the Abu Ghraib photos), humor shifts from incongruity to superiority, "black humor transforms into outright Schadenfreude, self-deprecation turns into actual self-dehumanization, and humorous understatement changes into moral numbness." Author also writes on moral injury, so this ties humor to moral injury directly.
- Access: free full text.

**B2. Johais 2025, "Schlagfertigkeit" as a soldier skill** [VERIFIED-OPEN, read]
- Johais, E. (2025). Schlagfertigkeit. A soldier skill. *Critical Military Studies*. DOI 10.1080/23337486.2025.2472101. https://cmi.no/publications/file/9509-schlagfertigkeit-a-soldier-skill.pdf (CC BY).
- Type: interview-based qualitative study (35 current/former Bundeswehr soldiers) + literature review.
- Gives a writer: (1) a compact literature review of the documented functions: humor as "controlled form of resistance" to hardship (Godfrey), cohesion and boundary-drawing, discipline and negotiation of power, individual stress relief; (2) the specific soldier skill: quick, spontaneous verbal counter-attack, "give as good as you get," with a gradient from affectionate banter to hazing; (3) concrete scenes (a soldier is "responsible for the company's morale"; a newcomer ordered to tell a joke as a test, who risks a joke at the Catholic Church in front of a strict Catholic commander). Excellent for writing wit as a character virtue within a military setting.
- Access: free full text. Its reference list is a good map of the field (Ben-Ari & Sion 2005, Godfrey 2016, Hockey 1986/2006, Tidy 2021, Madigan 2013, Fine & De Soucey 2005, Üngör & Verkerke 2015).

**B3. Johais 2024, "The WARFUN taboo" (why soldiers hide their humor from civilians)** [VERIFIED-META]
- Johais, E. (2024). The WARFUN taboo. *War & Society*, 44(1), 66-81. DOI 10.1080/07292473.2024.2409538.
- Type: qualitative (German soldiers).
- Gives a writer: documents that soldiers experience humor as integral to soldier culture, yet "cautiously control what they share with outsiders," and that this concealment "alienates soldiers from the society into which they are supposed to fit." Directly addresses how veterans' humor lands on civilians (they edit themselves).
- Access: listed hybrid OA; I could not open the PDF (403). Abstract read via OpenAlex.

**B4. Godfrey 2016, humor as a "disciplinary technology"** [VERIFIED-META]
- Godfrey, R. (2016; online 2014). Soldiering on: Exploring the role of humor as a disciplinary technology in the military. *Organization*, 23(2), 164-183. DOI 10.1177/1350508414533164.
- Type: qualitative analysis of memoirs of recently serving British military personnel.
- Gives a writer: three arguments: humor is a "controlled form of resistance" that ultimately serves the organization; it builds cohesion through inclusion/exclusion and "lines of normalization"; it pervades the whole institutional hierarchy. Good for a character who grumbles about the chain of command in jokes but never actually disobeys.
- Access: paywalled (abstract free).

**B5. Ben-Ari & Sion 2005, Israeli combat reservists** [VERIFIED-META]
- Ben-Ari, E., & Sion, L. (2005). "Hungry, weary and horny": Joking and jesting among Israel's combat reserves. *Israel Affairs*, 11(4), 655-671. DOI 10.1080/13537120500233789.
- Type: ethnography.
- Gives a writer: the soldiers' own self-description ("hungry, weary and horny") as a ritual of complaint; joking as creating "a shared universe of meaning" and insider/outsider boundary (as paraphrased by Johais). 
- Access: paywalled; abstract partly read (OpenAlex abstract truncated).

**B6. Sløk-Andersen 2019 and Bjerke & Rones 2017: where the joking goes too far** [VERIFIED-OPEN]
- Sløk-Andersen, B. (2019). The butt of the joke? Laughter and potency in the becoming of good soldiers. *Cultural Analysis*, 17(1), 25-56. https://ocf.berkeley.edu/~culturalanalysis/volume17/vol17_Slok_Andersen.html (abstract page read) and Copenhagen repository PDF (OpenAlex).
- Bjerke, T. A., & Rones, N. (2017). The fine line between funny and offensive humor in a total institution: An ethnographic study of joking relationships among army soldiers. *Res Militaris*, 7(2). https://www.ffi.no/en/publications-archive/the-fine-line-between-funny-and-offensive-humour-in-a-total-institution-an-ethnographic-study-of-joking-relationships-among-army-soldiers (publication page read; free PDF at FFI).
- Type: ethnographies (Danish conscripts; Norwegian army).
- Gives a writer: humor as discipline (mocking "wrong" ways of being a soldier: masculinity, sexuality, leadership), the "killjoy" cost of refusing to laugh, and the fear that stops people (especially women) from objecting ("just jokes" until the tally becomes unbearable). Also Andersen (2020, nordics.info, page read) summarizing Nordic findings. Use for the dark side of cohesion humor.
- Access: both free.

**B7. Tidy 2021, humor in British fatality obituaries** [VERIFIED-META]
- Tidy, J. (2021). The part humor plays in the production of military violence. *Global Society*, 35(1), 134-148. DOI 10.1080/13600826.2020.1828300.
- Type: document analysis (Ministry of Defense obituaries for British dead, Afghanistan and Iraq).
- Gives a writer: how a dead soldier's humor is remembered: "service humor" with the capacity to "give as good as you get" (per Johais's summary). The humor of the dead is a standard way the unit eulogizes them: a real-world convention you can use in funerals and memorial scenes.
- Access: paywalled.

**B8. Hockey, *Squaddies* (1986; reissued 2006/2016)** [VERIFIED-OPEN, repository record]
- Hockey, J. (2016 [1986]). *Squaddies: Portrait of a Subculture.* Liverpool: Liverpool University Press. https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/4215/
- Type: ethnography (participant observation of a British infantry unit, including South Armagh).
- Gives a writer: the classic ground-level ethnography of infantry joking and "winding up." Page numbers on humor are cited in Johais (56-57, 72, 172-175).
- Access: book purchase (no repository full text).

**B9. Mogstad 2024, soldiers' "fun" in Afghanistan** [VERIFIED-OPEN]
- Mogstad, H. (2024). "Playing war": Norwegian soldiers' experiences of fun and responsibility in Afghanistan. *War & Society*, 44(1), 46-65. DOI 10.1080/07292473.2024.2409539. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11835299/
- Type: qualitative interviews.
- Gives a writer: the counter-intuitive reality that deployment was sometimes simply FUN ("We were basically tourists"), with joking part of that; fun faded as risk or leadership responsibility increased. Prevents a writer from making every joke a trauma response.
- Access: free (PMC).

**B10. Bhowmick & Vijaya 2023, Indian Army** [VERIFIED-OPEN]
- Bhowmick, S., & Vijaya, R. (2023). Winning battles with a joke: A qualitative inquiry of humor in the Indian Army. *European Journal of Humor Research*, 11(1). DOI 10.7592/EJHR.2023.11.1.755. https://europeanjournalofhumour.org/ejhr/article/view/755
- Type: qualitative (12 retired officers, thematic analysis).
- Gives a writer: functions list (stress, cohesion, newcomer assimilation, positive climate) plus "subversive humor" as quiet resistance to hierarchy. A non-Western-military comparison.
- Access: free.

**B11. Madsen 2013 thesis, combat humor in Iraq/Afghanistan** [VERIFIED-META]
- Madsen, M. T. (2013). *Dead Babies, Bowel Disturbances, and Other Combat Humor in Afghanistan and Iraq.* Master's thesis, Texas A&M University. http://oaktrust.library.tamu.edu/bitstream/1969.1/154910/1/MADSEN-THESIS-2013.pdf (my fetch returned a Cloudflare/403 block; record and abstract read through OpenAlex).
- Type: folklore/cultural study from soldiers' narratives and interviews.
- Gives a writer: the title alone shows the subject range (dead children, bodily functions). Abstract: humor "to lessen the horror of armed conflict, to establish community among soldiers, and to build up and break down hierarchy"; written to introduce civilians to military culture through its humor. Likely the richest collection of actual joke content on the list.
- Access: free (open repository), but I could not open it.

**B12. Pinkston 2018 thesis, Wars on Terror** [VERIFIED-OPEN, repository page]
- Pinkston, G. (2018). *"Death Hilarious": The Humor of Combat and the American Wars on Terror.* M.A. thesis, University of Virginia. https://libraetd.lib.virginia.edu/public_view/w66343999 (DOI 10.18130/V3GF0MW1Q).
- Type: thesis analyzing wartime accounts (memoirs).
- Gives a writer: what Iraq/Afghanistan soldiers found funny: combat, relations with fellow troops and the enemy, command, and messages from civilians at home. Free PDFs.
- Access: free (not read in full by me).

**B13. Soldiers' songs and folklore: Cleveland (Vietnam), Fish bibliography** [VERIFIED-OPEN]
- Cleveland, L. (2003). Songs of the Vietnam War: An occupational folk tradition. *New Directions in Folklore*, 7. (Read in full: free PDF at https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/ndif/article/download/19888/25958.) Documents that fear of "booby traps, claymore mines, mortars" and of capture ("a guest at the Hanoi Hilton") was "brought under social control" through humor, and that guilt over striking civilian targets was relieved by black humor in pilots' songs ("Two, Four, Six, Eight, Who're You Going to Defoliate").
- Cleveland, L. (1994). *Dark Laughter: War in Song and Popular Culture.* Westport, CT: Praeger. [VERIFIED-META: publisher and library pages in search results.] Covers WWI to Vietnam, service songs and soldier parody; the reluctant warrior's "comic philosophy of grudging endurance." Book purchase.
- Fish, L. (compiler; last updated 1999). Folklore (excluding folksongs) of Americans in the Vietnam War: A bibliography. https://facstaff.buffalostate.edu/fishlm/folksongs/vietfolk.htm [VERIFIED-OPEN]. Humor items: Foster, *The Vietnam Funny Book* (Presidio, 1980); Myers (ed.), *A Treasury of Military Humor* (Lincoln-Herndon, 1990; has a Vietnam chapter); Pratt, *Vietnam Voices* (Viking, 1984); Tuso, "A folk drama: 'What the Captain Means Is...'," *Folklore Forum* 5(1), 1972; Zidek, *Choi-oi: The Lighter Side of Vietnam* (Tuttle, 1965). The best finding aid for Vietnam-era primary humor.
- Access: Cleveland article free; others are library/book.

**B14. WWI: Madigan 2013, Fuller 1990, Kantar 2018, Gregory 2016** [mixed]
- Madigan, E. (2013). "Sticking to a hateful task": Resilience, humor, and British understandings of combatant courage, 1914-1918. *War in History*, 20(1), 76-98. DOI 10.1177/0968344512455900. [VERIFIED-META] Argues the sarcastic, self-deprecating trench style expressed a rival model of courage ("sticking it": endurance despite fear) against the civilians' picture of the fearless hero. - Fuller, J. G. (1990). *Troop Morale and Popular Culture in the British and Dominion Armies, 1914-1918.* Oxford: Clarendon Press. Free (English abstract; article text may be in English).

**B15. Stalag humor and POWs** [mixed]
- Henman, L. D. (2001). Humor as a coping mechanism: Lessons from POWs. *Humor*, 14(1), 83-94. DOI 10.1515/humr.14.1.83. [VERIFIED-META: Crossref; search summary reports 138 American POWs, "put-down" humor aimed at captors raising sense of control.] Paywalled.
- Horn, K. (2011). "Stalag happy": South African prisoners of war during World War Two and their experience and use of humor. *South African Historical Journal*, 63(4), 537-552. https://scholar.sun.ac.za/ [VERIFIED-OPEN; abstract read; hdl.handle.net/10019.1/20645]. "Humor was a universal coping and morale boosting mechanism"; veterans also used humor when retelling, to manage the emotional weight of the memory. Free.
- Moran, C. (2008). Humor and its link to meaning and spirituality in war. In E. MacKinlay (ed.), *Aging, Disability and Spirituality* (pp. 200-216). London: Jessica Kingsley. https://researchoutput.csu.edu.au/en/publications/humour-and-its-link-to-meaning-and-spirituality-in-war/ [VERIFIED-OPEN, abstract]. Elderly WWII veterans: in combat humor was an immediate coping device; in old age it becomes "a search for meaning." Gives you how a veteran's humor ages. Book chapter (purchase).

**B16. Bizi, Keinan & Beit-Hallahmi 1988, humor and performance under stress (IDF)** [VERIFIED-OPEN, repository abstract]
- Bizi, S., Keinan, G., & Beit-Hallahmi, B. (1988). Humor and coping with stress: A test under real-life conditions. *Personality and Individual Differences*, 9(6), 951-956. DOI 10.1016/0191-8869(88)90128-6. https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/humor-and-coping-with-stress-a-test-under-real-life-conditions/
- Type: empirical (159 IDF combat-course trainees).
- Gives a writer: the finding that humor rated by PEERS (not self-report) predicted performance under stress, and that producing jokes ("active humor") mattered more than merely appreciating them. Basis for the "the funny one keeps the squad functional" trope.
- Access: abstract free; paper paywalled.

**B17. Books by historians (no confirmed humor chapter; read for context)** [VERIFIED-OPEN, publisher/review pages]
- Fussell, P. (1989). *Wartime: Understanding and Behavior in the Second World War.* New York: Oxford University Press (LRB review page opened: publisher, Sept. 1989, 330 pp.). Treats soldiers' irony, profanity, euphemism and contempt for home-front sanitizing as central to WWII experience.
- Kindsvatter, P. S. (2003). *American Soldiers: Ground Combat in the World Wars, Korea, and Vietnam.* Lawrence: University Press of Kansas (Project MUSE page opened; table of contents has NO humor chapter but includes "coping" and "comradeship").
- Linderman, G. F. (1997). *The World Within War: America's Combat Experience in World War II.* New York: Free Press (catalog pages; chapter list has no humor chapter). I could NOT confirm that any of these three discusses gallows humor specifically; treat as context sources, not as humor sources.

### C. Humor, PTSD, moral injury, and veterans' reintegration

**C1. Ward et al. 2021: humor as a protective factor in 20,901 soldiers** [VERIFIED-OPEN, summary page + Crossref]
- Ward, R. N., Carlson, K. J., Erickson, A. J., Yalch, M. M., & Brown, L. M. (2021/2024). Associations of humor, morale, and unit cohesion on posttraumatic stress disorder symptoms. *Military Psychology*, 36(3), 301-310. DOI 10.1080/08995605.2021.1996103. (Author order differs by source: Crossref and Molendijk list Ward first; one summary page lists Brown first.)
- Type: empirical (structural equation modeling, active-duty soldiers).
- Gives a writer: the strongest quantitative support that humor, morale and cohesion travel together and associate with fewer PTSD symptoms over and above deployment stressors. Basis for "the funny unit was the okay unit." Cross-sectional, so the arrow could run either way.
- Access: paywalled (abstract free).

**C2. Yoshimura et al. 2024: dark humor among U.S. veterans** [VERIFIED-OPEN, read]
- Yoshimura, S. M., Bilbrey, G., Johns, S. A., Hall, K., & Moore, N. (2024). Sanity through insanity: The use of dark humor among United States veterans. *Behavioral Sciences*, 14(8), 679. DOI 10.3390/bs14080679. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11351593/
- Type: empirical (n = 93 online survey).
- Gives a writer: (1) the theory it frames: dark humor as bonding, as a "shibboleth to identify those who belong," and as a way to feel in control; (2) a surprising result: dark humor did NOT correlate with connectedness and did NOT alienate civilian friends or family; (3) self-defeating humor WAS strongly associated with lower life satisfaction. Honest limits: small, volunteer, Marines/Army heavy, and the HSQ subscales are not dark-humor-specific. A useful corrective to the cliché that veterans' humor always alienates civilians.
- Access: free (PMC).

**C3. Argento 2025: combat medics, dark humor suppressed after discharge** [VERIFIED-OPEN, repository page]
- Argento, M. (2025). *Lived experiences of reintegration for Afghanistan veterans who were Army combat medics.* PhD dissertation, University of Northern Colorado. https://digscholarship.unco.edu/dissertations/1175 (169 pp., free PDF).
- Type: qualitative (3 medics).
- Gives a writer: "Every participant" described the combat medic's "dark sense of humor" as something others don't understand; they "actively suppressed that part of their personality" after leaving, and felt unable to be fully themselves anywhere. Small n, but it is exactly the pattern you want for a medic-type character. 
- Access: free.

**C4. Morris & Hanna 2023: British veterans on "banter" and misfires** [VERIFIED-OPEN]
- Morris, J., & Hanna, P. (2023). The military to civilian transition: Exploring experiences of transitions to "civvy street" and implications for the self. *Journal of Veterans Studies*, 9(3), 74-88. DOI 10.21061/jvs.v9i3.458. https://journal-veterans-studies.org/articles/10.21061/jvs.v9i3.458
- Type: qualitative (20 interviews).
- Gives a writer: participants describing learning, through workplace feedback, that their "ability to crack a tasteless joke, or to get a bit of banter wrong" did not translate, and "I've made rooms go quiet." Short, usable quotes about the recalibration of humor in civilian rooms.
- Access: free (open journal).

**C5. Besser et al. 2015: humor styles and PTSD in terror survivors** [VERIFIED-META]
- Besser, A., Weinberg, M., Zeigler-Hill, V., & Ataria, Y. (2015). Humor and trauma-related psychopathology among survivors of terror attacks and their spouses. *Psychiatry*, 78(4), 341-353. DOI 10.1080/00332747.2015.1061312.
- Type: empirical (105 dyads, Israel). Not soldiers but a trauma-exposed sample.
- Gives a writer: benign humor styles linked to lower symptoms in survivors and buffering for spouses (per search-result summary); self-defeating humor linked to avoidance and emotion-regulation trouble. Supports a two-sided model of a veteran's humor and its effect on a partner.
- Access: listed OA via Columbia Academic Commons (blocked to me); abstract read.

**C6. Olah et al. 2022: Comedy Bootcamp for veterans** [VERIFIED-META]
- Olah, A. R., Junkin, J. S., Ford, T. E., & Pressler, S. (2022). Comedy Bootcamp: Stand-up comedy as humor training for military populations. *Humor*, 35(4), 587-616. DOI 10.1515/humor-2022-0007.
- Type: program evaluation (n = 72, 18 months, control group).
- Gives a writer: stand-up as an after-service outlet; trained participants showed higher self-enhancing humor and better self-esteem, resilience, depression and stress (not anxiety), persisting at 3 and 6 months. Source for a veteran character who performs comedy.
- Access: listed hybrid OA; page blocked, abstract read.

**C7. Kopytin & Lebedev: humor in group art therapy with war veterans** [VERIFIED-META]
- Kopytin, A., & Lebedev, A. (2013). Humor, self-attitude, emotions, and cognitions in group art therapy with war veterans. *Art Therapy*, 30(1), 20-29. DOI 10.1080/07421656.2013.757758.
- Kopytin, A., & Lebedev, A. (2015). Therapeutic functions of humor in group art therapy with war veterans. *International Journal of Art Therapy*, 20(2), 40-53. DOI 10.1080/17454832.2014.1000348.
- Type: clinical/qualitative (Russian veterans).
- Gives a writer: how a therapist sees veterans' humor operating inside a group (cited by Yoshimura 2024 for "therapeutic functions"). Paywalled; I did not read the content.

**C8. Rice & Liu 2016: humor predicts resilience in veterans** [VERIFIED-META]
- Rice, V. J. B., & Liu, B. (2016). Personal resilience and coping Part II: Identifying resilience and coping among U.S. military service members and veterans with implications for work. *Work*, 54(2), 335-350. DOI 10.3233/WOR-162301. PubMed 27259181.
- Type: empirical (N = 191).
- Gives a writer: in veterans, more use of humor (Brief COPE) predicted higher resilience, alongside longer time in service and less self-blame; for active service members it was positive reframing instead. Bronze OA (my download was blocked).

**C9. Self-report data on the downside: Anders et al. 2025 and Kuiper's police study** [VERIFIED-OPEN / VERIFIED-META]
- Anders, R., Willemin-Petignat, L., & Putois, B. (2025). Humor as a double-edged weapon in psychopathology: Light and dark humor styles, coping, and personality in police mental health. *Journal of Occupational Medicine and Toxicology*, 20, 41. DOI 10.1186/s12995-025-00485-2. PMC article page read: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12632130/ [VERIFIED-OPEN]
- Type: empirical (2,291 Swiss officers, 8 humor styles: fun, humor, nonsense, wit | cynicism, sarcasm, satire, irony).
- Gives a writer: the sharpest "limits" data: dark comic styles were never associated with fewer symptoms, only more; cynicism and sarcasm tracked psychopathology most; light styles tracked better mental health; and the authors propose that heavy dark humor may be a screening signal of hidden distress. Caveats: cross-sectional, self-report, one country, police not soldiers.
- Kerkkänen, P., Kuiper, N. A., & Martin, R. A. (2004). Sense of humor, physical health, and well-being at work: A three-year longitudinal study of Finnish police officers. *Humor*, 17(1-2). DOI 10.1515/humr.2004.006 (pages not confirmed by me) [VERIFIED-META]. Cited by Kuiper (2012).
- Access: Anders free; Kerkkänen paywalled.

### D. First responders, medics and other high-lethality occupations (documented functions and failure modes)

**D1. Rowe & Regehr 2010, the standard review** [VERIFIED-META]
- Rowe, A., & Regehr, C. (2010). Whatever gets you through today: An examination of cynical humor among emergency service professionals. *Journal of Loss and Trauma*, 15(5), 448-464. DOI 10.1080/15325024.2010.507661.
- Type: review of theory and research.
- Gives a writer: the three-function model (venting feelings; eliciting social support through group cohesion; "distancing themselves from a situation, ensuring that they can act effectively") and the claim that black humor is "almost universally utilized" and "vital." Note that the UK ambulance review excluded it as secondary research, and the Molendijk article misdates it as 2019; correct is 2010.
- Access: paywalled; abstract free.

**D2. Sliter, Kale & Yuan 2014: coping humor buffers trauma in firefighters** [VERIFIED-OPEN, read]
- Sliter, M., Kale, A., & Yuan, Z. (2014). Is humor the best medicine? The buffering effect of coping humor on traumatic stressors in firefighters. *Journal of Organizational Behavior*, 35(2), 257-272. DOI 10.1002/job.1868. Free PDF: https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/210591/cdc_210591_DS1.pdf
- Type: empirical (179 firefighters, two time points).
- Gives a writer: traumatic events predicted PTSD, burnout, absenteeism, and coping humor buffered the effect on burnout and PTSD. Prospective design, better than most.
- Access: free (CDC stacks).

**D3. Dangermond et al. 2022: Dutch firefighters, and when the humor STOPS** [VERIFIED-OPEN, repository page + abstract]
- Dangermond, K., Weewer, R., Duyndam, J., & Machielse, A. (2022). "If it stops, then I'll start worrying." Humor as part of the fire service culture, specifically as part of coping with critical incidents. *Humor*, 35(1), 31-50. DOI 10.1515/humor-2021-0106. https://research.uvh.nl/en/publications/337d630b-d476-467f-9854-61a13da4b3af
- Type: ethnography (20 participant observations, 72 interviews, 37 stations).
- Gives a writer: three of the things you asked about in one paper: (1) humor is used "to start a conversation and, indirectly, to make it possible to discuss emotions"; (2) "whether a joke is perceived as funny depends on who makes the joke, the moment, the content, and the frequency"; (3) "black humor is absent in the case of certain critical incidents because of personal boundaries and unwritten rules," and "the absence of humor is a sign for the crew commander to pay extra attention to coping." Also notes the group is reluctant to discuss humor with outsiders. Use it for the scene where the funny man goes silent and the others notice.
- Access: hybrid OA (de Gruyter blocked to me; abstract read at the repository).

**D4. Mercer, Morgan & Lotto 2024: UK paramedics' own view of dark humor** [VERIFIED-OPEN, read]
- Mercer, J., Morgan, D., & Lotto, R. (2024). A qualitative exploration of the views of paramedics regarding the use of dark humor. *British Paramedic Journal*, 9(3), 37-43. DOI 10.29045/14784726.2024.12.9.3.37. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11610543/
- Type: qualitative (10 experienced paramedics).
- Gives a writer: concrete quotes on both sides. Benefits: resilience and camaraderie. Costs: "I notice you start detaching from personal experiences," "makes a barrier... but covers up processing," "creeps into family and personal life," "it's like Pandora's box... it'll get us eventually." Audience rules: "Appropriately inappropriate for the correct audience" and "not funny for non-medical professionals." Gives you the practitioner's own sense of the line.
- Access: free (PMC).

**D5. Scott 2007: A&E staff, paramedics and traffic officers** [VERIFIED-OPEN, repository abstract]

### E. Contemporary war: the moral-disengagement edge of dark jokes

**E1. A'Beckett 2024, black-humor memes about the enemy's death** [VERIFIED-META]

## 2. TOP 5 TO READ FIRST

1. **Molendijk 2025 (B1)**: the best single account of what military humor is, how it differs from generic "coping" humor, and where it turns rotten. Free full text; has quotable examples.
2. **Johais 2025 (B2)**: free; gives the functions-literature map, the wit/"verbal duel" model of soldier humor, and its reference list is the fastest way into the rest of the field.
3. **Dangermond et al. 2022 (D3)**: the clearest evidence that a joke's meaning depends on who/when/how often, that the funny person going quiet is a distress signal, and that responders conceal their humor from outsiders. Read the abstract and the repository page; get the paper if you can.
4. **Yoshimura et al. 2024 (C2) with Argento 2025 (C3) and Morris & Hanna 2023 (C4)**: the three free veteran-reintegration sources; together they give both the corrective (survey: dark humor did not alienate civilian friends) and the lived complaint (medics suppress it; "made rooms go quiet").
5. **Mercer, Morgan & Lotto 2024 (D4) with Anders et al. 2025 (C9)**: the practitioner's own words on where it goes wrong, plus the strongest quantitative warning that dark styles (cynicism, sarcasm) track distress rather than protect against it.

(Honorable mention: Freud 1928 [A2] and Obrdlik 1942 [A1] for the canonical vocabulary; both short.)
