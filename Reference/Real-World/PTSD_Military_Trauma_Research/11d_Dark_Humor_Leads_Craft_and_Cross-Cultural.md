# Leads: craft and cross-cultural angle on military gallows humor
Prepared 2026-10-05. Angle: how dark military humor is written well and badly (fiction, film, TV, games), cross-cultural differences, humor of defectors/exiles/deserters/objectors, and the civilian-facing register.

## (1) LEADS

### Category (a): craft discussions by veteran-authors and screenwriters

**A1. Phil Klay, interview with Nick Ripatrazone, *Image Journal* issue 87 (c. 2015)**
URL: https://imagejournal.org/article/conversation-phil-klay/
Type: author interview. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: Klay says Marines make "the funniest (and darkest) of jokes" in the worst situations, that the military's bureaucracy supplies "plenty of absurdity," and that "the comedy I like the best is always both humorous and serious at once" (extracted). He also cites Vonnegut's line that jokes are a viable literary form and that comedy lets us face what we would avert our eyes from. Gives the craft rule: the joke and the weight must arrive in the same beat, not alternate.

**A2. Phil Klay, interview with Benjy Hansen-Bundy, *Mother Jones*, 3 March 2014**
URL: https://www.motherjones.com/?p=246416
Type: author interview. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: the story "Bodies" opens "There are two ways to tell the story. Funny or sad. Guys like it funny, with lots of gore and a grin on your face when you get to the end." The interview frames the funny version as a shield used with civilians. Useful for a character who has a rehearsed funny version and a withheld sad version of the same event, and who chooses by audience.

**A3. David Abrams (20-year Army veteran), interview with Kyle Minor, *The Rumpus*, 10 September 2012**
URL: https://therumpus.net/2012/09/the-rumpus-interview-with-david-abrams
Type: author interview on *Fobbit*. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: Abrams says "there is just too much absurdity and irony at play in a combat zone not to pay attention to it" (extracted) and describes approaching the material "sideways and wearing a jester's hat" (Flannery O'Connor's way of puncturing earnest subjects). Also his worry that Iraq reached Americans as "just another TV show." Gives the target list for real soldier comedy: rear-echelon absurdity, bureaucratic euphemism, the gap between official narrative and daily reality.

**A4. David Abrams and Viet Thanh Nguyen, "On the Lost Art of the Comic War Novel," *Literary Hub*, 30 April 2015**
URL: https://lithub.com/on-the-lost-art-of-the-comic-war-novel/
Type: two-author conversation. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: Abrams: "If you don't laugh, you'll cry"; Vietnam produced little comic work for decades because of "guilt and anger" still in the culture, so *timing* (how far from the wound) governs what comedy is permitted. Nguyen, a refugee-turned-novelist, says *The Sympathizer* satirizes all sides and that being "both an insider of and outsider to" the culture is what made the satire possible. Directly relevant to category (d): the outsider-who-was-once-inside as the best vantage for the joke.

**A5. Joseph Heller, interview with Ramona Koval, October 1998**
URL: https://ramonakoval.com/interviews1/joseph-heller
Type: author interview (Manhattan apartment). Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: Heller says "you can't be funny just by saying a joke, you have to lead up to it," that strings of wisecracks fail because readers need progression, and that the absurdities in *Catch-22* came from postwar conditions, not his combat experience ("I don't think the war itself had any effect on it") (all extracted). Craft takeaway: the funniest military humor is built, not quipped, and its target is often the institution's logic rather than the enemy.

**A6. Kurt Vonnegut on black/gallows humor: Suzanne McConnell excerpt from *Pity the Reader* (Seven Stories, 2019), via *The London Magazine***
URL: https://thelondonmagazine.org/essay-kurt-vonnegut-black-humor/
Type: craft essay quoting Vonnegut. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: quotes Vonnegut that "laughter or crying is what a human being does when there's nothing else he can do," that "the biggest laughs are based on the biggest disappointments and the biggest fears," and ties black humor to German-Austrian-Polish gallows humor and Freud on humor (extracted). Companion item, read in full locally: Gregory Sumner, "Kurt Vonnegut: Brokenhearted American Dreamer," *RSA Journal* 21-22 (2010-11), https://ojs.unito.it/index.php/rsajournal/article/download/8720/7212 , which notes Vonnegut's self-protective deflection ("an attempt to quarantine the episode") and "there is nothing intelligent to say about a massacre." **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
Note: the primary source (Paris Review "Art of Fiction No. 64," 1977, https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3605/the-art-of-fiction-no-64-kurt-vonnegut) returned 403 to every fetch, including curl. **UNVERIFIED** (known only from a search snippet).

**A7. Larry Gelbart, *Laughing Matters: On Writing M\*A\*S\*H, Tootsie, Oh, God!, and a Few Other Things* (Random House, 1998), as summarized by The MASH Historian, 16 April 2024**
URL: https://themashhistorian.com/2024/04/16/from-the-mash-library-31/
Type: memoir/craft book (summary page opened, book not). Access: book is purchasable; summary free. Summary page **VERIFIED-OPEN**; the book itself **UNVERIFIED** (I did not read it).
What it gives a writer: Gelbart set out to avoid another light military comedy and instead "confront the reality of war and show the humanity of the people living through it," reporting that Korean War veterans said off-hours comedy was important to surviving. A search snippet (not opened; NPR page timed out) attributes to Carl Reiner the formulation of Gelbart's problem as "how not to trivialize human suffering by trying to be comic about it" (**UNVERIFIED**).

**A8. *Blackadder Goes Forth* ending, Adam Brooke, "Blackadder Goes Forth turns 33," *The Kicker* (Substack), 11 December 2022**
URL: https://thisisthekicker.substack.com/p/blackadder-goes-forth-turns-33-how

Also how the final slow-motion walk and silence (a salvage of unusable footage) turned a comic ending into the series' signature. Concrete model for "target the madness, never the dead," and for ending a comedy by cutting the jokes off.

### Category (c): cross-cultural characterizations

**C1. Eva Johais, "Schlagfertigkeit. A soldier skill," *Critical Military Studies*, 26 February 2025 (open access, CC-BY)**
URL: https://cmi.no/publications/file/9509-schlagfertigkeit-a-soldier-skill.pdf (DOI 10.1080/23337486.2025.2472101)
Type: scholarly article, based on 35 interviews with current/former German soldiers plus a literature review spanning British, German, Danish, Israeli and WWI sources. Access: free PDF. **VERIFIED-OPEN (read as full text).**
What it gives a writer: the best single entry point. Says military joking works as (1) "controlled form of resistance" to hardship, (2) cohesion and insider/outsider boundary drawing, (3) a test ground for power between ranks, (4) coping. Characterizes soldier humor as quick, situational repartee (Schlagfertigkeit) that counters expectation, and notes British "service humor" requires the capacity to "give as good as you get," with affectionate teasing called banter and the aggressive kind called hazing. Its reference list is a ready bibliography for category (c): Ben-Ari and Sion 2005 (Israeli reservists), Godfrey 2016, Hockey 1986 (*Squaddies*), Madigan 2013 (WWI Britain), Tidy 2021, Sløk-Andersen 2019, Bjerke and Rones 2017.
Companion: Johais, "No laughter, no war," *Public Anthropologist*, 30 January 2023, https://publicanthropologist.cmi.no/2023/01/30/no-laughter-no-war/ **VERIFIED-OPEN.** German soldiers' sayings used as criticism channel ("Wer nichts kann, kann Anzug"; "shooting to the left, handing out candies to the right" for Afghanistan COIN) and the line that humans need an outlet when "frank words" are "not allowed in the hierarchical system."

**C2. Joanna Tidy, "The part humor plays in the production of military violence," *Global Society* 35(1), 2021**
URL: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/166591 (accepted version, open)
Type: scholarly article analyzing UK MoD obituaries of British dead from Iraq and Afghanistan. Access: free accepted manuscript. **VERIFIED-OPEN (abstract level; I did not open the PDF).**
What it gives a writer: argues humor in British service culture often reinforces institutional power and normalizes violence rather than resisting it, with "give as good as you get" banter as a gendered practice. A necessary counterweight to the "humor is just coping" reading, and useful for a defector whose break with the institution includes seeing what the jokes were doing.

**C3. Beate Sløk-Andersen, "The Butt of the Joke? Laughter and Potency in the Becoming of Good Soldiers," *Cultural Analysis* 17 (2019)**
URL: https://ocf.berkeley.edu/~culturalanalysis/volume17/vol17_Slok_Andersen.html
Type: ethnography of Danish conscripts. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: humor polices who counts as a "good soldier" (sexuality, dominance, toughness); the recurring butt of the joke signals outsider status; soldiers fear being "killjoys" if they object. A defector's relationship to the joke culture (still fluent, no longer compliant) is a fertile characterization.

**C4. Nathan Wise, "Fighting a Different Enemy: Social Protests against Authority in the Australian Imperial Force during World War I," *International Review of Social History* 52 (2007)**
URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-review-of-social-history/article/fighting-a-different-enemy-social-protests-against-authority-in-the-australian-imperial-force-during-world-war-i/6B6CB43CA3890B1636FC0B32763A98F7
Type: scholarly article. Access: paywalled; abstract free. **VERIFIED-OPEN at abstract level.**
What it gives a writer: Australian soldiers used humor as protest against officers and regimentation, a vent for anger at the system, folded into identity and masculinity, and replaced by more somber protest after the war. The strongest Australian-specific lead (larrikin irreverence as institutionalized dissent). Pair with Chapman and Ellin, "Dominion cartoon satire as trench culture narratives: complaints, endurance and stoicism," *The Round Table* 103(2), 2014 (https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/65742/ ; abstract **VERIFIED-OPEN**, full text not opened), which finds Australian, Canadian, NZ and British soldiers redefining courage as endurance through complaint comedy. The Australian War Memorial's "Laughter: a powerful ally" exhibition page (9 December 2019, https://www.awm.gov.au/media/press-releases/laughter) **VERIFIED-OPEN** covers entertainers and morale, not dark humor specifically.

**C5. Elliott Oring, *Israeli Humor: The Content and Structure of the Chizbat of the Palmah* (SUNY Press, 1981)**
URL: https://sunypress.edu/Books/I/Israeli-Humor
Type: book (folklore). Access: paid. Publisher page **VERIFIED-OPEN**; book not read.
What it gives a writer: the chizbat ("lie" in Arabic root) was a narrative joke form among Palmah fighters that depends not on punchlines but on displaying incongruities of Israeli identity (sabra/European, Arab/Israeli, East/West); includes hundreds of translated examples. Worth reading for the point that humor can be a way of working out *which group you belong to*. The IDF-specific scholarly lead, Ben-Ari and Sion, "'Hungry, Weary and Horny': Joking and Jesting among Israel's Combat Reserves," *Israel Affairs* 11(4), 2005, 655-671 (doi 10.1080/13537120500233789): citation confirmed in Johais' reference list; Taylor & Francis returned 403; **UNVERIFIED.**

**C6. Soviet/Russian: (i) Daniela Hristova, "Negotiating Reality with Anekdoty: Soviet vs. Post-Soviet Humor Lore," *Russian Language Journal* 58(1), 2008; (ii) Konstantin Asimonov, "The art of a Russian joke: anekdoty about Putin and the war," Substack, 13 November 2024**
URLs: https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/rlj/vol58/iss1/10/ and https://tapwatersommelier.substack.com/p/anekdoty-about-putin-war
Type: scholarly article (abstract open); journalism interviewing folklorist Alexandra Arkhipova. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: the anekdot is narrative, not wordplay; post-2022 war anekdoty shifted to a scared "man on the street" protagonist and domestic-absurd deflations ("a soldier says he's a POW; mother: 'Exchange some dollars!'"). Also Leonid Bershidsky, *Moscow Times* (21 September 2018), https://themoscowtimes.com/articles/the-real-point-of-russian-and-soviet-jokes-op-ed-62967 **VERIFIED-OPEN**: Russian jokes mark the standing gap between rulers and ruled and are "a private endeavor." Soviet *army*-specific folklore scholarship was not found in a usable form (see log); the 1929 Red Army joke-book article search result (philology.nsc.ru) returned 404 on retry (**UNVERIFIED**), and Tvardovsky's *Vasily Tyorkin* (1942-45) appears only in a search snippet (**UNVERIFIED**).

**C7. WWI France/Germany: Philippe Faverjon, *Blagues de poilus: l'humor noir des tranchées 1914-1918* (Prisma, 2016)**
URL: https://www.mollat.com/livres/425545/philippe-faverjon-blagues-de-poilus-l-humour-noir-des-tranchees-1914-1918
Type: anthology of 200 jokes from trench newspapers. Access: paid. Listing **VERIFIED-OPEN**; book not read.
What it gives a writer: primary material (jokes from trench papers) showing the dark/absurd register of French soldiers; the listing says the jokes "mock the enemy while addressing the horrors of combat," with the aim of recovering lost humanity. The University of Melbourne piece "Comedy in the trenches" (https://pursuit.unimelb.edu.au/articles/comedy-in-the-trenches) returned 403 (**UNVERIFIED**; search snippet only, says French, Australian and German trench papers used humor as a "safety valve" and that naming the enemy as ridiculous is a way of mastering him). ### Category (d): defectors, exiles, deserters, objectors (thin area; read the caveats)

**D1. Olesia Krivtsova and Georgii Chentemirov, "'All right. I'm a soldier. I'm ready,'" *The Barents Observer*, 9 June 2026**
URL: https://www.thebarentsobserver.com/news/all-right-im-a-soldier-im-ready/452198
Type: journalism, interviews with two Russian deserters (pseudonyms Dmitry and Solomon) who fled to Finland. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: the single most on-point find. Their gallows humor is documented (they joke that a pile of bodies outside a dugout meant they were "heading in the right direction"; nicknames based on petty crimes; irony about school lessons on "peaceful skies"), and the piece reports that the humor dissolves when they discuss command brutality, torture during recruitment and self-wounding (extracted). Gives a usable rule: a defector's jokes about the war are intact; jokes about *why he left* are not available to him.

**D2. Matthew Sweet, *Operation Chaos: The Vietnam Deserters Who Fought the CIA, the Brainwashers, and Themselves* (2018), via Seth Ferranti's interview in VICE, 25 January 2018**
URL: https://vice.com/en/article/how-a-secret-cia-cell-fucked-with-vietnam-war-deserters
Type: interview about a history book on US deserters in Sweden. Access: free (article); book paid. Article **VERIFIED-OPEN**; book not read.
What it gives a writer: tragicomic anecdotes about deserters' chaotic exile (a failed bank robbery with a water pistol, a deserter claiming a CIA kidnapping). These are Sweet's narration of absurdity, not the deserters' own jokes; do not cite them as deserter humor. Pan Macmillan's "six of the deserters" page (https://panmacmillan.com/blogs/literary/operation-chaos-six-of-the-deserters) **VERIFIED-OPEN** has one dark line from a deserter about a handler ("You've got to admire him, like you admire the work of a butcher").

URL: https://themillions.com/?p=64936

A search snippet (not opened) cites the line about *The Zone*: "Almost any prisoner would have been suited to the role of a guard. Almost any guard deserved a prison term" (**UNVERIFIED** as to source). The Paris Review piece on him (https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2012/01/25/the-troubadour-of-honed-banality/) returned 403.

**D4. Viktor Belenko (MiG-25 defector, 1976), Reuben F. Johnson, *Free Beacon*, 22 November 2023**
URL: https://freebeacon.com/culture/from-russia-with-love-soviet-mig-defector-dies-at-age-76/
Type: obituary-profile. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: documented personality of a military defector: a "penchant for bold practical jokes," including following a Soviet embassy car in Washington and offering the diplomat the same defection deal (the man closed the window and sped off), and joking that he would resurface only to collect his checks from the "Beltway Bandits" (extracted). Brief, but a verified instance of a defector's humor aimed at his former side and at his own new dependence.

**D5. North Korean defectors on South Korean TV: NBC News, 21 June 2012**
URL: https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna47900931
Type: journalism about the show "Now on My Way to Meet You." Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: a defector performer says "I still feel uncomfortable when I have to make people laugh, or perform. I am still wedded to North Korea's stiff style," while the show mixes escape stories with jokes. Illustrates that the *register* of humor is itself learned in the institution and must be re-learned after leaving, and that defector humor is partly a performance for a host society.

**D6. Syrian black humor and defectors: Michael Chernin, "Why so Syrious?" *Brown Political Review*, 11 April 2013**
URL: https://brownpoliticalreview.org/why-so-syrious-black-humor-amid-horror-in-syria/
Type: journalism. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: "comedy develops because of, not despite, these horrors"; examples include the checkpoint joke about a mukhabarat officer discovering a Free Syrian Army checkpoint. A search snippet (from a Naharnet article that now returns "page not found") reports protest banners *mocking* defectors who waited in Turkey ("Dear defectors, the Syrian revolution is taking place in Syria, not in Turkey") and a "Charles de Gaulle Brigade" banner about Manaf Tlass's defection to Paris; those two examples are **UNVERIFIED**. If confirmed, they would document the defector as the *butt* of the joke, which the rest of this list does not cover. Counter-evidence: Kelly McEvers's 2012 NPR/WGBH report on Syrian army defectors (https://www.wgbh.org/news/2012-04-09/defectors-offer-insiders-view-of-syrian-army) **VERIFIED-OPEN**: the defectors told their stories matter-of-factly and seriously, not humorously.

**D7. Exile humor as a group practice: Rasa Zukiene, "O vis dėlto juokėmės" ("But we did have some laughs too"), *Oikos* 18 (2014); Andrew Hernann, "Joking Through Hardship: Humor and Truth-Telling among Displaced Timbuktians," *African Studies Review* 59(1), 2016**
URLs: https://www.lituanistika.lt/content/68249 and https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/african-studies-review/article/joking-through-hardship-humor-and-truthtelling-among-displaced-timbuktians/451682FFCC12E4D3D3BB07B1022F93E7
Type: scholarly (abstracts opened; Hernann paywalled). **VERIFIED-OPEN at abstract level.**
What it gives a writer: Lithuanian displaced-persons camps produced about twenty humor magazines in 1946-48 whose cartoons carried political messages for the whole exile community; Timbuktu refugees used joking to assert local truths, relieve anxiety and rebuild community in exile. These are not military, but they are the best found on how exiles (as opposed to soldiers) joke: humor as maintaining a lost community's shared reference points. Fits a defector who has no unit left to share jokes with.

**D8. Jaroslav Hašek and *The Good Soldier Švejk*; Czech occupation gallows humor (Obrdlik)**
URLs: https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Schweik (search result only) and https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/pdfplus/10.1086/219002 (403)
Type: novel; 1942 sociology paper. **UNVERIFIED** (neither opened). From search snippets: Hašek was captured by Russian forces in September 1915, joined the Czech Legion, then deserted it in April 1918 and became a Red Army commissar; Švejk is a soldier whose possibly feigned idiocy frustrates authority, and "the reader is left unclear" whether he is fool or genius of passive resistance. Antonin Obrdlik, "'Gallows Humor': A Sociological Phenomenon," *American Journal of Sociology* 47(5), 1942, 709-716, coined the term for humor of the oppressed in occupied Czechoslovakia (the full text is paywalled; its existence and thesis are confirmed by two other opened pages that quote it: Havok Journal and ClearanceJobs). Worth chasing: a soldier-satirist who switched sides twice is the nearest historical analogue to a defector humorist, but I could not verify the biography from an opened source.

### Category (e): civilian-facing register and humor after service

**E1. Stephen M. Yoshimura et al., "Sanity through Insanity: The Use of Dark Humor among United States Veterans," *Behavioral Sciences*, 2024**
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11351593/
Type: survey study (n=93 veterans, mostly Marines/Army, volunteer sample). Access: free (PMC). **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: dark humor use was *not* associated with feeling connected to veteran or civilian friends; connectedness predicted well-being; self-defeating humor strongly predicted *lower* well-being; aggressive humor showed no effect (extracted: beta = -0.60 for self-defeating). The most directly usable finding for characterization: a veteran whose jokes are mostly at his own expense is the one doing worse, and that is the style most writers assign as "charming."

**E2. Lisa M. Brown, Matthew Yalch, Alexander Erickson, Rachel Ward, Katie Carlson, "Associations of humor, morale, and unit cohesion on posttraumatic stress disorder symptoms," *Military Psychology*, December 2021**
URL: https://www.centreforevidence.org/article/20211217-associations-of-humor-morale-and-unit-cohesion-on-posttraumatic-stress-disorder-symptoms
Type: large survey (20,901 active-duty personnel). Access: abstract free. **VERIFIED-OPEN** (abstract-level summary page).
What it gives a writer: humor, unit cohesion and morale were positively associated with each other and negatively associated with PTSD symptoms. Note: this measures *in-service* humor with a unit; post-service isolation removes the cohesion half of that package.

**E3. Marc Levy and Susan Erony, "War Jokes Wanted: No Laughing Matter" (CounterPunch, 26-27 September 2008; VVAW.org copy)**
URL: https://vvaw.org/commentary/?id=918
Type: veteran-community essay. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: the authors solicited war jokes from Vietnam and Iraq/Afghanistan veterans and received mostly sanitized anecdotes; they argue authentic battle humor "strips war bare of its mythic bones," that civilians find battle jokes "repugnant" while combat veterans "belly laugh," and give examples of deadpan sergeant-major exchanges and a wishbone joke made from remains (extracted). Documents the *sanitizing for outsiders* reflex.

**E4. Matt Condon, "Veterans prove military life can be a laughing matter," *Task & Purpose*, 6 March 2020; and James Clark, "When It Comes To Military Humor, Go Easy On The Jargon, Bro," *Task & Purpose*, 29 February 2016**
URLs: https://taskandpurpose.com/culture/veterans-standup-comedy/ and https://taskandpurpose.com/humor/when-it-comes-to-military-humor-go-easy-on-the-jargon-bro
Type: journalism about veteran stand-up. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: comics describe deliberately *translating* military experience for civilians ("I started out doing zero military jokes, but eventually I realized if I can make those who have never served laugh about things that happened while I was in, I can bridge that gap a little"); jokes that rely on jargon ("outside the wire," "Wag Bag") fall flat without context, and jokes that land with civilians can bomb with active-duty troops. Concrete evidence of a two-register speaker.

**E5. Mat Best, interview by James Clark on *Thank You for My Service*, *Task & Purpose*, 14 August 2019; Steve Leonard, "The Dark Side: The Unique Humor that Binds Veterans," ClearanceJobs, 8 April 2025**
URLs: https://taskandpurpose.com/military-life/mat-best-thank-you-for-my-service/ and https://news.clearancejobs.com/2025/04/08/the-dark-side-the-unique-humor-that-binds-veterans/
Type: interview; opinion essay with citations. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: Best says he wanted the book to reflect how teams really joked, and warns a civilian partner will "stare at you like you're a lunatic"; he also reports being "a raging alcoholic, depressed" after leaving. Leonard describes ten years in civilian life without a "tribe" until dark humor connected him with another veteran, and cites Obrdlik 1942 and a 2021 *Patient* article. Opinion-grade, not research, but it states the post-service pattern in veterans' own terms.

**E6. Phil Klay, *Fresh Air*, 25 November 2014 (KCUR copy); Tom Peter, review of Roy Scranton's *War Porn*, 2 August 2016**
URLs: https://www.kcur.org/2014-11-25/in-redeployment-former-marine-explores-the-challenges-of-coming-home and https://newrepublic.com/article/135730/finally-realistic-iraq-war-novel
Type: radio interview summary; book review. Access: free. **VERIFIED-OPEN.**
What it gives a writer: Klay says he told invented "decoy stories" to civilians because they fit expectations and he could not comfortably share the real ones. The review cites survey figures that 91 percent of Americans felt proud of those serving and 76 percent had thanked a service member while majorities opposed the wars, and contrasts "the backstory we invent that compels us to thank a soldier" with "the unspoken reality." Background for the civilian-facing mask.

## (2) TOP 5 TO READ FIRST

1. **C1, Johais 2025 (open access)** and its reference list. The best map of what soldier humor does (resistance, cohesion, rank negotiation, coping) and how British, German, Danish and Israeli versions differ in style; one download, immediately usable.
2. **E1, Yoshimura et al. 2024 (PMC)**. The only open empirical study on veterans' dark humor *after* service; its finding that self-defeating humor tracks worse well-being is the single most useful corrective to the "charming wounded wisecracker" default.
3. **D1, Barents Observer 2026 (Russian deserters in Finland)**. The one verified example of deserters' actual gallows humor, with the boundary line (jokes about the war intact, jokes about why they left gone) that fits a principled defector.
4. **B8 plus B7, Army of Two postmortem and the Spec Ops thesis**. A designer's own account of the quip-after-the-kill failure, paired with the analysis of why Spec Ops' banter-heavy squad stops being caricature once it is visibly changed.
5. **A1/A2 (Klay) with A8 (Blackadder)**. Klay's "humorous and serious at once" and "funny or sad, depending on the audience," plus Robinson's "the madness that led to those people dying" principle, give the three craft rules: both registers in one beat, audience-selected versions, and targeting the institution not the dead.

## (4) OPEN THREADS

### Category (d), the thin area
1. **No source found documenting the *content* of a principled military defector's humor about his own break.** The closest verified items are D1 (deserters' war jokes stop at command brutality), D4 (Belenko's jokes at the expense of Soviet diplomats and his own situation), and D3 (Dovlatov). Everything else is humor *of exile groups* (Lithuanian DP cartoons, Timbuktu) or humor *about* the military from outside it. The honest conclusion for the character: defector humor must be built by analogy, not copied.
2. **Defector as the butt of the joke** is a real pattern (Syrian protest banners, per snippet) and is unverified. Chase: Naharnet 2012 (archived copy), Syria Untold, Syrian opposition humor coverage by Reuters/AP from 2012. Also search Israeli culture on reservists who refuse ("shministim" mockery), Russian-language jokes about "дезертир" and "беглый" (fugitive), and East German "Republikflüchtling" jokes.
