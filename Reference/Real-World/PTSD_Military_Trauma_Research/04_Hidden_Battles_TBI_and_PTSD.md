# Hidden Battles on Unseen Fronts: Stories of American Soldiers with Traumatic Brain Injury and PTSD — Extraction

**Source:** Patricia Driscoll (ed., President of The Armed Forces Foundation), *Hidden Battles on Unseen Fronts: Stories of American Soldiers with Traumatic Brain Injury and PTSD* (Casemate, 2009). Originally an epub; converted to plaintext via pandoc, ~10,528 lines.

**Coverage — read in full.** All 42 chapters (Foreword, Introduction, and an alternating sequence of first-person veteran/family "story" chapters and short professional essays by military/VA clinicians, chaplains, and program directors) were read continuously start to finish. The Acknowledgments, About the Contributors, and the Resources/Organizations directory (roughly the final 1,600 lines) were confirmed to be pure back-matter and were not extracted.

**What kind of book this is.** An Armed Forces Foundation anthology, real named individuals with real unit affiliations and medal lists, almost entirely Iraq/Afghanistan era (2003–2008). Unlike `03_Haunted_by_Combat.md`, this book is procedural and family-centered rather than memoir-and-mythology-centered: it is far richer in **concrete medical/institutional detail, caregiver and spousal perspective, and specific chronic-injury texture** than in interior monologue. Roughly two-thirds of the "story" chapters involve **polytrauma** (the real clinical term for co-occurring PTSD + TBI + physical injury) at a severity well beyond the Cancer defector's confirmed profile (functional, romanceable, mid-to-late-30s, "one or two chronic injuries") — that severity is flagged where it occurs and generally recommended for cross-series use (a more severely disabled secondary character, an Outer Tepenia case) rather than direct import into this one companion.

**Status: research only, not canon.** Same discipline as the rest of this folder.

## SECTION 1 — Ch. 1–4: Chris Harmon, military-ethics essay, Walter Blackston, TBI-care essay

**1. Undiagnosed TBI hiding behind a "obvious" physical/PTSD picture (Chris Harmon, Marine bomb-squad leader, Abu Ghraib siege).** His discharge paperwork documented physical injuries and "a two-year struggle with PTSD" but he was **never tested for TBI** despite a point-blank vehicle-IED blast — a real, named, systemic gap.
*Candidate application:* A realistic in-world justification for the defector's chronic condition being undiagnosed/unlabeled rather than clinically named — wartime medicine flagging only the "obvious" wound and missing a TBI-equivalent entirely.

**2. In-theater overmedication as a distinct, name-worthy failure mode.** Harmon was given 80mg/day of Prozac in-theater for PTSD, a dose that visibly shocked a stateside VA doctor ("I wouldn't give this to anyone in your condition, particularly in combat"); he then cycled through Zoloft, Paxil, Effexor, Cymbalta with side effects and no real relief.
*Candidate application:* Reusable texture for "over-medicated in the field, under-supported after" as a contributing reason self-management became the defector's default outside formal treatment.

**3. A full "decorated hero to homeless in under a year" arc, driven entirely by the SAME undiagnosed chronic condition compounding at every step.** Honorable discharge → a civilian job lost through no fault of his own → conflict with his own veteran father, who could not understand PTSD ("The way you're acting, you're spitting on all your dead brothers") → physically unable to sustain manual labor because of untreated injury → a security-guard job lost to physical pain → literal homelessness with a wife and children living in their car → rescue only through informal, word-of-mouth veteran-charity networks, not the official VA/military system.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE architecture for the defector's pre-Cancer years — informal charity/community succeeding where an institutional system failed or was inaccessible to a defector specifically.

**4. Plain-voice articulation of the "invisible wound isn't taken seriously" injustice.** "I don't think people understand the pain of what goes on in your head. You've lost yourself and it's scary as shit... it wasn't as though I'd lost a leg or gotten shot up. It was all in my head."
*Candidate application:* A strong, quotable frustration for the defector to voice about how Cancer/Concordia perceives him relative to visibly injured defectors.

**5. Fitness-for-Duty ethics essay (Alice Psirakis, LCSW, Army Behavioral Health) — a soldier choosing to keep fighting with open eyes.** "Sergeant K," diagnosed PTSD after two prior tours, volunteered for a third deployment; the treating clinician privately believed "this third deployment was going to be the psychological death of him. SGT K agreed" — and was deployed anyway, because his *level of functioning* (the actual DSM gating criterion, not the diagnosis itself) remained intact, and because he felt a concrete duty to his less-experienced soldiers: "If I could just sleep I'll feel better. My guys need me."
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — a soldier can be fully aware his own psychological state is deteriorating, agree explicitly that continuing is dangerous, and still choose duty over self-preservation — not denial, not bravado, a deliberate, informed, duty-driven choice. A much more specific and dignified shape for "why didn't he stop/leave sooner" than generic stubbornness, directly usable for why the defector may have continued fighting, or delayed defecting, after recognizing the toll.

**6. "Level of functioning," not diagnosis, is the actual operative clinical threshold.** Two people with an identical diagnosis can have wildly different presentations; the real gating question is impairment, not the presence of symptoms.
*Candidate application:* Reinforces keeping the defector's presentation specific (which domains are impaired, which aren't) rather than generic.

**7. Landmine blast, a devastating institutional near-death, and a durable talisman (Walter Blackston, Army SPC, Afghanistan).** Already recovering from a pre-deployment training-accident facial injury when a friend walking ahead of him stepped on a mine during a night casualty-recovery mission — Walter was blown unconscious with spinal injury and shrapnel; his friend died. Later, at Walter Reed, an infected surgical wound nearly killed him when on-duty staff was unreachable; his own mother, a retired surgical nurse, had to physically apply pressure to a severed artery herself during the ambulance ride to the OR. TBI symptoms, undiagnosed for years, included a recurring somatic detail: "I'd wake up in the morning and feel like someone was holding me down just like after the explosion when I came to" — a *daily*, not just acute-stress, re-experiencing.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — the "waking sensation replicates the injury moment" detail (a physical sensation on waking, echoing the explosion, every morning) is an excellent, specific, *recurring* physical symptom distinct from a nightmare — a strong candidate for the defector's own chronic manifestation. The medical-neglect-nearly-killed-him-after-the-war-didn't sequence is strong texture for how wartime medical infrastructure could plausibly have failed him at a vulnerable moment, independent of anything to do with his defection.

**8. Now-familiar externalized-memory tool, independently corroborated.** Blackston uses a VA-issued Palm Pilot to record everything he needs to remember, matching `07`'s Finding 122 (day-planner as externalized memory aid) in a real case.

## SECTION 2 — Ch. 5–9: Richard Gutteridge, "Give an Hour" essay, Tyler Boudreau, VA suicide hotline essay, Rob Kislow

**9. A recurring, self-imposed DUTY as the actual trauma source, distinct from the dramatic physical wound (Richard Gutteridge, Army CWO, 24-year career).** His sniper-wound was almost dismissed by him ("I felt like an idiot because I had been so careless" — he refused medevac and stayed with his men). His real wound was the sustained, 14-month task of personally recovering and searching for dog tags among dead soldiers' and Marines' remains — deliberately done by only three senior men, himself included, specifically to shield younger soldiers from it: "We took this task as seriously as defending our outpost, so we did it slowly with dignity and respect."
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — the "proven wartime loyalty" action need not be a single dramatic battle. A sustained, quietly selfless, recurring duty nobody else wanted, performed with discipline precisely because it was awful, is excellent, non-cliché architecture for what specifically earned the defector his place in Cancer.

**10. Peer-recognition, not clinical technique, repeatedly cited as the actual healing mechanism.** In a real, intensive 3-week VA/DoD PTSD program: "I looked at the other seven soldiers in the program and I saw the same worn, haggard, distant look that I became accustomed to seeing in the mirror each morning... it was the group therapy with my fellow PTSD sufferers that made the biggest difference." Even within a shared psychiatric ward, combat veterans instinctively self-segregated into their own peer subgroup, apart from young first-time trainees struggling with basic military adjustment: "Our experiences had nothing in common."
*Candidate application:* Corroborates `07`'s Finding 76 (Junger's "tribe" theory) with a concrete instance — even shared *space* doesn't substitute for shared *structural position*, reinforcing why the sole esteemed defector in Cancer is isolated in a way group therapy or general community warmth cannot fix.

**11. A commanding officer's own account of the mission-vs-welfare bind, and moral injury explicitly distinguished from fear-based trauma (Tyler Boudreau, Marine Captain).** Preparing to redeploy nine months after a brutal first tour, Boudreau had to weigh individual Marines' psychological welfare against unit readiness ("the mission always comes first; troop welfare comes after... he must love the mission more"), and his first reaction to a wave of Marines requesting psychiatric evaluation was suspicion — reading it as a trend to avoid deployment. In his own words: "Their trauma... was not all based strictly on those moments of powerlessness when they'd been shot at, mortared or IED'd. There were clear incidents of moral distress, too... As many Marines as I dealt with who were agitated by the dangers they encountered, there were as many more agitated by their own actions." He tried to talk Marines out of guilt over mistaken-identity killings using pure logic and found it had "little effect... their moral consternation existed in a vacuum beyond the scope of reason." He only understood this failure once he developed his own PTSD and had to resign his commission.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — an extremely precise, first-person articulation that moral injury is NOT resolved by logical justification, no matter how valid. If the defector's own wartime action involved a similarly "justified but still devastating" act, this gives precise language for why simple in-game reassurance from the player should not be able to "fix" his guilt — the design lesson is that logic doesn't work on this kind of wound, full stop. Also a strong template for a morally complex commanding-officer NPC in the defector's backstory: not simply cruel or simply compassionate, caught in a genuine, doctrinally-defensible structural bind.

**12. VA Suicide Hotline essay — procedural detail flagged, not narrative content used directly given its sensitivity.** Two broadly reusable structural points: (1) even a caller refusing to self-identify can often still be traced through indirect means (vehicle description, cell-tower/area code, medical-record elimination) — reusable procedural texture for an in-world crisis-response service; (2) across multiple real transcripts, what kept a caller engaged long enough to be helped was ONE consistent, already-trusted voice on the line, not the introduction of additional authority figures (a hostage negotiator was explicitly *less* trusted than the original hotline responder in one case). Given this project's "Mystery = Soft-Detail Delivery" convention, the case content itself is flagged but not recommended for direct narrative use.

**13. A pre-war hobby resumed post-war, without anyone calling it therapy, exceeding what clinical prognosis predicted (Rob Kislow, Army sharpshooter, Afghanistan, arm/leg trauma).** His childhood hunting and marksmanship with his father and brothers directly became his wartime specialty; after his injury, informal peer-suggested hunting trips ("It's pheasant season. Let's go hunting") were "the best therapy I could possibly have had," and he designed his own physical-therapy regimen against his doctors' advice, ultimately exceeding what they predicted he could do.
*Candidate application:* Directly corroborates `07`'s Findings 94–95 (Adventure/Equine Therapy — informal, self-directed return to a pre-trauma competency as accidental, unnamed therapy) with a rich, real case. A pre-war hobby resumed post-war without anyone naming it treatment is one of the strongest, most game-adaptable recovery beats in either book — especially portable given the defector's confirmed firearms/survival background.

**14. Advocating in opposite directions for two different injuries — a specific, textured disability-decision pattern.** Kislow fought his own doctors to SAVE a nearly severed, non-functional arm (13 reconstructive surgeries) while separately fighting to get an already-ruined ankle AMPUTATED faster (doctors kept trying exotic graft materials against his wishes — "I just wanted it gone"). Went through twelve prosthetic legs before finding a durable design, becoming the hospital's informal endurance-tester.
*Candidate application:* A much more textured, true-to-life model than uniform "wanting everything saved" — good if the defector's chronic injuries include a similarly counterintuitive, hard-won medical-decision history.

**15. Confirmed-kill knowledge bringing relief, not guilt — a deliberate counterweight to the moral-injury material elsewhere.** Kislow, describing the man who shot him before he killed him in return: "To be truthful, it helps me sleep knowing he is dead." Vivid, unprompted, present-tense sensory recall years later ("I wish I could go back and make a movie of that day... How the wind was blowing and the smell of the brush").
*Candidate application:* Not every combat kill produces guilt; for some veterans, confirmed lethal self-defense produces closure instead. Good nuance against writing the defector's relationship to violence as uniformly guilt-driven — one register among several available.

## SECTION 3 — Ch. 10–15: Polytrauma rehab essay, John "Medicine Bear" Radell, "weapon of choice" essay, Sylvia Blackwood-Boutelle, "I Just Want to Be Back in Iraq" essay, William Berger

**16. The precise mild-TBI/PTSD symptom profile most relevant to the defector's calibrated band (Minneapolis VA Polytrauma Transitional Rehab Program essay).** Unlike moderate-severe TBI patients, mild-TBI/PTSD patients retain *insight* into their own impairment and can show "hyperawareness of symptoms" — fixating on minor day-to-day changes from personal baseline that, on medical workup, have no organic cause. They also learn new compensatory tools rapidly and often teach themselves complex workarounds without instruction. A patient-theorized mechanism: combat-trained "360-degree" wide-area vigilance may come at a direct cost to selective, narrow, sustained attention, explaining difficulty with tasks like following a lecture despite no difficulty with broad situational awareness. A verbatim self-instructional coping phrase ("I will focus on the speaker, and try to echo each word in my head as it is said") measurably reduced lecture-distraction episodes by roughly two-thirds over 3–4 weeks.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE, directly load-bearing for the defector's specific symptom profile: self-aware, resourceful at building his own compensatory workarounds, prone to fixating on small baseline fluctuations, and a quotable in-world coping phrase ("I focus on one thing. I make myself echo it back before it can slip.") for exactly the subtle attention/concentration band `07` flagged as calibrated for him.

**17. The single strongest moral-injury case in either book, and a genuinely well-developed pre-war identity-search backstory (John "Medicine Bear" Radell, Army National Guard).** Killed a 12-year-old boy mistaken for an armed insurgent, and separately a family at a checkpoint; institutional response to his attempt to process this was "Suck it up and drive on." Too dark/specific to import verbatim, but it establishes the real upper bound of wartime moral injury and models the institutional-dismissal failure mode precisely. Separately: adopted at birth into a white family, born Blackfoot and Cherokee, spent roughly a decade of adult life actively "coming to terms with" his Native ancestry — an identity search that *preceded and was separate from* his military service — reuniting with his biological birth family only six months before deploying: "I left three families when I deployed to war: my adopted family, my biological family and Aiyana [his wife]."
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE, broadly reusable — a character whose search for belonging was *already* an active, unresolved thread before the war, who then had to leave multiple, still-forming senses of belonging behind simultaneously to deploy, is excellent architecture for the defector's own layered belonging problem (Upper Earth origin, defection, Cancer's conditional acceptance) — the war interrupting a not-yet-completed reconciliation, not starting one. Also: he was evaluated as "suffering from a moral flexibility" — his intact conscience, not a lack of one, was the disqualifying factor for redeployment — a sharp, ironic label that maps unusually well onto a defection story (the same moral clarity that got him disqualified from Upper Earth's military being what led him to defect).

**18. "I felt like I was stained. I saw blood on my hands" — a precise, plain self-description of moral-injury-driven touch-aversion specifically toward loved ones.** Radell could not hold his wife or daughter on return; became a self-described hermit for nearly two years.
*Candidate application:* A strong, portable line — numbing specifically toward intimacy rather than generalized flatness.

**19. Deliberately gaming a PTSD screening test to protect one's career — informed, willful self-management rather than denial (Sylvia Blackwood-Boutelle, Army 2nd Lieutenant, three deployments as a public-affairs/media officer).** Scored positive on every symptom on an honest first pass, refused the result, retook the test and deliberately answered differently to pass. Her non-combat MOS still produced severe PTSD from sustained secondhand violence exposure in a culture that explicitly required denial: "We were ordered to tell the good news about Iraq's reconstruction... no one talked about the gore. We denied it because if we didn't deny it we'd mess up our careers."
*Candidate application (flagged, broadly reusable — not for this male companion specifically):* A sharper, more dramatically interesting mechanism than simple denial: a person clinically self-aware enough to correctly diagnose herself, and who then consciously falsifies a second attempt to protect her position. Strong template for a non-combat-arms trauma case (a future female or non-infantry character) elsewhere in the setting.

**20. Isolation persists even inside a nominal peer group, unless someone actively breaks the silence ("I Just Want to Be Back in Iraq" essay, Alice Psirakis, LCSW — ~1,000 patients over 3 years).** Patients almost universally assumed OTHER veterans understood and discussed the same symptoms, and were "isolated in their symptoms" — none of them had actually compared notes with each other. The essay's central mechanism for *why* combat can feel easier than home: regimented structure, a clear chain of command, "automatic bonds... like no comparable bonds in the civilian world," and critically "lack of time to process what was happening. Things happened because they had to happen, without conscious effort... Otherwise, they would not have survived." Civilian life demands constant micro-decisions and ambiguous threat-assessment with no rules of engagement — exactly the unstructured processing combat never required or permitted.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — likely the single most useful abstract mechanism in this book for a veteran/defector's ongoing attachment to wartime structure, independent of enjoying violence. The defector need not miss killing to plausibly miss the war — he can miss unambiguous chain of command, automatic peer bond, and total clarity about what to do next, none of which Cancer or exile life can offer. Also directly implies that even if other defectors existed near him, he might still not spontaneously compare notes with them without a deliberate prompt.

**21. A bank of small, concrete, filmable trigger-behaviors.** Sleeping with a box cutter under the pillow; nightly perimeter "surveillance" patrols of one's own house; flinching at a door slam ruining a date, then never calling back out of shame; flashbacks from a mosque's call to prayer heard in one's own neighborhood; subway rush-hour crowding triggering rage; paralyzing fear driving under highway overpasses (drop/ambush association).
*Candidate application:* Several translate almost directly into Concordia texture — a district's soundscape/crowd density, an overpass/tunnel/transit space, a public announcement system.

**22. A distinctive, unpredictable chronic-injury type: suspected nerve-agent-exposure seizures (William Berger, Marine Corporal, engineer/security, Sunni Triangle).** Unpredictable Grand Mal seizures, including nearly drowning face-down in a shower at home; his overriding concern immediately afterward was avoiding humiliation — found standing, both legs stuffed into one pant leg, refusing to be seen as helpless. Nightmares described as qualitatively distinct from ordinary bad dreams: "They're not normal nightmares like the boogey man is going to get you. They're big and awful. I wake up and the bed is soaked... I don't go back to sleep after one of those nightmares."
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — a genuinely distinctive chronic-injury type not covered elsewhere, paired with a very human, non-melodramatic detail (dignity prioritized over safety mid-medical-emergency) — good material if the developer wants the defector's chronic injuries to include something more unusual than the pain/TBI/PTSD triad already well-covered in `07`.

## SECTION 4 — Ch. 16–22: DCoE essay, Jon Campaz, "Mental Transceiver" essay, Jose Pequeno/Nellie Bagley, Polytrauma-system essay, Brent Bretz, "Families of Honor" essay

**23. A clean, real-world TBI severity taxonomy.** Mild ("concussion" — brief change in consciousness, ~90% fully recover with no lasting effects), moderate (recognizable symptoms, may show imaging evidence without residual symptoms), severe (penetrating wound, obvious, immediate surgical treatment). Real institutional debate over favoring "combat stress reaction"/"post-traumatic stress syndrome" over "disorder," on the theory that many trauma reactions are reasonable responses to horrific events, not intrinsically pathological.
*Candidate application:* Reusable calibration scale, and further real-world precedent for a non-"disorder" in-world clinical term (consistent with `07`'s and this book's own proposed "traumatic stress injury"/similar alternatives).

**24. Never receiving a clinical diagnosis or vocabulary for years/decades — the cleanest real-world model for the defector's confirmed undiagnosed-injury profile (Jonnei "Jon" Campaz, Army Corporal, Desert Storm, 1991).** Discharged in 1993 with zero discussion of brain injury or PTSD: "No one sat me down and talked to me about mental conditions... I was walking again. I was outta there." Assumed his migraines, nightmares, flashbacks, and irritability were simply "part of what happened when you were in combat" — an unavoidable, non-remarkable cost, not a treatable condition, for over a decade.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — exactly the shape the developer's brief wants for the defector: chronic injuries, unspecified, undiagnosed by name, assumed to be a permanent unremarkable cost of having fought.

**25. A precise, mundane, food-adjacent trigger.** "I couldn't stand the smell of barbeque. Still can't. It reminded me of burning flesh... You could smell a burning body a mile away."
*Candidate application:* An excellent, plain, domestic (not obviously "war-coded") trigger — good texture for an unexpected everyday sensory tell.

**26. An extraordinary, granular, real cascading-crisis financial saga — the single most detailed account of institutional-failure-driven collapse in either book.** Undiagnosed PTSD/TBI compounded over years by diabetes, a stroke, a diabetic stomach condition that directly worked against his physical-therapy recovery, and bowel cancer — each new crisis arriving before the family recovered from the last — while a 2-year VA disability-claims backlog delayed already-approved benefits by two additional years. Specific institutional/charity gatekeeping indignities, each locally "reasonable" by the system's own rules while devastating in aggregate: a food-stamp caseworker refusing aid over an ordinary car; a charity worker denying aid after spotting a two-year-old TV bought "during better times"; a Congressional office declining to help because veterans' benefits were "not a federal agency" issue. The family pawned over $2,000 in jewelry for $300 to cover utility bills; his son was sent to live with an ex-wife because they could no longer afford to keep him.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE, broadly reusable — excellent, granular texture for how a Tepenian equivalent bureaucracy (a Cancer benefits system, an Upper Earth veterans' program) could plausibly and non-cartoonishly fail the defector during his post-defection years, with no individual actor acting in bad faith. His closing, dignified, non-bitter reframe — "I do not blame any one for what happened to me; I chose to be a soldier and I am proud to have given it my all... I choose to live humbly for one" — plus his stated motivation to become a social worker "because I've been there," is an excellent voice-register model distinct from despair or triumphant recovery.

**27. "Emotional hijacking" — a plain, non-jargon neuroscience metaphor with a worked example ("The Mental Transceiver" essay, Norman McCormack).** Trauma causes the brain to route information directly from the thalamus to the amygdala, bypassing the prefrontal cortex's reasoning step entirely — termed "emotional hijacking." The essay cites Campaz's own barbecue-smell/burning-flesh confusion (Finding 25) as a worked example.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE, broadly reusable — excellent in-world "explainer" vocabulary (a healer or the defector himself) for why a trigger produces an instant, disproportionate reaction that bypasses conscious reasoning entirely — not irrationality, a physically rerouted signal. The circuit/short-circuit framing is thematically resonant for the robot-consciousness pillar as well, not just for this one human companion.

**28. A named, institutionally-recognized room for delivering catastrophic medical news, and a caregiver's refusal to accept a fatalistic prognosis that later changed clinical practice (Jose Pequeno & mother Nellie Bagley — catastrophic grenade-blast brain injury).** Doctors told his mother he would die or be permanently vegetative six separate times over 29 months, including in a room families nicknamed "the Nutty Room" (specifically where bad news was delivered): "He'll be a vegetable. I don't give him two days, so be realistic and accept it." She refused this outright — "You've no right or power to make that call... Go back and do your job" — and the same doctor later told her this encounter permanently changed how he delivered news to other families.
*Candidate application (too catastrophic for the defector's own confirmed profile, but broadly reusable):* A named, informally-recognized "bad news room" is excellent, portable worldbuilding texture for a Cancer/Sanctuary hospital. The mother's refusal, later vindicated as having changed the clinician's own practice, is a strong template for a fierce advocate/caregiver secondary character anywhere in the setting.

**29. Caregiver secondary trauma articulated with precision.** "The big things I can handle great. It's the little things that hit me. I don't have the patience to deal with the details... how nobody will give me a direct answer about my son."
*Candidate application:* A precise, quotable articulation of secondary/caregiver trauma manifesting as an inversion of expectation — small bureaucratic friction undoing someone more than the large catastrophe itself.

**30. Heterotopic ossification — a genuinely distinctive, ongoing, active chronic physical complication (Brent Bretz, Army sniper, catastrophic IED).** Bone tissue growing abnormally around wound sites instead of soft tissue, described as "almost as lethal as the blast" — grew in painful lumps, made prosthetic use impossible, eventually pushed surgical hardware physically through his skin.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — a specific, unusual, ongoing (not static/healed-over) chronic complication that periodically requires further surgical intervention, if the developer wants the defector's injury to be more textured than "an old wound that still hurts."

**31. A third+ independent instance of family recognizing PTSD before official caregivers did.** Bretz's mother correctly identified PTSD (not simple "clinical depression," his official diagnosis) before his care team did, and was met with surprise rather than immediate agreement.
*Candidate application:* Worth treating as a deliberately repeated, load-bearing pattern across this research (also seen with Barlowe, Weinburgh, and others below) rather than coincidence.

**32. "Grieving a loss without death" — a precise, reusable clinical/emotional concept for how loved ones experience a changed survivor ("Families of Honor" essay, Dr. Mary Car-Blanchard).** Caregivers must simultaneously grieve the pre-injury person while getting to know a genuinely different post-injury person occupying the same relationship, without the closure death would provide: "This is not the same man I married... I miss that person."
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE, broadly reusable — directly useful vocabulary for how the defector's own surviving pre-war relationships (if any) experienced his change, independent of any specific incident.

## SECTION 5 — Ch. 23–28: David Emme, "Mental Transceiver" continuation, Duval Diaz, "Battle for Love" essay, Nicholas Firth, "Facing Our Fears" essay

**33. An unresolved, genuinely ambiguous memory held without needing resolution (David Emme, Army Sergeant, ex-Marine).** Approaching Talafar, he saw only teenage boys in the streets making a "cut your throat" gesture at his convoy; his in-the-moment read was hostile intent, his retrospective read is that they may have been trying to warn the convoy — immediately undercut by his own honest admission: "if they had tried we probably would have ran them over or even shot them."
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE, non-cliché moral-injury texture — an ambiguous memory the defector still turns over without resolution (threat vs. warning, both readings held simultaneously), rather than a clearly resolved guilt or clearly resolved innocence.

**34. "Word aphasia" — a precise, distinctive cognitive symptom, plus a realistic note that well-meant normalizing comparisons don't land as comforting.** Concept known, name lost — distinct from generic forgetfulness. Emme notes other people's "that happens to me too!" reassurance explicitly does *not* comfort him, because it isn't the same thing.
*Candidate application:* A precise, textured, distinctive cognitive-symptom candidate for the defector, paired with realistic texture that casual normalizing can feel dismissive rather than kind.

**35. A self-directed daily-phone-call peer-support relationship, explicitly credited with cognitive recovery.** A best friend called Emme every day for 18 months, one to three hours at a stretch — "Without him, I don't think I would have recovered as much as I have."
*Candidate application:* An informal, sustained "found family" recovery detail distinct from any formal program — good template for a defector's own informal support relationship maintained through sheer repeated small contact.

**36. Layered, compounding reenlistment motivation, not purely patriotic (Duval Diaz, National Guard, cook/driver/security, Afghanistan).** Re-enlisted the day after 9/11 while working airport security — but the motivation was explicitly compounded by a need for "a new purpose in life" following his father's death from Alzheimer's, then, seven months later, the death of his own 10-year-old son.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE architecture for why the defector might have thrown himself back into military service/danger at a specific personal low point rather than from pure ideology.

**37. A twice-repeated "reconciliation arrived too late" pattern across two generations.** Diaz's own father abandoned him at 2; by the time Diaz was ready to reconcile as an adult, the father's Alzheimer's meant he could not remember who Diaz was — and the identical pattern repeated with Diaz's own estranged son, reunited only shortly before the boy's sudden death.
*Candidate application:* Genuinely tragic, specific grief architecture if the defector has an estranged family member whose relationship was never properly resolved, especially predating his military service.

**38. An explicit, named cultural "machismo" framework around help-seeking, broken by one person's sheer persistence.** Diaz's half-sister: "Our men will never admit anything is wrong. In our culture men don't cry... Getting help doesn't mean you're a pansy." She spent "a thousand hours" on the phone with him before he acknowledged needing to talk to someone.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — directly reusable for the defector's own culture of origin having its own version of this exact taboo, broken only by one person's stubborn, ordinary-language persistence rather than any formal program.

**39. A single loaded word carrying opposite meanings for two partners — a clean romance-arc misunderstanding device.** When Diaz says "I've got to leave this house" during an argument, he means "I need a break"; his wife hears an existential abandonment threat: "Alarm bells go off for me."
*Candidate application:* A small, extremely relatable, portable device for a romance-arc miscommunication beat with the defector.

**40. Vulnerability is structurally incompatible with combat conditioning — the core mechanism of the entire intimacy problem ("The Battle for Love" essay, Dr. Mitchell Tepper).** "Vulnerability is inconsistent with survival on the battlefield," so the very trained self-protection that kept a soldier alive becomes a direct structural barrier to both emotional and sexual intimacy, which both require exactly the vulnerability combat conditioning suppressed. "Secondary traumatization" named explicitly as a real phenomenon in partners who stay.

**41. George Alexander case study — a witness-protection identity change as an almost literal metaphor for the defector's own situation, and a mature "fourth relationship, after three failures" romance-arc shape.** Son of a traumatized WWII Marine sniper, developed his own secondary-traumatization symptoms by age six; at 19, entered the federal Witness Protection Program and took on an entirely new legal identity — which did not erase his trauma: "his new life... was haunted by his past traumatic experiences." 35 years, three divorces, and two broken engagements later, he finally achieved a stable relationship — his fourth attempt — with a partner who "taught him the difference between love, sex and intimacy," defined concretely as "sharing our feelings, our values, our thoughts, and most importantly, our love." His own plain articulation: "I had built a wall around me to protect myself, which ultimately wound up preventing anyone from getting close enough to love me."
*Candidate application (flagged, broadly reusable — this is civilian/secondary trauma, not combat, but unusually resonant):* The witness-protection identity change is a strikingly literal parallel to any defector/exile's situation — trauma follows the *person*, not the name, exactly the shape of the Cancer defector's predicament (a new life in Cancer does not erase what happened in Upper Earth). The "fourth relationship, after three real failures, finally worked" arc-shape is a mature, non-cliché model directly counter to a first-try-succeeds romance cliché, useful if the developer wants the defector's romance arc with the player explicitly framed as following a string of real past failures rather than his first attempt at closeness since the war.

**42. "Compassionate" vs. "self-centered/conditional" love, explicitly distinguished as two structurally different modes a partner can offer.** The essay frames sustaining a relationship through this kind of damage as "a new type of bravery, a new type of hero," explicitly extending heroism to partners who stayed and fought for the relationship, not only to the veteran.
*Candidate application:* A clean, thematically-loaded distinction the player's own choices could legibly embody in a romance arc with the defector.

**43. Fighting FOR elective amputation against medical caution — a second independent case, reinforcing Kislow's (Finding 14) as a real, recurring pattern (Nicholas Firth, Army Corporal, IED gunner injury).** Advocated for 8+ months, against doctors' recommendation to wait "four or five years," for immediate amputation of an already-nonfunctional foot — motivated concretely by wanting to stand unaided at his own wedding.
*Candidate application:* A legitimate, recurring real pattern (not a fluke) if the defector's own chronic injury involves a similarly hard-won, patient-driven medical decision made against institutional default caution.

**44. A conscious, deliberate caregiving TECHNIQUE — performing calm because the patient is reading your face for the true severity of his condition.** Firth's mother: "I never let on that I was shocked, or fearful, or angry or depressed... because I knew he was watching me. He was measuring his condition by the look in my eyes." She broke down only once, privately, at the unrelated sight of a different, unaccompanied amputee soldier — grief displaced onto a stranger's isolation rather than her own son's condition.
*Candidate application:* An excellent, precise, *conscious* (not naive) coping mechanism — good texture for how a defector's own family/partner/mentor figure might have deliberately managed their visible reactions around him during his worst period.

**45. Permanent long-term autobiographical amnesia, distinct from short-term forgetfulness.** "I don't remember most of my childhood in the Philippines or when I was a teenager back in the states. My past is just gone."
*Candidate application:* A genuinely distinctive form of memory loss (permanent gaps in his OWN personal history, not just short-term lapses) — good candidate if the developer wants the defector's TBI to include holes in his own pre-war history that even he cannot fill in, distinct from war-trauma memories he's actively avoiding.

**46. An itemized, non-obvious taxonomy of specific fears driving PTSD-avoidance ("Facing Our Fears" essay, Barbara Romberg PhD).** Fear of appearing weak to others AND to oneself; fear of being labeled "crazy" and fear of actually being "crazy"; fear of stigma; fear of one's own capacity for anger/disengagement *harming* the people who welcome them home (fear of becoming dangerous to loved ones, not just fear of being hurt); fear of the numbness itself, experienced as a further loss rather than relief. A late addition, requested by a veteran reader: **"fear of dishonor"** — silence to protect the reputation of one's unit, service, and family, a collective rather than personal stake. Also cites Jonathan Shay's Vietnam-era clinical observation that traumatized veterans habitually test, push, and provoke helpers, specifically *seeking confirmation* of an expected betrayal, and that years of accumulated disappointment eventually destroy the basic *capacity* to trust, not just the willingness.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — a ready-made menu for selecting which specific, non-obvious fears drive the defector's guardedness (fear of harming loved ones; fear of dishonoring his old unit/cause rather than personal pride; fear of numbness as loss). The "testing/provoking to confirm an expected betrayal" mechanic is an excellent, precise, dramatically useful design note: the defector's occasional hostility or provocation toward the player, especially early on, could be modeled as unconscious testing behavior rather than random rudeness.

## SECTION 6 — Ch. 29–34: Bryan Lane, evidence-based-psychotherapy essay (two full case studies), Nathan Toews, "Falling Through the Cracks" essay, Michael & Suki Mills, "Tending, Attending and Healing" essay

**47. Explicit, unashamed self-report of professional emotional hardening as a neutral, adaptive fact — not heroic stoicism, not traumatic breakdown (Bryan Lane, Army Special Operations sniper).** Photographing a dying, still-breathing suicide-bomber for ID purposes: "I don't care about this guy. This doesn't mean a thing to me... I hope he stops breathing soon" — offered by a man who elsewhere describes himself as "at heart a humanist."
*Candidate application:* A rare, plainly-stated acknowledgment of hardening as simply what the job required, offered flatly rather than with shame or pride — a cooler emotional register than the heavily moral-injury-weighted material found elsewhere, good for a different flavor of veteran.

**48. A fierce, specific attachment to one hand-made piece of gear, mid-medical-crisis.** Still conscious during emergency surgery, Lane fought hospital staff who wanted to cut off his self-sewn body-armor vest: "I made my vest myself, and it took me over a month to sew it up. No way were they going to chop it up."
*Candidate application:* A small, specific, non-generic detail — good texture for a defector's own disproportionate attachment to one hand-made or hand-maintained piece of equipment.

**49. Two full CPT/PE case studies with verbatim "stuck point" language ("Realizing the Promise of Evidence-Based Psychotherapies" essay).** "Mr. C," a sniper, identified specific stuck points including "I am weak for having PTSD," "I no longer fit in society," "I could have prevented my friend's death," and — the most useful one — **"No one respects me unless I get angry"** — discovering his primary underlying emotion was anger, fueled by this unexamined belief. Successfully treated (CAPS 70→22), he proactively brought his fiancée into couples counseling to discuss the impact of his choosing to return to military service *before* re-enlisting, not after. "Mr. B" retrospectively blamed himself for "not protecting his men more effectively" during an IED blast — an assessment his own carefully re-examined memory directly *contradicted* (he had, in fact, continued leading and taking defensive action until he lost consciousness); his resolution, delivered verbatim: "I did, in fact, do the best that I could." He arrived at his final session in his best suit.
*Candidate application:* "No one respects me unless I get angry" is an excellent, specific, quotable stuck-point belief for a defector who unconsciously equates anger with earned respect — good if the writers want an unexamined belief driving a specific behavior pattern. Mr. B's arc is an excellent template for "the truth turns out kinder than the guilt" — his own detailed, carefully revisited memory disproving his self-blaming narrative, rather than either full guilt or full innocence being simply asserted. The "arrived in his best suit" detail is a strong, wordless signal of restored self-respect.

**50. "Self-discovery" — an actual clinical term for repeatedly, viscerally re-learning a fact about one's own body/injury that was already "told" but not absorbed (Nathan Toews, Army mechanic-turned-gunner, severe polytrauma).** Contradicted his own ophthalmologist to his face about having lost his eye — "What are you retarded? Of course I can see. Check it out" — immediately before the exam proved otherwise.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — a precise, reusable term and mechanic for a scene where the defector must be told something about himself (an injury, a fact from his own past) that he's technically already been told, with the "real" discovery landing later, independently, with fresh emotional impact.

**51. A "recovery as a competitive personal mission" register, distinct in tone from the more common faith- or therapy-driven recoveries.** Told a "few years" was the average recovery time for his condition, Toews "made it my mission to prove her wrong" and was cleared for discharge from intensive therapy within three weeks. Closing philosophy after a single acknowledged breakdown: "I'm not a believer in wallowing in self-pity or 'woe is me'... I made my own choices and now I have to get on with the rest of my life."
*Candidate application:* A colder, more disciplined, agency-focused register that could suit the defector's confirmed 1w2/Type-1 personality especially well — a clinical prognosis treated as a challenge to be defeated through sheer discipline, rather than a passive prediction to accept.

**52. The "bridge" metaphor — the single strongest reusable structural framework in this book ("Falling Through the Cracks" essay, Lt. Col. Cynthia Rasmussen, Combat Stress Officer/SARC).** Reentry from war reframed as crossing a bridge riddled with "cracks" (subtle, slow-accumulating problems — not fitting in, not feeling safe, communication difficulty) and "holes" (catastrophic failures — divorce, prison, suicide, homelessness), with survival depending heavily on access to an informal, well-placed guiding "supply sergeant" figure rather than on willpower alone. Precise articulation of why hard-won wartime trust calibration becomes a direct barrier at home: "we teach our warriors not to trust anyone or anything except other warriors, those close to them who talk, act and dress just like they do."
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE, broadly reusable across the whole setting — an outstanding structural device for designing the defector's own post-defection years as a sequence of near-misses and close calls (cracks he almost fell through, one or two holes he genuinely fell into and climbed back out of) rather than a single clean redemption arc. The trust-calibration point directly and precisely explains why even a warm, welcoming Cancer community fundamentally cannot meet his calibrated trust threshold, since it was built entirely around resembling a unit that no longer exists and has no analog in Concordia.

**53. A wife's escalating, self-taught medical advocacy, and a simultaneous dual-family-crisis (Michael & Suki Mills, Army National Guard, HETT convoy IED/fire).** Suki caught a missed pressure-ulcer, stood her ground against a condescending doctor ("I made sure I completely understood who, what, where and why"), and was forced to split herself between her own dying mother and her injured husband at once, resolved practically (his sister covering caregiving in her absence) rather than sentimentally. Physical hallucination/confusion detail: mistaking a wound-vacuum device on his own hand for an embedded knife, culminating in him screaming "You don't fu**ing understand" at her — absorbed without excusing or resenting: "I knew he didn't mean it, but that didn't make it hurt at the moment any less." His own turning point: "I was a victim but I had a choice."
*Candidate application:* The simultaneous, divided-caregiving crisis is a realistic complicating detail if the defector's own support network has competing obligations elsewhere. "I was a victim but I had a choice" is an excellent, compact, quotable line distinguishing victimhood (a fact) from agency (an ongoing choice).

**54. "Transform ghosts into ancestors" — the single best thematic phrase in this book for long-term trauma integration ("Tending, Attending and Healing" essay, Dr. Joseph Bobrow, quoting psychoanalyst Hans Loewald).** Healing reframed not as symptom reduction but as moving a haunting, ever-present past into something integrated and honored, settled into history rather than perpetually re-lived.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — an elegant, quotable thematic phrase, directly portable as an in-world concept (a Cancer/Sanctuary spiritual-therapeutic framework, or the defector's own private vocabulary for what he's slowly trying to do with his memories of the dead). Strong candidate for a title or recurring line in his personal questline.

**55. Two small, wordless bonding/expression details from a real peer-support retreat.** Two newly-met, visually-impaired TBI veterans wordlessly touching each other's facial scars in greeting — an instant, deep, nonverbal bonding ritual unique to shared injury. A severely TBI/PTSD-affected veteran was found to have secretly drawn three small "family trees" cataloguing everyone lost as collateral consequences of his unit's actions, discarded on the floor as "nothing" — evidence of intact, ongoing moral processing beneath a controlled surface, found by accident rather than volunteered.
*Candidate application:* The scar-touching ritual is an outstanding, specific, nonverbal bonding beat, directly portable between the defector and another wounded character (human or robot) who shares his structural position. The discarded drawing is an excellent model for how the player might discover the depth of the defector's inner life — not through him telling them, but through finding something he made and dismissed.

## SECTION 7 — Ch. 35–42: Duval Diaz continuation, Kelly Meister & Cody Sepulvida, "Road Back to Self" essay, John & Lindsey Weinburgh, closing community-outreach essay

**56. Building 18 — a notorious, real, squalid transitional-housing building — recurs across THREE independent chapters (Blackston, Diaz, and referenced elsewhere), and self-advocacy/outright refusal repeatedly (not passively) changed the assignment.** Diaz refused a doctor's housing letter over his back condition, was told he had no choice, and simply refused until reassigned.
*Candidate application:* Reinforces that active refusal, not passive acceptance, is what actually worked against institutional inertia — a recurring, corroborated pattern.

**57. A counterpoint "same person, intensified, not replaced" identity framing.** Diaz explicitly resists the "I'm a new person now" language used by several other veterans in this research: "I am the same Duval I always was, but what I personally went through has made me more jumpy and irritable... It has also made me more human."
*Candidate application:* A useful counterpoint voice to the identity-reconstruction language logged elsewhere (e.g., Devore Barlowe's "I'm making up a new person") — worth keeping BOTH framings available depending on which reads truer for the defector's specific psychology.

**58. A precisely-specified friendly-fire trauma with permanently unresolved personal culpability — the strongest, most non-cliché moral-injury architecture in this book (Kelly Meister & Cody Sepulvida, Marine Corps Sergeants, a 19-year same-sex military relationship ending in permanent, devastating separation).** During a third ambush, without the standard countdown-before-firing protocol being followed, Cody's unit fired on fellow American soldiers under heightened alert; she never learned whether her own rounds personally wounded anyone.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — a specific, procedurally-nameable cause (a broken protocol) combined with permanent, irresolvable uncertainty about personal culpability is exceptionally strong moral-injury architecture — the *not-knowing itself*, rather than confirmed guilt, is the wound. Directly useful if the defector's own defining incident should hinge on a comparable protocol failure with permanently unresolved personal culpability.

**59. A caregiver-exploited-by-institutional-staff subplot, rare and sharp.** During Cody's intensive PTSD treatment, a hospital nursing assistant deliberately introduced her to street drugs and pursued an "illicit relationship" with her as a patient — later investigated and fired specifically because of Kelly's own persistence.
*Candidate application (flagged, broadly reusable, too catastrophic for the defector's own arc):* A genuinely rare betrayal-of-trust variant — good material for a Cancer/Sanctuary institutional-failure subplot involving a corrupt or predatory caregiver figure, distinct from the far more common "merely slow/underfunded" institutional failure mode logged repeatedly elsewhere in this research.

**60. An unsentimental, hard-won closing philosophy offered as the dark end of the outcome spectrum.** "No matter how strong your love is for each other... when one partner goes off to war they come home in their own 'mental world of hell.' There are no doctors, no medicines, and no programs that can make them the same person they were before they left for war. What I learned was that miracles will never happen." Yet Kelly chose to live in the town Cody loved, years after a restraining order, specifically "so she'll know where to find me if she ever needs me."
*Candidate application:* A stark, honest counterweight to every "love conquers PTSD" narrative logged elsewhere — worth preserving precisely because this project's "no costless good endings" rule means not every relationship thread should resolve well. The final gesture (enduring, unconditional love persisting without expecting reciprocity) is a genuinely moving, non-cliché image distinct from either a happy or a bitter ending.

**61. "Supported employment" — job-first, stability-follows, a counterintuitive real vocational-rehab model ("The Road Back to Self" essay, David Carroll PhD).** "Joe," diagnosed bipolar post-deployment, stabilized via keeping a job of his own choosing with ongoing rehab-specialist support, rather than being required to demonstrate stability *before* working; he explicitly credited having a job — not therapy alone — as "life-changing." A therapist's single reframing question produced the turning point for a different veteran, "Phil": rather than accepting his framing of "what do I need to do to satisfy the court" (post-DUI), the therapist asked what was "in his own best interest instead" — surfacing that Phil's real crisis was feeling his "decorated combat service and successful rehabilitation did not mean much now that he was back home," not the drinking itself, which was only the visible symptom.
*Candidate application:* Phil's reframe is an excellent, small, exportable therapeutic-dialogue technique — a good model for a key conversation where an ally deliberately refuses the defector's minimizing framing of his own problem and redirects to what he actually needs. Phil's root cause (feeling his sacrifice is now irrelevant/unseen) is a clean articulation of the "public standing vs. private worth" tension already central to the defector's confirmed Boone-style premise.

**62. A training-accident (not combat) injury as a genuinely distinctive origin mechanism, and checkpoint/gatekeeper duty as a sustained, repeated-decision source of moral fatigue (John & Lindsey Weinburgh, Army Sergeant, C5 quadriplegic + PTSD + TBI).** Weinburgh's catastrophic spinal injury occurred not in combat but during STATESIDE TRAINING for a planned redeployment — a tree, struck by his swerving vehicle, split and fell on him in an open turret. His actual in-country trauma was the grinding daily moral ambiguity of Green Zone checkpoint duty — not one incident, but a repeated, unresolvable judgment call: "A car would come toward me, and something would stand out or look funny and I'd say to myself, 'Is this going to be the guy who blows himself up?'" — "always walking a thin line between not wanting to be a jerk but at the same time getting sort of mean about who we let pass."
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — a training-accident-during-war-preparation origin is a genuinely distinctive, non-cliché mechanism, carrying its own bitter irony without diminishing severity, if the developer wants the defector's defining injury to stem from the war's logistics rather than direct enemy action. Checkpoint/gatekeeper duty as sustained moral fatigue (rather than one climactic battle) is excellent, underused trauma architecture — good fit if his wartime role involved a comparable ongoing, ambiguous-judgment duty (screening refugees, deciding who to trust, deciding who passes a line).

**63. An extremely specific environmental (not personal/auditory) trigger, made categorically crueler by an unrelated physical limitation.** "Any closed door freaks me out. Even if I'm not in the room and I know the door is closed, I want it opened" — later shown literally trapped, panicking, during a hospital fire drill when his own room's fireproof door sealed shut, now unable to physically get up and open it himself.
*Candidate application:* HIGH VALUE — a strong, portable, mechanically concrete trigger (a state of the environment, not a sound or person), and a good demonstration of how an ordinary trigger becomes categorically worse when compounded by an unrelated physical limitation.

**64. A couple's DELIBERATE decision to hire outside care specifically to preserve their marriage, rather than the caregiving spouse performing total self-sacrifice.** Lindsey's own candid admission: "When you're taking care of a member of your family, the intimacy can make it difficult... I began to feel resentful." The Weinburghs' solution was not heroic endurance but practical outsourcing (three shifts of professional nursing) specifically to protect the relationship space.
*Candidate application:* An unusually mature, practical, non-martyrdom-coded relationship-preservation choice — good, reusable texture: sometimes the correct "good ending" move for a caregiving partner is to accept help and preserve the relationship, not to perform total self-sacrifice.

**65. "The first time (outside of therapy) that anyone asked him to share his story" — a devastatingly simple illustration of how rare genuine listening is (closing community-outreach essay, Barbara Tiner).** A decades-post-war Vietnam veteran, at a church discussion series, said this was the first such invitation he'd ever received outside a clinical setting.
*Candidate application:* Reusable as ambient world-detail for how starved of unprompted listening any veteran/defector character in Concordia might be, independent of formal counseling access.

## Cross-cutting synthesis — highest-value findings across the whole book

1. **A sustained, quietly selfless recurring duty (Finding 9), not a single dramatic battle**, is the strongest available real-world model for what the defector's "proven wartime loyalty" action might actually have been — something awful, ongoing, and voluntarily shouldered to spare others, rather than one cinematic moment.
2. **A specific, procedurally-nameable failure with permanently unresolved personal culpability (Finding 58)** — a broken protocol, an unknown outcome — is the single strongest moral-injury mechanism surfaced across either book in this folder: the not-knowing, not a confirmed act, is the wound.
3. **The "bridge" metaphor (Finding 52)** is the best available structural device for designing the defector's post-defection years as a sequence of near-misses (cracks) and genuine crises survived (holes), rather than one clean arc.
4. **"Level of functioning" as the real gating criterion, not diagnosis (Findings 6, 16)**, and **the mild-TBI/PTSD-specific symptom profile (Finding 16)** together give the most clinically precise available calibration for exactly the "functional but still carrying it" band the developer's brief asks for.
5. **Moral injury is not resolved by logical justification (Finding 11)** is the single most important design constraint this book surfaces: no dialogue option built on reassurance or argument should be able to "fix" the defector's guilt outright — only reframing, ritual, and time can, and even those only partially.
6. **The romance/intimacy material (Findings 40–42, 46, 60, 64)** is unusually rich and directly load-bearing given the defector's confirmed romanceable status — vulnerability-as-structural-barrier, the "fourth relationship after three failures" arc-shape, and the deliberate-outside-help-to-preserve-intimacy model together sketch a mature, non-cliché romance arc that doesn't rely on the player being his first attempt at closeness since the war.

None of the above is proposed as settled backstory — it is the strongest, most convergent material this extraction surfaced, offered as candidates for whoever writes the defector's full character file next.
