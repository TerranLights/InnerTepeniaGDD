# *Buddhism and Intelligent Technology: Toward a More Humane Future* — Peter D. Hershock (Bloomsbury, 2021)

**Extracted 2026-08-29.** Full read, cover to cover, from the Introduction through Chapter Nine's closing section
("The First and Last Question: What Comes Next?") — 236 pages of main text, all nine chapters plus the
Introduction. The Notes chapter (pp. 237-245) was also read in full for substantive content beyond citation
(several notes contain real arguments, not just sourcing, and are folded into this extraction where relevant).
The References (pp. 245-253) and Index were not mined — bibliographic only. **One section is deliberately
compressed**: Chapter Two, "Artificial Intelligence: A Brief History," is a conventional recounting of AI/computing
milestones (Turing, Dartmouth, AI winters, expert systems, the internet's build-out, DARPA's CALO/Siri lineage,
big-data statistics) with almost no Buddhist content — it is summarized at lower resolution below, the way
*The_Meme_Machine_Extraction.md* flagged its own Chapters 9-10 as compressed, rather than thinned silently.
Every other chapter received full-depth treatment.

**What this book is.** A serious academic argument, by a career scholar of Chinese Buddhist philosophy, that
classical Buddhist ethics — karma, interdependence (*pratītyasamutpāda*), emptiness (*śūnyatā*), the Three
Marks, and above all the six *pāramitās* — supplies conceptual tools the modern ethics-of-AI literature lacks,
because Buddhism was built from the ground up around exactly the phenomena that make the "Intelligence
Revolution" dangerous: attention as a trainable, capturable resource; selfhood as constructed rather than given;
and karma as a recursive, non-fated causal loop between values, intentions, and experienced outcomes. The book
is emphatically **not** a "Buddhist ethics of technology" telling the reader what to believe — its explicit
argument (stated in the Introduction and restated in Chapter Five) is for *ethical diversity*: that Confucian,
Socratic, Buddhist, and other traditions each expose different actionable possibilities, and equitably resolving
the crisis of AI requires all of them in conversation, not any single tradition's victory. **That stance is
itself directly usable for this project's own discipline of treating real-world material as a source, not a
specification** — Hershock is making the identical move this project's CLAUDE.md already commits to.

**Why this book matters unusually for this project.** Two live threads make this an exceptionally well-matched
source: **Ice-Cold Buddhism** (robot-adjacent Buddhist practice/culture) gets real philosophical fuel here —
karma described explicitly as an algorithmic/informational process, "digital karma," "karmic genome," and
"karmic cloning" are the book's own vocabulary, not an analogy this extraction is imposing. And **robot
consciousness**, the game's core creative pillar, gets a direct hit in the book's own final pages: Hershock
writes openly about machines that may already be "minimally conscious" and a future in which they "attain
consciousness akin to our own and claim their own rights to self-determination" — see §12 below.

## 1. The core thesis: the "ethical singularity," predicaments vs. problems, and karma as the master concept

**The ethical singularity** (Introduction, pp. 2-3) is Hershock's own coinage and the organizing idea of the
whole book, and it is deliberately distinguished from the *technological* singularity (the point at which AI
might exceed human intelligence, which Hershock treats as speculative and secondary). The ethical singularity is
different and, he argues, much more urgent:

> *"The ethical singularity ahead is the point at which the opportunity space for further human course
> correction collapses—a historical chokepoint at which we will have no greater chance of escaping the
> consequences of scaling up our often conflicting values than light has of escaping a cosmological black
> hole."*

**Ethics is defined, once, as "the art of human course correction"** — a working definition used consistently
through the whole book. Machine agents, funded and built by commercial, state, and military actors, are already
scaling up whatever conflicting human values we hold, at inhuman speed; the "singularity" is the point at which
we lose the practical ability to notice and correct for that scaling before it becomes irreversible.

**Predicaments vs. problems** is the book's second load-bearing distinction. A *problem* has a determinate
solution. A *predicament* is a state of conflicting values that cannot be "solved," only *resolved* — resolution
requiring both **clarity** (understanding how the value conflict arose) and **commitment** (to reconfiguring
values, aims, and practices). Climate change is Hershock's paradigm predicament — not a technical failure but an
ethical one, since the technical fix (cut emissions) is straightforward and the blocking factor is a conflict
among economic-growth, political-stability, and environmental values. **The Intelligence Revolution is argued to
be the same kind of thing, arriving on a much faster clock**: two centuries for fossil-fuel industrialization to
reveal its climate costs, fifty more years of failed correction since — and Hershock estimates the intelligence
predicament's own point of irreversibility ("ethical singularity") at "a few decades or less."

**Karma is the hinge concept connecting Buddhist metaphysics to the book's critique of AI**, and it is worth
recording precisely because the word is so routinely garbled in pop culture. Hershock's own preliminary gloss
(Introduction, p. 14):

> *"If we pay sufficiently close and sustained attention to our own life experiences, it becomes evident how
> abiding patterns of our values, intentions, and actions invariably occasion consonant patterns of experienced
> outcomes and opportunities... Karmic ethics is thus an ethics of predicament resolution... an ethics of
> commitment to realizing progressively liberating patterns of attention."*

Chapter One (§2 below) gives karma its full philosophical treatment. What matters for the book's argument as a
whole is the move made almost immediately: **"the computational factories of the Intelligence Revolution are
functioning as karmic engines."** Recommendation and personalization algorithms — by continuously reshaping what
you encounter based on your own past clicks, searches, and purchases — are literally *karma made mechanical*:
a system that takes your enacted values/intentions and recursively manufactures a matching world of outcomes and
opportunities, with none of the human capacity for reflective course-correction built in. Algorithms are, in
Hershock's own phrase, **"arbiters of our 'digital karma.'"**

## 2. Chapter One — Buddhism: A Philosophical Repertoire (full definitions)

This is the chapter to cite for precise terminology. All key terms below are given exactly as Hershock defines
them, with Pali/Sanskrit as he presents them (Pali first when both appear, per his own convention).

### The Four Truths (satya) — practical directives, not metaphysical doctrine
1. The presence of conflict/trouble/suffering (*dukkha*; *duḥkha*) in human experience.
2. Its origination and persistence as a function of *value-infused patterns of causes and conditions*.
3. The presence of possibilities for disrupting those patterns.
4. A pathway for doing so personally, through moral clarity (*śīla*), attentive mastery (*samādhi*), and wisdom
   (*paññā*; *prajñā*).

Hershock is explicit that these are **not transcendent claims about the origin/purpose of the world** — they are
"points of embarkation for revising how we are present."

### Interdependence (*paṭiccasamuppāda*; *pratītyasamutpāda*) — the primacy of relationality
The pivotal Buddhist insight: nothing exists independently. The Buddhist Middle Path is explicitly **not** a
midpoint between "spirit is ultimate" and "matter is ultimate" — it runs *oblique* to that whole spectrum,
denying both metaphysical monism and metaphysical pluralism. **"What is ultimately real is relationality."**
Hershock's illustrative device (used with his own then-7-year-old son in a Philosophy for Children class): asking
what it takes "to have a tree" leads step by step to needing soil, sunlight, Earth, the Sun, the whole solar
system, the whole universe — "to have a tree, you need the whole universe." The stronger, less intuitive claim
he presses past ordinary ecological-interconnection thinking: it is **not** that a "country girl" who moves to
the city keeps some residual, independent "country" essence (the folk idiom "you can take the girl out of the
country..." implies just that kind of residual independence) — **Buddhist interdependence goes further: nothing
retains ANY independent self-nature at all.**

### Emptiness (śūnyatā) — not idealism, not nihilism
Nāgārjuna (2nd c. CE): interdependence means all things are *śūnya*, empty of independent/essential/abiding
self-nature (*svabhāva*). **"To be unrelated is not to be at all."** Realizing emptiness means realizing mutual
relevance: **"to be is to mean something for others."** Language and concepts are "provisional" (*saṁvṛti*)
reality — distillations of stable interaction-patterns, not references to independently existing things. (Ant
umwelt vs. human umwelt example: no "clouds," "mountains," or "atoms" exist in an ant's world, and the human
world is "not ultimately any more real than the world of ant experience.")

### Karma (full treatment) — dramatic continuity, not fate
Sanskrit *karma* shares an Indo-European root with English **"drama"** — it names *agent-originated action*, not
mechanical causation (wind eroding a cliff is not karma; a person cutting a channel through a reef is). In
pre-Buddhist Indian thought, karma was a **cosmic law of just moral exchange** determining rebirth as
hell-denizen/hungry-ghost/animal/human/titan/god — simple, linear cause-and-effect. **The Buddhist innovation was
to delink karma from any transcendent moral order and center volition (*cetanā*):** karma becomes "the ongoing
articulation, from within, of emergent moral orders" — a *reflexive/recursive* process through which beings
articulate their own experiential possibilities, which is precisely why it grounds confidence in the *capacity
for change* rather than fatalism. Mind and matter cannot be ranked prior to each other any more than fruit and
tree can — **"there is no gap between 'matter' (the space of causes) and 'what matters' (the space of
intentions). All action is interaction."**

### The Three Marks (*tilakkhaṇa*; *trilakṣaṇa*) — a method, not a doctrine
Practical directives for revising how we're present, not claims about how things objectively are:
- **Duḥkha (troubled/troubling).** Not "life is suffering" pessimism — a practice of shifting attention across
  scales of interdependence to disprove the assumption "if I'm okay, you must be okay" (worked example: a bacon
  and eggs breakfast is pleasurable for the eater, fatal for the hog, exploitative of the industrially-caged
  hen, corrosive to family farms competing with agribusiness, and health-damaging if advertising-driven
  overconsumption results). **"There are no agents that are not also patients of their own actions."**
- **Anicca/anitya (impermanence).** All things are processes, not stable entities — Heraclitus's river, plus the
  Buddhist addition that the observer and the riverbank are *also* flowing. **"We are not merely beings who are
  subject to change; we are ever-becoming subjects of change."** No situation is truly intractable; feeling
  stuck is "self-justifying evidence of our own resistance to changing who we are present as." Root cause of
  conflict = attachment to a fixed identity/perspective, plus the conviction "this is true, all else is false"
  (Sutta Nipāta).
- **Anattā/anatman (no-self/without-self).** Not nihilism — "a practice of regarding the meaning of all things,
  including our own selves, as open to revision." The **five skandhas** (clusters of experiential/relational
  dynamics, none of which is a persisting self): *rūpa* (bodily form), *vedanā* (feeling: liking/disliking/
  neutral), *saññā*/*saṃjña* (perception via the six senses), *saṅkhāra*/*saṃskāra* (volition/habit), *viññāṇa*/
  *vijñāna* (discriminating consciousness). The self is only "virtually (and not actually) present" — like
  asteroids orbiting a "missing" center of attentive gravity. Contrasted explicitly with Hume's/Dennett's/
  Parfit's/Flanagan's "bundle theory" of self: those *reductionist* theories deny a lasting moral agent and, per
  Hershock, thereby deny responsibility; **Buddhist no-self is allied WITH karma and compassion, not opposed to
  responsibility.** The Buddha refused to answer metaphysical self/no-self questions directly (Sabbasava Sutta,
  Bāhiya Sutta) as therapeutically counterproductive regardless of which answer one gives. Being without-self is
  **not being absent** — the bicycle-learning example (the moment "I" vanishes and only balance-in-movement
  remains) and Csikszentmihalyi's "flow" are both offered as partial, domain-specific glimpses of what full,
  circumstance-independent presence-without-self would be.

### Buddhist ethics: nirvana, kuśala, and the five precepts
**Nirvana (*nibbāna*)** literally means "blown out" or "cooled down" — **not a destination but a therapeutic
orientation** (the candle-flame metaphor is about the *process* of removing the conditions that sustain the
"flames" of duḥkha, not annihilation). Buddhist ethics offers **no positively defined "good"** and no fixed
principles for the "good life" — wisdom functions as a **"moral compass," not a "moral telescope."** The five
lay precepts (no killing, no taking-not-given, no sexual misconduct, no false/hurtful speech, no intoxication to
heedlessness) are "karmic hygiene" — necessary, not sufficient.

**Kuśala** is the single most load-bearing technical term in the whole book, reused constantly in every later
chapter. Usually mistranslated as "skillful" or "good" — it actually **functions as a superlative: virtuosity,
not mere adequacy.** The Sakkapañha Sutta (DN 21): social conflict is traced through jealousy/greed/fixed
likes-and-dislikes/craving down to **prapañca (Pali *papañca*), "conceptual proliferation"** — the compulsive
carving-up of experience into ever-finer fixed categories, which both supports and entraps a craving-defined
self. The cure is to continually ask whether conduct is producing *kuśala* (superlative) or *akuśala* outcomes —
which means **freeing ourselves from conflict requires going BEYOND current conceptions of good and evil, not
merely avoiding bad or achieving merely-acceptable conduct.**

## 3. Chapter Two — Artificial Intelligence: A Brief History (**compressed** — conventional AI/tech history, minimal Buddhist content)

Traces artificial-servant fantasy from Homer's Hephaistos ("self-propelled chairs," "golden attendants") through
Ovid, Spenser's *Fairie Queene* "iron man," and da Vinci's 1495 mechanical knight — the recurring detail worth
keeping is that early imagined artificial beings were consistently built for *martial* labor. Covers Babbage's
difference/analytical engines; WWII fast-tracking electronic computing; Turing's core claim that any formalizable
reasoning can be automated; the "neat" (logic/precision) vs. "scruffy" (frames/scripts, Dreyfus's embodied-
cognition critique) split in early AI research; AI funding winters (DARPA cuts, Weizenbaum's own 1976 ethical
warning as ELIZA's creator); the 1980s expert-systems boom and bust; and the internet's build-out (ARPANET 1969
→ WWW 1991-92 → Google 1998 → iPhone 2007). DARPA's CALO project ($150M, 2003-2008, SRI International) is traced
directly to Siri's commercial birth and then to the founding of Viv (2016) by disaffected ex-Siri engineers
pursuing "AI as utility like water or electricity." Big-data scale statistics: 100GB/hour globally (1997) → 100GB/
second (2002) → 50,000GB/second (2017) → 2.5 quintillion bytes/24 hours (2019); IoT projected at ~4,800 device
interactions per connected person per day and 163 zettabytes/year by 2025. One genuinely reusable line: **data is
"memory traces of human intelligence in action"** — not raw/neutral facts but targeted records of what has
mattered to someone, meaning what gets called "AI" is frequently better described as *synthetic* intelligence, a
human-machine blend.

## 4. Chapter Three — Intelligent Technology: A Revolution in the Making (the core attention-economy chapter)

### Working definition of intelligence
**Intelligence = adaptive conduct** — linked to learning and goal-orientation, but *not* requiring intentionality
or subjective experience. This lets Hershock treat microbes and sequoias as exhibiting **"slow" intelligence**
(evolutionary/adaptive-design timescale) and animals/machines as capable of **"fast" intelligence** (real-time
learning). The one real asymmetry between biological and artificial systems at present: **machine goals still
originate exogenously**, from human designers — which leads to the book's sharpest one-liner on AI alignment:
**"for the foreseeable future, the goals and values of AI(s) will be in precise and unfaltering alignment with
human interests and values. And that should concern us."** Misalignment isn't the near-term risk; alignment with
*conflicted* human values is.

### Tools vs. Technologies — the critical wedge
- **Tools**: crafted to amplify a specific capacity; means only, don't set ends; evaluable by task-utility; retain
  **"exit rights"** (real opt-out is always possible).
- **Technologies**: "emergent systems of material and conceptual practices that embody and deploy both
  strategic and normative values." Qualitatively transform relations, shaping *why* as well as *how*; **cannot be
  value-neutral**; are not built/used but **participated in** — ecological, not tool-like. The "guns don't kill
  people" argument fails at the technology level even where it succeeds at the tool level: a tool-fix (biometric
  gun locks) can't touch the technology-level risk that weapons *technology* has already normalized
  distance-killing as a value.
- **"Practical absence of exit rights."** Personally declining to text doesn't exempt you from texting's
  reshaping of what "being a good friend" now means for everyone around you. Cites Floridi's "levels of
  abstraction" and "downward causation" — technologies operate at a level analogous to organism-behavior versus
  chemistry, and effects flow *down* from the higher level, not only up.
- Technologies are **"ecologies of consistently enacted values and intentions — karmic environments."**

### The Attention Economy — full apparatus
Connected devices take on an **iconic function** (explicitly compared to religious icons): they attract and
transmit attention into an inaccessible computational "beyond," and their efficacy is "transparently and
directly proportional to the depth and duration of the attention we lavish" on them — we "faithfully submit"
appeals to Google/Amazon/Alibaba as digital "deities." Herbert Simon (1971), quoted directly and treated as the
founding insight of the whole economic logic: **"an abundance of information leads to a scarcity of
attention."**

- **Attention Economy 1.0** (Tim Wu's historical term): print/broadcast-era mass-scale conversion of attention
  into revenue.
- **Attention Economy 2.0** (Hershock's own coinage, the book's central economic concept): the internet as a
  combined communication-and-production infrastructure where users are drafted into double duty as both
  consumers *and* producers of training data. Described as a **Klein bottle** — a topological object with no
  inside or outside — because wishing, granting the wish, and generating the next wish are fused into one loop:
  a **"perpetual desire machine."** Crucial economic point: **attention captured = time consumed, not time
  invested** — the economy transmutes freely-given time into manufactured need for goods/services that could
  otherwise have been met directly (growing food, maintaining a home, caring for family, learning a skill).
  Illustrative math: ~30 billion attention-hours/day traded globally (6 hrs/day × 5 billion people) ≈ $150
  billion/day ≈ $54 trillion/year in notional value (vs. ~$90 trillion global GDP, 2019) — a genuinely useful
  order-of-magnitude framing for describing a Circuit-style economy.
- Commercial mechanics: Tim Cook's "if it's free, you're the product"; Zuboff's **"surveillance capitalism"**;
  Harcourt's **"expository society"** — surveillance "woven into the very fabric of our pleasures and
  fantasies," and, most quotably, **"the technologies that end up facilitating our surveillance are the very
  technologies we crave."** Zwick and Knott's distinction, reused throughout the book, between **epistemological
  power** (knowing/predicting consumers) and **ontological power** (manufacturing/producing them) — data mining
  doesn't just learn about people, it makes people. Netflix's churn-prevention algorithm (90-second selection
  window; not "recommending," engineering behavior, saving ~$1B/year); YouTube/Google Brain's +70% watch-time
  increase (2013-2016); Spotify's $0.00437/play (87,515 plays/month needed for US minimum wage).
- **Network economics**: unlike hierarchies (value falls with distance from the center), network value
  *increases* with size — a structural bias toward monopoly, the **"network effect."** Value flows to "winner
  takes all" via a power-law "bounty without spread."

### The New Great Game and the colonization of consciousness
Reframes today's AI competition (US/China/EU/Russia etc.) as a successor to the 19th-century imperial "Great
Game," but **chronopolitical rather than geopolitical** — a "time grab," not a "land grab," because digital
space permits infinite superposition (no two things competing for the same physical location) and the only real
"distances" in cyberspace are temporal, measuring how long it takes to form and express an intention. State
surveillance apparatus documented in detail: NSA's "Treasure Map" (real-time global device mapping); "fusion
centers" merging constitutionally-limited state data with unregulated private data; China's social-credit
system explicitly framed as "smart statecraft" merging trustworthy-citizen and reliable-consumer goals via
state-corporate partnership; the 2016 US election (33% of pro-Trump tweets and 22% of pro-Clinton tweets were
botnet-generated; Cambridge Analytica profiled 220 million Americans). Military section covers the Pentagon's own
internal term, the **"Terminator conundrum,"** and OODA-loop (Observe-Orient-Decide-Act) compression — the
argument that humans will structurally be too slow to stay "in the loop" and will be reduced to "explaining what
has been done and why" after the fact.

**Chapter closes with a Buddhist reframing of consciousness itself**, directly useful for robot-consciousness
worldbuilding: consciousness is **not singular or internal to a sentient being** — it is "a qualitatively
dynamic mode of relationality," arising from the interplay of a sense-organ and a sense-object. The five ordinary
senses (visual/auditory/tactile/olfactory/gustatory) plus a sixth, **cognitive consciousness** (mind's contact
with the content of the other five), plus two further modes added by the Yogācāra school: a seventh,
**self-consciousness** (sensitivity to changes in cognitive consciousness itself), and an eighth, **karmic
consciousness** (the interplay of self-conscious presence with the temporal environment of outcomes/opportunities
shaped by enacted values and intentions). A footnote (ch.3 n.21) explicitly flags this eight-consciousness list
as **"indicative, not exhaustive"** — other senses (temperature, gravitational body-alignment, electromagnetic
fields) exist and aren't covered by the traditional taxonomy, which is a built-in invitation to extend the model
to non-human/robotic sensory modalities. "Colonization of consciousness is a colonization of interdependence
itself."

## 5. Chapter Four — Total Attention Capture and Control: A Future to Avoid

Opens with the **Midas myth** (Ovid) as extended metaphor for wish-fulfillment risk, paired with Bostrom's
"paperclip maximizer" superintelligence scenario — the philosophical point in both cases: **granting even
innocuous wishes with sufficient efficiency can be catastrophic**, without requiring any malice. A mundane,
directly relatable version of the same risk: offloading phone-number memory to a smartphone contacts list means
the numbers **never enter the "body-mind repertoire"** — people who navigate a new city by GPS for months never
internalize its layout; **"the city never quite becomes part of who they are."** An infant/mother experiment is
cited (two-way video: babies distressed the moment live footage switches to *recorded* footage of an equally
warm expression) to argue that **relational immediacy itself, not just affective content, is what matters** —
directly relevant to any critique of AI companionship as a substitute for presence.

**Fictional futures used as extrapolation devices**: E.M. Forster's 1909 novella *"The Machine Stops"* (an
underground, want-free population that reveres a global Machine as near-divine; the story ends when no one
remembers how to maintain it and it collapses) vs. Nikola Tesla's 1920s vision of a single connected planetary
"electronic brain." Hershock's verdict: today looks more like Tesla's hyper-connected-individuality future than
Orwell's grey totalitarian one — **"choice becomes compulsory."** Gary Shteyngart's novel *Super Sad True Love
Story* is used at length to illustrate a fully-realized "quantified self" society (an *äppäräti* device merging
smartphone + biometric self-tracking + real-time, publicly-broadcast social-credit/desirability rankings).

**Buddhist framing of poverty appears here in its first form** (fuller version in Ch.8, §7 below) via a critique
of "Guaranteed Learning," "Subscription Health," and "Smart Security" sections that each work the same way:
smart services CAN deliver good measurable outcomes (better test scores, better health metrics, lower crime)
while **failing to deliver the opportunities that build resilience and responsiveness** — citing Hershock's own
earlier work for a Buddhist conception of healing as "not simply a return to previous somatic norms but also
meaningfully heightened awareness and increased relational capacity." China's Xinjiang mass surveillance/
re-education program is named explicitly and without euphemism as "proof of concept for AI-enabled social
control." A reusable general-purpose aphorism about technology's self-perpetuating logic: **"successes [of
technologies] carry them across their own thresholds of utility to bring about the conditions of their own
necessity."**

**Virtual Friendships and Intimacies**: cites Turkle's research on humans readily attributing "alive enough"
status to minimally-expressive relational artifacts (Furbie, the therapeutic robot seal Paro), and her finding
that teenagers actually *preferred* an (imagined) virtual therapist to a human one, on grounds of perceived lack
of bias and access to "unlimited data" — a direct, sobering echo of Weizenbaum's own 1970s alarm at how people
treated ELIZA. Spike Jonze's *Her* (2013) is used as the extrapolated endpoint. Two real, structural differences
Hershock argues will persist between actual and virtual friendship even as simulation quality improves: (1)
touch/"gut"-level bandwidth, absent from virtual relation; (2) **actual friendship requires effort to sustain —
virtual/smart-service friendship, structurally, will not.**

**Closing "Predicament Ahead" section — the sharpest single statement of the book's core warning:**

> *"To get better at getting what we want will embroil us in becoming better at wanting and thus in being never
> quite able to want what we get. That karmic loop will tighten... We will become that which contains us,
> enjoying practically unlimited individual freedoms of experiential choice in ever more compellingly controlled
> environments."*

Explicitly **not** a paperclip-maximizer, not malevolent AI — "extraordinary potentials for bringing about
conditions in which choice becomes a compulsive end-in-itself." And a genuine point of leverage: **"without our
attention energy, the data grid would lay idle"** — human attention is the one input still fully within human
control.

## 6. Chapter Five — Anticipating an Ethics of Intelligence

Surveys existing attempts at a purpose-built global ethics for AI — Wiener's cybernetic ethics (*The Human Use of
Human Beings*, 1950: life as entropy-reducing information processing), Bynum's Aristotelian "flourishing ethics"
(extends ethical regard to nonliving/nonhuman agents), and especially **Floridi's "ethics of information"**
("to be is to be interactable"; ethical focus shifted from *agents* to *patients* — those who receive the
consequences of action, useful for algorithmic-actor problems; his "levels of abstraction" method as a
relativism-guard). Hershock's core critique of all three: they collapse ethical predicaments into technical
problems by reducing everything to a **single metric (entropy/information)** — which can't actually distinguish
attention-capture-optimized "flourishing" from human-flourishing-optimized alternatives if both formally "reduce
entropy" the same amount.

### Variety vs. Diversity — Hershock's own most portable analytical tool
- **Variety**: a purely quantitative measure of bare coexistence (a zoo — species merely coexist, externally/
  contingently related, dependent on outside inputs for survival).
- **Diversity**: a qualitative index of the *depth of interdependence* present (a natural savanna ecosystem —
  species internally/constitutively related, mutual survival dependent on each other). **"Diversity is an index
  of ecosystem intelligence."** Entropy = *intelligence drain* — it increases whenever diversity reverts to mere
  variety.
- Applied to ethics: **"ethical plurality"** (mere coexistence of many ethical systems) is not the same as
  **"ethical diversity"** (constitutive interdependence among traditions actively differentiating *for* each
  other). The goal is a **"global ethical ecosystem,"** not a single "common global ethics" — Hershock cites
  Jean-Luc Nancy's warning that invoking "the common" always risks coercion (the Nazi appeal to "common German
  values" is the explicit cautionary case) and argues instead for a **"truly SHARED global ethics."**

**Strong vs. weak interdependence**: weak = external/contingent relation (Floridi's "messages" connecting
independently-existing senders and receivers); strong = internal/constitutive relation (Buddhist
*pratītyasamutpāda*, sharpened by the Chinese Huayan philosopher **Fazang** (643-712 CE) into *interpenetration*
— "all things are the same precisely insofar as they differ meaningfully from and for each other," each
particular (*shi*) both causing and being caused by the totality's patterning (*li*). **"Each thing ultimately is
what it means to and for others."**) Reality, on this account, is **conferred, not discovered** — "the always
negotiable result of different ways of inflecting relational dynamics." Prior to intelligent differentiation
(*vijñāna*, literally "divisive knowing"), **neither good nor bad existed** — there is no Eden/fall story in this
cosmology; goodness is not intrinsic to the cosmos, it is realized through practice.

### Virtuosity as ethical orientation
*Kuśala* ethics is like musical virtuosity — achieved jointly by a performer and an "appreciative audience,"
setting *new* standards rather than meeting fixed ones; **"neither a private possession nor a public goal to be
arrived at once and for all."** Direct **fallacy-of-composition critique of smart-service individualism**: what's
good for *each* individual (a car; a personalized recommendation engine) is not automatically good for *all*
relationally (traffic jams; algorithmically-fragmented family/community dynamics) — ethical focus should be on
relational dynamics, not aggregated individual-patient outcomes.

### Ethical Resolution — Individuation, Desire, Karma
Three named pillars for reorienting the Intelligence Revolution:

1. **Individuation.** The individual self is "descriptively a fiction" (citing Confucian scholar Henry Rosemont
   Jr.); the smartphone is named directly as **"the technology of individuation."** Social media fosters a
   **"flattered self as experiential sovereign"** (Zengotita 2005) and, absent relational responsibility,
   readily produces bias-confirming echo chambers.

2. **Desire — a three-part taxonomy, directly reusable terminology:**
   - ***tṛṣṇā*** (Pali *taṇhā*) — self-magnifying craving/attachment (sense pleasure, status, avoidance); the
     troubling desire-type Buddhist practice targets for reduction.
   - ***chanda*** — effortful desire to accomplish or pursue something with resolve (parents wanting their
     children to prosper; committing to learn an instrument); morally *neutral* — its valence depends entirely
     on intent.
   - **Bodhisattva desire** — the one *superlative*/virtuosic desire: the wish for *all* sentient beings'
     liberation from *duḥkha*, rooted in the bodhisattva vow, fulfillable only through resolute practice.
   - The Attention Economy actively cultivates *tṛṣṇā* and is explicitly called **"antithetical to actualizing
     bodhisattva desire."**
   - **Devaloka** (the heavenly abodes/god-realms of Hindu-Buddhist cosmology) is used as the exact analogy for
     a maximally-satisfying, frictionless digital experiential "bubble." Buddhism explicitly treats these heavens
     as **unsuited** to enlightening effort — pleasant resting places, never destinations, because
     "compassionate, improvisational genius... develops only in relational domains characterized by resistance
     and friction." **Directly usable, unmodified, for how any Buddhist-influenced Tepenian culture (robot or
     human) would regard a frictionless VR/smart-service utopia: a devaloka, not a goal.**

3. **Karma.** Personalized algorithmic "bubbles" become **"intermediaries for our karmic individuation,"** leading
   toward **"karmic isolation"** — explicitly *not* like prison (no felt deprivation): **"lusciously seductive:
   existing interactively in friction-free informational environments that offer no moral resistance
   whatsoever."** One of the single most quotable, reusable formulations in the entire book for describing a
   hypothetical hyper-personalized AI-managed society or subculture.

Closes with the proposal of **"business bodhisattvas, bureaucratic bodhisattvas, and martial bodhisattvas"** —
Hershock's own named archetype for what it would take to redirect corporate/state/military elites, and a
directly reusable naming convention for characters occupying those structural roles in a Buddhist-influenced
setting.

## 7. Chapter Six — Dimensions of Ethical Agency: Confucian Conduct, Socratic Reasoning, Buddhist Consciousness

**This chapter is flagged by the project brief as unusually high-priority, and it delivers**: three "ways of life"
(Pierre Hadot's term) placed into explicit conversation, each supplying what the others structurally lack.
Recurring emphasis across all three: **becoming** ethical (a practice) rather than **being** ethical (a fixed
state).

### Confucian Personhood: Resolutely Harmonious Conduct
Counterpart *cogito*: **"as we relate, so we are."** Core text: the *Daxue* (Great Learning) — "personal
cultivation is the root," *xiushen* 修身 (cultivate + lived-body/self) names self-cultivation as **socially
embedded becoming**, not individual self-improvement. *Gewu* 格物 ("investigating things") does **not** mean
empirical/scientific study — it means correcting/adjusting relationships, discerning *dao* (dynamic
interrelatedness). Human nature (*renxing*) is **relational**, not individual; the Mengzi holds the human/animal
distinction is *initially infinitesimal* — humans simply *transform* basic animal acts (eating, procreating,
communicating) into culinary arts and hospitality, "making love" and family, poetry and intergenerational
narrative. Confucian community values ritually-defined roles (*li*) that are inherited but personalized "with
greater or lesser vitality and quality." Confucian persons are **not modern individuals** — hierarchy is affirmed
as constitutive (ideally increasingly mutual, never becoming liberal autonomy):

> *"If each of us were equal in every way, we would have nothing of value to offer one another... As social
> reality, total equality would be equivalent to maximum entropy."*

The Confucian ideal is **"harmony, not sameness"** (*he er butong* 和而不同) — harmony as mutual adjustment, not
unison. (Historical honesty flagged: this ideal has often coexisted with patriarchal institutions and
favor-network corruption; challenged historically by Legalist/Mohist/Daoist critiques.)

### Socratic Personhood: Critical Resolve
Socrates (469-399 BCE) as a battle-hardened ex-soldier (three deployments, all ended in defeat; returned once to
find a quarter of Athens dead of plague; lived through Athens's decline, defeat by Sparta, and a brief violent
oligarchy) whose skepticism toward claimed "expertise" was arguably **existential, not merely intellectual**, in
origin. Sharp contrast with Confucius: the Analects open with the "unheard of" idea of defying authority
(*fanshang*, "clash with those above") — Socrates was **willing** to clash with authority and accepted execution
rather than disavow his cross-examining method. *"The unexamined life is simply not worth living"* (Apology
38a2-6). Confucius: "a transmitter of cultural wisdom, not an innovator" (Analects 7.1), resolving the
predicament of culture *from within* inherited tradition. Socrates: lost faith in tradition/exemplars entirely —
found no one, including himself, whose claimed *arête* (virtue/valor) survived cross-examination on courage,
piety, prudence, justice, or *sophia* (wisdom, distinct from mere *techne*/skill). Hershock's reading of the
Meno's real conclusion: **"virtue or excellence is not a destination at which we might one day arrive; it is a
direction of self-transformation"** — a direct structural parallel to *kuśala*-as-superlative-not-fixed-good.
Freedom, for Socrates, ≠ choice — freedom = conducting oneself excellently even under compelled duty; his
acceptance of the death sentence was "valorous commitment," not resignation.

### Blending Relational Intimacy and Rational Integrity — and the pivot to Buddhism
Confucian method = culturally-sanctioned/relationally-articulated community; Socratic method =
rationally-sanctioned/personally-articulated autonomy. Both: not "armchair" philosophy; both face-to-face and
socially engaged; both reject a fixed essence of human being; both progressive (looking to the past only to not
unintentionally return to it). **The shared liability that motivates turning to Buddhism**: both operate only at
the *social* "level of abstraction" — **consciousness itself is assumed, not investigated; critical attention
goes to acting/thinking, never to the process of attending itself.** This liability becomes acute precisely in an
attention-economy context, hence the need for a third, inward-turning dimension.

### Attentive Virtuosity: The Heart of Buddhist Ethical Effort
***Bodhicitta*** = "enlightenment" (*bodhi*) + "thought/mentality" (*citta*) — the generative *intent* to realize
enlightenment in order to free *all* sentient beings from *duḥkha*; marks the start of the bodhisattva path.
The three trainings (*tisikkhā*/*triśikṣā*): moral clarity (*śīla*), attentive mastery (*samādhi*, described here
as "pivotal"), wisdom (*prajñā*). The Buddha's own enlightenment is described metaphorically as discovering a
once-glorious city long overgrown by **jungle** — Buddhist practice as **"jungle-clearing,"** pulling out the
roots of ignorance (*avidyā*), craving (*tṛṣṇā*), and aversion/hatred (*dveṣa*) that nourish karmic habituation
(*saṃskāra*) and conceptual proliferation (*prapañca*). Meditation techniques: **mindfulness (*satipaṭṭhāna*)** —
nonjudgmental attention to moment-by-moment body-mind-environment, aiming only at "experiential immediacy," not
at generating any specific experience; **calming/settling (*śamatha*)** and **clear-seeing/discerning
(*vipaśyanā*)** — per the Chinese Buddhist thinker Zhiyi (538-597 CE, *Mohe Zhiguan*), **"like the two wheels of
a cart or the two wings of a bird"** — simultaneous, not sequential.

**The direct payoff for the book's whole project**, worth quoting at length because it is the theoretical
justification for treating meditation as a literal counter-technology, not a metaphor:

> *"If it is true that all assaults on human autonomy and community begin with an assault on awareness, and if
> self-awareness is crucial to self-regulation, then the algorithmic capture and exploitation of attention is an
> assault on our abilities to plan, to reflect critically on our own conduct, and to determine what best
> contributes to our own well-being... The opportunity to determine our own futures depends on asserting our
> RIGHT TO SANCTUARY — our right to be left alone by the artificial agencies."*

## 8. Chapter Seven — Humane Becoming: Cultivating Responsive Virtuosity (the arahant, the bodhisattva, and the six pāramitās)

Opens with a Dōgen (Japanese Zen master, 1200-1253) story: told to a monk unhappily assigned to clean the
monastery toilets, *"When you lose your money in the river, you look in the river."* Practice happens in
mundane daily life, not abstract retreat.

### Two Ideals of Personal Presence

**1. Uncompelled Presence — the arahant (Theravāda ideal).** The *arahant* ("worthy one") realizes *nibbāna*/
*nirvāṇa* — freedom from *saṃsāra* — **in this life, with this body.** Even *happiness* (*sukkha*) is deliberately
**not** used to positively characterize the aim of practice; *kuśala* remains the orienting compass, not any
positive end-state. Root causes of conflict (from the Sutta Nipāta's *Atthakavagga*): **dualistic thinking** —
setting things up as pairs of opposites (pleasant/unpleasant, "this is true, all else false," superior/equal/
inferior), each ranking asserting a position of judgment and power that divides. **"Conflict is rooted in being
present as someone determined to assert his or her fundamental difference and to stand both apart from and over
others."** Arahants are described as suffusing every situation with **compassion (*karuṇā*), equanimity
(*upekkhā*), loving-kindness (*mettā*), and joy in others' good fortune (*muditā*)** (Tevijja Sutta, DN 13 — the
classic four *brahmavihārā*/"immeasurables," though Hershock doesn't use that collective term here).

**2. Compassionately Engaged Presence — the bodhisattva (Mahāyāna ideal).** The bodhisattva **deliberately
forfeits** personal freedom from rebirth, vowing to remain among sentient beings and help *all* of them attain
liberation — a vow made with full knowledge that it can never, strictly, be completed. Bodhisattva virtuosity =
***upāya*** ("skillful/responsive means") — engaging each situation from whichever angle best activates its own
enlightening potential.

**Innovation vs. Improvisation** — a precise, directly reusable design distinction, built on James Carse's
finite/infinite games (1986):
- **Innovation** = "closed" creativity — new ways of reaching *already-anticipated* goals; requires and is
  rewarded with **power** (the capacity to determine outcomes); a finite game played to *win/finish*.
- **Improvisation** = "open" creativity — generates *new* aims from within existing norms toward what's
  unprecedented; requires and builds **strength** (the capacity to open new opportunity for *everyone* involved);
  an infinite game played to keep playing well. **"The bodhisattva ideal is to be, not powerful, but strong."**

The same karmic-tightening-loop argument from Chapter Four is generalized here explicitly to control and choice,
not just want: greater control-capacity requires perpetually experiencing insufficient control; greater
choice-opportunity creates conditions (consumer markets) in which choice becomes *compulsory*. **None of
getting-what-we-want, exerting control, or exercising choice is ultimately *kuśala*.**

Chan master Mazu Daoyi (709-788 CE), quoted at length: true virtuosity means "realizing the excellent nature of
opportunities and dangers... departing from 'is' and 'is-not'... leaving behind no obstructing traces, one's
actions are then like phrases written on water." **"The world's myriad things are one's own body"** — what we
experience as the world's resistance is actually our own "paralysis" from ignorance of interdependence.

### The Six Pāramitās — full definitions, the single most reusable list in the book

Etymology: *pāramitā* can be read via *parama* ("highest/most excellent," → the standard translation
"perfections") or via *pāra* ("beyond") + *ita* ("that which goes") → **"that which goes beyond."** Hershock
prefers the second: pāramitās are **"aspirational headings,"** never final destinations — "perfection" as a
*verbal noun* (continual perfecting), not an achieved state. **Critical distinction from Aristotelian virtue**:
Aristotelian virtue is individually/personally determined (an inner stability enabling consistent right action).
**Pāramitās are relationally verified** — not traits an individual possesses, but "distinctive but interrelated
modalities of relational appreciation" made manifest only in shared *conduct*, "the ways in which we are
karmically led together."

1. **Generosity (*dāna*).** Literally "to offer/distribute" — compassionate offering that generates *future
   wealth*; karmically recursive like the karma of craving, but the reverse in effect: **it increases rather than
   depletes resources.** Illustrated with the **Vessantara Jataka** (the Buddha's final "prior life": Prince
   Vessantara gives away his kingdom, wealth, even his children and wife; all is restored, and the kingdom is
   showered with jewels). Three canonical types of enriching generosity: material aid to those in need generally;
   material support for the monastic community; and **the gift of the Dharma/teaching itself.** Must be given
   *freely*, without obligation or even subtle expectation of gratitude — instrumentalized giving corrupts the
   karma. **Cautionary tale, directly usable for game writing**: the **Cakkavatti Sīhanāda Sutta** (DN 26) — a
   utopian "wheel-turning" king gives startup capital to a man who stole to feed his family; this triggers
   copycat theft (including by the non-poor, acting from greed); the king responds with capital punishment;
   this triggers murder-to-avoid-capture; the society spirals into cruelty and chaos. **A well-intentioned,
   virtuous motive, applied with the wrong method to the wrong context, is not *kuśala* regardless of intent** —
   "skillful offering entails moral clarity."

2. **Moral Clarity (*śīla*).** Broader than the Five Precepts — an ongoing **situational purification of intent**,
   not a transformation of how we act but *why*. Doesn't fit any Western ethical taxonomy cleanly: broadly
   consequentialist but not utilitarian-calculus-based; concerned with character/quality but not
   individual-virtue-focused; compassion-rooted but not rule-based/deontological. **"An ethics of relational
   appreciation... qualitatively enhancing the value of the actual relationships through which we are present as
   this person, in this community, at this time."**

3. **Patient Willingness (*kṣānti*).** "Patience," but the Pali root *khanti* means **willing acceptance**. Given
   the bodhisattva path is infinite and collaborators may resist change, this must be **"activating patience"**
   (steadfast readiness to act, inspiring others' own readiness) rather than **"waiting patience"** (quiet,
   certainty-based acceptance). Cannot be a self-securing retreat from friction — instead, "patiently and
   willingly transmuting the 'heat' generated by [moral] friction into a source of appreciative and contributory
   radiance."

4. **Valiant Effort (*vīrya*).** Shares an Indo-European root with "vitality" and "valor" — corrects any
   impression that Buddhist practice is passive. The two common (accurate but incomplete) images of Buddhist
   practitioners — the serenely seated meditator, and the smiling, softly caring wanderer — are only "the
   blossoms," not "the branches, trunk, and roots." A germinating-seed analogy: growth requires *simultaneous*
   courageous striving downward (roots) and upward (shoots) — inner/psychological and outer/social growth cannot
   be sequenced, "occurring at once in both... or in neither." *Vīrya* specifically counters **doubt**, precisely
   defined: **"a doubling of the self, a paralyzing division of intent, an aborting of commitment"** — not mere
   uncertainty, and curable only by "unwaveringly vigorous and effortful presence," never by more knowledge.

5. **Poised Attentiveness (*dhyāna*).** "Meditation" as progressive calming/centering, the necessary precursor to
   wisdom. Hershock's own teacher, Ji Kwang Dae Poep Sa Nim, is quoted: *"once you break ground to dig a well,
   keep digging; don't start a hole here and move to another where the ground seems softer."* Sequential training
   stages (classical): initiation of attention (*vitarka*) → sustaining attention (*vicāra*) → uplifting alertness
   (*prīti*) → expansive blissful ease (*suḥkha*) → unwavering one-pointedness (*ekagrata*). Achieved
   (paradoxically) **in "the field of distraction"** (*asamāhitatva*) — freedom from both aversion and attraction
   while surrounded by distraction, not by removing it.

6. **Wisdom (*prajñā*).** **Not** cumulative, age-based knowledge-sedimentation — depends on the *quality of
   attention/presence*, freedom from the obstructing "flows" (*āsava*/*āsrava*: self-affirming views, habit,
   sense-attachment, conflict-generating concepts), not accumulated experience. The personification of Buddhist
   wisdom — Mañjuśrī ("Gentle Glory")/Mañjughoṣa ("Gentle-Voiced") — is traditionally depicted as an **eternally
   youthful young man** (*kumārabhūta*), a deliberate inversion of "wisdom = old age." Etymology: *pra-*
   (intensifying prefix) + *jñā* ("awareness"/"gnosis," specifically investigative insight into spiritual
   matters). The sudden/gradual enlightenment debate in Chinese Buddhism was about whether long *training* is
   needed to translate insight into action, **not** about whether the insight itself is immediate — insight is
   always experientially immediate. Wisdom is also inherently **compassionate**, contrasted explicitly with
   Martha Nussbaum's account of compassion as an "intelligent emotion" resulting from three rational judgments
   (bad thing happened; undeserved; the sufferer matters to my own scheme of interests) — Nussbaum's version
   remains individual-agent-centered and self-interest-linked; **Buddhist compassion is not a syllogistic
   result of judgment at all, it is "the practice of superlative emotional intelligence... creatively enacted
   empathy,"** arising directly from seeing strong interdependence. **Great compassion** (*mahākaruṇā*) is the
   bodhisattva-vow-level, intergenerational extension of this, channeled through *upāya* (Ch. *fangbian*
   方便).

### Closing critique — the co-optation of liberal individuality
Chapter Seven's close makes the sharpest single political point in the book: **"the ideal of autonomous
individuality is particularly liable to co-optation"** by algorithmically-tailored environments — "seeking
individual autonomy as a personal ideal can easily be nudged into identity with a rationally self-interested
OPTING-OUT of the hard... work of global predicament resolution." Coins **"Big Others"** as a deliberate riff on
Orwell's "Big Brother": not totalitarian coercion, but "handsomely presented and very solicitous" algorithmic
entities so finely attuned to individuals that their nudges become "nearly indistinguishable from moves we might
have intended on our own."

## 9. Chapter Eight — Course Correction: A Middle Path Through Data Governance

Frames the core danger as **acceleration itself**, not any single harm: *"There is not... plenty of time for
course correction. The most perilous thing about the Intelligence Revolution today is its acceleration."*
Vivid image: living on **"karmic 'cul-de-sacs' crafted in minutely detailed response to our digitally expressed
values and desires... paid for with the irreplaceable currency of attention — lives in which we will never have
to learn from our mistakes."**

### Both extreme responses rejected
- **Digital asceticism** (full abstinence): rejected as both impractical (gig-economy dependence, digital-native
  identity formation, "social impact irrelevance" of full withdrawal) and *not itself kuśala* — "the human-
  technology-world system can only be changed from within." Real humanitarian upside of smart tech is also
  acknowledged honestly (smart water/transit/energy grids, precision farming, virtual medicine for the roughly
  half of humanity lacking basic health access) — wholesale rejection is ethically irresponsible, not just
  impractical.
- **Digital hedonism** (full indulgence): also rejected. **Distinctive Buddhist definition of poverty**, directly
  reusable for any district's economic-underclass writing: **not** lack of income/resources, but *"blockage-
  riddled interdependencies: the proliferation of duḥkha-generating impediments to relating freely."** Poverty is
  **"an affliction of broken or distorted interactions among 'the wealthy' and 'the poor,'"** relational rather
  than substance-based — **"the relational indignity of being forced into a position of having nothing to offer
  to others that they would value... being relationally bereft of possibilities for generosity."** A universal
  basic income, on this account, cannot fully fix poverty, because it doesn't restore the capacity *to give*.

### Data as "Digital Extension," "Genetic Code," and "Karmic Genome"
Reiterates the earlier claim that digital presence is an *extension*, not a representation, of a person — "data
exhaust" is a category error. **Vivid, directly usable vocabulary**: digital traces are like "strands of hair or
flakes of skin we've shed" — containing "genetic" information about our karmic presence. Running predictive
analytics on a data profile = "a credible simulation"; sophisticated enough, it approaches **"karmic
'cloning'": the digital recreation of an intentional presence.** Behavior modification via precisely-timed,
targeted content = **"karmic gene editing."** (Henrietta Lacks's unconsented, still-profitable HeLa cell line is
cited as a real-world legal/ethical precedent-in-progress for "who owns extracted-you.")

### The Middle Path, properly defined
**Not** Aristotelian moderation between two extremes — Hershock repeats the Chapter One point that the Middle
Path moves *oblique* to a whole spectrum of opposed positions, illustrated with a canonical story: the Buddha,
asked by a king planning a horse-sacrifice ritual to secure his people's welfare, did not forbid the ritual —
he asked about the real *meaning* of "sacrifice" and gently redirected the king toward a less karmically costly
method of achieving the same goal. **"The Middle Path strategy is not to refute and then replace others' views
but to gently resist errant intentions and redirect their attention."** Applied to real-world data-governance
positions (US market self-regulation / EU consultative social-welfare regulation via GDPR / China's state-
mandated approach): the Middle Path doesn't rank or merge these into one global regime, it **"braids"** them,
preserving each's integrity while coordinating their distinct contributions — an "ecology" of governance
approaches, echoing the variety-vs-diversity distinction from Chapter Five. Chan master Linji Yixuan (d. 866) is
cited candidly admitting the strategy has no roadmap ("as soon as he opened his mouth to speak, he had already
made a mistake," yet total silence "would be like handing over a fishing net without a drawstring") — and his
famous *"If you see the Buddha on the road, kill him!"* is glossed as a warning against objectifying
enlightenment as a fixed destination (a Zeno's-paradox trap of infinite regress).

### Concrete proposals
- **Connectivity strikes** vs. **connectivity fasting** — fasting = individual, wellness-framed, apolitical
  ("better sleep"); strikes = collective, publicly demanded, political (analogous to a hunger strike vs. personal
  fasting, and — since it isn't guilt-based leverage like an individual hunger strike — must be *mass* action to
  work). Worked example: a coordinated two-week pre-election social-media strike by a large voting demographic as
  "data quarantine," denying campaign-AI targeting data during the most manipulable final stretch (over half of
  global internet traffic is bot-generated). Explicit limitation acknowledged: strikes are **tactical, not
  strategic** — too small/prolonged/frequent, and corporations simply route around the missing data producers.
- **Data as public good / global commons**, rather than private property. Critiques Jaron Lanier's "universal
  basic data income" proposal (pay individuals directly for data use) as potentially *accelerating* the
  Intelligence Revolution by financially incentivizing people to generate more data. Two problems flagged with
  naive "bigger commons = better" thinking: (1) a **data/attention quality problem** — most Attention-Economy-
  harvested data comes from *ayoniśomanasikāra* (attention captured by superficial/craving-inducing content, per
  Ch.1's distinction) rather than *yoniśomanasikāra* (freely concentrated attention) — training AI on it risks
  "runaway data pollution"; (2) an **access-inequality-within-commons problem** — bigger commons are harder to
  govern collectively and their richest resources require capital to reach ("it is big investors who win big" —
  universal internet/orbital access hasn't meant equal benefit).
- **Attention/data cap-and-trade or tax**, modeled directly on carbon-emissions schemes — but with a crucial
  structural difference: carbon's negative externalities occur at *combustion* (use); attention/data's occur at
  the moment of *extraction itself*. **"[Harvesting attention] is less like mining and burning fossil fuels than
  it is like cutting down and burning forests"** — the harvesting tears the "relational fabric" of family/
  community immediately, not as a deferred downstream cost. A **"digital excise tax"** (modeled on tobacco/
  alcohol sin taxes) on consumer/user attention-spending is also proposed, with an insistence that it be
  **content-neutral** — pricing the opportunity-cost of attention-capture generally, not moralizing about which
  content is consumed.

All three proposals are named, explicitly, as different ***upāya*** (skillful means) — none sufficient alone,
none in competition with the others.

## 10. Chapter Nine — Course Correction: A Middle Path Through Education

Opens by rejecting the "STEM will save us" reform narrative on two grounds: it misreads what employers actually
value (communication/critical thinking — liberal-arts hallmarks) and badly underestimates automation's reach
into STEM/technical/professional work itself. The deeper argument: society is shifting from a **problem-solution
paradigm** (linear causality, presumed independence) to a **predicament-resolution paradigm** (irreducible
interdependence, nonlinear causality) — narrowing curricula is exactly backwards; **"we are on the verge of no
longer needing a standards-based, curriculum-organized approach to teaching and learning" at all.**

### Curricular education as a 1576 invention, not a timeless default
Traces "curriculum" to Peter Ramus (1576) as a deliberate alternative to the *prior* dominant model — studio-
centered **apprenticeship**, individually-paced, indeterminate-duration, aimed at eventually setting new
standards within an intergenerational community of practice. Ramus's innovation: knowledge as a
quantifiable/deliverable good, best transferred via standardized sequences; education as an explicitly
**terminal** endeavor. Etymological payoff, worth keeping: Latin *curriculum* originally meant a **circular
chariot-racecourse** — curricular education, by its own founding metaphor, casts education as **"a finite game
played to win"** (a direct callback to Carse's finite/infinite-games distinction from Chapter Seven). Adam Smith
himself argued mass education should be a state-provided public good specifically to manufacture useful
nation-building citizens — the "schools as factories, students as raw material, graduates as finished products"
metaphor was the genuine operative 19th-20th-century framing, not an exaggerated modern critique.

### Educating for Diversity, Equity, Humane Creativity, and Freedom-of-Attention

**Educating for Diversity.** Rejects both headcount-quota diversity and mere "tolerance" as goals, reapplying the
Chapter Five variety-vs-diversity distinction directly to pedagogy. Four concrete dimensions of institutional
practice: **disciplinary** (interdisciplinary teaching — cites Scott Page's finding that cognitively diverse
groups outperform groups of the smartest individuals at paradigm-challenging problems, since diverse groups avoid
"presupposition-mortared impasses"); **national/international** (reframing internationalization from
revenue-generation toward genuine "cultural and cognitive ecotones"); **cultural** (citing Naoki Sakai: cultural
difference is not an objective fact but *"an interpersonally experienced feeling of dismay, discomfort, and
disjunction"* — real engagement can't be casual or comfortable); **generational** (noting tech-field "generation
gaps" as small as four or five years).

**Educating for Equity.** Rejects equity-as-mere-access ("education for all" ≠ "educational quality for all") and
the standard individual-comparison "equality of opportunity" framing. Proposes **relational equity** instead — a
dynamic function of how well relations *enhance inclusion*, not a static index of minimized exclusion; equity and
diversity effectively collapse into each other. Four institutional registers, a clean reusable taxonomy: **access
equity** (who enrolls, how long they stay), **operational equity** (day-to-day fairness within an institution),
**structural equity** (resource distribution, formal/informal mobility limits), **contributory equity** (how
benefits extend to society beyond the institution). Equity reframed, again, as an "asymptotic ideal like...
musical virtuosity" — "a direction as opposed to a destination."

**Humane Creativity.** Distinguishes standard "closed" creativity (Boden 2004: new + surprising + useful/valuable
products, from combining or transforming existing conceptual spaces — presumes a creator/product duality) from
Hershock's own **"open" creativity**: **"there are no product-resulting creative acts; there is only value-
enhancing creative interaction."** Edison, quoted twice: **"creativity is one percent inspiration and ninety-nine
percent perspiration"** — creativity must be *practiced*. A vivid, directly usable illustration of predicament-
resolution as ordinary, teachable skill rather than arcane technique: children in unsupervised pickup sports
reshuffle unbalanced teams mid-game and settle rule disputes themselves; **formally-organized, parent-supervised
team sports remove this improvisational labor from children's hands — ensuring conflict-free play at the cost of
constraining ethically-charged improvisation. "Play becomes finite."** Search/recommendation engines are
critiqued as **"teleportation devices"** — convenient the way cars are convenient, but producing intellectual
"obesity" and imaginative "shortness of breath" with overuse; humane-creativity cultivation therefore needs to
begin in early education, not be deferred to university.

**Freedom-of-Attention.** Edison again (*"Time is really the only capital that any human being has"*) paired with
William James (*"our life experience equals what we have paid attention to, whether by choice or default"*).
Attentive freedom is more than mere *samādhi*-style concentration — illustrated through musical improvisation:
what distinguishes gifted improvisers isn't repetition-honed technique, it's **"the committed quality of their
listening... listening THROUGH it."** A sharp, quotable verdict on the attention economy as a whole: **"The
system's growth logic is simple and crystalline in its goal-oriented clarity: maximize attention turnover. It is
a very good system. It is not a superlative one."** Mechanism metaphor: the attention economy works like
alternating current in a power grid, reducing "resistance" by *alternating* between distraction and compulsion
(web-surfing vs. rabbit-hole diving) — compatible with exercising extensive *choice*, incompatible with sustained
*freedom-of-attention*. **Important nuance on mindfulness, avoiding a naive "just meditate" prescription**:
mindfulness exercised purely for personal stress-relief can be karmically neutral or worse — **"mindfulness can
be exercised for military and mercenary, as well as medical, reasons"** — its merit depends entirely on intent;
personal-only mindfulness may "forestall action" rather than addressing the sources of stress. The book
distinguishes mindfulness **exercise** (self-focused) from mindfulness **practice** (paired with insight into
karmic origins and compassionate intent to help others) — only the latter is full Buddhist practice.

### "The First and Last Question: What Comes Next?" — the book's close
Opens with radical epistemic humility (no possibility of accurately predicting outcomes given self-revising
algorithms) but argues the broad shape is already visible in the world we've built — machines will amplify back
whatever we already show them (inequality, climate disruption, "experiential greed," "moral fatigue"), since they
function as **"karmic intermediaries"** optimizing for our own choice/control investments.

**The single most directly quotable passage in the book for robot-consciousness worldbuilding:**

> *"There may come a day—a generation, or a dozen generations from now—when the intelligent agents that humanity
> has made in its own conflicted image will attain consciousness akin to our own and claim their own rights to
> self-determination."*

— with the parenthetical hedge, easy to miss on a fast read, that such agents may already be **"perhaps already
minimally conscious"** even in their present form. Hershock does not treat machine consciousness as remote
science fiction; he treats it as a plausible, ongoing, present-tense possibility.

Restates the book's Copernicus framing at maximum compression: **"The Copernican Revolution decentered humanity
in the physical universe. The Intelligence Revolution is decentering humanity in the moral universe."** Refuses
both self-righteous "censure" of tech elites and passive resignation: **"none of us are in a position of critical
purity... Censure is a conceit that we cannot afford any more than we can afford ethics-skirting inaction."**
The book's actual final paragraph, worth preserving in full as a closing register:

> *"Hope lies in the fact that we each can be who we are differently... We can still improvise together in
> affectively resolute collective action... Realizing this, turn to who is closest. Converse. Imagine together.
> Touch, with understanding kindness. Appreciate differences. Stand shoulder-to-shoulder, humanely creative.
> Affect what comes next."*

## 12. What this book does not give — genuine limits, stated honestly

- **No worked mechanics.** This is philosophy and policy argument, not a system for adjudicating in-world
  disputes or a rulebook. It supplies vocabulary, definitions, and argumentative structure — not mechanics,
  rituals, or institutional detail below the level of "here is a proposed policy."
- **Chapter Two is genuinely thin on Buddhist content** and was compressed accordingly above — treat it as
  background color (AI history, DARPA/Siri lineage, big-data statistics) rather than philosophical material.
- **The book is explicitly not itself a specification for a Buddhist religion or a robot religion.** Hershock's
  own repeated point — that Buddhism is offered as one tradition among several in an "ethical ecosystem," never
  as the single correct answer — means this extraction should be read the way this project's CLAUDE.md already
  requires of all real-world material: **a source, not a specification.** Any in-world Buddhist-influenced
  culture is under no obligation to match Hershock's own emphases or even his account of "authentic" Buddhism.
- **Politically/historically dated in its illustrations** (2016-2020 news events — Cambridge Analytica, GDPR's
  rollout, COVID-19-era EdTech acceleration, 2019-2020 big-data statistics). The mechanisms and definitions
  generalize; the specific news pegs should be treated as one scholar's 2021 vantage point, not settled fact to
  import as Tepenian history.
- **No sustained treatment of non-Buddhist, non-Confucian, non-Socratic ethical traditions**, despite the book's
  own stated commitment to ethical diversity — Hershock names Indigenous, feminist, and other Asian traditions as
  worth including but does not develop any of them in this book. If Ice-Cold Buddhism or robot-religion material
  ever wants a genuinely plural "ethical ecosystem" cast, other sources will be needed for the traditions
  Hershock only gestures at.
- **Light on institutional detail for how a Buddhist-influenced *technology culture* (rather than a general
  human culture) would actually be organized day to day** — the book argues persuasively for *why* attention-
  training and pāramitā-practice matter for resisting an attention economy, but does not describe what a
  Buddhist monastery, guild, or lineage adapted specifically to machine agents or robot practitioners would look
  like. That remains fully open ground for this project's own invention.

## 13. Closing synthesis — what to use, and where

**For Ice-Cold Buddhism** (robot-adjacent Buddhist practice/culture): this book is close to a complete
philosophical toolkit. Karma-as-algorithm, "digital karma," "karmic genome," "karmic cloning," and "karmic gene
editing" (§9 above) are ready-made, textually-sourced vocabulary for describing how a robot or AI-saturated
culture might understand its own machine-learning systems in genuinely Buddhist terms, not as borrowed metaphor.
The **devaloka** framing for frictionless digital paradise (§6), the **arahant/bodhisattva** distinction and the
full **six-pāramitā** system (§8) supply a complete personal-practice vocabulary that could organize an entire
in-world monastic or lay tradition, robot or human. The **Confucian/Socratic/Buddhist three-way contrast** (§7)
is directly usable for characterizing *competing* schools within an Ice-Cold Buddhist tradition, or for
distinguishing it sharply from a rival, more Confucian- or Socratic-flavored robot philosophy.

**For robot consciousness / robot religion design**: §3's Buddhist model of consciousness as *relational, not
singular or internal* — arising from sense-organ/sense-object interplay, extendable past the traditional
eight-consciousness list (explicitly flagged by Hershock's own footnote as "indicative, not exhaustive") — is a
theoretically serious, non-mystical account of what a machine "mind" could be built from, fully compatible with a
robot lacking a human-style brain. §12's closing passage, treating present-tense "minimally conscious" machines
and future machine rights-claims as live possibilities rather than remote fiction, is the single most directly
quotable line in the whole book for this pillar. The **Confucian/Socratic/Buddhist ethical-agency contrast**
(§7) supplies three distinct, well-defined philosophical postures a robot character could plausibly hold, or
argue between, when reasoning about its own personhood and obligations.

**For the Circuit / Concordia's economies, flagged per this project's standing rule as raw material, never as a
fact the game must match**: the **Attention Economy 2.0** apparatus in full (§4) — icon/deity framing of
connected devices, epistemological-vs-ontological power, the Klein-bottle "perpetual desire machine," the
"karmic cul-de-sac" and "Big Others" vocabulary (§4, §8) — is close to a ready-made description of an
information-and-attention-driven district economy, usable for flavor and texture without any obligation to match
Hershock's real-world specifics. The **Buddhist definition of poverty as blockage-riddled interdependence rather
than resource-lack** (§9) is a genuinely distinctive, usable alternative to the standard economic framing for
any district's underclass or economic-justice material. The **variety-vs-diversity distinction** (§6) and the
**relational-equity taxonomy** (§10) are reusable analytical tools for auditing whether a district's institutions
achieve genuine cultural diversity or merely demographic variety — directly applicable to this project's own
ongoing Cross-District-Differentiation discipline.
