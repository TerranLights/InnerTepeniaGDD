# SOC 2018 Manual — Minor Group Summaries

**Companion file to `SOC_Table_of_Contents.md`.** Brief, roleplay-grade summaries for all 98 minor groups —
one tier below the 23 major groups, specific enough to mean something for a "play as a [profession]" content
creator, deliberately short of the manual's own 867-detailed-occupation depth. Each entry: what the cluster
actually does, plus a handful of representative example job titles pulled from the real manual (not the
exhaustive list).

**Source:** `soc_2018_manual.pdf`, same folder. Compiled 2026-07-26 via four research passes across the manual's
full "Occupational Classification Definitions" section (printed pages 31–220).

---

## 11-0000 Management Occupations

**11-1000 Top Executives** — Sets overall direction and policy for an organization from the highest level, or holds elected legislative office. Covers everyone from corporate chief executives to city council members.
Examples: Chief Executive, General/Operations Manager, City Council Member, Senator.

**11-2000 Advertising, Marketing, Promotions, Public Relations, and Sales Managers** — Plans and directs how an organization promotes itself, prices and moves its products, and manages its public image or fundraising.
Examples: Advertising Director, Marketing Manager, Sales Manager, Public Relations Manager, Fundraising Manager.

**11-3000 Operations Specialties Managers** — Runs a specific internal function of an organization — IT systems, finance, production, purchasing, logistics, HR, or training — rather than the whole enterprise.
Examples: Computer and Information Systems Manager, Financial Manager, Purchasing Manager, Human Resources Manager, Training and Development Manager.

**11-9000 Other Management Occupations** — A catch-all for directors and managers of specific industries or facility types — agriculture, construction, schools, hospitals, hospitality, entertainment, and emergency response.
Examples: Farmer/Rancher Manager, Construction Manager, School Principal, Hospital Administrator, Emergency Management Director.

## 13-0000 Business and Financial Operations Occupations

**13-1000 Business Operations Specialists** — Handles the analytical and coordinating work that keeps a business running smoothly — buying, compliance, cost estimating, HR administration, project/event planning, market research.
Examples: Human Resources Specialist, Compliance Officer, Cost Estimator, Project Management Specialist, Market Research Analyst.

**13-2000 Financial Specialists** — Handles the money-facing analytical work of a business or institution: recording and auditing finances, appraising value, assessing credit risk, and determining tax liability.
Examples: Accountant/Auditor, Budget Analyst, Financial Analyst, Loan Officer, Tax Preparer.

## 15-0000 Computer and Mathematical Occupations

**15-1200 Computer Occupations** — Builds, secures, and maintains computer systems, networks, databases, and software — from writing code and designing websites to running help desks and researching new computing theory.
Examples: Computer Systems Analyst, Information Security Analyst, Software Developer, Database Administrator, Web Developer.

**15-2000 Mathematical Science Occupations** — Applies mathematical and statistical theory to real-world problems — forecasting risk, optimizing operations, analyzing data, pure or applied math research.
Examples: Actuary, Mathematician, Operations Research Analyst, Statistician, Data Scientist.

## 17-0000 Architecture and Engineering Occupations

**17-1000 Architects, Surveyors, and Cartographers** — Plans and designs buildings and land use, or measures and maps the Earth's surface for legal, construction, and geographic purposes.
Examples: Building Architect, Landscape Architect, Surveyor, Cartographer/Photogrammetrist.

**17-2000 Engineers** — Applies engineering principles across a huge range of disciplines to design, build, and test everything from aircraft and chemical plants to nuclear reactors, bridges, and computer hardware.
Examples: Aerospace Engineer, Civil Engineer, Electrical Engineer, Mechanical Engineer, Petroleum Engineer.

**17-3000 Drafters, Engineering Technicians, and Mapping Technicians** — Produces the technical drawings and hands-on support that turns engineers' and surveyors' designs into usable specifications.
Examples: Architectural and Civil Drafter, Mechanical Drafter, Electro-Mechanical/Mechatronics Technician, Calibration Technician, Surveying and Mapping Technician.

## 19-0000 Life, Physical, and Social Science Occupations

**19-1000 Life Scientists** — Conducts research into living systems — agriculture, biology, wildlife, forests, medicine — usually in a laboratory or field research setting.
Examples: Animal Scientist, Microbiologist, Zoologist/Wildlife Biologist, Conservation Scientist, Medical Scientist.

**19-2000 Physical Scientists** — Studies the physical universe: matter, energy, the atmosphere, and the Earth itself, through research, observation, and experimentation.
Examples: Physicist, Astronomer, Meteorologist, Chemist, Geoscientist/Hydrologist.

**19-3000 Social Scientists and Related Workers** — Studies human behavior, society, economies, politics, and history through research and analysis, plus applies psychology clinically/organizationally and plans land use for communities.
Examples: Economist, Psychologist, Sociologist, Urban and Regional Planner, Political Scientist/Historian/Anthropologist.

**19-4000 Life, Physical, and Social Science Technicians** — Provides hands-on lab and field support to scientists — running tests, collecting samples, maintaining equipment.
Examples: Chemical Technician, Biological Technician, Environmental Science Technician, Forensic Science Technician, Social Science Research Assistant.

**19-5000 Occupational Health and Safety Specialists and Technicians** — Reviews workplaces to identify and control chemical, physical, biological, and ergonomic hazards, then enforces or supports safety-regulation compliance.
Examples: Occupational Health and Safety Specialist, Industrial Hygienist, Ergonomics Technician.

## 21-0000 Community and Social Service Occupations

**21-1000 Counselors, Social Workers, and Other Community and Social Service Specialists** — Provides direct counseling, casework, and support services for mental health, addiction, family, educational, or rehabilitation needs, plus broader community health and probation support.
Examples: Substance Abuse Counselor, Marriage and Family Therapist, Child and Family Social Worker, Probation Officer, Community Health Worker.

**21-2000 Religious Workers** — Leads worship, provides spiritual and moral guidance, and organizes religious education and outreach programs for a faith community.
Examples: Clergy (Priest, Imam, Rabbi), Youth Ministry Director, Missionary.

## 23-0000 Legal Occupations

**23-1000 Lawyers, Judges, and Related Workers** — Practices, clerks for, or adjudicates law: representing clients in litigation and legal transactions, assisting judges, arbitrating/mediating/ruling on disputes.
Examples: Attorney, Public Defender, Judicial Clerk, Circuit Court Judge, Ombudsman.

**23-2000 Legal Support Workers** — Non-attorney roles supporting legal work — researching facts, preparing documents, searching property/insurance records — without practicing law themselves.
Examples: Paralegal/Legal Aide, Escrow Officer, Title Officer, Legal Technician.

## 25-0000 Educational Instruction and Library Occupations

**25-1000 Postsecondary Teachers** — College and university faculty teaching virtually every academic discipline, often combining instruction with research.
Examples: Accounting Professor, Computer Science Professor, Physics Professor, Nursing Professor, History Professor.

**25-2000 Preschool, Elementary, Middle, Secondary, and Special Education Teachers** — Classroom teachers instructing children from preschool through high school, in general-education or special-education settings.
Examples: Preschool Teacher, Kindergarten Teacher, Elementary School Teacher, High School Teacher, Special Education Teacher.

**25-3000 Other Teachers and Instructors** — Instruction outside the standard K-12/college track: adult basic education and ESL, hobby/self-enrichment classes, short-term substituting, private tutoring.
Examples: Adult ESL Instructor, Knitting Instructor, Short-Term Substitute Teacher, Reading/Algebra Tutor.

**25-4000 Librarians, Curators, and Archivists** — Administer, preserve, and provide access to books, records, and collections — libraries to museum artifact conservation and archival appraisal.
Examples: Archivist, Curator, Museum Technician/Conservator, School Librarian, Library Technician.

**25-9000 Other Educational Instruction and Library Occupations** — Instructional-support and coordination roles that aren't classroom teachers: developing curricula, assisting teachers/faculty, agricultural/home-management extension education.
Examples: Instructional Coordinator, Teaching Assistant, Agricultural Extension Educator, GED Examiner.

## 27-0000 Arts, Design, Entertainment, Sports, and Media Occupations

**27-1000 Art and Design Workers** — Visual artists and commercial/product designers spanning fine art and craft to fashion, graphic, interior, and product design, set/exhibit design and animation.
Examples: Fine Artist, Graphic Designer, Fashion Designer, Interior Designer, 3D Animator.

**27-2000 Entertainers and Performers, Sports and Related Workers** — Performers and competitors across entertainment and athletics — acting, directing/producing, professional sports, coaching/officiating, dance, music.
Examples: Actor, Professional Football Player, Boxing Coach, Ballerina, Musician/Singer, Comedian.

**27-3000 Media and Communication Workers** — Content creators and communicators for broadcast and print media: announcing, journalism, public relations, writing/editing, language interpretation/court reporting.
Examples: Radio Disc Jockey, News Anchor/Journalist, Public Relations Specialist, Playwright, Court Interpreter.

**27-4000 Media and Communication Equipment Workers** — Technical crew operating the equipment behind media production — audio/video, broadcast, sound, lighting, photography and camera/film editing.
Examples: Sound Engineering Technician, Gaffer, Photographer, Camera Operator, Film Editor.

## 29-0000 Healthcare Practitioners and Technical Occupations

**29-1000 Healthcare Diagnosing or Treating Practitioners** — Licensed clinicians who directly diagnose and treat patients: chiropractors, dentists, pharmacists, physician assistants, podiatrists, therapists (OT/PT/speech), veterinarians, nurses (RN/anesthetist/midwife/practitioner), audiologists, and physicians/surgeons across dozens of specialties.
Examples: Dentist, Pharmacist, Physical Therapist, Registered Nurse, Family Practice Physician, Cardiologist, Veterinarian.

**29-2000 Health Technologists and Technicians** — Allied-health technical staff who run diagnostic equipment and provide supervised clinical support.
Examples: EMT/Paramedic, Medical Laboratory Technologist, X-Ray/Radiologic Technologist, Licensed Practical Nurse, Dispensing Optician.

**29-9000 Other Healthcare Practitioners and Technical Occupations** — Remaining specialized clinical/technical roles: athletic training, genetic counseling, surgical assisting, health information/medical registrar work.
Examples: Athletic Trainer, Genetic Counselor, Surgical Assistant, Health Information Technologist/Medical Registrar.

## 31-0000 Healthcare Support Occupations

**31-1100 Home Health and Personal Care Aides; and Nursing Assistants, Orderlies, and Psychiatric Aides** — Direct hands-on caregiving with daily-living needs under the supervision of licensed nursing/medical staff.
Examples: Home Health Aide, Personal Care Aide, Certified Nursing Assistant, Hospital Orderly, Psychiatric Aide.

**31-2000 Occupational Therapy and Physical Therapist Assistants and Aides** — Assist licensed occupational and physical therapists in carrying out treatment plans and preparing patients/rooms for rehabilitative care.
Examples: Occupational Therapy Assistant, Occupational Therapy Aide, Physical Therapist Assistant, Physical Therapy Aide.

**31-9000 Other Healthcare Support Occupations** — Remaining support roles: massage therapy, dental/medical assisting, equipment sterilization/prep, medical transcription, pharmacy aide work, veterinary assisting, phlebotomy.
Examples: Massage Therapist, Dental Assistant, Medical Assistant, Medical Transcriptionist, Phlebotomist.

## 33-0000 Protective Service Occupations

**33-1000 Supervisors of Protective Service Workers** — Directly oversee and coordinate protective service teams — correctional staff, police officers, firefighters, security personnel.
Examples: Corrections Sergeant, Police Lieutenant, Fire Lieutenant, Security Guard Supervisor, TSA Screener Supervisor.

**33-2000 Firefighting and Prevention Workers** — Controls and extinguishes fires and responds to life/property/environmental emergencies; separately inspects buildings/forests for fire hazards or investigates causes.
Examples: Firefighter, Smoke Jumper, Arson Investigator, Wildfire Prevention Specialist.

**33-3000 Law Enforcement Workers** — Enforces laws and maintains order — patrolling, investigating crimes, guarding courtrooms/inmates, protecting wildlife, ticketing violations, policing transit systems.
Examples: Police and Sheriff's Patrol Officer, Homicide Detective, Correctional Officer, Fish and Game Warden, Parking Enforcement Officer, Transit Police.

**33-9000 Other Protective Service Workers** — Non-sworn protection and safety roles: animal control, private investigation, security guarding, gambling surveillance, public-space safety monitoring.
Examples: Animal Control Officer, Private Investigator, Security Guard, Casino Surveillance Officer, Airport Security Screener, Lifeguard.

## 35-0000 Food Preparation and Serving Related Occupations

**35-1000 Supervisors of Food Preparation and Serving Workers** — Chefs/head cooks who direct and often participate in food preparation, and supervisors who coordinate broader kitchen/dining staff.
Examples: Executive Chef, Sous Chef, Banquet Supervisor, Kitchen Supervisor.

**35-2000 Cooks and Food Preparation Workers** — Prepares and cooks food across settings from fast food to fine dining to institutions and private homes, plus non-cooking prep work.
Examples: Fast Food Fry Cook, Restaurant Line Cook, Private Chef, Griddle Cook, Salad Maker.

**35-3000 Food and Beverage Serving Workers** — Takes orders and serves food/drinks to patrons — behind a bar, at a counter, at a table, or in non-restaurant settings.
Examples: Bartender, Barista, Waiter/Waitress, Room Service Server.

**35-9000 Other Food Preparation and Serving Related Workers** — Support roles that keep dining service running smoothly: bussing/clearing tables, dishwashing, hosting/seating patrons.
Examples: Busser, Dishwasher, Restaurant Host/Hostess, Kitchen Steward.

## 37-0000 Building and Grounds Cleaning and Maintenance Occupations

**37-1000 Supervisors of Building and Grounds Cleaning and Maintenance Workers** — Directly supervise cleaning/janitorial crews or landscaping/groundskeeping crews.
Examples: Custodial Supervisor, Grounds Maintenance Supervisor, Head Greenskeeper.

**37-2000 Building Cleaning and Pest Control Workers** — Keeps buildings clean and orderly, and applies chemicals/traps to remove pests and vermin from structures.
Examples: Janitor, Housekeeper, Window Washer, Exterminator.

**37-3000 Grounds Maintenance Workers** — Landscapes and maintains outdoor property — mowing, planting, irrigation, pesticide application, and tree trimming/removal.
Examples: Groundskeeper, Lawn Caretaker, Pesticide Sprayer, Tree Surgeon/Arborist.

## 39-0000 Personal Care and Service Occupations

**39-1000 Supervisors of Personal Care and Service Workers** — Supervises gambling-floor staff and other personal-service workers (childcare, hair salons, entertainment/recreation venues).
Examples: Pit Boss, Slot Key Person, Arcade Supervisor, Hair Salon Supervisor.

**39-2000 Animal Care and Service Workers** — Trains animals for work, performance, or assistance roles, and provides day-to-day feeding, grooming, and welfare care for non-farm animals.
Examples: Guide Dog Trainer, Horse Breaker, Dog Groomer, Zookeeper, Kennel Worker.

**39-3000 Entertainment Attendants and Related Workers** — Runs the operational side of gambling floors, movie projection, and venue attending.
Examples: Blackjack Dealer, Movie Projectionist, Theater Usher, Wardrobe Attendant, Ski Lift Operator.

**39-4000 Funeral Service Workers** — Prepares bodies for burial or cremation and coordinates the logistics and rites of funeral services.
Examples: Embalmer, Crematory Operator, Funeral Attendant, Mortician.

**39-5000 Personal Appearance Workers** — Provides hair, nail, skin, and makeup services to clients for grooming or appearance enhancement.
Examples: Barber, Hairstylist/Cosmetologist, Manicurist, Special Effects Makeup Artist, Electrologist.

**39-6000 Baggage Porters, Bellhops, and Concierges** — Handles travelers' and guests' luggage and provides personalized hospitality assistance at hotels and terminals.
Examples: Skycap, Bellhop, Hotel Concierge.

**39-7000 Tour and Travel Guides** — Escorts individuals or groups through sightseeing destinations or plans and leads longer-distance tours and expeditions.
Examples: Museum Guide, Sightseeing Guide, Cruise Director, River Expedition Guide.

**39-9000 Other Personal Care and Service Workers** — Broad grouping covering childcare, personal fitness coaching, recreation programming, residential/dorm advising, and roles like butlers and valets.
Examples: Nanny, Personal Trainer, Camp Counselor, Dormitory Counselor, Butler, Valet.

## 41-0000 Sales and Related Occupations

**41-1000 Supervisors of Sales Workers** — Directly supervises retail or non-retail sales staff, often also handling budgeting, purchasing, and personnel duties.
Examples: Cashier Supervisor, Insurance Sales Supervisor, Telemarketer Supervisor.

**41-2000 Retail Sales Workers** — Handles money transactions and sells merchandise directly to consumers in stores and similar settings.
Examples: Cashier, Retail Salesperson, Auto Parts Salesperson, Car Rental Agent.

**41-3000 Sales Representatives, Services** — Sells intangible services — advertising space, insurance, securities/investments, travel packages, business services — to clients.
Examples: Insurance Sales Agent, Stock Broker, Travel Agent, Advertising Account Executive.

**41-4000 Sales Representatives, Wholesale and Manufacturing** — Sells goods on behalf of wholesalers or manufacturers to businesses, sometimes requiring technical/scientific product knowledge.
Examples: Pharmaceutical Sales Representative, Wholesale Diamond Broker, Surgical Instruments Sales Representative.

**41-9000 Other Sales and Related Workers** — Covers modeling/product demonstration, real estate sales, technically-trained sales engineers, telemarketing, and door-to-door/street selling.
Examples: Fashion Model, Real Estate Agent, Sales Engineer, Telemarketer, Auctioneer, Street Vendor.

## 43-0000 Office and Administrative Support Occupations

**43-1000 Supervisors of Office and Administrative Support Workers** — Directly supervises clerical and administrative support staff.
Examples: Clerical Supervisor, Payroll Supervisor, Teller Supervisor.

**43-2000 Communications Equipment Operators** — Operates switchboards and telephone systems to route calls, relay messages, and provide directory/billing assistance.
Examples: Switchboard Operator, Directory Assistance Operator, Radio Operator.

**43-3000 Financial Clerks** — Handles routine financial transactions and recordkeeping — billing, bookkeeping, collections, payroll, teller work, gambling-cage cash handling.
Examples: Bookkeeper, Bank Teller, Debt Collector, Payroll Clerk, Casino Cage Cashier.

**43-4000 Information and Record Clerks** — Interviews, registers, and processes information for the public — brokerage transactions, correspondence, government eligibility, hotel check-in, libraries, loans, reservations.
Examples: Hotel Front Desk Clerk, Loan Processor, Library Assistant, Airline Ticket Agent, Census Taker.

**43-5000 Material Recording, Scheduling, Dispatching, and Distributing Workers** — Tracks and routes the movement of goods, mail, and service crews — freight/courier logistics, 911/dispatch, meter reading, postal handling, shipping/inventory recordkeeping.
Examples: Freight Agent, 911 Dispatcher, Mail Carrier, Tow Truck Dispatcher, Warehouse Clerk.

**43-6000 Secretaries and Administrative Assistants** — Provides clerical and administrative support from routine correspondence and scheduling to high-level executive support, with legal/medical specializations.
Examples: Executive Assistant, Legal Secretary, Medical Secretary, Office Secretary.

**43-9000 Other Office and Administrative Support Workers** — Wide catch-all for clerical production tasks: data entry, word processing, desktop publishing, insurance paperwork, mail handling, general office clerking, proofreading, statistical/actuarial clerking.
Examples: Data Entry Keyer, Office Clerk General, Desktop Publisher, Notary Public, Proofreader.

## 45-0000 Farming, Fishing, and Forestry Occupations

**45-1000 Supervisors of Farming, Fishing, and Forestry Workers** — Directly supervises and coordinates agricultural, forestry, and aquacultural crews out in the field.
Examples: Corral Boss, Cranberry Bog Supervisor, Fish Hatchery Supervisor.

**45-2000 Agricultural Workers** — Inspects farm products for regulatory compliance, breeds and grades livestock/crops, and performs the hands-on manual labor of planting, tending, and harvesting.
Examples: Agricultural Inspector, Animal Breeder, Combine Operator, Sheep Shearer, Irrigation Worker.

**45-3000 Fishing and Hunting Workers** — Hunts, traps, catches, or gathers wild animals, aquatic animals, and plants using nets, traps, or other gear.
Examples: Deer Hunter, Predatory Animal Trapper, Seaweed Harvester, Wild Oyster Harvester.

**45-4000 Forest, Conservation, and Logging Workers** — Performs manual labor to develop, maintain, or protect forests/wildlands, and separately fells, skids, hauls, and grades timber.
Examples: Reforestation Worker, Lumberjack, Grapple Skidder Operator, Timber Estimator.

## 47-0000 Construction and Extraction Occupations

**47-1000 Supervisors of Construction and Extraction Workers** — Directly supervises and coordinates construction trades or extraction (mining/drilling) crews.
Examples: Carpenter Supervisor, Quarry Boss, Rig Supervisor, Solar Panel Installation Supervisor.

**47-2000 Construction Trades Workers** — The hands-on skilled trades that physically build and finish structures: masonry, carpentry, flooring, concrete, drywall, electrical, glazing, insulation, painting, plumbing, roofing, metalwork.
Examples: Electrician, Carpenter, Roofer, Brickmason, Plumber, Structural Iron and Steel Worker, Solar Photovoltaic Installer.

**47-3000 Helpers, Construction Trades** — Assists skilled construction tradespeople by supplying materials/tools and cleaning work areas.
Examples: Brick Carrier, Carpenter Assistant, Wallpaper Helper, Plumber Assistant.

**47-4000 Other Construction and Related Workers** — Catch-all of specialized construction-adjacent trades: building inspection, elevator installation, fence erection, hazmat removal, highway maintenance, rail-track laying, septic/sewer servicing.
Examples: Home Inspector, Elevator Mechanic, Wood Fence Installer, Asbestos Abatement Worker, Track Repairer, Septic Tank Cleaner.

**47-5000 Extraction Workers** — Operates drilling rigs, mining machines, and explosives to extract oil, gas, coal, metal ores, and stone, on the surface and underground.
Examples: Oil Well Driller, Mining Shovel Operator, Blast Setter, Roof Bolting Coal Miner, Rock Splitter.

## 49-0000 Installation, Maintenance, and Repair Occupations

**49-1000 Supervisors of Installation, Maintenance, and Repair Workers** — Directly supervises and coordinates mechanics, installers, and repairers.
Examples: Automobile Body Repair Supervisor, Fleet Maintenance Supervisor, Railroad Car Repair Supervisor.

**49-2000 Electrical and Electronic Equipment Mechanics, Installers, and Repairers** — Repairs, installs, and maintains electrical/electronic gear — office machines, radios/telecom towers, avionics, motors, industrial controls, home AV gear, security/fire-alarm systems.
Examples: ATM Servicer, Radio Frequency Technician, Avionics Technician, Home Theater Installer, Burglar Alarm Installer.

**49-3000 Vehicle and Mobile Equipment Mechanics, Installers, and Repairers** — Diagnoses, adjusts, and repairs aircraft, automobiles, buses/trucks/diesel engines, farm and heavy construction equipment, rail cars, small engines.
Examples: Aircraft Mechanic, Auto Body Customizer, School Bus Mechanic, Combine Mechanic, Motorcycle Service Technician, RV Mechanic.

**49-9000 Other Installation, Maintenance, and Repair Occupations** — Broad bucket of specialized repair trades: valve/door mechanisms, HVAC and appliances, industrial machinery/millwrights, power/telecom line installation, precision instruments, general building maintenance, wind turbines, locksmiths, riggers, commercial divers, vending machines.
Examples: HVAC Mechanic, Millwright, Electric Utility Lineworker, Piano Tuner, Wind Turbine Mechanic, Locksmith, Commercial Diver.

## 51-0000 Production Occupations

**51-1000 Supervisors of Production Workers** — Directly supervises and coordinates production and operating workers.
Examples: Assembly Line Supervisor, Machinist Supervisor, Printing Worker Supervisor.

**51-2000 Assemblers and Fabricators** — Assembles, fits, and fastens parts into finished products — aircraft components, electrical/electronic equipment, structural metal, fiberglass items, timing devices, consumer goods.
Examples: Aircraft Riveter, Circuit Board Assembler, Fiberglass Boat Builder, Team Assembler, Doll Maker.

**51-3000 Food Processing Workers** — Bakes goods; butchers and processes meat/poultry/fish; operates machines that roast, batch-mix, or cook food products for manufacture.
Examples: Bread Baker, Butcher, Poultry Eviscerator, Cheese Maker, Doughnut Machine Operator.

**51-4000 Metal Workers and Plastic Workers** — Sets up and operates metal/plastic-shaping machine tools, plus machinists, furnace/casting workers, patternmakers, molders, tool-and-die makers, welders, platers.
Examples: Machinist, Tool and Die Maker, Welder, Foundry Mold Maker, Nickel Plater.

**51-5100 Printing Workers** — Formats and proofs material for print (prepress), operates printing presses, and binds/finishes printed products.
Examples: Plate Mounter, Offset Press Operator, Bookbinder.

**51-6000 Textile, Apparel, and Furnishings Workers** — Launders/dry-cleans and presses textiles; operates sewing/textile machines; hand-sews, tailors, makes patterns; works leather/shoes; upholsters furniture.
Examples: Sewing Machine Operator, Tailor, Cobbler, Furniture Upholsterer, Jacquard Loom Weaver.

**51-7000 Woodworkers** — Cuts, shapes, and assembles wood into cabinets, furniture, and models; refinishes furniture; operates woodworking/sawing machines.
Examples: Cabinet Builder, Furniture Sander, Wood Lathe Operator, Wood Carver.

**51-8000 Plant and System Operators** — Controls and maintains large-scale industrial systems — nuclear/power plants, boilers, water/wastewater treatment, chemical plants, gas plants, petroleum refining/pipelines.
Examples: Nuclear Reactor Operator, Boiler Room Operator, Sewage Plant Operator, Oil Refiner.

**51-9000 Other Production Occupations** — Large miscellany of production work: chemical processing, crushing/grinding/mixing, cutting, molding/extruding/pressing, furnace/kiln tending, quality inspection, jewelry-making, dental/medical/optical appliance fabrication, packaging, painting/coating, semiconductor processing, CNC operation.
Examples: Brewmaster, Goldsmith, CNC Machinist, Wafer Fabricator, Glass Blower, Tire Builder.

## 53-0000 Transportation and Material Moving Occupations

**53-1000 Supervisors of Transportation and Material Moving Workers** — Directly supervises and coordinates aircraft cargo handling crews, material movers, machine/vehicle operators, and passenger attendants.
Examples: Air Cargo Ground Crew Supervisor, Warehouse Supervisor, Truck Driver Supervisor, Flight Service Manager.

**53-2000 Air Transportation Workers** — Pilots and navigates aircraft (scheduled and nonscheduled), controls air traffic and airfield operations, and staffs flight attendant cabin-safety/service roles.
Examples: Airline Captain, Helicopter Pilot, Air Traffic Control Operator, Airline Flight Attendant.

**53-3000 Motor Vehicle Operators** — Drives ambulances (non-EMT), delivery/sales route trucks, heavy tractor-trailers, light delivery trucks, school buses, transit/intercity buses, shuttles, taxis.
Examples: Pizza Delivery Driver, Dump Truck Driver, School Bus Operator, Cab Driver.

**53-4000 Rail Transportation Workers** — Operates locomotives and rail yard engines, works railroad brakes/signals/switches, coordinates train crews, runs subway/streetcar trains.
Examples: Train Engineer, Railway Switch Operator, Freight Conductor, Trolley Car Operator.

**53-5000 Water Transportation Workers** — Serves as deckhands/sailors maintaining a ship; captains/pilots/operates ships and motorboats; serves as ship engineers maintaining engines and mechanical systems.
Examples: Merchant Mariner, Harbor Pilot, Water Taxi Operator, Tugboat Engineer.

**53-6000 Other Transportation Workers** — Tends bridges/locks, attends parking lots, fuels/services vehicles and aircraft, conducts traffic studies, inspects transportation equipment, attends to passenger safety/service.
Examples: Lighthouse Keeper, Valet Parker, Gas Pump Attendant, Aircraft Refueler, Train Attendant.

**53-7000 Material Moving Workers** — Operates conveyors, cranes, dredges, hoists/winches, forklifts, pumping stations; performs hand-labor material moving; collects refuse/recyclables.
Examples: Forklift Operator, Boom Crane Operator, Warehouse Stocker, Garbage Collector, Grocery Store Bagger.

## 55-0000 Military Specific Occupations

**55-1000 Military Officer Special and Tactical Operations Leaders** — Commissioned officers who plan and direct combat, reconnaissance, and support operations across air crews, armored vehicles, artillery/missiles, command-and-control, infantry, special forces.
Examples: Flight Deck Officer, Tank Officer, Infantry Weapons Officer, Sea-Air-Land (SEAL) Officer.

**55-2000 First-Line Enlisted Military Supervisors** — Enlisted personnel who supervise and coordinate air crew members, weapons specialists/crew members, and other tactical operations specialists, often while performing the same duties themselves.
Examples: Armor Senior Sergeant, Field Artillery Senior Sergeant, Command Post Superintendent.

**55-3000 Military Enlisted Tactical Operations and Air/Weapons Specialists and Crew Members** — The enlisted personnel who actually crew and operate combat systems: air crews, carrier launch/recovery equipment, armored vehicles, artillery and missiles, command-and-control systems, infantry weapons, special forces operations.
Examples: Infantryman, M1A1 Tank Crewman, Gunner's Mate, Combatant Swimmer (SEAL), Mortarman.
