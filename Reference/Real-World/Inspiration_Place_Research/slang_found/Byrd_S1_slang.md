# Byrd S1 — slang and jargon found by chance (2026-11-01)

Found while researching unit S1 (physical site: ice, ground and climate). Real-world usage only; not checked for wider currency.

| Term | Meaning as used | Where found |
|---|---|---|
| zero-zero | Aviation: zero ceiling and zero visibility; used by a Byrd weather observer for blowing-snow conditions ("For aircraft it is zero-zero, since blowing snow makes it even worse as you get closer to the ground.") | 1966 US Navy cruise book (Burton Island), page 74, https://www.e-yearbook.com/yearbooks/Burton_Island_AGB_1_Cruise_Book/1966/Page_74.html |
| coffee-can method | Glaciologists' name for measuring ice-sheet thickness change at a point: a marker anchored at depth in the firn, its vertical motion tracked by GPS and compared with long-term snow accumulation | Search snippets of Hamilton, Whillans et al. (1998), "First point measurements of ice-sheet thickness change in Antarctica," *Annals of Glaciology* |
| the Dragon | Field name for a measurement site just outboard of Ice Stream B (West Antarctica) | Same paper (snippet) |
