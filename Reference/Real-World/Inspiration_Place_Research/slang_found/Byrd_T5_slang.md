|---|---|---|---|
| **hitch** | North Slope oilfield workers, Deadhorse/Prudhoe Bay, Alaska | A rotation on site, usually two weeks on, then two at home: *"two weeks on-site (a hitch, they call it)"* | City Journal, 17 July 2026 |
| **"Sleep when you're dead"** | A Deadhorse worker, asked about sleep | Stock reply to long shifts on rotation | City Journal, 2026 |
| **Haul Road** | Alaska drivers | The Dalton Highway, Fairbanks to Deadhorse | NPS Coldfoot page (search snippet) |
| **the caves** | USPS Stamp Fulfillment Services staff, Kansas City | Their name for the underground SubTropolis complex | *The Postal Record*, February 2023 |
| **Pillar 210** (pillar addresses) | SubTropolis tenants | Units are addressed by the number of the rock pillar they sit at | *The Postal Record*, 2023; Wikipedia |
| **the Hub of the North / "the hub"** | Hay River, Northwest Territories | Town nickname: where freight changes between road, rail, barge and air | Town of Hay River strategy, 2025; Cabin Radio, 2024 |
| **"the longest school zone in the world"** | Ron Near, road manager, Tibbitt to Contwoyto Winter Road | The ice road, for its 25 km/h (or 15 km/h) loaded speed limits | Sevunts, 20 February 2013 |
| **problem lakes** | Same ice road | Lakes with thinner ice, where limits drop to 15 km/h | Sevunts, 2013 |
| **Express Lanes** | Same ice road | Lanes on big lakes where empty trucks may run 60 to 70 km/h | Sevunts, 2013 |
| **punched (loads)** | Mike Lowing, Rio Tinto winter-road director | "This year we punched just a tad under 6,200 loads": loads sent north | Cabin Radio, 19 April 2021 |
| **super B** | Canadian trucking | A B-train combination; on the ice road, a two-tank fuel tanker of about 41 to 42 t | Sevunts, 2013 |
| **truckies** | Australia | Truck drivers | Many Australian sources (search snippets) |
| **"the crossroads of Australia"** | Port Augusta, South Australia | Nickname from its position at the head of Spencer Gulf, where the east–west and south–north highways meet | Wikipedia mirrors (search snippet) |
| **assembly yard** | Australian road-train operators | Yard at a town's edge where road trains are split into single trailers (or joined up) | Wikipedia, "Road train" |
| **dog trailer / dolly / converter dolly** | Australian trucking | Trailer on a permanent dolly with an A-frame drawbar; the dolly that turns a semitrailer into a towed trailer | Wikipedia, "Road train" |
| **Long Vehicle / ROAD TRAIN** (signs) | Australia | Mandatory signs: "Long Vehicle" on 22 to 30 m combinations, "ROAD TRAIN" on over 30 m | Wikipedia, "Road train" |
| **channel 40** | Australian truckies on UHF CB | The national road channel; "call them on 40" before overtaking a road train | Wikipedia "UHF CB"; caravan guides |
| **transfista** | Laredo, Texas, border freight | The transfer (drayage) driver who shuttles a trailer across the bridge between the two border yards | Cargado broker guide |
| **transportista** | U.S.–Mexico freight | A Mexican drayage carrier who crosses the border and clears customs | Broker guide (search snippet) |
| **agente aduanal** | Mexico | Licensed Mexican customs broker (formal term, not slang) | Cargado broker guide |
| **check calls** | U.S. dispatch and brokerage offices | Routine calls to drivers to confirm a load's position and status | Vendor blog (search snippet) |

| **Memphis Superhub** | FedEx, Memphis | The FedEx Express World Hub | Search snippet |

| **Schimmi** | Duisburg | Nickname of the TV detective Horst Schimanski, whose setting was Ruhrort | Duisburg Kontor leaflet, 2020 |
