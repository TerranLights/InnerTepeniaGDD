# Slang and special terms found by chance in unit C6 (2026-11-01, resume session)
Real-world terms only; collected, not researched. Sources are in the C6 file (sections 6.3-6.5 and C-1) and its search log.
- nukes: US Navy submariners' name for the engine-room crew (reactor operators, nuclear electricians, nuclear mechanics) (The War Zone, Aaron Amick, 2020).
- coners / the Cone: US submariners' name for everyone, and every space, forward of the engine room (same source).
- "Push the Cone": the engine-room crew's phrase for its own mission (same source).
- NUB ("Non-Useful Body"): a new, unqualified crew member on a US submarine (same source).
- shower techs: nickname for sonarmen (same source).
- A-Gangers: auxiliary-division sailors, called "the natural predator of the sonarman" (same source).
- hot-racking: sharing bunks in shifts on a submarine (CNN, USS Missouri, 2016; also in C9).
- familigramme / "famili": the French Navy's short one-way family message to a submariner on patrol (Cité de la Mer press kit on Le Redoutable, 2017).
- bannettes chaudes ("hot bunks"): French submariners' term for two berths shared by three people (same source).
- oreilles d'or ("golden ears"): French sonar operators trained to identify about 90% of the sounds around the submarine (same source).
- remote hands: data-center work done on site on behalf of a customer who is not there (racking, cabling, hardware checks) (recruiter and job-posting summaries; unverified).
- Panama / Pitman schedule: the 12-hour 2-2-3 shift rotation common among data-center technicians (recruiter summary; unverified).
- kentai ("body donation"): Japanese term used for Aibo given up as spare-parts donors for repairs (Forbes Japan, 2018).
