## 1 · The entry, in the picks file's format

```
##### Relung Panen
- Dutch polders and their water boards, Netherlands [PRIMARY] // seabed pumped dry and kept dry by an elected water board with its own tax; the state chose who could farm it (3,018 applicants for 135 farms in 1951) and placed village churches at the edges so no faith dominated
- Westland greenhouse cluster, Netherlands [PRIMARY] // the world's largest connected glasshouse region, about 2,300 ha, grew from fruit walls on ground built up with shipped-in manure and dune sand; nearly half the regional workforce is in horticulture, with about 17,000 migrant workers in summer
- Turpan's karez water system, Xinjiang, China [SECONDARY] // desert oasis watered by underground channels that carry mountain meltwater by gravity; working channels fell from 1,084 in 1949 to 169 in 2023 as electric wells took over, while raisins dry for weeks in perforated mud-brick houses
- Al-Ahsa oasis, Saudi Arabia [SECONDARY] // palm oasis once fed by artesian springs, where farmers held written water rights by the day and hour; a state drainage scheme fought waterlogging and salt, its outflow made a bird lake, and thirty-six weekly markets are named by day
- Almería's Campo de Dalías greenhouse region, Spain [SECONDARY] // a dry coastal plain covered since 1963 with plastic greenhouses: Almería province grew 3.9 million tonnes of vegetables in 2023-24 and exported 70%, while its migrant workforce's wages and housing remain open public disputes
- The Arava valley, Israel [SECONDARY] // a desert rift valley of about 600 family farms that grows half or more of Israel's fresh vegetable exports, worked mostly by Thai migrants who keep their own festival apart; its southern half drinks desalinated seawater
- Matanuska Valley Colony, Alaska [SECONDARY] // a 1935 state-planned farm colony in a cold valley that briefly supplied over half of Alaska's farm sales, then largely failed to short seasons, steep freight and distant markets, leaving a co-op habit and a harvest fair
- Israeli kibbutz and moshav settlements, Israel [SECONDARY] // desert farm villages where a few hundred families, a regional research station and a large separately housed migrant workforce grow winter vegetables for export under high winter sun on under an inch of rain a year
- SEKEM, Egypt [SECONDARY] // a farm begun in 1977 on 70 hectares of desert, ringed with trees and fed by its own compost yard, that built schools, a clinic, a university and work-time culture around production and exports about half its sales
- Aalsmeer and Royal FloraHolland, Netherlands [SECONDARY] // a growers' cooperative flower auction that moved beside a major airport in 1972, so flowers flown in chilled from four continents reach a falling-price clock within hours of landing, worked largely by registered migrant labor
- Wageningen "Food Valley," Netherlands [SECONDARY] // a small university town whose agricultural university, trial fields, climate rooms and corporate food labs draw students from across the country and abroad into shared corridor housing
- Ísafjörður and the Westfjords, Iceland [SECONDARY] // a town where winter roads long held back development; tunnels retired its avalanche passes, a fifth of the region's residents are foreign citizens, and residents meet for coffee when the sun returns to one street
- Baracoa, Cuba [SECONDARY] // the country's main cacao region, reached only by sea or a long unpaved track until a mountain road opened in 1965; that one road now carries 96% of travelers and 83% of goods
- Juneau, Alaska [SECONDARY] // a state capital of about 32,000 with no road out, walled by a 3,900 km2 icefield; freight arrives by barge twice a week, and a glacier-dammed basin has flooded the town every year since 2011
- Cordova, Alaska [SECONDARY] // a salmon town with no road out, where half of households fish or process; the harvest leaves by jet, and when the state cut winter ferry service in 2019, shipping by barge cost about four times as much
- Ilulissat, Nuuk and Tasiilaq, Greenland [SECONDARY] // no road links any town to another across the ice sheet; one company holds the freight monopoly, and Tasiilaq's ships come only from about June to early November, with winter food flown in
- Big Sur, California [SECONDARY] // when a 2017 bridge failure cut the coast's only through road, groceries came by helicopter for five to six weeks, then over a half-mile footpath that workers commuted on and children walked to reach the school bus
- Whittier, Alaska [SECONDARY] // the only land link is a one-lane tunnel shared by cars and trains, run as alternating half-hourly convoys and closed overnight, so the town is sealed every night by timetable
- Lüderitz, Namibia [SECONDARY] // fishing is the main employer, but firms granted quotas have moved processing to a larger port because of cost and remoteness; a planned green-hydrogen project and worker townships are expected to change the town

- Caleta Tortel, Chile [SUPPORTING] // a settlement with no road until 2003, built on kilometers of cypress walkways instead of streets; its first venture ended in 1906 when 120 imported timber workers died, probably of hunger and scurvy
```

## 3 · Corrections the research made to the first-pass reasoning

- **Dutch polders:** settlers were chosen from within one nation and selected state-side, so "settled by people from everywhere" is weaker than stated; the polder is real heavy clay, so it does not parallel soilless growing.
- **Longyearbyen:** it has two ways in (air and sea), not one.
- **Matanuska:** settlers were deliberately drawn from three similar-climate states, so it is selection for sameness; it mostly failed as a farm colony.
- **Arava:** the "one gateway outward" claim is unsourced; the export route was not found.
- **Kibbutz and moshav:** shared dining and work rotation are largely historical; the Arava farm labor is mostly bound Thai migrants.
- **Wageningen and Rehovot:** the national agricultural research organization is not in Rehovot (it left in the 1950s).
- **Skagway and Haines:** "single door to an interior" was true only in the gold-rush era.
- **Seyðisfjörður:** it has a second door by sea.
- **Ísafjörður:** no longer sealed; it has tunnels, an airport and a cruise port.
- **Baracoa:** "many-origin settlement" is not supported; the real parallel is a farming region behind one mountain road.
- **Caleta Tortel:** "growing without soil" is not borne out; the walkways are for getting around, not growing. It also has had a road since 2003.
- **Flåm, Aurland and Geiranger:** Geiranger is not tunneled.
- **Cinque Terre:** made ground that is mostly failing (95% of terraces abandoned), and the produce is wine.
- **Lüderitz:** the "distinct built culture" is a sensitive German colonial inheritance.
- **Care notes:** the first-pass table said "none" for Cinque Terre and Amalfi; the logs record heavy tourism and resident loss.
