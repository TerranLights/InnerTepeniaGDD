# Mawson: candidate inspiration picks (draft from knowledge, unverified)

Picks are judged on structural and thematic parallel alone. A candidate is neither included nor excluded because of the nation it sits in, the nation of the real-world station, or the nation of any founder or exile group. Founders come only from the Founding Register, and nothing here touches them.

## 3. Gaps and doubts

**Thin facets**
- **F14 (robot majority).** Nothing real has a non-human majority. Singapore, Dubai and Harbin cover only "most people come from elsewhere" or "no single origin defines the city." The robot-majority side is left open on purpose.
- **F11 (a lapsed claim kept as ceremony).** Only three rows (City of London, Tynwald, Tangier), and none is quite a territorial claim kept up after its authority ended. Needs more looking.
- **F2 (communications node).** The cable stations match the infrastructure but not the role of being "the face of the subnet." F1 rows carry that part.
- **F15 (research).** Thin on purpose: the spec gives research one word, so Woods Hole and Hobart are enough until the spec says more.
- **F9 (learning from the record).** Two rows, both about building. A candidate where *institutional practice* (not architecture) was rebuilt from manuals would be stronger. None came to mind with confidence.
- **F10 (patron).** Explorer veneration has few real civic rituals. Most rows are patron-saint festivals that offer the form, not the content.

**Rows to check first (unsure they are as described)**
- #11: who planted the Nichinan coast for honeymooners, and how much a royal honeymoon actually started the boom.
- #7 and #43: ropes or chains on streets in Trieste and Punta Arenas; I recall both but cannot confirm either.
- #19: how big Ærø's international wedding service is.
- #34: the name of the Verona letter-answering volunteers and who funds them.
- #26: whether the "import the tourists" quote is real.
- #10: the honeymoon certificate at Niagara.
- #48: the name of the volunteer millers' association.
- #54: the story that Alexandria copied books from visiting ships may be legend.
- #73: the Tianyi Pavilion's access rules.
- #6: whether Wellington actually has wind-specific building rules (wind tunnel testing for towers is recalled).
- #61: the early handoff of Orcadas between operators.
- #68: whether the toast at the grave is a set custom or an occasional one.

**Other notes**
- **Polar comparables** (#13, #25, #61, #68, #78, #79, #80) are flagged in their care notes. Each is included for one structural feature only.
- **Left out on purpose:** the real-world sites of the namesake explorer's own expeditions. The patron facet is about the *form* of veneration, not his biography.
- **Couples barred at home** (#18, #19, #20): these match the human-robot honeymoon facet structurally. Their real political backstories should not carry over.
- The spec's founding notes say some passages predate the current founding and are due for review. Nothing here relies on founders or on nations.
