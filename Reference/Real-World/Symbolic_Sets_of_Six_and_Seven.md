# Real-World Symbolic Sets of Six and Seven

**What this is:** a reference catalog of real-world "amalgamations of metaphorical/archetypal symbols" that
come in sets of exactly six or seven members — the same category of thing as the Zodiac (12, used as
Concordia's own district-reference scheme), the Four Greek Elements, the Five Chinese Elements, or the 22
cards of the Tarot's Major Arcana. Compiled for possible future use as a symbolic/organizational framework
elsewhere in the project. Not yet applied to anything — pure reference material.

---

## Sets of Seven

### The Seven Classical Planets

**Members, in Ptolemaic order outward from Earth:** Moon, Mercury, Venus, Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn.

These are the seven "wandering stars" (Greek *planetes asteres*) visible to the naked eye and distinguishable
from the fixed stars by their motion — the only bodies in the solar system known to pre-telescopic
civilizations. This set is the deep root of an enormous amount of downstream Western symbolism:

- **The seven-day week** is built directly on this set. Each day was assigned a ruling planet in Hellenistic
  astrology (an hour-by-hour rotation through all seven determined which planet "ruled" each day's first
  hour). The Romance-language day names are transparent: *lundi/lunes* (Moon), *mardi/martes* (Mars),
  *mercredi/miércoles* (Mercury), *jeudi/jueves* (Jupiter), *vendredi/viernes* (Venus). English swaps four of
  the Roman gods for their closest Norse/Germanic equivalents (Tiw for Mars → Tuesday, Woden for Mercury →
  Wednesday, Thor for Jupiter → Thursday, Frigg for Venus → Friday) but keeps Sunday, Monday, and Saturday
  transparent.
- **Domains/associations**, as developed across astrology and alchemy:
  - **Sun** — vitality, identity, the self, will to live, illumination.
  - **Moon** — emotion, intuition, memory, the home, the unconscious, cyclical change.
  - **Mercury** — communication, intellect, travel, commerce, quickness, duality.
  - **Venus** — love, beauty, harmony, attraction, pleasure, art.
  - **Mars** — action, conflict, aggression, drive, courage, war.
  - **Jupiter** — expansion, luck, abundance, wisdom, growth, higher law.
  - **Saturn** — restriction, discipline, time, mortality, structure, consequence.
- **The "Chaldean order"** (by apparent orbital speed, slowest to fastest, used for the day-ruler rotation
  above): Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon.
- Each planet also anchors one of the **Seven Metals of Alchemy** (see below) and, later, one of the seven
  days' own associated color, mood, and virtue in various esoteric systems.

This set is unusual among the ones below in that it has a genuine **built-in ordering principle** (orbital
distance/speed) rather than being a flat, unordered list — the same structural quality the Tarot's Major
Arcana has (Fool through World as a life-journey sequence) and the Zodiac has (a wheel with a starting point,
Aries, and a closing point, Pisces).

### The Seven Deadly Sins (and the Seven Heavenly Virtues)

**Sins:** Pride (*Superbia*), Greed (*Avaritia*), Lust (*Luxuria*), Envy (*Invidia*), Gluttony (*Gula*), Wrath
(*Ira*), Sloth (*Acedia*).

**Counterpart virtues:** Humility, Charity, Chastity, Kindness, Temperance, Patience, Diligence.

Codified in their familiar form by Pope Gregory I in the late 6th century, building on an earlier list of
eight "evil thoughts" from the Christian monastic tradition (Evagrius Ponticus, 4th century) that Gregory
condensed to seven, merging *vainglory* into *pride* and adding *envy*. The set became one of the most
generative structures in medieval art and literature — Dante's *Purgatorio* organizes the seven terraces of
the mountain of Purgatory directly around these seven, from Pride (heaviest, closest to Hell) to Lust
(lightest, closest to Heaven), each terrace pairing a punishment that inverts the sin with an exemplar of its
opposing virtue. Each sin is also traditionally paired with one of the seven classical planets/days and a
particular demon in later grimoire traditions, though that mapping is less standardized than the sin-virtue
pairing itself.

### The Seven Chakras

**Members, bottom to top:** Root (*Muladhara*), Sacral (*Svadhisthana*), Solar Plexus (*Manipura*), Heart
(*Anahata*), Throat (*Vishuddha*), Third Eye (*Ajna*), Crown (*Sahasrara*).

A core structure of Hindu and later yogic/tantric tradition, describing seven energy centers arranged along
the spine from the base to the top of the skull, each governing a domain of physical, emotional, and
spiritual life:

- **Root (base of spine)** — survival, grounding, security, the physical body's basic needs.
- **Sacral (lower abdomen)** — creativity, sexuality, pleasure, emotional flow.
- **Solar Plexus (upper abdomen)** — willpower, confidence, personal power, identity.
- **Heart (center of chest)** — love, compassion, connection to others, the midpoint of the system (three
  centers below concerned with the self and material world, three above concerned with expression and
  transcendence).
- **Throat** — communication, self-expression, speaking one's truth.
- **Third Eye (center of forehead)** — intuition, insight, inner vision, seeing beyond the literal.
- **Crown (top of head)** — spiritual connection, unity consciousness, enlightenment.

Popular Western depictions assign each chakra a color following rainbow order (red at the root climbing to
violet or white at the crown), directly paralleling the seven-color rainbow below — the chakra system and
the rainbow are two of the more common places the number seven and a color spectrum get explicitly fused in
modern usage, even though the two traditions developed independently.

### The Seven Colors of the Rainbow ("Roy G. Biv")

**Members:** Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet.

Isaac Newton, in his 1672 work on optics, deliberately divided the continuous visible spectrum into seven
named bands — not because human color perception naturally breaks that way, but because Newton wanted the
number of colors to match the number of notes in a musical scale and the number of known classical planets,
reflecting his own era's broader belief in numerological correspondence across different domains of nature.
**Indigo** in particular is the weakest, most debated member of the set — many modern observers struggle to
distinguish it as a distinct band from blue and violet, and it's widely understood today as included mainly
to preserve the count of seven rather than because it's a naturally obvious, separate color the way red or
blue are.

### The Seven Liberal Arts

**Trivium (language arts):** Grammar, Logic (Dialectic), Rhetoric.
**Quadrivium (mathematical arts):** Arithmetic, Geometry, Music, Astronomy.

The core curriculum of medieval European education, understood as the necessary foundation before a student
could move on to philosophy or theology. The two halves have a clean internal logic:

- The **Trivium** (literally "the three roads/ways") trains mastery of language and thought itself: Grammar
  (correct structure), Logic (correct reasoning), Rhetoric (persuasive, effective expression) — the tools
  needed to think and communicate at all.
- The **Quadrivium** (literally "the four roads/ways") trains mastery of number and pattern across the four
  ways number can manifest: Arithmetic (pure number), Geometry (number in space), Music (number in time, as
  ratio and harmony), Astronomy (number in space *and* time, the motion of the heavens) — echoing the
  Pythagorean idea that music and the movement of celestial bodies were both, at root, expressions of the
  same underlying numerical order (the "music of the spheres").

Together, the seven arts were considered the complete foundation of a "liberal" (i.e., free-person-worthy,
as opposed to purely vocational) education.

### The Seven Metals of Alchemy

**Members and their planetary rulers:** Gold (Sun), Silver (Moon), Copper (Venus), Iron (Mars), Tin
(Jupiter), Mercury/Quicksilver (Mercury), Lead (Saturn).

Each of the seven metals known to antiquity and the medieval world was believed to correspond to, and be
astrologically "ruled" by, one of the seven classical planets above — the same set generating a second,
material-world expression of itself. Alchemical transmutation (most famously, turning lead into gold) was
understood on at least two levels simultaneously: a literal claim about transforming matter, and a symbolic
or spiritual claim about the soul's own ascent from a base, heavy, mortal condition (Saturn/lead — slow,
dark, associated with limitation and death) toward a perfected, incorruptible, radiant one (Sun/gold —
associated with life, illumination, and the divine). The full planetary-metal correspondence gave alchemists
a working vocabulary in which every metal, and by extension every stage of the Great Work, carried a
readable astrological and spiritual meaning.

### The Seven Hermetic Principles

**Members, as stated in *The Kybalion* (1908):** Mentalism, Correspondence, Vibration, Polarity, Rhythm,
Cause and Effect, Gender.

A modern (early 20th-century) esoteric system presented by its anonymous authors ("Three Initiates") as an
ancient teaching handed down from the legendary Hermes Trismegistus, though most scholars consider it a
genuinely modern synthesis dressed in ancient framing. Each principle claims to describe a fundamental law
governing all of reality:

- **Mentalism** — "The All is Mind; the universe is mental." Reality is fundamentally a mental/spiritual
  phenomenon, not purely material.
- **Correspondence** — "As above, so below; as below, so above." Patterns repeat across every scale and
  plane of existence.
- **Vibration** — "Nothing rests; everything moves; everything vibrates." All matter and energy exist in a
  state of constant motion, differing only in the rate of vibration.
- **Polarity** — "Everything is dual; everything has poles; everything has its pair of opposites." Apparent
  opposites are actually two extremes of the same underlying quality (hot/cold as degrees of temperature,
  love/hate as degrees of the same emotional spectrum).
- **Rhythm** — "Everything flows, out and in; everything has its tides; all things rise and fall." A
  pendulum-swing quality underlies all process and change.
- **Cause and Effect** — "Every cause has its effect; every effect has its cause." Nothing happens by pure
  chance; everything follows from a prior cause according to law.
- **Gender** — "Gender is in everything; everything has its masculine and feminine principles." Every
  creative or generative act requires the interplay of an active/projective principle and a
  receptive/formative one, not necessarily tied to biological sex.

## Sets of Six

### The Six Realms of Existence (Buddhist Cosmology)

**Members, as depicted on the Bhavacakra (Wheel of Life):** the Deva realm (gods/heavenly beings), the Asura
realm (demigods/titans), the Human realm, the Animal realm, the Preta realm (hungry ghosts), and the Naraka
realm (hell).

A cosmological map of the possible states a being can be reborn into within samsara (the cycle of birth,
death, and rebirth), each realm defined by a dominant emotional/psychological affliction as much as by a
literal cosmological location:

- **Deva (gods)** — a realm of tremendous pleasure, longevity, and power, but ultimately still trapped in
  samsara; its danger is that comfort and pride make liberation-seeking effort feel unnecessary until the
  realm's own long but finite bliss runs out.
- **Asura (demigods/titans)** — a realm of ambition, jealousy, and constant conflict, often depicted at war
  with the Devas over a shared wish-fulfilling tree whose fruit grows in the Asura realm but whose branches
  bear fruit in the Deva realm above them.
- **Human** — a realm of mixed pleasure and suffering, considered uniquely valuable among all six precisely
  because that balance gives humans both the motivation (enough suffering to seek an end to it) and the
  capacity (enough stability and awareness to actually pursue liberation) that no other realm combines; only
  from the human realm is enlightenment traditionally considered directly achievable.
- **Animal** — a realm dominated by ignorance, instinct, and servitude to more powerful beings, lacking the
  self-awareness needed to pursue liberation.
- **Preta (hungry ghosts)** — a realm of insatiable craving, traditionally depicted as beings with vast,
  empty stomachs and tiny, needle-thin necks or mouths, physically unable to ever satisfy the hunger that
  defines them — a direct visual metaphor for compulsive, unsatisfiable desire.
- **Naraka (hell)** — a realm of intense suffering and aggression, the result of accumulated harmful
  karma, though (as in every realm) still ultimately temporary rather than eternal damnation.

A being's realm is determined by karma rather than assigned permanently, and movement between all six realms
continues indefinitely until liberation is achieved — making this set, unlike the others in this document, an
explicitly cyclical, non-hierarchical wheel rather than a ladder or fixed ranking.

### The Six Perfections (Paramitas)

**Members, in cultivated order:** Generosity (*Dana*), Discipline/Ethical Conduct (*Sila*),
Patience/Forbearance (*Kshanti*), Diligence/Energetic Effort (*Virya*), Meditative Concentration (*Dhyana*),
Wisdom (*Prajna*).

A progressive path of qualities cultivated by a bodhisattva (a being dedicated to achieving enlightenment for
the benefit of all beings) in Mahayana Buddhism, each perfection building on the ones before it:

- **Generosity** gives freely of material goods, protection, and teaching, loosening attachment to
  possessions and self.
- **Discipline** channels that generosity into consistent, ethical action rather than scattered good
  intentions.
- **Patience** sustains that disciplined effort through hardship, insult, and difficulty without collapsing
  into anger or despair.
- **Diligence** supplies the sustained energy needed to keep practicing across a lifetime (or many
  lifetimes) rather than losing momentum.
- **Meditative concentration** focuses that sustained effort into genuine, stable insight rather than
  scattered activity.
- **Wisdom** — the culmination — is the direct, experiential understanding of the true nature of reality
  (emptiness, interdependence) that the first five perfections make possible, and without which the other
  five remain well-intentioned but ultimately incomplete.

### The Six Cardinal Directions

**Members:** North, South, East, West, Up (Zenith), Down (Nadir).

A complete spatial framework extending the familiar four compass directions with the two vertical poles,
used across a range of cosmological and ritual systems (various Native American traditions, Chinese
geomancy/feng shui, and others) to describe total orientation in three-dimensional space rather than just the
horizontal plane. Each direction is frequently assigned its own color, guardian animal or spirit, season, or
element depending on the specific tradition, giving the six-direction framework a similar symbolic
"filled-in" quality to the sets above rather than functioning as pure, neutral geometry.

## Worth Your Attention

The Seven Classical Planets and the Six Realms of Existence stand out as the two strongest candidates for any
future symbolic framework, because both have genuine internal structure rather than being a flat, arbitrary
list: the planets have a real ordering principle (orbital distance/speed) the way the Zodiac has a wheel and
the Tarot's Major Arcana has a journey, while the Six Realms form an explicitly cyclical, weighted system (one
realm — Human — is structurally privileged over the other five) rather than a simple checklist. The Seven
Deadly Sins/Virtues pairing is the strongest candidate specifically if a paired, oppositional structure
(vice/virtue, shadow/light) is wanted rather than a single unified set.
