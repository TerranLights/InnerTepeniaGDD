## 1. Rules followed, and one place where the brief and the site disagree

- **User-Agent on every request to the three archives and Amplify:** `InnerTepeniaResearch/1.0 (personal literature research; low-rate)`. No browser spoofing, no logins, no CAPTCHA or challenge pages encountered.
- **Per-host spacing actually used:** StoryCorps 2.5 s (3 requests total); Witness to War 2.6 s minimum (one process at a time; a few server responses took 6 to 8 s on their own); AWM `www.awm.gov.au` 10.5 s at first, **30 s after the 429 episode**; AWM media store (`s3-ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/awm-media/`) 10.5 s; Amplify 3 s. Never two processes on one host at once.
- **robots.txt read first for every host.** StoryCorps: allows everything except `/wp-admin/`. Witness to War: `/robots.txt` returns 404 (no rules). AWM: `Crawl-delay: 10` for all agents, and `Disallow: /advanced-search*` ("Deep searching") and `/search/`. Amplify: no restrictions.
- **StoryCorps Terms of Use conflict (the one real stop).** https://archive.storycorps.org/terms-of-use/ (read 2026-10-05) lists among prohibited uses: "use or launch any robot, spider, crawlers or other automatic device, process, software or queries that intercepts, 'mines,' scrapes or otherwise access the Services to monitor, extract, copy or collect information from or through the Services, or any manual process to do the same", and licenses use "for your personal and non-commercial use only". robots.txt would allow the crawl; the contract does not. The brief says "do not circumvent anything" and to stop on a block and record it; a contractual prohibition on exactly this activity was treated as a block. **Nothing was searched or fetched on StoryCorps beyond `robots.txt`, the home page (to read the search form) and the terms page.** Decision for the developer: accept the terms risk, ask StoryCorps in writing (https://archive.storycorps.org/contact-us/), or read by hand (section 5, Tier C).
- **Tool use outside the archives' own servers:** the harness `WebSearch` tool (an outside search engine) was used to discover AWM transcript PDFs and, for StoryCorps, to produce *leads only*. StoryCorps lead pages were not opened.

## 3. Witness to War (https://www.witnesstowar.org/)

### 3.1 What the site exposes
- **Interviews: video only.** About 10,810 videos (WWII 5,318; Korea 538; Vietnam 3,424; Iraq 558; Afghanistan 310; other 661, per the home page). Each clip page carries a title, unit, duration, a one-to-three sentence description, and a keyword list; **no transcript, no captions text, no summary beyond that.** Video was not downloaded or transcribed.
- **Docs section: written text.** 148 listed documents (Full Memoirs 60, Personal Accounts 57, Kilroy Was Here compiled stories 17, Diaries & Combat Logs 11); 145 were reachable in the paginated listing. Downloaded from `https://www.witnesstowar.org/download/<id>` (public). 61 have a text layer; 82 are scanned images (OCR'd locally with tesseract 5.3.4, `--psm 6`, 300 dpi; uncorrected). Photos: 49 albums (not touched).
- **No robots.txt, no terms page linked.** Footer: "Copyright (c) 2026 Witness To War. All Rights Reserved." Kilroy PDFs state that permission to use must come from the compiler via WtW. Everything is kept local and git-ignored.

### 3.2 How the search works
- **Quick search:** `POST /search_keyword` (field `keyword`) sets session state and 302-redirects to `GET /search_result`; the page shows tabs Videos / Photos / Docs / In-Process with counts. **A cookie session is required** (state is held server-side).
- **Paging:** `GET /change_limit/40` sets 10/20/30/40 per page; then `GET /search_result/videos/<offset>` (offset = item index, step 40) and `/search_result/docs/<offset>`.
- **Matching is substring/prefix on description plus keywords, not whole-word, and is American-spelled:** `humor` (69) also returns `humorous`; `humour` returns 0; `gag` returned 128 (substring noise), `trick` 110, `known as` 59, `morale` 49; the query `wit` matches almost every record ("with") and was aborted after at least 560 results; **multi-word queries behave as exact phrases** (`black humor` 0, `gallows humor` 0, `dark humor` 1, `practical joke` 1, `we laughed` 0).
- **Advanced search:** `GET /advanced_search` is a form; each click calls `POST /advanced_search_process` (fields `keyword`, `cat` = `videos-p|docs-p|photos-p`, `war_id`, `theater_id`, `branch_id`, `perspective_id[]`, `experience_id[]`, `valor_id[]`, `battle_id[]`, `unit_id[]`, `type_id[]`, `limit_search`) and returns JSON counts; the result list is then `GET /search_result`. **The "Experience" facet has a curated tag `Humorous Events` (`experience_id[]=12`): 662 clips.** `experience_id=21` is `Returning Home` (872 clips). Other experience ids: 17 Daily Life in War, 25 Training, 13 Ambush/Booby Traps, 10 Civilian Interaction, 8 Only in War.
- **Clip detail:** `POST /details` with `id` and `type=video` returns title, unit, branch, keyword list and the share link `https://www.witnesstowar.org/combat_stories/<WWII|Korea|Vietnam|Other>/<id>` (Cold War, Gulf War, Iraq, Afghanistan and other conflicts all live under `Other`; verified on four ids). In result lists only the first clip of each interviewee shows the name; later clips of the same man show a blank name.

### 4.4 The 429 episode (rate limiting) and the response
At 20:29:59 PDT, after 11 requests to `www.awm.gov.au` in about 11 minutes (the last four, C88030 to C88033, at 11-second spacing, i.e. at the stated Crawl-delay), the host began returning `429 Too Many Requests` (empty-ish 448-byte body, `Retry-After: 0`, Varnish) for **every** URL, including `robots.txt`. Four 429s were recorded; the job was stopped at once. Probes at 20:34 and 20:39 (one request each) still returned 429; at 20:48 the host answered 200 again. From then on the site was fetched at **30-second spacing** and no further 429 occurred. Lesson for later runs: AWM did not tolerate even one request every 11 seconds when they came in a tight run (the stated Crawl-delay allows 6 per minute); use 30 s. The cause could also have been the same IP address being used by other jobs at that moment; this log cannot tell.

## 6. Dead ends and corrections, in order of occurrence
1. **Shared scratch directory.** The session's scratch folder was shared with other agents running at the same time (files such as `last_req.json`, `host_state.json`, and a `lastreq.json` with another host in it appeared from others; the shared request log later disappeared). Fix: moved all work into a private subfolder `tb_work/` before the bulk pulls. To my knowledge no other agent's file was edited by me; I did create (and once edit) a generic `fetch.py` in the shared root early on and left it alone afterward.
2. **Background-job shell mistakes:** two `pkill -f` calls killed my own shell; replaced with `kill $(pgrep -f 'name[.]py')`. A nested `sh -c` quoting error made one WtW batch fail silently; reran from a script file.
3. **AWM 429** (section 4.4).
4. **Wit-query overreach** (section 3.3): a substring query would have paged through most of the site's 10,810 records; killed after a few pages and replaced by capped runs (`WTW_CAP=900` skips paging when a query reports more than 900 hits).
5. **First-pass AWM sample read-through was too generous.** About half of the "funny" hits were wording like "funny thing, ..." (meaning strange), and "laugh" hits were mostly chuckles; both were screened out by reading context. Eight transcripts were dropped from the output folder for lack of a usable passage.
6. **Descriptor honesty.** Narrator descriptors use only what each file's own header says (rank, unit, conflict); where AWM headers did not state a unit, the unit was left out. For S02124, the file's header accession (S02125) disagrees with its URL (S02124); both recorded.
7. **Excreta and graphic content.** A Kilroy anecdote built on a defecating soldier was excluded (doc 36). AWM passages keep two items that the project's content boundaries would not allow in game design (a toilet prank in S00811; S00802 and S00810 contain graphic cruelty and, in S00810, a callous sexual remark about a killed civilian that the speaker himself calls "the attitude"). They are real testimony and are kept verbatim as research; the boundaries govern design output.
