# Dark Humor Research — Master Pattern Synthesis

*2026-10-06. Built from the five lens syntheses `S1`-`S5` (this folder), which were built from the 38 extraction files `E01`…`E25e` (about 2,260 numbered findings). **Research synthesis, NOT canon. No character voice, dialogue or sample joke is written here, for the defector or anyone else.** Citations in square brackets are extraction-finding IDs (e.g. `[E15c-40]`) or lens pattern IDs (e.g. `S3-P11`); every quotation in the lens files is a short phrase taken from an extraction file and must be checked at its source before it is used in a design document. Sassoon's *Memoirs of an Infantry Officer* (`E26a`, `E26b`, 179 findings) was extracted after the first syntheses were run and has been folded into `S4` as an addendum (patterns `S4-P45…P64`, 11 corrections, 10 more design candidates); §4 and §5 below reflect it.*

## 1. How to read this, and the one weighting caveat that matters

**Evidence tags** (used throughout): **A** = documented by three or more *different kinds* of source (e.g., scholarship + primary text + oral history or modern voices) · **B** = several independent items in one kind of source, or two kinds · **C** = single source or anecdote · **D** = contested (sources disagree).

**The corpus is large but narrow.** Seven WWI British and Anzac primary texts supply 1,202 of the 2,259 findings (53%); the Wipers Times alone supplies 423 (19%), across seven files, from **one divisional officers' newspaper** in which other ranks speak only through officer-written dialect. *Those findings are not independent observations: the whole run counts as one source.* Richards and Graves served in the same regiment, so officer-vs-other-ranks comparisons also cannot separate rank from regiment. The roughly 490 oral-history passages are **keyword-selected harvests** (counts are passages, not rates; interviewers often supplied the "gallows humor" label). The surveys are small, cross-sectional, self-report studies of different constructs, and **none measures gallows-humor content**. The corpus is strong on forms, devices, rules of license and register shifts in English-speaking armies (above all WWI British officers); weak on frequency, other armies, and enlisted, women's, minority, enemy-side and Afghanistan/Iraq voices. [S5-P1…; S5 §3A-3B]

## 2. The ten strongest findings (tags A or B)

1. **License to joke about death and injury is relational, not topical** (A). Three checks operate together: *who* (shared risk and presence; the injured own the first joke), *before whom* (in-group; rank present or absent; civilians), and *reciprocity* (the joker must also be able to take it). The same person can be fluent with one group and silent with another. Silence is the cheapest sanction; refusing to laugh costs standing, and the documented costs fall mainly on women and minority soldiers. [S3-P1…P10; E01-27, E01-31, E25c-4]
2. **Named own-side dead are outside the joke** (A, about 25 items in WWI text, oral history and modern writing). What gets joked about is the periphery: the ritual, the paperwork wording, the institution, staff, the odds, oneself. Exceptions are narrow and conditioned (an unflattering truth first and honor after, a retold last exchange, one comic's stated honor, an unpopular officer's death). Anonymous or enemy dead appear as form-bound punchlines in a minority of items (B). [S3-P11; S1-P25; S2-P37, P38]
3. **A limit shows as a register drop, not an announcement** (A). One short plain sentence, nothing added after it; a farewell; a stated refusal by an editor; silence; an expected expletive left out. Peers read a change in a person's humor as a distress signal (B; the claim is a proposal from civilian studies, not a validated practice, C). [S3-P23…P28; S2-P17, P18; E25b-53, E08-38, E15c-40]
4. **The commonest build is a plain official or civilian form laid over a hard fact** (A): advertisements, returns, honors lists, intelligence summaries, wire squibs, mock bureaucracy, with one or two flat words carrying the horror. It runs from the 1916-18 trench paper to modern blogs and webcomics. Understatement, euphemism and renaming, nicknames, song and verse parody, repartee and pranks each appear in at least three kinds of source. [S2-P1…P9]
5. **The strongest build rule is "the plain turn"** (A): the comic voice runs, then the death, grief or disclosure is placed in a flat or separate register and is *not explained*. *Open question:* it is documented in writing, while the oral sets describe funny-only retelling, so it may be a written form more than a speech practice. [S2-P17, P18; S2 §8]
6. **Functions: tension release, cohesion and boundary-making, morale, controlled complaint upward, and distancing are well supported (A)**, and the release is mostly located *after* the danger, not under fire; incidents are funny in the telling, afterward. Grief is documented mostly by absence. "Humor protects against PTSD" is **contested (D)**: five self-report studies measuring five different constructs, one in-service soldier sample, none measuring gallows content, and a split on mocking styles. [S1-P1…P17; S4-P5]
7. **The enemy is a minor target in soldiers' own unit-facing humor** (A, with qualifications); dehumanizing enemy humor is documented in letters home, songs and memes (B). **Targets with the strongest support:** the self, newcomers, seniors and staff teased by type or role rather than by name, the institution's language and forms, the home front and press, everyday misery. [S1-P18…P24]
8. **The documented failure mode is humor working too well** (A/B): standing in for debriefing where disclosure is penalized; cohesion that disciplines the objector (the "killjoy"); banter and hazing sharing one logic. Resistance humor frequently serves the organization, which also uses it publicly and polices it at the edges. [S1-P35, P36, P38; S3-P29…P36; S1 §6-7]
9. **After service the funny version circulates and the heavy material goes unsaid** (A); humor persists but narrows (to veterans, private channels, or a managed product for civilians); reflexes and vocabulary persist for years (A); the civilian-facing register is decoy stories, translation, sanitizing and concealment, often as tact toward family. Graves's 1919-29 chapters are the longest dated first-person record: humor survives as comedy of scenes and puns, the war intrudes on a schedule, and the book closes "even anecdotes fail." [S4-P14…P18, P25…P27; E15c-40, -50, -58, -92]
10. **Craft (B):** both registers in one beat; the funny version and the sad version chosen by audience; the quip tacked onto a kill is a documented failure (the one developer postmortem puts the cause in a niche joke for a mass audience, celebration after killing for pay, a real war setting, and an unreadable target), and trauma-as-backstory is lazy shorthand; advisors are documented fixing procedure, never humor; veterans split on depictions. Reliability is modest (novelists' statements, one developer admission, one remembered TV ending). [S5 §3D; E22a, E22b-19, E22b-1]

## 3. Contested (D): where the corpus disagrees

- **Whether veterans' dark humor alienates civilians:** a null result in one survey [E04-6, E04-7] against repeated narrative misfires ("rooms went quiet"); the evidence is mixed in kind, not just in direction. [S4-P31]
- **Whether dark or mocking humor styles are harmful:** one survey ties self-defeating humor to lower life satisfaction [E04-8], another finds the injurious styles nearly unrelated to symptoms [E04-29], a police sample ties cynicism and sarcasm to distress and never to protection [E04-15…22]. Different outcomes and populations; unresolved. [S1-P15; S4-P19]
- **Self-wounders and desertion:** admired in one regular's memoir, unspeakable elsewhere (rank, battalion or period may explain it). [S3-P17]
- **Whether staff are exempt from satire or its main target** (the Wipers editors' own rule vs. the Anzac and memoir material). [S3 contradictions]
- **Suicide jokes:** honor for one comic and a failed shield for another; do not make it a fixed trait. [S3-P16]
- **Whether joking fades with exposure; whether veteran-only company helps or entrenches isolation; the quip after a kill** (audience-relative); **fear named plainly vs. only in disguise** (trench paper vs. Anzac and oral). [S4-P6, P13; S2-P27, P40]

## 4. What the corpus cannot tell us (and the corrections the passes forced)

**About a principled defector (S4 §12 and its Sassoon addendum; S3-P18; S5):**
1. How a person who left a military on principle jokes about the reason for leaving: **still no source.** The corpus now holds **one fictionalized officer's protest from inside the Army and its handling** (Sassoon's *Memoirs of an Infantry Officer*, narrated by a persona; E26b-49…88): reasons stated flat in a written statement placed beside the jokes, jokes aimed at the self and the procedure, humor dropping out of the *document and the cause* rather than out of the person, a mentor and a friend using both seriousness and humor, an institution that answers the dissenter by **medicalizing** rather than hushing him (class- and rank-specific gentleness; do not generalize to other ranks). **Caveats:** the novel's dissenter never leaves the Army and the book ends at the hospital door; it is one narrator, retrospective by about thirteen years, linked to Graves's own account of the same events (E15c), so the two are **not independent** and they disagree in detail (the declaration differs by a word; who rigged the Medical Board differs). Strength ceiling C (D where the two accounts conflict). [S4 addendum A1-A7; S4-P45…P64]
2. Whether and how such a person jokes about the institution afterward: one prank told by an obituarist [E23-18]; the Russian deserters' jokes **stop where the subject becomes the command's treatment of its own men** [E23-1…E23-7: about a dozen lines from two men, translated, inside one journalist's feature, months after exit]; and, from the novel, humor aimed at command through a ladder of motives that **stops at the men who paid and at the cause** (so the "stops at the command's treatment of its men" rule is not general) [S4 addendum A6].
3. How former comrades speak of the leaver: one prediction (the regiment would read protest as "cold feet" or a lapse of form [E15c-26]), one sanction (a sent-home soldier treated as a deserter [E25d-9]), and in the novel a friend's prediction and a Colonel's argument but no scene of the unit itself [S4-P33].
4. Humor over years alone in exile (the best case covers months); whether a leaver keeps the old institution's humor or learns a new one (one performer's statement [E23-20]); whether jokes about defectors circulate (a lead's claim could not be confirmed; the source reports shunning, not jokes [E23-22]).
5. Whether humor eases, expresses or masks moral injury: **no source ties them** (Molendijk is not yet collected: Tier C C-01, C-09).
6. Anything longitudinal inside the **ten-to-fifteen-year window**: the evidence is a webcomic author's notes [E19-49, -51], one retired officer's essay [E21-41], a pub scene fourteen years after the war [E13b-155], and Graves at about a decade; nothing follows one person through it.
7. Women and minority leavers, non-Western armies, **and the humor of Boone**: no source discusses Boone's humor (all five Boone-related pages checked [E22b-12]); the fan reading is "restraint, venting only through violence," not a lighter side.

**Corrections the extraction passes made to earlier assumptions (S5 §3C):** the Buzzell blog feed is about 3 posts of connected prose out of 72 [E18-35]; the Terminal Lance sample is 73% serious topics against 12% of listed titles [E19-58]; "Old Bill" is named twice in the Bairnsfather text [E11b Gaps]; the Witness to War clips: 26 of 50 carry the "Humorous Events" tag; the AWM Ross-series interviewers never asked about humor; several index descriptions over-read their texts [E25c-14]; Jensen's thesis shows no humor fading in *Spec Ops*; the first copy of one source was the wrong document. **The content filter withheld about 700 register lines** (graphic death or wounds roughly a third, sexual and enemy-dead/atrocity about a quarter each, slurs and caricature about a fifth; the lens agent's own approximate tally), so **atrocity, slur, sexual and suicide humor are under-represented in the quoted record** (see `../../Content_Filter_Denials_Log.md`).

## 5. Consolidated design-relevant candidates

*Each is a candidate drawn from documented mechanism, not a specification, not canon. The strength tag is the pattern's. The character's origin military is undecided, and Tepenian culture is produced locally: use these as constraints on what is **realistic**, not as content.*

**License and no-go zones**
1. **License is three checks at once** (who shared the risk; who is in the room above and below; whether the joker can also take it). [A] S3-P1
2. **The named dead comrade is outside the joke;** any use is a marked exception with a cost. [A rule; C for each exception] S3-P11
3. **The safe target is the periphery:** the institution's wording, staff, the odds, rations, the self. [A] S1-P18-22
4. **A narrow category with no humor at all can coexist with a fluent comic voice elsewhere** (a specific site and aftermath; a particular dead comrade). [B] S3
5. **Silence is the first sanction;** a person who snapped or stopped laughing once can be remembered for it. [A] S3-P23
6. **A joke is unit- and occupation-specific** and can die in transit (with troops of another specialty, with civilians who need jargon translated, with a listener the teller is testing). [A] S3; S2-P42
7. **Women and minority soldiers pay documented costs** for refusing or answering back (the "killjoy" label, honorary acceptance, silence): a character can be the target, the one who laughed along, or the one who refused. [A; advocacy sources included] S3
8. **The right to laugh arrives later than the event;** a person whose position is not vindicated may not yet have access to a joke about it (an inference). [A; C for the inference] S3-P37
9. **Suicide jokes, self-wounding and desertion are contested:** do not make them fixed traits. [D] S3-P16, P17

**Forms**
10. **The form is built from the institution's own paperwork** (notices, returns, regulations, ads, contracts); a character's idiom can be drawn from the document types of whichever service they left, and the form, not a punchline, does the work. [A] S2-P1, P7
11. **A story can have a seam:** one sentence where the register drops, with nothing added after it, and which others in the scene recognize without being told. [A] S2-P17, P18; S3
12. **Understatement keeps its bill:** documented gallows understatement is followed by cost; the genre's lazy version forgets the death. [A] S2-P2
13. **A nickname is evidence of being known;** losing one by leaving can be a loss of being seen. [A] S2-P4
14. **Repartee is reciprocal and scored;** rank play is available to a former NCO or officer who knows the forms; dissent can ride a polite phrase everyone decodes. [A] S2-P8, P13, P14
15. **Lead-up versus quip** are different comic types; short exchanges may favor lists and procedure over punchlines; announcing one's own daring is a documented tell (C). [B] S2-P20, P24
16. **If the character was an officer, wit more plausibly runs through written, institutional and mess-ritual forms; if enlisted, through yarns, sayings, nicknames and deniable defiance.** Both keep the dead out of the joke. [B] S1 §7
17. **Do not generalize the WWI British officers' forms** (mock ads, bills, serials) **to any army:** they are one officers' mess in 1916-18. [A] S5

**Functions and the wound**
18. **Specify humor by function and by target separately;** a scene can show which job a joke is doing (release, belonging, complaint, test, signal) without stating it. [A] S1-P1-5
19. **The release is retrospective;** under danger the register is flat or absent and the funny version is told afterward. [A] S1; S4-P5
20. **A character with a chronic injury may hold the first license over jokes about it** and may police outsiders' jokes about the wounded. [B] S1-P26
21. **Humor can substitute for debriefing where disclosure costs:** a fluent, funny character who cannot say a plain sentence about themself is a documented type. [A] S1-P35
22. **Fear is named in the past tense, in disguise, or through a form;** casual admission of past fear is acceptable; contempt for present fear in another man is uneven; pranks on the frightened exist. [B/D] S3; S2-P27
23. **Cohesion has a price:** the objector becomes the killjoy; the group may remember a departed member for the one time they stopped laughing. [A] S1-P36
24. **Self-mockery is both a leader's currency and the "take it" half of reciprocity;** a character may have lost the taking half without noticing the group's cohesion rested on it. [A] S1
25. **Do not write humor as reliably protective against trauma, or as reliably a symptom;** benign, relationship-building humor goes with better outcomes more consistently than mocking styles; nothing measures gallows content. [D] S1-P15

**After service**
26. **A time-lag:** experiences become funny in the telling, long after; the character may know which laugh was relief and which was distance. [A] S4-P5
27. **Versions sorted by audience:** a funny, true, beside-the-point story for strangers; a plainer one for a few; the heavy one mostly unsaid. [A] S4-P14, P26; S5
28. **Persistent reflexes:** vocabulary, command voice, tactical reading of rooms, a title or nickname that outlives the unit; civilians notice, the character may not. [A] S4-P16
29. **Concealment as tact:** withholding humor to spare a partner or family reads to them as distance. [A] S4-P25
30. **Veteran-only company is relief from translating, not a cure.** [D] S4-P13
31. **Warning signs the character may not name:** disgust at one's own joke; laughter at the wrong moment; laughter that is a symptom; a companion who reads the register as it changes (an untested proposal). [B] S4-P20, P21; S3
32. **Humor as labor:** when unwell, the character invents rituals or hoaxes to cheer others, and calls it work. [C] S4-P8
33. **The mask is usually named by others** (spouse, relative, interviewer); veterans seldom call their own humor a cover. [B] S3
34. **Humor thins when the unit is gone:** group forms give way to solitary ones; shared jokes may stop being funny without the people; a small circle (a game, a meme thread, one comrade) can serve as a substitute audience. [B] S3; S4-P41, P43; S5

**If the character left on principle (thin; all C unless marked)**
35. **The best-documented contour is thin:** jokes about the enemy, the dead and the old side's eccentrics intact; humor stops where the subject is the command's treatment of its own men; reasons for leaving stated flat; the escape, if any, told as a caper; **a true statement said lightly is heard by civilians as a joke** (camouflage and isolation at once). [C] S4-P36
36. **Comrades classify leaving as cowardice or a lapse of form,** which the leaver may know while agreeing with the principle. [B] S4-P33
37. **Dissent in service was small, deniable and upward** (a countersign, a hidden mercy, a whispered comment), aimed at conditions, brass and paperwork, not the war's aim; a character who left on principle may have run out of deniable forms. [B for the source pattern; C for the inference] S4-P32; S3-P19
38. **Institutions read sardonic humor literally;** a character from such a setting may self-censor in writing. [B] S3
39. **An unexplained exit:** the one modern leaver in the corpus gives no reason; how a leaver talks about the exit has no documented model. [C; n/d] S4-P39

**Craft (writers)**
40. **Do not follow a kill with a quip** unless it has consequences; the model that works aims at the machine, not the dead. [B] S5
41. **Trauma is one trait among several, not the whole characterization;** history can leak through events and the player's build rather than a confession tree. [B/C] S5
42. **Unprompted material is darker than prompted material:** what a character will say depends on whether the question is about humor or about the hard thing. [B] S5
43. **Advisors check procedure and vocabulary, not what is funny;** use a veteran of the right kind to read tone, and expect veterans to split. [B] S5
44. **Boone-style reticence is a fan reading and Boone's humor is unknown;** do not cite the model for or against a lighter side. [C] S5

## 6. How to use this

- Treat §5 as **constraints on realism**, each with its strength and independence caveat; read the lens file for the underlying findings before relying on any item.
- **Everything is a candidate, not canon.** Nothing here is Tepenian: real-world material is mechanism, not specification; the origin military is undecided; the project's divergence operator (time, separation, local setting, hardship, goals, habits) applies.
- **Do not draft the defector's voice from this alone.** The checklist's guard stands: no voice until the thin areas in §4 are addressed by more source material or direct input. When a draft is written, it should be tested with real people (see the leads file's routes).
- **Check every quotation at its source** before it enters a design document (the extraction files quote through a pipeline that included a summarizing fetch tool for some web pages).
