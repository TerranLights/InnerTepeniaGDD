# Tier C Shopping List — sources only a human at a browser can fetch

**Drafted 2026-10-05** · **Where to put what you fetch:** `Tier_C_Dropbox/` (git-ignored; see its README for formats and tips) ·
**Tick the boxes** as you go · IDs `C-nn` are what I will match to filenames (`C-03_Dangermond_2022.pdf`). Priority: **P1** = highest
value for the defector's humor and for the whole research question · **P2** = good · **P3** = nice to have.

**Why these are Tier C.** Each was tried with an honest, rate-limited request and came back blocked (403, a bot challenge, an unreachable
server, a paywall, or lending-only). I do not work around blocks. Many of these are **free for you** — they are only blocked to *my*
tools — so the first section costs you a few clicks each.

> **How to read an entry:** *Why* = what it adds; *Get it* = exact URL or steps; *Save* = what to drop in the Dropbox.
> If something is paywalled for you too, skip it and note `C-nn: not available` in `Tier_C_Dropbox/NOTES.txt`. Nothing here is urgent.

## Section 1 — EASY WINS: open-access items that are only blocked to my tools (about 2 minutes each)

- [ ] **C-01 (P1) — Molendijk 2025, "Humour in dirty work with high-impact ambiguities and paradoxes: the looking glass of military jokes and laughter," *Public Anthropologist* 7(1).**
  *Why:* the single best account of what military humor *is* and where it turns rotten; written by a moral-injury scholar; CC-BY. The one top-priority paper I could not get.
  *Get it:* `https://doi.org/10.1163/25891715-bja10066` → "Download PDF" (open access). Fallback: `https://hdl.handle.net/2066/317974` (Radboud repository; it timed out for me).
  *Save:* the PDF.
- [ ] **C-02 (P1) — Dangermond et al. 2022, "If it stops, then I'll start worrying: humor as part of the fire service culture…," *Humor* 35(1):31-50.**
  *Why:* who/when/how often a joke works; the funny person going quiet as a distress signal; concealment from outsiders. CC-BY.
  *Get it:* `https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/humor-2021-0106/pdf` (or `https://doi.org/10.1515/humor-2021-0106`). *Save:* the PDF.
- [ ] **C-03 (P2) — Olah, Junkin, Ford & Pressler 2022, "Comedy Bootcamp: Stand-up comedy as humor training for military populations," *Humor* 35(4):587-616.**
  *Why:* the veteran comedy program evaluated with a control group; after-service humor as skill. CC-BY.
  *Get it:* `https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/humor-2022-0007/pdf`. *Save:* the PDF.
- [ ] **C-04 (P1) — Bjerke & Rones 2017, "The fine line between funny and offensive humour in a total institution," *Res Militaris* 7(2).**
  *Why:* joking relationships in an army unit; the line between bonding and wounding; Norwegian ethnography. Free from the research institute.
  *Get it:* `https://www.ffi.no/en/publications-archive/the-fine-line-between-funny-and-offensive-humour-in-a-total-institution-an-ethnographic-study-of-joking-relationships-among-army-soldiers` → the **"Download publication"** button near the top. *Save:* the PDF.
- [ ] **C-05 (P1) — Argento 2025, dissertation, *Lived Experiences of Reintegration for Afghanistan Veterans Who Were Army Combat Medics* (Univ. of Northern Colorado).**
  *Why:* combat medics' dark humor and its suppression after discharge (3 participants, rich quotes). Free.
  *Get it:* `https://digscholarship.unco.edu/dissertations/1175` → **Download**. *Save:* the PDF (169 pp.).
- [ ] **C-06 (P2) — Hristova 2008, "Negotiating Reality with Anekdoty: Soviet vs. Post-Soviet Humor Lore," *Russian Language Journal* 58(1).**
  *Get it:* `https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/rlj/vol58/iss1/10/` → **Download**. *Save:* the PDF.
- [ ] **C-07 (P1) — Madsen 2013, master's thesis, *Dead Babies, Bowel Disturbances, and Other Combat Humor in Afghanistan and Iraq* (Texas A&M).**
  *Why:* probably the richest collection of **actual joke content** from the Iraq/Afghanistan era, assembled from soldiers' narratives and interviews. Free.
  *Get it:* `https://oaktrust.library.tamu.edu/handle/1969.1/154910` or direct `http://oaktrust.library.tamu.edu/bitstream/1969.1/154910/1/MADSEN-THESIS-2013.pdf`. *Save:* the PDF.
- [ ] **C-08 (P2) — Potter 2023, honors thesis, "Laughing through the pain: An analysis of dark humor in trauma-and-crisis-centered occupations" (Portland State).**
  *Why:* a convenient cross-occupation survey; check its references. *Note:* the DOI in my earlier lead notes (`10.15760/honors.1335`) resolves to an **unrelated thesis**, so search by title at `https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/honorstheses/` instead. *Save:* the PDF.
- [ ] **C-09 (P2) — Molendijk 2018, "Toward an interdisciplinary conceptualization of moral injury…," *Social Science & Medicine* (DOI 10.1016/j.socscimed.2018.06.042).**
  *Why:* the same author's moral-injury frame, for connecting humor and moral injury. Free author version.
  *Get it:* `http://hdl.handle.net/2066/194973`. *Save:* the PDF.
- [ ] **C-10 (P3) — Rice & Liu 2016, *Work* 54(2):335-350** (humor predicts resilience in veterans; N = 191). *Get it:* `https://content.iospress.com/download/work/wor2301?id=work%2Fwor2301` (bronze open access). *Save:* the PDF.
- [ ] **C-11 (P3) — Wear et al. 2008, "Derogatory and cynical humour directed towards patients," *Medical Education*.** *Get it:* `https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/j.1365-2923.2008.03171.x`. *Save:* the PDF.
- [ ] **C-12 (P3) — Horn 2011, "'Stalag happy': South African prisoners of war during World War Two and their experience and use of humour," *South African Historical Journal* 63(4).**
  *Why:* POW humor; veterans also used humor when retelling. *Get it:* `https://scholar.sun.ac.za/items/e387c97d-2cb6-4f7d-b1a9-995eae11269b/full` → download the file if one is listed. *Save:* the PDF.
