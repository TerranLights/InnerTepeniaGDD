# Candidate Mechanism Variants — Multiple Options, Deliberately Not Converged

**Purpose of this file.** `06_Synthesis_Candidate_Mechanism.md` blends everything extracted in `01`-`05`
into a single maximalist candidate. That's useful as a "here's everything real science can offer" reference,
but it isn't the only usable story, and it isn't necessarily the *best* one — some of the pieces it blends
together are genuine alternatives to each other, not layers that have to stack. This file pulls those
alternatives apart into independently viable, fully-formed candidates, on the developer's own instruction:
document multiple possibilities rather than forcing convergence now, since **which one fits best may
depend on how the rest of Concordia's districts develop** — a decision better made later, with more
context, than locked in today. Same discipline as `06`: none of this is canon.

---

## The three axes these findings actually split along

Rather than N independent candidates, the underlying material really varies along three largely
independent choices. Understanding these axes matters more than any single worked example below — they're
what a future decision would actually be adjusting.

### Axis 1 — What triggers the collapse?

- **(a) Pure scale, no discrete trigger.** `01` section 7's proven result: a sufficiently large
  interdependent network collapses completely with *zero* external failures, purely from its own
  accumulated structural interdependency crossing a threshold. Nothing anyone did wrong, no single
  incident — the clinics' device network simply grew past the point where collapse became structurally
  guaranteed.
- **(b) A specific, discoverable seam.** `04` section 3's LogoFAIL precedent: real malicious/exploitable
  code has entered real systems through completely innocuous, cosmetic features (a boot-splash logo).
  Applied here: some ordinary, unrelated-sounding clinic feature (a calibration profile, a personalization
  setting) is the actual point where the cascade could break containment — a *specific* thing that, in
  principle, an in-world investigator could eventually trace, even if nobody could have anticipated it in
  advance (firmware-level compromise is a documented blind spot for ordinary detection tooling).
- **(c) A legitimate practice meeting a fragile moment.** `03` section 3a's reconsolidation finding: real
  memory science says recall itself is the single most vulnerable moment in a memory's existence. Applied
  here: the clinics' *actual advertised, semi-legitimate service* (assisted memory recall/sharing) is what
  put people's memories into that vulnerable window in the first place — the cascade didn't have to reach
  for memory specially, ordinary clinic operation already did.

### Axis 2 — What exactly breaks: content, or the label on it?

- **(i) Content itself gets shared.** `01`'s "mutual giant connected component" / "explosive
  synchronization" — nodes (minds) genuinely forced into a shared state, memories actually flowing between
  people. The more overtly psychedelic, boundary-dissolving reading.
- **(ii) Only the bookkeeping breaks.** `03` section 3's `owner`-field finding — memory is a separate,
  reassignable bookkeeping label, not an intrinsic property of the data. Applied here: nobody's memories
  actually move or blend at all; only the *label* saying whose they are gets corrupted. A person could
  have perfectly intact, unchanged memories and simply lose the ability to trust whether the "mine" tag on
  them is accurate — a quieter, more uncanny, more quietly horrifying version than literal merging.

### Axis 3 — One event, or many?

- **(I) A single uniform Flood.** Individual experience varies (transcendence vs. trauma, per `01`
  section 6's bimodal-outcome finding) but the underlying event is one thing that happened to Pisces as a
  whole.
- **(II) Many separate, simultaneous local collapses.** `02` section 4's rendezvous-clustering finding:
  convergence doesn't have to be universal — it can produce multiple separate clusters that never merge
  with each other. Applied here: "the Flood" is really a *mosaic* of many smaller floods, each pulling a
  handful of nearby connected people into their own private shared state, with real variation between
  clusters, not just between individuals within one event.

These are genuinely separable — any combination is coherent, and none is required by the others. `06`'s
existing synthesis effectively picks (a)+(i)+(I) as its primary throughline while mentioning the others as
secondary color; the worked candidates below make different combinations the *primary* story instead.

## Four worked candidates

### Candidate A — "The Growth Collapse" — axes (a) / (i) / (I)

**Pitch:** No villain, no discoverable point of failure, no single bad night. The clinics' device network
had been quietly, unremarkably growing more interdependent for years — more devices, more shared backend
services, more overlapping support relationships, nothing anyone would ever have flagged as dangerous.
There is a real, proven mathematical point at which that kind of growth alone guarantees eventual total
collapse, with nothing external required to trigger it. The Flood was, in this reading, *always* going to
happen once the network crossed that invisible threshold — a fact embedded in the topology from early on,
undetectable by anyone actually living through the years that led up to it.

**Register:** the bleakest and most deterministic of the four — cosmic-horror-of-pure-systems rather than
tragedy. Nobody to blame, nothing to have prevented, nothing to investigate after the fact except the
math itself.

**Uniquely enables:** a Pisces where the Flood reads as something closer to a natural law finally
expressing itself than an event — useful if the district's whole thematic register benefits from feeling
like an inevitability that arrived on schedule. Weakest fit for any gameplay built around player
investigation, since there is no discrete "cause" to uncover — only an accumulated condition.

### Candidate B — "The Cosmetic Backdoor" — axes (b) / (i) / (I)

**Pitch:** Somewhere in the clinics' improvised device network was one small, completely unremarkable
feature — nobody now agrees on which one, and in-world debate about it never really settled — that turned
out to be the seam the cascade broke through. Real precedent (LogoFAIL) shows this is exactly how serious
compromises actually happen: not through some obviously dangerous system, but through a cosmetic or
convenience feature nobody thought to scrutinize. The rest of the collapse (spatial q_c=0 fragility,
memory-sharing via genuine content-merging) proceeds as in `06`.

**Register:** tragic rather than deterministic — there *was* a point of failure, even if nobody could
reasonably have seen it coming (firmware-level compromise is a real, documented blind spot for detection
tooling). Leaves room for blame, guilt, and "if only" in a way Candidate A doesn't.

**Uniquely enables:** an actual investigation/quest hook — a player or NPC could plausibly go looking for
"what specifically broke," find partial answers, argue about attribution, even if the full truth stays
genuinely contested (satisfying `10b`'s own "no fully solved, undisputed public cause" constraint more
naturally than Candidate A, which has nothing to investigate at all).

### Candidate C — "The Reconsolidation Window" — axes (c) / (i) / (I)

**Pitch:** The clinics weren't purely passive victims of infrastructure — their actual advertised service
was something like assisted memory recall or consensual memory-sharing, a real extension of Pisces'
established dissolution/escape-tech character. That service, entirely as designed and intended, already
meant touching people's memories at their single most vulnerable moment (real neuroscience's
reconsolidation window). The Flood happened when the network's underlying structural fragility (spatial
q_c=0) turned an ordinary night of ordinary clinic business into a catastrophe — the clinics didn't do
anything they didn't already do every day; the ground underneath what they were already doing gave way.

**Register:** morally the most complicated of the four — the clinics carry real, if unintentional,
culpability (they were already doing something genuinely risky on purpose, for profit, before anything
went wrong), rather than being purely swept up in someone else's infrastructure failure. Best fit for a
version of Pisces where its economy is meant to carry real ethical weight, not just be an innocent bystander.

**Uniquely enables:** direct narrative continuity with Pisces' *pre-Flood* daily life and economy — the
Flood isn't a bolt from elsewhere, it's what the district's own ordinary business already was, one bad
night. Strongest option if the developer wants the clinics' present-day survivors/operators to carry
genuine, contested guilt.

### Candidate D — "The Mosaic" — axes (a) / (ii) / (II)

**Pitch:** There was no single Flood — there were dozens, maybe hundreds, of small simultaneous local
collapses the night the network's accumulated growth finally caught up with it (Candidate A's trigger),
each pulling a handful of physically/technically nearby connected people into their own private cluster.
Crucially, nothing about anyone's actual memories changed — only the bookkeeping saying whose memories
were whose broke, cluster by cluster, in a way that never fully repaired itself. Some clusters settled
into something stable (their members experiencing something like the "transcendence" reading); others
never stabilized and are, in some quiet sense, arguably *still happening*, low-grade, to this day.

**Register:** the strangest and most quietly uncanny of the four — no literal merging, just a permanently
scrambled sense of ownership over intact memories, scattered unevenly across many small groups rather than
one uniform district-wide event.

**Uniquely enables:** genuine doctrinal variety within the Syncretic Religion (different founding clusters
→ different sects, per `03` section 4's "shared pattern thinking" material on real orthodoxy/heresy
dynamics) without needing to invent why the religion has internal schisms — the mechanism itself produces
them. Also the best fit if the developer ever wants an NPC whose "Flood experience" is still, in some
sense, unresolved and ongoing, rather than a settled historical fact about their past.

## Why waiting on other districts is the right call

Several real dependencies on how the rest of Concordia develops:

- **If Gemini's Great Corruption ends up thematically connected to Pisces** (both being, at bottom,
  failures of *records/attribution* rather than content), **Candidate D** is the natural pairing — both
  districts' crises become variations on "the labels broke," not "the data broke."
- **If other districts' crises turn out to be personal/political** (a specific actor, a specific decision,
  as Gemini's targeted corruption already is), **a purely faultless Candidate A** may read as an odd tonal
  outlier — though that contrast could also be the point, if Pisces is meant to be the one district whose
  catastrophe was genuinely nobody's doing, standing apart from the rest of Concordia's more human-scaled
  crises.
- **If the design wants a Pisces-specific investigation questline**, **Candidate B** is structurally
  necessary — it's the only variant with an actual discoverable "seam," even a contested one.
- **If Pisces' clinics are meant to carry forward moral weight into the present** (guilt, reparations,
  ongoing distrust), **Candidate C** gives that a real mechanism rather than requiring it be asserted.

None of this needs deciding now. This file exists so the choice can be made later, once those other
answers exist, without having to re-derive the underlying science from scratch.
