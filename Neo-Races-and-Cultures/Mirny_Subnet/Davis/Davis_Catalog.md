## City-Type Parallels (Phase 1b-ii — functional/civic identity, not geography)

**City type, resolved 2026-07-16:** a combined ecological/limnological research hub AND Tepenia's
foremost sheltered-agriculture/greenhouse city — the "breadbasket of Tepenia." Both functions grow
directly out of the same underlying fact: Davis sits on the largest ice-free oasis in the country
(~400km², diverse lakes and fjord-like inlets), giving it more exploitable fresh water and usable land
than any other city in Tepenia. Real-world matches below draw on both functions per nation, since a
single city rarely embodies both in the real world.

- **China (Primary):** Ecological — Poyang Lake, China's largest freshwater lake and a major wetland
  research area. Agriculture — Shandong's greenhouse vegetable belt, the core of China's status as the
  world's largest greenhouse-vegetable producer; an excellent match on both fronts, especially fitting
  given China's own Primary-tier presence here.
- **Japan (Significant):** Ecological — Lake Biwa, Japan's largest lake and home to a major
  limnological research institute. Agriculture — Kōchi Prefecture's greenhouse horticulture industry.
- **Germany (Significant):** Ecological — Lake Constance (Bodensee) research. Agriculture — the Lower
  Rhine greenhouse-vegetable region, a modest but real match.
- **UK (Significant):** Ecological — the English Lake District's Freshwater Biological Association at
  Windermere, a strong match that also ties back to the Lake District terrain match already used in
  this city's own Real-World Parallel Locations section. Agriculture — the historic Lea Valley
  glasshouse district, once Britain's defining greenhouse-growing region.
- **South Korea (Significant):** Ecological — no strong domestic large-lake-research match; flagged as
  weak. Agriculture — Korea's Jeolla province greenhouse/smart-farm belt, a solid match.
- **Indonesia (Significant):** Ecological — Lake Toba, Indonesia's largest lake, a real research site.
  Agriculture — no strong domestic greenhouse-agriculture identity given the tropical climate; flagged
  as weak on this front specifically.
- **Australia (Significant):** Ecological — no strong domestic large-permanent-lake-research match;
  flagged as weak. Agriculture — the Bowen/Bundaberg horticulture belt, a modest match.

### China (Primary, 20.51%)

**Real-world parallel community/communities identified:** Poyang Lake (geography/ecological — China's
largest freshwater lake, a major wetland research area) and Shandong's greenhouse vegetable belt (civic
type/agriculture — the core of China's status as the world's largest greenhouse-vegetable producer).
Both strong, especially fitting given China's own Primary-tier presence here.

#### Surface Culture findings
- **Food:** fresh-produce-forward cuisine (greenhouse vegetables as a daily staple, not a luxury) plus
  freshwater-fish dishes echoing Poyang Lake's own real fishing tradition.
- **Flags:** *(deferred — civic heraldry is a later, dedicated pass, not invented here.)*
- **Festivals:** a harvest-and-migration festival, timed to both the greenhouse harvest cycle and (echoing
  Poyang Lake's own real seasonal migratory-bird arrivals, a major real tourism/cultural event at the
  actual lake) a seasonal wildlife-arrival observance.
- **Fashion:** practical field/greenhouse wear — waterproof waders for wetland fieldwork, breathable
  layered clothing suited to humid greenhouse conditions.
- **Holidays:** Tepenian Independence Day, plus a locally-observed harvest-cycle commemoration.
- **Music:** planting/harvest work-songs, echoing rural Chinese agricultural song tradition.
- **Performances:** seasonal harvest processions.
- **Dances:** present but modest, tied specifically to harvest-festival context rather than a
  standalone civic tradition.
- **Games:** patience-and-strategy games (Go/Weiqi-adjacent traditions), fitting the population's own
  observational temperament.
- **Arts & Crafts:** wetland ecological survey and scientific-illustration traditions (echoing Poyang
  Lake's own real research institutions), alongside greenhouse-horticulture craft skill (grafting,
  seed-selection expertise).
- **Literature:** field-journal and research-log traditions from wetland science, alongside farming
  almanacs.
- **Language:** agricultural and ecological technical vocabulary threading into everyday speech.

#### Deep Culture findings

**Communication styles and rules:**
- Facial expressions, gestures, eye contact: understated and patient — sustained eye contact during
  careful listening, not performative or exaggerated expression.
- Personal space, touching, body language: comfortable working in close physical proximity during
  shared field/greenhouse labor, more socially reserved outside work contexts.
- Conversational patterns in different social situations: a formal, technical register in work settings
  (species names, growing conditions, water quality) shifting to a warmer, informal register in family
  and social settings.
- Handling and displaying of emotion: reserved in public display; emotion more often expressed through
  action (extra care given to a struggling crop or species) than through words.
- Tone of voice: even and measured — a "greenhouse voice," calm and unhurried by default.

**Notions of:**
- Courtesy and manners: not disturbing others' focused observational work; the same patience extended
  to slow-growing processes gets extended to people.
- Friendship, leadership: leadership earned through demonstrated ecological/agricultural knowledge
  accumulated over real years of observation, not asserted quickly or claimed by title.
- Cleanliness, modesty, beauty: cultivated abundance itself is the aesthetic standard — a thriving
  greenhouse or a healthy wetland is prized over ornamentation for its own sake.

**Concepts of:**
- Self, time, past and future: self understood partly through one's own accumulated growing/
  observational knowledge; time experienced as cyclical (seasons) rather than strictly linear; the
  future measured in growing seasons ahead rather than fixed calendar dates.
- Fairness and justice: equitable distribution of Davis's own agricultural output to the rest of
  Tepenia treated as a genuine justice question, not merely a logistics detail.
- Roles related to age, sex, class, family: age-based authority tied directly to depth of observational
  experience — an elder who has watched forty growing seasons genuinely, verifiably knows more.

**Attitudes toward:**
- Elders, adolescents, dependents: elders treated as primary knowledge-holders, actively and routinely
  consulted; adolescents apprenticed into fieldwork early rather than kept apart from real labor.
- Rule expectations, work, authority: authority grounded in demonstrated expertise rather than
  position or title.
- Cooperation vs. competition: cooperative by structural necessity — both ecosystems and greenhouse
  operations are collective systems that don't reward individual heroics.
- Relationships with animals, age, sin, death: migratory wildlife (echoing Poyang Lake's real crane
  populations) treated with genuine reverence; death treated as an unremarkable part of natural cycles,
  in the same register as a harvest cycle simply ending.

**Approaches to:**
- Religion, courtship, marriage: follows the established Tepenian-wide baseline (see
  `project_sexuality_rules` memory); no strongly distinct local variant surfaced in this pass.
- Raising children, decision-making, problem solving: children raised through hands-on fieldwork
  apprenticeship; decisions made through patient observation and data-gathering rather than fast
  intuition — a bad growing season or disrupted ecosystem gets studied and adjusted for next cycle,
  not treated as an emergency demanding an immediate fix.
