## City Snapshot

- **Nations present, by tier:** Primary — China, USA. Significant — Japan, France, South Korea,
  Russia, Indonesia, Australia (founding wave — shortest Australia-to-Antarctica route of any Tepenian
  city). Notable — Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines, Malaysia, New Zealand. Exact per-nation share % in
  `Specs/Casey.md` and `Official_Population_Census.md`.
- **Geography:** Budd Coast, Wilkes Land, Vincennes Bay — sits almost exactly on the Antarctic Circle
  threshold (66°16'S), giving it minimal but real polar night/midnight sun. Wilkes Station ruins (a
  genuine pre-exile artifact) stand nearby.
- **Geological composition:** Sheltered coastal inlet, moderate katabatic influence funneling from the
  plateau — milder than the more extreme Janbogo subnet coastal cities.

## Real-World Parallel Locations (Phase 1b — location identification only, no cultural findings yet)

**Terrain profile:** sheltered coastal inlet with moderate katabatic influence — milder than the
extreme-wind cities elsewhere in the country, closer to a genuinely liveable sheltered-bay profile.

- **China (Primary):** Qingdao's sheltered bay coastline, Shandong — a real, moderate sheltered
  Chinese coastal city.
- **USA (Primary):** Sitka, Alaska — a genuine sheltered coastal inlet town with moderate
  mountain-influenced wind, a strong match.
- **Japan (Significant):** Wakasa Bay, Fukui — a real sheltered Japanese bay with moderate seasonal
  wind.
- **France (Significant):** Gulf of Morbihan, Brittany — a real sheltered French coastal bay.
- **South Korea (Significant):** Jinhae Bay — a real sheltered Korean bay with moderate
  mountain-funneled wind.
- **Russia (Significant):** Avacha Bay, Kamchatka (near Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky) — a genuine
  sheltered bay with nearby volcanic mountains producing real wind funneling, a strong match.
- **Indonesia (Significant):** No strong match — tropical Indonesia lacks a comparable moderate
  katabatic-bay profile; flagged as weak.
- **Australia (Significant):** Hobart / the Derwent estuary, Tasmania — a real sheltered
  hill-protected Australian bay; a fitting match given Casey's own direct founding wave from
  Australia.

## City-Type Parallels (Phase 1b-ii — functional/civic identity, not geography)

**City type:** highway junction/transit hub, PLUS the project's own second worked example for this
whole category: "Splinters," Tepenia's largest and most famous bar (Classic Rock/Jazz/Blues/Acoustic
Folk music scene), famous along the whole Dumont d'Urville Sea and Indian Ocean coastline.

- **China (Primary):** Chengdu's teahouse-and-live-music culture — a genuine, nationally-known Chinese
  leisure/entertainment-district match.
- **USA (Primary):** Nashville's Broadway strip / the Grand Ole Opry — a nationally (and
  internationally) famous live-music destination; an excellent, direct match.
- **Japan (Significant):** Osaka's Dōtonbori — a real, nationally famous Japanese entertainment
  district.
- **France (Significant):** Paris's Pigalle/Moulin Rouge district — a nationally (and
  internationally) famous French entertainment-leisure landmark.
- **South Korea (Significant):** Hongdae, Seoul — a real, nationally famous Korean nightlife/live-music
  district.
- **Russia (Significant):** Moscow's Tverskaya nightlife strip — a moderate domestic match, weaker
  than the others; flagged for Phase 2 review.
- **Indonesia (Significant):** No single comparably famous national leisure landmark — flagged as
  weak.
- **Australia (Significant):** Kings Cross, Sydney — historically Australia's most famous
  entertainment/nightlife district; an especially fitting match given Casey's own direct founding wave
  from Australia.
