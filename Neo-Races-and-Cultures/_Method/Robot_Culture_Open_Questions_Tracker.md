## Foundational — shape a lot of what comes after them

### [x] What "build" actually consists of, as its own concept distinct from Personality /// ESSENTIAL

**Corrected 2026-08-06 — Build and Personality are not the same thing and must not be treated as one
concept.** Personality (the Personality Module) is the separate psychological seed — a partially-specced,
partially-randomized conceptual-weighting that determines values-orientation tendencies (see
`Robot_Physiology_and_Cultural_Practices.md`). Build is the physical and circumstantial side: physical
construction (height, general build/"bulkiness," aesthetic design) and the circumstances of creation. A
partial lead already on hand: builds are predominantly driven by *local interests* — a local robot
community's own need or want, not any single robot's personal whim, and not human commissioning either
(though that hasn't vanished as a possibility); body type is statistically weighted per locality, not
fixed. This also means build is tied to locality, not independent of it the way Gen/Mark is — see the
cultural-boundary item, below. Still open: **who *specifically* initiates a given build within a local
community** (solo decision, collective decision, something else), and **what else, beyond physical
construction and creation circumstances, a build might involve** (any functional specialization, for
instance). This is the single most load-bearing open item — several other items below depend on it.

**Notes:**

The question of "who initiates build" can really depend on context. Exactly as you've stated, it could be:
- solo decision
- collective decision
- other possible options (which may arise from context)

There isn't really any one specific way that a robot can be requested to be built (which is part of, though not entirely, the reason why it's possible to see multiple different, and sometimes contradictory, physical builds within one setting)

### [x] The kinship analogue's actual shape /// ESSENTIAL

Build is confirmed as the stronger candidate axis than Gen/Mark for this — but "build is the right axis"
isn't yet an actual answer. A partial lead already on hand, **corrected 2026-08-06:** since builds happen
because a *local robot community* specifically wanted to bring this one into existence (not a neutral
commission process, and not an individual robot's personal whim), that's a plausible seed for an
emotionally-loaded maker/made bond — though a *communal* one, closer to "your community chose to bring you
into being" than a one-to-one parent-style bond. Still open: does something like a mentor/mentee bond, a
communal maker/made bond, or pure found-family end up filling the kinship role, in full? (Depends partly on
the item above — you can't fully answer "who built you and how" as a kinship basis until "build" itself has
more complete content.)

**Notes:**

There is absolutely a culture of "mentor/mentee" bonds, for sure. Maker/made isn't really much of a phenomenon, since the currently-alive-and-existing robots have comparatively little control over what kind of person a newly-synthesized robot is, and the newly-created robot was created by an infrastructure, rather than any sort of "creator", so the concept of "maker/made" doesn't really hold any weight in this world. Robots do have a sense of "clan" and "tribe", but this is more on the basis of shared experience of togetherness (for example, being fabricated in the same city environment, living through the same struggles, sharing the same end goals for how to live their lives, etc) than they would for any sort of "build specs". In this sense, robots are actually missing one of the fundamental human concepts of "tribalism"; where humans can form tribes/clans based on possible options of:
- genetics
- geography
- ideas/experiences
Robots only have geography and ideas/experiences to draw upon for a sense of tribe and clan, though they are able to experience something resembling a sense of kinship with other robots as well as with humans

### [x] The cultural-boundary question /// {{secondary}}

Does robot cultural identity track the existing 35-city structure one-to-one, or does build (or Gen/Mark,
at lower weight) deserve to be a genuinely separate, cross-cutting axis layered on top of city identity —
the way humans simultaneously hold national, regional, and generational identity at once? Flagged
repeatedly as the single biggest fork in the whole framework. **Sharpened and complicated, 2026-08-06:**
since build turns out to be locally driven rather than individually driven, a shared-build identity would
plausibly track *with* city/locality rather than cut across it — the opposite of what was originally
assumed. That leaves Gen/Mark as the axis that's actually structurally city-independent, even though it
carries less cultural weight than build. Whether a felt in-group identity can form around a lower-weight
fact like Gen/Mark, or whether robot cultural boundaries just track city lines after all, is the sharper
version of this question now. (Also depends on the "build" item above.)

**Notes:**

There would be much more weight given to which city/culture a robot is from, than there would to what Gen/Mark a robot is built to, as the shared experience would be an much, much stronger driver of culture, than physical build. A human comparison would be something like:
- a somewhat fat, though very strong, man who works as a garage mechanic
- a rather slender, tall man who wears a suit and works in an office
- a buxom, thick-thighed woman who works as a flagger directing traffic around a construction site
- a very skinny woman who works as a fast-food cashier
and all four of these people grew up in, and share struggles in, Guadalajara, Mexico. Their fundamental identity is infinitely more bound to their shared experience in the culture they grew up in, rather than what kind of bodies they have.

### [x] Does robot language keep human-style phoneme-range constraints? /// ESSENTIAL

Robots also have the Arcanet as a digital communication channel humans never had. Does that open the door
to a genuinely different linguistic structure, unbound by the ~10-70 phoneme range Brown ties to human
vocal-tract physiology — or do robot vernacular languages (per the locally-conditioned-language rule) stay
within human-style constraints regardless?

**Notes:**

The short answer is "no". The Arcanet is fundamentally just the local internet for Tepenia. Its name is a "shortening" of "Antarctican Internet", since Tepenia is essentially a robot country (populated by both robots and humans) in what is, for practical purposes, effectively "New Antarctica". In order to connect to the regular internet, that being, the one we know in Upper Earth (i.e., the rest of the world), there would need to be extensive underwater giant data cable networks connecting the Antarctican internet to the broader internet of the rest of the world, similarly to the way it's done to connect Australia to the rest of the world, and to connect North America and Europe to each other. Thus, it becomes fundamentally necessary for Tepenia (Antarctica) to have its own internet, which Tepenians populate with data pulled from both contemporary and pre-war datadrives from Upper Earth's internet infrastructure. Thus, the Antarctican Internet (i.e., the Arcanet) is something roughly, approximately more akin to our notion of the "TOR Network", although for different purposes; where the TOR Network is about privacy and secrecy, the Arcanet is about simply having a functional internet-connectivity infrastructure.

## Resolved (2026-08-06)

Both items below were resolved by deriving the answer from information already established elsewhere in

### [x] Does the "cognitive imperative" transfer to robots?

**No, not as an innate mechanism.** Only the Personality Module and, where applicable, the Language Module
are ever programmed into a robot — there's no room for a third built-in drive. Robot religion is a **New
Universal**, reached the same emergent way robot cooperation and morality are: through lived experience and
accumulated culture, not a hardwired explanatory drive.

### [x] Do robots have anything resembling disease?

**No — Non-Transfer.** Robots' established "things that go wrong" already fully account for the territory
disease would occupy: gradual component degradation (the aging-analogue) and corruption events like
Calethina's Split Brain (the mortality-analogue). No third slot is left over for something disease-shaped.
