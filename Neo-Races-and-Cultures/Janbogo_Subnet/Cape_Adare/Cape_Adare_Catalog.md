## City Snapshot

- **Nations present, by tier:** Primary — China, USA. Significant — Japan, UK, South Korea, Canada,
  Indonesia, Australia (founding wave). Notable — Philippines, Malaysia, New Zealand (earliest founding
  wave), Chile. Exact per-nation share % in `Specs/Cape_Adare.md` and `Official_Population_Census.md`.
- **Geography:** Headland at the northern tip of the Adare Peninsula, where Victoria Land meets the
  Ross Sea — the most accessible point on the Ross Sea coast from the open Southern Ocean, the first
  natural landfall for ships from the north.
- **Geological composition:** Low, rocky, exposed coastal headland — hosts one of the world's largest
  Adélie penguin rookeries; more maritime-exposed than the sheltered Terra Nova Bay cities to the south.

## Real-World Parallel Locations (Phase 1b — location identification only, no cultural findings yet)

**Terrain profile:** low, rocky, exposed maritime headland at the tip of a peninsula — the first
landfall point for ships, open-ocean-facing rather than sheltered.

- **China (Primary):** Chengshantou, Shandong Peninsula — China's own easternmost exposed rocky
  headland ("Land's End of China"), a real populated maritime cape.
- **USA (Primary):** Acadia / Down East Maine's rocky headlands — genuine exposed granite coastal
  capes with real fishing communities.
- **Japan (Significant):** Cape Erimo, Hokkaido — an exposed, wind-battered rocky headland with a real
  small population.
- **UK (Significant):** Land's End, Cornwall — the archetypal open-Atlantic-facing exposed headland.
- **South Korea (Significant):** Ganjeolgot / Homigot capes — real exposed rocky Korean headlands.
- **Canada (Significant):** Cape Spear, Newfoundland — easternmost point in North America, exposed
  rocky headland with a real nearby population.
- **Indonesia (Significant):** Uluwatu headland, Bali — exposed rocky coastal cliff terrain, though a
  much warmer climate register; flagged as a weaker match for Phase 2 review.
- **Australia (Significant):** Wilsons Promontory / Cape Otway, Victoria — genuinely storm-exposed
  rocky southern headlands, real populated coastal communities nearby.

## City-Type Parallels (Phase 1b-ii — functional/civic identity, not geography)

**City type:** historic/heritage site — Borchgrevink's Hut (1899), the oldest human structure on the
Antarctic continent, is Cape Adare's most historically important pre-exile feature; the city's
identity is built around heritage/first-landfall significance rather than a dominant industry.

- **China (Primary):** No single directly comparable "oldest structure of its kind" heritage site with
  the same national-landmark weight — flagged as a weak match.
- **USA (Primary):** Jamestown, Virginia — the first permanent English settlement site in America, a
  genuine "oldest human structure/settlement" national heritage landmark; a strong structural match.
- **Japan (Significant):** Yoshinogari — one of Japan's oldest and most significant archaeological
  settlement sites, a strong heritage match.
- **UK (Significant):** Skara Brae, Orkney — a genuinely ancient, nationally significant "oldest
  standing structure" heritage site.
- **South Korea (Significant):** No single directly comparable landmark — flagged as weak.
- **Canada (Significant):** L'Anse aux Meadows, Newfoundland — the oldest confirmed European
  structures in North America; an excellent, direct structural match.
- **Indonesia (Significant):** No directly comparable landmark — flagged as weak.
- **Australia (Significant):** No single comparable "oldest structure" landmark of this specific type
  — flagged as weak, though Australia's own Aboriginal heritage sites are vastly older in a different
  historical register not directly analogous here.
