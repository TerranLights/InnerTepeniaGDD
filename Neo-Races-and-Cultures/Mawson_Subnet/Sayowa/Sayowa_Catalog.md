- **Geography:** East Ongul Island, off the Prince Harald Coast in Lützow-Holm Bay — separated from the
  mainland by the bay, giving it a maritime/island character distinct from the mainland coastal cities.
- **Geological composition:** Small island terrain, sea-ice-covered in winter, icebreaker-accessible in
  summer — westernmost city in the Mawson subnet.

## Real-World Parallel Locations (Phase 1b — location identification only, no cultural findings yet)

**Terrain profile:** small offshore island, separated from the mainland by a bay, sea-ice-covered in
winter and only icebreaker-accessible in summer — matches should specifically favor islands with real
seasonal sea ice, not just any offshore island.

- **China (Primary):** Bohai Sea islands near the Liaodong Bay coast — one of the few places in China
  with genuine seasonal sea ice, a real (if narrow) match.
- **Japan (Significant):** Hokkaido's Okhotsk coast, e.g. Monbetsu's offshore drift ice — real
  seasonal sea ice with a real coastal population; an especially strong match given Japan's own
  founding-operator heritage at this city (the real Shōwa Station is Japanese).
- **Germany (Significant):** Weak match — Baltic islands such as Rügen see occasional winter ice, but
  nothing to the reliability of Sayowa's; flagged for Phase 2 review.
- **France (Significant):** No domestic seasonal-sea-ice islands — flagged as weakest, likely needs a
  diaspora-community approach.
- **UK (Significant):** No domestic seasonal-sea-ice islands — flagged as weakest.
- **South Korea (Significant):** Incheon Bay's outer islands see occasional winter ice — a modest,
  weaker match; flagged for Phase 2 review.
- **Indonesia (Significant):** No match — equatorial, no sea ice of any kind; flagged weakest, likely
  needs a diaspora-community approach.
- **Australia (Significant):** No match — flagged weakest, likely needs a diaspora-community approach.
