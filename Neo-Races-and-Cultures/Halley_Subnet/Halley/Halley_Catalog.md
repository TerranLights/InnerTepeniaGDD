## City Snapshot

- **Nations present, by tier:** Primary — USA. Significant — Germany, France, UK, Canada, Brazil.
  Notable — Poland, Netherlands, Argentina, Czech Republic, Hungary, South Africa, Slovakia, Chile,
  Croatia, Serbia, Slovenia. Exact per-nation share % in `Specs/Halley.md` and `Official_Population_Census.md`.
- **Geography:** Brunt Ice Shelf, Coats Land coast, Weddell Sea — a floating platform of ice, not
  bedrock, moving 400-700m/year toward the sea. The city itself has been rebuilt/relocated multiple
  times (ski-mounted modules) to keep pace with ice dynamics, inherited from the real Halley VI design.
- **Geological composition:** Frozen ocean/compressed snow/accumulated ice — a moving, non-fixed
  foundation, fundamentally different from the nunatak-bedrock cities in the same subnet (Abowasa,
  Sanay, Troll, Princess Elisabeth).

## Real-World Parallel Locations (Phase 1b — location identification only, no cultural findings yet)

**Terrain profile:** genuinely unique — no true real-world non-polar analog exists for "floating ice
shelf, ground itself in motion." The closest real-world parallels are communities living on *other
kinds* of unstable, shifting, or actively-engineered ground, where the population had to develop the
same fundamental relationship to impermanent terrain, even though the physical mechanism differs.

- **USA (Primary):** Louisiana Gulf Coast / Mississippi River Delta — real communities on actively
  subsiding, shifting land, with a documented history of relocating structures and infrastructure as
  the ground itself changes under them.
- **Germany (Significant):** North Sea Wadden Sea / polder land — centuries of engineered land
  reclamation on shifting tidal terrain, requiring constant infrastructure adaptation.
- **France (Significant):** Camargue (Rhône River delta) — shifting wetland/marsh terrain, real
  populated communities.
- **UK (Significant):** The Fens (eastern England) — historically reclaimed marshland, genuinely
  sinking/shifting ground requiring ongoing engineering to remain habitable.
- **Canada (Significant):** Canadian Arctic coastal communities — permafrost thaw zones, real,
  currently-documented ground instability affecting existing settlements.
- **Brazil (Significant):** Amazon River delta / Marajó Island — shifting alluvial terrain, though a
  very different climate register; weaker match, flagged for Phase 2 review.

**Design note carried forward:** this terrain type may be the one place in the whole project where the
real-world analog needs to be "communities that manage impermanent ground," not "communities on similar
rock/geology" — worth deciding explicitly before Phase 1c synthesis work reaches this city.

## Per-Nation Entries (Cultural Iceberg findings — Phase 1c, complete 2026-07-16)

**No National Stereotypes compliance note:** every finding below is framed as arising from Halley's own
conditions (a moving, impermanent physical foundation, and a civic purpose built entirely around being
a waypoint rather than a destination) that a given population's originating patterns fed into or
reinforced — never as "this population does X because they're from nation Y."

**A structural note before the per-nation entries:** Halley's City-Type category — "pure
highway-gateway town, no significant independent economy" — is narrow enough that only two of six
Primary/Significant nations (USA, Canada) landed a strong match on it; the other four (Germany, France,
UK, Brazil) all carry a weak-to-moderate geography match but a weak City-Type match. That's a citywide
pattern this time, not a Brazil-specific gap the way it partly read at Belgrano — worth remembering
before generalizing about any one nation's match quality across cities.

### USA (Primary, 23.53%)

**Real-world parallel community/communities identified:** Louisiana Gulf Coast / Mississippi River
Delta (geography — real communities on actively subsiding, shifting land) and Coldfoot, Alaska (civic
type — a real highway waypoint town with essentially no independent economy). Both strong matches.

#### Surface Culture findings
- **Food:** functional, easily-relocated food storage and preparation, echoing Delta communities'
  adaptation to recurring subsidence/flood cycles; roadside-hospitality food culture for travelers
  passing through, echoing Coldfoot's own waypoint identity.
- **Festivals:** a communal "relocation" observance tied to the literal, periodic towing of Halley's
  own ski-mounted modules to a new position — genuinely dramatic and meaningful rather than symbolic,
  since it's a real recurring civic event unique to this city's physical design.

#### Deep Culture findings

**Concepts of:** self and home defined by adaptability rather than a fixed structure — "home is what
you rebuild," not a permanent place, echoing Delta communities' own resilience culture directly.
**Concepts of time:** organized around relocation cycles and ice-movement rate rather than a daily or
shift-based rhythm, a genuinely distinct time-sense from every other Halley subnet city cataloged so
far.

**Attitudes toward:** travelers and strangers — strong hospitality norms for people passing through,
echoing Coldfoot's own real culture as a highway rest-stop community.

### Canada (Significant, 10.34%)

**Real-world parallel community/communities identified:** Canadian Arctic permafrost-thaw coastal
communities (geography — real, currently-documented ground instability) and Watson Lake, Yukon (civic
type — a real Alaska Highway waypoint town, home to the actual "Sign Post Forest," where generations of
travelers have left physical markers of having passed through).

#### Surface Culture findings
- **Arts & Crafts, Language:** a marker/sign-leaving tradition, echoing Watson Lake's real Sign Post
  Forest — travelers and residents alike leaving some physical trace at Halley to mark having passed
  through, a fitting practice for a city whose whole civic identity is transit rather than settlement.

#### Deep Culture findings

**Concepts of:** the waypoint itself as a kind of memory-keeper — the town may not be a fixed place,
but the record of who passed through it can be, converging closely with the USA population's own
adaptability-focused self-concept above while adding a specifically *commemorative* dimension.

### Brazil (Significant, 11.69%)

**Real-world parallel community/communities identified:** Amazon River delta / Marajó Island
(geography — shifting alluvial terrain, flagged weaker given the very different climate register) — no
strong City-Type match, consistent with the citywide pattern noted above rather than a Brazil-specific
gap this time.

#### Surface Culture findings
- **Food, Music:** general Brazilian-diaspora communal warmth persists as a baseline (same pattern
  found at Sanay and, more thinly, Belgrano), without a strong local City-Type anchor to give it
  Halley-specific texture.

#### Deep Culture findings

**Open flag:** this is the second city (after Belgrano) where Brazil's imprint reads thinner than its
population share would suggest, though for a different underlying reason each time (at Belgrano, all
three matching dimensions were weak; here, only the City-Type dimension is weak, shared with four other
nations). Worth tracking as a possible pattern across more cities before drawing a firm conclusion.

#### Surface Culture findings

#### Deep Culture findings

### France (Significant, 7.31%)

**Real-world parallel community/communities identified:** Camargue, Rhône River delta (geography — real
shifting wetland/marsh terrain, populated communities) — weak City-Type match.

#### Surface Culture findings
- **Food, Arts & Crafts:** Camargue pastoral/wetland tradition — a softer, lower-tech coexistence with
  shifting ground than the German/UK engineering register (wild-horse husbandry and traditional
  transhumance rather than heavy reclamation infrastructure).

#### Deep Culture findings

**Concepts of:** ground instability as something to be lived *with* rather than actively engineered
against — a genuine third register alongside the German/UK heavy-engineering approach and the
USA/Canadian adaptability-and-hospitality approach, giving Halley's population three distinct cultural
postures toward the same underlying physical fact.

## Synthesis Notes (Phase 2 — draft, not yet developer-confirmed as final)

**Working name: "Halleyan."** *(Placeholder, same status as the other three cities' working names.)*

Halley is unique among the cities cataloged so far in having its civic identity built around
impermanence on *two* independent axes at once: the physical ground itself moves (the ice shelf,
400-700m/year), and the city's own civic purpose is to be a place people pass through rather than a
destination (the Hwy 59 gateway role, no independent economy). Every population here has developed some
response to the first axis; only USA and Canada — its two strongest-matched populations — have a
developed response to the second, giving them outsized influence on Halley's actual lived culture
despite Brazil (11.69%) and UK (10.57%) each holding a larger or comparable population share.

Three distinct cultural postures toward ground instability emerge from the six populations: **heavy
engineering** (Germany's Wadden Sea polders and UK's Fens reclamation, genuinely convergent
traditions), **soft pastoral coexistence** (France's Camargue-derived living-with-instability
approach), and **adaptability-plus-hospitality** (USA and Canada, who also happen to be the two
populations with a genuine waypoint-town precedent). Brazil is again the least distinctly textured
population, though — importantly — for a citywide reason (the narrow "pure gateway town" City-Type
match) rather than a Brazil-specific one this time, unlike Belgrano.

Canada's Watson Lake "Sign Post Forest" precedent suggests a strong, concrete piece of Halley-specific
material culture: a tradition of travelers and residents leaving physical markers behind, turning a
city that can't stay in one place into a city that nonetheless keeps a permanent record of everyone who
passed through it. This feels like a genuinely usable, specific detail for future Course of Events or
level-design work, not just an abstract cultural finding.
