## Real-World Parallel Locations (Phase 1b — location identification only, no cultural findings yet)

**Terrain profile:** genuinely unique — no true real-world non-polar analog exists for "floating ice
shelf, ground itself in motion." The closest real-world parallels are communities living on *other
kinds* of unstable, shifting, or actively-engineered ground, where the population had to develop the
same fundamental relationship to impermanent terrain, even though the physical mechanism differs.

- **USA (Primary):** Louisiana Gulf Coast / Mississippi River Delta — real communities on actively
  subsiding, shifting land, with a documented history of relocating structures and infrastructure as
  the ground itself changes under them.
- **Germany (Significant):** North Sea Wadden Sea / polder land — centuries of engineered land
  reclamation on shifting tidal terrain, requiring constant infrastructure adaptation.
- **France (Significant):** Camargue (Rhône River delta) — shifting wetland/marsh terrain, real
  populated communities.
- **UK (Significant):** The Fens (eastern England) — historically reclaimed marshland, genuinely
  sinking/shifting ground requiring ongoing engineering to remain habitable.
- **Canada (Significant):** Canadian Arctic coastal communities — permafrost thaw zones, real,
  currently-documented ground instability affecting existing settlements.
- **Brazil (Significant):** Amazon River delta / Marajó Island — shifting alluvial terrain, though a
  very different climate register; weaker match, flagged for Phase 2 review.

**Design note carried forward:** this terrain type may be the one place in the whole project where the
real-world analog needs to be "communities that manage impermanent ground," not "communities on similar
rock/geology" — worth deciding explicitly before Phase 1c synthesis work reaches this city.

## Per-Nation Entries (Cultural Iceberg findings — Phase 1c, complete 2026-07-16)

**No National Stereotypes compliance note:** every finding below is framed as arising from Halley's own
conditions (a moving, impermanent physical foundation, and a civic purpose built entirely around being
a waypoint rather than a destination) that a given population's originating patterns fed into or
reinforced — never as "this population does X because they're from nation Y."

**A structural note before the per-nation entries:** Halley's City-Type category — "pure
highway-gateway town, no significant independent economy" — is narrow enough that only two of six
Primary/Significant nations (USA, Canada) landed a strong match on it; the other four (Germany, France,
UK, Brazil) all carry a weak-to-moderate geography match but a weak City-Type match. That's a citywide
pattern this time, not a Brazil-specific gap the way it partly read at Belgrano — worth remembering
before generalizing about any one nation's match quality across cities.

## Synthesis Notes (Phase 2 — draft, not yet developer-confirmed as final)

**Working name: "Halleyan."** *(Placeholder, same status as the other three cities' working names.
Note the city's own official second-interwar identity, per `City_Vision_Notes/Halley.md`, is **"Built
to Move"** — impermanence as routine collective civic business, not crisis.)*

**Philosophical core, folded in 2026-07-16:** Halley's own established faction, "The Methodologists,"
frames the city's whole outlook in one line: *"rigor is whatever survives being wrong about the ground
under your feet."* This is a genuinely precise crystallization of the Deep Culture findings below —
every population's own adaptability-and-engineering response to instability is, at bottom, this same
philosophy in practice. Worth treating as the city's actual guiding civic value, not just an incidental
faction slogan.

**Corrected 2026-07-16, per Vision Notes:** Halley's relocation isn't a periodic "moving day" event —
it's continuous, ongoing propulsion via digging tracks built into every module's platform, staying
ahead of the ice shelf's own drift. This sharpens the "impermanence as routine, not crisis" reading
throughout this file; Halley's populations don't periodically brace for a move, they live inside one
continuously.

Halley is unique among the cities cataloged so far in having its civic identity built around
impermanence on *two* independent axes at once: the physical ground itself moves (the ice shelf,
400-700m/year), and the city's own civic purpose is to be a place people pass through rather than a
destination (the Hwy 59 gateway role, no independent economy). Every population here has developed some
response to the first axis; only USA and Canada — its two strongest-matched populations — have a
developed response to the second, giving them outsized influence on Halley's actual lived culture
despite Brazil (11.69%) and UK (10.57%) each holding a larger or comparable population share.

Three distinct cultural postures toward ground instability emerge from the six populations: **heavy
engineering** (Germany's Wadden Sea polders and UK's Fens reclamation, genuinely convergent
traditions), **soft pastoral coexistence** (France's Camargue-derived living-with-instability
approach), and **adaptability-plus-hospitality** (USA and Canada, who also happen to be the two
populations with a genuine waypoint-town precedent). Brazil is again the least distinctly textured
population, though — importantly — for a citywide reason (the narrow "pure gateway town" City-Type
match) rather than a Brazil-specific one this time, unlike Belgrano.

Canada's Watson Lake "Sign Post Forest" precedent suggests a strong, concrete piece of Halley-specific
material culture: a tradition of travelers and residents leaving physical markers behind, turning a
city that can't stay in one place into a city that nonetheless keeps a permanent record of everyone who
passed through it. This feels like a genuinely usable, specific detail for future Course of Events or
level-design work, not just an abstract cultural finding.
