  into one settlement.
- **Geological composition:** Nunatak bedrock — stable rock foundation protruding through the ice
  sheet, unlike the ice-shelf cities in the same subnet (Neumayer, Halley).

## Real-World Parallel Locations (Phase 1b — location identification only, no cultural findings yet)

**Terrain profile:** exposed granite/rock-outcrop highland, inland, elevated, low precipitation
(continental, drier than the coastal Halley subnet cities).

- **USA (Primary):** Appalachian granite highlands — White Mountains (New Hampshire), Adirondacks (New
  York). Real, populated bedrock-highland communities with genuine four-season climate variation.
- **Germany (Significant):** Harz Mountains, or Black Forest granite terrain — populated rural highland
  communities on comparable bedrock.
- **France (Significant):** Massif Central — granite plateau highland, real populated rural terrain.
- **UK (Significant):** Scottish Highlands — ancient Precambrian granite/gneiss bedrock, sparsely
  populated but genuine highland communities (per search: most of Scotland's population is actually in
  the Midland Valley, leaving the Highlands themselves comparatively remote — worth keeping this
  population-distribution nuance in mind for Phase 2).
- **Russia (Significant):** Ural Mountains — rocky highland terrain, real mining/rural communities on
  exposed bedrock.
- **Brazil (Significant):** Brazilian Highlands (Planalto), e.g. Serra da Mantiqueira — genuine granite
  outcrop terrain, though much warmer/wetter; weakest match of the five, flagged for Phase 2 review
  rather than forced.

Scotland — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Scotland)
