descended residents a comparable, if differently-mechanised, special relationship to Juan Carlos's own
civic identity.

## Synthesis Notes (Phase 2 — draft, not yet developer-confirmed as final)

**Working name: "Juan Carlan"** *(or possibly "Carlino" — genuinely uncertain which reads better;
placeholder only, same status as every other city's working name so far).*

Juan Carlos reads as a genuine **fishing-port-plus-archive city**, with USA, Germany, France, and UK
all converging on real maritime-labor and administrative-record traditions (New Bedford, Bremerhaven,
Boulogne-sur-Mer, Grimsby), differentiated mainly by how strongly each leans administrative (USA,
Germany) versus purely maritime-labor (UK, France). Spain holds a distinct, non-quantitative claim on
the city's identity via the direct "Juan Carlos I" namesake connection — a second instance (after
Esperanza's Argentina) of a population's *symbolic* relationship to a city mattering independently of
population share, though via a different mechanism this time: naming/identity rather than a literal
historical event.

**A genuinely new methodological finding:** Italy — essentially tied for the largest Significant-tier
population here (14.24%) — has no strong match on either geography or City-Type, and this doesn't fit
the Brazil "settlement-type" predictive rule established at Halley. This looks like a fresh, distinct
category: a population that's simply hard to anchor at a *specific* city, independent of any
recognizable pattern. Worth watching for further Italy-specific cases across the remaining subnets to
see if a rule eventually emerges, rather than assuming this is just Brazil's pattern under a different
name.
