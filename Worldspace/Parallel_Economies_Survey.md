# Parallel Economies Survey — Working Reference (DRAFT, tentative)

**Status: research/brainstorm reference, not canon.** Same discipline as the recent `Ice-Cold_Buddhism_Research/`
and `Gemini_Circuit_Attention_Economy_Vocabulary_Sheet.md` work — nothing here is settled until the developer
signs off, and this file is expected to be revised, reorganized, or partly discarded. Its purpose: survey every
phenomenon within Concordia (and, where evidence generalizes, Tepenia more broadly) that could realistically
qualify as **an economy** — not just literal currency-and-goods trade — as a basis of reference for eventually
determining what Tepenia's actual, full economic picture looks like.

**The rubric used below**, adapted from the real-world attention-economy analysis already done for the Circuit
(`Reference/Real-World/Gemini_Circuit_Attention_Economy_Vocabulary_Sheet.md`, itself drawn from Peter Hershock's
*Buddhism and Intelligent Technology*): something counts as a genuine economy, not just "a thing people value,"
when it has —
1. a **scarce, desired resource** (not infinitely available to everyone),
2. real **production and circulation** (the resource is generated, spent, and moves between people),
3. a **valuation/exchange mechanism** (some way of comparing or trading it, even informally), and
4. **structural power effects** (who controls the flow gains real leverage over others).

**Relationship to existing canon.** `Worldspace/National_Economy_and_Currency.md` already establishes Tepenia's
*formal* monetary system in detail (an energy-backed national currency during the Second Interwar Period,
fracturing into regional currencies plus a cross-subnet trade standard after the Planetary Split Brain). That file is not
superseded or duplicated here — §1 below places it explicitly on this same rubric for completeness, then every
other section covers economies that run *alongside, underneath, or independently of* that formal currency, the
same way the black market already does per `National_Economy_and_Currency.md`'s own "What's Still Open"
section and the confirmed `10c_Pisces_Black_Market_Origin.md` mechanism.

## 1. Energy/charge — already-confirmed canon, included here as the baseline case

**Not a candidate — this is Tepenia's actual, established formal currency**, per `National_Economy_and_Currency.md`.
Included here only so the rubric above has a worked, confirmed example to check the more speculative entries
against:

1. Scarce resource — verified grid energy allocation, catastrophically scarce since Amundsen Tower's
   destruction fragmented continental grid regulation
2. Production/circulation — regional grids generate it; regional currency and a cross-subnet trade standard
   circulate it
3. Valuation — currency redeemable for guaranteed grid capacity; cross-subnet exchange at negotiated,
   non-fixed rates
4. Structural power — whoever controls a subnet's grid controls that subnet's entire formal economy; the
   Tower's destruction is *the* structural event of the setting's whole post-war economic picture

**Open question worth surfacing here specifically**: does every other candidate economy below ultimately
reduce to this one (repair parts, food, and labor all cost energy to produce), or do some run on genuinely
independent scarcity logic that energy abundance wouldn't fix? Worth deciding per-entry as each gets developed
further — flagged again in §6.

## 2. Physical/resource economies

### 2a. Repair and maintenance (parts, siligel, coolant, labor)

Robot bodies are not self-sufficient indefinitely — siligel and coolant function as food/drink (already
established, and explicitly *not* currency-commodities per `National_Economy_and_Currency.md`'s "ruled out"
note), and the Ice-Cold Buddhism research separately surfaced that repeated thermal cycling causes real
mechanical wear. That means spare components, repair labor, and consumables are a genuine scarce resource
distinct from money itself — you can have currency and still be unable to get repaired if the parts or the
skilled labor aren't available locally.

- Rubric check: scarce (components, especially post-Tower with regional supply fragmentation) — circulates
  (siligel/coolant "food" trade already established; repair labor is a real service) — valued (presumably
  currency-priced, but also plausibly barterable given siligel/coolant's already-confirmed barter-suitability) —
  structural power (whoever controls parts supply or repair expertise in an isolated subnet has real leverage,
  especially post-Split-Brain fragmentation)
- **Plausible home district**: Capricorn — The Yards (industrial/labor character implied by the name, not yet
  deep-dived on this specific angle)
- Genuinely new ground — no existing file develops a repair/parts economy specifically

### 2b. Physical goods / black market

**Already confirmed canon**, not speculative: `10c_Pisces_Black_Market_Origin.md` establishes Pisces as a
250-year-old gray economy (smuggling, escape-tech) that hardened into a real shadow economy for physical goods
during the Long Night War, specifically *because* the formal currency and supply system collapsed in the same
catastrophe. This is the clearest existing example of a genuine parallel (non-currency) physical-goods economy in
the whole repo, and the mechanism already explains *why* it exists structurally (a founding-era enforcement gap
under the Continuity and Stability Act, never revisited).

- Rubric check: fully satisfied, and already written into canon — see that file for the full mechanism
- **Home district**: Pisces — The Markets (confirmed)
- Not a candidate — flagged here so this survey is a complete picture, and as a template for how a "candidate"
  economy graduates into confirmed canon

## 3. Informational/institutional economies

### 3a. Knowledge and research access

**Aquarius — The Labs'** own name implies a scientific/research economy distinct from money: who gets lab
time, access to findings, or scientific credibility. Given the district's likely role in Concordia's technical
infrastructure, control over knowledge (not just its content, but *access* to it) is plausibly a real form of
leverage independent of formal currency.

- Rubric check: scarce (specialized expertise, lab access, possibly restricted/classified findings) —
  circulates (publication, citation, mentorship, institutional affiliation) — valued (credentialing, academic
  reputation) — structural power (gatekeeping who gets to do or use research is real leverage, especially
  post-war when technical capacity is scarcer)
- Genuinely new ground — no existing file develops this angle for Aquarius specifically

### 3b. Political and bureaucratic influence

**Libra — The Government District's** name implies an access/favor economy: legal standing, permits, official
attention, bureaucratic priority — distinctly different currency from money, closer to the "epistemological
power" (knowing/predicting) vs. "ontological power" (producing/shaping) distinction already documented for the
Circuit, but applied to political rather than attention capital. The Continuity and Stability Act (already
established as the mechanism behind at least four districts' founding-era conditions, including Pisces') is
itself evidence that Libra has long controlled a real, scarce resource — administrative discretion — that
other districts have had to live with the downstream effects of for centuries.

- Rubric check: scarce (bureaucratic discretion, legal favor, official priority — explicitly already shown to
  be finite and triaged even during Libra's own founding era) — circulates (political favors, legal rulings,
  triage decisions) — valued (informally, via influence and connections) — structural power (very high —
  Libra's founding-era triage choices are already shown to have shaped Pisces' entire subsequent history)
- **Home district**: Libra — The Government District (confirmed name; this specific economic angle not yet
  developed)

### 3c. Attention — cross-reference only, not duplicated here

Already fully surveyed in `Reference/Real-World/Gemini_Circuit_Attention_Economy_Vocabulary_Sheet.md` —
**Gemini — The Circuit's** attention economy, karmic/algorithmic vocabulary, and governance-debate material.
Included in this list only for completeness; see that file for the full treatment rather than duplicating it
here.

## 4. Social/relational economies

### 4a. Trust and vouching

Concordia's population is substantially refugee/diaspora (see `District_Refugee_Diaspora_Composition.md`), and
the Tepenian criminal justice system runs a 3-tier exile → supervised labor → Aquarius-subject track. Both
facts imply that **social standing itself is a scarce, tradeable resource** distinct from money — someone
rebuilding a life post-exile, or newly arrived from another subnet, plausibly needs *vouching* (a trusted
resident staking their own reputation on a newcomer) to access housing, work, or basic community trust, in a
way currency alone can't buy.

- Rubric check: scarce (trust takes time/relationship to build, can't be printed) — circulates (vouching,
  reputation, word-of-mouth) — valued (informally, but with very real consequences for access) — structural
  power (long-established residents/families would hold outsized leverage over newcomers)
- Genuinely new ground — no district currently has this developed as an explicit economic mechanic, though it's
  implied by existing hostility-rubric and diaspora-composition material

### 4b. Devotion and pilgrimage

Ice-Cold Buddhism's research (see `Reference/Real-World/Ice-Cold_Buddhism_Research/09_Vocabulary_Sheet.md`)
already flagged that repeated thermal cycling — the mechanical cost of devout pilgrimage practice — genuinely
shortens practitioner lifespan. That makes devotion a literal, mechanically expensive resource being "spent,"
not just a metaphor: **spiritual capital that costs real operational lifespan to acquire.**

- Rubric check: scarce (a finite body/lifespan being spent) — circulates (via pilgrimage, visible wear as a
  mark of status among the devout) — valued (informally, as prestige/holiness within the faith) — structural
  power (elder, visibly-worn practitioners plausibly hold real authority within the religion specifically
  because of this cost)
- **Home locations**: Dome Fuji, Kunlun (confirmed sacred sites)
- Genuinely new ground as an *economy* specifically, though the underlying cost mechanism is already
  established

### 4c. Accomplishment / History Points — a mechanical precedent, not (yet) a diegetic economy

**Worth including with a caveat.** `Game-Mechanics/Core-Mechanics/Accomplishment_Weight_System.md` is a real,
fully-designed system (weighted accumulation of a companion's meaningful actions, gating personal-questline
progression) — but it's a *game-mechanical* trigger system, not something written as an in-world economy NPCs
perceive or trade in. It's included here as a **structural precedent**: the project already has a working
model for "weighted accumulated deeds function as a spendable resource," which could inform how an in-world
reputation or deeds economy (§4a above) might actually be modeled, if one gets designed. Not itself a
candidate economy — a design pattern that could seed one.

## 6. Open / not yet decided

- **Does everything reduce to energy?** The central structural question this survey raises but doesn't
  answer: energy/charge is the confirmed formal currency, and several candidates above (repair/parts
  especially) plausibly cost energy to produce. Worth deciding, per-economy, whether it's a genuinely
  independent scarcity logic (trust and devotion clearly are — you can't buy either with watts) or a shadow
  price denominated in something else (repair parts plausibly are, ultimately, energy-priced)
- **Which of these, if any, deserve a district-specific deep-dive pass** (repair/parts → Capricorn; knowledge →
  Aquarius; political influence → Libra) versus staying as citywide texture not tied to one district
- **Whether "economy" is even the right frame for trust/vouching and devotion**, or whether they're better
  written as social/religious dynamics that merely *resemble* economies under this rubric without needing
  formal in-world mechanics — a real question, not a foregone conclusion
- No cross-district differentiation check has been run on any of these yet (per `CLAUDE.md`'s standing rule)
  — before any of §2-4 gets written into an actual district file, check
  `Cross_District_Differentiation_Table.md` first
