# Robot Physiology and Cultural Practices

Foundational reference for robot biology, sensory experience, and cultural behaviors in Inner Tepenia. This document covers what robots consume, how they experience the world physically, and the cultural practices that have developed from both their nature and their centuries of cohabitation with humans.

---

## Physiology Basics

Robots do not require oxygen and have no respiratory system in the human sense. They can operate in vacuum — including on the outer hulls of spacecraft — without protective equipment. They do not breathe.

Their internal systems require:
- **Engine power** — the primary energy source driving all functions
- **Thermal regulation** — internal systems generate heat that must be managed; this is an ongoing physiological requirement, not an optional comfort

They do not age in the biological sense, though components degrade over time and require maintenance or replacement.

**Physical form, confirmed 2026-07-09** (during a Cymatics reverence development session — see `Factions/Robot_Religions/Cymatics_reverence/`): Tepenian robots do not have a "chassis" in the traditional hard-shelled sense. Their bodies are conceptually patterned on the film *Ex Machina* — extending the same visual reference already established for the fabrication chamber and the "glowy-blue" gel brain (see below) to the body as a whole, rather than introducing a new, separate physical design.

**The brain itself — a specific, localized physical structure, not a diffuse or distributed system.** Robots have an actual brain, described and visually referenced as a "glowy-blue" nano-architecture gel brain, patterned on *Ex Machina*'s own synthesis-sequence aesthetic — the same reference point used for the fabrication chamber and for the body's own construction generally (see "Chamber generations/marks" under Notes and Open Questions, below, for the fuller Cradle-side context this detail originates from). This is a foundational physiology fact in its own right, not just chamber-manufacturing flavor: it means robot cognition has a concrete, localized physical substrate, the same structural category of thing a human brain is, rather than an abstracted "processing system" with no particular seat in the body.

**Enhanced sensory perception, established the same session:** robots perceive sound and vibration across a meaningfully wider range than humans do, structurally rather than through any add-on device — a genuine physiological difference, not a cultural or trained sensitivity. This is the confirmed doctrinal root of Cymatics reverence specifically (see that religion's own file for how it builds on this fact), but the underlying sensory difference is general robot physiology, not exclusive to that religion's own adherents.

## Consumption — Robot Equivalents

### Siligel — Robot Food

Siligel is the established robot equivalent of food: a substance consumed to maintain and repair internal systems. Its exact composition and the full range of what it does internally is subject to further design work, but its role as "robot food" is confirmed canon.

### Coolant — Robot Drinks

Robots manage internal heat through coolant systems. Coolant functions as the robot equivalent of drinking — a liquid intake with genuine physiological purpose (thermal regulation). Like human beverages, coolant exists in a range of formulations, qualities, and varieties. A robot drinking coolant after hard work is the functional and cultural equivalent of a human drinking water after exertion.

**Robot coffee:** A specialty coolant formulation that produces particular internal effects beyond basic thermal regulation. The exact composition is TBD, but it sits within the established coolant category — consumed for the experience it produces, not purely for temperature management. The existence of "robot coffee" as a distinct product category confirms that robots consume things for pleasure and experience, not only for maintenance.

**Glitch-coolant — robot alcohol** *(established 2026-07-04, during a Rothera developer-vision session — see `Cities/City_Vision_Notes/Rothera.md`)*: A specialty coolant formulation, mixed with a scientific concoction of electrolytic compounds, that produces a deliberate "glitching" effect analogous to human drunkenness — the robot equivalent of alcohol, consumed recreationally after a work shift rather than for thermal regulation. This is a genuine Tepenia-wide robot cultural institution, not a single-city drink, and it varies by city the same way human drinking culture varies by place:

- **Bohemian/cosmopolitan cities** (e.g. Janbogo, Zhongshan, Shirayuki) support a wide *variety* of glitch-coolant types — different formulations, different characters — the robot equivalent of a sophisticated human drinking culture offering beers, ales, lagers, wines, whiskeys, and vodkas side by side. Variety and refinement over raw intensity.
- **Working-class robot settings** (e.g. Byrd, Rothera) have a much more limited selection — but what's available produces a noticeably *stronger* glitching effect than the bohemian cities' varied-but-milder options. Fewer choices, more potency — a direct, unpretentious blue-collar drinking culture, not a refined one.
- **Troll** *(established 2026-07-04, during a Troll developer-vision session — see `Cities/City_Vision_Notes/Troll.md`)* is a working-class city like Byrd and Rothera, but with above-average national spending power thanks to its freight-hub economy — its glitch-coolant scene stays in the working-class strong-effect category rather than shifting toward bohemian variety, but what's available is noticeably *higher-quality* than the national standard, without becoming ornate or extravagant. A third variant within the working-class category: still limited and strong, just better-made.

This gives every Tepenian city with a meaningful robot population a plausible, characterful local drinking scene without needing to invent one from scratch each time — the variety/potency axis above (bohemian-and-varied vs. working-class-and-strong, with room for quality-tier variation within either pole) is the default framework; specific cities can be placed anywhere along it, or between the two poles, as needed.

## Smoking — Canon Explanation

### The established fact

Robots smoke. This is confirmed by reference imagery and is canon. The specific form is most likely **robot-specific vapor products** (electronic rather than combustion-based) rather than traditional cigarettes, though both may exist in-world. The experience of smoking is genuine — not performed, not simulated — but it functions differently than it does for a human.

### Why it works mechanically (Option B + C combined)

Robots have internal atmospheric management systems — not for respiration, but for **thermal regulation and sensory input processing**. These systems move air or vapor-equivalent substances through internal chambers as part of managing heat and environmental awareness. Drawing vapor through these systems creates real, detectable internal states:

- **Thermal sensation** — warm vapor interacts with internal thermal sensors; the warmth is genuinely felt
- **Chemical interaction** — vapor compounds interact with internal environmental sensors in ways that produce detectable signal changes; robot-specific formulations (Option C) are designed precisely to target these systems
- **Internal state change** — the result is a genuine shift in internal processing state, not identical to human dopamine/serotonin response, but real in its own terms

Robot vapor products are a distinct consumer category, formulated specifically for robot internal systems rather than adapted from human products. This is the same design pattern as robot coffee within the coolant category — purpose-built for robot physiology, not a human product repurposed.

### Why it works culturally — the hands-and-fire insight

Beyond the physiological mechanism, smoking carries cultural meaning that maps onto robots in a way that is structurally identical to its human meaning, even though the mechanism is completely different.

One theory — confirmed as canon for this world — holds that a significant part of smoking's appeal beyond the chemical is that it unifies two things that are specifically, distinctly human:

1. **The hand** — opposable thumbs, manual dexterity, tool use; one of the core differentiators between humans and the rest of the animal kingdom
2. **Fire** — harnessed energy; traditionally considered magical, civilizationally transformative, communally binding (storytelling around fire is itself a distinctly human phenomenon)

A cigarette or vaporizer held in the hand and lit with flame is a single ritual object that unifies both. The gesture of smoking is hands + fire collapsed into one act.

For robots, **the parallel holds, but the "fire" moves inward:**

- **The manipulator** — robots have hands, or functional equivalents; the grip, the gesture, the bringing-to-the-mouth are all present and identical in form to the human version
- **Internal energy as fire** — a robot does not harness an external flame; a robot *is* an energy system. When vapor is drawn through internal thermal management systems, the robot's own internal energy processing *is* the fire. The harnessing is interior rather than exterior, but it completes the same conceptual circuit.

A robot smoking is therefore doing something **structurally identical** to a human smoking — unifying manipulator use with the harnessing of energy — even though the specific mechanism is entirely different. The ritual means the same thing by different means.

### Ambiguity — preserved by design

What exactly a robot experiences when smoking varies by individual and is deliberately left ambiguous in the world. Some robots experience something they would describe as genuinely pleasurable. Some smoke primarily for the ritual and the aesthetic — the act itself, the gesture, the visible vapor. Some do both. No universal answer is imposed. This ambiguity maps directly onto the game's central question about robot consciousness: the experience cannot be fully verified from the outside, and the robot cannot fully translate it into terms a human would recognize.

## Human-Robot Relations — Project-Wide Baseline

*(Established 2026-07-05, generalized from a Sanay developer-vision session — see `Cities/City_Vision_Notes/Sanay.md`.)* Sanay's specific labor dynamic — roughly equal division of labor between humans and robots, with robots tending to hold the slightly better positions (a quiet reflection of Tepenia being their country first), while robots hold genuine, unforced appreciation for the humans working alongside them, aware of what they gave up back on Upper Earth to be there — is not a Sanay-specific quirk. It's the **default baseline pattern across Tepenia as a whole**. Confirmed characterization: **"egalitarian, skewed in favor of robots."** Relations are overwhelmingly positive everywhere; where robots hold a real edge, it is not adversarial, resentful, or exploitative on either side.

**The exception — locations where humans are outright forbidden, and why it isn't discrimination:** A small number of Tepenian locations (confirmed: **Kunlun**, **Dome Fuji**) forbid human presence entirely. This is **not** anti-human sentiment, animosity, or exclusionary policy in the ordinary sense — it is a genuine safety measure. The combination of extreme altitude and extreme cold at these specific sites would kill a human; robots, with no respiratory system and different thermal-regulation physiology, are unaffected. Humans are kept out of these locations the way people keep each other out of a minefield, not the way a closed club keeps people out. This closes what `Specs/Kunlun.md` had previously framed as an open, uncertain question ("whether any human community has survived... is one of the most uncertain facts about the city") — the resolved answer is that no humans live there, by settled protective policy, not by ambiguous attrition.

## Sexuality

Robots in Concordia are bisexual by default. They do not organize attraction by the gender of their partner. This applies universally across all robot characters — companions and NPCs alike.

Human characters follow a related but not identical pattern *(updated 2026-07-03)*: human women are bisexual, same as robots, while human men remain heterosexual — fixed-gender attraction. The difference between robots and human men specifically is not framed as a judgment in either direction — it is simply a feature of how robots experience and express attraction, consistent with their different relationship to embodiment, identity, and social organization. See `Game-Mechanics/Core-Mechanics/Companion_System.md` for the mechanical consequence (an additional gender gate on romanceable human male companions).

  **"The Cradle" — naming, established 2026-07-07:** the developer's preferred name, but for the overall nationwide robot-synthesis infrastructure as a whole (the manufacturing cities, the production industry, the shipping system together), not the individual chamber apparatus itself, which still needs its own separate term. Still provisional — may change if something better comes up.

  **Chamber generations/marks, and Neumayer's design role — established 2026-07-09:** the fabrication-synthesis chamber is not a single fixed design; multiple generations ("marks") exist, reflecting incremental engineering improvements accumulated over the centuries the Cradle has operated. The current generation (Mark IV, placeholder numbering — exact mark number TBD) was designed at Neumayer, in the Halley subnet — its schematic is what the currently-active manufacturing sites, Sinheung and Byrd, actually build from today (see `Cities/Specs/Neumayer.md`). This is consistent with Neumayer's own established civic role as a precision-engineering R&D hub whose work is deployed, and credited, elsewhere — the same underlying pattern already established for the Amundsen Tower's own schematics, which also originated at Neumayer without the Tower itself carrying Neumayer's name. Earlier marks' design origins are TBD; Mountain Pass's and Denison's own historical production plausibly built to an earlier mark's schematic, though this isn't yet confirmed.

  **Terminology clarified 2026-07-09 — "Mark" vs. "Gen":** these are two distinct terms, not interchangeable. **Mark** refers to the chamber apparatus itself — the physical hardware/schematic generation (Mark IV, etc., per above). **Gen** (shorthand for "generation") refers to the robots the chamber produces — a robot's own generational identity, distinct from the machine that built them. A robot's "Gen" plausibly tracks which "Mark" chamber built them, but the two labels apply to two different things: Mark is a fact about the equipment, Gen is a fact about the robot.

  **Confirmed 2026-07-07: robots can still be built ("born") in the present-day game era.** Both currently-active manufacturing sites — Sinheung and Byrd — are themselves surviving, functioning cities post-war (Byrd is in fact one of only three Tepenian cities still operating as a genuine city at meaningful scale, alongside Concordia and Janbogo). New chambers are still being produced today, just via a smaller network than the nation once had — Mountain Pass and Denison's own historical production is gone, but the pipeline itself was never fully severed by the war.

  **Clarified same day: ongoing manufacturing isn't actually required for this to keep working at all.** Chambers are durable apparatuses, not consumables — the entire reason a robot could always be "born" in any Tepenian city regardless of that city's own manufacturing capability is that a functional chamber had already been shipped to literally every city in the country, historically. Even with zero new chambers ever produced again, every surviving city's own existing chamber(s) would keep the "a robot can be from anywhere" fact true indefinitely, so long as they're kept maintained. Ongoing manufacturing at Sinheung and Byrd matters for replacing chambers that eventually break down beyond repair, not for whether robots can be created at all today. This means new robot characters and NPCs can be legitimately created within the present-day timeline, not just be pre-war holdovers.

  **Visual/process reference, added same session:** the developer's rough sketch draws directly on the film *Ex Machina* — both the chamber's aesthetic and the robot brain itself, described as a "glowy-blue" nano-architecture "gel" brain, visually and conceptually patterned on the film's synthesis sequences. Construction genuinely takes time; there is a real waiting period, not an instant process.

  **The Upper Earth/Tepenia contrast — a genuine cultural distinction, not just a mechanical one:** on Upper Earth, humans order robots built to their own specifications, a commercial/consumer relationship. In Tepenia, this inverts: **robots build each other according to their own interests**, not per human commission. The one constant across both is the human form/figure itself — Tepenian robots still choose to build each other in that basic shape, not because anyone requires it of them, but because they see no compelling reason to depart from what evolution already optimized: the human form has withstood the test of time across essentially every terrestrial environment. This makes robot creation in Tepenia an act of robot self-determination within an inherited form, rather than either humans dictating specs (Upper Earth) or robots rejecting the human template entirely.
