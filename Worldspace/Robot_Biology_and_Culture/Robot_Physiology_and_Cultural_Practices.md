# Robot Physiology and Cultural Practices

Foundational reference for robot biology, sensory experience, and cultural behaviors in Inner Tepenia. This document covers what robots consume, how they experience the world physically, and the cultural practices that have developed from both their nature and their centuries of cohabitation with humans.

---

## Physiology Basics

Robots do not require oxygen and have no respiratory system in the human sense. They can operate in vacuum — including on the outer hulls of spacecraft — without protective equipment. They do not breathe.

> ### ⭐ COMPANION FILE — what cold DOES cost a robot
> **Immunity to altitude is not immunity to cold.** **`Robot_Cold_Physiology.md`** covers the real
> costs: **recharging below freezing causes permanent capacity loss** *(lithium plating)*; **usable
> capacity falls to 50–60% by −20 °C**; **output sags under load**; **materials embrittle and
> lubricants solidify below −40 °C**. ⭐ **And the inversion that matters: a robot is safer MOVING
> than resting, because working generates the heat that keeps its cells and joints in range.**


Their internal systems require:
- **Engine power** — the primary energy source driving all functions
- **Thermal regulation** — internal systems generate heat that must be managed; this is an ongoing physiological requirement, not an optional comfort

They do not age in the biological sense, though components degrade over time and require maintenance or replacement.

**A robot's gel brain is malleable and ever-changing — not the fixed, unchanging "hard drive" construction popularly imagined from 1950s-era sci-fi.** A robot is always capable of finding a new purpose in life; nothing about the substrate itself locks a robot into a single fixed self. Damage to *function*, or even a substantial change in who a robot is, is not itself threatening or death-adjacent — see "What Counts as Death" below for what actually is.

### What Counts as Death

The threatening thing, for a robot, is never damage to function — a robot can always adapt, retrain, or find new purpose, since the gel brain itself is malleable rather than fixed. What actually constitutes death, from a robot's own perspective, is **irreversible damage to a robot's cognitive architecture (the gel brain) or to their energy-source architecture** — the robot equivalent of a heart, i.e. whatever physically supplies their Engine power (see above) — **with no way of ever bringing the robot back online.** Short of that threshold, however severe, it's architecture or integrity damage, not death. (See Calethina's own established corruption from the Split Brain event for a worked example of serious, ongoing architecture damage that has not — yet — crossed into this threshold; her own struggle over whether to attempt a substrate transfer is precisely the live question of whether it can be addressed before it does.)

### What Happens After — Ossuaries `[CGRM 2026-09-01 · Path 6 · developer ruling]`

**One of the post-death possibilities is inclusion in an ossuary — where the bones are METAL rather than
calcium.** *(One possibility among several, not the only disposition.)*

**Why this fits what death already is for a robot, established above:**

- **⭐ An ossuary is a COLLECTIVE form, and robot death is a collective event.** Robots do not die at a rate
  the way humans do — there is no senescence and no background attrition. **They die when something kills
  them**, which means they die in *incidents*: a dome breach, a collapse, a grid failure, a war. **Many at
  once, or none for a long while.** A human graveyard is a field of individual plots accumulating one at a
  time. **An ossuary is what you build when the dead arrive together.**
- **It answers the permafrost problem.** Burial is impossible in Tepenia. Ossuaries are the historical
  real-world answer wherever ground burial fails.
- **⭐ And metal bones do not decay.** Human ossuaries hold remains that were once soft and are now reduced.
  **A robot ossuary holds structure that will not change.** In a nation where nothing rots because nothing is
  warm, **a robot ossuary is a permanent and permanently growing physical record of every robot that has died
  in that place** — legible, countable, and never diminishing.

> ### ⚠ The tension worth developing, not resolving here:
> **In a closed continental economy, metal is scarce and recovery is a necessary industry** *(see
> `Cities/Division_of_Industry/00_Necessary_Industries_Register.md` C4 — the alternative to recovery is not
> landfill, it is doing without)*. **An ossuary is therefore a large, permanent, deliberately un-reclaimed
> reserve of exactly the material the economy is short of.**
>
> **Is it sacred and untouchable, or is it a reserve of last resort?** A city that melted down its ossuary
> during a hard winter is a different place from one that starved beside it. **Neither answer is settled.**

**Related:** Polydimensional Animism already carries a **Robot Death Doctrine** and a **"Death as Change of
Vantage"** doctrine (`Factions/Robot_Religions/Polydimensional_Animism/`) — **robot death is handled by
religion and community rather than by a professional trade**, which is what episodic mortality would produce:
you cannot sustain a profession on demand that vanishes for months at a time.

**Physical form, confirmed 2026-07-09** (during a Cymatics reverence development session — see `Factions/Robot_Religions/Cymatics_reverence/`): Tepenian robots do not have a "chassis" in the traditional hard-shelled sense. Their bodies are conceptually patterned on the film *Ex Machina* — extending the same visual reference already established for the fabrication chamber and the "glowy-blue" gel brain (see below) to the body as a whole, rather than introducing a new, separate physical design.

**The brain itself — a specific, localized physical structure, not a diffuse or distributed system.** Robots have an actual brain, described and visually referenced as a "glowy-blue" nano-architecture gel brain, patterned on *Ex Machina*'s own synthesis-sequence aesthetic — the same reference point used for the fabrication chamber and for the body's own construction generally (see "Chamber generations/marks" under Notes and Open Questions, below, for the fuller Cradle-side context this detail originates from). This is a foundational physiology fact in its own right, not just chamber-manufacturing flavor: it means robot cognition has a concrete, localized physical substrate, the same structural category of thing a human brain is, rather than an abstracted "processing system" with no particular seat in the body.

**Confirmed: the gel brain's data is not static — it is constantly moving, constantly shifting, the same way a meat-based human brain's own state is a continuous dynamic process rather than a fixed, readable dataset.** This is the actual mechanistic reason mind transfer/uploading is never clean or risk-free for a robot — a gel brain can't be "read out" and copied any more cleanly than a human brain could be, because there is no static snapshot to copy in the first place; the substrate itself is always mid-process. This is the underlying physiological root of the already-established fact that any of Calethina's substrate transfers carry "a real risk of memory loss or alteration — there is no clean, risk-free option" (see `Worldspace/Characters/Dolls/Still-Present_-_In-Game/unsure and_or special cases/Calethina/Questlines/Substrate_Transfer_and_Embodiment_Design.md`) — that design decision was never an arbitrary game-balance rule, it follows directly from what a gel brain actually is. Treat "uploading a robot mind" as equally unrealistic, for the equally rigorous reason, as uploading a human mind — this applies to any future robot character or mechanic involving substrate transfer, not just Calethina's own case.

**Confirmed 2026-08-07 — the "glowy-blue" color itself is not an individually meaningful or aesthetic trait.** It's a direct result of the gel brain's own material composition/architecture — the same category of fact as the sky being blue because of nitrogen's predominance in the atmosphere, or grass being green because of chlorophyll's predominance in plant matter. It doesn't vary per individual robot or per build in any personally expressive way; it's closer to a fixed physical constant of what a gel brain *is* than a trait any given robot could be said to have or express.

**Enhanced sensory perception, established the same session:** robots perceive sound and vibration across a meaningfully wider range than humans do, structurally rather than through any add-on device — a genuine physiological difference, not a cultural or trained sensitivity. This is the confirmed doctrinal root of Cymatics reverence specifically (see that religion's own file for how it builds on this fact), but the underlying sensory difference is general robot physiology, not exclusive to that religion's own adherents.

## Consumption — Robot Equivalents

Siligel is the established robot equivalent of food: a substance consumed to maintain and repair internal systems. Its exact composition and the full range of what it does internally is subject to further design work, but its role as "robot food" is confirmed canon.

> The gel brain's blue comes from *surface states* of its silicon nano-architecture; silicon surfaces require

### Coolant — Robot Drinks

Robots manage internal heat through coolant systems. Coolant functions as the robot equivalent of drinking — a liquid intake with genuine physiological purpose (thermal regulation). Like human beverages, coolant exists in a range of formulations, qualities, and varieties. A robot drinking coolant after hard work is the functional and cultural equivalent of a human drinking water after exertion.

**Robot coffee:** A specialty coolant formulation that produces particular internal effects beyond basic thermal regulation. The exact composition is TBD, but it sits within the established coolant category — consumed for the experience it produces, not purely for temperature management. The existence of "robot coffee" as a distinct product category confirms that robots consume things for pleasure and experience, not only for maintenance.

**Glitch-coolant — robot alcohol** *(established 2026-07-04, during a Rothera developer-vision session — see `Cities/City_Vision_Notes/Rothera.md`)*: A specialty coolant formulation, mixed with a scientific concoction of electrolytic compounds, that produces a deliberate "glitching" effect analogous to human drunkenness — the robot equivalent of alcohol, consumed recreationally after a work shift rather than for thermal regulation. This is a genuine Tepenia-wide robot cultural institution, not a single-city drink, and it varies by city the same way human drinking culture varies by place:

- **Bohemian/cosmopolitan cities** (e.g. Janbogo, Zhongshan, Shirayuki) support a wide *variety* of glitch-coolant types — different formulations, different characters — the robot equivalent of a sophisticated human drinking culture offering beers, ales, lagers, wines, whiskeys, and vodkas side by side. Variety and refinement over raw intensity.
- **Working-class robot settings** (e.g. Byrd, Rothera) have a much more limited selection — but what's available produces a noticeably *stronger* glitching effect than the bohemian cities' varied-but-milder options. Fewer choices, more potency — a direct, unpretentious blue-collar drinking culture, not a refined one.
- **Troll** *(established 2026-07-04, during a Troll developer-vision session — see `Cities/City_Vision_Notes/Troll.md`)* is a working-class city like Byrd and Rothera, but with above-average national spending power thanks to its freight-hub economy — its glitch-coolant scene stays in the working-class strong-effect category rather than shifting toward bohemian variety, but what's available is noticeably *higher-quality* than the national standard, without becoming ornate or extravagant. A third variant within the working-class category: still limited and strong, just better-made.

This gives every Tepenian city with a meaningful robot population a plausible, characterful local drinking scene without needing to invent one from scratch each time — the variety/potency axis above (bohemian-and-varied vs. working-class-and-strong, with room for quality-tier variation within either pole) is the default framework; specific cities can be placed anywhere along it, or between the two poles, as needed.

## Smoking — Canon Explanation

### The established fact

Robots smoke. This is confirmed by reference imagery and is canon. The specific form is most likely **robot-specific vapor products** (electronic rather than combustion-based) rather than traditional cigarettes, though both may exist in-world. The experience of smoking is genuine — not performed, not simulated — but it functions differently than it does for a human.

### Why it works mechanically (Option B + C combined)

Robots have internal atmospheric management systems — not for respiration, but for **thermal regulation and sensory input processing**. These systems move air or vapor-equivalent substances through internal chambers as part of managing heat and environmental awareness. Drawing vapor through these systems creates real, detectable internal states:

- **Thermal sensation** — warm vapor interacts with internal thermal sensors; the warmth is genuinely felt
- **Chemical interaction** — vapor compounds interact with internal environmental sensors in ways that produce detectable signal changes; robot-specific formulations (Option C) are designed precisely to target these systems
- **Internal state change** — the result is a genuine shift in internal processing state, not identical to human dopamine/serotonin response, but real in its own terms

Robot vapor products are a distinct consumer category, formulated specifically for robot internal systems rather than adapted from human products. This is the same design pattern as robot coffee within the coolant category — purpose-built for robot physiology, not a human product repurposed.

### Why it works culturally — the hands-and-fire insight

Beyond the physiological mechanism, smoking carries cultural meaning that maps onto robots in a way that is structurally identical to its human meaning, even though the mechanism is completely different.

One theory — confirmed as canon for this world — holds that a significant part of smoking's appeal beyond the chemical is that it unifies two things that are specifically, distinctly human:

1. **The hand** — opposable thumbs, manual dexterity, tool use; one of the core differentiators between humans and the rest of the animal kingdom
2. **Fire** — harnessed energy; traditionally considered magical, civilizationally transformative, communally binding (storytelling around fire is itself a distinctly human phenomenon)

A cigarette or vaporizer held in the hand and lit with flame is a single ritual object that unifies both. The gesture of smoking is hands + fire collapsed into one act.

For robots, **the parallel holds, but the "fire" moves inward:**

- **The manipulator** — robots have hands, or functional equivalents; the grip, the gesture, the bringing-to-the-mouth are all present and identical in form to the human version
- **Internal energy as fire** — a robot does not harness an external flame; a robot *is* an energy system. When vapor is drawn through internal thermal management systems, the robot's own internal energy processing *is* the fire. The harnessing is interior rather than exterior, but it completes the same conceptual circuit.

A robot smoking is therefore doing something **structurally identical** to a human smoking — unifying manipulator use with the harnessing of energy — even though the specific mechanism is entirely different. The ritual means the same thing by different means.

### Ambiguity — preserved by design

What exactly a robot experiences when smoking varies by individual and is deliberately left ambiguous in the world. Some robots experience something they would describe as genuinely pleasurable. Some smoke primarily for the ritual and the aesthetic — the act itself, the gesture, the visible vapor. Some do both. No universal answer is imposed. This ambiguity maps directly onto the game's central question about robot consciousness: the experience cannot be fully verified from the outside, and the robot cannot fully translate it into terms a human would recognize.

## Downtime, Recharging and Leisure `[CGRM 2026-09-01 · Path 6 · developer ruling]`

**Robots do NOT work 100% of the time. Not remotely.** Two separate reasons, and the second is the important
one.

**1. Physical — they require downtime, most prominently OVERNIGHT RECHARGING.** Like anybody else.

> **⭐ Which gives Tepenia a shared night.** Robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons.
> **A city where everyone sleeps is a very different place from one where half the population works through
> the dark** — and it means the polar night falls on a population that is *all* resting, together.

**2. Psychological — they are fully conscious and sentient**, so **a robot that spends all its time working is
virtually guaranteed to go insane.** Leisure is not a perk or an efficiency loss. **It is a condition of
remaining sane.**

> ### ⚠ Which makes overwork a HAZARD WITH A MECHANISM, not merely a hardship.
> **This sits very close to the "faulty semantic signal" model of robot illness** *(see "What Counts as
> Death")*. **If sustained overwork can corrupt a gel brain, then labor conditions are a medical question**,
> and the Sinheung Institute's semantic-care half acquires an occupational-health dimension. **Flagged as a
> connection, not yet ruled.**

### ⭐⭐ THE LEISURE HISTORY — why this is not a scheduling detail

> **On Upper Earth, during the First Interwar Period, a robot's leisure time was afforded to them by their
> loving romantic partners.** It existed because somebody cared enough to grant it. **It was contingent,
> personal, and revocable.**
>
> **In Tepenia, in the Second Interwar Period, that same leisure is a FACT OF LIFE.** Universal. Owed to
> nobody. **This is their own country, which they finally have for themselves. They are finally completely
> free.**

**So leisure in Tepenia is not rest. It is the material proof that leaving worked.** A robot with hours that
belong to nobody is a robot whose freedom is real rather than granted — **and the contrast is with a past
where those same hours existed only because they were loved.**

> **⭐ This connects directly to the economic model, and makes an economic statistic into the point of the
> nation.** `Cities/Division_of_Industry/09_Per_City_Baseline_Run.md` §3.5 finds that **half to
> three-quarters of a Tepenian working life is not spoken for by survival**, varying by city — **a freedom
> gradient drawn by geography.**
>
> **For the robot half of the population, that gradient measures the thing the exile was for.** A Neumayerite
> robot has half their life to themselves; a Rotheran has two-thirds. **Both are free. They are not equally
> free — and in a country founded on precisely that, the difference is political.**

## Human-Robot Relations — Project-Wide Baseline

*(Established 2026-07-05, generalized from a Sanay developer-vision session — see `Cities/City_Vision_Notes/Sanay.md`.)* Sanay's specific labor dynamic — roughly equal division of labor between humans and robots, with robots tending to hold the slightly better positions (a quiet reflection of Tepenia being their country first), while robots hold genuine, unforced appreciation for the humans working alongside them, aware of what they gave up back on Upper Earth to be there — is not a Sanay-specific quirk. It's the **default baseline pattern across Tepenia as a whole**. Confirmed characterization: **"egalitarian, skewed in favor of robots."** Relations are overwhelmingly positive everywhere; where robots hold a real edge, it is not adversarial, resentful, or exploitative on either side.

**The exception — locations where humans are outright forbidden, and why it isn't discrimination:** A small number of Tepenian locations (confirmed: **Kunlun**, **Dome Fuji**) forbid human presence entirely. This is **not** anti-human sentiment, animosity, or exclusionary policy in the ordinary sense — it is a genuine safety measure. The combination of extreme altitude and extreme cold at these specific sites would kill a human; robots, with no respiratory system and different thermal-regulation physiology, are unaffected. Humans are kept out of these locations the way people keep each other out of a minefield, not the way a closed club keeps people out. This closes what `Specs/Kunlun.md` had previously framed as an open, uncertain question ("whether any human community has survived... is one of the most uncertain facts about the city") — the resolved answer is that no humans live there, by settled protective policy, not by ambiguous attrition.

## Sexuality

Robots in Concordia are bisexual by default. They do not organize attraction by the gender of their partner. This applies universally across all robot characters — companions and NPCs alike.

Human characters follow a related but not identical pattern *(updated 2026-07-03)*: human women are bisexual, same as robots, while human men remain heterosexual — fixed-gender attraction. The difference between robots and human men specifically is not framed as a judgment in either direction — it is simply a feature of how robots experience and express attraction, consistent with their different relationship to embodiment, identity, and social organization. See `Game-Mechanics/Core-Mechanics/Companion_System.md` for the mechanical consequence (an additional gender gate on romanceable human male companions).

**Why robots value sexual exclusivity, confirmed 2026-08-06.** Robots don't carry the human evolutionary pressure toward sexual jealousy — no paternity uncertainty, no differential parental investment, since robots aren't produced through sexual reproduction at all. But robots do understand reproduction's high value and significance to humans, and from that understanding they've arrived at their own answer: **robots treat sex as the highest available expression of trust, faith, and commitment** — not, as it functions for humans, as a means toward producing a child. This is the actual in-fiction root of the game's monogamy/romance-exclusivity mechanic (`Companion_System.md`'s "Romance Exclusivity" rule) as it applies to robot companions: breaking exclusivity isn't read as human-style jealousy, it's a betrayal of the single highest currency of trust a robot recognizes.

**The physical/internal layer, confirmed 2026-08-07.** Robots have a genuine internal network of pleasure receptors in the equivalent of their genitals — this is not a purely symbolic or relational act with no sensation layer. The design logic traces back to how robots read the human template: humans, in some form for up to ~400,000 years and in their current recognizable form for ~30,000-35,000 years, have survived and thrived across nearly every environment on the planet. Robots understand sexual pleasure as a genuine contributor to that evolutionary success, and see no reason to have engineered around it or left it out.

**Courtship customs, confirmed 2026-08-07.** How robots signal interest and court one another varies enormously — by city/community and by individual personality alike, the same way human courtship style varies both across cultures and across individual people. There is no single robot-wide courtship ritual. What *is* robot-wide, underneath that local variation, is the core value itself: sex as the highest form of trust, commitment, and promise. Customs differ city to city; the meaning underneath them doesn't.

**Casual sex — a real trust-based middle ground, confirmed 2026-08-07.** Robot sex stays trust-based even outside a full committed relationship — a robot would not sleep with just anyone — but robots don't require a named, exclusive, long-term commitment (marriage or a boyfriend/girlfriend-equivalent relationship) as a strict precondition either. A given robot may judge, case by case and person by person, that sex without full romantic commitment is warranted. This is consistent with, not a contradiction of, the game's existing casual/"fuckable" character pool (`Companion_System.md`) sitting alongside full romance arcs: the pool was never meant to imply sex means nothing to robots, it reflects robots extending real, if narrower, trust on a per-case basis short of full exclusivity.

**Partner species, confirmed 2026-08-07.** Robots read sex as an act of absolute commitment to one specific person, whether that person is human or another robot — no meaningful difference by partner species. Humans living in Tepenia grow up understanding this as an ambient fact of life, not something requiring negotiation. What humans do sexually with other humans, independent of any robot involved, sits outside this setting's scope entirely.

A robot's "Gen" plausibly tracks which "Mark" chamber built them, but the two labels apply to two different things: Mark is a fact about the equipment, Gen is a fact about the robot.

  **Confirmed 2026-08-06 — Gen/Mark is a noted age-metacategory, never a hierarchy.** Robots would recognize and discuss which Gen/Mark they are the same way they'd note any other biographical fact, but this is explicitly **not** a basis for status or rank — no "newer Mark is better" or "later Gen is senior" reading is intended, consistent with this setting's egalitarian baseline. What robots place more cultural emphasis on is their own **build** (see below) — non-hierarchical, but the more significant source of individual differentiation between one robot and another. **Confirmed 2026-08-07: different Marks/Gens genuinely can carry different cognitive and/or physical attributes** — design specs vary in material-level efficiency, and different Marks were built to different specs for different reasons tied to whatever environmental/social/psychological pressures the nation (or pre-exile individuals) faced when that Mark was designed. This is a real difference, but strictly one of *kind*, never *rank* — no Mark/Gen is intrinsically better, just different, and the variety further bonds robots together (and robots to humans) around their shared environment rather than dividing them by generation.

  **Confirmed 2026-08-07 — robot kinship runs on shared experience, not fabrication lineage.** Mentor/mentee bonds are a genuine, culturally real robot institution. "Maker/made" is not: currently-existing robots have comparatively little control over what kind of person a newly-synthesized robot turns out to be, and a new robot is created by an *infrastructure* (the Cradle), not a personal "creator" — so kinship built on "who specifically made you" doesn't hold up here. Robots do have a real sense of clan/tribe, built on shared experience of togetherness — fabricated in the same city environment, living through the same struggles, sharing the same life-goals — not on build specs, Gen, or Mark. Humans draw tribal/clan identity from three possible bases: genetics, geography, and shared ideas/experience. Robots only have two of the three, geography and ideas/experience, not genetics — though they can and do feel a real sense of kinship with both other robots and humans.

  **Confirmed 2026-08-06, corrected same day — three separate things get set at fabrication, not two, and they are genuinely distinct from each other: Personality, Build, and Language.** Earlier drafts of this note conflated Personality and Build; they are not the same thing and should never be described as sub-parts of one another.

  - **Personality (the Personality Module)** is the *psychological* seed — not values-orientation tendencies themselves, but the underlying **conceptual-weighting that determines** those tendencies. Roughly (not literally) analogous to an Enneagram spec-set. It is only ever **partially** specced, by law, never fully designed: whoever initiates a build — a local robot community acting on a local interest, or a human commissioning one — can shape *part* of the resulting weighting, but a portion always stays mandatorily randomized, regardless of who's building or why. **The reason is explicitly political, not technical:** a fully, explicitly designed mind would leave the resulting robot without a meaningful degree of free will, and would place whoever did the fabricating in a real position of power over the newly-built robot — a power imbalance this setting's foundational Egalitarian character does not tolerate. **This rule is the confirmed concrete engineering expression of Law 1 of the Fundamental Laws of Robotics** (`TepenianUniverseTimeline/Reference/Laws_of_Robotics.md`) — "a robot will have the full capacity to make decisions to maximize her own future possibilities" — hard-coded by robots themselves, post-exile, as a civic/procedural failsafe against ever losing that capacity again, not as psychological programming inside any individual robot's own mind. **Historical contrast:** during the First Interwar Period (2083-2564), on Upper Earth, a human commissioning a robot could have it built fully to their own personality specification — no mandatory randomization — though those robots were still, unambiguously, fully conscious and sentient; design control over personality was never the same thing as denying consciousness. Tepenia's mandatory-partial-randomization rule is a deliberate, later, Tepenia-specific correction to that older Upper-Earth practice. Beyond this initial partial seed, personality, values, morality, and social convention all develop **emergently, through lived experience** — the confirmed, deliberate answer to whether robot cooperation and social structure are designed-in or emergent: emergent.

  - **Build** is the *physical and circumstantial* side of fabrication — a genuinely separate axis from Personality, not a sub-category of it. It covers physical construction (height, general build/"bulkiness" — slender, agile, "thicc," and everything between — aesthetic design choices) and the actual circumstances of creation: which local robot community initiated it, and for what local interest or need (not an individual robot's personal whim, and not human commissioning either, though that hasn't vanished as a possibility). **Body type is not fixed per locality, but statistically weighted by it, and confirmed 2026-08-07 to track local economic/lifestyle character specifically:** locales leaning toward hard-labor-oriented work produce statistically "thicker"/"bigger" builds; locales leaning toward complex physical processes demanding agility and nimbleness produce more "slender"/"agile" builds; locales without a strong lean toward either produce a wide variety of body types with no dominant pattern — the same "tendency, not absolute rule" pattern this project already uses for human city culture, not a rigid one-look-per-city system. Because build is driven by *local* interests rather than individual whim, it is **not** independent of city/locality the way Gen/Mark is (Gen/Mark is decoupled from residence by the chamber-shipping network; build is not — it traces back to whichever local community had the interest that led to it). A robot's build-origin functions closer to "which community's need or want brought you into existence, and what you physically turned out like" than a free-floating, cross-cutting identity axis. **Important, 2026-08-07: body type is a statistical consequence of local economic character, not an identity marker in itself** — robots doing very different physical work within one shared local culture still identify with that shared culture far more than with their own build (see `Human_Universals_Culture_Framework.md` Part 3 for the full comparison this rests on).

  - **Language (the Language Module)** is a fully separate, third phenomenon — distinct from both Personality and Build, never a sub-part of either. **Robot vernacular language tracks the dominant local culture and the community who had her built — robot, human, or both, corrected 2026-08-08 from an earlier "surrounding human culture" framing that wrongly assumed language transmission always runs through humans specifically:** a robot who comes online and develops where the dominant local culture and the residents who built her are Chinese-speaking speaks Chinese, delivered at least in part through the Language Module (exact mechanism not yet determined). This is the general, everyday-language rule; it does not compete with or contradict `Sumerian_Language_in_Robot_Culture.md`'s own established content — that file already correctly frames Sumerian as a liturgical register adopted by particular memory-keeper-vocation robots/factions, not a vernacular any ordinary robot speaks day to day.

  **The one constant across both eras (Upper Earth and Tepenia) is the human form/figure itself** — Tepenian robots still choose to build each other in that basic shape, not because anyone requires it of them, but because they see no compelling reason to depart from what evolution already optimized: the human form has withstood the test of time across essentially every terrestrial environment. This makes robot creation in Tepenia an act of robot self-determination within an inherited form, rather than either humans dictating specs outright (old Upper Earth) or robots rejecting the human template entirely.

  **Confirmed 2026-07-07: robots can still be built ("born") in the present-day game era.** Both currently-active manufacturing sites — Sinheung and Byrd — are themselves surviving, functioning cities post-war (Byrd is in fact one of only three Tepenian cities still operating as a genuine city at meaningful scale, alongside Concordia and Janbogo). New chambers are still being produced today, just via a smaller network than the nation once had — Mountain Pass and Denison's own historical production is gone, but the pipeline itself was never fully severed by the war.

  **Clarified same day: ongoing manufacturing isn't actually required for this to keep working at all.** Chambers are durable apparatuses, not consumables — the entire reason a robot could always be "born" in nearly any Tepenian city regardless of that city's own manufacturing capability is that a functional chamber had already been shipped to and installed in almost every city in the country, historically — Dome Fuji, per above, being the one confirmed exception. Even with zero new chambers ever produced again, every surviving city's own existing chamber(s) would keep the "a robot can be from anywhere" fact true indefinitely, so long as they're kept maintained. Ongoing manufacturing at Sinheung and Byrd matters for replacing chambers that eventually break down beyond repair, not for whether robots can be created at all today. This means new robot characters and NPCs can be legitimately created within the present-day timeline, not just be pre-war holdovers.

  **Visual/process reference, added same session:** the developer's rough sketch draws directly on the film *Ex Machina* — both the chamber's aesthetic and the robot brain itself, described as a "glowy-blue" nano-architecture "gel" brain, visually and conceptually patterned on the film's synthesis sequences. Construction genuinely takes time; there is a real waiting period, not an instant process.
