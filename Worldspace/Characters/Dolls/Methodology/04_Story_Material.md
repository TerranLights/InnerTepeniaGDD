# Character Development Methodology — Stage 4: Story Material

**Pipeline position:** converting a finished Character Data profile (Stage 3) into actual story scaffolding —
role, supporting cast, thematic shape — before any beat-by-beat work (Stage 5) starts.

**Status:** complete (2026-08-09) — consolidation pass, a full worked example (Calethina) validating the stage
end-to-end and surfacing one concrete design gap (a missing Tempter figure), and the Player-Necessity Rule
synthesized from this project's already-mature, in-production Personal Questline Design Rule. No open items
remaining.

---

## The Front-Gate / Sequencing Trio

Not a merge — three calibration tools answering different questions at different points, run in order:

1. **MICE Quotient** (Card: Milieu / Idea / Character / Event) — asked *first*: what kind of story or
   Under-Questline is this? This sets the depth ceiling before any character work starts. A Milieu- or
   Event-type thread doesn't need (and shouldn't be forced to carry) full companion-grade characterization.
2. **Character Hierarchy** (Card: walk-on / minor / major) — asked *second*, once MICE confirms "Character"
   mode genuinely applies: how much narrative weight does *this specific* character carry? Walk-ons disappear
   into stereotype on purpose; minor characters get one of three tools (eccentricity, exaggeration,
   obsessiveness) deployed once, briefly; major status is signaled through eight independent, controllable
   levers (strangeness, narrative time, choice-weight, others' Focus on her, frequency, active involvement,
   audience sympathy, POV weight) — not raw characterization depth.
3. **Story Problem vs. Character Problem** (Seger) — the troubleshooting fallback, run only when something
   still isn't working after 1 and 2 were done correctly: if a character is genuinely necessary and the
   surrounding story is sound, why wouldn't she come alive? If she only exists to mechanically service the plot
   ("walk in, throw a switch, get off"), the fix is structural — replace her narrative function, or admit the
   story doesn't need her to be a full character at all — not more added quirks or backstory.

## Consolidated Node (genuine merge)

**NODE: Contagonist** — merges Weiland's **Contagonist** (Dramatica) and Corbett's **Counterweight**. Both name
the identical function: someone close to the protagonist who pulls her backward toward the status quo or an old
trap, in direct tension with whoever's pulling her forward, and — critically, in both sources independently —
**this role does not have to be villainous.** Canonical name: **Contagonist**, since it's the more formally
grounded term (borrowed from established Dramatica theory, with the "devil-and-angel-on-the-shoulder" framing
already attached). Corbett's specific nuance folds in as an attached refinement: the role can simply represent
the legitimate emotional pull of tradition, loyalty, or normalcy, with neither the Contagonist nor whoever she's
opposing being clearly "correct."

## Kept Deliberately Separate (a near-merge that turned out not to be one)

**Impact Character** (Weiland) and **Revenant** (Corbett) look, at first pass, like the same function under two
names — both are close, sustained-proximity figures who catalyze a companion's inner change. On closer
inspection they're defined by genuinely different mechanisms, and merging them loses real information:

- **Impact Character** is defined by **worldview contrast** — she embodies the Truth, and the friction against
  the protagonist's Lie comes from opposing worldviews, not from mirroring anything in the protagonist's past.
  Weiland explicitly allows this function to be filled by a collective (her own example: the whole town of
  Radiator Springs in *Cars*).
- **Revenant** is defined by **recurrence** — she's specifically whoever forces the companion to confront "that
  *same* unresolved problem *again*, now," a present-day echo of the original Ghost. Corbett is explicit that
  the Revenant should almost always be an actual person, not a diffuse situation or group — a direct boundary-
  condition conflict with Weiland's collective-Impact-Character option, not just a difference in emphasis.

A Revenant doesn't have to be Truth-aligned to do her job — she can simply be a new source of the same pressure
the original wound came from, without herself modeling anything healthier (a new authority figure making the
identical demand the companion's original abuser made, for instance). **This is exactly where Revenant shades
toward antagonist-adjacent territory that belongs to
`Character_Development_Methodology_-_Villains_and_Antiheroes_-_DRAFT_Ideas.md` rather than this file** — a
non-Truth-aligned Revenant is functionally closer to a Contagonist or an outright antagonist than to an Impact
Character, and should be built using that sheet's material (the Predatory Villain subtype, the Sadist/Bully
distinction) when that's the shape she's taking.

**Practical use:** treat these as two separate, composable slots, not one function with two names.
- The *strongest* single-character version does both jobs at once — Corbett's own recommendation is that this
  role is "often most powerful when the character also helps with the protagonist's external, plot-level goal,"
  which is exactly the convergence case where a Revenant also happens to embody the Truth.
- They can also legitimately split across two different characters (a Revenant who tests without teaching, and
  a separate Impact Character who teaches without any connection to the original wound's specific shape) — or
  one can be present without the other (a Truth-embodying collective Impact Character with no single Revenant
  at all).

## The Player-Necessity Rule and Related Constraints

Synthesized 2026-08-09 from `Companion_System.md`'s already-established, battle-tested "Personal Questline
Design Rule" and a read-through of the actual companion questline files already built on it (Favi della Torre,
Vosora Lashár Tanslock, Ayako Hayashi, and others). This is mature, in-production methodology this project
already runs on for personal-questline design — not new theory, but not yet reflected anywhere in this
methodology's Stage 4 until now. It sharpens and mechanizes the Impact Character/Revenant material immediately
above into an explicit, checkable design rule with real teeth.

- **The core rule**: every companion's personal questline should hinge on something the *player specifically*
  can do that the companion cannot — not because she's generally incapable, but because of a genuine categorical
  exclusion (wrong architecture, no access, barred by her own history or institutional position). The
  companion's own resources — competence, faction, connections — get her most of the way; the last, personal
  step has to be something only the player could have done for her. Without this, the player is reduced to a
  bystander watching the companion's own faction or skills solve her problem off-screen.
- **The categorical-block sanity check**: confirm the exclusion is real before designing how the player
  succeeds at it. Favi della Torre's questline runs this explicitly — she has Investigation 10, higher than most
  approaches would need, but the check holds anyway because she structurally cannot jack into a post-Split-Brain-
  fractured Arcanet node at all; no stat of hers changes that. **If a companion could in principle also clear the
  threshold given enough time or luck, the "exclusion" isn't one — it's just the player being better at
  something she could also, eventually, do herself, which collapses the whole rule.** Run this check explicitly
  before treating any categorical block as settled.
- **The compounding-reasons technique**: a single categorical reason doesn't have to carry the whole weight
  alone. Vosora Lashár Tanslock's block stacks three independent reasons (dividing focus risks the recovery
  itself; confronting the pressure openly would tip off exactly what she's trying to keep hidden; and — the
  reason that actually matters most — doing so would destroy the institutional credibility every one of her
  possible endings depends on). When one reason feels thin on its own, look for a second and third that
  independently support the same exclusion rather than forcing a single weak justification to do all the work.
- **The no-escort-quest constraint** (a binding structural rule, established via Ji-Eun Kim's design): cost,
  difficulty, and vulnerability fall on the *player*, never on the companion as an NPC who has to be kept alive
  through the mechanism that resolves her own questline. Worth treating as a standing constraint on any
  personal-questline mechanism this stage produces, not just a stylistic preference.
- **Retrofit discipline**: when revising an already-built questline's mechanism rather than designing one fresh,
  scope the change to exactly what needs fixing and say explicitly what's *not* being touched. Both Favi's and
  Vosora's retrofits replaced only the antagonist/access mechanism while leaving already-locked material (Favi's
  full ending set, Vosora's existing Dual-Outcome Perk structure) untouched and explicitly noted as such — this
  stops a targeted fix from silently drifting into an unplanned full redesign.

## Standalone Tools

- **The Normal World as symbolic dramatization of the Lie** (Weiland) — the opening world/circumstances actively
  built to reinforce the Lie, not just contain the character; the contrast with wherever her questline takes her
  should be maximized, not softened.
- **The Characteristic Moment** (Weiland) — the master first-scene checklist, now explicitly absorbing two
  looser versions that were left orphaned in earlier passes:
  - Boutros's **Four-Fact Opening** — reveal a minimum of ~4 distinct facts about her, spread across different
    categories (physical, personal, social, familial, societal, hobby), within her first meaningful scene,
    rather than concentrated in one kind of detail or delivered as exposition.
  - Alderson's **Strength/Longing/Fear staging rule** — establish all three quickly, in the same early scene(s),
    rather than leading with wound/flaw alone; Fear specifically should be *hinted*, not fully displayed, in an
    opening (save its full weight for the Black Moment in Stage 5).
- **Antagonist vs. Antagonistic Force** (Weiland) — the concrete-agent vs. abstract-obstacle distinction, worth
  naming both deliberately for any significant antagonist (a Dragon as antagonist standing in for the
  antagonistic force of "stagnation," for instance).
- **The Twelve Archetypal Antagonists** (Weiland — six outer/inner pairs mapped to the six Life Arcs) — a
  pre-built content library for giving a companion's arc a concretely-shaped opponent, including the
  forward-looking technique of drawing an antagonist from the *next* arc-stage's aggressive shadow rather than
  the protagonist's own stage.
- **Four-Corner Opposition & the Character Web** (Truby) — the general-purpose roster/rival design method: a
  companion plus three others pushed to the corners, each attacking her core weakness differently, each with a
  distinct cluster of values in conflict with the others'.
- **The Four Elements of Relationship Sizzle** (Seger) — the one dedicated pair-design checklist in the file:
  something genuinely in common, a real conflict, contrasting qualities, and mutual-transformation potential.
- **Corbett's remaining functional-role catalogue** — Crucial Ally (including the "remorseless sidekick"
  sub-pattern, who performs morally compromising violence so the protagonist can retain a clean moral center),
  Betrayer and Sympathetic Heavy (a betrayal only lands if real trust was established first; the Sympathetic
  Heavy is its inverse — aligned with the opponent but risking exposure to help the protagonist anyway), Visitor
  and Stranger (an outside-perspective character who makes an established setting feel freshly strange), and the
  Village (the diffuse surrounding cast whose accumulated small demands and minor betrayals carry weight no
  single major relationship can).

## Composability Notes (orthogonal systems — cross-reference, not merge)

- **Corbett's functional-role catalogue × Weiland's Six Flat Archetypes.** A character's *function* (Crucial
  Ally, Contagonist, Betrayer, etc.) and her *life-stage content* (Child/Lover/Parent/Ruler/Elder/Mentor) are
  independent axes, not competing systems — any functional role can be cast using any Flat Archetype's content.
  A Mentor-stage Crucial Ally reads differently from a Parent-stage one, and both are valid.
- **Truby's Four-Corner Opposition × Weiland's Twelve Archetypal Antagonists.** The general method (any four
  characters, pushed to the corners) and the pre-built content library (specific named pairs with ready-made
  thematic freight) aren't competing — Weiland's pre-built pairs can fill one or two of Truby's "corners," with
  the remainder built via Truby's general method when nothing pre-built fits.

## Newly Surfaced During This Pass — Not in the Original Scaffold List

Flagging honestly rather than silently treating the first scaffold pass as complete: two staging/sympathy
techniques from material already fully read but filed nowhere in Stages 1-5 originally.

- **Card's general sympathy-lever catalogue** (the non-villain half of "Characters We Love/Characters We Hate")
  — Altruism calibrated by role (Victim/Savior/Sacrifice, each with a specific way to misfire), visible Plan and
  Purpose, Courage paired with Fair Play, the **Draftee-or-Volunteer rule** (volunteering for a thankless task
  earns sympathy, being forced into it reads as weak — and the reverse for a glory-bringing task), Dependability
  (a costly kept promise is one of the strongest reusable sympathy engines in fiction), Cleverness without
  displayed Intelligence, and Endearing Imperfections. The chapter's antagonist-specific half (Sadist/Bully,
  Self-Appointed/Usurper Rule, Assassin-vs-Avenger, Understandable Villain) stays correctly cross-referenced to
  the villain/anti-hero supplement sheet rather than reproduced here.
- **Boutros's cat-save and delay-the-worst-act staging techniques** (from "Making a Hard-to-Like Character
  Likeable") — an early, authentic act of unearned kindness near a character's first appearance, and holding
  back a morally compromising act until the audience already has real investment in her, are both staging
  decisions about *when in the story material* certain beats land, not character-data fields.

## Worked Example: Calethina

Built 2026-08-09, closing the item that was previously open here. Chosen specifically for being an atypical
case — not a normal companion (no party slot, holographic, exempt from Companion Slot Rules) — to genuinely
stress-test whether these tools flex to an unusual character rather than just confirming an easy one. Everything
below is checked against her actual `README.md`; where this exercise generated a new interpretation rather than
restating something already confirmed in her file, it's flagged explicitly as such, per this project's own
standing discipline against silently inventing canon.

1. **MICE Quotient (Card).** Her *personal* arc — as distinct from her narrator/exploration-hook function, which
   is genuinely Milieu/Event-type texture serving the wider game — is unambiguously **Character**-type: a real
   intolerable-role problem (fragmented, single point of failure, silently causing the player's own identity
   changes without full acknowledgment) that only resolves through her own change. This warrants the full
   Lie/Want/Need/Ghost treatment, confirmed by how much of that machinery is already present in her
   documentation independent of this methodology: the double-wound structure (a deliberate wipe layered under an
   accidental one), the Bond/Grief escalation across repeated re-specs, and the tragic irony of amnesia about her
   own heroic act (she personally created the Triage Protocol and doesn't know it).

2. **Character Hierarchy (Card).** **Major** — clears essentially all eight of Card's levers: romanceable,
   highest Personality Grief-Multiplier of any character, a dedicated perk set, a full Accomplishment Weight
   System tie-in, multiple ending branches, and she's literally the game's narrator (maximum POV/narration
   weight). Per Stage 3's tier table, Major Hierarchy should pair with a full Drive/major-tier Character Data
   profile — that checks out; nothing about her established material reads as Drift.

3. **The Characteristic Moment.** Her canonical opening (activating the player at her lab) hits several checklist
   beats — name, distinctive physicality, narrative role — but deliberately withholds any hint of her Lie, which
   stays hidden until her Romance questline specifically, per already-confirmed canon ("this truth is uncovered
   over the course of her Romance questline specifically"). This is Weiland's "gradual reveal" Ghost-timing
   option already in active use, without anyone having framed it that way before. **Genuinely useful find**: her
   signal-dependent visual degradation — "her visual quality is a direct, literal readout of world/grid health" —
   is a real, pre-existing instance of the Mystery Caution principle from Stage 2 (full resolution in the design
   notes, delivered only through soft ambient detail — here technical/visual rather than lifestyle detail, but
   the identical mechanism). Worth citing as a concrete illustration wherever that principle gets referenced
   again.

4. **Impact Character / Revenant / Contagonist casting.** **New interpretation, not yet canon**: a plausible Lie,
   built from her established material rather than stated anywhere in her own files — *"I am only worth keeping
   around out of necessity, not because I'm genuinely wanted for who I am."* This tracks precisely onto the
   already-documented Devotion (relationship born from choice) vs. Accord (relationship born from necessity)
   ending distinction. Under this reading, **the player is cast as both Revenant and Impact Character
   simultaneously** — the strongest-convergence case Corbett flagged as ideal: every re-spec re-stages her core
   wound (being repurposed without full agency, echoing the Fort McMurdo wipe), while the choice to pursue
   Devotion is the only thing that can actually disprove the Lie. **No Contagonist candidate exists in her
   current material** — flagged as an open gap this exercise surfaced, not filled in.

5. **Antagonist selection.** Her personal arc maps unusually well onto the **Crone Arc** (an already-formed
   identity stripped by catastrophe, facing down instability rather than growing into power) — a closer fit than
   any of the other five Life Arcs. Its antagonist pair, **Death Blight and Tempter**, both have natural fits:
   the Death Blight maps directly onto her own Fragmenting → Unstable → Critical progression, already a tracked
   mechanic via the Fragmentation Matrix. **No Tempter figure currently exists in her documentation** — the one
   concrete design suggestion this test run generated rather than confirmed: a figure or temptation offering her
   a way to avoid the real vulnerability of embodiment (staying safely non-corporeal forever) would be a natural,
   currently-missing piece, worth considering if her arc gets built out further.

**What this test run actually validated:** the Stage 3/Stage 4 tier consistency holds (Major Hierarchy correctly
implies a full Character Data profile, not a lighter one); the Characteristic Moment's gradual-reveal option and
the Mystery Caution both show up naturally in material that predates this whole methodology, which is a real
independent confirmation rather than a self-fulfilling check; and the antagonist-selection tool produced one
concrete, actionable design gap (the missing Tempter) rather than just rubber-stamping what already existed —
which is the actual point of running a worked example instead of only consolidating vocabulary.
