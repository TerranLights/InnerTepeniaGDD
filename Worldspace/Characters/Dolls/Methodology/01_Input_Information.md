# Character Development Methodology — Stage 1: Input Information

**Pipeline position:** all the information that's required at the very beginning, before any processing starts —
everything that has to be true, known, or gathered about a Doll before Stage 2's diagnostic toolkit can
meaningfully be run on her. Built 2026-08-09 by tracing every single node, tool, and worked-example dependency
established across Stages 2, 3, 4, and 5 back to its raw input requirement — every field below is justified by
naming the specific downstream consumer that needs it, not asserted in the abstract.

**Status:** complete (2026-08-09). Two sections: **Section A** (necessary — required to populate the nodes in
Stages 2 through 5), and **Section B** (optional — not required by any specific downstream node, but genuinely
enriching to the material those nodes produce).

---

# SECTION A — NECESSARY FIELDS

Every field in this section is required because a specific, named tool or node in Stage 2, 3, 4, or 5 cannot be
run, or cannot be run well, without it. This is the actual minimum viable input set for a full, major-tier
character-development pass.

---

## A1. Foundational Identity Facts

The bedrock facts that have to exist before any diagnostic work can even be framed.

- **Name.** Feeds Card's Ten Ways We Come to Know a Character (Stage 3) directly — a name is one of the ten
  named delivery channels, and the DRAFT file itself flags a companion's name as "one of the most consequential
  characterization decisions," capable of substituting for physical description entirely if chosen well, or
  denying an easy mental image if deliberately plain. Also required before the Characteristic Moment (Stage 4)
  can be written, since revealing her name is one of its checklist items.
- **Robot or human status.** Governs which parts of the Robot-Universal Friction Bank (below, A6) even apply —
  human companions don't have a Personality Module, a Gen/Mark identity, or the same integrity/mortality
  threshold a robot does, so this single fact gates a large fraction of what Stage 2's diagnostic material is
  even relevant.
- **Gen/Mark identity, if robot.** Directly required for the "outlier Gen/Mark identity" friction point (Robot
  Universals Ch.14, part of the Robot-Universal Friction Bank) to be usable at all — without knowing her own
  generation and manufacturing batch, there's nothing to check her against her city or peer group for.
- **Age or apparent age.** Feeds the Background Taxonomy's Body sub-category (Stage 3) and is one of Card's Ten
  Ways (demographic basics, part of the Characteristic Moment checklist).
- **City, district, and subnet of origin.** Feeds the Background Taxonomy's Environment sub-category (Stage 3)
  directly, and is required before any of the Robot-Universal Friction Bank's "city/locality belonging" material
  (Ch.13) can be applied. Also required before the Trigger-Type Design Pattern (Stage 5) can be built, since a
  companion's trigger type has to be thematically derived from her own established nature — which usually
  includes where she's from.
- **Current residence and location, if different from origin.** A Doll who has relocated has a distinct
  Environment input from one who hasn't; also directly needed for the Normal World (Stage 4), which is
  specifically the *current* physical/social circumstances actively dramatizing the Lie, not necessarily her
  birthplace.
- **Physical appearance and Reference Images.** Feeds the Body sub-category of the Background Taxonomy (Stage
  3), and is required for the Characteristic Moment's physical-characteristics checklist item (Stage 4). Per
  this project's own established practice, companions ARE their Reference_Images doll folders — this is not
  optional flavor, it's the actual visual source of truth.
- **Occupation, social role, or established function.** Feeds Card's Ten Ways (Talents and Abilities), the
  Strength standalone field (Stage 3 — "what is she good at, apart from what hurts her" requires knowing what
  she does), and is frequently the anchor for her Want's General Goal (Stage 3).

## A2. Enneagram Assignment

- **Type, wing, and instinct/subtype.** Explicitly mandatory, not optional, per Stage 3's own Fields-That-Stay-
  Standalone section: "already load-bearing project-wide infrastructure... not optional, not one option among
  equals." This has to exist *before* Stage 2 processing starts, since the Enneagram-Rooted Contradiction
  principle (from the main DRAFT file) requires inner conflict to be *derived from* the type's own structural
  core-fear/core-desire tension, never invented independently of it — meaning Stage 2's diagnostic tools are
  actually being run *against* this assignment, not used to produce it.

---

## A3. Intended Story Role and Scope

Design-level facts about what kind of content this Doll is meant to carry, established before any processing —
distinct from the Stage 3 Front-Gate (Drift vs. Drive) and Stage 4 Front-Gate (MICE Quotient, Character
Hierarchy) *judgments*, which are diagnostic conclusions informed by these facts, not raw inputs themselves.

- **Intended content tier** — is she meant to be a full recruitable companion, a Majyao-pattern
  romanceable-but-non-recruitable fixture, a district Notable Figure, a minor NPC, or a walk-on? This is the
  single most load-bearing Stage 1 fact for calibrating how much of the rest of this methodology to actually
  run — it's the input the Stage 3 Front-Gate and Stage 4 Front-Gate trio are both making their determination
  from.
- **Base game vs. DLC placement, and whether she recurs across multiple installments.** Required before the
  Series-Spanning Arc Models (Stage 5) can be applied — the choice between "one overarching arc across the whole
  series" and "a fresh arc per installment" can't be made without first knowing whether she appears in more than
  one place at all.
- **Any already-known MICE Quotient leaning**, if the concept for her is already skewed toward one factor (a
  Milieu-heavy archive-narrator, an Event-driven crisis figure, a pure Character study) — not required to be
  fully resolved at Stage 1, since the MICE Quotient determination itself is Stage 4's job, but if this is
  already obvious from the initial concept, recording it here saves re-deriving it later.

## A4. Existing Biographical and Historical Material

The single most input-hungry category — this directly feeds the Ghost node (Stage 3), which the Ghost's own
entry describes as needing "the past wound that explains why she believes the Lie," and a wound needs an actual
history to be drawn from.

- **Origin, construction, or activation history.** For a robot companion, this includes any established
  construction chain (which cities were involved in her design, engineering, manufacture, shipping — following
  the same pattern already established for Calethina's nine-stage chain) and the circumstances of her
  activation. For a human companion, birthplace and early-life circumstances serve the same function.
- **Her position relative to the Long Night War and the wider project timeline.** Did she exist before, during,
  or only after the war? Was she directly caught up in it, or does she only know it as history? This is a
  primary source of Ghost material across this project's existing companion roster and needs to be established
  before Ghost construction starts, not discovered mid-process.
- **Any already-documented trauma, loss, or hardship.** Whatever's already on record — a specific documented
  incident, not a vague sense that "something bad probably happened." Directly feeds Ghost, and should be
  cross-checked against the Five Fundamental Types of Death (Social, Spiritual, Psychological, Professional,
  Physical) to identify which type, if any, is already in play before inventing a new one.
- **Existing relationships**, itemized by category, since each category feeds a different downstream need:
  - **Family or creator** — for a robot, whoever built or activated her; for a human, her family of origin. Both
    are frequent Ghost sources and frequent Impact Character/Revenant candidates (Stage 4).
  - **Mentors** — directly required for the Robot-Universal Friction Bank's "mentor/mentee bond lost, betrayed,
    or never had" friction point (Robot Universals Ch.11), and a common source for the Flat Archetype casting
    (the Mentor/Elder/Ruler slots, Stage 4's Composability Notes).
  - **Siblings or generation-mates** — relevant to Gen/Mark identity (A1, above) and to Corbett's already-
    captured sibling material (who's an ally, who's a competitor for status).
  - **Rivals** — direct input for Four-Corner Opposition (Stage 4) and for casting a same-stage antagonist
    pairing (the Twelve Archetypal Antagonists, Stage 4).
  - **Friends** — direct input for the Village (Stage 4) and for Card's Network delivery channel (Stage 3).
  - **Romantic history**, if any exists prior to the player. Feeds the Lie/Flaw node directly for romanceable
    companions via the "I can't love him because ___" quick-fill variant, and is required context before the
    Four Elements of Relationship Sizzle (Stage 4) can be built for her eventual player-facing romance arc.
- **Any existing established perks or mechanical hooks tied to her backstory.** Where a companion already has
  bespoke mechanics on record (as with Calethina's re-spec provider role, her Fragmentation Matrix exception, her
  Direct Participation Count), these need to be known before Stage 2 processing starts, since the whole
  processing pass has to remain consistent with mechanics that are already locked, not invent psychology that
  contradicts them.

## A5. Existing Mechanical and Surface Data

Explicitly distinguished by Stage 3's own file header from the deep psychology layer this methodology produces —
but still required as Stage 1 input, since the deep layer has to remain consistent with what's already
mechanically true about her.

- **`Character_Spec_Fill-In_Sheet_Template.md` contents**, if already filled in for this Doll.
- **`Companion_and_Romance_Questline_Fill-In_Sheet_Template.md` contents**, if already filled in.
- **MACHINE stats**, if already assigned (Calculation, Investigation, Nerve, Engine, Humanity, and any others
  established for this project). A companion's psychology should read as plausible given her mechanical build —
  a high-Humanity, low-Nerve companion implies a different Coping/Defense Mechanism tier (Stage 3) than the
  reverse.
- **Any already-assigned Perks, Traits, or Skills.** Same reasoning as MACHINE stats — the psychology has to be
  consistent with, not contradictory to, mechanics already locked in.
- **Any existing romance-gate design**, if one is already sketched (a MACHINE-stat threshold, a conduct-based
  gate in the style of Kendra Heinrich or Calethina's redesigned gate, or an as-yet-undecided approach). Required
  before the Ending-Shape Cross-Mapping Table (Stage 5) can be fully applied to her, since romance eligibility
  interacts directly with the Ending Distribution law's Double Gate.

## A6. World-Context Reference Access

Not per-Doll data — existing project canon that has to be *accessible* (read, cross-referenced) before Stage 2's
diagnostic tools can meaningfully draw from it. Listed here as a checklist of what to have open/available, not
material to be newly written per Doll.

- **Robot Universals**, specifically the six chapters that make up the Robot-Universal Friction Bank: Ch.4
  (mortality/integrity threshold), Ch.5 (Personality Module tension), Ch.10 and Ch.17 (trust-bond romance
  stakes), Ch.11 (mentor/mentee bonds), Ch.13 (city/locality belonging), Ch.14 (Gen/Mark identity). Required
  before the Enneagram-Rooted Contradiction and Robot-Universal Friction Bank candidate mechanisms (both cited
  directly in the main DRAFT file) can be applied to a robot companion.
- **The relevant District Megasheet(s)** for her city, district, and subnet of origin (and any other district
  she's meaningfully tied to). Required for Background Taxonomy's Environment sub-category, and — per the
  Calethina worked example's own explicit finding — required before the Trigger-Type Design Pattern's actual
  district-specific triggers can be designed at all (the Second Pinch Point and the specific trigger content for
  Calethina's own system were both left open specifically because this context wasn't yet available).
- **Historical Pressures documents** for her relevant district(s). A common source of Ghost material tied to
  place, and required context for whether her city's own founding-nation bugs, tier-ordering anomalies, or
  historical mismatches are relevant to her personal history.
- **Community Infrastructure documents** for her relevant district(s). Feeds the Environment sub-category and
  gives concrete texture for the Village (Stage 4).
- **Relevant faction or religious canon**, if applicable — any of the five established robot religions
  (Polydimensional Animism and the others), or a national ideology she holds. Directly required for the Ideas
  sub-category of the Background Taxonomy (Stage 3), which is explicitly flagged as project-relevant given "this
  project's unusually rich religious/philosophical landscape."
- **Robot Biology and Culture canon** (siligel, coolant type, robot coffee, glitch-coolant, smoking), for robot
  companions. Feeds the Body sub-category and is directly useful raw material for the Mystery Caution's
  soft-detail delivery mechanism (Stage 2) — what she drinks, what her routines look like.
- **The Enneagram_Dynamics.md reference** (and, once built, the planned `Worldspace/Enneagram/` deep-dive
  folder). Required to actually apply the A2 Enneagram assignment correctly rather than just naming a type
  number.

## A7. Standing Design-Law Constraints

Not per-Doll information — the fixed rules every downstream node's output has to obey, needed as context before
any generative work starts so nothing gets built that later has to be discarded for violating an existing law.

- **No Good Endings / Ending Distribution law** (`Companion_System.md`) — minority-negative, majority-
  bittersweet, costly-positive; the mutual-sacrifice mechanic. Required before the Ending-Shape Cross-Mapping
  Table (Stage 5) can be applied without contradicting canon.
- **The Double Gate and Romance-lock rule** (`Companion_System.md`) — a negative Companion-questline ending
  permanently disqualifies Romance. Required for the same reason as above.
- **No National Stereotypes.** Directly constrains the Background Taxonomy's Environment and Ideas sub-
  categories, and specifically constrains how the Trigger-Type Design Pattern's district-bound triggers get
  built (flagged explicitly in Stage 5 regarding Mordred's Cornwall-shorthand technique from the villain/
  anti-hero sheet — this project's own version of that technique must stay built entirely from invented in-
  fiction reputation, never real-world stereotyping).
- **No Level-Scaling.** Not a direct character-psychology concern, but a standing constraint on any downstream
  content (encounters, loot, difficulty) this Doll's questline eventually generates.
- **The Dual-Outcome Companion Perk law** (`Companion_System.md`) — 2-5 mutually exclusive branches per
  questline, genuine trade-offs. Required before Stage 5's branching beat structure can be built, since it's the
  mechanical skeleton that structure has to serve.
- **The Cross-DLC Bypass Design Law**, if this Doll is confirmed to recur across DLCs (see A3, above). Required
  before Series-Spanning Arc Models (Stage 5) can be applied to her specifically.
- **The Standing Scope Note** (main DRAFT file) — every beat is defined by functional role, never by fixed
  percentage-of-runtime placement; this is the entire reason the Midpoint Menu (Stage 5) exists as a menu of
  interchangeable mechanics rather than a single canonical beat.

## A8. The Writer's Own Priming Answers

- **The writer's own answers to the Mirror Interview's fixed question set** (happiest memory, saddest memory, a
  time of genuine shame, a belief that regularly creates conflict and where it comes from, a time of being
  emotionally hurt) — gathered *before* Stage 2's Self-to-Character Memory Mining technique is run on the
  character. This is explicitly a Stage 1 input-gathering step even though its purpose is to prime Stage 2's
  interrogation: Boutros's own technique requires the self-pass to happen first, to prime honest, non-generic
  answers for the character pass that follows.

## A9. Existing Narrative Introduction Context

- **Where, when, and how the player first meets her, if this is already planned or already exists in-game** (as
  with Calethina's canonical opening at her Activation Lab). Required before the Characteristic Moment (Stage 4)

  without knowing what that scene actually is.

## A10. Existing Supporting Cast and Relationship Web

- **A named list of every character already established as connected to her** — not just the categorized
  relationships already covered in A4 (family, mentors, rivals, friends), but the full roster of who exists in
  her orbit, regardless of category. Required before Impact Character, Revenant, and Contagonist casting (Stage
  4) can be done properly, since the strongest version of that casting draws directly from her *already-
  existing* relationships rather than inventing new figures wholesale — exactly as the Calethina worked example
  did, casting the already-established player-character relationship as both Revenant and Impact Character
  rather than introducing a new figure.

---

## A11. Existing Questline and Ending Concepts

- **Any already-drafted ending ideas, branch concepts, or Dual-Outcome Perk outcomes**, even in rough or
  contradictory form (as with Calethina's three not-yet-reconciled ending concepts — the embodiment branches,
  the Furthest Signal, and the Pariah Accord). Required before the Ending-Shape Cross-Mapping Table (Stage 5)
  can be applied, and required so that this methodology's own output doesn't silently contradict or duplicate
  work that already exists.
- **Any already-established structural timing facts about her arc**, such as a decision point already locked to
  occur "at the midpoint" or any other named beat. The Calethina worked example's Midpoint determination worked
  specifically because this fact already existed in her README before the methodology touched it — this is
  exactly the kind of pre-existing structural anchor Stage 5 needs to check for before inventing a new one.

---

## A12. Existing Memory and Prior Design Notes

- **A check of project memory and any prior session notes specific to this Doll**, before starting. Given this

  gaps, hypotheses, or open questions on record that this pass should build on rather than silently rediscover

# SECTION B — OPTIONAL, ENRICHING FIELDS

Nothing below is required by any specific node or tool named in Stages 2 through 5 — the pipeline is fully
runnable without any of it. But each item genuinely enriches the material those nodes and tools produce, and is
worth gathering when available or when time allows.

---

## B1. Soft-Detail Delivery Material

Directly enriches the Mystery Caution (Stage 2) — full resolution belongs in private design notes, delivered in
actual media only through soft ambient detail, and this material is exactly what fills that delivery channel.

- **Consumption habits and vice preferences** — favorite drink (per this project's own robot-alcohol-equivalent
  canon, glitch-coolant, or robot coffee, or a human companion's equivalent), specific coolant type if relevant,
  smoking habits if any.
- **Daily routines and small habitual gestures** — the kind of detail St. John's *Longmire* case study
  demonstrates (a makeshift shower, scattered beer cans, a specific way of handling a piece of equipment) that
  characterizes without a word of exposition.
- **Clothing and personal style choices**, beyond what's captured in the Reference Images themselves — how she
  wears what she owns, day to day.
- **What entertainment she owns or engages with in her own space**, if she has one.

---

## B2. Regional Speech and Dialect Notes

Enriches the Speech-Pattern Correlation Table (Stage 3) beyond its baseline wound-and-self-esteem-driven
correlation — a regional or subnet-specific speech texture layered on top of the psychological correlation table
gives a more specific voice than the correlation table alone would produce.

---

## B3. Existing Reputation and Rumor Material

What other NPCs, districts, or factions already say about her, if anything is on record — enriches Card's
Reputation delivery channel (Ten Ways, Stage 3) beyond what her own direct actions establish. Not required, since
Reputation can be built fresh, but genuinely richer when it already has texture to draw from.

---

## B4. A Pre-Existing Basic Headline

If a single compact behavioral-compass sentence has already organically emerged for this Doll (Maisel's
technique, folded into the 12-Category Notebook as one of its categories, Stage 3) — normally this is an
*output* of Stage 2/3 processing, but if one already exists as an informal shorthand before formal processing
starts, recording it here saves re-deriving it and can usefully anchor the rest of the pass.

---

## B5. Extended Family Tree and Relationship Web

Beyond the load-bearing relationships already required in A4 and A10 (family/creator, mentors, siblings, rivals,
friends, romantic history, and the full named supporting-cast list) — a fuller extended web (cousins,
acquaintances, professional contacts, casual enemies) enriches the Village (Stage 4) beyond its minimum viable
version, giving more texture to "the diffuse surrounding cast whose accumulated small demands and minor
betrayals carry weight no single major relationship can."

---

## B6. Food and Music Preferences Tied to Origin

Corbett's own material (already captured in the main DRAFT file) flags food and music specifically as two areas
"never to neglect" when building out a character's geographic/community background, since regional cuisine
defines home/hearth and music shapes emotional cueing. Enriches the Background Taxonomy's Environment
sub-category beyond its minimum requirement.

---

## B7. "Tribe" — Specific In-World Group Norms

Distinct from district/subnet origin (which is required, A1) — the specific group whose company she practices
in and whose norms she actually measures herself against, if this differs from or is more specific than her
geographic origin (a particular guild, a particular faction, a particular found-family). Enriches Compare-and-
Contrast Self-Image (Stage 2) by giving it a sharper yardstick than "her home district" alone.

---

## B8. Existing Humor, Wit, or Memorable Lines

Any specimens of her voice already on record, beyond what's captured in A9's introduction-scene material.
Genuinely useful raw material for the Speech-Pattern Correlation Table (Stage 3) and for Card's "Cleverness

examples.

If any exists — reactions, confusion points, or praise already gathered about this specific Doll from any

## B13. The Developer's Own Unformalized Instincts

Aesthetic preferences, conceptual gut-feelings, or "I just want her to feel like X" instincts about this Doll
that haven't yet been written down as canon anywhere. Explicitly worth capturing even in loose form — this
project's own established practice (see the Calethina roster-expansion precedent: "I don't know what sorts of
character backstories, situations, etc, they have, but I'm certain that I want them in the game") treats this
kind of instinct as legitimate, load-bearing input in its own right, not something to discard until it's been
formalized.
