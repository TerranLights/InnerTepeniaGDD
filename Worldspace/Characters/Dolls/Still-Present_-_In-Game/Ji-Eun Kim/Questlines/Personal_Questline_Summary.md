# Ji-Eun Kim — Personal Questline: "The Shape of a Key"

**Status: TENTATIVE.** Substantially more developed than most other companions' broad-scope summaries (three structural options for the companion quest already exist, plus a fully-designed recruiting quest, "The First Secret") — but Option A's core premise (who is hunting her, and why) remains an open question flagged 2026-07-20, and everything downstream of that is written not to depend on the answer. Full detail lives in `README.md` in this folder — this file is the short-form pointer matching the format used for the other companions' broad-scope summaries.

## Overview
**Themes:** Exile and self-determination; the difference between disappearing and being free; what it means to build something for someone who isn't there to receive it; the cost of the first thing you ever do for yourself.

**Emotional Core:** A robot who built something remarkable in exile, then disappeared — not because she was driven out, but because she chose to go. Finding her means confronting someone who made a deliberate, costly, and defensible decision to stop being findable. The questline is not about rescuing her; it's about whether she wants to be found, and what finding her costs both parties.

**Structure:** the companion quest has three combinable options. **Option A** ("The Threat Gets Handled First") is an investigative task and is the one this pass's Personal Questline Design Rule applies to. **Options B and C** are cost/price mechanics layered on top (a targeted reputation hit, or a direct permanent cost the player pays) and are unaffected by this retrofit.

**Mechanism (Option A):** Ji-Eun's own concealment protocol is the categorical block — investigating or eliminating the threat hunting her herself would require exactly the exposure that protocol exists to prevent, at the precise moment she's most vulnerable. The player does it in her place via 5 non-build-gated stat approaches (Investigation, Calculation, Nerve, Humanity, Engine) plus 9 non-stat world-state routes — including a faction-antagonism route reusing Option B's own already-documented list of five districts with confirmed negative relationships with Aquarius (Virgo, Aries, Cancer, Capricorn, Gemini), and a Wild Child/Aquarius route using a persuasion/leverage flavor (modeled on Fallout: New Vegas's Wild Child/NCR mechanic in Arcade Gannon's questline) rather than the bureaucratic-filing default used elsewhere in this pass. Full detail in `README.md`.

**Open, unresolved:** the hunting threat's identity, motivation, what it stands to gain, and whether it has any justifiable rationale within the game's established non-malice tone. Flagged 2026-07-20, needs real design attention.

**Player Agency Level:** High — not just which of the 14 approaches to Option A the player takes, but which of Options A/B/C (or what combination) resolves the questline, which resources get spent, and what Ji-Eun concludes about the player from how it was handled.
