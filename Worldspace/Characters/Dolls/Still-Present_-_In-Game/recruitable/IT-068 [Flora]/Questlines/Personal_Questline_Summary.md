# IT-068 [Flora] — Personal Questline: "[Quest Title — working title "Old Reliable," not final]"

**Status: broad-scope guiding direction only, charted 2026-07-20.** Full step-by-step design is deliberately deferred until the relevant Concordia locations (her Capricorn (TBN) home territory, plus wherever the present-day echo of her inciting incident ends up staged) are developed enough to root plot beats in real, established places rather than placeholders — same discipline as Calethina's and Ayako's own questline deferrals. This exists to give future design work a real throughline, not to lock in specifics. Working title reuses the name of her existing max-loyalty perk ("Old Reliable") deliberately — the perk and the questline should read as the same idea from two different angles.

## Overview
**Themes:** Competence as both genuine strength and a shield. Earned trust in specific people versus institutional promises that were never really anyone's to keep. What it costs to be the one who never needs saving.

**Emotional Core:** Flora's established low institutional trust (city governance, Libra, central management) and her high trust in people who prove themselves through actual work aren't just a general personality trait — this questline should give it a real origin. She has built her whole identity around not needing anyone to keep a promise to her: she trusts only what she can personally verify or fix with her own hands. That's genuine strength, but it also means she doesn't let herself be helped — not by her own crew, and not (on the romance path) by the player. Her Type 6w5 disintegration direction (per her own file: "the need to be seen as capable") is the thing under quiet pressure throughout — not a flaw to be corrected, but a real cost of the way she's chosen to survive. The questline's core movement is her learning that accepting help isn't the same as being failed again.

**Likely inciting hook:** Somewhere in her years working Capricorn industrial maintenance, a crisis hit her crew hard — resources were reallocated away from them mid-emergency under genuine, non-malicious triage pressure (a real crisis, a real trade-off, nobody chose to sacrifice her crew out of contempt), and whatever compensation or follow-through was promised afterward simply never materialized — not from malice, but because the crisis moved on and nobody circled back. That's the quiet wound her institutional distrust rests on. The questline's present-day trigger is a new crisis that rhymes with the old one closely enough that she can't treat it with her usual clean detachment — an authority figure or institution offering her crew something that looks, on the surface, exactly like the broken promise from before.

**Categorical block (established 2026-07-20, per `Companion_System.md`'s Personal Questline Design Rule):** Flora's institutional distrust hasn't stayed private — years of publicly, vocally clashing with city governance and central management over the original broken promise mean the relevant officials already know exactly who she is, and exactly what she thinks of them. Whether or not it's fair, she is too closely and personally identified with the original grievance to get an honest hearing, real paperwork, or an actual decision-maker this time — she'll be met with the same institutional deflection that failed her crew before, precisely because it's *her* asking. **This is a structural exclusion, not a skill gap** — no amount of Investigation or Calculation on her part changes who the officials see standing in front of them. The player, with no history at all with that institution, can get in the door she can't.

**Structural parallel worth studying when this gets full design:** like several other companions, her personal questline likely runs *through* her actual work rather than beside it — a real maintenance/crisis-response situation, not a separate emotional cutscene bolted onto her job. Her crew (names still TBD per her own open questions) should have real presence in it; this is as much about who she lets herself lean on as it is about her and the player specifically.

**5 stat-based approaches to verifying the new offer in her place (non-build-gated, deterministic, per `Companion_System.md`):**
1. **Investigation-driven:** dig through procurement paperwork and prior commitments to see whether the resources behind this offer actually exist.
2. **Calculation-driven:** analyze the budget/resource-allocation numbers directly — does the math behind the offer even add up, or is it another promise nobody can actually fund.
3. **Nerve-driven:** confront the specific official or authority figure responsible, directly, past whatever deflection they try first.
4. **Humanity-driven:** build genuine rapport with a lower-level clerk or official who wants to help but is themselves boxed in by the system, and get them to go to bat.
5. **Engine-driven:** sheer persistence — repeat visits, follow-through, outlasting the stonewalling that would have made anyone else give up.

**Non-stat, world-state-based approaches (target 7–12, floor of 3; grounded in what's already established about her world):**
1. **Capricorn guild-reputation route:** standing built with the Capricorn guild elsewhere gets the player a contact who can vouch for the request or hand over documentation Flora couldn't get on her own.
2. **Aries route:** Aries' own tradespeople share Flora's exact "people who do actual work" value system; an Aries contact could have informal insight into whether the offer is legitimate, or knowledge of who actually controls the resources in question.
3. **Virgo route:** Virgo carries the closest thing to Flora's own wound — chronic institutional under-recognition — and a Virgo contact who's lived it could vouch for the player to an otherwise skeptical official, or share their own hard-won knowledge of how to actually get a straight answer out of the system.
4. **Libra route (the faction-antagonism pattern, per `Companion_System.md`):** Flora's institutional distrust is aimed squarely at city governance and central management — Libra's own domain. Her history there works against her; it doesn't work against the player. A player with genuine positive standing at Libra (Accepted or better) can request the actual paperwork and reach an actual decision-maker precisely because they don't carry what she carries.
5. **A sympathetic official/clerk route:** someone inside the bureaucracy who remembers the original broken promise, still feels quiet guilt over it, and is willing to help make it right this time — no stat check, purely relational, if the player finds and treats them well.
6. **A former crew member route:** someone who was on Flora's crew during the original crisis and has since moved on, but kept old paperwork, memories, or contacts — reachable through unrelated content.
7. **A legacy document route:** the original unfulfilled requisition or promise, in writing, turning up somewhere unexpected (an old Capricorn archive, a Retired Frame Museum-adjacent context) — an item-based route requiring no check at all.
8. **Wild Child/Libra route (added 2026-07-20):** distinct from route 4 above — a player holding actual Wild Child status at Libra (not just positive standing) can't be filed into its normal categories at all, forcing individualized administrative handling. An official trying to make sense of an uncategorizable case, while pulling Libra's own records to figure out how to process the player, could stumble onto or be persuaded to surface the documentation on Flora's crew's original broken promise as part of that same process — a different mechanism from route 4's plain positive reputation, keyed instead off the player being impossible to categorize rather than simply trusted.

**Player Agency Level:** Medium-High. The core wound is specific to her, but which of the 13 routes above the player takes — and whether the present-day crisis resolves toward her extending trust to the institution, to a specific person within it, or not at all — is a real, live choice for the player to shape rather than a foregone conclusion.

## Endings & Variations
Not yet designed. Broad shapes worth keeping in mind from the Themes/Emotional Core above:
- A version where she extends trust again and it holds — importantly, this doesn't need to mean trusting the *institution* itself; it can mean trusting one specific, proven person within or despite it, which fits her established pattern (people over systems) better than a clean institutional redemption would.
- A version where she doubles down on self-reliance rather than risk it again, and it costs her something real — possibly her crew's own trust in her, if her refusal to accept help this time visibly makes things harder for people she's protecting.
- An abandonment-adjacent version if the player is careless with her trust mid-questline, echoing the original betrayal from her own past rather than repairing it.
