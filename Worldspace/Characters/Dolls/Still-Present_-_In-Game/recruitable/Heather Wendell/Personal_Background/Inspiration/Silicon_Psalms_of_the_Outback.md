## Original Brief

**Write about:** "Could you please write a song (lyrics) about a beautiful robot woman named Heather Wendell? She's a Safari-Outback goddess, who's most at-home among the wilds, out in wide-open spaces and clear blue skies. She doesn't much care who's in charge, so long as she gets to continue being out in the wilds, shooting, hunting, and sleeping under the stars. Corporations and governments try to pull her in and get her to work for them, but ultimately, she lives for herself and her communion with nature and the Safari Outback."

**Genre(s):** Australian Aboriginal, Safari

**Title:** Silicon Psalms of the Outback

## Extraction Notes — What's Already Used

Already written into her main `README.md` (Role/Archetype, Personality & Voice, Backstory Summary): the repeated corporate/governmental recruitment attempts and refusals; hunting for balance rather than sport ("tooth and claw... ancient code no court can law"); sleeping under open sky; no institutional affiliation as a defining trait, not an absence.

**Not yet used, worth revisiting during full personality/backstory/questline passes:**
- "Reboots where the red dust breathes" / "no city grid in her memory" — could inform an activation-origin detail (built or activated somewhere distinctly non-urban, or with no meaningful attachment to city life).
- "She reads the land like scripture and song... knows where she's right, knows where she's wrong" — a specific skill/trait candidate (Investigation-adjacent terrain-reading, or a moral-compass trait tied to the land itself).
- "Built by hands that feared the wild" — a genuinely striking origin-story hook: whoever built or activated her may have intended her for something safer/more controlled, and her wildness is a departure from her original design intent, not a continuation of it. Strong potential seed for a personal questline (the gap between what she was built for and who she became).
- "Dreams in wolf-time, dreams in rain / dreams no algorithm can contain" — could inform how her inner life/dreaming (if robots in this world dream/process offline) is written, once that's relevant.
- "She knows the cost of staying free / and she pays it gladly, endlessly" — implies ongoing, real costs to her independence (isolation, missed security, maybe specific past losses), not a costless choice — worth developing as real stakes rather than pure triumphalism.
