## Background

**Structural and Safety Inspector**

Mallory Dufay was a structural and safety inspector based in Texas, in The CSA. Her job was to ensure nobody died in space — specifically, to prevent deaths from structural hazards or other preventable orbital workplace hazards. She oversaw the **first launches and construction** of orbital infrastructure without any doubt; that was her job specifically.

**Timeline placement**

Placed closer to the **mid-2300s** — active during the period when Hana Jinn's earlier metamaterials/mass driver research (theoretical, conducted at A.R.U.Ta.G. shortly after the April 27, 2318 Jeju-do ruling) was translated into actual built, operational orbital infrastructure. Hana did research and analytics only; Mallory's work represents the point where the tangible results — real launches, real structures, real orbital workplaces — started arriving, and needed safety oversight.

**Connection to Hana Jinn**

The two represent two ends of the same origin thread: Hana's theoretical groundwork (metamaterials for mass drivers) enabled the launches and construction that Mallory then inspected and safeguarded. Whether they ever had direct contact — professional or otherwise — is unknown; they were not colocated (Hana at A.R.U.Ta.G. in the vicinity of Hawaii; Mallory in Texas).

**Historical Status**

What happened to Mallory Dufay after this period — and whether she survived to see the Falkland Treaty or the wars — is not yet established.

## Potential Narrative Hooks

- **The other half of the thread:** Hana Jinn dreamed it up; Mallory Dufay built it and kept people alive doing so. A pairing of theorist and hands-on engineer/inspector spanning two different post-war nations (Hawaii-adjacent research union vs. The CSA/Texas).
- **The CSA/Texas connection:** Her origin ties orbital infrastructure's earliest tangible steps to a part of "New North America" not yet otherwise represented in established lore — an opening for CSA/Dixieland worldbuilding.
- **Safety inspector as protagonist of a quiet epic:** "Ensuring nobody died in space" during the literal first orbital construction effort is a compelling, unglamorous heroism — worth contrasting with Hana's more romantic research-driven arc.
