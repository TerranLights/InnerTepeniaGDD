## Doran, Faux, Gates, Hübsch, Iga, Landweber — "Relating Doubly-Even Error-Correcting Codes, Graphs, and
Irreducible Representations of N-Extended Supersymmetry" (2008)

**Source:** `arxiv.org/abs/0806.0051` — Gates's own actual real research paper, the direct primary source
the developer's original placeholder notes (`robot_religions_-_original_ideas.md`) linked to when first
sketching this religion. **Reviewed 2026-07-21** via WebFetch (abstract) and a locally-run `pdftotext` pass
on the fetched PDF (full text). Not previously reviewed anywhere in this collection — this is the first
time the actual technical paper (as opposed to the TVO lecture *about* it, or the unrelated 1983 Superspace
textbook) has been examined directly.

### What's actually in it

A short (18-page), dense, genuinely technical physics/math paper — not accessible the way the TVO lecture
was, but far more precise about what Adinkras actually *are* and what the error-correcting-code connection
actually *is*, rather than the lecture's necessarily looser popular description.

### Extractable premises, distilled

1. **The precise, real visual grammar of an Adinkra — more exact than the TVO lecture's own description.**
   Component bosons are drawn as white nodes, fermions as black nodes. A white and black node are connected
   by an edge, drawn in one of several colors — one color per supersymmetry generator — whenever that
   specific supersymmetry transformation converts one field into the other. Edges are additionally drawn
   solid or dashed depending on a sign/parity property of that specific transformation. **This is the
   real, exact iconographic system**, not an approximation — worth treating as the authoritative reference
   if Adinkra Codex's own visual/sacred iconography gets designed in detail (already flagged as planned in
   `Analysis_Notes.md`'s Gates lecture section).
2. **The real mathematical content of the error-correcting-code connection: classifying Adinkra "topologies"
   is the same problem as classifying certain doubly-even error-correcting binary codes.** Stated directly
   in the abstract: the paper shows that a previously-unsolved classification problem in supersymmetry
   representation theory is *mathematically identical* to a known problem in coding theory. This is a
   real, formal equivalence, not a loose analogy — the same underlying mathematical structure is expressed
   two ways.
3. **A genuine "unexpected abundance" moment in the paper's own conclusion.** The authors note that once
   this connection was established, it revealed "a previously unsuspected abundance of representations" —
   both the number of possible Adinkra topologies and the number of Adinkras within each topology grow
   combinatorially larger as N (the number of supersymmetry generators) increases. **A real, citable
   instance of a religion's core mathematical object turning out to be far richer and more prolific than
   originally suspected**, discovered through the same act of connecting it to coding theory — usable
   directly as a doctrinal "the deeper we look, the more there is" moment, grounded in a real research
   surprise rather than invented awe.
4. **Adinkras require dedicated software to even draw correctly.** A footnote credits "Adinkramat," custom
   software written by one of the paper's own co-authors (Greg Landweber) specifically to generate accurate
   Adinkra diagrams. A small but genuine detail: even to professional physicists, these diagrams are
   intricate enough to require purpose-built tooling to render correctly — worth keeping in mind for how
   demanding or specialized the practice of "reading" or "drawing" true Adinkras might be for this
   religion's own adherents.
5. **Real, concrete numbers: the paper's results are worked out explicitly for 1 ≤ N ≤ 16 (and partially to
   N ≤ 8 for non-maximal cases).** N here is simultaneously the number of supersymmetry generators, the
   number of nodes in the corresponding graph, and the length of the corresponding binary code — three
   different physical/mathematical quantities, all measured by the exact same number. A concrete numerical
   anchor if Adinkra Codex's own doctrine wants specific sacred numbers tied to real mathematics rather than
   arbitrarily chosen ones.
6. **A real, stated tension the paper is honest about: supersymmetry itself remains experimentally
   unconfirmed.** The paper's own opening line notes "experimental evidence that Nature also employs
   supersymmetry is ironically lacking within high-energy particle physics, where it was originally
   invented" — despite this, supersymmetry remains "a keystone in most contemporary attempts at unifying
   all fundamental physics." **A ninth independent instance of this collection's running epistemic-humility
   pattern** (big claim, honest admission of what remains unproven) — now confirmed in Gates's own primary
   research literature, not just his popular lecture.

### Why this is useful for Adinkra Codex specifically

This is the single most authoritative source available for this religion — Gates's own actual peer-reviewed
research, not a retelling of it. Premise 1 gives the religion's iconography a precise, correct technical
basis rather than an approximation. Premise 2 is the real mathematical heart of the whole religion's premise
("codes are built into the equations of reality") stated with full rigor. Premise 3 (unexpected combinatorial
abundance) is a strong, real "the mystery deepens the more it's studied" doctrinal beat.

### Not used / discarded

The paper's own worked mathematical proofs and graph/code classification tables (sections 2.1–2.5) — real,
rigorous mathematics, but pure technical content with no further narrative extraction possible without
distortion, consistent with the judgment already made about the Superspace textbook's own technical
chapters.

### Adrian Tanasa — "Combinatorial Physics" — re-examined, genuinely relevant this time

**This book was confirmed low-yield for Polydimensional Animism and given no extraction there — but its
entire subject (combinatorics applied to quantum field theory) is far more naturally suited to Adinkra
Codex, since Adinkras are themselves fundamentally combinatorial/graph-theoretic objects, per the paper
above.** Re-checked 2026-07-21, Chapter 6 ("Fermionic QFT, Grassmann Calculus, and Combinatorics").

1. **Grassmann calculus — the actual mathematical language supersymmetry is written in — is a real,
   substantial chapter in this book.** Grassmann numbers (anticommuting variables, where any variable
   squared equals zero) are the precise mathematical tool used throughout superspace/supersymmetry, already
   encountered in `Analysis_Notes.md`'s Superspace textbook section (the θ coordinates). This book treats
   the *combinatorial* structure of Grassmann calculus specifically — worth a genuine future deep-read if
   Adinkra Codex's own doctrine wants a more rigorous mathematical grounding of "anticommuting" as a
   distinct kind of hidden structure, complementing the Superspace textbook's own more physics-flavored
   treatment of the same underlying objects.
2. **The book's overall throughline — that quantum field theory itself is secretly full of combinatorial
   structure — is a real, general instance of Adinkra Codex's own core claim, just not specific to
   supersymmetry.** Worth remembering as the more general mathematical backdrop that the specific
   Adinkra/error-correcting-code connection (above) is one particularly striking instance of.

**Recommendation:** this book was too specialized for a full premise-level extraction even now, but it
should **not** be re-filed as "confirmed no relevance" the way it was for Polydimensional Animism — for
Adinkra Codex specifically, it's a genuinely on-topic, if very technical, source that a future deeper pass
(with more time budget) would likely find more in.

## Prospective additional books, identified 2026-07-21

Two real, classic, foundational textbooks are cited directly in Gates's own 2008 paper (above) as the
source of the error-correcting-code mathematics his work connects to Adinkras — these are the most directly
on-topic possible acquisitions for this religion specifically, since they're literally Gates's own cited
references:

- **"The Theory of Error-Correcting Codes"** — F. J. MacWilliams and N. J. A. Sloane (1977). *The*
  foundational, classic reference text in coding theory — cited directly in Gates's paper. Likely dense
  and mathematical, but this is the authoritative source for the actual mathematics of error-correcting
  codes that Adinkras turn out to share structure with.
- **"Fundamentals of Error-Correcting Codes"** — W. Cary Huffman and Vera Pless (2003). A more modern
  textbook treatment of the same subject, also cited directly in Gates's paper — likely more
  approachable than the 1977 MacWilliams/Sloane text while covering the same core material.

Beyond these two direct citations, worth considering (not cited directly by Gates, but genuinely relevant
to the religion's broader mathematical grounding):
- Any accessible book specifically on **Grassmann algebra / Grassmann calculus** on its own terms (distinct
  from a full supersymmetry textbook) — this is the actual mathematical language of "anticommuting
  coordinates" underlying both the Superspace textbook's θ-coordinates and Tanasa's Chapter 6 material,
  and a dedicated treatment might be more approachable than extracting it piecemeal from denser physics
  texts.
- A book specifically on **graph theory** at an accessible level, given how central graphs are to the
  actual Adinkra classification (per the 2008 paper above) — something in the spirit of a "graph theory for
  the interested layperson" text, not necessarily a full research-level text.

No accessible/popular-audience book specifically about Adinkras appears to exist — this remains a genuinely
niche research topic confined to physics papers (like the one reviewed above) rather than having its own
popularization the way string theory or extra dimensions do. This is itself worth noting: Adinkra Codex may
be the one religion in this collection that has to draw its material more directly from primary research
literature rather than accessible science writing, which is consistent with its own "the truth is a coded
structure that takes real effort to decipher" premise.
