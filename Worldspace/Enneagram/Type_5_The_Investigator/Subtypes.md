# Type Five — Subtypes

**Sources:** *Wisdom* (Riso & Hudson) cross-referenced with *Complete Enneagram* (Chestnut). See
`README.md` for provenance/distillation notes and the real-world example names for each Wing.

## The Wings

### 5w4 — The Iconoclast

**Healthy:** curiosity and perceptiveness combine with a desire to express a genuinely personal vision — more
emotional, introspective, and creative than 5w6. Seeks a niche no one else has explored, often gravitating
toward the arts; whimsical, inventive, and willing to tinker with familiar forms in ways that lead to real
innovation.

**Average:** primarily mind-identified but wrestling with real, intense feelings that can complicate sustaining
effort or working with others. More independent than 5w6, resistant to imposed structure, and drawn toward the
surreal and fantastic rather than the rational — can get genuinely lost in their own cerebral landscapes, and
struggle to stay practically grounded while pursuing what interests them.

### 5w6 — The Problem-Solver

**Healthy:** observation combined with organization and real attention to detail — able to draw meaningful
conclusions from scattered facts and make useful predictions from them. Seeks a niche that offers both security
and a place in a larger context; often drawn to engineering, science, or philosophy, combining innovation with
real practical or business acumen.

**Average:** perhaps the most purely intellectual of the two Wings — interested in theory, technology, and
accumulating facts, more directed at things than at people (though strongly identified with the few key people
in their lives). Restrained and private about feelings, more argumentative and defensive than 5w4, with a real
tendency to actively antagonize anyone who disagrees.

## The Instinctual Subtypes

Both books note that Fives, more than most types, look genuinely similar to each other across subtypes —
Naranjo's own observation is that the differences are more "monochromatic" here than in a type like Four, where
the three subtypes diverge sharply. Even so, Avarice channels through three distinct emphases: hiddenness and
boundary-building (Self-Preservation), knowledge and higher ideals (Social), and trust within a single
idealized connection (Sexual).

### Self-Preservation Five — "Isolation and Hoarding" / "Castle"

The most unmistakably "Five-ish" of the three — the clearest expression of the type's core archetype of
isolation and introversion. Builds real, deliberate boundaries (psychological, and sometimes physical) as a
form of shelter, wanting everything necessary contained safely inside them so there's little reason to venture
out. The least communicative and most withdrawn of the three subtypes, with real difficulty expressing anger
directly — displeasure shows up instead as withdrawal or silence rather than confrontation. Some
Self-Preservation Fives develop a genuinely convincing social camouflage, closely observing and mimicking how
others interact specifically so their own boundaries won't be challenged or noticed. At the unhealthy extreme,
isolation can deepen into genuine eccentricity or reclusiveness, sometimes shading into paranoid or distorted
thinking, especially paired with a Six-wing.

**Distinguishing from other types:** the least likely of the three subtypes to be mistaken for anything else —
this is Type Five in its most concentrated, recognizable form.

### Social Five — "The Specialist" / "Totem"

Channels Avarice into knowledge itself, treating intellectual mastery as a substitute for the nourishment
relationships would otherwise provide — expertise becomes the identity marker and the connection point, both at
once. Gravitates toward idealized, admired figures (experts, teachers, thinkers) held at a comfortable
intellectual distance rather than ordinary day-to-day relationships; one Social Five described this as
"collecting people" who share specific interests and values. Drawn to the extraordinary and the ultimate over
the ordinary and everyday — a search for capital-M Meaning that can tip into what's sometimes called "spiritual
bypass": using a lofty intellectual or spiritual system as a sophisticated way of avoiding the more mundane
emotional work of actually growing. Prefers not to feel, can present as mysterious or omnipotent through sheer
command of a subject, and — quietly, despite appearances of indifference to status — genuinely wants
recognition and prestige within whatever field they've claimed as their own.

**Distinguishing from Type Seven:** both can be outgoing and visibly excited about ideas and people. This Five
is more reserved, less self-interested, and less emotionally driven than a genuine Seven — the engagement is
real, but it stays more contained.

### Sexual Five — "This Is My World" / "Confidence" (Countertype)

Flagged as the **Countertype** — the version of the type that runs against its own core grain, since Avarice
here doesn't produce withdrawal from intimacy so much as an intense, if guarded, hunger for one ideal
connection. Searches for a single, exceptional bond — trust, complete transparency, an almost mystical union —
rather than the Social Five's distant admiration or the Self-Preservation Five's walled-off self-sufficiency.
The most emotionally intense and romantic of the three, resembling a Four in depth of feeling even while
remaining recognizably Five underneath it. Sets an exacting, near-impossible standard for a partner's honesty
and openness, which makes disappointment easy to come by — a partner who falls short of that standard (as any
real person eventually will) tends to trigger real withdrawal, since the underlying fear of being hurt runs
just as deep here as in the other two subtypes, simply hidden behind more visible longing.

**Distinguishing from Type Four:** despite the emotional intensity and resemblance, this subtype remains
recognizably Five in its underlying structure — unlikely to actually be mistaken for a Four by anyone who knows
the type well.
