# Type One — Subtypes

**Sources:** *Wisdom* (Riso & Hudson) cross-referenced with *Complete Enneagram* (Chestnut). See
`README.md` for provenance/distillation notes and the real-world example names for each Wing.

## The Wings

### 1w9 — The Idealist

**Healthy:** discerning, wise, civilized — scholarly and dispassionate, oriented toward long-range, "big
picture" concerns rather than day-to-day battles. Often introverted and reclusive, drawn to quiet natural
settings over crowds; emotionally reserved but genuinely generous and kind, with a soft spot for nature,
animals, and innocence. Wants to improve things, but with a gentler, more detached touch than the other Wing.

**Average:** more idealistic than activist — happier explaining their ideals than doing the "dirty work" of
actually persuading or organizing to enact them. The type's characteristic anger is harder to spot here,
tending to surface as stiffness, impatience, or sarcasm rather than direct confrontation. Prefers solitary work
and can become remote, otherworldly, even disdainful of the "messiness" of ordinary human relationships.

### 1w2 — The Advocate

**Healthy:** blends the drive for ideals with genuine empathy and compassion — less purely idealistic than the
other Wing, more willing to get into the actual trenches of the causes they care about. More overtly
passionate and interpersonal, energized by "political" give-and-take, persuasive and effective at getting
others to care about the same things they do.

**Average:** active, outgoing, and can be notably aggressive and forceful in pursuit of their reforms — still
needs real down-time to recharge, but is energized by engaging others, especially in debate. Altruism here is
genuinely focused on others' needs, for as long as it feels like it's making a difference; becomes critical,
irritable, and vocally discontented when frustrated. More fiery and action-oriented than 1w9, and correspondingly
more exposed to frustration when people or events don't cooperate.

## The Instinctual Subtypes

Both books track the same underlying split: each subtype channels the type's Anger through a different target
— perfecting *oneself* (Self-Preservation), embodying "the right way to be" and thereby perfecting the *model*
(Social), or perfecting *other people* (Sexual).

### Self-Preservation One — "Worry" / "Self-Control"

The truest perfectionist of the three, and the subtype where anger is most fully repressed — Reaction
Formation converts the heat of anger into warmth, turning an underlying angry person into an outwardly gentle,
tolerant, supportive one. What surfaces instead is chronic worry: a compulsive need for foresight, an
ever-present sense that something could go wrong unless vigilance is maintained, and real difficulty relaxing
even when things are objectively fine. Often the most conspicuously responsible person in a chaotic childhood
household. Under real stress, this subtype oscillates between strict self-denial and periods of excess —
bingeing on comfort, then swinging back to austerity to "undo" it.

**Distinguishing from Type Six:** both can look anxious, rule-bound, and black-and-white in their thinking, but
the engine is different. A Self-Preservation One is driven by *resentment* at being the one who always has to
fix things ("why am I always the one improving reality, when it would benefit everyone?") and has real
confidence in their own standards. A Six is driven by *doubt* and *fear*, and continually questions whether
what they're doing is even right in the first place — the One rarely doubts the standard itself, only whether
it's being met.

### Social One — "Non-Adaptability" / "Rigidity" / "The Crusader"

Less an anxious perfectionist than a paragon of correct conduct — this subtype doesn't strive so much as
*already knows* the right way, and models it for others rather than agonizing over reaching it. Anger here is
neither fully repressed (as in Self-Preservation) nor overt (as in Sexual) — it cools into an unconscious
sense of intellectual superiority: "I'm right, you're wrong," which becomes a quiet claim to authority over
whoever's on the other side of a disagreement. Naturally slides into the role of teacher, with a genuine belief
that modeling the correct behavior matters as much as, or more than, stating it. Tends to resist adapting to
changing norms once convinced of the "right" way, which can read to others as a rigid know-it-all streak the
Social One is often the last to notice in themselves.

**Distinguishing from Type Five:** both can present as introverted, emotionally detached, and a little "above
it all," standing apart from whatever group they're nominally part of. But a Five's detachment is about
conserving limited inner resources; a Social One's detachment is about occupying the correct, superior vantage
point — their anger sits much closer to the surface than a Five's ever does, even when both look equally cool
from the outside.

### Sexual One — "Zeal" (Countertype) / "Shared Standards"

The one subtype where the type's anger is openly, explicitly expressed rather than repressed or cooled — the
book flags this as the **Countertype**: the version of the type that runs against its own core grain. Rather
than perfecting the self or modeling the right way, this subtype is focused on perfecting *other people*, with
a real sense of entitlement to do so — impatient, intense, willing to be invasive about it, and often
experiencing the drive as excitement or idealism about how much better things could be if others would just
reform. In relationships, this shows up as high expectations of an idealized partner, real fear of abandonment
hidden under a controlling, critical surface, and — at lower health — vulnerability to a kind of double life,
where suppressed impulses break out sideways in exactly the "bad" behavior the One would otherwise crusade
against in someone else.

**Distinguishing from Type Eight:** both can look forceful, confrontational, and entitled to impose their will.
The difference the book draws: Eights are fundamentally under-social (asserting independently of any group's
approval), while Sexual Ones are over-social — even their most forceful moves are in service of a higher
standard they believe *everyone* should be answering to, not simply their own will asserted for its own sake.
