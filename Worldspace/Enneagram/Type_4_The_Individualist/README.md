## Overview

Fours build an identity out of being fundamentally unlike everyone else — not as a pose, but because they
genuinely feel it, often from very early on. Underneath that felt difference sits something both books locate
in the same place: a real or perceived early loss of love, which the child unconsciously concluded must have
been *their own fault*, since that at least offers a shred of control over an otherwise senseless loss. From
there, a Four's whole strategy becomes organized around that original wound — longing for an idealized
connection that would finally redeem it, while simultaneously convinced, on a deeper level, that they're too
flawed to actually receive it. The result is a real paradox: Fours are often the type most comfortable with
emotional depth and authenticity in general, while remaining the type most likely to sabotage the specific
relationship that could satisfy the longing driving the whole search.

**In the stories both books use as touchstones:** Homer's *Odyssey* gives Fours two adjoining episodes. First,
Hades — where Odysseus performs a blood ritual to consult the dead, hears both his fate and a chorus of regret
from spirits mourning lives they can no longer live differently, and comes away having faced (rather than
simply carried) his own grief. Then the Sirens — whose song promises the exact thing every Four secretly wants:
someone who already understands the full depth of their suffering. Odysseus doesn't refuse to hear it; he
*chooses* to listen, but binds himself to the mast first, so the longing can be fully felt without destroying
him. Together, the episodes suggest the type's healthy resolution isn't avoiding grief and longing, but engaging
them deliberately, with real containment in place. Dante's *Inferno* gives Fours the Suicides — souls who
answered scorn with self-destruction, turned into barren, thorn-covered trees that can only "speak" by bleeding
when a branch is broken. Even in the underworld, these souls remain unable to see their own envy clearly — a
stark image of what happens when the type's core wound is allowed to become the entire self, with no room left
for anything else.

## Who This Type Looks Like

Real-world figures the source material itself cites as recognizable examples of each Wing (see `Subtypes.md`
for the full Wing descriptions) — a quick read on flavor, not an exhaustive list:

- **4w3, The Aristocrat** (more sociable, achievement-oriented, presentation-conscious): Jeremy Irons, Jackie
  Onassis, Tennessee Williams, Judy Garland, Vivien Leigh.
- **4w5, The Bohemian** (more introverted, exploratory, defiant of convention): Bob Dylan, Anne Rice, Allen
  Ginsberg, Edgar Allan Poe, Sylvia Plath.
