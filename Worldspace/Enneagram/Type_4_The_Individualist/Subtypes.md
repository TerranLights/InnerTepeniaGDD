# Type Four — Subtypes

**Sources:** *Wisdom* (Riso & Hudson) cross-referenced with *Complete Enneagram* (Chestnut). See
`README.md` for provenance/distillation notes and the real-world example names for each Wing.

## The Wings

### 4w3 — The Aristocrat

**Healthy:** combines creativity with real ambition — self-improvement and goal-achievement matter here in a
way the other Wing doesn't share. More sociable, cares about finding the right mode of expression and avoiding
anything in bad taste, and creates with an audience genuinely in mind.

**Average:** more self-conscious about self-worth and how they're coming across than the other Wing, and puts
noticeably more effort into self-presentation. Practical but also extravagant — drawn to refinement, culture,
and sophistication, often seeing themselves as elegant or high-class. Can be competitive and openly
disdainful of others, with grandiosity and narcissism expressed more directly than in 4w5.

### 4w5 — The Bohemian

**Healthy:** extremely creative — emotionality and introspection combined with genuine perceptiveness and
originality. Less concerned with acceptance or status than the other Wing, highly personal and idiosyncratic
in self-expression, creating more for the process itself than for any audience. Defiant of convention almost
by reflex whenever self-expression is at stake.

**Average:** more introverted and socially withdrawn than 4w3 — dwells more exclusively in imagination, and
finds the real world genuinely less interesting than the inner landscapes they construct. Drawn to the exotic,
mysterious, and symbolic; personal style often reads as eccentric. Prefers a minimalistic, downbeat lifestyle,
can be intensely private, sees themselves as a rebellious outsider — capable of brilliant insight but often
struggling to sustain practical follow-through in the real world.

## The Instinctual Subtypes

Both books track the same underlying split — Envy channeled into three different relationships with suffering:
the Self-Preservation Four internalizes and denies it, the Social Four dwells in and displays it, and the
Sexual Four converts it outward into competition.

### Self-Preservation Four — "The Sensualist" / "Tenacity" (Countertype)

Flagged as the **Countertype** — the version of the type that runs against its own core grain, since it denies
rather than displays the Envy actually driving it. Where the other two subtypes either suffer visibly or make
others suffer, this Four is *long-suffering*: enduring pain without complaint, treating stoicism itself as a
virtue, and hoping (without saying so) that the endurance will eventually be noticed and rewarded. Rather than
dwelling in longing, this subtype works hard to actually acquire what feels lacking — cultivating a materially
and aesthetically rich "nest" of beautiful, emotionally resonant objects, and drawing real comfort from sensual,
tangible surroundings. Often channels its pain outward into genuine care for others' suffering — many
Self-Preservation Fours gravitate toward humanitarian or protective roles, easing "the suffering of the world"
as a way of not having to speak directly about their own. Under real stress, this subtype's desire for emotional
intensity can start overriding basic stability — a throw-caution-to-the-wind streak alternating with
self-indulgent lows, and, at the unhealthy extreme, real vulnerability to addiction or destructive relationships
pursued almost as if by design.

**Distinguishing from Type One:** both value autonomy, self-sufficiency, and hard work. This Four feels a
noticeably wider emotional range than a One, even when — like a One — they don't always show it.
**Distinguishing from Type Three:** both work hard toward a sense of security and can present as anxious. Unlike
a Three, who tends to actually achieve what they're working toward, this Four frequently works at
cross-purposes with their own stated goals, unconsciously undermining the very efforts meant to secure what's
missing.
**Distinguishing from Type Seven:** the high-energy, fun-seeking streak some Self-Preservation Fours display
(an escape valve from constant enduring) can look Seven-like, but this Four retains far greater ongoing access
to their own emotions than a genuine Seven does.

### Social Four — "The Outsider" / "Shame"

The subtype most likely to experience themselves as fundamentally unlike everyone else — and, notably, the most
socially *active* and engaged of the three, wanting real involvement in the social world while feeling
persistently unsure how to actually belong in it. Constant comparison against others produces a chronic sense
of falling short, alongside real envy of others' apparent ease — often covered with a deliberately glamorous or
alternative image (historically, subcultures built partly around a shared sense of not-belonging have drawn
this subtype specifically). The central issue here isn't suffering alone, it's a genuine sense of *inferiority*
— guilt attaches to almost any personal want, and desire itself gets expressed obliquely, through visible
suffering, rather than stated outright. A real, mostly-hidden competitiveness runs underneath the visible shame
— wanting recognition and to be first, even while the surface presentation stays modest or self-deprecating. At
lower health, fear of rejection can produce near-total social withdrawal, alongside real dependency on whichever
few people remain trusted.

**Distinguishing from Type Six:** both can fixate on what's wrong or missing in their lives. The key difference
is the underlying want — this Four wants specifically to be *special*, where a Six identifies with being an
"everyman"; this subtype also spends noticeably more time in sadness, pain, and shame than in Six's
characteristic fear.

### Sexual Four — "Infatuation" / "Competition"

The most romantically intense and least concealed of the three subtypes — turbulent, stormy, and unlikely to
let a fantasy stay purely a fantasy for long. Envy here shows up less as conscious longing and more as active
competition: rather than dwelling in comparison, this Four converts the pain of envy into a drive to prove
themselves superior, and can feel genuinely better simply by outdoing whoever seemed to have more. Naranjo's
term for this subtype is "the mad Four" (against the Social Four's "sad") — anger comes far more readily here
than in the other two subtypes, in part because expressing it is easier than sitting with the vulnerability
underneath it. Arrogance functions as overcompensation for a real, private sense of inferiority; this subtype
can be genuinely elitist, drawn to "chosen" in-groups, and quick to treat any criticism as a serious affront.
Relationship problems often follow a specific pattern: admiring someone for a quality the Four wants for
themselves, then resenting and eventually rejecting them for having it. At the unhealthy extreme, this envy can
tip into real sabotage or a wish for revenge against whoever is seen as having what the Four was denied.

**Distinguishing from Type Eight:** both access anger more readily than most types. The difference is source —
an Eight often simply doesn't need to get angry, where this Four frequently feels misunderstood or envious and
reaches for anger specifically because it's easier than the pain underneath; this Four also has a wider,
more available emotional range overall.
**Distinguishing from the Sexual Two:** both can be aggressive and seductive in relationships. A Sexual Two is
fundamentally oriented toward *pleasing* the other person; this Four is oriented toward being recognized as
singular and superior — being one-of-a-kind matters more here than being liked.
