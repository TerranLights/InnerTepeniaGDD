**Also called:** The Altruist, The Lover, The Caretaker, The Pleaser, The Enabler, The Special Friend.

**The Caring, Interpersonal Type: Generous, Demonstrative, People-Pleasing, and Possessive.**

## Overview

Twos build a life around being indispensable to the people they love — and, less consciously, around a bargain:
if I take care of you, you will take care of me. The love and attention a Two gives is often completely
genuine — healthy Twos really are some of the most generous, attuned people in any room — but underneath the
generosity sits a repressed conviction, formed early, that their own needs are "too much" and will drive people
away if shown directly. So the need doesn't disappear; it goes underground and gets pursued sideways, through
being wanted, needed, and unrefusable rather than through ever simply asking. Both books flag the same core
irony: the more successfully a Two manages this strategy, the less anyone — including the Two — can tell what
they actually feel or need, since so much of their attention runs outward, reading and matching whoever they're
with.

Structurally, this is also the archetype closest to Jung's "anima" — the nurturing, seductive, emotionally
attuned feminine principle — regardless of the actual gender of the person embodying it, and it shares real
DNA with the "codependent" pattern: superficially selfless giving that is, underneath, a strategy for getting
needs met through the promise of reciprocal care.

**In the stories both books use as touchstones:** Homer's *Odyssey* gives Twos Calypso — a nymph so extraordinary
that she detains Odysseus on her island for years, pampering him with everything he could want, while the one
thing she withholds is his freedom to go home to his own wife. Her hospitality is real, but so is the control
underneath it — "under the guise of helping, she wants to own him forever." When Zeus finally forces her hand
and she lets Odysseus go anyway, helping him prepare for the journey with nothing left to gain from it, she
shows the type's healthy resolution: real generosity, once the need to be needed stops running the transaction.
Dante's *Inferno* gives Twos something starker — Pride is the sin that toppled Lucifer for daring to elevate
himself above his own maker, and Dante places Pride at the absolute bottom of Hell, the foundational sin all the
others spring from. Type Two's own version of that same impulse is subtler (self-inflation rather than open
rebellion), but the underlying move — placing one's own will above the natural order of things, in this case
by trying to control who likes whom — is the same shape in miniature.

## Who This Type Looks Like

Real-world figures the source material itself cites as recognizable examples of each Wing (see `Subtypes.md`
for the full Wing descriptions) — a quick read on flavor, not an exhaustive list:

- **2w1, The Servant** (more serious, principled, self-sacrificing): Mother Teresa, Eleanor Roosevelt, Desmond
  Tutu, Florence Nightingale, Albert Schweitzer.
- **2w3, The Host/Hostess** (more outgoing, sociable, focused on personal connection): Luciano Pavarotti, Sammy
  Davis Jr., Merv Griffin, John Denver, Delta Burke.
