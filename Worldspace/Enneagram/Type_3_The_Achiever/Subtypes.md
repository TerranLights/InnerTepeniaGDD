# Type Three — Subtypes

**Sources:** *Wisdom* (Riso & Hudson) cross-referenced with *Complete Enneagram* (Chestnut). See
`README.md` for provenance/distillation notes and the real-world example names for each Wing.

## The Wings

### 3w2 — The Charmer

**Healthy:** more emotional and spontaneous than the other Wing — a vivacious quality that can resemble a
Seven's, combined with the friendliness and generosity of a Two, all while keeping a Three's own poise and
genuine personal achievement. Wants real closeness with people, though can sometimes substitute public
recognition for the harder work of a satisfying private life.

**Average:** works to suppress anything that might interfere with being desirable — self-worth is tied to the
ability to attract and dazzle. Knows how to "turn it on" to impress, which can become a preoccupation, and the
resulting polish can carry an artificial edge that undercuts the popularity it's aiming for. Often competitive,
usually covertly, and may cultivate several different "images" for different social and intimate contexts.

### 3w4 — The Professional

**Healthy:** self-esteem is rooted more in work and career accomplishment than in personal charm — wants their
work itself to be outstanding, and is willing to make real personal sacrifices to maintain professional
integrity. Diplomatic but generally more serious and task-oriented than the other Wing, taking real pleasure in
their chosen craft.

**Average:** powerful ambition mixed with real self-doubt, creating substantial internal pressure — perfection
becomes a way of avoiding rejection or the shame of being seen as inferior, so nearly every project can feel
like it's putting the Three's entire self-worth on the line. Often projects competence and poise while staying
privately guarded, sometimes with an undercurrent of pretentiousness or self-contempt that makes this Wing more
internally conflicted than it looks from outside.

## The Instinctual Subtypes

Both books track the same underlying split — Vanity channeled into three different arenas: material stability
and self-sufficiency (Self-Preservation), social recognition and status (Social), or being attractive and
supportive within a specific relationship (Sexual).

### Self-Preservation Three — "Workaholism" / "Security" (Countertype)

Flagged as the **Countertype** — the version of the type that runs against its own core grain, since it denies
rather than displays the Vanity actually driving it. Works hard specifically to build a sense of material and
personal security: financial stability, self-sufficiency, competence at handling life without needing to lean
on anyone. Presents as calm, solid, and organized — the kind of person others go to for advice — while carrying
real anxiety underneath about how much effort the appearance of having it together actually costs. Strives to
be the best model of quality in whatever role they occupy (best parent, best worker, best partner) — not
merely to look good, but to actually *be* good, since this subtype holds a private ethic that genuinely
virtuous people shouldn't be vain. The result is a real paradox Naranjo names directly: this subtype has "vanity
for having no vanity" — wanting to be seen as successful while actively not wanting anyone to catch them wanting
that. Downtime can feel like incapacity; a few unproductive days can feel like the whole structure might come
down.

**Distinguishing from Type One:** both are rigid, responsible, self-sufficient, and oriented around being a
model of virtue. The difference is the reference point — this Three still conforms to what social consensus
recognizes as "doing it well," where a One answers to an internal standard of right and wrong regardless of
outside recognition, and generally moves at a notably slower, more deliberate pace.
**Distinguishing from Type Six:** both can present as anxious and vigilant. This Three's drive is fundamentally
image-oriented, working harder specifically in response to felt insecurity about status; a Six finds protection
through different means entirely (allegiance, established systems), and unlike this Three, doesn't generally let
productivity outrun genuine doubt.

### Social Three — "The Status Seeker" / "Prestige"

The most obviously vain of the three, and the one most people picture when they picture "a Three." Needs
recognition and applause, genuinely enjoys the spotlight, and reads status cues fluently — degrees,
credentials, job titles, brand names, whatever a given culture treats as proof of having arrived. Highly
competitive, often more assertive and even authoritarian than the other subtypes, though this can hide behind a
smooth, decorous, even humorous presentation. Corporate-minded in orientation: image and results tend to take
priority over sentiment, and this subtype has real confidence about taking over a group's direction if the
existing leadership isn't moving fast or effectively enough for their taste. Anxiety here centers on
overexposure — the fear of being seen as having no real worth once the polish is stripped away — which is part
of why criticism, while rarely shown outwardly, lands especially hard.

**Distinguishing from other types:** the least easily confused of the three subtypes — this is the "classic"
Three most Enneagram material describes by default, and doesn't typically get mistaken for another type.

### Sexual Three — "The Catch" / "Charisma"

Pursues attractiveness and desirability specifically within relationships rather than achievement in the world
at large — sweeter and shyer than the Social Three, uncomfortable directly promoting themselves, and more
inclined to put their considerable competence and energy into making someone *else* look good instead. Achieves
love through being pleasing, supportive, and attractive rather than through visible personal accomplishment —
it's enough, for this subtype, to be genuinely beautiful or genuinely helpful; conventional achievement isn't
required to earn love the way it is for the other two subtypes. Carries real disconnection from feelings and
from a stable sense of self underneath the charm — often described by people with this subtype as an "out to
lunch" quality, with a persistent felt emptiness where identity should be. The most openly emotional of the
three Threes, and the least likely to wear the Social Three's polished social mask, though what shows tends to
be curated toward warmth and enthusiasm rather than the type's underlying sadness.

**Distinguishing from Type Two:** both connect through being pleasing and attractive. This Three focuses more
narrowly on physical attractiveness itself and less on the Two's broader shape-shifting, prideful
self-elevation, and active meeting of others' emotional needs.
**Distinguishing from Type Seven:** both can be enthusiastic, positive, excellent supporters and
cheerleaders. The difference is where the reference point sits — Sevens are fundamentally self-referencing and
generally know what they themselves want; this Three references others to determine how to be, and stays
correspondingly more disconnected from a stable sense of self.
