## Overview of this range

This range is the end of the book's central section, the "Directory of Relationships," followed by the Epilogue, notes and bibliography. The Directory takes each pair of Enneagram types in turn (this part runs from the end of Three with Five through Double Nine). For every pair it gives (1) the **couple** dynamics: attraction, what each wants, characteristic voices, typical conflicts and blind spots, effects of diagram lines (security/stress points, wing points, "meeting at" a third type) and "it helps when" advice; and (2) the **Partnership at Work** dynamics: how the pair operates as peers, then each as manager over the other, with typical disputes and mediation approaches. Material comes from "oral tradition" panels of couples and colleagues, with subtype and wing variations noted. The Epilogue restates the Enneagram as nine emotional passions rooted in sacred tradition (the seven capital sins plus two generic tendencies), explains the oral-tradition method, and sets out Palmer's view of type as tripartite (type proper plus security and stress movement) within a "flow pattern" from essence. Notes explain Gurdjieff/Ichazo lineage points; the bibliography is grouped by tradition.

## Terminology used throughout this range (the author's own labels)

Palmer's type nicknames in this range: One = Perfectionist; Two = Giver; Three = Performer; Four = Romantic; Five = Observer; Six = Trooper ; Seven = Epicure; Eight = Boss; Nine = Mediator. "The diagram" = the Enneagram figure; "shares a line in the diagram" = the pair are connected by an inner line (stress/security points). "Security point" and "stress point" = the types each type moves to in security and stress. "Subtype" = self-preservation, sexual (one-to-one) and social instincts, with named variants (competition subtype, shame subtype, dauntless/counterphobic subtypes for Fours and Sixes). "Wing" = adjacent type. The book's recurring format for each pair: first a **couple** section, then a **Partnership at Work** section (peers/partners, then each type as manager with the other as employee), then ideas "it helps when..." for mediation.

# PART A. THE DIRECTORY OF RELATIONSHIPS (continued from part 2)

## Three with Five: Performer-Observer (end of pair; chapter continues from the previous part)

*Pair began in the previous part; only its closing material, on the partnership at work, is in this range.* [p.324-325]

- **Natural division of labor:** The Performer (Three) initiates contacts and handles adversarial matters, which shields the Observer (Five) from conflict and unannounced intrusion. Threes want control of the visible, image-oriented sector; Fives may reach significant conclusions in private reverie and then be unable to carry the work to the public. A Performer can be the conduit through which an Observer's solutions become marketable. [p.324]
- **Weakness as problem solvers:** Both suspend feelings and avoid conflict, so negative truths are easy to turn away from. Threes reframe negative feedback to protect image; Fives let a troubling matter drop. Both have a delayed reaction time for feelings (Threes suspend emotion by working; Fives need a long time to work out what they feel). A fix: break a hard issue into stages and set dates for a sequence of meetings; a "mechanical" device that suits people who prefer to resolve differences methodically. [p.324]
- **Three as manager, Five as employee:** The Three wonders why the employee will not come forward ("Where's the spark of enthusiasm? Why does she or he disappear?"). Needing personal recognition, Threes feel devalued by a Five who works alone and withholds vocal support. The Five may indeed be only partly committed; a Three's work style can drain Fives ("the more you do, the more the manager wants"; no downtime, driving pace). A Three's need for recognition may go over a Five's head: manager feels disrespected, employee feels coerced into a show of interest. Fives hate being used for others' purposes and withdraw to conserve energy. Remedy: schedule so Fives know exactly what is expected; they will carry a large load when they know "exactly how much it weighs and how far they have to carry it." [p.324-325]
- **Five as manager, Three as employee:** Poor fit with the Three's interactive style. Placing Threes in people jobs (advertising, sales) helps somewhat but does not quench their wish to be personally validated by authority. Threes are pushy, Fives hate intrusion: the manager works behind closed doors and the employee finds reasons to knock, a mutual frustration. By withdrawing, Fives can become attractive to employees who want contact. Fives often wear a business mask of Three-like behavior (extroverted pose in a rehearsed professional role; confident when protected by a set agenda; generous when attention is on task, not self). Five managers must be explicit: innuendo and subtle signs will not work; a precise definition of roles and expectations relaxes the Three's need to compete for favored status; with a clear goal Threes implement agendas wonderfully (Five as analyst, Three as frontline performer). [p.325]

## Three with Six: Performer-Trooper Couple

- **Frequency and central tension:** Not a frequently found couple; success depends on resolving "the tension between performance and performance anxiety." Threes are go-getters whose key concept in relationship is **stop**: having to stop and talk about the relationship activates the Three's anxiety about self-worth. Terrified of powerful feelings, they speed up exactly when the more internalized Six wants to stop and discuss doubts. [p.325]
- **Six's key concept is go** (toward success, pleasure, trusting the partner). A wise Six times discussions of emotion to coincide with shared activity, since a Three relates through the filter of activity and needs reassurance that feelings do not mean a dead halt to the pleasures and rewards of work. [p.326]
- **Diagram line:** The couple slides to and fro along a line in the central triangle; that line shows that Threes doubt their own emotions and that Sixes are stressed by performance. [p.326]
- **How they help each other:** Threes feel frightened by relating to someone whose worldview hinges on doubt; Sixes challenge image and doubt success, the very features Threes count on to feel good. Threes are saddened to see a Six caught in a cyclical return to the underdog position. Paradoxically, by encouraging a Trooper to do his or her best, Performers can become the object of paranoid attention; for Sixes public image feels like a setup ("you can be attacked and ridiculed when you're publicly visible"), so the Three's well-intended efforts feel like a trap or test, adding to performance anxiety. [p.326]
- **Prescriptions:** Threes can drop success as the measure of personal worth and focus on the feelings under a Trooper's self-doubt; if dealing with success is framed as a life task, Threes are the perfect partner for it. If a Performer dismisses a Six's recurrent doubt as silly, that reveals an inability to look within at their own anxiety. Sixes are chronically disillusioned by high-profile Performer boasting; the two see success through different eyes: Threes feel accomplished when they perform a task; Sixes have "amnesia about success" and need repeated reassurance; Threes feel like pros when successful while Sixes "have to go back to the beginning." Sixes should focus on the task without deflecting into doubt; dismissing the Three's skills as superficial announces the Six's own inability to hold concentration on goals. [p.326-327]

### Three-Six Partnership at Work
- **Natural alignment:** Six = creator of original ideas, Three = creative promoter. Troopers stay with a controversial idea long enough to refine the theory; their creativity is sharpened by adversity and they can forgo immediate recognition. Performers excel at packaging and promoting, sensing the market, tailoring materials to public interest. In a partnership of equals Six is the idea person and troubleshooter; Three promotes and sells. [p.327]
- **Three manager, Six employee:** The Three must respect the Six's input. Performers are famous for jumping on good ideas without crediting contributors. If a Six is invested in recognition of his or her part and does not get it, the manager looks like an oppressive, untrustworthy authority and the manager's vision and long-term leadership look like self-advantage. Apportion credit, especially for conceptualization: Sixes are territorial about mental contributions and may want them valued above any other public recognition. If the Three suspends feelings and misses gathering perception of opportunism, the Six takes an underdog stance and broadcasts "a workplace call to arms"; managers should treat it with respect, since it probably comes from a wider base than one dissatisfied Trooper. Threes especially discount negative feedback, which fans opposition. The shared diagram line teaches Threes to doubt their self-image as popular leaders and entertain Six-style self-questioning. [p.327-328]
- **Six manager, Three employee:** The Six may be too cautionary and rule-bound for an ambitious Three. Fearing loss of control, Sixes point out negative intentions and discount the positive side of competition and initiative. Threes are especially sensitive to being passed over and quickly seek other jobs when no room for advancement exists; they flourish in rapid turnover and expansion, countered by the Six's ambivalence about visibility. Sixes must risk in the ways Threes exemplify; rather than control loyalty with regulations, they can model the image and efficiency Threes admire. [p.328]

## Three with Seven: Performer-Epicure Couple

- **Overview:** A great energy match producing "a lifestyle of successful adventure"; Three's goal orientation matches Seven's multioptional mind. No complaints of depression or boredom while projects multiply, but they may spend little time together unless interests are narrowed to a common purpose; otherwise they "give each other a weekly news update." [p.328-329]
- **Shared flaws:** Both overrate the fun and gloss over defects (finances, disciplining the kids are uninteresting). Grand ideas with marginal resources collude to make life happy regardless of cost. Limitation signals fast-forward to both: Threes deceive themselves that everything is OK and work harder to outspeed failure; Sevens avoid pain and rationalize failure by envisioning the future. Accumulated neglect leaves them vulnerable to "surprising and unforeseen" external problems (financial crises, a child acting out). [p.329]
- **"Diagnosis joke":** you can spot Threes and Sevens because they like themselves. Upside: positive outlook, expect others to be impressed; downside: superficiality, hard to see when you have gone wrong. Both vanish when public image is questioned: Threes by changing image and telling partial truths, Sevens by switching options and rationalizing. They conspire to let each other do his or her own thing instead of using each other as allies in growth. [p.329]
- **Anxiety triggers:** Threes become anxious when they love more and work less (suspended feelings surface); Sevens fear losing options and being stuck with one commitment. [p.329]
- **Mature version:** Face the anxieties rather than bury them in activity; Threes can be as tenacious about overcoming relationship anxiety as work obstacles. First step: concrete short-term goals (a weekly sauna together; two hours a week photographing each other). Sevens contribute pleasure and choice, liberating to Threes who think about looking right rather than feeling right. Long-term couples describe "productive fun." The Seven partner has developed steadiness of purpose; some of Three's product orientation has become a pleasure. [p.330]

### Three-Seven Partnership at Work
- Flourishes at the start of a project, founders before completion. They are "Enneagram lookalikes" (go-ahead attitude, expand the scope to stay interested, many balls in the air). Threes take on breathtakingly boring work for a personal win (prestigious title, financial benefit); Sevens take a "gourmet's approach to livelihood," compete well and present an appealing image but the agenda is adventure over achievement; they step back from leadership that limits freedom. "Sevens focus on work that feels good, and Threes focus on what looks good to the crowd." [p.330-331]
- **Three manager, Seven employee:** The Three mistakes the Seven's enthusiasm for willingness to drudge. Both cut corners (Threes for efficiency, Sevens to avoid boredom), jeopardizing quality control. Sevens like the work process and spend time talking and experimenting. Threes command; Sevens try to equalize authority. Threes delegate, which supports the Seven's co-management style. Common mediation dilemma: a Three forged ahead believing commitments were kept while a Seven derailed in the boring phases. Sevens, uncowed by hierarchy, take off in new directions without realizing they act on their own orders. Help: the manager directly supervises the final phase of a Seven's project; specify lines of command; Sevens like a designated leader as focus but work around that leader's authority for their own freedom. [p.331]
- **Seven manager, Three employee:** Sevens are inspired and popular early; delegate once the project is in place and position themselves as networkers and promoters, returning for short demanding bottlenecks. A Three employee can bring the project to fruition if the Seven is practical and specific. Most frequent misperception: fuzziness of purpose; Sevens who can't delegate leave holes in implementation; plans change; a frustrated Performer may have to take on management to make the Seven's theories coherent; erratic leadership lets Threes fill the vacuum. Respect Threes in their vocabulary (salary, public recognition); give a specific title and role rather than abdicating leadership. [p.331-332]

## Three with Eight: Performer-Boss Couple

- **Overview:** Another "Enneagram endangered species list" candidate. Both are used to being the active partner: Eights want to protect, Threes want to provide. Eights take control and deny softer feelings; Threes work hard to numb out feeling. Personal power is accentuated in both; neither is experienced with emotional surrender. A breakthrough commonly occurs when either partner faces failure; couples say adversity made them lean on each other. Eights are loyal to Threes as people even when scandal shatters public image; a Boss is disarmed by a Three who saves the day in an out-of-control emergency. [p.332]
- **Confidants:** Secure in each other's capabilities, likely to be successful. Threes must learn to be appreciative (they tend to think they do it all alone); Eights in love show marked Giver-like tendencies and need reassurance and attention when the tough persona first melts. They bond in activity and action; feeling and being are new territory ("a doing bond is entirely different from emerging bonds of being and feeling"). An Eight's feelings open when control can be safely surrendered; Threes' emotions flourish when they feel loved for who they are, not what they achieve. [p.332-333]

### Three-Eight Partnership at Work
- Prominent American leadership styles; both aggressive and territorial; both value product over process; both enraged when tasks are interrupted: "an unbeatable action team when they're on the same side, and a guaranteed power struggle if they're not." Eight style is direct and confrontational ("what you see is what you get"); Threes act like chameleons, change hats to be liked, "duck and weave"; Eights build a strong platform, set a goal and launch; Threes get in motion, patch a strategy from what is available and learn on their feet. As adversaries: "Threes see Eights as bullies, and Eights see Threes as liars." [p.333]
- **Eight manager, Three top producer:** If Three feels valued the partnership grows. Threes want tangible rewards; a wise manager respects this rather than controlling. Eights are wary of challenges to power and Performers are one; set guidelines for salary and status; Threes respond to opportunities within a system (want a ladder to climb). [p.333]
- **Three manager, Eight employee:** Managers must show fairness. Eight's questions: "Can a chameleon be trusted? Is the manager loyal to the staff?" "Will I be sold out when it's convenient? Is the manager honest? Let's find out!" Eights may foment unrest and the manager will face a rebellion; the real question is the integrity of management. Eights cannot invest unless they feel secure; reaffirm the Eight employee's place in the future (Eights need to know their position in long-range business plans). [p.333-334]

## Three with Nine: Performer-Mediator Couple

- **Attraction pattern:** Performers want to impress intimates; Nines merge with others' ideas, so Threes project an image that captures a Nine's heart; the Mediator supports the Three's course, and performance goals become the centerpiece. Hungry for a personal life goal, Nines are animated by a mate's agenda ("sometimes for years"). Impressionable Nines try out the Three's public persona and think, "I have found myself. At last I have discovered what I'm supposed to do in life." The couple shares a line in the diagram: Mediators gain security by merging with the image and identity a work role provides; if Three's goals reflect Nine's discounted inner needs, a productive lifestyle spearheaded by Three and shared by Nine can emerge. [p.334]
- **Reverse line:** Threes in stress fall into Nine-like uncertainty about goals, feeling aimless; both dislike lethargy from difficulty with choice. Mediators dread energy grinding down when focus is lost; Performers prioritize quickly so there is always something to do. [p.334]
- **Low side:** Without someone else's priority as rudder, Nines fall into a routine "akin to sleepwalking": lowered energy, mechanical duties, presence and enthusiasm numbed; a crisis can emerge with little said. Three drawn to outside activities, Nine going to sleep; neither recognizes the dilemma (Threes work "for us"; Nines work to numb disappointment and anger). [p.334-335]
- **Impasse:** Nines dragged along by an agenda start asking "How did we get to this impasse?" "Did I choose this course of action?" "Do I belong here or not?"; questioning can become obsessive, moving them to stress point Six, doubting the relationship yet unable to propose solutions. It drives Performers wild when mental questioning halts family expectations; by default Threes act (burying themselves in work, veering to another relationship, or initiating change). [p.335]
- **Help:** Recognize that the Nine's search for personal direction applies to both partners; do not trivialize it as self-indulgent. Threes can be impatient: "Why dredge so deeply when short-term projects beckon?" The focus on identity from one's own being alarms chameleon-like Threes. Interesting feature: Nines who find themselves may not change careers or leave, but feel recommitted to "a life that became my own, when I could see that I wanted it." [p.335-336]

### Three-Nine Partnership at Work
- Performers' activity rate sets the workplace standard; Nines admire people who "find themselves" through a career and, molding to Three's work persona, develop go-getter habits, mainly pacing with deadlines. Nines can look and act like Threes (their security point) but still see all sides, merge with others' agendas and avoid conflict; Threes see that as slowing the pace; Nines think attending to other views "means conducting a business with heart." [p.336]
- **Three manager, Nine employee:** Urgency to move on after an agreement; hard to get attention unless task related, so office morale is off-limits; Mediators can be "pounded by unarticulated employee resentments" the Three misses. Performers get conveniently called away from quarrels and become belligerent if the schedule is interrupted; prime directive "Keep going"; with task tunnel vision, employees become objects that do or do not serve; "logical to drop people who have no value and to drive those who do." Mediators feel threatened first and speak last; merged with employee opposition they express dissatisfaction indirectly ("it just won't go the manager's way"). [p.336-337]
- **Nine manager, Three employee:** Indecisiveness threatens goal-oriented Threes; extended search and discovery unsettling; data collection becomes an end; too much information overloads a Three; Three sees concise short-term goals, Nine sees goals changing under fluctuating conditions; a Three frantically pushes for closure while the manager still examines evidence. [p.337]
- **Bridging:** Third-party input helps: Nines declare themselves to a safe listener more easily than face to face; Threes like to put problems on a schedule that ensures results. Nines need to prioritize, confront, see the value of assertive leadership; Threes need to wait, value others' insight, and see the link between performance and people's needs. [p.337]

## Double Four: Double Romantic Couple

- **Overview:** Another "endangered species list" candidate: Palmer has seen only a few double-Four intimate relationships, though Romantics love to hang out and often become best friends, supported by a mutual avocation (opera, petitions for children's rights, jogging and talking about relationships, crying, dressing well, making up after a fight). Best friends affirm each other's value as people sharing a unique vision, neutralizing underlying shame of not being good enough to merit love; Fours can be more open with friends than intimates because they believe revealing a fatal flaw to an intimate leads to abandonment. [p.337-338]
- **Bond:** Attraction to intensity evoked through aesthetic sensitivity, tenderness and equally powerful depression. "A good relationship is permeated with the whole spectrum of feeling." Questions they ask: "How can joy be authentic without repercussions about the loss of joy? Doesn't emotional union have its counterpart in separation and pain?" [p.338]
- **Problems:** They elevate the search for a fulfilling relationship; simple affection seems a "superficial sham"; "a real relationship" should be an artistic achievement ("Where's the magic? We can't let that happen to us!"). Each overvalues his or her own sensitivity; questioning the quest for love seems to cheapen it. Needs are idealized; ecstatic moments are transient and insisting on too much guarantees "a missing piece" (house never big enough, mate not ardent enough, coffee without enough cream); a litany that the partner must change: "I can't bear to leave you, and I can't send you away; but if you were enough, then I'd feel different, so you're the source of my pain." [p.338-339]
- **Low point:** Each suffers and sees the other as the source of suffering, wants a perfect lover while feeling flawed; each criticizes the other and wants to bail as flaws are unearthed. They share a move to One (their security point); if the relationship bottoms out, both fall to the low side: "Perfectionistic Fours can be biting." Subtext: fear of abandonment, "I fear rejection because I am flawed, and to save myself I'll dump on you first." [p.339]
- **High side:** Can keep the spark alive for a lifetime; nostalgia of separation; once entwined, forever connected through feelings; years processing nuances with loving acceptance of kisses and tears. [p.339]

### Double Four Partnership at Work
- Fours can be high-profile achievers: drive often motivated by the assumption that a high-performance schedule kicks depression; they need tangible evidence of value; success in others' eyes is balm for low self-esteem. Fours favoring the Three wing, especially the competition subtype, mobilize against a rival (valued in the American marketplace). All Fours put a spin of specialness on achievements; even a shame subtype is activated by "If they can do it, I can do it! What do they have that I haven't got?" [p.339]
- Romantics often go undetected at work (feelings set aside); in team-building sessions, when a Four identifies, others respond with round-eyed surprise ("You? ... I would never have guessed!"). Most Fours swing between a drive to succeed and "fatal disinterest": rising euphoria with challenge turns to disappointment when the job becomes routine; they sabotage gains at the point of success. Fours favoring the Five wing and shame subtype are shy of public exposure. Success signals boredom and fear of becoming ordinary; stagnation is charged because Romantics need "a hit of magic," the kind that comes from throwing caution to the wind. [p.340]
- **Double-Four combos:** An unsettled environment (untried theories, long shots, unlikely personnel, emotional rescue missions); produce well when there is no competition; without special attention for each, cooperation shifts to envy and spiteful covert attacks. Four managers develop a personal signature, innovate, may confuse emotional impulse with creative insight, forget to verify intuition with research, and form an in-group (some staff interesting, others not), entangled through crisis and losing interest after it; an in-group sets up unhealthy competition. As employees, Fours suffer if out of favor and may encourage chaos as an attention-getting device. Mediation: structural guarantees that eliminate in-group favoritism; binding agreements not compromised by shifting affiliations or emotional cronyism. [p.340-341]

## Four with Five: Romantic-Observer Couple

- **Likeness and difference:** Both enclose themselves in an interior world, but Fives live in intellectual abstraction and mental imagery seemingly devoid of emotion while Fours are exquisitely attentive to mood. Heart and mind are such different organs of perception that each feels misrepresented: the Romantic thinks "My lover has no heart," the Observer "My mind is filled with my beloved"; ships passing in the night. [p.341]
- **Shared worldview:** Both believe principles and keys of hidden meaning operate beneath ordinary events; real life lived "behind the scenes of superficial appearances" (not magical thinking, but a private world of perception differing from the mainstream). Years together blend Four's aesthetic self-presentation with Five's observational powers into "a kind of aesthetic distance"; they communicate through a symbol-laden lifestyle (choosing colors for a fall garden = "We will sit together in that garden when it flowers"; pouring each other's morning tea acknowledges tenderness). Alternatively they become isolated in separate worlds with little need to communicate. [p.341-342]
- **Boundary issues:** Fives conserve energy and control time with others; Romantics need much attention and their moods shatter Five's protective concepts; Romantics rail at scheduled intimacy (worst case: Five closeted in the study, Four abandoned in the bedroom pining). The more a Four laments, the more a Five withdraws to think, "That's your idea of our relationship, not mine." [p.342]
- **What helps:** Each finds the proper degree of contact; long-term couples begin to look alike. Observers have to stay with feelings, Romantics throttle back. Commonly Fours fall into a "half-distant relationship": far enough not to feel abandoned, not close enough for flaws to be seen. Fives should value "the knowing of the heart" (a key to secret knowledge lies in the nuances of feeling); Fours modify greediness for love by identifying with Five's emotional detachment. [p.342-343]

### Four-Five Partnership at Work
- Observers can be more expressive professionally than intimately; caught by a cause or venture and highly emotional when the focus is task rather than purely personal; increased availability gains a Romantic's confidence. [p.343]
- **Five manager, Four employee:** Fives operate within a set agenda, timed meetings and arranged calls; employees run interference as a protective filter; once concentrating, hard to disengage, so privacy is a prime directive. Fours privy to the inner circle may run the office in a personal, stylish way; the Five lets the Romantic shape the look and feel of public appearances. Fours overreact to lack of attention, especially when the Five uses intermediaries and memos; a Romantic who feels ignored may use moodiness or negligence to force a confrontation; the manager may want to fire the Four, which gives the Romantic fuel to force a face-to-face meeting. Standoff averted by a show of interest: Fours want personal recognition, Fives organizational efficiency without personal presence; the employee moves from an emotional stance and the manager from a logical one; Fives encourage discussion, Fours offer objective solutions relying on structure. [p.343-344]
- **Four manager, Five employee:** Both want a private relationship (perhaps just a special project). Observers shine in a circumscribed area of expertise; Fours need the stabilizing effect of well-documented support. If trouble arises the Five withdraws behind intellectual formality; "Distressed Fives can control a whole department by giving nothing": no answer unless you ask the exactly right question in precisely the right vocabulary; periods of no communication and single-page reports; the Five hides behind an appropriate office pose so the Four cannot pinpoint the problem. When cornered, Fives can say exactly what they think bluntly, unaware of the humiliation to others; deadlock if the shame-based Four evens the score by demeaning Five's expertise. Romantics are shamed by exposed flaws, Observers hurt when intellectual contributions are questioned. Mediation: both can drop old grievances and start over without regrets, especially when Fives are intellectually recognized and Fours rescued from anonymity by a guarantee of special mention. [p.344]

## Four with Six: Romantic-Trooper Couple

- **Likeness:** Romantics can be as fearful as any Six; Troopers plug into the universal suffering typical of Fours. Shared perceptions strengthen rather than divide; standing together in sorrow they share emotional victories; both privy to the depth of purpose produced by "voluntary suffering." [p.344-345]
- **Different motives, same worldview:** Both identify with fear and sadness; Troopers see themselves as outcasts/underdogs fearing persecution, Romantics fear being misunderstood and abandoned. Bonded by a similar destiny and the surge of creativity that drives oppressed people to an act of significance. Both expect much of themselves, feel a need for a conceptual or creative breakthrough rooted in early conviction of being set apart; protect each other as people who have endured together; "Fours are attracted to those with emotional depth, and Sixes are faithful to an underdog cause." [p.345]
- **High and low:** High: support each other's vulnerability, see the beauty of the human condition. Low: vulnerability mired in inaction; each blames the other for low self-esteem; Romantic: "I would feel better if I had a better partner"; Trooper: "How can this be love when I both love my partner and entertain my doubts?" Stagnation: each preoccupied with proving himself or herself. Breakups and reunions are reported because mutual blame breeds mistrust. Fours writhe under criticism; Sixes want unwavering support even when they fail. Crisis if both collude in worst-case thinking (easier to go alone). In crisis Romantics turn contrary, refusing to see their flaws and trying to make the partner wrong; Sixes push people away by arguing against whatever a partner thinks important. [p.345-346]
- **What helps:** Backing down long enough to reaffirm commitment ("This is a difficult phase; but remember, we're committed to changing and staying together"); fair-fight guidelines since either type quits under fire. After a breakup Romantics idealize the vanished relationship and want the Trooper back; Sixes, deeply mistrusting broken commitments, are less eager to reconcile. Breakthrough: seeing the similarity between Four's push-pull (loving versus wanting to reject) and Six's alternation between belief and doubt: "all these changes of heart are merely self-protective"; safer to reject first than face the fear of not being enough. [p.346]

### Four-Six Partnership at Work (begins)
- Either type entertains catastrophic thinking, paradoxically spurring action; risk is mesmerizing to competitive and reckless ("dauntless") subtype Fours and counterphobic Sixes, who feel creatively alive in risk-taking ventures. At the other end the shame subtype Four and phobic Sixes of any subtype are cautionary, think dutifully about hierarchy, and entrench in a professionally safe niche shielded from visibility and open competition. [p.346]

### Four-Six Partnership at Work (continued)
- **Four manager, Six employee:** Four managers need their public image validated: competence accolades are not enough; they need recognition of a personal statement "I am different." A Six will back any trustworthy authority however idiosyncratic and is obedient so long as management is accountable; when an authority seems above the rules, Sixes grow wary. Four managers should present a consistent image and avoid high-handed policy changes; suspicious Sixes read hidden intention into every gesture. Antidote: the manager becomes ordinary and unthreatening by informing, explaining and asking for feedback; become visible to employees rather than holding apart. The partnership either rallies through mutual trust or disintegrates "into the kind of personality struggle that wrecks a business." A struggling Six can appeal to a Romantic (rescue mission, nurturing a budding talent), producing a people-oriented environment that inspires fearful people to succeed. [p.347]
- **Six manager, Four employee:** Troopers must contain their doubt; a Six manager may see himself as causing employee dissatisfaction or feel personally attacked and attributes too much importance to minor disagreements; pinpoint sources of irritation; Romantic employees help by disclosing. [p.347]
- **Mediation:** Expose the differences between each type's perception of crisis. Fours feel at home in struggle and honor authentic feelings emerging when ordinary events are disturbed; Sixes overreact to conflict and see Fours as erratic and dangerous to business interests. Gulf widens when Four's boredom creates an outburst that Six tries desperately to contain; Romantics find answers in the midst of crisis while Troopers suppress crisis and seek an analytic solution. Focus on the positive results of conflict, Four's eagerness to point out what is missing, and Six's fear of crisis. [p.347-348]

## Four with Seven: Romantic-Epicure Couple

- **Overview:** An "attraction of opposites," commonly found; balance between Seven's many options and Four's commitment to emotional depth. Fours experience the world through feelings, Sevens are primarily mental; each has a joy to share (heart/mind), producing either true union of opposites or alienation. [p.348]
- **Mutual view:** They see the extraordinary in each other: Fours feel special, Sevens feel entitled; they support each other's talents or unconsciously expect the union to produce too much. An Epicure's emotional unavailability oddly fits a Romantic's longing for missing things; Fours yearn for a better relationship even when it goes well; both seek intensity (Seven focused on the event, Four on the Seven). Epicure optimism deters Four's possessive melancholia; Sevens are not much affected by the Romantic push-pull and Fours respect a partner not drawn into seduction-and-rejection drama, though they may pine to be matched. [p.348]
- **Pitfall:** Seven's intolerance of negative emotions: when a difficult topic arises other plans beckon. Positive feelings are far more acceptable. Activity displaces depression and stabilizes Four's moods without much being said, but Romantics are impatient with Seven's inability to tolerate pain: "Why do you leave just because I'm sad? Where do you go when we need to talk?" They argue over how to deal with loaded issues: Fours think negative feelings must be discussed, Sevens see negativity as a waste of time; each feels controlled. Epicure's charming reply: "I do feel deeply and fully, but only briefly." Sevens must be convinced feelings are worth it; deep experience feels like getting mired and fear of limitation sets in. [p.349]
- **All-time low:** Fours say the Epicure is shallow; Sevens feel strangled by Four's emotional needs. With maturity, Four's depth engages Seven, and Seven lifts the Romantic out of depression; when emotions become "interesting," a Seven will stay with negative feelings. Outsiders feel pressured to take sides (Four's plaintive note and Seven's positivity invite friends to act as counselors). [p.349-350]
- **Guidelines:** Fours are entitled to time for genuine emotional questions; Sevens are entitled to expect a resolution. Fours must distinguish a genuine need with a potential solution from emotional neediness that creates problems to get comfort. Sevens must talk despite boredom and frustration; Epicures offer quick "brilliant" solutions or excellent reasons to keep it short; guidelines should differentiate reasons from excuses. Seven commits time for processing feelings; Four realizes that feelings can interfere with communication. [p.350]

### Four-Seven Partnership at Work
- Seven sees the cup half full, Four half empty, yet the pair often works well; each energized by the other's distinctiveness; combined perception of what is possible and what is missing leads in novel directions ("as if they conspire in believing they can develop something that's never been seen before"). Seven's refusal to be pinned into a dead end spurs activity; Four's emphasis on form and appearance prevents a hyperactive explosion of plans. [p.350]
- **Four manager, Seven employee:** Both susceptible to charm and may insulate each other from drudgery in a cocoon of special dispensations, alienating other staff. Four feels bombarded by brilliant speculation and dislikes separating cleverness from feasible solutions. Both are mesmerized by unusual possibilities; execution feels tedious; closure is hard (evaluation looms); both lose focus in final phases. Four managers lose interest when goals materialize and shift priorities prematurely, feeding the Seven's ever-changing matrix and producing confusion. Romantic managers are poised amid chaos and noted for last-minute rescue missions; Epicures rationalize their mistakes and believe it, forcing the manager to enforce standards. Most frequent dispute: incompletes; the Four feels disrespected and banishes the Epicure from favor. A **prodigal-son relationship** can develop: the Seven withdraws without a trace until conflict blows over; the staff wonder why they seem less interesting to the manager when they worked hard in the prodigal's absence. Fours must set absolute professional standards (both bend rules); regulations and deadlines must be unusually clear when a Four who feels above the rules colludes with an employee who evades limits. [p.350-351]
- **Seven manager, Four employee:** Sevens are fine promoters but erratic day-to-day managers; plan and delegate follow-through; set the project in motion and let Romantics get the job done. Motivation: Seven's conviction that good ideas will materialize + Four's perception of the missing components that actualize plans. Key to engaging a Four employee: **inner-circle rapport**; the Four must feel special while Seven managers take staff for granted; establish ongoing personal connections or Romantics feel slighted and let affairs slide. Helps when Sevens set a chain of command allowing them freedom to come and go; Romantics are delighted with in-group responsibility and may be the perfect representative for a manager who wants to get away. [p.351-352]

## Four with Eight: Romantic-Boss Couple

- **Overview:** "A partnership of intensity acted out by fight, flight, fascination, and flair"; each feels awed by the other (Eights feel coarse and blunt beside the elegant, socially adept Romantic; Fours are magnetized by the "socially shameless Boss"). Romantic drama syncs with Eight's lust for life: "a sex, drugs, and rock-and-roll relationship, acted out either literally or metaphorically." One couple's twenty-year relationship: "a continuous lightning storm... We've moved all over the country, had five children, worked incredibly hard, and had thousands of great fights." [p.352]
- **Fit:** Fours demand a huge range of feelings; Eights love to match strong energies; both push limits and ignore convention; both fight boredom by intensifying the emotional climate (Eights by aggressive demands, Fours by dramatic acts and suffering). Eights want inclusion in matters of taste and presentation. Fours are attracted to the Boss's blunt stance as "real" or "authentic" feeling; Eights waste no time worrying what people think. Fours can hide behind a glamorous image; Eights are remarkably natural. Romantics read the Eight's ability to move into a fight and hold ground as proof of authenticity; Eights do not become enmeshed in Four-like depression and hold a consistent course through push-pull; Bosses "pride themselves on being able to see through the pull of seduction." [p.352-353]
- **Natural congruencies:** An Eight prefers a Four's company when upbeat and may withdraw if depressed, going off to have a good time elsewhere; ignored Romantics are infuriated, which may break the depression. If depression is anger turned inward, Eights are ideal partners for making that anger direct; Eights dislike self-absorbed drama and provoke a fight to evoke real feelings, which can bring the couple closer. [p.353]
- **Impasse:** Four becomes self-occupied, masochistically drawn to Eight's punitive side, or embroiled in push-pull; Eights blame the mate and walk out, with little insight into their own half. Help: see that anger may merely intensify the relationship; if one partner has a blazing personal interest it ignites the other's support; the Four shifts focus from wanting Eight's attention to a project Eight assists; a Boss applies pressure to make others' projects happen and profits from witnessing a Romantic's interior life. Between Four's emotional range and Eight's practical stability, they stay interested for a long time. [p.353-354]

### Four-Eight Partnership at Work
- Both think they are above the rules; both have an "amoral streak" (usual rules don't apply to Fours, who are special enough to be above the law, or to Eights, who feel stronger than the law). Unbeatable when focused on task; Eights waste energy on appetites, Fours lose focus to emotion. Competition is the key issue. Fours demand special status to cover inner shame yet are innovative and committed to others' emotional needs; Eights are competitive but embody the will and staying power every successful business depends on. [p.354]
- **Cooperation depends on absolute honesty:** both territorial, both want leadership; neither submits, but trust builds when intentions are known. Eights want predictability rather than blind submission and admire a good competitor; Fours want recognition for special contributions. Fours need not be poetic Romantics: also scientists, cowboys, doctors, librarians drawn to self-expression, each wanting a unique contribution never seen before; if powerful Eights support the vision they get complete cooperation. [p.354]
- **Four manager, Eight employee (successful configuration):** the Four initiates a creatively daring project and the Eight shepherds it to completion. Romantics envision and inspire but may not care about the boring work of materializing a concept; a wise Four orients the Eight and relinquishes control. Eights are dedicated once embarked but overreact when their base is threatened; do not change rules mid-motion; Eights oppose policy shifts on principle unless they understand management's view, and may stall a project to force management to the table, which seems personally insulting to Fours. Managers must create opportunity for feedback even when feeling abused; Romantics sensitive to suffering can see the insecurity behind an Eight employee's offensive behavior. [p.354-355]
- **Eight manager, Four employee:** Eights make the rules and then break them, appealing to Fours so long as they have a special connection; rules don't matter, emotional connection matters. Fours who fall out of favor develop a thirst for vengeance matching any Eight's; an irritated Eight becomes vindictive or dismisses the Four without consideration; "the stuff of legend for mediation teams." Coach Eight managers on their impact: what seems good leadership to a Boss seems insulting to a Romantic. [p.355]

## Four with Nine: Romantic-Mediator Couple

- **Overview:** Dramatic emotional bonding. Fours describe a lifelong yearning to be "awakened through love"; Nines seek vitality and an agenda from a mate. Best times: each loves the other dispassionately without expectation or blame; low point: the Romantic craves permanent emotional satisfaction, the Mediator wants "to be handed a life." [p.355-356]
- **Attraction:** Romantics are thrilled to feel accepted, flaws and all; a Nine's patient presence inoculates against fear of abandonment and low self-esteem; Nines avoid conflict and tolerate others. In return Nines want unconditional acceptance and bridle at Four-like conditions ("I could love you if only," "I see what's missing in your character," "I can't love you fully until you make a change"). Both hate criticism and blame others for their fate: Fours see what is missing in a lover; Nines see a partner as the active, aggressive force. [p.356]
- **Disappointment:** Romantics despair at a Mediator's lack of intensity ("going softly to sleep instead of authentic love"; "What's happened to us? We've lost it. Where's the juice?"); they want strong signals of recognition, "acute attraction, exquisite pain, and the full cycle of passion." Being ignored ignites smoldering drama flaring into blame. During an impasse Nines wait and do nothing: space out, busy themselves with secondary activity, energetically disappear; they can truthfully say, "I've done nothing. I have not left you. How could I be neglectful when I'm standing right here?" [p.356]
- **Escalation:** Abandoned Fours "go for the jugular" ("I've been hurt and you did it" form); say the worst to save face. Provocation can be a wake-up call: "You can't fall asleep with your mate acting out." Nines feel alternately rejected and adored without changing their behavior: "Why change if it won't matter anyway?" Long-term couples say Mediators develop special interests (fishing, cards) to be alone, creating distance that makes Mediators attractive again. Nines can see the positive intention behind attacking (being told to wake up feels like criticism but the purpose is to intensify the relationship). Couple profits when either partner finds personal purpose: Nines are patient; Fours are inspired by a self-directed mate. [p.357]

### Four-Nine Partnership at Work
- Common partnership: Four as frontline figure dealing with the public, Nine supervising mechanics. Differences complement at work (goals before bonding). Fours like stimulating creative moments over predictable long-range ventures; Nines want consistent guidelines to eliminate constant choice. Productive: Four relies on Nine to stabilize ideas; Nine's practical input gets equal weight. Crisis: Four bored and alienated by a Nine favoring safe known solutions; "Expect trouble when a disappointed Four meets a Nine on autopilot. The Four may sabotage the business before Nine wakes up." [p.357-358]
- **Four manager, Nine employee:** The stereotype of Romantic managers as preoccupied with appearances is often wrong; Romantics can be as ambitious as any Three in competitive professions; a Four-Nine duo is likely to "meet at Three" and produce with high visibility. A Four manager sets an energized pace that draws employees in and encourages Nines to cultivate the Performer in themselves. Nines blend with surroundings and must be sought out; they may look steadfast while inwardly thinking, "I don't belong here. My efforts are being ignored"; with no grievance stated the manager feels secure; Mediators slow down or spread allegiance to other factions until the letter of resignation arrives. [p.358]
- **Nine manager, Four employee:** Nine managers seem enigmatic (yes and no at once), threatening Four's special status and triggering fear of public humiliation. Extend a welcome that includes Romantics (invite them to observe a meeting, seat them next to an influential figure). Romantic employees watch who gets what ("Who's close to leadership? Who's getting special attention?"); covert favoritism rattles stability, driving withdrawal in shame or intense competition. Both profit from unambiguous guidelines on status and benefits, implemented "with some sweetening from the person in charge." [p.358-359]

## Double Five: Double Observer Couple

- **Opening story:** An American man met his Five wife in Paris; no shared language, they communicated by signs and guidebook phrases, cooked together, rented a rowboat, visited favorite places, and married within two weeks. Wife later: the silence of the courtship allowed a profound nonverbal commitment; not having to speak freed her to feel; no explaining, no drained by expectations, free to interact or not. [p.359]
- **Living together:** Double Fives respect boundaries; from outside little seems to happen (little small talk, hugging) but the unspoken bond can be palpable. Fives relax around people who contain emotions; they describe "an automatic contraction" when others' expectations invade the silence. They may inhabit separate sections of the house each with its own entrance, divide floor space down the middle, meet at prearranged times (children at home, an evening out); plenty of family activity if enough recovery time. "Five hell is an uncontrolled environment where anyone can barge in on you." [p.359-360]
- **Problems:** Withdrawal option frustrates developing intimacy: it's too tempting to cut communication, yet each resents the other's detaching. Anger is a likely meeting place as each moves to Eight at home; "Fives know that they're committed when they're willing to show up and fight." Most annoying: being ignored by a similar; "they had never realized how painful noncommunication could be, until they wanted something and their Five partner silently went away." Censored information may be the norm; both value the freedom of silence but can dread the emotional emptiness of prolonged detachment. Fives begin relationships to fill inner emptiness, but involvement opens a barrage of feelings; even a good time drains when you fight an undertow back to solitude. Each wants an invitation to participate, positions to be drawn out, and feels threatened when it is their turn to initiate. [p.360]
- **Sex:** Not likely the issue: sex is an exciting short-term happening, strictly confidential; Fives sometimes report "feeling used" or "had" when their emotions engage ("Now look what you've done, I can't detach"). Lifestyle: short, meaningful interactions followed by emotional rest; time apart lets each review feelings suspended face to face; conclusions in solitude may be poignant because they fill the inner emptiness. [p.360-361]

### Double Five Partnership at Work
- Positioning determines success. Fives do not flourish in frontline interactions requiring charm or rapid conceptual shifts; they gravitate to information-driven settings, research, depth analysis. Both need time to digest facts and insist on private space, punctuality, precisely worded instructions. [p.361]
- Vision can drop off at the edge of the designated area of responsibility, leaving connectives dangling; each may focus endlessly on a private topic without communicating; Five managers delegate pieces while neglecting a forum for open exchange; Five employees produce pieces that stand alone but don't integrate. Focus: long-range planning, part-to-whole logic, predictable results; competes well on cost efficiency and product excellence; "Economical excellence" is a byword: Fives like what the Scots call **canny transactions** (quality united with thrift). Control of personal time is an attractive part of the job; autonomy in simple matters is a major perk (e.g., disappearing for a break without signing out); free time valued in the same bracket as salary. [p.361]
- Two Observers collude in avoiding confrontation; orderly meetings within formal roles. Five managers commonly adopt wait-and-see; prepare long, ignore pressing signals; difficulty committing resources under uncertainty; fall back on protocol and formal negotiating stance; letters replace face-to-face; intermediaries handle meetings. "Five systems are prone to poor in-house communication": vital connections are lost; Fives reduce data to core constructs and symbols and may overlook remote associations and an emerging matrix of interactions. Helps to interface with aggressive, outgoing promoters/sellers. Closure is hard: "the definitive answer always seems just a few days away." [p.361-362]

## Five with Six: Observer-Trooper Couple

- **Mood:** Like drawing inward during winter: blanketing ourselves with companionship; relate through quiet continuity (reading in the same room without interrupting; meals without pressure to talk). Observer's detachment makes even a phobic Six the active agent; the active role satisfies Six's preoccupation with strength and beauty and relieves Five of leadership. Troopers are motivated when called to duty for others; tenderness safely expressed through loyalty. Primary bond usually mental; "Both understand that there's more to love than feelings." [p.362-363]
- **Danger:** It flourishes as long as Six feels accepted; if the Observer contracts by withholding sex or information, Six paranoia "goes wild" ("met by a blank screen"). Fives are secretive; demands for self-disclosure may drive them deeper into a hidden pocket; silence reads as an admission of guilt to Six. [p.363]
- **What helps:** Six backs off, agrees on a time for discussions, assumes neutrality. Observers need time for real feelings. A Trooper deactivates Five's fear of engulfment by circumscribing a trouble spot ("I want to talk about who does the dishes" beats "talk about us"). Five helps by revealing thinking: an honest "I don't know" is more reassuring than silence; otherwise Sixes imagine divorce court. "An Observer holds enormous power in this relationship because a Six's fears are activated by lack of presence." [p.363]
- **Affection:** Insecure Sixes badger for reassurance ("Do you still love me?" is a genuine concern); just showing up to deal with trouble is a Five's testimony of commitment. Both periodically want to step back (Five: "I can do without this"; waiting is torture to a Six struggling with fight or flight). Long-term staying power: both known for long relationships; low-maintenance; pull together against adversity. Observers cerebralize feelings by defining romance as an action ("Are flowers appropriate for birthdays?" "How can I prepare for a 'spontaneous date'?"); Sixes are susceptible to affection: warmth disarms doubt; "a single tender moment can become a cornerstone of commitment when it is cemented in the mind forever." [p.363-364]

### Five-Six Partnership at Work
- Both mental types; risk of a partnership "gone dry"; ideas replace action; both in the fear triad (exaggerated attention to obstacles; overestimate competitors; much troubleshooting). Not consistently high profile; not self-promotional; strength in strategy, teaching, planning, discriminating analysis. Observers want to see how pieces fit the long-term whole; hard to close until every factor is analyzed; mirrors Six's procrastination in the face of success. Fix: others do the first cut or first draft; the pair sets the mission and reenters to refine. [p.364-365]
- **Five manager, Six employee:** Fives may not see that Sixes need personal contact; technical communication; logical not supportive; information parceled out by need to know, making Sixes feel disadvantaged or played off against peers. Open forums are a plus (Sixes can question authority gracefully); closed meetings bother Troopers: "Why wasn't I notified? Why are we being separated from each other? Where is all this leading?" Faced with isolation and censored information, a Six's quest for reassurance can turn ugly; ill-prepared for conflict, the Five retreats or tries to force the recalcitrant out; any move toward visibility relaxes employee suspicions. [p.365]
- **Six manager, Five employee:** Six managers fear future insubordination; Five employees control by silent nonparticipation. Six sees withheld hostility; Five sees intrusive expectations; amplified when the Six's style seems ambivalent (Trooper trying out an idea to see where employees stand; imprecision alienates an Observer). A Five wants to know exactly how much energy for how long, and falls into an "I don't get it" stance rather than improvising. Manager should stop speculating and stick to a plan without backtracking: Five employees feel respected by a fully conceived directive. A weekly meeting is the most effective formality: Six managers need information, Five employees need time to think before committing. [p.365-366]

## Five with Seven: Observer-Epicure Couple

- **Overview:** Epicures "eat experience" (travel, classes, several projects). Their variety acts as an in-house sorting system for Fives who spend energy cautiously. Anecdote from Enneagram couples panels: the Epicure discovered the Enneagram first, checked out a class, went home to convince the Five; the Five often continues to pursue the study. Another common scenario: parallel play, "You do your thing and I'll do mine." Outer-directed Epicure not limited by Five's inner orientation because both are independent; Sevens find things to do that Fives enjoy vicariously; neither demands much time together. Shared diagram line breeds understanding: Fives admire Seven's social ease, Sevens are quieted by Five's steady presence. [p.366-367]
- **Intimacy:** through a common vision or children; Sevens need exterior focus; Fives access feelings when not the center. "Paradoxically the detached Observer often becomes the couple's emotional pillar." Fives at home move to Eight (powerful force in the family); Sevens find security in Five-like retreat in short stretches (Epicure: a day or two of retreat makes me a homebody; Five: "Yes, dear, but you're rarely at home"). [p.367]
- **Likeness:** Both fear types ward off danger differently and both confuse thinking with feeling. Fives living in Seven's stress look like they're coming alive but report terrible inner tension; if their search for safety succeeds they discover Epicurean pleasure in company; the common scenario places the Seven as the couple's "social wedge." Fives don't notice analysis replacing feelings; Sevens don't see activity as escape; both divert attention from emotion (Sevens distract, Fives detach). Bad sign: Seven moves enthusiastically forward while Five backs into isolation. Each secretly looks down on the other (Seven bored that the relationship isn't radiant; Five sees superficiality); parallel play divides them into separate orbits. Remedy: each embodies the other's orientation (Fives invest time, energy, enthusiasm; Sevens develop single-minded concentration). [p.367-368]

### Five-Seven Partnership at Work
- Both mental types; ideas replace action; Sevens' minds resemble a **decision tree** (one concept spawns branches that all seem viable); Five thinking is more logical, a single idea investigated for years in isolation. Favors strategy and research; without follow-through and closure, ideas never actualize. [p.368]
- **Usual arrangement:** Five in the office, Seven in the field. Five managers: orderly, need full data, secure when limiting effort to a particular area. Five systems either anchor a Seven's efforts or become so narrow the employee rebels. Sevens favor "sweet rebellion": the wise see how the manager's central interests can be augmented by an appropriate network; unwise Seven employees revolutionize the central concept, do as they please, rationalize results. Five managers need accountability and find loosely defined connectives stressful; on the low side they constrict and repeat a formula that has worked; on the high side a Seven discovers the coordinates unifying the manager's researched sectors. [p.368-369]
- **Conflict avoidance:** Epicures rationalize problems out of existence; Observers entrench behind answering machines; both see through problems rather than deal with them; unrecognized tension builds and it can be startling when feelings finally surface. Mediation: break problem areas into small manageable chunks, which "works wonders for people in the Enneagram fear triad" by softening the belief that anger creates permanent damage. [p.369]
- **Seven manager, Five employee:** Seven managers enjoy last-minute decisions, look scattered; Five employees caught in contradictory information retreat and wait rather than press for clarification; wait-and-see does not imply agreement but protects from reaching out. Hand over a clearly demarcated project not depending on networking; Fives produce precisely what is expected without elaboration or coordination with other sectors; volunteer information only when asked; when committed they stabilize chaos long enough for Seven to find an angle. [p.369]

## Five with Eight: Observer-Boss Couple (begins)

- The Boss is arguably the most assertive type and the Observer the most retracted; Eights want more, Fives have minimal needs; Eights are outer-directed, Fives retreat. Yet frequently paired with intrinsic affinity; each is the other's point of security and stress, so long-term partners resemble each other: the aggressive Boss looks remarkably tame and the Observer seems to have taken assertiveness training. [p.369-370]
- Fives are far more outspoken in the security of home, dovetailing with Eight's demand for truth; an Observer can experience emotional energy and, though detesting every battle, usually concur that "Boss lessons" produce beneficial results; anger pushes Fives to have feelings in the moment ("It's hard to detach when you're highly energized"); the relationship can initiate Observers into emotional life while Eights learn to wait. [p.370]
- Common interaction: Five becomes overwhelmed; Eight complains about holding back. Fives can drain force from an interaction as efficiently as a partner pumps it in. Currents like tides: Eights press with oceanic force, checked by an undertow of withdrawal; Fives' emotions well up when safely alone; Eights' emotions contract forcefully under stress. The couple often lives at Eight for long periods: loud, meaningful late-night debates. [p.370]

### Five with Eight: Observer-Boss Couple (continued)
- Observers are initially overwhelmed and want to flee, but living with a Boss opens feelings; Eights are often disarmed by inner quiet; an Observer's self-control can encourage Eights to look within rather than pick a fight. [p.370]
- Both prize personal autonomy and freedom of action; neither gets enmeshed in a partner's agenda. High side: blunt, honest speech. Low side: no compromise, little guilt about effect on the other; Fives sometimes think emotional pain stems from poor self-control, which lets them escape responsibility for others' feelings; an Eight in pain may even the score; revenge can play a part, with Eight pushing for top-dog position. [p.371]
- When the couple meets at Five they develop respect for uninterrupted time alone ("like living by yourself, all the while knowing that someone you love is in close proximity"). Low side: each withholds affection; a Five's withdrawal "broadcasts through the house as a loud, petulant pout"; impossible to force an unwilling Five to cooperate (energy drains from the encounter); Eights hang out their own Do Not Disturb sign: "a silence that shouts, 'Go away! I don't need you and I don't care.'" [p.371]
- **Exchange of qualities:** Fives see the Boss's assertiveness as an antidote to their chronic inability to go public ("knowing that life is worth fighting for can be healing to detached people"; speaking impulsively is exhilarating); Eights trust an Observer who keeps confidentiality and shows emotional control. A successful marriage of qualities yields an Observer who springs into action and a patient Boss who can wait. [p.371-372]

### Five-Eight Partnership at Work
- Fives are trusted in business transactions because they neither seduce nor oppose with emotions; low-impact; deflect attention by concentrating on tasks. People talk about private interests and the Five nods, creating an impression of agreement. [p.372]
- Observers rarely implement their own creations: "from the boardroom to the boiler room." Implementation involves heated personal interaction, so Fives conduct business by e-mail or fax. **Classic Enneagram story:** a Five enters his own building after everyone has gone home, leaves instructions on each employee's computer terminal, avoids the cleaning team, and disappears before 9 a.m.; his trusted Eight picks up the messages and goes straight to work, communicating by e-mail, often without personal contact for months. [p.372]
- Five managers' neutral quality attracts employees; they radiate in-control leadership, are resilient negotiators (do not take pressure personally), and bring dispassionate analysis that frees Eights to speak freely. Good interaction: Eight explodes, Five detaches, both drop the incident without grudge. [p.372]
- **Most frequent conflict:** An Eight employee fills the power vacuum of absentee management; on return the manager sees the vocal Eight as insubordinate and staff feel squeezed. Five managers are famous for minimal presence. The situation brightens when an Observer comes into the open; once facts are out an Eight can surrender control. [p.372-373]
- **Eight manager, Five employee:** "My way or the highway" polarizes staff (love them or hate them); a wise Five supplies data and stands back. Natural alliance but strategy differences can be deadly: Fives develop conclusions slowly and dislike risking premature analysis; Eights wanting action publicly vilify a cautious voice. Observers find a niche in developing reports, best presented face to face "however irrelevant the meeting may seem": "The way to an Eight's heart is through personal presence and full information, both of which run counter to Five's privacy stance." Information updates are a neutral way for Fives to show presence; reworking an analysis reaffirms commitment. [p.373]
- **Mediation focus on delivery style:** Eights are intrusive; Fives are sensitive to intrusion; each claims the other is controlling (the Eight by stepping forward, the Five by stepping back to exercise "remote control"). Each relies on the other's endowments: Fives shielded from confrontation when a Boss moves; Eights working with an Observer learn to think before acting. [p.373]

## Five with Nine: Observer-Mediator Couple

- **Nonverbal appreciation:** Both value being understood without having to ask (asking risks rejection or humiliation); they funnel feelings through an external focus (planning for the family, the supermarket run); shared activities create presence; they define emotional well-being as "the way we feel when we travel" or "how it feels when we go on a walk"; "presence" so embedded it seems ordinary; they feel each other in their children and sightsee through each other's eyes. [p.373-374]
- **Scrutiny:** The Observer may become an object of scrutiny; Nines take on feelings of significant others; Five's nonverbal signals fall on a sensitive receptor; the Observer's needs may become the centerpiece of Nine's life. Fives cannot endure emotional dependency and can be cold and aloof when a mate develops "unwarranted" expectations; an affectionate Mediator wants an evening hanging out, but open-ended time makes a Five think, "And do what?" [p.374]
- **Severe situation:** the Observer habitually retracts, leaving Nine angry and abandoned. Improves when Nines establish an ongoing goal, which gives evenings a positive focus and stations the Five as "trusted adviser"; both are more effective when attention is deflected toward others (Fives like to counsel loved ones; Nines take on another's life as their own). [p.374]
- **Delayed reactions and no-conflict agreement:** Both have delayed emotional reactions (Mediator's position emerges slowly; Fives suspend feelings during interactions); both dislike pressure (Nines dig in and go stubborn; Fives withdraw from expectations). A no-conflict agreement lets each be absorbed in a private world; the Mediator's spread of activities doesn't disturb an Observer unless the home routine is affected; an oblivious Nine won't question the Five's compartmentalized life. It can also deaden the relationship: Fives detach from feelings, Nines go on autopilot with obsessive routine; both censor information, so orbits spin apart; "harmony in the home" can really be conflict avoidance, dropping Nine's energy and making Five remote. Paradox: the relationship profits from "a small explosion that breaks the holding pattern": "A good fight enlivens people, and a good cry can bring them together." Confrontation thoughts: Observer "It would be easy to drop this"; Mediator's "Should I be here or not?" The relationship is an ideal arena for Fives to get comfortable with feelings and for Nines to develop unequivocal, permanent commitment. [p.374-375]

### Five-Nine Partnership at Work
- Neither self-promotional; both dislike competing for status; Fives want validation for their thinking, Nines flourish with unsolicited personal respect. Downside of apparent harmony: lack of initiation; each waits to be drawn out, so work roles define the relationship. Usual division: Nine foreground (calls, meetings, outreach), Five background (data analysis); the Observer's sparse well-directed schemes are the foundation Nines shape into a workable system. Productivity follows the **law of inertia**: at the peak, new goals follow from a completed cycle; at the low, "Nines follow the rule book and Fives communicate by phone." Both need a jump start: Nines are enlivened by camaraderie, Fives by exchange of ideas. Short meaningful policy meetings; Fives give input, Nines keep the ball rolling. Hard to get each other's attention (Fives nearly unreachable when absorbed; Nines cling to a position for fear of derailment). [p.375-376]
- **Five manager, Nine employee:** Remote control; meetings content-driven, isolated areas of a project; employees feel decisions were finalized in private; sensitive to being overlooked, Nines inwardly rebel at "being told without being asked." Worst-case: brief directives at a short general meeting, no feedback; offended Nines "without speaking an angry word" defuse follow-through. Helps: a forum of collegiality; Nines need to know who and what they are working for; mottoes like "Shoulder to shoulder, and eye to eye"; Mediators don't flourish in isolation. Fives, interested in information pipelines, may find Nines are barometers of group dynamics and can articulate group aspirations and political currents. Five's precise thinking cuts through a Mediator's tendency to spread attention; Five managers who interact draw a following among Nines. [p.376-377]
- **Nine manager, Five employee:** Nines develop a system, suiting Fives, who focus best when expectations are predictable and who produce wholeheartedly within a time frame, with little of the competitive edge Nines have trouble managing. Mediators promote and network within a secure context requiring no renewed decisions. Style adjustment: Fives want highly controlled contact about specific tasks versus Nine's determination to hear everyone; Fives want concise talk, Nines prolong discussion indefinitely; Nines learn closure; Fives learn meetings serve solidarity as well as structuring. [p.377-378]
- **Intervention:** Both avoid conflict, creating distance when open communication is needed; a disgruntled Five reduces performance and withholds advice; each in a corner; a Nine manager resorts to rules while resentment targets Five's unwillingness to speak. Establish an arena for negotiations because both "control by nondoing and by hoarding energy"; schedule difficult discussions well in advance (Nines need time to discover an independent opinion; Fives negotiate better with a predetermined format for handling emotions). [p.378]

## Double Six: Double Trooper Couple

- **Contrary thought vs negative thinking:** A Six's habit of intense internal questioning makes the Six seem oppositional ("Yes, but," "What about the other side?"). Sixes distinguish negativity (a put-down, a denial of the positive) from contrary thought (clarifying, illuminates the opposite side). Double Sixes may welcome a partner raising hard questions; cross-examination eliminates doubt. [p.378]
- **Seesaw:** When one falls into fear it elevates the other to envision brighter possibilities. Pay close attention to Troopers without confirming or dismissing ideas as worst-case thinking; Sixes feel misunderstood and retreat if anxiety is trivialized. When both are anxious about the same external event, they unite in an us-against-the-world alliance; mutual doubt can produce a **folie a deux** (shared false belief): "If it frightens us now, think how bad this could get!" [p.379]
- **High side:** a source of strength; pull together under pressure; join civic action groups; effective in underdog settings where doubt is realistic ("We all feel somewhat powerless when we're beginners, newcomers, or when we play against the odds"); easier to act for an underdog cause than an overtly promising venture. [p.379]
- **Practical:** Double Sixes need activities where they coast on routine ("the schedule says lunch at noon, and once they both arrive, doubts melt away with a welcoming smile"); scheduled check-ins are a must because lack of contact spurs paranoia; routine vanishes contrary thinking. Pleasure heals worriers; like Ones, Troopers mistrust a rosy future; they need something to look forward to (a trip to the country, a walk in the park). Envisioning the future can also give pleasure; hope propels action; a shared dream generates faith. [p.379]
- **Mentalized commitment:** Sixes are highly emotional but their feelings dedicate to a mental vision; they think of each other constantly, probe motives, analyze commitment; from outside the couple may not look affectionate; preoccupied with meaning they may neglect the physical and emotional attraction that drew them; sexuality and adventure feel like "wonderful side effects"; they need a "mentalized" commitment: to be certain and know what's "really happening"; the preoccupation can crowd out other ways of relating. [p.379-380]

### Double Six Partnership at Work
- Troopers differ widely at work; common denominator: preoccupation with authority. **Phobics** seek a strong protector and are grateful when someone holds the line in confrontation; **counterphobics** (equally afraid) come off as office agitators by questioning those in charge. [p.380]
- **Illustration:** a phobic Six who is a self-preservation subtype focuses on affection to reduce fear and seeks approval from people in power, easily passing as a Two; counterphobic Sixes fight fear aggressively, resembling an Eight or a One; if also the strength/beauty one-to-one subtype, the aggression focuses on individuals holding power at work. [p.380]
- **Six manager (with Six employee):** Careful not to overreact; inner doubt makes employee concerns sound like criticism. Loyalty is built on trust: generous to proven loyal employees; tolerant of a great deal of neurotic or selfish behavior as long as the alliance holds. Low side: a mutual comfort operation where the manager fears alienating the employee and the employee fears confronting the boss. Sixes want reliable backup from higher-ups and test employee loyalty; the most common test: lay out an ambiguous problem and see who acts for the benefit of others and who for self-interest. Managers vacillate between too lax (uncomfortable in authority) and too strict (afraid of being challenged); both from inner uncertainty; focus on task because it determines how people behave toward each other; discuss worst-case outcomes openly, including that following a safe solution can be dangerous when real risks must be taken; invite a trusted authority to back the manager's opinions; a friendly voice in the hierarchy plus realistic assessment of obstacles liberates Six managers "afraid to state their fears for fear of looking fearful." [p.381]
- **Procrastination and ambiguity:** attention wanders to counterarguments; ambiguous messages paralyze; an innocent comment like "We need an evaluation" brings major doubt attacks and triggers the manager's fear of antiauthoritarian sentiment. Soften communication: "This is my thinking about the project"; "The project's being continued, and we need to evaluate." Provide prior explanation of any deviation from expectations; Troopers expect the worst until informed and notice discrepancy between promise and delivery. "Forewarning forestalls rebellion in working with Troopers." [p.381-382]

## Six with Seven: Trooper-Epicure Couple (begins)

- Both in the fear triad, handling anxiety differently: Six overtly tentative and cautious; Seven breezy; Sevens diffuse fear through backup plans and may unconsciously depend on Sixes to act out their underlying paranoia. Both embellish events with an imagined outcome (Seven's positive future visioning, Six's worst case, both projections). An Epicure may dismiss a Six's concerns as figments; told to lighten up, Troopers become more afraid. Opposite assumptions: Seven sees unlimited possibilities, Six feels bound by duty and hard work. Reality testing is central; best case and worst case can meet in the middle; staying in close contact (daily calls, shared projects, family meetings) forestalls unreal speculation. Seven's pleasure is an antidote to Six's doubt; a loyal Six in trouble heals a mate terrified of pain. Difficulties arise if Seven manipulates the truth in the interest of freedom. [p.382-383]

### Six with Seven: Trooper-Epicure Couple (continued)
- **Inconsistency provokes paranoia:** Six retaliates by threatening to bail out; Sevens are magnetized by a disappearing option, so Six's wavering loyalty can make the relationship look more desirable. [p.383]
- **Commitment issues:** Sixes want guarantees before committing; Sevens hate being pinned down. Seven's flights of fancy and time-consuming sidetracks read as betrayal to an insecure partner. Reassurance both ways: for the Trooper that Seven is faithful; for the Epicure that Six can relinquish control. Six should focus on actions rather than possibilities ("A Seven's schemes are far more elaborate than what will actually materialize"). [p.383]
- **Self-indulgence cycle:** To a cautious Six, Seven's plans look hugely self-indulgent ("What's the line between outside interests and hedonism?" "Are you committed or not?"). If Six becomes meddlesome, Six suffers and Seven wants out; the self-absorbed Epicure may not empathize and thinks the Trooper is stirring up trouble: "If I feel pain, then I'm in the wrong relationship." Criticism cuts to Seven's positive outlook ("There could be something wrong with me if I have to ask for help"). [p.383]
- **Timing and approach:** An oblique approach beats a forced discussion; Sixes are notorious for insisting on sit-down panic sessions airing doubts, but Epicures hear a buffered complaint in short manageable doses; a frightened Seven rationalizes and runs, but if there is no immediate threat complaints eventually come to the table. Frame differences in the perspective of past goodwill and aim at "the light at the end of the tunnel"; do not fixate on an admission of guilt or remorse about the past. [p.383-384]
- **Sevens get the couple out of the house:** "A sunny afternoon of shared activity can do more toward reassuring Six about commitment than hundreds of hours of talk." Long-term couples meet in the middle: Six learns to tell private doubts from what Seven will actually do; Seven learns to keep baseline agreements without inserting an escape clause. [p.384]

### Six-Seven Partnership at Work
- Can compound procrastination or move toward mutual goals; both change their minds on short notice (Sixes doubt, Sevens get distracted); ideas broached and forgotten; endless talk and meetings; neither identifies own behavior as procrastination (Sevens see options as facets of a single plan; Sixes see doubts as accurate problem solving). Both live in the future (Sixes warding off catastrophe, Sevens envisioning plans). Balance: Epicures sustain a vision, Troopers flourish in demanding work; they overcome "dilettante and doubter tendencies": Sevens by staying focused during difficulty, Sixes by daring to believe. [p.384-385]
- **Six manager, Seven employee:** Sixes dislike having to inspire or promote obedience and resent supervising an evasive staff; may overreact to what feels like deliberate disrespect from Sevens who may be oblivious; Sevens innovate without realizing they have redefined duties; resentments build if evasion is sensed; the Epicure may need an explosion to wake up and won't admit malicious intent. Sixes sometimes think a full admission of guilt prevents backsliding; Sevens follow the spirit not the letter; what looks guilty to others seems "a learning experience." Staged checkpoints clarify intentions; chicanery is magnified in the mind of a Six who expects deception; "Sevens are unusually slippery in supervision." [p.385]
- **Seven manager, Six employee:** Sevens delegate and parcel out; prefer short-range goals; change the course of action without notice; inconsistency raises a Six's hackles ("The goals changed, and I wasn't consulted. Why? Am I being dumped?"); Sixes are actually flexible given adequate explanation and very loyal once reassured about job security. Mediation: each enters the other's point of view; Sixes learn to take action more easily; Sevens learn to do one thing at a time without becoming afraid. [p.385-386]

## Six with Eight: Trooper-Boss Couple

- **Courtship:** Eights, male or female, are active in courtship, eliminating much of Six's doubt; Eights find security in taking charge and offering protection, which fits Sixes' insecurities; freed from initiating, Troopers feel sought after and needed; Eights are confident about sexuality and success, liberating to Sixes who contract and feel guilty about pleasure. [p.386]
- **Body and mind, not sentiment:** Shared aversion to "gushy sentimentality"; sweet support can sound faked to types who see the world as dangerous; devotion expressed through physicality and ideas; trust emerges through observation (seeing that the partner acts for the benefit of others). Both expect adversity and stand together: phobic Sixes stand behind Eight partners; counterphobic Sixes stand alongside. Excellent interaction: the bigger-than-life Eight relies on the advice of the mental, strategic Six (Sixes wait, observe complicated motives, are political about repercussions); "Equipped with good counsel, Eights can move mountains." Eights value loyalty, Sixes value strength; Eights are most determined when challenged. [p.386]
- **Typical position and its limits:** Eight in control, Six in support. Without a barricade to storm, Eights find it easier to challenge life or support someone else's thrust than to search for a personal agenda; they coast, make trouble, or support the Six's agenda; helpful in a support position, "a royal pain when they take charge of someone else's life instead of improving their own." [p.386-387]
- **Protector or bully:** Sixes shift from seeing Eights as protectors to bullies; Eights see a thin line between Sixes as worthy of protection and wimps. Sixes need constant reassurance, which seems weak to Eights who deny their own weakness; impatience with doubt and vacillation: "Why can't you make up your mind?" "Are you committed to this, or are you lying?" Pushing for truth or to revitalize, Eights become punitive and say the worst; Six's imagination "moves to the divorce court"; the cornered Six, phobic or counterphobic, will fight. Subtext is power: the Eight can't surrender control until the mate looks strong and trustworthy; the Six can't commit fully until the Eight looks less dangerous. Paradoxically horrendous battles can have positive repercussions: the Six goaded into terrifying anger has said the worst and survived; the bullied Six has set necessary boundaries; the Eight sees the limits and need not push further. [p.387-388]

### Six-Eight Partnership at Work
- Under pressure Eights charge ahead, throw energy, take over; Sixes fall back and consider consequences, widening the gap. Eights minimize opposition (inaction feels dangerously weak); Sixes' imagination magnifies the opposition and the negative consequences of Eights' rash actions, so Eights look like the problem. [p.388]
- **Six manager, Eight employee:** Six managers must take command from the start. Sixes are impressed by Eights' will and tenacity, which intimidate if Eights bend rules, shift priorities and argue and self-justify "under the guise of improving the system." Caught between intimidation and wanting to be liked, the Six wants either to fire the Eight or pacify by overcompensating; ambivalence about authority is projected onto the Eight, who now looks capable of the worst (complaints to superiors/shareholders, litigation, misrepresentation). Most likely behavior: wavering between nonenforcement and "nailing" the Eight for infractions. "Clear boundaries and confrontation are comforting to Eight employees. They see enforcement as competent leadership." [p.388]
- **Eight manager, Six employee:** Secure Eight managers see themselves as top dog and spread the mantle of power and protection over "their" people, alleviating Sixes' doubt; Eight managers can become workplace tyrants, driving fear types into fight or flight; Sixes may doctor bad news or devise ways to avoid the manager ("Why put yourself in the line of fire when it's easier to duck?"); crises when Sixes hide and Eights pursue; "Troopers see Eights as controlling, and the Boss sees Sixes as out of control." Eight managers should realize how their forceful personas affect others; set up face-to-face meetings; "Sixes have excellent ideas that are often coupled with doubt. Eights can see through doubt to make ideas happen." [p.389]

## Six with Nine: Trooper-Mediator Couple

- **Overview:** Nines emanate a calm, reassuring presence ("coming home to genial surroundings after a hard day"). Typical: Nine the comforter, the couple's fears carried and voiced by Six; Six's skittish movement between commitment and doubt is familiar to Mediators who also struggle to choose. They share a significant line in the diagram: Nines in stress take on a startled Six-like persona ("cold panic") which elicits sympathy from Troopers. Both find it easier to act in the name of another than for themselves: mutual support or a battle about "who should go first"; each must define personal goals; loyalty and merging flip into lack of initiative. [p.389]
- **Meet at Three:** Either can suppress emotions when moving into action; Nines who forget feelings can conduct a love affair on automatic pilot; doing without feeling is typical of a Six in stress; doubting Troopers suspend feelings and fall back on conditional commitment ("just until we see how it works out"). High side: a lively couple; Nines are unstoppable once geared up and love finding the Performer in themselves; for Troopers action eliminates doubt and makes success feel safe; full participation if they don't pack the schedule to block out feelings. [p.390]
- **Anger:** Each avoids anger, though Sixes find Nine's passive anger less threatening than the critical anger of One or the periodic rage of Eight (an angry Mediator looks stubborn rather than dangerous). A no-conflict agreement reduces tension or lets energy sink into safe routine; the couple should risk anger (Mediators find a position when angry; anger is often buried at the root of a Trooper's fears). [p.390]
- **Low:** Inaction feeds doubt; the doubtful Six prods Nine ("Get moving and quit spacing out"), Nines entrench when pushed. All-time low: Nine indecision colludes with Six doubt; each blames the other; "You go first" and "Why should I change when you aren't trying?" Conditional love in an uncommitted period: Six commits on conditions ("This is to please our parents"; "Until we're launched in our careers"); Nines sit on the fence ("Should I be here or not?"). "Strangely, these two can overlook the depth of their feelings about each other for many years before they finally make up their minds that they're actually in love." Remedy: either initiates action, breaking the stalemate; personal agendas without insisting the other join; action breaks Nine's inertia and realistic progress softens Six's fears. [p.390-391]

### Six-Nine Partnership at Work
- Usually meets at Three, "the position of our American corporate ideal." Nines shift into Performer mode when secure; Sixes doubt achievements but rise to professional demands. Both overbook (Nines can't say no, distracted by details; Sixes struggle with continuous production and fall behind). [p.391]
- **Six manager, Nine employee:** Six managers should never become isolated; the best medicine for doubt is continuous interaction with staff; reliable feedback absorbs anxiety; periodic time off to regroup; time to debrief doubts with staff who keep abreast of the timetable brings out leadership. Nines measure energy against task requirements and lapse into perfunctory routine when energy drops; Six managers, sensitive to insubordination, may see lack of initiative as deliberate sabotage; excuses ("It wasn't my responsibility"; "I didn't have time") enrage them; Troopers build a case; Nine's passive evasion creates a perception of hidden agendas; both mobilize at the deadline for a last-ditch save; deadly combination if each blames the other. Conflict resolution: focus on high points of past projects; Nines produce when accepted, a positive framework softens doubt. [p.391-392]
- **Incentives:** Both dislike win-lose situations; Mediators want tangible rewards but not a competitive atmosphere; a Trooper's performance may plummet after a public win. Good Six incentive: long-term benefits, job security. Good Nine incentive: unsolicited recognition (saves putting themselves forward). [p.392]
- **Nine manager, Six employee:** Mediators avoid conflict, which frightens Troopers; ambiguous loyalty read as breach of trust; Nines reconcile by hearing every position and apportioning action to each, triggering Six's fight or flight (fighting Six: "Who's side are you on?"; fleeing Six: "So why stick around?"). Nines build procedure-oriented systems that "run by themselves": neutral, methodical, safe, an employment haven cushioning Six's anxiety. Once committed to a goal, Nines implement predictably and noncompetitively, giving certainty. Long-standing Six-Nine partnerships are common; most reported: Six generates ideas, practical Nine shapes them into a product or service. [p.392-393]

## Double Seven: Double Epicure Couple

- **Mirror-image mate:** On wish lists Epicures choose the features they see in themselves ("energetic, independent, optimistic, successful, and adventurous"); a companion who hikes their trails, goes to their movies, organizes their pet projects. Double Sevens commonly bond as playmates and confidants but rarely commit lastingly; relating to an idealized version of yourself lacks depth, substance or "worthy opposition"; Sevens form relationships all around the Enneagram, typically with dramatically different mates; fascination and gluttony make it tempting to "play the field" once the initial peak passes. [p.393]
- **Highs:** "We can have it all. It's like owning an imaginary jet. You could wind up anywhere on the planet and keep on flying forever." Example: a California Seven nicknamed Blondie by her husband has the license plate "All Now" on her BMW. Sevens as the most spontaneous type: want it all and want to share it right now. [p.393-394]
- **Lows (not obvious to the couple):** They collude that spontaneity and freedom are the mainstays of healthy relating; "predictable" and "permanent" mean "boring" and "stuck." Example: a monogamous double Seven couple who saw each other daily but lived in separate apartments for 15+ years, then bought a house "renovated into condos with separate doorbells and exits for when it gets boring." "Boredom can really be a code word for terror": fear of "losing the whole world for just one person"; "Binding yourself to one sacrifices the many"; Unused to the sobering effects of deep emotion, Sevens conspire to keep things light. Helps when a partner sees the built-in limitation of avoiding limits and entertains the full cycle of pleasure, sorrow, joy and pain. [p.394]

### Double Seven Partnership at Work
- Success depends on positioning: short-term, fast-paced projects are safer than long-range plans; beginnings easy, follow-through difficult; treat goals as a series of independent tasks not needing continuous management; Sevens learn quickly, efficient in new situations or when they plan and delegate; disaster if responsible for follow-through. Every idea generates instant "thought trees" ("Look at all the ramifications!"); a new approach either draws the pair into inventive collaboration or separates them as each pursues a personal vision. [p.394-395]
- Most doubles stay balanced by accessing wing points and security and risk reactions; unlikely to find a double Seven professionally viable by bouncing from option to option; Sevens can be goal-oriented and aggressively Eight-like in competition, especially when their sense of entitlement is questioned; they can look like frightened Sixes when options close (text continues). [p.395]

### Double Seven Partnership at Work (continued from p.395)
- Sevens who lean to the security point of Five may retire into a self-maintaining solitude that lets the other Seven run the business alone. The most difficult interaction: when either partner leans toward the perfectionism of One. "Criticism is a real Achilles' heel for Sevens because it calls their positive self-image into question"; planning fills in reality's rough spots, and criticism disturbs "the mix of fantasy and fact." [p.395]
- **Entitlement:** "I want more" shifts to "I deserve more" ("I deserve more than I'm getting, and I don't deserve to worry about how much it costs"); Sevens don't postpone gratification; anticipated abundance justifies taking their share now. Hard for this team to streamline or consolidate; when more is around the corner, why do with less? They plan a new approach or start another system instead of downsizing; plans speed up and become more elaborate in troubled times. [p.395-396]
- **Seven manager:** creates and delegates; wise ones find a detail person; unwise ones dismiss priorities as details; once captivated by an idea, problems of actualization blur; delegating follow-through to a Seven employee compounds confusion, especially when the manager delegates and disappears. **Seven employees:** equalize authority; please peers and befriend management through charm and shared interests; "sunny-morning feeling... a perpetual good start, regardless of the weather"; if one stays focused the other remains enthusiastic; work is fine as long as it's interesting; if the manager veers off, the employee follows. [p.396]
- **Typical dispute:** magnified expectations (expected more than got, via faulty communication). Mediation: discriminate between ideas and actualities; be explicit lest direct orders be heard as suggestions and thinking out loud mistaken for fact. Negative feedback to two Sevens who won't face pain: structure negotiation in the spirit of better communication rather than emphasizing poor judgment; Sevens are enraged when positive self-image is questioned and deflect with "prolific excuses"; set error in the context of a fresh start. [p.396]

## Seven with Eight: Epicure-Boss Couple

- **"Fun hunger":** Plenty of juice. The Enneagram positions the pair as wing points of each other, producing the blend of lust plus gluttony Palmer calls "fun hunger": a promising match for creative entertainment, good sex, adventure; both play hard, relatively guilt-free; account to themselves rather than others; both dislike the word "should." Independence is a mutual must: a Boss makes and breaks rules; "an Epicure sees dogma as death"; both free agents, collude in preferring self-expression to self-improvement; an unspoken agreement gives each time and space; both look to themselves for support and withdraw for self-repair when hurt. Eights would rather die than admit "You hurt my feelings"; Sevens switch to other options as a defense against pain. [p.397]
- **Power struggle:** Eight tries to limit Seven's activities and control the schedule; the Epicure makes excuses. "Maybe we can meet for lunch" heard as possibility by one, commitment by the other; a disappointed Eight wants someone to blame; fine lines between a good excuse and lying (rationales sound like lies to the truth-minded Boss); frustrated by lack of contact, Eights pursue and try to wrench a full confession; Sevens are hard to pin down. Growing commitment stresses the Seven into critical One-like behavior. A good fight enlivens relationships for Eights; for Sevens anger is a gauge of increasing commitment. Sevens find it easier to walk away than compromise ("Why endure pain unless it matters? Why stand and fight unless you care?"). [p.397-398]
- **Meet at Five:** Eights stressed contract into Five to think and heal (loud Do Not Disturb behind closed doors, but they love private space anyway); secure Sevens relax into Five (less important options fall away; turn to the home). Mutual quiet restores peace of mind but at the expense of emotional insight; they "forget" and forgo the tedium of discussing and forgiving; a cycle of denial in which the same problem recurs. Good development: either initiates a safe way to talk rather than deny and forget. They resolve differences when seated in each other's wing-point behaviors; "fun hunger" cushions "the high-decibel clashes that occur when Seven narcissism meets a punishing Eight." [p.398]

### Seven-Eight Partnership at Work
- Both self-motivated; little lag between impulse and action; each assumes his or her own perceptions are accurate; more aware of what they want than their impact on others; unlikely to question themselves or realize they've veered off track. Sevens veer to explore options not goal related (several projects related in a Seven's mind but not to the literal-minded Boss). Eights veer through an **all-or-nothing production pattern** (full throttle or shut down; deadly boring when energy is dead, but at the deadline they galvanize into fast-forward and work around the clock). Differences magnified under pressure: the Boss steaming toward the goal while the Epicure's attention is divided. [p.398-399]
- **Seven manager, Eight employee:** Seven managers must prove themselves to Eights; multidirectional leadership looks ill advised and full of loopholes; Eights want clear regulations and a fair-minded supervisor; unusually sensitive to manipulation; see negative intentions; black-or-white: manager is fair and honest or unfair and a liar. Eights, attracted to power and protecting their interests, often rise within organizations; an astute manager can rely on Eights to "beg, borrow, bully, and bless" their way through adversity; given reciprocal loyalty and a logical game plan an Eight provides practical day-to-day support that lets the Epicure network and broaden scope; Seven's visionary capability produces an inventive technology or ingenious process that enrolls Eight's support. [p.399]
- **Eight manager, Seven employee:** Eight managers are famous for selectively enforcing rules, which Sevens take as permission to selectively obey; neither sees bending rules as dishonest ("rules are more like suggested guidelines, something to fall back on when you get confused"). Eight managers famously deny difficult information (can literally forget what they don't want to hear); a Seven reinforces denial; when trouble hits the Eight blames the employee for oversights. Remedy: Seven methodical about documentation; Eight learns good listening. Partly from feeling bullied, a Seven improvises small changes "when it feels right"; Eights go ballistic ("This is only the tip of the iceberg. What else have they been up to?"). Most often reported dispute: an Eight "just trying to assess the damage" versus a Seven "just trying to help"; Sevens stand and fight when persuasion fails or integrity is publicly questioned; Eight's fear of losing control is matched by Seven's fear of domination; a third party can discriminate between the actual breach of trust and exaggerated ideas. "The Seven-Eight combination will stand or fall on the question of trust." Constant updates help; Sevens should report in and account in detail: "Eights can deal with bad news, but no news makes them very angry." [p.400-401]

## Seven with Nine: Epicure-Mediator Couple

- **Overview:** Epicures are highly experimental (classes, events, cutting-edge ideas); infuse vitality into family life; "the wick that draws a predictable Nine out of familiar habits." Panel Mediators say they fell into interests from competition Ping-Pong to mysticism after being dragged to a first experience by insatiable Seven curiosity. Shared gigantic worldview: Sevens organize lives around multiple options (optimism from looking forward to tomorrow's plans); Nines are big-picture thinkers whose worldview incorporates most points of view and their opposites; breadth gives appreciation of diversity (wide interests, different friend groups, letting children be unique). Down side of "anything is possible": inability to choose. [p.401]
- **Decision paralysis:** Too many options and no fixed plan; Sevens spread thin, neglect priorities; Nines overwhelmed by choice; they collude in choosing not to choose ("if everything's possible, why bother?"); Nines avoid anger and Sevens avoid pain, so both ignore hard questions. [p.401]
- **Styles of avoiding choice:** Epicures move very fast; in Buddhist maps of consciousness they are called **monkey mind** (attention shifting from thing to thing to satisfy gluttony for experience); Sevens need to limit themselves. Nines get obsessively attached to a single course, circulating around all possibilities of the same question; Nine mind likened to the elephant "that never forgets and stays in the same place until it gets tired"; Nines need to work toward what is essential. [p.402]
- **Self-referencing vs merging:** Seven is among the most self-referencing types; Nines typically merge, losing their own agenda; "Mediators focus on Epicures, and Epicures focus on themselves." The Seven typically introduces new perspectives, giving a Nine choice; works if Nines try something new; or they form separate orbits. Nines are creatures of habit and can develop sustaining activities or fall into an "armchair depression"; periods in which Nine is depressed while Seven stays busy and neither notices; drift until an outside event pulls them together. [p.402]
- **What helps:** Alerted, Sevens pull a Mediator out of the armchair; once in motion Nines follow an energetic routine; formulate a guiding plan ("What are we aiming for as a family? What's essential between us? What's productive, and what can be left behind?"). The couple may fight more once on track because they're more invested: a sign of renewed commitment when Sevens deal with pain and Nines find a position worth defending. [p.402-403]

### Seven-Nine Partnership at Work
- Most Nines are energetic workers but produce under deadline pressure or a structured schedule minimizing decisions; rapid changes disorient; Mediators are secure when they know exactly what to do that day; Sevens continuously revamp the schedule; Sevens are flexible, Nines run on habit; styles complement but they may communicate only when deadlines pull them together. [p.403]
- **Illusion of limitless time:** Work has unending sameness for Nines laboring for someone else's agenda; when they are not identified with the work, there is always time for something extra; "You can tell when Nines are vitally interested because they manage their time by refusing additional commitments." Mediators take on more than is humanly possible; Epicures do several projects at once and stay up late so as not to miss out; Sevens explore the whole net of associations while Nines meander along "tributaries and backwaters"; a little done on many projects until a deadline creates focus. [p.403]
- **Entitlement upstaging a low-profile Mediator; story "So who did the work?":** Steve the Nine bought Pete a lathe for his sixtieth birthday, set it up secretly with Pete's wife's help; a Seven arrived at the party, realized he had no gift, asked to chip in ten dollars, and when Pete arrived stepped forward, put a hand on the lathe and gave a presentation speech saying it was from both of them. Steve: "you can't call the guy on this stuff. It wasn't premeditated; he isn't really mean, but he walks in and takes the credit." [p.403-404]
- **Seven manager, Nine employee:** An unappreciated Nine goes mute, gets stubborn ("I'm not being paid to do someone else's job. This isn't my problem"); time accounted but the work doesn't progress. Sevens rally disaffected employees with half promises and charm, the worst tactic for Nines who want something concrete; they need to listen to grievances. Epicures are impatient with lengthy "sagalike" presentations; Nines hold the floor long when they have something to say; a Seven manager should tap "the gold mine of information" Nines have about their environment (they've noticed bottlenecks and procedural glitches), and Nines are excellent candidates for the organizational detail Sevens dislike and would welcome a reliable system. Seven managers are famous for "bare presence," breeze through the office at odd times to boost excitement; Mediators like predictable assignments and slow down when uncertain or "used." [p.404-405]
- **Nine manager, Seven employee:** Nines value workplace harmony and routine; decision making and departures are hard, countering the Epicurean belief that routine stifles creativity; Seven employees keep inventing a job to their own advantage and navigate around authority assuming management will agree. Mediators must supervise clearly even if it means confrontation (sticking point: both avoid anger; they collude to let things slide, re-engaging the cycle of Seven evasion and Nine's entrenched passive aggression). Scheduled check-ins are a must or an Epicure carries on without noticing and a Mediator begins to keep score: "Sevens don't notice and Nines don't speak." [p.405]

## Double Eight: Double Boss Couple

- **Dead-ahead focus:** Eights are literal about going for it; a dead-ahead orientation to simple chores makes them seem oblivious to others (may pick you up and move you aside from the sink; reach around you at the cutting board to open the cutlery drawer against your stomach: "Where's my paring knife? Where did you hide the spoons?"); other people exist as movable objects in the line of sight; double Eight is especially prone. [p.406]
- **Reception and appearance:** Double Eight draws an "Oh, wow!" response in classes and "Did they both survive?" Outsiders see combat: planted body language, assertiveness, directive tone; in actuality Eights may be assertive just to get each other's attention. Panel stories are usually history: "When I dated this Eight" is a common opening line. They enjoy high-decibel contact: deep belly laughs, pushing the energy throttle up; relief at letting it all hang out after being told to hold their presence back. [p.406]
- **Panel anecdote:** A couples panel pair sounded like "two Titans about to clash," making the audience squirm; the couple enjoyed the attention, neither felt out of control; what stirred the audience was that two people could have a big fight, go to bed, make love, and wake up still not speaking; no abuse; attraction enacted through sexuality and fighting. [p.406-407]
- **Argument as contact:** For most of us argument arises from anger or passion; for double Eight "they like to argue because it's high-intensity contact, and discharging energy feels physically pleasurable"; not invested in the outcome; argument is a way of trying others out; stirring enough energy "to feel vibrant and alive." Fights can be a way to get closer; anger doesn't mean hurt; not breaking up just because not speaking. [p.407]
- **Configurations:** Stereotype: volatile mix of anger and sexuality; but each couple is unique: some Eights lean to a wing (Nine-like or Seven-like). Most common: one partner with all the extroverted flamboyant attributes, the other withdrawn and Five-ish: "There can be only one real Boss in any household, or the action would periodically escalate beyond the point of return." Long-term couples have an obvious Eight (male or female) coupled with an Eight strongly positioned in Five or the Two-like pleasing stance; "Eights 'at home' are often mistaken for Twos": they go toward people, comfort, draw people out; Eights in love look very different from the aggressive "masculine" style (text continues). [p.407-408]

### Double Eight (continued)
- Affection elicits the receptive, surrendered aspects of any type ("Eights in love look very different from the aggressive 'masculine' style we are used to seeing in the workplace"). What helps: either partner begins to focus inward; Eights lose track of how they come across and look outward to challenge what angers them; if either becomes self-observing the other can be protector; "Eights rarely go first when they are unsure of the outcome" (easier to be a strong supporter than to expose themselves in unexplored emotional territory). [p.408]

### Double Eight Partnership at Work
- Eights focus on winning rather than on looking good as a winner: respected and feared; gravitate to positions of power because they dislike being controlled; often successful founders; valued during growth periods when people fall in behind a figure who handles confrontation and keeps moving under fire. [p.408]
- Double Eight "almost guarantee[s] a power struggle": each a strong territorial force unless on the same side and needing each other. Exchange of favors is the usual way to ensure cooperation ("Eights like to respond in kind"): a fair offer gets an honest response, unfairness stimulates open resistance. [p.408]
- **Vengeance anecdote:** a young Eight entrepreneur, betrayed, had a large marble tombstone engraved with his ex-partner's name, delivered, noisily uncrated and left standing in the front office while dissolution meetings went on. "The whole point of revenge is to even the score so that you don't feel disadvantaged." At the least Eights write an "informative" letter or withhold useful information. Trick: make boundaries so clear and advantages of cooperation so obvious that the two end up supporting each other. [p.408-409]
- **Eight managers:** Respect ambitions that build a power base; expecting challenge they use benefits to align employees with the company or against a rival faction (control the action rather than leave a power vacuum; fits protective-or-adversarial tendency). With exaggerated lean to stress point Five, "stingy controlled generosity": "Giving with one hand (Eight) and taking back with the other (Five)." [p.409]
- **Eight employees:** Less comfortable as employees, especially under an unfair boss ("Fair means room for advancement, and unfair is being kept in your place"). Managers should heed early warning signs: an Eight often becomes spokesperson for disaffected factions and a natural rallying point for dissension (will handle confrontation, extend a protective mantle). Mistrustful Eights want to punish; can't distinguish the issue from the person holding the view; may lose track of the issue and focus on taking out an opponent; if they feel betrayed anything goes as long as defensible in court. Most know all-out fighting equals being fired, but escalating power struggles can be conducted within the letter of the law and polarize the workplace; personnel pressured to take sides. Acknowledge difficulty immediately, talk face to face; Eights have little awareness of impact and "turn up the volume until they feel heard"; third-party referees keep discussion on track; do not assume problems vanish; "withholding information is a red flag for Eights." [p.409-410]

## Eight with Nine: Boss-Mediator Couple

- **Stereotype vs reality:** Stereotype is the dynamic Boss with docile mate; actual story seldom fits. Both acknowledge Eight's dominance and that Nines seldom say no, but it is not a dominant-compliant partnership; "Who initiates?" "Who's the follower?" draw baffled smiles. "What does happen when an irresistible force meets an immovable object? In a meeting of colossal powers, both sides have to give." [p.410]
- **Wonder:** A radically different style of relating works: Eights chronically overpower their environment, Nines contain energy; Eights push through obstacles, Nines try to forget. Bosses learn to defer; Mediators witness the positive effects of rage. Impulse and inertia either cancel out or produce a distinctive blend. Image: the irresistible storm barely affects the immovable mountain; mountains don't initiate, only react; a relationship needs both excitement and acceptance; Eights spark a partner's energies; "like mountains standing fast during emotional storms, Nines are suited to weather periodic rage." [p.410-411]
- **Wing points:** They are wing points of each other; the juncture of lust with self-forgetting (sloth) commonly produces an attraction to creature comforts; steady domestic routine; each matches the other's energy level. High: a good home, generous to each other and friends. Low: "comfort passions" drain time and energy; inertia sets in; Eights like to control resources, Nines have an appetite for ease; "not-giving and not-doing"; decisions by default; a holding pattern. [p.411]
- **Break-out:** The Eight usually acts, constructively or by stirring trouble; trying to escape boredom Eights can disrupt relationships beyond repair. Helps when either moves ahead in his or her own sphere without pushing the other to go first; when either falls into self-forgetting, attention wanders from real needs to lesser agendas and both think "Why should I change when you don't make the effort?"; find a personal priority by turning attention inward rather than a cycle where Eight controls and Nine resists. [p.411]

### Eight-Nine Partnership at Work
- Can move rapidly forward or become a test of wills. Eights anger easily and assert control directly; Nines express anger indirectly through passive aggressive resistance. Both are in the **anger triad** (Eight, Nine, One): Eight's lust is a passionate demand for satisfaction from angry frustration; anger is also at the root of sloth/Nine's impulse to forget. Combined energies are highly constructive: Eight initiates and handles conflict; Nine mediates and supports. [p.411-412]
- **Eight manager, Nine employee:** Eights set comprehensive, complicated guidelines ensuring top-down control; rules erratically enforced according to the Boss's interest ("All rules are off when the manager feels expansive"); make rules then break them; delegate power then change their mind; unannounced site visits, ominous "beetle-browed inquiries" then sudden permissiveness; control lies in ignoring regulations others must obey and personally administering rewards and punishment. Nine employees appreciate firm leadership but can't endure personal confrontation. Best case: decisive Eight rallies employees for a massive push; Nines swept up in momentum line up behind aggressive leadership; once the goal is reached Nines turn to consolidating gains; a wise manager lets Mediators set a reasonable lasting pace. Worst: manager can't shift from combat mode and pressures employees to take on more responsibility than they want; Nines entrench for a war of wills (the manager enforces and the employee shifts blame; the manager argues and the Nine goes mute); "Stubborn Nines are a match for assertive managers": shield from confrontation and wait it out for years; amiable, no open opposition, impossible to supervise. [p.412]
- It's difficult to control passive aggression with rewards and punishments (rules work only if people obey or disobey). Eight tightens control to forestall feeling helpless; Nine resists and ducks; eventually the employee's digressions trigger an explosion; open anger is more comfortable to Eights than covert passivity and may bring Nine's grievances to the surface. "Eights want to know the truth, and Nines are relieved when they can tell it." Both blame (Eights before questioning themselves; Nines because dragged along by others' ideas). Eight managers should periodically reaffirm high points; Mediators avoid conflict, won't take center stage but appreciate unsolicited recognition. Eights can be oblivious to relationship dynamics (act without consultation, don't offer compliments, rarely draw people out). If the Eight affirms actual contributions the Nine sees good intentions behind the intimidating persona and learns to hold ground; danger of Nine being driven out after "the last straw"; "if Nine holds ground then Eight can listen, and when Eight can listen Nine will stay." [p.412-413]
- **Nine manager, Eight employee:** Nine managers who digress or get distracted frighten Eight employees (looks incompetent), inviting a full-out challenge to test capability; staff pressured to take sides as an uneasy Eight sorts enemies from friends. Eights want clear communication with objective rewards and penalties; Nines favor consensus management ("everybody gets a little bit of the action, often just enough to maintain the status quo"); the little-pieces approach makes Eights lobby for attention and indecisiveness leads to open revolt; "Eights want certainty, and if they can't get it, they exert influence by making enough noise to get management's ear." [p.413-414]

## Double Nine: Double Mediator Couple

- **Outer picture:** Appearance of being merged (similarity of resonance and a way of speaking that reaffirms an ongoing agreement); seemingly settled, few divisive questions or jagged emotions; live-and-let-live; unconditional acceptance without forcing change; mutual desire to stay comfortable. [p.414]
- **Inside story:** Roiled by conflicting inner forces, each looks to the other to stimulate personal identity, standing for or against a partner's priority rather than for self; symbiotic merging as long as no conflict; with full commitment pressure mounts: "Am I doing this for myself or for others? Did I choose to be here, or is this someone else's idea?"; "Who's choosing here?"; no fixed reference point to agree with or oppose; options seem similar; "Should we or shouldn't we? Is it worth fighting about? Probably not." They collude in not-doing to build a harmonious lifestyle; the **catch-22:** "keeping discomfort away requires a lot of energy." [p.414-415]
- **Low:** each enmeshed in routine interests, unessential undertakings keeping the status quo afloat; "a little bit of everything gets done, but nothing seems finalized," since the energy that could force a wake-up call might create conflict. **High:** nonverbal, unified accord; if either engages a personal reason for being, the other merges and supports; each should separate from the other's interests, go deeply within, find a personal reason for being, and stand up rather than be for or against the other's priority. [p.415]

### Double Nine Partnership at Work
- Of about twenty double-Nine couples interviewed, sixteen also work together; the business gives an external focus that animates them and a familiar daily arena; mutual reliance on schedule; avoidance of complicated decisions; each carries out duties, refining the system little by little until "the business seems to run on its own." [p.415]
- **Decisions:** Both must be drawn out to take a position; Nines see decisions as arbitrary ("Don't all roads eventually lead to the same end?" "Why take this route rather than that one?"); conflicts appear resolved then erupt later if no real position was uncovered; settlement delayed until the full story and resentments emerge from both sides. Nines who feel compromised may "wake up" later feeling sold out; third parties ask Nines to voice all opinions involved and find their own voice by elimination: "Not that, not that" until a position emerges. [p.415-416]
- **Nine managers:** Must learn clear directives; not wanting to influence others, say no or stir controversy produces lethargic leadership; identify with all sides, overburdened by input rather than the business's best interests; would rather mediate for others than defend a position; "Of all the types, they can most easily slip into temporary agreement with an adversary," placing them at a disadvantage in negotiating for themselves; easier to disclose thinking to neutral parties than go public, making them good candidates for arbitration. [p.416]
- **Nine employees:** Vulnerable to merging with the workplace atmosphere; with other Nines it feels unsettling to push the energy throttle above the next job's requirements: "don't rock the boat"; path of least resistance is repeating a routine, which can be physically exhausting but feels secure; they prefer improving a known system with expected outcome and preset quality standards to initiating an experiment; known procedures give a feeling of flow; "the exhaustion is familiar, protective, and attractive." [p.416]
- **Helps:** Periodically review grievances and resolve in stages; Nines must feel they make the decision on their own without pressure into compromise; set a schedule of dates for stages ("Can you hand in documents by the end of next month?") rather than pushing for immediate closure; each piece discussed and given time to settle; if the Nine has anything to lose, conflict resolution must be assimilated piece by piece. [p.416-417]

# PART B. EPILOGUE (PDF pp. 421-423; printed 405-407)

- **Palmer sees something of herself in all nine types:** each is grounded in an appropriate emotional response; one need not be a Nine to merge with a loved one's agenda or a Four to share another's pain; "How natural to feel afraid when we are threatened. How human to be angry if we feel misused." [p.421]
- **The model:** "a way of organizing the human condition around nine emotional passions, each of which is based in a common emotional concern." The characteristics that form around the nucleus of the passion correlate well with contemporary personality theory, but "the Enneagram's power lies in the view that a passion can act as a transformer from ordinary to higher consciousness." [p.421]
- **Lineage:** The passions have a long history in sacred tradition; best known as Christianity's seven capital sins plus two generic tendencies that all types hold in common (as pointed out in Part I). The Enneagram of personality types is "a modern rediscovery of a very old spiritual tradition"; a "jewel of sacred tradition" being reset in a framework of contemporary psychological ideas. [p.421]
- **Living teaching and oral tradition:** The Enneagram lives in the self-observations of people who inhabit the types; those who embody the passions "can speak as living authorities." The Directory of Relationships is based on information from participants in "oral tradition classes," where representatives of the types speak with each other and significant others on stage while the audience witnesses and questions couples directly. The couples are normal, high-functioning people attending to improve relationships, see themselves through loved ones' eyes, and examine inner biases; if these biases are distorted aspects of human essence, the couples "win twice": insight into relationships and a key to spiritual nature. [p.421-422]
- **Future Directions:** Over the last ten years the Enneagram moved from small private classes to a broad public arena; many authors offer personal views; Palmer sees differences of opinion as healthy and expects the views to coalesce into a unified teaching "in the next few years." She considers the whole model, not a single point, worthy of attention. [p.422]
- **Type as tripartite:** Each personality is a dynamic relationship between the concerns of the type proper and the ways the type alters in security or under stress and risk. The model is a **flow pattern**: energies descend from essence ("the realm of pure being") into ordinary events; the arrows chart energy movement between types, normal flow at the security point, "bottlenecked at the type point when childhood defenses form"; flow is weakest after the bottleneck, in the behaviors attributed to the point of risk; this does not mean stress-point characteristics are more compulsive: they are coping strategies appearing when the type proper's defenses are inadequate. She also observes a dynamic relationship between the two wing points adjacent to each type point, which she believes contributes to the formation of type. [p.422-423]
- She and co-teacher David Daniels, M.D., are preparing a book about Enneagram theory and the psychological and spiritual strategies for healing it suggests. [p.423]
