**Reading key.** Throughout, material is labeled **[SYSTEM]** (what the book teaches about the Enneagram itself: types, passions, gifts, defenses, subtypes, growth) or **[CHRISTIAN]** (the authors' Christian-theological framing: Bible figures, saints, scripture, "redemption," the cross, God's will, prayer). ## Overview of this range

This part opens at the tail of the TYPE THREE chapter (the sin of deceit, the "gift" of truthfulness, symbols, biblical and saintly examples, the call to hope), then runs through the full chapters on FOUR (the remaining heart type in this range), the three head types (FIVE, SIX, SEVEN) and two of the three gut types (EIGHT and NINE; the NINE chapter ends in this range with Pope John XXIII and Teresa of Avila). For each type the authors give: a descriptive overview of motivation, childhood root and everyday behavior; a "Dilemma" section naming the defense mechanism, the root sin ("passion"), the pitfall and the shadow behavior; "Symbols and Examples" (animals, countries, colors, biblical ancestors, famous people); and "Conversion and Maturation" (the invitation, the gift or "fruit of the spirit," life tasks, a model saint). The tone is anecdotal and illustrative, mixing observation of typical behavior with Christian spiritual direction. Terms to note: "root sin" (the passion), "gift, or fruit of the spirit" (the virtue, defined as the reverse of the sin), "pitfall" (the typical trap of the immature type), "defense mechanism," "invitation" (the call to redemption), "life tasks," "wing" (the neighboring type), and "heart / head / body types" (the three centers).

Chapter subtitles (as the authors give them): FOUR "The Need to Be Special"; FIVE "The Need to Perceive"; SIX "The Need for Security"; SEVEN "The Need to Avoid Pain"; EIGHT "The Need to Be Against"; NINE "The Need to Avoid."

## TYPE THREE (end of chapter; continues from the previous part)

*The chapter began in the previous part. This range picks up mid-discussion of the THREE's deceit. Overview-level traits of THREE (needs for achievement, image, the heart-type group) are in Part 1.*

### The sin of deceit and the used-car-salesman THREE [p.67]
- **[SYSTEM]** The THREE's root sin (named earlier in the chapter, deceit/lying) is here developed through M. Scott Peck's "people of the lie": evil people attack others instead of looking at their own failure, repressing guilt and projecting it, which ends by destroying the culprits themselves. Authors: THREEs who believe their own lies are "among the most deformed personalities in existence." [p.67]
- **[SYSTEM]** Danger of misplaced trust: a dishonest THREE looks self-confident and seems to know what he is doing, so people trust him blindly. THREEs are "the proverbial used car salesmen": everything polished, speech so fast and impressive you can hardly follow. They can sell anything because they are primarily selling themselves (how cool and competent they are), and the scene is perfectly staged. [p.67]
- **[SYSTEM] Pitfall: vanity.** Secondary, external things (packaging, clothing, outside impact) matter more than essentials (substance, person, content). Trapped THREEs live as if standing alongside their own body and soul watching themselves perform. "Born actors." Authors' examples: Ronald Reagan; perhaps Pope John Paul II. THREEs know how to use the masses and like to stand before large crowds and "press the flesh," but in one-on-one conversation often feel unsure, because there people demand genuineness, vulnerability and profundity. [p.67]
- **[SYSTEM] Gift / fruit of the spirit: truthfulness or honesty** (the reverse of the sin). A redeemed THREE has found the way to truth. [p.67]

### Case: the older woman who "outed herself" as a THREE [p.67-68]
- A woman at Ebert's seminar: wanted to be an actress as a child, became "a grand role-player on the stage of life"; everything worked out (husband, comfort, health) until she fell seriously ill and within a year her parents and husband died. Her tricks and rehearsed patterns failed; she contemplated suicide; rage, fear, hostility. She heard an inner voice ("Let it be. Just let yourself fall."), lived through "a great emptying," and then discovered genuine feelings for the first time: "Before that I had always produced the feelings myself." She became a caregiver for seriously ill and dying patients and gained "an infallible sense" of whether a person is telling the truth or pretending, without condemning it, because she knows self-deception from inside. [p.67]
- **[SYSTEM]** The path: THREEs reach their gift only through painful self-knowledge, looking their life-lies (big and little) in the face. Because this means insight into one's own failure, it is very hard for them. Redeemed THREEs put huge gifts to work helping people competently and effectively, motivating others to discover their own potential ("helping them to help themselves"), organizing groups and communities sensibly, exposing society's lies and spreading truth "professionally, efficiently, up-to-date." [p.67-68]

### Symbols and Examples (THREE) [p.68-69]
- **Animals.** Chameleon (adapting to the expectations of the environment; danger of exchanging a variety of roles and masks for the real self, to which they have no access; an unredeemed THREE whose roles are removed can panic or "literally dissolve into nothing"; a THREE woman in love would think at length which female type to embody at the next rendezvous in order to "go over well"). Peacock (also applied by some to TWO or FOUR; all heart types are "peacock-like" because their behavior aims at a reaction: the TWO poses as lovable and helpful, the THREE plays the role that goes over best, the FOUR appears as special; the long-term goal of counseling heart types is to "dock the tail" so it is clear that without finery he is an ordinary chicken). Eagle for the redeemed THREE (looks into the sun; swiftness, power, endurance, renewal). [p.68]
- **[CHRISTIAN]** Eagle tied to Isa. 40:31. [p.68]
- **Country: the United States.** Rohr's argument: in the U.S. "there is no motivation not to be a THREE"; whoever masters the THREE game reaches the top (CEOs, bishops, presidents); leaders are disappointing because they have been busy climbing the ladder; THREE is the prototype of the white male American, the senior class president; those who do not match the THREE ideal feel inferior. Americans find it hard to see through their system's lies; "kingdom of the good" is exalted above impure motives; world over-coated with THREE ideals (achievement, efficiency, success; "Anyone who can't keep up gets booted out"). "The THREE is a type that grows out of affluence": the poor learn early that you seldom get what you want. [p.68]
- **Color: traffic-light yellow.** Catches the eye; urgent, dynamic, radiant; the redeemed THREE: yellow as the brightest color that makes meaning transparent, a "directional element" that asks pressing questions and guides. But yellow is also the most vulnerable color: the slightest dirtying makes it ugly or poisonous. "Muddied yellow" = envy, treachery, falseness, doubt, mistrust, insanity (Judas painted in muddied yellow in Giotto and Holbein). A color-symbolism source is quoted (footnotes 5-6). [p.68-69]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Biblical ancestor: Jacob the deceiver** (Gen. 25-33). The authors tell the whole story: fought Esau in the womb; buys birthright for lentils; cheats Isaac's blessing by impersonating Esau; flees to Laban; dream of the ladder (ladder as symbol of ascent and descent "a message every THREE can understand"); deceived himself by Laban (Leah for Rachel); serves fourteen years; grows rich by a sophisticated trick with the herds; returns, despite fears, to reconcile with Esau (a positive sign: "when THREEs confront their past and are ready to take the consequences of their mistakes"); sends lavish gifts ahead; wrestles with the unknown man at the Jabbok, will not let go without a blessing, is renamed Israel. "Scarcely any biblical figure can be so unequivocally assigned to an Enneagram type." [p.69]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Judas and Pilate as unredeemed THREEs.** Judas (theory: betrayed Jesus to force him to seize power as Messiah; miscalculated; suicide; greed for money as a symbol of success). Pilate: career politician convinced of Jesus' innocence but fearing for his career; asks "What is truth?" (John 18:38); sees through the game but plays along, never making the breakthrough from lie to truth. [p.69]

### Conversion and Maturation (THREE) [p.69-72]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Invitation: the call to hope.** Hope beyond ostensible successes lets a THREE acquire depth and endure failure (2 Cor. 4:17-18; Matt. 6:33): anchoring life in God's will and God's reign rather than one's own goals. [p.69-70]
- **[SYSTEM] Feelings atrophy.** THREEs "really have to work to gain depth." Contrast: TWOs may wallow in emotion; THREEs often cannot say how they feel. All heart types, especially THREEs, struggle to perceive authentic feelings; feelings interfere with efficiency, so THREEs suspend emotions during a task, and as they are always pursuing some project (sometimes three or four at once), the inner world falls by the wayside. [p.70]
- **[SYSTEM] Learn to be alone.** Like TWOs, THREEs must learn solitude with no public feedback, applause or admiration. Contemplative prayer and silent meditation are the "prescriptions." But they first make this a project too ("meditate successfully"), then try to "learn nothing" with as much success as possible. Needs patience; readiness to experience nothing spectacular and be confronted with hostility, boredom and superficiality. They must chew and digest shadow sides, failure and defeats; the confession "I've failed; I was wrong; I lied" costs enormous effort. [p.70]
- **[SYSTEM] Culture reinforces the disease.** In Western civilization THREEs are "practically never challenged" because cultural criteria for health (capacity to work, love, enjoy) match; redemption can mean "taking leave from the understanding and applause of the world around us." [p.70]
- **Literary illustration:** Isaac B. Singer, *The Penitent*: a successful Polish-born Jewish businessman in America, betrayed by lover and wife, chooses between suicide and a radical new start, becomes strictly Orthodox in Mea Shearim; peace through fidelity to the Law. Singer's own gloss: the remedies will not heal everyone but the nature of the disease will be recognized. [p.70]
- **[SYSTEM] What THREEs really long for.** Not just praise but real love: unmerited, unconditional love; they get so much applause for success that they think that is all they want. Anecdote: a nun, high school principal, "wonderful and highly competent THREE": "Everybody elects me for all the jobs... I'd like just once to feel that everybody loved me for what I am. But I know that I myself contribute to making things the way they are." THREEs seldom cry but can break out in violent tears that surprise others; one reports feeling comfort after crying. [p.70-71]
- **[SYSTEM] Motto "I produce, therefore I am."** Sickness and old age feel threatening because THREEs can no longer offer anything. Quote: a THREE whose father worked on papers in bed after a heart attack realized at fifteen it was a false compulsion but reacts the same way when sick. Life task: grasp sickness as a signal and chance for transformation. [p.71]
- **[SYSTEM] Life tasks listed [p.71]:** stand still and stop the "eternal hunt for new successes and projects"; answer "How do I really feel?"; purposeful care of the body and attention to dream pictures as "a bridge to the soul"; listen to the voice of their own feelings instead of what promises outside recognition; sharpen conscience and refuse "insignificant" deviations from the truth; overcome the ungrounded fear that behind roles and masks there may be no true self; beware a hyperactive imagination busy with new projects; take on projects needing patient detail work and no quick results; do not immunize themselves against criticism (like TWOs and FOURs) but search for the grain of truth in all criticism.
- **[CHRISTIAN]** "The secret of the cross, which is the secret of failure: out of our defeats God makes his victories - not ours!" (cannot be integrated into any system of promotion). Redeemed THREEs free themselves from vanity, hope in God's sovereign activity (which cannot be manipulated), confront inner emptiness and longing for love, renounce the security won by status, money and power, and renounce building their own kingdom for the coming of God's. [p.71]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Saintly model: Dorothy Day (1897-1980).** Brooklyn-born; nonreligious upbringing; socialist at sixteen; journalist at *The Call*; interviewed Trotsky; anarchist; arrested six times; prison cell awakened interest in religion and self-criticism ("discovered how much egotism lay behind her commitment to the oppressed"); baptism; marriage failed; a partner "jealous of Christ"; 1933 founded *The Catholic Worker* with Peter Maurin (sold for one cent; circulation 100,000 in the first year); soup kitchens, houses for the homeless, strikes; fought for effective social change (unlike alms-giving, "unlike Mother Teresa"); pacifist in WWII; accused of communism by Cardinal Spellman; for a long time the struggle for the poor was her kind of prayer; found rest only in nature (nature "doesn't demand and doesn't judge"); in her last years grew into silent prayer. Funeral: masses came; "no tears, only hallelujahs." [p.71-72]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Saintly model: Thomas More** (1478-1535), "image of a purified THREE": brilliant career, Lord Chancellor 1529, resigned 1532 rather than go along with Henry VIII's break with Rome, imprisoned and beheaded 1535; author of *Utopia* (1516), argued for abolition of private property and a primitive Christian community of love. [p.72]

## TYPE FOUR: The Need to Be Special [p.73-82]

### Overview (FOUR) [p.73-78]
- **[SYSTEM] Gifts and talents.** FOURs awaken a sense of beauty and harmony around them; highly sensitive, almost always artistically gifted (dance, music, painting, theater, literature); everything with vital energy attracts them; they read moods and feelings of people and the atmosphere of places and events "with seismographic precision." By nature "ecumenically oriented"; reject the division of the world into "sacred" and "profane"; more at home in the unconscious, symbols and dreams than in the real world; they help others develop an eye for beauty, dreams and symbols. [p.73]
- **[SYSTEM] Life question:** "What do you think of me? Do you notice me? Do I catch your eye?" They strive to be aesthetically attractive, exceptional, creative, or esoteric, eccentric, extravagant, exotic. The style and "spontaneity" of an immature FOUR is artificial (they emerge saying "I just threw a few things on in a hurry," but the effect is carefully chosen). [p.73]
- **[SYSTEM] Core: longing.** The life of FOURs is shaped by longing for beauty and for the world to fit into a harmonic whole. Dostoyevsky: "The world will be saved by beauty" (FOURs believe this). [p.73]
- **[SYSTEM] Childhood root: loss.** The present felt unbearable and meaningless; often a painful loss (real: death of a parent, illegitimate birth, divorce, uprooting, an undependable parent, another child preferred or born; or felt "only" emotionally). Positive role models missing; child turns inward; because the original source of love was missing or weak, new sources of love had to be created in the imagination. Longing is at once "a yearning to go home and to go far away"; they await the great love that will redeem them. [p.73]
- **[SYSTEM] Anger turned inward; hidden shame.** The anger over loss is so deep it cannot be tolerated and is directed against themselves; they conclude they are guilty and "bad"; many are ruled by a hidden shame; they keep cultivating their "badness" and producing situations of rejection or abandonment; the dark and forbidden have a peculiar attraction. [p.73]
- **[SYSTEM] Outsider/elitist stance.** Most feel society's norms do not hold for them; on the strength of extraordinary suffering feel like strangers and outsiders; they lay down their own norms; many have elitist consciousness; they try to meet special standards and feel deficient when this fails. [p.73]
- **[SYSTEM] Recognizable by:** odd clothes; melancholy side shown by preference for black and violet, or as motley and crazy as possible; many are vegetarians, animal rights activists, feminists, adherents of eccentric health ideas. [p.73-74]
- **[SYSTEM] Longing outweighs possession.** Possession brings little joy; disappointment once the object is possessed; complicated love partners. Case told in first person: a woman who longed for her future husband; at the wedding her romantic feelings "melt into thin air"; when he left she fell in love again; when he returned her love died, and she reproached him; as he turned away her love awoke again. Authors: part of the terrible dilemma: they cannot live in the present (always full of defects); if the longing is realized there is always something to find fault with. [p.74]
- **[SYSTEM] Authority.** FOURs revere great authorities with something "deep" or "special"; formal authority without personality makes no impression; "nose for the 'authentic' is infallible." [p.74]
- **[SYSTEM] Taste and snobbery.** Natural eye for beauty; artists, musicians, poets, playwrights; in the church, designers of creative services with a sense of liturgy, ritual and shaping space; exquisite taste; buy in secondhand shops or boutiques; "would rather die" than settle for mass-produced items; arrogance that makes others feel their "aesthetic superiority"; hate everything stale, old-fashioned, plain, average, styleless, "normal." Secretly envy normal consumers; idealize the "unwashed masses" and write romantic novels of the noble poor (Victor Hugo) from an ivory tower, while unable to endure real dirt and poverty. [p.74]
- **[SYSTEM] Life program: the eternal quest for the Holy Grail.** [p.74] The authors then give the Grail legend (late twelfth century, Old French and Provencal literature; vessel of the Last Supper; confers happiness on the pure knight; Wolfram von Eschenbach's *Parzifal* ca. 1200, a stone guarded by angels; Wagner used it), the search for a flower in the *Roman de la Rose* (Guillaume de Lorris, early 13th c.; garden of love; allegories of hatred, betrayal, greed, envy, melancholy on the walls; Danger, Slander, Shame, Fear hold the hand back even when the kiss is obtained), and Novalis's Blue Flower (symbol of the soul's striving for fulfillment and wholeness), with a short quoted passage. [p.74-75] **[note: the authors claim the Roman de la Rose and similar works may have been influenced by the Sufis; a claim of the authors, [p.74].]**

### Dilemma (FOUR) [p.75-78]
- **[SYSTEM] Temptation: to strive frantically for authenticity.** Children and nature awaken longing for lost simplicity; the harder unredeemed FOURs strive for authenticity, the more "mannered" they seem. [p.75]
- **[SYSTEM] Defense mechanism: artistic sublimation.** Feelings are expressed indirectly via symbols, rituals and dramatic styling, to alleviate real grief and fear of rejection. Belief: "anyone who would see me directly the way I am couldn't bear the sight." More at home in their art than with people; others can be used merely as "emotional trigger mechanisms" for longings, memories, dreams; they must learn "really learn" the authentic capacity to love. [p.75]
- **[SYSTEM] Life as Gesamtkunstwerk.** Clothing, decor, hobbies, circle of friends, habits aligned and carefully staged. "Bohemia": melancholy music, half-wilted roses or lilacs, incense, dripping candles, diary by the bed; long nights over tea or red wine. [p.75]
- **[SYSTEM] Root sin: envy.** They instantly see who has more style, class, taste, talent, genius; who is simpler, more natural, more normal, "healthier." Quoted FOUR (Helen Palmer): "How is it that other people seem to hold hands and smile a lot?... You get on a Holy Grail search to find the something more." Envy appears as jealousy in relationships: fear that someone else is more attractive or interesting as a partner; inner child feeling "I don't deserve to be loved. I have to make an impression so that I'm not overlooked and abandoned again"; close relationships become an "arena for combat and competition." [p.75]
- **[SYSTEM] Avoidance of ordinariness.** Everything current, conventional, normal; the demand to be like everyone else can cause panic; they resist change even more stubbornly than other types ("I like to be different"). Their status, circle of friends, flair and admiration were acquired through striking behavior; unredeemed FOURs do not want the game spoiled until they taste its dark side and notice it prevents them from loving (egocentricity); it takes a long time to give up the self-image; they can be "pigheaded." They joke about moodiness and elitism but real self-criticism is much harder. [p.75]
- **[SYSTEM] Example:** in monasteries FOURs were thrown out for not conforming; Franciscan retreat anecdote: all in brown habit, one pinned a big red rose to his chest. Inner logic: "I don't know who I am if I'm like all the others." [p.75-76]
- **[SYSTEM] Pitfall: melancholy**, a "sweet sadness" like fog over their whole life; FOURs "have to be depressed and suffer from time to time in order to be happy"; Helen Palmer: "tragic romantics." Quotations from Bettina von Arnim, Victor Hugo ("Melancholy is the happiness of being sad"), Petrarch (feeding with silent lustfulness on tears and pains); Goethe's *Werther* (wave of suicides). The greater the pain and depression, the more creative they can be. [p.76]
- **[SYSTEM] Affinity with death.** Perhaps because death is the ultimate lament, the definitive longing, or because only death makes beauty eternal; great love stories must end in death for dramaturgical reasons (Romeo and Juliet marrying and having children would be "too banal"). Anecdote: Rohr's FOUR Franciscan friend as a young man planned in detail an aesthetically perfect death in springtime under a cherry tree, after loved ones had hurt him ("the definitive punishment"); "such morbid reveries are not unusual among FOURs." Romantic poems revolve around love, beauty and death (Platen's "Tristan" quoted). [p.76-77]
- **[SYSTEM] Aggression turned on themselves; the body.** Disgusted with themselves and their bodies; though generally slender and attractive, find themselves too fat or ugly; new diet plans; anorexia "fairly frequent among FOUR women." [p.77]
- **[SYSTEM] In relationships.** Need friends and partners who bear with them without being drawn into mood shifts; a steady loyalty. The partner, being present and "easily had," is deficient and criticized, can be refused sexually (even impotence); "hot-and-cold treatment" (seduction, then rejection; lure back if the partner withdraws; dramatic scenes up to suicide threats). Rehearsed dance: "If you take a step forward, I take a step back." Example: Kierkegaard broke his engagement to Regine Olsen after a year, thinking he should not burden her with his melancholy; converted it into his first aesthetic works. [p.77]
- **[SYSTEM] Quiet happiness is both attractive and repellent**, since it could mean the end of the sweet wistfulness FOURs need to feel "themselves." Example: Rilke refused therapy fearing "when the devils left him, the angels might leave him too." [p.77]
- **[SYSTEM] Wings and subtypes.** Many FOURs vacillate between exaggerated activity and withdrawal/quasi-paralysis ("manic-depressive structure"); highly introverted FOURs (stronger FIVE wing) can become altogether depressive; FOURs with a dominant, success-oriented, extroverted THREE wing are often hyperactive. "These two 'subtypes' of FOUR do not look very similar at first glance." [p.77]
- **[SYSTEM] Depression vs. normal grief.** The unredeemed FOUR's depression is bound up with the feeling of the uniqueness and vastness of their own suffering and unwillingness to accept help; behind "nobody would understand me" lies refusal to mourn; they cling to what has been lost. [p.77]
- **[SYSTEM] Touchiness and self-criticism.** Take feelings very seriously, deeply offended when "hurt"; criticism of their art wounds and drives them into retreat; yet they run themselves down; "a painter who is a FOUR is the only one allowed to criticize his pictures." [p.77]
- **[SYSTEM] Examples of FOURs in film and the arts.** Hollywood is the "El Dorado of FOURs"; they view their whole life as a stage; famous FOURs: Marilyn Monroe, Marlon Brando, James Dean (lost his mother at eight; enfant terrible; sat in a coffin for photos; bongos for attention; "That's the only time I feel whole" about racing; died at twenty-four in a speeding crash; posthumous cult). Dazzling figures invite projections; those who reach out may "grasp the void." Marilyn Monroe: orphan, raped at nine, first suicide attempt at sixteen; Ernesto Cardenal's "Prayer for Marilyn Monroe" quoted ("She hungered for love, and we offered her tranquilizers"). [p.77-78]
- **[SYSTEM] Gift / fruit of the spirit: balance.** By twenty-five FOURs have lived all emotional spaces from agony to ecstasy and understand the human soul better than anyone. If they muster discipline to bring emotional life into balance they become impressive personalities; "It's discipline that makes the difference between a second-class 'misunderstood genius' and a real artist." Great FOURs concentrate and discipline emotions, distance themselves from them and purify them. They must stop bathing in feelings, "playing with their moods" and foisting them on others. Healthy FOURs have depth of feeling most lack; express concentrated sense of the beautiful and painful in works "of something universally valid" (Shakespeare, T. S. Eliot cited); redeemed FOURs are better than others at guiding people in psychic distress, not intimidated by dark feelings since they have lived them. [p.78]

### Symbols and Examples (FOUR) [p.78-82]
- **Animals:** mourning dove (plangent cooing; the style of speech by which FOURs can be recognized is "the longing complaint or lament"); basset hound (sad eyes; the eyes of FOURs reflect undefined sadness, "smiling through tears"); noble black racing horse (cool aesthetics). **Redeemed FOURs: the oyster** (transforms dirt into pearls: loss into beauty and universal validity; Musil: "Writing is like the pearl of a sickness"). [p.78]
- **Country: France.** FOURs are often Francophiles: France refuses to be like others, always special; refined, cultivated, somewhat elitist; haute cuisine, haute couture; "Everything has to be 'high' and unusual"; some FOURs speak with an affected French or British accent. [p.79]
- **Color: the bright violet of the hollyhock.** Shimmering, extraordinary, melancholic and mystical-conflicting; liturgical color of Passiontide; Goethe: violet as both highest rapture and darkest, most painful moments (passion and intoxication, liberation and decay, death and resurrection, pain and redemption, disease and purification, mystical vision and madness); androgynous color mediating red (masculine) and blue (feminine); the redeemed FOUR embodies synthesis, mediation and balance. [p.79]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Biblical figures.** Shulamith of the Song of Solomon embodies the longing erotic romanticism of FOURs ("no real existing man... can match this ideal image"). Joseph (Gen. 36-50 as cited): father's favorite, many-colored cloak, dreams of the sheaves and stars, sold into slavery, refuses Potiphar's wife, interprets dreams, becomes prime minister; "with the dramatic talents of a FOUR Joseph stages the reconciliation." Prophets with FOUR traits ("singled out," unusual symbolic acts): Isaiah walking naked through Jerusalem; Hosea marrying a harlot; Jeremiah unmarried as a sign, the lamentations as "the oldest texts of humanity in which an individual directly reflects on and formulates his psychological state." [p.79-80]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Conversion and Maturation (FOUR).** *Invitation: the call to originality.* FOURs find naturalness on the way to union with God; love of children and nature are early hints; if they can admit they live "in God" and God "in them," their soul comes to rest and balance. [p.80]
  - **[SYSTEM] Life tasks:** develop healthy realism and direct longing to reachable goals; keep attention in the present, not past or future; find energy without swinging from euphoria to depression; the "objective observer" asks: "Isn't a little joy and a little sadness enough - at least now and then?"; unredeemed FOURs "love ritual more than reality" and glorify memories as more beautiful than the event; hence **incarnation**: accept reality even when ugly and dirty; social commitment, peace and justice work do FOURs good because they must handle the dirt of the world that "cannot be aesthetically transfigured." [p.80]
  - **[SYSTEM] Confront loss and mourn.** FOURs need to confront real experiences of loss, admit the rage against the person in question and stop adulating him or her; Mitscherlich's "inability to mourn" hampers liberation. **[CHRISTIAN]** 2 Cor. 7:10 (godly grief vs. worldly grief). Critically examine snobbishness and hidden elitism; compare less, become grateful for inner treasures and share them; need a network of people who "won't let themselves be manipulated" and demand authentic communication. Without FOURs, the world would lose most art and poetry; serving others with their gifts they help "redeem this world through beauty." [p.81]
  - **[CHRISTIAN] Saints of redeemed FOURs: Daniel Berrigan and Thomas Merton.** Berrigan (Jesuit, Christian peace movement): symbolic, illegal, nonviolent actions designed to get attention; burned draft cards; the Pentagon action (costumes as spirits of the dead, pouring blood, a cross with weapon names); the authors: he put "his longing and his pleasure in dramatization at the service of peace and justice." Merton (1915-68): born in Prades into a family of artists; lost mother at six, father at sixteen ("Thus I became a complete twentieth-century man"); Cambridge, bar-hopping, cartoons, womanizing; disgust with himself; Communist Party; baptized Catholic 1938; rejected by the Franciscans after telling the truth about his life; Trappist abbey of Gethsemani, postulant 1941; *The Seven Storey Mountain* a bestseller (1946); about sixty more books; abbot thought he took subjective feelings too seriously; forbidden to write; novice master; encouraged individual personalities; understood monks as people overcoming the "false self"; essays against "just war" and American militarism; assassination attempt; hermitage in the woods; love affair with a student nurse (Suzanne Zuercher's Enneagram biography sees it as a necessity for his "grounding"); dreamed of Alaska, then drawn East; Bangkok 1968, met Sufis, Zen Buddhists, the Dalai Lama; accidentally electrocuted by a fan; flown home by a U.S. military plane. [p.81-82]

## TYPE FIVE: The Need to Perceive [p.83-91]

### Overview (FIVE) [p.83-87]
- **[SYSTEM] Center.** "FIVEs, SIXes, and SEVENs are head people": they think before they act and have, "or so it seems," a certain objectivity. [p.83]
- **[SYSTEM] Gifts.** Open and receptive to new facts and impressions; discoverers of new ideas, researchers, inventors; objective, questioning, detail-oriented; original, provocative, surprising, unorthodox, profound; good listeners who help others perceive the truth more soberly; some have strong contemplative gifts; redeemed FIVEs link knowledge to a search for wisdom ("a sympathetic knowledge of the heart") and have a quiet inner power, being tender, loving, polite, hospitable, gentle. [p.83]
- **[SYSTEM] Primary experience: emptiness.** Some had, in the womb, the "experience" that "I am not wanted"; psychically or physically intrusive parents, or very cramped surroundings (inner world the only free space); or, apparently the opposite, little tenderness and intimacy as children, so the capacity to show feelings and express them physically stayed underdeveloped. Lack of security, homelessness, loneliness; FIVEs "creep inside themselves like an animal that plays dead." [p.83]
- **[SYSTEM] Taking and collecting.** FIVEs gather what they can to fill the inner vacuum. "If TWOs are under a certain compulsion to give, FIVEs are equally obsessed with taking." Collecting directed at thoughts, ideas, knowledge, silence and space; the hardened ones hoard books, stamps, beer mats, old newspapers, fabric remnants, toothpaste tube tops, milk cartons. [p.83]
- **[SYSTEM] Private fortress.** Need a closed-off protected private sphere ("My home is my castle"); mostly introverts; "by nature monks, hermits, ascetics, bookworms, librarians, technical sticklers." Often wear glasses; eyes "like vacuum cleaners," energy concentrated on seeing and taking it all in; like microscopes, telescopes, photography; observer roles; try not to be drawn into the whirlpool of feelings, to keep calm externally and keep emotions under control; "demonstrative 'fuss'" is odious; can seem snooty and cold, as if needing nobody. In reality there is an intense emotional life, but feelings are blocked and "come limping behind": they register events with eyes, ears and brain; alone, they evaluate it from the head and feelings are "ordered and brought into line." Symbolic plant: green lettuce, "the plant that has its heart in its head." [p.83-84]
- **[SYSTEM] Relationships.** Like FOURs, FIVEs feel more connected to those absent than those present; show warmth through small gestures rather than directly, so partners feel the FIVE has little interest; partners complain FIVEs "are forever taking and seldom give." Friendship works if one does not expect three things: initiative-taking, continual physical nearness, total surrender. "FIVEs are afraid of giving their little finger lest people want the whole hand." Those content with the little finger find "a true companion, a patient, silent listener, and a fair counselor." [p.84]
- **[SYSTEM] Famous FIVEs (authors' list).** Philosophers: Plotinus (Porphyry: "resembled a man who is ashamed of being in a body"), Thomas Aquinas ("dumb ox," silent), Bonaventure, Descartes (spectator rather than actor; chose Holland to live unnoticed; "I doubt, therefore I am"), Spinoza, Feuerbach (frugal student life), Heidegger (hut in the Black Forest), Popper. Wittgenstein's restless sequence of fields is told as an example of FIVE's inability to feel ripe for any undertaking. Also Greta Garbo (see below). [p.84-85]
- **[SYSTEM] Mysticism.** Along with philosophy FIVEs are drawn to religious mysticism (Buddhism, American Indian religion, Sufism, Hassidism, Kabbala, Meister Eckhart, Philokalia and Athos hesychasm, Teerstegen); the "inner eye" is easier for FIVEs. [p.84-85]
- **[SYSTEM] Perpetual preparation.** Rohr's example of religious priests who are FIVEs, "still not finished with their training for any service": degree in Chicago, paper in Rome, biblical studies in Jerusalem; they need the certainty of the whole picture, "and so their flesh never touches the flesh of the world." [p.85]
- **[SYSTEM] Avoiding attention.** Contrast with FOURs: FIVEs avoid attracting attention; rehearsed behavior serves adaptation ("How should I behave so that as few people as possible will notice that I'm here or want something from me?"); steer personal topics away; clam up if someone seems to "sound them out"; hate words like "share" or "communicate"; wait before expressing themselves; communicate as little as possible; but listen well, nothing escapes them. [p.85]
- **[SYSTEM] Parenting and intrusion.** Many have problems with parental roles because children make permanent demands on time, space and energy; a woman FIVE found raising children "hell"; many shrink from marrying and having children. In monastic communities want a garret at the end of the corridor; hate intrusion (running through their room without knocking can trigger rage); regularly need solitude "to refuel"; closeness and crowds exhaust them. [p.85]
- **[SYSTEM, anecdote]** Rohr at Gethsemani: many contemplative monks sitting three hours before the Blessed Sacrament are "nothing but FIVEs"; the monks laughed. For FIVEs sitting for three hours looking at something is rest: nobody wants anything and they do not have to give. [p.85-86]
- **[SYSTEM] Caveat.** Not all FIVEs are profound or intelligent: "there are perfectly stupid FIVEs too"; their head is the control tower; what they do not understand they do not meddle with. Unredeemed FIVEs can take on schizoid traits, develop forms of autism, or end in nihilism, "the ultimate consequence of 'pure thinking' without the body, emotion, value judgments, and deeds." The autistic person is "the full caricature of a compulsive FIVE." Film *Rain Man*: a dynamic, upwardly mobile THREE meets his autistic brother; mutual "conversion" begins. [p.86]

### Dilemma (FIVE) [p.86-88]
- **[SYSTEM] Temptation: knowledge.** "For FIVEs knowledge is power." They think they can secure their lives by being informed about everything in detail; the information is never sufficient; another course, seminar, book or silent retreat. Overrepresented at Enneagram workshops, sitting in the back row; fascinated by systems explaining the universe or soul; some reject the Enneagram because it "uncovers their life program." Their strength is also their sin: "I know more than other people... I'm above the sentimentality" [p.86]
- **[SYSTEM] Travel as collecting.** FIVEs like to travel (studying foreign cultures without being known); "limited adventures" without strings; experience happens later reviewing slides at home; souvenirs and keepsakes as props for memory; some collect "totems" covering phases of their life. [p.86]
- **[SYSTEM] Defense mechanism 1: withdrawal.** Afraid of nothing so much as emotional engagement; shy from feelings, sex, dependency-creating relationships; start or jump back when touched; many have a celibate make-up, sometimes for false motives, becoming "eccentric bachelors and old maids." Example: Greta Garbo (aliases, disguises, no name on the door, bodyguards, hated watching her own films, spoke about abstract subjects to avoid talking about herself, never married, short love affairs, simple clothing like a nun's habit, left rooms empty, withdrew completely from 1941). [p.86-87]
- **[SYSTEM] Fear of concrete commitment.** Stay in the abstract world of theories; Karl Marx's reproach to philosophers; Abraham Maslow on "being knowledge" (understanding without judgment, decision or action; "a not-being-in-the-world"). Hence conservatism; value-free research "made many discoveries... a scourge on humanity"; scientists who refuse to consider ethical implications. Dürrenmatt's *The Physicists* (Möbius pretends madness to protect the world from his formula) is cited. [p.87]
- **[SYSTEM] "Unenlightened Buddha"** (Helen Palmer's name for the immature FIVE): the enlightened Buddha breaks free of the world after living and suffering through it; the unenlightened one renounces emotions because he cannot and will not commit to them; reaches premature intellectual solutions; scorns the "sour grapes of the world"; Zen can serve as "immunization against the 'world' and the 'flesh.'" [p.87]
- **[SYSTEM] Defense mechanism 2: compartmentalization.** Life divided into segments in practical independence; friends in each area never learn of each other; attention and devotion are available within the established boundaries. [p.87]
- **[SYSTEM] "Limitation."** Afraid of being co-opted and of emotional overstrain, FIVEs feel safe only when the time and space of a relationship are staked out; want to know how long a meeting will last to prepare internally; surprise visits and unexpected challenges threaten; emotional expectation is unpleasant; "you get something from a FIVE... only when you neither expect it nor ask for it"; in open conflict only retreat and intellectual arguments are available. [p.87]
- **[SYSTEM] Root sin: avarice.** Not givers; hoard intellectual and material possessions; "needs a challenging kick in the behind: 'Now it's time that you finally fork over something from your treasures.'" [p.87]
- **[SYSTEM] Pitfall: emotional stinginess.** Fear that sharing themselves means losing themselves; can become misers like Scrooge; they do not enjoy life but are stingy so that future repose is guaranteed; pathological cases: Howard Hughes and J. Paul Getty. But most FIVEs are very modest in demands, naturally ascetic ("need only a bit of everything"; some count the pieces of toilet paper), proud of modesty; because their primary experience was not getting what they needed; "From this perspective the miserliness and frugality of FIVEs are not real opposites." [p.87-88]
- **[SYSTEM] Greatest gifts** (the reverse of obsessions): contemplatively gifted, understand connections, invent grand intellectual systems. **Horror vacui**: FIVEs avoid emptiness; outsiders find them mysterious and deep, but FIVEs fear they have little value and little real wealth in them; "The fear of emptiness (horror vacui) is the real impetus behind much that unredeemed FIVEs do." [p.88]
- **[SYSTEM] Gift / fruit of the spirit: objectivity.** "Immature FIVEs have to distance themselves; mature FIVEs can distance themselves." Valuable for every community; outstanding counselors, able to listen for hours; their emotional withdrawal helps seekers see their situation more soberly; can say "Now I think the issue can be viewed from this side and from that." "Detachment is at once the gift and sin of the FIVE": the only type for which the same word describes the greatest strength and greatest weakness. [p.88]

### Symbols and Examples (FIVE) [p.88-89]
- **Animals:** owl (immobile eyes, hearing; Egypt/India symbol of death; Athena and Athens; sees everything, hard to locate); fox (loner predator; narrow pupils; Chinese myth: at one hundred can change form at will, at one thousand white fur, nine tails, omniscient; sly and crafty; in Christian symbolism deceitfulness, greed, despair; in fairy tales a helper in need); hamster (cheek pouches; hoarding "food" for worse times). [p.88]
- **Country: Great Britain** (the conservative, polite, reserved, coolly distanced English gentleman); other side: the stingy Scot. [p.88]
- **Color: blue** (introversion, repose, distance; more receptive than radiant; traditionally feminine; blue of the Mother of God's starry mantle = human receptiveness to the mystery of the universe; sky and sea; dark shades = passivity, silent contemplation, immobility; Kandinsky: blue leads away from others into our own center). [p.88]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Biblical patrons: Mary (mother of Jesus), the apostle Thomas, the tax collector Zacchaeus.** Mary: receptiveness, mystical-contemplative side, "capable of receiving before she gives"; "Mary kept all these things, pondering them in her heart" (Luke 2:19); secrets are safe in FIVEs; church history made her a spiritualized untouchable virgin, but liberation theology, working from the Magnificat, discovers a combative Mary: "the EIGHT side of Mary (EIGHT, the energy of the 'deed,' is the integration point of FIVE)." Thomas: before Easter says "Let us also go, that we may die with him" (John 11:16), sign of nihilistic resignation; the post-Easter doubter who trusts only what he has seen; Jesus demands that "Thomas the Rationalist make body contact," in contrast with Mary Magdalen the TWO told "Do not cling to me": "While TWOs have to break away from symbiosis and develop their capacity for genuine distance, FIVEs have to go from the head to the body, from thought to the deed." Legend: Thomas founded the church in India. Zacchaeus (Marion Kuestenmacher's discovery; Luke 19:1-10): climbed a tree to see without being seen (an overview, avoiding bodily contact); Jesus tracked him down and invited himself to his house, "crossing the boundary"; Zacchaeus became capable of letting go of what he hoarded and of giving. [p.88-89]

### Conversion and Maturation (FIVE) [p.89-91]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Invitation: wisdom.** Wisdom = deep knowledge of the connections of world and life, won from thought and real-life experience: "Wisdom is reflected experience." FIVEs incline to "preflection" (thinking before acting or instead of acting); reflection is the subsequent processing of lived life. Part of wisdom is trust in God's dispensation; letting mysteries remain mysteries instead of dissecting everything "with the rational scalpel." [p.89]
- **[SYSTEM] Life tasks.** Learn commitment and action; FIVEs "have to fall in love passionately" (nearness crashes with equally strong wish for distance; a FIVE can fall head over heels but go numb with the beloved; feelings come afterward); those who allow no passion are "very incomplete persons." Meditation and prayer are uncommonly important sources of power: cultivate the inner world to find the courage for the outer world, which becomes possible when the inner world feels less threatening. [p.89]
- **[CHRISTIAN]** Meditate on the Incarnation: "the God who heals human beings precisely by touching them." [p.90]
- **[SYSTEM, anecdote]** A FIVE woman became a medical masseuse (up to nine massages a day), "a part or continuation of my prayer life"; she remains a FIVE. Principle: **agere contra**: act against the grain of natural compulsions; requires clear decisions; "unnatural" or "supernatural." FIVEs must cut loose and make mistakes; they fear doing something unreasonable (FIVE and SIX are neighbors; fear is no stranger to FIVEs). Helpful: Gestalt therapy, manual labor, creative externalization (pottery, painting), political and social commitment. [p.90]
- **[SYSTEM] What FIVEs need:** secure love in the inner world (experience of God) and outer world; encouragement such as "You can feel safe here. We're glad that you're here. You have a right to be here. You're welcome. You belong to us." Guard against arrogance and conceit; renounce secretiveness and artificial mystification; express emotions directly instead of storing them up "for the silent little chamber"; temperamentally FIVEs would prefer Buddhism, but Eastern world-denial can prevent them from discovering the Incarnation and the cross. [p.90]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Saints: Hildegard of Bingen** (1098-1179; universal learning; visions caused suffering and sickness; she recovered when she wrote down and communicated them; mystics must pass from "morbid introspection into deeds"; sharp-eyed spiritual and political visionaries) **and Dietrich Bonhoeffer** (1906-45; the path "from thought to action": upper-bourgeois family demanding self-control and objectivity, bookworm and chess player, doctoral thesis at nineteen, Barcelona, Harlem, Confessing Church seminary director, banned from teaching, returned to Germany on the last ship, joined the circle planning Hitler's assassination, arrested April 1943, planned suicide to avoid betraying others under torture, hanged at Flossenbuerg April 9, 1945). Prison quotation: "Not to do just anything, but to do what is right, and to dare; not to float about in the possible, but to bravely seize the real. Freedom is not in the flight of thoughts, but only in action." [p.90-91] His vision: a really poor church with a simple lifestyle in the following of Christ. [p.91]

## TYPE SIX: The Need for Security [p.92-]

### Overview (SIX) [p.92-93]
- **[SYSTEM] Gifts.** Cooperative, team players, reliable; fidelity in relationships; warmhearted deep friendships; often highly original and witty, droll humor; "give body and soul" for loved ones. Redeemed SIXes combine sound traditions with readiness for new paths; sense of the possible and impossible; spot unsuspected weak points of a project in good time ("a sixth sense for threatening dangers"); can be farsighted and bold in opening new frontiers. [p.92]
- **[SYSTEM, with CHRISTIAN framing] Unrecognized root sins.** THREEs and SIXes matter because their root sins (fear for SIX, deceit for THREE) "were not recognized in Western Christianity as such"; while unrecognized, they are "a source of great danger for our society." The authors report that many Enneagram practitioners consider SIX "by far the most frequently encountered human type" in Western society. [p.92]
- **[SYSTEM] Core.** Self-doubt, fearful and mistrustful looking ahead, constant sense of danger; in the most immature form, paranoia. Child's early anxieties lead to the attitude "the world is dangerous; you have to be on the lookout; I don't have enough inner authority to be up to all that, so I have to look for security somewhere outside myself." Riso's description of the torn condition (quoted): emotionally dependent yet not revealing; want closeness yet test others first; worship authority yet fear it; obedient yet disobedient; fearful of aggression yet sometimes highly aggressive; search for security yet feel insecure; likable yet can be mean; believe in traditional values yet may subvert them; want to escape punishment yet may bring it on themselves. [p.92]
- **[SYSTEM] Childhood roots.** Uncontrolled, unpredictable, violent or cold parents; punished or beaten without evident reason; three consequences: look for a protector; learn to detect slightest signs of danger to find cover; or aggressively anticipate the danger. [p.92]
- **[SYSTEM] Seeking authority.** The first case: lack of self-confidence leads to authorities who tell them where the limits are; need an institution (church, party, state, science) or a book (Bible, canon law, penal code, Qur'an, *Mein Kampf*, *Das Kapital*) with reliable answers; long for certainty, a black-and-white world and "a clearly spelled-out truth they can take home." Worst case: the authoritarian type who wants truth in totalitarian, self-righteous fashion and carries out every order from above (Eichmann's trial statement cited: "an oath is an oath"). [p.92-93]
- **[SYSTEM] Breaks in life history; "going around" success.** Paralyzing fear of failure before exams; scrutinizing every detail and eliminating contradictions; question their own position rather than defend it; "Sisyphean task of making their own opinion watertight." Many produce situations in which they lose in the end; pessimists, anxious about success: "If they never succeed, then the danger is not so great that envious competitors will appear." They pass success along to others or set unreachable goals; "SIXes fight for their survival, but never for success"; forget successes at once. "If immature THREEs are notorious winners, SIXes are notorious losers"; masochistic features; Woody Allen's film persona. [p.93]
- **[SYSTEM] Praise.** Most SIXes cannot accept praise (suspect a trick); to be accepted by a SIX add "a minimum of constructive criticism" to praise. [p.93]
- **[SYSTEM] Phobic vs. contraphobic SIXes** (the key distinction, "Both look so different"). **Phobic:** careful, hesitant, mistrustful; do not trust themselves or their instinct; evade danger; "easy to lead"; with a trustworthy counselor can slowly face anxieties and become more relaxed, autonomous, free. **Contraphobic:** drawn to aggressive radical movements; seek risky situations (mountain climbing, auto racing) preferring to "take the bull by the horns"; disguise the fear with put-on hardness, strength and daredevil behavior; no access to the fear that rules them; fly off the handle easily (scream, curse, lie, blows); cannot bear criticism or deviation from what they consider right. [p.93] Watzlawick's "Story with the Hammer" illustrates projective escalation (man imagines neighbor's refusal until he shouts "Keep your stupid hammer!"). [p.93]

### Dilemma (SIX) [p.93-96]
- **[SYSTEM] Temptation: exaggerated striving for security.** Love orthodox closed systems; traditionalism or fundamentalism; need an infallible source of truth ("Fundamentalists Anonymous" groups organized like AA). Thomas Meyer: fundamentalisms as a reaction to the "loss of consolation" in skeptical, secularized modernity. [p.93-94]
- **[SYSTEM] Fascination with law.** Seek occupations dealing with the law, protecting or breaking it: judges, prosecutors, defense attorneys, detectives, police, writers of whodunits, criminals "take part in one way or another in the SIX game." Helen Palmer calls SIXes "devil's advocates" (advocatus diaboli in canonization trials); a sixth sense for absurdities and suspicious elements. [p.94]
- **[SYSTEM] Defense mechanism: projection.** Rich imagination for apocalyptic scenarios; anticipate the worst; project hostility and negative thoughts onto others on scanty evidence (e.g., exaggerated notions of a partner's unconscious motives); mistrust of themselves leads to imagining their own negative motives in others; classic scapegoat mechanism. [p.94]
- **[SYSTEM] Root sin: fear**, "a sort of primal anxiety." **[CHRISTIAN]** The Bible continually says "Fear not!"; fear gets code names such as diplomacy, prudence, reasonableness, loyalty, obedience; false obedience is "the rotten fruit of fear," whereas authentic obedience grows from the freedom to hear, make a conscientious decision and, if appropriate, say no. Fear's seat is the head (phantasms and apocalyptic scenarios), only then the feelings and gut. [p.94]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Church critique.** The Sufis supposedly labeled the Roman Catholic Church the "church for SIXes"; fear of God, priesthood, mortal sins, themselves, their body (attitude toward sexuality); Eugen Drewermann; Dostoyevsky's Grand Inquisitor (freedom taken back "out of true love"); Eco's *The Name of the Rose* (Jorge, laughter as weakness; "Anxiety and humor, freedom and fear are incompatible"). Before Vatican II the church attracted insecure people as a "bulwark of absolute and infallible truth"; authors predict traditionalism and fundamentalism will grow unless people see the "SIX pitfall" and name anxiety as fear instead of faith or loyalty; Protestantism did the same ("people are afraid of God, instead of falling in love with God"; the "fear of God" concept; Tilmann Moser's anti-prayer quoted). [p.94-95]
- **[SYSTEM] Pitfalls.** Phobic SIX: cowardice; contraphobic SIX: taking foolish risks. All SIXes both overestimate and mistrust authorities, feel weak and exposed, may kowtow in blind obedience, or join other underdogs to find strength in common; contraphobics panic before fantasies gain power, then plunge into risks or "rebel with the courage of despair." SIXes avoid inappropriate behavior and see that no one breaks agreements. [p.95]
- **[SYSTEM] The positive side of the root sin.** Communities with SIXes can depend on their loyalty, self-sacrifice, readiness to assume responsibility; they expect the same loyalty from superiors; devotion turns to rebellion when the "infallible" leadership openly fails; because they feel stepped on, they can be "passionate and courageous fighters for the cause of the oppressed." [p.95]
- **[SYSTEM] Gift / fruit of the spirit: courage.** In moments of crisis SIXes overcome fear more easily than anyone; they get tired of being "the eternal chicken" and transcend themselves heroically. Anecdote: the woman at New Jerusalem, "a fearful little mouse," springs between a car and a child. [p.95-96]
- **[SYSTEM] Sense for what is "in the air."** Helen Palmer (herself a SIX per the authors) says her empathy and therapeutic talent grew from her fear ("Actually I developed this ability because I was afraid"); in earlier days she would have been called a witch or clairvoyant. [p.96]

### Symbols and Examples (SIX) [p.96-]
- **Animals.** Phobic SIX: hare in full flight (watchfulness, camouflage, doubling back), fearful gray mouse, shy deer. Contraphobic: wolf (needs the pack), loyal obedient German shepherd, the rat (aggressive pleasure when cornered). [p.96]
- **Country: Germany.** Stereotype: the heel-click and "Achtung!" = contraphobic style, artificial self-assuredness over uncertainty; repressed fear "has taken its revenge more than once in German history"; difficulty facing what was done in two World Wars as "just doing their duty"; "conversion" in the Bible's sense = becoming aware and accepting history. Luise Rinser (via Erich Fromm's biophile/necrophile): Germany is an "animus-country" of the fathers, Italy an "anima-country." [p.96]
- **Color: beige brown.** Does not strike the eye, fits the environment; color of bark protecting the tree; combination of red and green in which dynamic red disavows itself: the color of self-denial; closeness to the earth and security; *humus* (earth) and *humilitas* (humility) share a Latin root, so the medieval mendicant orders (and the Sufis before them) wore brown habits. [p.96-97]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Biblical patrons.** Phobic SIX: **Timothy**, Paul's disciple, "the perfect follower, whom Paul must reassure" (young leader of the Ephesus community by Paul's assignment; name means "God-honoring"; letters read as directives from superior to subordinate; first appearance of the hierarchical "pastor"/functionary and office of bishop, reflecting "early Catholic Church... transformed from a movement to an institution"; scholars dispute Pauline authorship). Contraphobic SIX: **the Apostle Peter** (ready to die for his master, Matt. 26:35; cuts off the slave's ear as "forward flight"; denies Jesus from fear of mockery; the Peter-Paul clash in Gal. 2:11-21 as "the typical collision between a reformer (ONE) and a person with an authoritarian structure (SIX)"; compromise at the Apostolic Council, Acts 15; yet brave before the council, "We must obey God rather than any human authority," Acts 5:29; martyred). [p.97] (Cross-reference: the Peter/Paul clash was treated "in the treatment of type ONE," Part 1.)

### Conversion and Maturation (SIX) [p.97-99]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Invitation: faith.** Few people have healthy self-confidence. Faith "in the biblical sense" is not maintaining assertions but "a relationship of trust between humanity and God. God believes in us"; on this basis a healthy self-confidence. Anecdote: a priest begged Rohr "Promise me, Richard, that you'll always trust yourself" (permission to be one's own "inner authority"). [p.97]
- **[SYSTEM] Life tasks.** Break free from external direction, take responsibility for life and feelings; "look their fear in the eye and call it by name" (**[CHRISTIAN]** Jesus asked demons their names; the spell broke once the name was spoken); shake off dominating phantasms. SIXes prefer structured, ordered, controlled-from-the-head piety ("Am I saying the right prayers? ... in the right way?"; the preconciliar church "fixated on the right words," "too much left brain"); women's influence brings right-brain, intuition, body, "heart" forms of prayer. Men in Western society have "profited from this game"; SIX men especially should free themselves from preestablished "logic," even at the risk of mistakes. [p.97-98]
- **[SYSTEM] Practices for SIXes.** Strengthen personal trust in God and self; a warm, intimate relationship with a personal God; a community where people talk about fears and feelings (not abstract exchange of thoughts); ask friends to scrutinize fears and test whether suppositions about others' motives are projections; make decisions without permission from "authorities"; remember successes and strive for more; martial arts (tae kwon do, judo) to react "from the gut"; body movements that support self-confidence; find by themselves what is best for them; **humor**: when we laugh from the heart fear cannot remain; "dictatorships and all systems that operate on fear are afraid of nothing so much as being unmasked through laughter, mockery, and satire." Cabaret verse by two SIXes at the first German Enneagram seminar (self-ironic: "Germany must stay German! Distrust the foreigner, distrust yourself!... Like Timothy, ask Paul for advice... Loyalty and Loyola"; "Those who swim with the current finish dead"). [p.98]
- **[SYSTEM] Needs.** Places of security free from fear where they need not defend themselves; a God who is not at once "dear" and "angry," who allows mistakes and weaknesses. **[CHRISTIAN]** 1 John 4:18: "perfect love casts out fear." [p.98]
- **[SYSTEM] Underidentification.** At workshops few people identify as SIXes though it is presumably very common; "no type is 'better' or 'worse' than the others. Each one contains terrible and splendid possibilities." [p.99]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Saint for SIXes: Oscar Romero** (1917-80), archbishop of San Salvador: "a classic SIX, a shy, subtle, and conservative bookworm, a waverer, a man of the system"; appointed primate 1977 (conservatives rejoiced); within three months turned by the murder of a Jesuit priest, an old farmer and an altar boy, and a military attack on Aguilares where soldiers desecrated the church and refused him entry to save the hosts ("conversion"); "ultimate, essential loyalty" overtook political and ecclesiastical considerations ("a prophet out of obedience"); began discussing with co-workers instead of deciding alone; radio sermons; asked believers to "not wait for what the bishop says on Sunday"; attacked by the oligarchy as "psychopath"; John Paul II rejected his view that revolutionary violence is permitted as a last resort; called on soldiers to refuse commands; shot March 24, 1980 during his sermon; eighty thousand at the funeral where the military massacre killed thirty-nine more; "poor people in the villages and mountains have long since declared their shepherd a saint." [p.99]

## TYPE SEVEN: The Need to Avoid Pain [p.100-]

### Overview (SEVEN) [p.100-101]
- **[SYSTEM] Gifts.** Radiate joy and optimism; alive to the precious ingredients of each moment; childlike astonishment, life as a gift; impression "that there's enough of everything beautiful and good"; idealism and plans; pass on enthusiasm; help others see the sunny side; infectious humor and ability to laugh at themselves; children gather round; relaxed, imaginative, playful, disarming charm, "until one day they notice that all this also serves to protect them from anxiety and pain." [p.100]
- **[SYSTEM] Origin and double strategy.** Many had traumatic experiences they did not feel equal to; to avoid repetition they (1) repressed or whitewashed painful experiences (paint their life story in positive colors: "Of course we had difficulties too. Doesn't everybody? But you can't let it get you down."), and (2) "went into their heads" and planned life for as much "fun" and as little pain as possible; combining pleasant and useful gives many SEVENs visible prosperity. [p.100]
- **[SYSTEM] Image.** The Mary Poppins type ("everything's wonderful"); life as Disneyland; the "eternal child": Peter Pan or Mercury with winged shoes; stock phrases "Great! Cool! Brilliant! Super! Far out! Awesome!"; authors would like to tell them "Get some solid ground under your feet." [p.100]
- **[SYSTEM] Curiosity, stimulation, avoidance.** What they know or have is never enough; need change, stimulation, new experiences, maximizing *joie de vivre*; calendar full of exciting dates; unpleasant tasks pushed aside, put off or ignored, or cushioned with "a little bit of happiness on the side" (nice CD for the drive to a hearing; a record-store detour on a business trip). "Adrenaline addicts" with "champagne in their blood" (Palmer); much of what they do is "a flight from the painful abysses of their own soul." [p.100]
- **[SYSTEM] Generalists.** Not specialists but "generalists" (Riso); several irons in the fire; keep options open and unconsciously avoid committing deeply to a thing or person because "in depth they always see pain lurking" and total devotion would make their own limits visible; "master the art of bluffing," all-around dilettantes who seem many-faceted; "A handful of facts, cleverly combined, sometimes suffice." [p.100]
- **[SYSTEM] Work.** Hard to tie themselves to one career; several interesting jobs at once; prefer self-employment or a smoothly functioning team; anti-authoritarian; dislike having subordinates (pressure to exercise power means painful conflicts). [p.101]

### Dilemma (SEVEN) [p.101-105]
- **[SYSTEM] Temptation: idealism.** Must be sure they work for a good cause that brings joy to them and others; deny and repress the aspects of their activity that might hurt others, especially when their need for happiness collides with others'; the idea of structural sins (prosperity financed at the cost of the Third World) can provoke vehement contradiction. [p.101]
- **[SYSTEM] Defense mechanism: rationalization.** E.g., repressing thoughts of world-economic injustice ("it wouldn't make the poor happier if we gave up our money"); softening a separation by finding rational reasons it failed and turning to the positive in the new situation ("Freedom's nice too!"); death of a family member made bearable by calling it a "blessing" or "taken up to be with God"; "the pain is generally not felt, but shifted." Insight: "the happiness and joy of SEVENs are produced in the head just as the horror-filled scenarios of SIXes are." [p.101]
- **[SYSTEM] Blindness to shadow.** Can live for years without sensing the dark side; label those who press on sore points "whiners" or "cultural pessimists"; need a long time to see shadow sides of a relationship or themselves; like THREEs risk "ego inflation": "THREEs can refuse to see failures; SEVENs can refuse to feel pain." As children afraid of the dark, needed a nightlamp; like primary colors and bright lights. [p.101]
- **[SYSTEM, anecdote]** Rohr's priest retreats: when talk turns to dark side and shadow, Irish-descent priests (Ireland: "the land of the SEVENs") turn away or tell jokes in the back rows; "One of the most effective methods of avoiding pain is to laugh. 'Nervous laughter.'" [p.101]
- **[SYSTEM] Optimism and pessimism are neighbors.** SEVENs stand "directly alongside the professional worriers, the SIXes": both "are intellectual mechanisms for managing the abysses and dangers of life." [p.101]
- **[SYSTEM] Difficulty with emotional problems.** A SEVEN left ministry because bedside counseling meant facing pain ("I sat alongside someone who was suffering and told myself, 'I'm healthy, I'm doing all right.'"); unhappy when others are unhappy; need "good vibrations"; can cheer others up but may try to turn an ailing person "inside out"; deflect with "Well, you'll be up and around in no time," or "Things aren't all that bad." Anecdote: a woman smiling while telling Rohr that her child was in hospital and her husband had lost his job: "I simply don't trust myself to feel the pain and I smile." [p.101-102]
- **[SYSTEM] Hidden sadness; the clown.** Cheerfulness is often "put on and very deceptive"; they sometimes know the smile conceals great sadness; long for someone to see through the merriment; others pin them to their "character" and do not believe they are "screaming inside"; relapse into "Don't worry, be happy!" and the clown role (*Ridi, pagliaccio*). [p.102]
- **[SYSTEM] Root sin: intemperance (formerly gluttony).** Motto: "More is always better." Exaggerate everything: eating, drinking, working, beginning projects, seeking recognition, beautiful places, buying, possessing; "an excessive need for fun, joy, and pleasure." [p.102]
- **[SYSTEM, anecdote]** Rohr lived in Albuquerque with SEVEN Franciscans who embellish stories ("I'm embellishing a bit"); SEVENs have the gift of "pumping up a comic event like a balloon," finding where there is fun. Rohr (a ONE) is "too serious; they're too funny." [p.102]
- **[SYSTEM] Talking, asceticism.** Many talk too much; pastoral advice: "If you think you have to talk this much, cut it in half... drink... half is still too much... cross out every other [free-time activity]." "Less is always more when an immature SEVEN wants to be liberated from themselves." [p.102]
- **[SYSTEM] Escapism, weight, addiction.** Love science fiction, futurism, fantasy (anything out of the present, which they find unpleasant); lust to travel; weight problems like TWOs; on diets they hate (deprivation); love good food, candy; "TWOs store up love; SEVENs store up joy and happiness"; "SEVENs, TWOs, and NINEs are especially prone to addiction"; SEVENs drink or take drugs to deaden pain (Little Prince's drinker "I drink to forget... That I'm ashamed... That I drink."). [p.102]
- **[SYSTEM] Epicureans.** The authors present Epicurus's philosophy (happiness as absence of pain and presence of pleasure; refined pleasures such as friendship and ideas; excess is counterproductive; passionless soul; "When we are, death is not, and when death is, we are not") as "a perfect example of how SEVENs try to eliminate fear and pain through rational arguments." [p.103]
- **[CHRISTIAN/SYSTEM] No Good Friday.** SEVENs stage a life "where there is no Good Friday, where it's Easter all the time." Anecdote (Ebert): a SEVEN Protestant minister had "Christ Is Risen" sung at the end of Good Friday service because "they had to feel that everything would be all right." [p.103]
- **[CHRISTIAN/SYSTEM] Charismatic movement and New Age as SEVEN-marked.** The charismatic movement "is in danger of becoming a pure SEVEN movement": theology of resurrection and glory, avoiding the theology of the cross and "Jesus the Man of Sorrows"; Rohr's anecdote of the woman who abused him for talking about the cross; "more is always better" (fourteen opening hymns, forty-five "Praise the Lords"; majesty titles only); a "theology of glory" leads to "increasing superficiality and banality"; avoidance of social issues (Corinth, 1 Cor. 11:17-34); Christianized "positive thinking" (mourning treated as unbelief; "successful prayer," visualization, prosperity promises in the U.S. and Scandinavia); cognitive therapy that renounces examining causes: authors say it is "downright poison" for SEVENs if it is the only approach (danger of "religious brainwashing," "marionette"). The Bible contrast: Psalms, Gethsemane and the cross show suffering faced, not suppressed (Jesus refused the gall and vinegar). SEVENs "should not be too quick to 'visualize positively,' but learn first of all to admit and express pain." [p.103-104]
- **[SYSTEM] New Age.** Courses and books promising "harmony, enlightenment, and happiness" for cash; gurus with "boundless ambition for possessions and pleasures"; Palmer traces a line from 1960s flower children to "postmodern narcissism"; "spiritual consumerism"; if the Enneagram is misused thus, "its primal intention is missed: to call us to take the hard, stony path of conversion." [p.104]
- **[SYSTEM] Criticism.** Hard to challenge a SEVEN (charming smile or witty remark; criticism does not seem to reach them); afraid of attacks because failure hurts, "In this respect they resemble THREEs." [p.104]
- **[SYSTEM] Gift / fruit of the spirit: joy.** They should not become "weepy wet blankets"; joy should not come at the expense of the dark side, nor banal idealism deny reality. **Sober joy** = joy "in the face of and despite all the difficulties of life," the "nevertheless" of the Psalms (Ps. 73:23). "Sobriety is what makes the difference between superficial optimism and deeply grounded hope." [p.105]

### Symbols and Examples (SEVEN) [p.105-]
- **Animals:** monkey (leaping from branch to branch; practical jokes, slapstick); butterfly for redeemed SEVENs (beauty, lightness; metamorphosis as an old symbol of transformation; from the Church Fathers the Christian way of salvation by transformation through death to resurrection). [p.105]
- **Countries: Ireland and Brazil.** Ireland: every fourth house a pub; the Irish wake with singing, dancing and drinking; whiskey at nine in the morning. Brazil: enormously rich and extremely poor united in carnival where trouble is forgotten; cutting the rain forest as the symbol of what happens "when excess gets the upper hand." [p.105]
- **Color: green.** Vitality, *joie de vivre*, health; Hildegard's *viriditas*; also "greenhorns," unripe fruit (reminder that SEVENs must work to mature); in Christian art the color of Paradise and the cross (Chagall's green-gold crucified Christ in Zurich). [p.105]
- **Example: Mozart** (film *Amadeus*; monkey side; clowning; chronically broke; ribald, childlike wordplay letters to his cousin (quoted at length in the book; not reproduced here); glutton for work; danced from six in the evening until seven in the morning; sought friends for merrymaking and "scarcely entered into deeper relationships"). Pattern noted: at the hardest times he wrote the lightest music and in gayest periods gloomy pieces; authors' reading: "When the external circumstances provide enough security and acceptance, a SEVEN sometimes dares to confront the dark side of life." [p.105-106]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Biblical patrons: King Solomon and the rich young man.** Solomon (reigned 965-925 B.C.E.): son of Bathsheba (David's murderous plot against Uriah), court splendor (daily provisions, gold, ivory throne, seven hundred wives, three hundred concubines), pacified the country enabling "a unique intellectual, cultural, and religious flowering," built the temple, poet; Queen of Sheba tested him with hard questions (Ethiopian imperial house traces its genealogy to their encounter). [p.106]
  Solomon's extravagance had fatal consequences: his foreign wives seduced him into building temples to their gods; he succumbed to religious syncretism (1 Kings 11:5); after his death the kingdom divided and never regained its splendor. Proverbs, Ecclesiastes and the Wisdom of Solomon are ascribed to him ("no doubt wrongly"); parts of Ecclesiastes reflect "a kind of Epicurean worldview" (life meaningless; enjoy bread, wine and the beloved wife; Eccles. 9:7-9). [p.106]
- **[CHRISTIAN] The rich young man** (Mark 10:17-31 and parallels): "can positively be read as a summary of the Gospel." He asks what he must do to inherit eternal life; Jesus loves him and says to sell all and follow; his countenance falls "for he had great possessions." Authors' reading: he can imagine what he lacks only as an addition to existing material and religious wealth; Jesus points to a source of life reached by giving up planning one's own life and becoming poor before God; the Gospel quiets the drive for possessions "not through the promise of more possessions but through a kind of wealth that is more than a possession" and creates "a new justice in the relation of rich and poor"; it is a challenge to all, not only SEVENs; hasty spiritualizing misses the point. [p.106-107]

### Conversion and Maturation (SEVEN) [p.107-108]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Invitation: cooperation with God.** Unredeemed SEVENs think they fashion their own happiness and keep planning to optimize their lives; cooperating with God means confronting the world's reality (joy and pain together), accepting both, and going "God's way, which leads through death to resurrection"; able to bring joy and hope where grief reigns, and to actually go there. "The step from idealism to a wide-awake realism." [p.107]
- **[SYSTEM] Gift of redeemed SEVENs: sober joy.** Authentic and deep if it does not lose itself in superficial addiction to pleasure; for a beautiful celebration or a cheerful liturgy, hand it to SEVENs. [p.107]
- **[SYSTEM] Life tasks.** Get wise to overhasty rationalizations; because of "all the planned and organized joy" they may lose capacity to rejoice spontaneously; pigheaded and tense in avoiding pain. **Wing:** "SEVENs have a SIX-wing and for this reason can be astonishingly dogmatic": merry people can suddenly seem "narrow, absolutist, and authoritarian," especially when someone tries to spoil their mood; "The merriment of unredeemed SEVENs is the result of fear and a tool of their instinct for self-preservation. Head energy always pulls back from reality"; their commitment is often retreat, concealed by a grand to-do. [p.107]
- **[SYSTEM] Self-acceptance and presence.** Find deep self-acceptance by discovering they are accepted whole (not only the radiant side) by God and a few close people; live in the beautiful and painful present instead of fleeing into daydreams of future or past. On the beach: try lying in the sun instead of dragging books, Walkman, notepad, laptop "to leave all possibilities open." Overcome exaggerated fear of physical and psychological pain; meditation confronting the darker side; sickness or freely chosen limitation as a gateway to deep joy; healthy SEVENs who work in hospitals or with the dying. Those who love SEVENs must help them "eat, chew, swallow, and digest their pain," slow down, stop the chatter. William James: "once-born" vs "twice-born" believers (twice-born keep trusting after pain; once-born keep childlike optimism without having lived through much pain); the life task of many SEVENs may be "coming into the world a second time and growing up." [p.107]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Saint of SEVENs: Francis of Assisi (1182-1226).** Began as "the pleasure-seeking party king"; sensed flight from himself, withdrew, fell sick; the meeting with the leper was the turning point (Testament: what seemed "repulsive and bitter" was "transformed into sweetness for body and soul"); authors: SEVENs have a natural aversion to anything that stinks, is dirty or ugly; Francis overcame revulsion, embraced pain, moved in the opposite direction from his nature ("a cut-up, a comic, a cock of the walk"), chose "extravagant" poverty ("Less is more!"), yet remained "a merry saint and beggar" with an eye for beauty and needing a beautiful environment (like Mozart) to confront inner fears; the search for "perfect joy"; stick as a violin; greeted death as sister and friend; stigmata; 2 Cor. 4:10. [p.107-108]

## TYPE EIGHT: The Need to Be Against [p.109-]

### Overview (EIGHT) [p.109-114]
- **[SYSTEM] Gifts.** Strong and mighty, able to impart a feeling of strength to others; "a second sense for justice and truth"; instinctively know where something "stinks" (injustice, dishonesty) and address it openly and directly; a rock of reliability; tremendous sense of responsibility and solicitude; enormous energies for a cause; "The word of an EIGHT can be trusted." [p.109]
- **[SYSTEM] Center and stance.** The gut domain (EIGHTs, NINEs, ONEs). "Just as we ONEs want to be good boys and good girls, EIGHTs want to be bad boys and bad girls." Early impression "that the world punishes soft tendencies, and so they put their money on hardness." [p.109]
- **[SYSTEM] Childhood roots.** Repressed or pushed around as children; trusted no one but themselves; children of the Holocaust and of the slums (cannot show weakness or cry) often become EIGHTs; cliques and gangs with tests of courage; parents who rewarded strength ("Don't take it! Hit back!"); belief that the strong rule and the weak draw the short straw; decision not to be good or conform but to resist, break rules, order others; sometimes a counter-reaction to overly liberal, indulgent parents (testing how far until "someone calls them on the carpet"). [p.109]
- **[SYSTEM] Distinguishing EIGHT from ONE and from contraphobic SIX.** ONE vs EIGHT: both seem governed by aggression; EIGHTs "do not apologize or take anything back," have a hard time admitting mistakes (could look like weakness); nephew anecdote at four years old ("Not once have I managed to get him to say he was sorry"); "I'll show you all. You won't get me to come down a peg." They can be very harsh with themselves, unseen. SIX vs EIGHT: SIX aggression comes "from their head" as anxiety and self-protectiveness; EIGHT aggression "comes from the gut" and is directed at hypocrisy and injustice. [p.109]
- **[SYSTEM] Basic experience.** Life is threatening or hostile; trust only given on evidence; seek or create conflict; "take the gloves off"; "notorious nay-sayers" who "enjoy being against"; resistance and negation are the first reaction to new ideas, people, situations (Ebert's confirmation-class girl; his mother: "we could tease her... with a wink"). [p.109-110]
- **[SYSTEM] The inner child.** EIGHTs side with the weak; will not put up with high-handed authority; behind a façade of hardness, invulnerability, curses or brutality there is "a little boy or a little girl," the opposite of the projected strength; tenderness buried; shown to at most two or three people in a lifetime; EIGHTs find that child in others and wish to protect it. [p.110]
- **[SYSTEM] Self-image: "I've got power. I'm stronger than you."** Male EIGHTs often beefy or athletic. Group examples given by the authors: culture of the black ghettos ("bad" meaning "good"), liberation movements, the feminist movement; "People who have been pushed around and oppressed often develop EIGHT energy"; "Liberation theology in Latin America is largely an EIGHT theology." [p.110]
- **[SYSTEM] In power.** Subordinates feel oppressed; EIGHTs do not notice; they express anger immediately and move on, while victims do not get over it quickly. [p.110]
- **[SYSTEM] "Confrontational intimacy."** EIGHTs fight as a way of making contact; delight in attacking they see as "playful"; assume others enjoy conflict; blows go below the belt; attack to shake the artificial façade of the other; hate unclear messages; need to know friend from enemy; great respect for an equal enemy (Guareschi's Don Camillo and Peppone, fighting opposed value systems with crude means and respect). Excel at cards and competitive sports; sense others' weaknesses and exploit without remorse. [p.110]
- **[SYSTEM] Unmasking as a gift (therapists and teachers).** Some EIGHTs become influential therapists and spiritual guides: they "upset the false self-image of others" so the genuine can come to light. The authors' examples: **Gurdjieff** (an EIGHT per the authors; methods "stepping on others' corns" and "offering a toast to the idiot": hammering sensitive points until defenses appear, alcohol to break defenses; "turkey cock who'd like to be a peacock"); **Fritz Perls** (Gestalt: here-and-now; noticing mismatch between statements and gestures; "topdog" and "underdog"; taking the role of "opposition"; "unfinished business"); **Chuck Dederich** (Synanon: group indictment of a member with exaggerated details, group attacks the style of defense; comic but demands a strong trusting support group). "More than any other type, well-developed EIGHTs have the gift of leading other people to their real potential." [p.110-111]
- **[SYSTEM] Leaders and revolutionaries.** Martin Luther King Jr., Fidel Castro, Che Guevara, Jesse Jackson, Saul Alinsky; charismatically inspire commitment; people sense "they will finish what they start." Contrast: "While ONEs reform the system from within, EIGHTs tend to climb out of the system and throw stones at it"; EIGHT aggression mobilizes the aggression of the other side, so EIGHTs are easy to fear and hate; they seem to enjoy hatred and rejection, "bull in the china shop," "no diplomats." [p.111]
- **[SYSTEM] Avoid helplessness, weakness, subordination.** Treat opinions as absolutely correct and closed to other arguments; overbearing, arrogant; coworkers as doormats; put down opponents; false, unfounded hierarchies; friend-foe schema. "Woe betide anyone whom an EIGHT finds too self-confident": professor anecdote (harsh on the self-assured but "tremendous" to the failing). "When you're really poor, helpless, and weak, the EIGHTs' protective instinct is aroused... But as soon as you express in any way that you have power, then EIGHTs will prove that they have more power. You can never win a dispute with EIGHTs." [p.111]
- **[SYSTEM] What EIGHT energy actually is.** "The actual energy of EIGHTs is not anger or rage... Rather it is a passion and a total commitment to truth, life, and justice." Advice on dealing with them: do not be intimidated or give ground; either get fully into the fight or talk to the little boy or girl in the EIGHT; EIGHTs protect the weak but despise cowardice and softness; dark side: they finish off those they deem stupid or incompetent "even if they are already lying on the ground." Ebert's mentor anecdote (the feared mentor admired his unflustered independence). [p.111-112]
- **[SYSTEM] Physical.** Like hard contact sports (rugby, football), fast cars; a football player running on a half-shredded kneecap; "EIGHTs can enjoy pain; they endure it better than anyone else"; seldom show fear; daring; risk-taking; "often live on the edge of catastrophe"; "One doesn't quickly forget an encounter with an EIGHT." [p.112]

### Dilemma (EIGHT) [p.112-114]
- **[SYSTEM] Temptation/pitfall: retaliating justice.** The struggle for justice is both strength and temptation; EIGHTs may appoint themselves avengers; their concept of justice is "balancing out"; the "bad" person must be punished, even themselves; when thrown back on themselves the aggression turns inward; always look for a guilty party; **pitfall: revenge and retaliation**; all-or-nothing, friend/foe, so an EIGHT may find their greatest enemy in themselves and no longer trust themselves when confronted with their guilt. Terrorism "comes out of the energy of 'retaliating justice'" (self-appointed people's courts). "No greater torture for an EIGHT than to be isolated and thus cut off from all possibilities of external action." [p.112]
- **[SYSTEM] Defense mechanism: denial.** Deny what does not fit their concept of truth and justice, especially their own weaknesses and the limits of their power. [p.112]
- **[SYSTEM] Root sin: shamelessness** (the older "lust"). Authors define lust as "the violation of another person for pleasure or for passion": the other is shamelessly used, possessed or suppressed, without respect for dignity; an immature EIGHT has no respect for the vulnerability or dignity of another. They may demand high moral standards of others without holding to them, swing between rigid moralism and generous laissez-faire; like SEVENs, inclined to excessive instinctual satisfaction but seldom "cultivated"; enjoy food, alcohol, sex without guilt; guilt arises when they feel they were unjust or untruthful; the last at parties; "SEVENs enjoy in order to avoid pain. EIGHTs celebrate because that is part of the well-rounded, full life." [p.112]
- **[SYSTEM] Power and control.** Enjoy power, stake and expand their claim; want to be in the know; furious when deceived or outfoxed (advice for subordinates: admit a mistake at once); need to control everything, even trivia ("the first step toward everything getting out of control"); fantastically fussy; need control over possessions and others; want to draw the lines without dependency. [p.112-113]
- **[SYSTEM] Love.** Partnership requires adaptation, compromise, sacrifice; partners who subordinate themselves "have found only a seeming solution" because "the only people EIGHTs respect are the ones who put up resistance"; conflict between tender impulses and acquired hardness; need space alone; hunting, fishing, mountain climbing ("Marlboro men"; John Wayne "a classic EIGHT"); "I want to go to bed with you, but I don't want to be continually with you." [p.113]
- **[SYSTEM] Gift: passionate lovers of life.** "Passion" is the essence of EIGHTs, "in the double sense of the power of life and the readiness to suffer." **Fruit of the spirit: innocence**: the unprotected little child in the EIGHT that can trust; task: see the vulnerable child outside themselves and be good to the defenseless child in their own soul (bound up with anxiety, since it means seeing their own weakness); prerequisite: **sincerity** (they demand honesty of others and must demand it of themselves). EIGHTs prefer to see tenderness in others (a child, animal, soft gentle woman); **Obelix** as caricature ("hard shell, soft core"). [p.113]
- **[SYSTEM] Literary EIGHTs.** Kazantzakis (*The Greek Passion*, *Zorba the Greek*: chauvinistic "manliness" combined with joie de vivre and love of humanity; the struggle to "spiritualize" animal passions in *Francis of Assisi* and *The Last Temptation of Christ*). **Hemingway** as "the clearest literary shape to the energy of EIGHTs": strong, self-involved, combative, adventurous, brutal characters; his own tough-guy image (obscene curses from boyhood, despised tears and pain; the legend of 237 shrapnel pieces; Spain "the land of EIGHTs" and bullfighting, with the bull as symbolic animal; boxing, hunting, fishing, *The Old Man and the Sea*; absolute obedience on his yacht; feared rage and plotted revenge when his writing or masculinity were attacked; Capa: "angrier than God on a bad day"; generous to those in trouble through no fault of their own: "compassion and the ability to block punches"; four marriages; derangement, failed attempts, suicide June 1961 by rifle; Walter Nelson: he "made the last decision his own"). Kenneth Lynn's biography "disenchants the myth": inflated war heroics, "pathologically macho," confused sex role in childhood (dressed in girls' clothes), father's suicide, fear he was not "all man," challenged rivals to boxing. Margaret Frings Keyes: EIGHTs are ruled by inner voices "Don't be you." "Don't feel what you feel." [p.113-114] **[note: the authors present Lynn's analysis as conditional ("If Lynn's analysis is correct").]**

### Symbols and Examples (EIGHT) [p.114-]
- **Animals:** rhinoceros, rattlesnake, tiger, bull (aggressive; power, phallic energy, vitality). Bullfight as "one of the great life themes of EIGHTs": life-and-death duel, "Blood must flow; only one will survive." [p.114]
- **Country: Spain.** Authors tie it to "the disastrous machismo of Spanish-speaking countries": women know "behind the hard shell of their men there is a little boy"; the mask of strength hides insecurity and inferiority; destructive effects (children afraid of the father, wife oppressed, degraded, beaten; "The macho man has to be boss"). Spanish popular piety's bloodthirstiness in crucifixion images ("EIGHTs want to see blood"). Oppressed societies ("Don't be yourself") concentrate EIGHT energy until it erupts in revolution. [p.114]
- **Colors: black and white.** EIGHTs want clarity; reject intermediate tones and compromise as weak; either/or: friend or foe, good or bad, strong or weak. Black = nothingness, death, abyss, but also eros and primal chaos. White = blinding absolute light (Moses at the burning bush), totality of creative powers; in the Bible the color of innocence, the blessed, angels. "Whoever isn't for me is against me." The numeral 8 is two circles touching at one point, "the union of opposites." [p.114]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Biblical EIGHTs:** a series of female figures, plus Judges (Samson) and Kings (Saul and David). Authors note the Bible shows how women's historical role was "superimposed, whitewashed, and falsified," with traces of women who left a mark on history: **Miriam** (equal leader with Moses and Aaron; the oldest Exodus tradition, Exod. 15:21), **Deborah** ("a mother in Israel," Judg. 5:7), **Hannah** (1 Sam. 2:4; the Magnificat echoes it); "To protect the weak, they become combative and strong." [p.114-115]
  - **Samson:** prototype of the fabulously strong hero who "doesn't fit into the scheme of pious men of God"; always chasing women; uncut hair as consecration; tears a lion apart; kills thirty Philistines after a deceived wedding; snaps bowstrings; a thousand Philistines with the jawbone of an ass; carries off the city gates of Gaza; betrayed by Delilah, hair cut, blinded, imprisoned, pulls down the temple of Dagon with three thousand Philistines (Judg. 13-16). [p.115]
  - **Saul:** divine call, anointed by Samuel; when the Ammonites seize land "the spirit of God came mightily upon Saul," his anger is kindled, he cuts a yoke of oxen into pieces and sends them to all Israel (1 Sam. 11:6-7); first king; later melancholy and fits of madness; "with the instinct of the power-person" senses a rival in David and tries to spear him. [p.115]
  - **David:** guerrilla leader of dubious characters, vassal of the Philistines, absent at Gilboa; "Machiavellian skill and sophisticated use of political marriages"; reign 1000-965 B.C.E.; ark brought to Jerusalem; wanted a dynasty to replace the elective monarchy; family disasters (Amnon and Tamar, Absalom). The Uriah-Bathsheba episode shows EIGHTs like David "do not necessarily allow their feelings for justice to stand in the way of their self-interest." Nathan's parable of the poor man's ewe lamb: David rages ("the man who has done this deserves to die"); Nathan: "You are the man." Authors' point: **EIGHTs tend to become aggressive toward others who display a negative trait resembling the one the EIGHT unconsciously denies**; Nathan acts as David's true conscience. [p.115-116]

### Conversion and Maturation (EIGHT) [p.116-117]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Invitation: mercy.** "Unredeemed EIGHTs are merciless toward themselves and others." Only the encounter with truth frees them to see and accept their own weakness, and from that to bear others' weakness. [p.116]
- **[SYSTEM] Resistance to inner work.** Afraid of their "soft core," seldom ready for therapy, inner work or regular meditation; afraid of being checked up on or manipulated by a therapist. Counter-example: a well-known woman therapist and EIGHT: "My EIGHT-ness saw me and struck out at me. This work on myself is uncompromising and is bearing splendid fruit." [p.116]
- **[SYSTEM] Life tasks.** Confront the question of power (power is "not in itself bad: it can become a blessing or a curse"); do not degrade, humiliate or intimidate; respect other standpoints; not "narcotize their feelings with alcohol and wild celebrations"; look for compromises; obey the rules they expect of others; redeemed EIGHTs "can protect others with their power and vitality instead of dominating them." The world needs EIGHTs who "tear down the lying façades of institutions and societies," but they must allow room for their soft side, concede when wrong and ask forgiveness ("no weakness but real strength"). [p.116]
- **[SYSTEM] Gender notes.** Female EIGHTs have it especially hard (a self-confident woman is labeled "libber" or "masculine"; difficulties accepting femaleness, "maternal," "soft" images); "Just as FOURs are often identified with so-called feminine energy, EIGHTs are often identified with so-called masculine energy"; male EIGHTs need access to their female side instead of "delegating" warmth to women. [p.117]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Saint of redeemed EIGHTs: Martin Luther King Jr. (1929-68).** Learned from Gandhi "the power of powerlessness": "Nonviolence is power, but it is the right and good use of power!"; Montgomery bus boycott 1955-56; arrested more than thirty times; "proud of my crime"; FBI letter urging suicide; opposition to the Vietnam War cost him liberal white friends; suspected of communism like Day and Romero; assassinated 1968. Shadow: "a sensitive, soft side," lifelong self-doubts, twice attempted suicide as a young man, "deep and uncontrollable sexual impulsiveness" weighing on his marriage; authors: it is "comforting" that saints "remain imperfect and dependent on God's mercy." [p.117]

## TYPE NINE: The Need to Avoid [p.118-127]

### Overview (NINE) [p.118-119]
- **[SYSTEM] Gifts.** Peacemakers; accept others without prejudice so people feel understood; unbiased arbitrators who see positives on both sides; fairness; committed fighters for peace and justice; express harsh truths so calmly and matter-of-factly they are easy to "swallow"; others come to rest in their presence. In radical contrast NINEs often feel "inwardly lashed by fears and restlessness, even when no one notices." [p.118]
- **[SYSTEM] Position on the figure.** NINEs sit at the vertex of the Enneagram because "in a certain way NINE describes the original and unspoiled human essence." "We would probably all be NINEs, if we hadn't grown up in a technologically 'civilized' world." Rohr's inference from villages in Africa, the Philippines and elsewhere in the Third World; slaves from Africa, "by nature NINEs," dragged into "America's THREE society," where they are seen as "failures" (rat race, careerism are not their game). Quoted: *The Papalagi* (1920; fictional speeches of the South Sea chieftain Tuiavii on Westerners' time sense: "run through life like a thrown stone"). [p.118]
- **[SYSTEM] "Laziness" reinterpreted.** Civilized nations call the original attitude "laziness or indolence"; the authors read the lack of drive as "a kind of internal vagueness": NINEs have a hard time understanding their own nature; must first find out what they want; "everywhere and nowhere"; generalists, "master something of everything, but nothing totally"; lack focus and determination. Quoted self-description by a young German theologian NINE (an all-arounder: skiing, surfing, guitar, singing, dancing, preaching, listening very well, languages "to a degree," "only" a B on the exam; all "for domestic consumption"). [p.118-119]
- **[SYSTEM] Fading in and out.** Lack courage or do not consider themselves important enough to display talents; fade in and out unnoticed; take up others' topics without great passion; "NINEs like to swim with the current." [p.119]
- **[SYSTEM] Childhood roots.** Overlooked or "lost"; ignored or rejected for opinions; parents' or siblings' interests took precedence; even outbursts of rage not registered, so they kept anger to themselves. Others maneuvered between fronts to "understand" both sides (resembling TWOs: flair for others' needs, sensing them better than their own). Others experienced dull harmony without highs, lows or challenges, or were spoiled and comfortable early. School anecdote: slow in a mental-arithmetic contest, concluded "I'm a bad arithmetician"; "it must have taken enormous amounts of energy to avoid real work." [p.119]
- **[SYSTEM] Everyday impression.** "Easy to care for"; sometimes "blurry" as persons; prefer the path of least resistance; afraid of decisions that pin them down; put off important responsibilities; consider themselves simple and uncomplicated; **honest, no hidden motives**: say what they feel, even if they must sweat to discover it; some feel an inner compulsion to answer honestly and afterward get angry for having given themselves away to someone unworthy of trust. [p.119]

### Dilemma (NINE) [p.119-123]
- **[SYSTEM] Temptation: belittling themselves**, "especially in their own eyes"; apparent humility often conceals false modesty and fear of revealing themselves; self-image of "not being anything special"; can enter and leave a room unnoticed; dependent on being noticed ("Oh, you noticed me!"). Few prominent NINEs; example given: Gerald Ford ("Who was that?"). [p.119]
- **[SYSTEM] Defense mechanism: narcosis, or numbing.** Refuge in some addiction more than other types ("a little drink or... a little joint"); seek stimulants and strong sensations from outside because it is difficult to stimulate themselves; absent-minded or befuddled; fall asleep in broad daylight; sleep as retreat; but often insomnia at night. [p.119-120]
- **[SYSTEM] Withdrawal under stress.** Do not want to burden others and do not expect help; at deadlock they need outside help; "Love and attention are true wonder drugs in getting worn-out NINEs back on their feet." Life task: discover self-worth and inner drive, becoming independent of continual outside impulses. [p.120]
- **[SYSTEM, with CHRISTIAN tradition] Root sin: laziness.** Evagrius Ponticus and the Desert Fathers' **acedia, the "noonday demon"** (quoted at length: attacks at the fourth hour until the eighth; the sun seems to stand still; the monk looks out the window, aversion to the place, manual labor, and the idea that love has disappeared among the brothers; paints how long life lasts and the hardships of asceticism). [p.120]
- **[SYSTEM] Immature NINEs.** Comfortable, weak instinctual drives, "can make others see red"; problems with initiative, projects, perspectives, carrying jobs through; avoid tying themselves down or being tied down; **work with clear "contracts"** ("By April the 19th, twelve noon...": they will do it, "though not a day sooner"); if left much free space, "usually nothing will move ahead." [p.120]
- **[SYSTEM] Pitfall: lethargy and comfort.** "Why should I stand when I can sit? And why should I sit when I can lie down?"; the motto "Take it easy. Cool down. Relax." A NINE's retort to Rohr: "You're working yourself to death, Richard, and I'm not-and in the end it all boils down to the same thing." Rarely take the first step in friendship (six months' silence, then delight when contacted); NINE parents may seem not to care but are loving when children take initiative; self-deceit anecdote (buying a book titled *Hesitation* and knowing she would never read it). [p.120]
- **[SYSTEM] Secret cynicism and passive-aggression.** Rohr's NINE spiritual director: "we NINEs are at bottom great cynics about ourselves and about human nature. We believe that we're worth nothing, and that ultimately nothing is worth anything." The structure is "passive-aggressive": "We don't commit ourselves" conceals "You're not worth my driving myself crazy." NINEs (as gut persons) belong to Karen Horney's "hostile types" with a well-hidden deep distrust of life. Expressed as stubbornness ("Wild horses can't get them"). [p.121]
- **[SYSTEM] Conflict avoidance.** Indirect communication of displeasure: stubbornness, sleep, **sitting it out** (refusing to contribute to change, hoping it solves itself) or **retreating**; acting as if they noticed nothing and not complying with unspoken expectations ("the only form of 'dissimulation' to be expected from a NINE"); "Nonviolent resistance comes naturally to a NINE." [p.121]
- **[SYSTEM] How rage emerges.** Slowly: either others are provoked by their passivity until they explode, or the NINE's own explosion is "prepared for" by a long process: the other's position first seems plausible, then a testing phase shows disagreement, then internal ascertainment that anger is justified, then "a volcanic explosion of wrath" that astonishes people who thought the NINE easy and uncomplicated. [p.121]
- **[SYSTEM] Simplicity.** Do not load heads with ballast; like lucid, concrete books and lectures; find complex/abstract things boring; "seek simplicity because they are looking for their own simple center and are afraid there might be nothing there." [p.121]
- **[SYSTEM] "Children of paradise," no defenses.** Immature NINEs can avoid everything (life, the world, good, evil, themselves); they lack the defense mechanisms the other eight types use to protect their inner selves; almost everything from outside is exhausting and draining; spend energy avoiding or deadening conflicts and strong feelings; composed and calming outside, "seething and boiling inside"; moments of inner repose are "the most beautiful moments of their lives." (Compare: the authors earlier assign NINE the defense "narcosis"; the "no defense mechanisms" statement refers to the protective mechanisms of the others.) [p.121]
- **[SYSTEM] Partnership.** Torn between wishes for fusion (symbiosis) and autonomy; commitment slow ("years before all reservations have been dropped"); hard to let go ("If I can't live in this person and through this person-how am I to live at all?"); real love when they find their own center and can meet a partner without fusing. [p.121]
- **[SYSTEM] Anxiety about sex and aggression.** Immature NINEs keep them under control so they "largely drop away as motive forces," producing "an all-encompassing laziness"; many are highly gifted but do not actualize gifts. [p.121]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Parable of the pounds (Luke 19:11-26)** "probably told to NINEs." Usual reading: warning against wasting talents. **Alternative reading from Latin American liberation theology:** the nobleman gets rich by usury (a sin in the prophets' and the church's view until the high Middle Ages), so he is the "bad guy"; the last servant refuses the exploitative game and tells his lord to his face; Jesus is describing "capitalistic" laws of the world, not divine justice, and the closing maxim criticizes making the rich richer; "the passivity of the servant would be the silent but effective protest of an enlightened NINE." [p.121-122]
- **[CHRISTIAN/SYSTEM] Church types.** "If the Roman Catholic Church is a SIX system, liberal Protestantism in particular displays many features of NINE": avoids clear dogmatic statements, tries to please everybody, pluralism as an end in itself, "supermarket of possibilities"; but also a free space to be oneself. To avoid irrelevance it must keep reflecting on its Reformation roots (ONE) and keep alive the question of truth and justice (EIGHT): "These are the necessary wings of the NINE." [p.122]
- **[SYSTEM] Rabbi joke.** A rabbi hears a husband then a wife (each announcing divorce) and says "Yes, you're right" to each, and to his disciple's objection: "Yes, and you're right too." [p.122]
- **[SYSTEM] Gift / fruit of the spirit: the deed (decisive action).** At first NINEs waver and put off everything, but when they decide it happens "in a moment of absolute clarity" without doubt or revision: "no one will be able to stop them." Anecdote: a young man in New Jerusalem dodged for a year, then said "I talked to her. We're going to go out!", years later "We're getting married!"; "He will be true to her forever. He knows what he wants." (Rohr notes his own TWO-wing as active matchmaker.) [p.122-123]
- **[SYSTEM] Peacemakers and mediators.** Want a world of little conflict; do not believe in unbridgeable oppositions; having no clear standpoint they can shift to and accept any; "I can understand both sides and bring them under one umbrella"; "It really is hard to be truly aggressive when a NINE is there"; they may not feel they radiate peace. **Wings:** NINEs have "two energy-laden high-tension wings, EIGHT and ONE," so "NINEs are gentle prophets, but they are prophets"; effective because indirect; ONEs and EIGHTs frighten others while NINEs awaken trust and are allowed to "get away with things." [p.123]
- **Literary prototype: Goncharov's *Oblomov* (1859).** Landowner in Petersburg, passive, idle, irresolute; bed and dressing gown his world; more than two hundred pages before he gets out of bed; seven visitors (analogue of the seven deadly sins, each a worldly temptation) fail to motivate him; dream of lost paradise (Oblomovka); German friend Stolz ("pride") gets him into social life, but he finds emptiness; love for Olga awakens him as "a madman, possessed by passion" but she is too exhausting and demands that he "stay the course" while he asks "take me as I am"; settles with the motherly landlady Agafya ("the good one") and his life "ends one day silently, without pain and without fuss." Tatiana Goricheva: Oblomov has a "hidden and paradisiacal soul" and wants to "live from the inside out," without taking the step; his utopia is "realer than all the maxims of everyday life." Franz Xaver Kroetz's play leaves open whether "Oblomovism" might be a virtue. [p.123]

### Symbols and Examples (NINE) [p.124-125]
- **Animals:** elephant (gentle, not especially aggressive, "can be very unforgiving"); sloth; whales; dolphin (**whales and dolphins are animal patrons of the redeemed NINE**). Fuchs's poem "Sloth in the Zoo" ("Whoever would conquer me trips over my yawn... Oblomov petted my fur"). Whales (via Heathcote Williams's *Continent of the Whales*): three times as much time playing as searching for food; musical; highly complicated and evolving communication; ears twenty times more sensitive; large brains; maintain plankton ecology; reproduce only as nourishment allows; males woo with songs and stunts; mothers carry dead calves; "no whale has ever attacked a human"; "holocaust of the seas"; "a whale refuses to believe in an attack"; Eskimo saying "It does humans good to think about the whale." Lesson: intelligent creatures can survive "without extirpating one another and without destroying the environment"; "redeemed NINEs embody it more than all others." [p.124]
- **Country:** "any country at all before it was ravaged by civilization"; the often-used ethnic image is Mexico (sombrero, siesta; authors disclaim prejudice); in Europe, Austria (welcomed Hitler, then the Allies; political neutrality; Gemuetlichkeit and naturalness with secret passive aggressiveness toward foreigners). [p.124]
- **Color: gold.** Gods, kings, saints; Buddhist saffron robes as enlightenment; gold sought in the depths of the earth like NINEs' gifts; golden age and golden city as archetypes of peace, harmony, fulfillment. [p.124]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Biblical patron: Jonah, "the unwilling prophet."** Commanded to Nineveh, flees toward Tarshish; storm; asleep in the hold; thrown overboard; the great fish; preaches; the people repent; God spares the city; Jonah, displeased, again wants to die; the castor-oil plant and the worm; God's lesson of unconditional love (Jonah 4:10-11). Authors: "Jonah's laziness is really resignation and infatuation with death"; sleep and death seem to end all of life's conflicts; God challenges him to deeds of love. **Maslow's "Jonah syndrome"** quoted: fear of our own greatness, "fear of magnificence." [p.125]

### Conversion and Maturation (NINE) [p.125-127]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Invitation: (unconditional) love.** NINEs need the experience of being wanted, important, having something to give; need to learn others (God and fellow humans) believe in them so they can believe in themselves. Text statement: "Redeemed FIVEs can love unconditionally like no other type" **[note: the source says FIVEs here; context and the invitation to NINEs suggest NINEs was meant; flagged as an apparent slip in the source]**. Ambivalence of gift: unredeemed NINEs' acceptance is partly path of least resistance and may accept "completely unacceptable conduct" to avoid confrontation. [p.125]
- **[SYSTEM] Deciding.** "NINEs know better what they don't want than what they do want": decide by eliminating options that meet inner resistance, then try what survives. [p.125]
- **[SYSTEM] Life tasks.** Overcome secret cynicism; believe there is "a golden kernel" and an energy source for purposeful decisive action; as gut types they must "act boldly, enjoying the risk, in order to experience themselves" (pondering leads to resignation); need a point of orientation on which to focus all their power; happy when they distinguish essentials from nonessentials, set priorities and act consistently. [p.125-126]
- **[SYSTEM] Longing for wholeness.** The double-edged longing for harmony and rest includes bringing together resistant, complex, unresolved things and uniting opposites; the authors say **Carl Gustav Jung was a NINE** (integrating good and evil, masculine and feminine; the "collective unconscious" where opposites cancel out and all are "one"); NINEs feel connected with "the primal Ground of Being" and can help others find their way back. [p.126]
- **[SYSTEM] Practical advice.** Struggle to find their own standpoint instead of orienting to others; ordered structures and an invariable daily routine (stop energy being used up in planning and distractions); avoid passive fatalism, "roll into a defensive ball," or give up; finish projects; take "the closest, most obvious job in hand, and make the first step"; Helen Palmer: feel and express rage and aggression in imagination until blockages are loosed. [p.126]
- **[SYSTEM] Maturity.** "Mature NINEs may be the only ones to whom we could entrust the world with a good conscience," without fear of enrichment, exploitation or self-advantage; they are the last to work up ambition to climb to influential positions. [p.126]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Saint of redeemed NINEs: Pope John XXIII (1881-1963)**, "the pope nobody would have thought capable of anything." Angelo Roncalli: poor peasant family, frugality, supported striking metalworkers as bishop's secretary, no ecclesiastical ambition (would have liked to be a country pastor); ambassador to Bulgaria and Istanbul (continual mediation; motto "Let us have understanding for each other"); WWII: contributed to the rescue of two hundred thousand Jews through false baptismal certificates; nuncio to Paris 1944 ("de-Nazify" the church); patriarch of Venice at seventy-two (open doors, public transport); elected "transitional pope" 1958 (compromise, age seventy-seven) but completed the church's transition into the twentieth century; maxim "Simplify what is complicated, and don't complicate what's simple"; surprised the cardinals with the idea of a council, pursued "stubbornly and unwaveringly" (fresh air; dialogue with world religions and atheists; bishops' voice; laity's position); "the good pope"; mediated secretly between Khrushchev and Kennedy; *Pacem in terris*; sayings: "Patience and rest, these are two beautiful qualities. To be always busy and not to suffer from haste, this is a piece of heaven on earth" and "Angelo, don't take yourself so seriously!" [p.126-127]
- **[CHRISTIAN] Teresa of Avila** "probably a NINE (an EIGHT?)": shares John's self-irony ("a result of the fact that NINEs have little self-importance"); motto: "Let nothing disturb you, let nothing frighten you. God alone is enough." [p.127]

### Wings, arrows and related structure stated in this range
- **FOUR:** FIVE wing (introverted, depressive subtype) vs THREE wing (extroverted, success-oriented, hyperactive subtype) [p.77].
- **FIVE:** EIGHT is "the integration point of FIVE" (the energy of the deed) [p.89]; FIVE and SIX are neighbors, fear "no stranger to FIVEs" [p.90].
- **SEVEN:** has a SIX-wing (can be dogmatic, narrow, authoritarian) [p.107].
- **NINE:** wings EIGHT and ONE ("high-tension wings"; "necessary wings of the NINE": Reformation roots/ONE and the question of truth and justice/EIGHT) [p.122-123].
- **Centers named here:** heart types (TWO, THREE, FOUR share "peacock-like" self-presentation [p.68]); head types FIVE, SIX, SEVEN [p.83]; gut types EIGHT, NINE, ONE [p.109]. Fear (SIX) and happiness (SEVEN) are both said to be "produced in the head" [p.94, 101]. The Enneagram figure's vertex is NINE [p.118].
- **Type contrasts drawn by the authors:** ONE vs EIGHT (both aggressive; EIGHT does not apologize) [p.109]; contraphobic SIX vs EIGHT (head vs gut aggression) [p.109]; SEVEN vs EIGHT (enjoy to avoid pain vs enjoy as part of full life) [p.112]; THREE vs SIX ("notorious winners" vs "notorious losers") [p.93]; THREE vs SEVEN (refuse to see failure vs refuse to feel pain) [p.101]; TWO vs FIVE (give vs take) [p.83]; TWOs/SEVENs/NINEs prone to addiction [p.102]; FOUR vs FIVE (seek attention vs avoid it) [p.85].
- **Growth principle named:** *agere contra*, acting against the grain of natural compulsions [p.90].

## Gaps and cautions
- **Range boundaries.** The range begins mid-chapter in TYPE THREE (the chapter's opening, with THREE's overview, is in Part 1) and ends at the end of the NINE chapter on [p.127]; the page-128 Part III marker begins the next part. Nothing has been filled from memory.
- **Not extracted at length (by design):** long literary quotations (Platen's "Tristan," the Novalis Blue Flower passage, the Song of Solomon passage, Dostoyevsky's Grand Inquisitor, Tilmann Moser's anti-prayer, the Evagrius quotation, Mozart's letters, Maslow's "Jonah syndrome") are summarized in a line; the biblical stories of Jacob, Joseph, Samson, Saul, David, Jonah and the parable of the pounds are compressed but their Enneagram-relevant points are kept. Footnote numerals appear in the text but footnote bodies are not in this range.
- **Possible slip in the source:** [p.125] "Redeemed FIVEs can love unconditionally like no other type" in the NINE chapter; flagged above as probably meaning NINEs. Also the Joseph story is cited by the authors as "Gen. 36-50" [p.80].
- **Authors' strongly stated generalizations** (not endorsed here; recorded as the authors state them): national or ethnic "symbolic countries" for each type [p.68, 79, 88, 96, 105, 114, 124]; claims about racial or ethnic groups (e.g., the "black ghettoes," Africans as "by nature NINEs," the "white male American" as THREE prototype [p.68, 110, 118]); gender claims (e.g., "Men in Western society have hitherto felt very good with SIX energy" [p.98]; female EIGHTs' difficulties [p.117]); and the diagnostic labeling of historical figures (Reagan, John Paul II, Gurdjieff, Jung, Hemingway, Teresa of Avila and others) are the authors' attributions. Some passages (e.g., FOUR and anorexia [p.77], Samson/Hemingway analyses [p.114]) include psychological or medical-sounding claims stated without sources in this range.
- **OCR/text quality:** the text is clean; no passage in this range was garbled enough to require visual checking against the PDF. Minor: accented names are stripped or altered in places (e.g., Kuestenmacher, Dürrenmatt, Flössenburg), normalized here.
- **Terminology notes.** The authors use "root sin," "gift, or fruit of the spirit," "pitfall," "temptation," "invitation," and "life tasks"; the book labels the seven classical sins thus: THREE deceit (not a classical deadly sin; the authors note it was unrecognized), FOUR envy, FIVE avarice, SIX fear (also unrecognized), SEVEN intemperance (gluttony), EIGHT shamelessness (lust), NINE laziness (sloth/acedia). The TWO and ONE labels are in Part 1.
