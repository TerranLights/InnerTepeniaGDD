# Type Six — Subtypes

**Sources:** *Wisdom* (Riso & Hudson) cross-referenced with *Complete Enneagram* (Chestnut). See
`README.md` for provenance/distillation notes and the real-world example names for each Wing.

## The Wings

### 6w5 — The Defender

**Healthy:** excels at technical expertise — outstanding practical problem-solvers, analysts, teachers, or
opinion leaders, drawn to well-established systems of knowledge like mathematics, law, or the sciences. Greater
powers of concentration than 6w7, though sometimes narrower in scope; often takes up political causes or
community service, serving as a champion for disadvantaged individuals or groups.

**Average:** more independent and serious than 6w7, less likely to seek reassurance or advice from others —
often something of a loner. Finds reassurance in systems and beliefs while remaining skeptical of people; can
develop a genuinely dangerous-feeling view of the world, leading to partisan or reactionary positions.
Secretiveness feeds suspicion; sees itself as rebellious and antiauthoritarian while, ironically, remaining
drawn to systems and alliances with real authoritarian undertones — reactive and prone to blaming or
scapegoating whatever feels like the current threat.

### 6w7 — The Buddy

**Healthy:** engaging and genuinely funny, less serious than 6w5, tending to stick to concrete security-related
concerns (bills, taxes, office politics) rather than heavier topics. Takes commitments seriously despite the
lighter tone, making real sacrifices for the safety and well-being of family and friends; combines
interpersonal warmth with humor and a real zest for shared experience, including a capacity for self-deprecation
that turns fear into a bonding, joking connection with others.

**Average:** eager to be liked and accepted, but more hesitant than 6w5 to speak openly about personal problems.
Sociable but visibly insecure, leaning on loved ones for reassurance before big decisions; struggles with
procrastination and initiating projects independently. Prone to distraction and diversion (shopping, sports,
simply "hanging out") as anxiety-management, with real risk of overeating, drinking, or substance issues; not
especially political, but can be vocal and opinionated about personal likes and dislikes. Anxiety about
personal failings or key relationships can tip into depression.

## The Instinctual Subtypes

Both books note that the three Six subtypes diverge more sharply from each other than almost any other type's
— all three share the underlying Passion of Fear, but resolve it through genuinely different survival
strategies: alliance-building (Self-Preservation), rule/ideology-adherence (Social), and confrontational
strength (Sexual). Naranjo describes their respective "energetic temperatures" as warm, cool, and hot.

### Self-Preservation Six — "Responsibility" / "Warmth"

The most visibly phobic of the three, and the one who feels fear most consciously. Manages a deep sense of
insecurity by building friendly, mutual-protection alliances — offering warmth, reliability, and support with
the expectation (often unstated) that it will be reciprocated. Doesn't easily disguise anxiety or neediness, and
may in fact lean on visible vulnerability as a way of drawing in allies. Genuinely domestic, often the one
managing household security matters (bills, insurance, stability) directly. Carries a real taboo against
expressing anger or aggression — being "good," in this subtype's internal logic, specifically means not being
angry, since anger risks the very connections the whole strategy depends on. Externally warm and tender;
internally, often quite anxious, guilty, and doubt-ridden — a genuine split between the outward and inward
experience. At the unhealthy extreme, can become clingy, panicky, and paradoxically aggressive when its own
security systems feel threatened, sometimes destroying the very relationships it was trying so hard to protect.

**Distinguishing from Type Two:** both are warm, friendly, and invest heavily in relationships. The
underlying motive differs — this Six is building safety, not pursuing the pride-driven approval-seeking that
drives a genuine Two.

### Social Six — "Generating Support" / "Duty"

Copes with fear by relying on an impersonal authority — rules, ideology, or "the authority of reason itself" —
rather than a specific person. The name "Duty" points less at actually *doing* one's duty (though this
subtype often does) than at a preoccupation with *knowing what the duty is* in the first place — the party
line, the benchmark, who's right and who's wrong. Where the Self-Preservation Six stays mired in unresolved
ambivalence, the Social Six resolves the same underlying insecurity by becoming *too* certain — capable, at the
extreme, of becoming a true believer or fanatic. Cooler and more precise than the other two subtypes, drawn to
clear categories and intolerant of ambiguity; can be legalistic, controlling, self-critical, and formal, with
real difficulty accessing instinct or spontaneous feeling. Adheres strongly to a chosen system, sometimes past
the point the system itself still deserves the trust invested in it.

**Distinguishing from Type One:** both are controlled, critical, hardworking, and rule-adherent. A One is
guided by an internal standard held with genuine confidence; this Six's rule-following is driven by fear of
external consequence, not internal conviction.
**Distinguishing from Type Three:** both value precision and efficiency. This Six's underlying motivation is
managing anxiety through reliable reference points, not accomplishing goals in service of looking good.

### Sexual Six — "Symbols of Power and Connection" / "Strength/Beauty" (Countertype)

Flagged as the **Countertype** — the most counterphobic of the three, turning fear into its opposite by
adopting a stance of strength and intimidation rather than avoidance. Believes, in effect, that the best
defense against an unpredictable world is a credible offense — developing real physical strength, toughness, or
striking presence specifically to keep potential threats at a distance. Often cultivates this through visible
means (the gym, physical conditioning), less for health than for the feeling of protection strength provides.
The most openly defiant of authority among the three subtypes, and the most contrarian — instinctively arguing
whichever position isn't currently dominant, regardless of personal conviction. Despite the outward confidence,
this subtype remains driven by an underlying fear it rarely shows or consciously registers — the toughness is
real, but so is what it's covering. At the unhealthy extreme, can become erratic and depressive if its own
reactivity has damaged an important connection, alternating impulsive self-destruction with irrational lashing
out, sometimes with a focused, personal, almost obsessive edge to the paranoia.

**Distinguishing from Type Eight:** both can appear strong, intimidating, and powerful. An Eight is genuinely
fearless; this Six is driven by fear even when it doesn't show. Eights also tend to *create* order, where this
Six often stirs up disruption instead.
**Distinguishing from Type Three:** both are action-oriented, fast-moving, and assertive. This Six's
assertiveness is rooted in fear and carries a real undercurrent of paranoid fantasy, not the achievement-driven
need to look good that motivates a genuine Three.
