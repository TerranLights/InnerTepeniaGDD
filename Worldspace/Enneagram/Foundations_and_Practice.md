# Enneagram Foundations & Practice

**Source:** *The Wisdom of the Enneagram* — Don Richard Riso & Russ Hudson, Part I: "The Inward Journey,"
Chapters 1–4 (of 6; Chapters 5–6 are covered separately in `Triads_Wings_and_Variants.md`, since they're the
structurally load-bearing material — Triads/Centers, Hornevian/Harmonic Groups, Wings, Instincts, Levels).
**Status:** Distilled into original wording, not reproduced verbatim, per this project's standing book-mining
practice. Chapters 1–4 are oriented toward *why the system works and how to use it responsibly* rather than
new structural taxonomy — lower direct mechanical utility for the character-development methodology than
Chapters 5–6, but still real design-discipline material, most of it about how to avoid misusing type as a
character-writing tool.

## Chapter 1 — Identifying Your Personality Type

### What Type Is and Isn't For

The book's own framing: type is a starting point for self-knowledge, not a destination or a label to hide
behind. Two cautions from this chapter matter directly for how Dolls get written:

**The self-justification trap.** Riso & Hudson explicitly flag a real misuse pattern: a person leaning on their
own type to excuse behavior instead of examining it — "of course I get paranoid, I'm a Six," "you know how we
Sevens are, we have to stay on the go." Applied to Doll-writing: a Doll's Enneagram type explains *why* a
behavior pattern exists, it should never function as a permission slip that makes the behavior exempt from
consequences or growth. A Six's suspicion or a Seven's restlessness are things a companion questline can
work with and against — not settled facts the narrative treats as untouchable because "that's just her type."

**The "typing others" caution.** The authors are explicit that assigning type to someone else (rather than
diagnosing your own) is more failure-prone — blind spots and personal bias toward/against certain types both
distort the read. Directly relevant to this project's own practice of assigning Enneagram types to hundreds of
Dolls from the outside: a snap type read from a brief personality sketch deserves the same "confidence-tagged,
revisit if evidence surfaces" treatment `00b_Clarification_Protocol.md` already applies to other inferred Stage
1 fields in the InnerTepeniaGDD methodology, not a locked-in-forever assignment.

**Type is not the whole person.** Explicitly stated: knowing someone's type says a great deal about how they
see the world, what they value, how they react under stress — but nothing on its own about their specific
history, intelligence, talent, or integrity. A shared type between two Dolls is a shared *pattern*, not a
shared personality; the individuating detail has to come from everywhere else in a Doll's character data, not
from the type alone.

### Other Stated Ground Rules

- **Type is stable across a life.** People (and by extension Dolls) don't change from one base type to another
  as they grow — development happens *within* a type (moving up/down the Levels — Chapter 6), not between types.
- **Numbers are deliberately value-neutral.** The 1–9 numbering carries no built-in hierarchy; a Nine is not
  "better" than a One for being a higher number. Titles like "the Reformer" or "the Helper" are convenience
  labels, not rankings.
- **No type is objectively better or worse than another** — each has its own genuine strengths and genuine
  liabilities. (A given in-world culture or district might *value* one type's traits over another's — that's a
  cultural fact about the district, not a fact about the type itself.)
- **Everyone contains all nine types to some degree**; one is the "home base" a person returns to under
  pressure or in comfort. This is the same underlying logic the Integration/Disintegration charts in
  `Enneagram_Dynamics.md` already encode mechanically — this chapter just states the principle in prose.

### The Riso-Hudson QUEST — and Why It's a Useful Cross-Check, Not Just a Quiz

The QUEST is a two-question forced-choice sorter: pick one paragraph from Group I (three options) and one from
Group II (three options); the two-letter combination maps to a specific type via a fixed lookup table.

Read against the material already mined into this project (the Hornevian/Harmonic lookup table built for
`TepenianUniverseTimeline/Worldspace/Characters/seed-to-README-process.md`), **Group I is the Hornevian axis and
Group II is the Harmonic axis** — not a coincidental resemblance, the same three-way split, just phrased as
first-person self-description instead of clinical labels:

- Group I **A** = Assertive Hornevian (independent, meets life head-on, wants impact)
- Group I **B** = Withdrawn Hornevian (quiet, keeps to themselves, lives partly in imagination)
- Group I **C** = Compliant Hornevian (dutiful, self-sacrificing, responsible to others' expectations)
- Group II **X** = Positive Outlook Harmonic (stays upbeat, reframes problems, other-focused)
- Group II **Y** = Reactive Harmonic (strong feelings, wants others to visibly react too, guarded)
- Group II **Z** = Competency Harmonic (self-controlled, logical, uncomfortable foregrounding feelings)

Because every one of the 9 types sits at a *unique* intersection of one Hornevian group and one Harmonic group,
the two axes together are sufficient to fully determine type — which is exactly why this simple two-question
tool works at all. The code table below is that same 3×3 grid, and it independently **confirms** the lookup
table already in use (every cell matches):

| Code | Type | Hornevian | Harmonic |
|---|---|---|---|
| AX | 7 — The Enthusiast | Assertive | Positive Outlook |
| AY | 8 — The Challenger | Assertive | Reactive |
| AZ | 3 — The Achiever | Assertive | Competency |
| BX | 9 — The Peacemaker | Withdrawn | Positive Outlook |
| BY | 4 — The Individualist | Withdrawn | Reactive |
| BZ | 5 — The Investigator | Withdrawn | Competency |
| CX | 2 — The Helper | Compliant | Positive Outlook |
| CY | 6 — The Loyalist | Compliant | Reactive |
| CZ | 1 — The Reformer | Compliant | Competency |

**Practical use for this project:** given a short personality sketch for a Doll whose type isn't yet pinned
down, ask the same two questions the QUEST asks — which of the three Hornevian temperaments does she read as,
and which of the three Harmonic coping styles — and the table above narrows nine possibilities to exactly one
candidate type in two steps, before ever consulting the fuller per-type descriptions in Part II.

*(The book also describes a longer, 144-statement instrument — the RHETI — for a more precise self-scored
read. Not relevant to this project's needs: RHETI is a self-report tool for a live human test-taker, not
something applicable to assigning type to an already-written fictional character from the outside.)*

## Chapter 2 — Ancient Roots, Modern Insights

Mostly a provenance chapter — genuinely useful for understanding what kind of source this is (a modern
psychological synthesis wearing ancient clothing, not a literal unbroken oral tradition), but low direct
mechanical utility for Doll-writing except for one table.

**The symbol and the typology are two separate histories, later fused.** The nine-pointed Enneagram *symbol*
is genuinely ancient (Riso & Hudson trace its geometric/mathematical roots to Pythagoras, Plato, and the
Neoplatonists). The nine-*type* personality system is 20th-century: Gurdjieff brought the symbol itself out of
his own travels and taught it as a model of universal process (his "Law of Three" and "Law of Seven"), not as a
typology at all — nowhere in his own teaching did he link the symbol to nine personality types. Oscar Ichazo
made that connection in the 1950s, drawing on the Neoplatonic tradition of nine Divine attributes and their
Christian-tradition distortions (the Seven Deadly Sins, plus two more — fear and deceit). Claudio Naranjo
brought Ichazo's work to California in 1970 and began expanding the type descriptions using psychiatric panel
interviews — this is where Riso first encountered the material, through Jesuit retreat circles in North America.
Riso's own major original contribution, arrived at in 1977, was the **Levels of Development** (see Chapter 6);
Hudson joined in 1991 and co-developed the RHETI testing instrument.

**The Nine Passions.** Ichazo's link to the Seven Deadly Sins tradition (plus Fear and Deceit, completing the
set of nine) gives each type a named core "sin" — read not as literal wrongdoing but as the type's characteristic
way of "missing the mark":

| Type | Passion | What it actually means for that type |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Anger (more precisely: Resentment) | Repressed anger, producing chronic frustration and self/world dissatisfaction |
| 2 | Pride (also: Vainglory) | Inability to acknowledge their own suffering while "helping" — pride in their own virtue |
| 3 | Deceit (also: Vanity) | Self-deceiving belief that they *are* the ego-self; effort goes into the image, not the true self |
| 4 | Envy | The conviction that something essential is missing — fixation on what others have that they lack |
| 5 | Avarice | Belief that engaging with others will deplete already-scarce inner resources — hoarding contact and self |
| 6 | Fear (more precisely: Anxiety) | Chronic apprehension about things that aren't actually happening right now |
| 7 | Gluttony | Insatiable need to "fill up" on experience to escape a felt inner emptiness |
| 8 | Lust | Not sexual specifically — a constant need for intensity, control, and self-extension into the world |
| 9 | Sloth | Not laziness — an unwillingness to bring full vitality to bear and fully engage with life |

**The Locksmith parable.** A Sufi teaching story the book uses as its closing metaphor for the whole method: an
unjustly imprisoned locksmith's wife smuggles him a prayer rug; years into faithful, unwitting daily prayer on
it, he finally notices the rug's woven pattern *is* the shape of his own cell's lock. He can't escape alone or for
free, though — he has to first win his guards' trust and cooperation, forging them useful things from scrap
metal before he can forge the key. The stated moral: the lock is our personality, the rug is the Enneagram, the
key is the Work — and the pattern that frees us has been sitting in plain sight the entire time.

## Chapter 3 — Essence and Personality

The book's philosophical/spiritual scaffolding: personality is framed as a *temporary, necessary cast* laid over
a person's true "Essence" (their fundamental nature) in response to real childhood wounding — useful, even
protective, at the time it formed, but something a person can get stuck inside if it's never examined. Compressed
here since it's more about human spiritual practice than character mechanics, but three of its tables are
directly reusable Doll-writing tools.

**Basic Fear and Basic Desire.** The engine the authors say drives the whole personality structure: each type
flees a specific Basic Fear and single-mindedly pursues a specific Basic Desire in compensation. Fear and Desire
are typically mirror images of each other. This is the same underlying concept this project's own
`Weakness (fundamental flaw)` / `Goal (broad-scale desire)` seed fields are already reaching for — this table is
the canonical, type-linked version of exactly that pair:

| Type | Basic Fear | Basic Desire |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Being bad, corrupt, evil, or defective | To have integrity |
| 2 | Being unworthy of being loved | To be loved |
| 3 | Being worthless or without inherent value | To be (or feel) valuable |
| 4 | Being without identity or personal significance | To be oneself |
| 5 | Being useless, incapable, or incompetent | To be competent |
| 6 | Being without support or guidance | To be secure |
| 7 | Being deprived or trapped in pain | To be happy |
| 8 | Being harmed or controlled by others | To protect oneself |
| 9 | Loss of connection, of fragmentation | To be at peace |

Each Basic Desire has a stated "deterioration" — the self-defeating way the type tends to chase it: 1 → critical
perfectionism; 2 → needing to be needed; 3 → chasing after success; 4 → self-indulgence; 5 → useless
specialization; 6 → attachment to beliefs; 7 → frenetic escapism; 8 → constant fighting; 9 → stubborn
neglectfulness.

**Unconscious Childhood Messages and Lost Childhood Messages.** Two paired per-type tables — the message a
child of that type tended to internalize as a *limit*, and the message that child most needed to hear but usually
didn't get:

| Type | Unconscious limiting message received | Lost message never adequately heard |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "It's not okay to make mistakes." | "You are good." |
| 2 | "It's not okay to have your own needs." | "You are wanted." |
| 3 | "It's not okay to have your own feelings and identity." | "You are loved for yourself." |
| 4 | "It's not okay to be too functional or too happy." | "You are seen for who you are." |
| 5 | "It's not okay to be comfortable in the world." | "Your needs are not a problem." |
| 6 | "It's not okay to trust yourself." | "You are safe." |
| 7 | "It's not okay to depend on anyone for anything." | "You will be taken care of." |
| 8 | "It's not okay to be vulnerable or to trust anyone." | "You will not be betrayed." |
| 9 | "It's not okay to assert yourself." | "Your presence matters." |

**Practical use for this project:** the Lost Message column in particular is a ready-made line for exactly the
kind of "soft detail" delivery this project already favors (see `feedback_mystery_soft_detail_delivery.md`) — a
moment where a companion is finally, quietly given the thing she never got, without the scene ever having to
state the psychology directly.

*(The rest of the chapter — the Essence/personality/soul distinction, the "cast on a broken limb" metaphor for
why personality isn't something to simply discard — is spiritual-practice framing rather than a reusable
design tool, and isn't reproduced in table form here.)*

## Chapter 4 — Cultivating Awareness

The most practice-oriented, least directly game-mechanical chapter — its subject is a human reader's own
meditative self-observation ("catching yourself in the act," the "inner observer," presence). Condensed hard
here, except for three tables of real per-type content and one narrative idea worth flagging for character-arc
use.

**Core Identifications.** What each type powerfully identifies with in order to sustain a specific self-image, and
what it correspondingly resists recognizing in itself:

| Type | Identifies powerfully with | Resists recognizing | Self-image sustained |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The evaluating/comparing/discerning faculty (superego) | Anger-based tension | Reasonable, moderate, good, sensible, prudent, rational, objective, moral |
| 2 | Feelings for and about others | Own feelings about self and own needs | Loving, caring, selfless, thoughtful, warm-hearted, concerned, kind, compassionate |
| 3 | A self-image built from others' perceived admiration | Feelings of emptiness, own self-rejection | Admirable, desirable, attractive, outstanding, well-adjusted, effective, "unlimited potential" |
| 4 | Feelings of "otherness," being flawed | Authentic positive qualities in self, being like others | Sensitive, different, unique, self-aware, gentle, intuitive, quiet, deep, honest with self |
| 5 | Being a detached, outside observer of the world | Physical presence/state, feelings and needs | Perceptive, smart, curious, self-contained, insightful, unusual, alert, objective |
| 6 | Responding/reacting to inner anxiety about lack of support | Support and their own inner guidance | Reliable, dependable, trustworthy, likable, regular, careful, having foresight, questioning |
| 7 | Excitement from anticipating future positive experience | Personal pain and anxiety | Enthusiastic, free-spirited, spontaneous, cheerful, eager, outgoing, energetic, positive |
| 8 | Intensity from resisting/challenging others and environment | Own vulnerability, need for nurturing | Strong, assertive, direct, resourceful, action-oriented, tenacious, robust, independent |
| 9 | Inner stability from disengaging from intense impulses/feelings | Own strength and capacity | Peaceful, relaxed, steady, stable, gentle, natural, easygoing, friendly |

**Spiritual Jump Starts.** A one-line problem-pattern per type the book flags as the single highest-leverage thing
to bring awareness to:

- **1** — Value-judging, condemning self and others
- **2** — Giving your own value away to others
- **3** — Trying to be other than you authentically are
- **4** — Making negative comparisons
- **5** — Overinterpreting your own experience
- **6** — Becoming dependent on something outside yourself for support
- **7** — Anticipating what you're going to do next (instead of being where you are)
- **8** — Trying to force or control your life
- **9** — Resisting being affected by your own experiences

**The Invitations.** The book's positive-growth framing per type — what each type's healthy expression is
already naturally oriented toward, if it can trust that orientation:

| Type | Invitation |
|---|---|
| 1 | To live for a higher purpose — it's your true nature to be wise and discerning |
| 2 | To nurture yourself and others — it's your true nature to have goodwill and compassion, including for yourself |
| 3 | To develop yourself and set an example — it's your true nature to take pleasure in your own existence and to esteem others |
| 4 | To let go of the past and be renewed by your experiences — it's your true nature to be forgiving and to use everything for growth |
| 5 | To observe yourself and others without judgment or expectation — it's your true nature to be engaged with reality |
| 6 | To have faith in yourself and trust the goodness of life — it's your true nature to be courageous under any condition |
| 7 | To joyously celebrate existence and share your happiness — it's your true nature to add richness to others' experience |
| 8 | To stand up for yourself and speak out for what you believe — it's your true nature to affect the world positively |
| 9 | To bring peace and healing into your world — it's your true nature to be a font of serenity and acceptance |

**One idea worth flagging for character-arc use:** the book is explicit that moving in the Direction of
Integration (see Chapter 6) "requires conscious choice" — it isn't automatic the way disintegration under stress
is. That's a real structural difference worth remembering when writing a companion's late-arc growth beat: her
movement toward her integration point should read as a deliberate, effortful choice the player's presence makes
possible, not a passive drift that happens to her.

*(The remainder of the chapter — awareness/presence philosophy, the inner-observer concept, the bridge-collapse
anecdote about personality-driven fatal decisions, the three markers of being "awake" — is general spiritual
self-observation practice for a human reader working on themselves, not reusable Doll-writing material, and
isn't distilled further here.)*
