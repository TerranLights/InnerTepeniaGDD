# Cross-Phase Discipline — General Population, Not Narrow Context

**Added 2026-08-16, after a real, repeated mistake caught during the Scorpio pass.** This applies across all 8
phases, not just one — read it alongside `00_Index.md` before starting any phase for any district.

---

## The rule

Any Finding describing "this district's [Architecture / Sensory Profile / Fashion / Religious Landscape /
Ordinary Daily Life / Thematic Breadth / Robot-Specific Culture]" is describing the district's **general
population** by default. Before finalizing a Finding, ask explicitly: **does this describe what's broadly true
of the people who live here, or one specific role's, ritual's, or context's version of it?** If it's the latter,
it cannot silently stand in for the former — it has to be presented as an explicitly-scoped exception on top of
a genuine general answer, not as the answer itself.

**The developer's own diagnostic question, worth asking on every category that feels profession- or
context-adjacent:** *"Are we talking about [this specific narrow role], or the overall general people who live
in a place?"*

---

## Why this specific failure mode keeps happening

The Compilation Guide's own core good habit — formalize what's already established rather than inventing from
nothing — is correct and should stay the default first move. The actual failure isn't formalizing; it's failing
to check *what population share* the strongest available existing material actually describes before reusing
it. Existing material is very often written for a narrow original purpose (a rare armor set's combat context, a
specific ritual's specific participants, a specific job's specific uniform) — not as a deliberate statement
about the general population. Reusing narrow material as if it were general is the actual mistake.

**Confirmed real instances, not hypothetical:**
- **Scorpio's Fashion** originally used the transformation-phase mask system — worn only by residents currently
  mid-transformation — as the district's general fashion answer. Caught directly by the developer: "not a full
  district-wide 'mask culture'... stop repurposing everything into new packages."
- **Cancer's Fashion** originally used a hospital/caregiver-staff work vest as the general answer — but the
  Mega-Init's own population list includes elders, refugee families, and non-caregiving workers who'd never
  wear it.
- **Leo's Fashion** originally used performer stage-visibility dressing as the general answer — directly
  contradicted by that same district's own Ordinary Daily Life finding, which establishes most residents hold
  non-performing support work.

All three were caught the same way: the narrow answer contradicted something else already established about
who actually lives in the district.

---

## The actual process fix

1. **Identify what population share the strongest existing material describes.** If it's a subset — a
   profession, a ritual-specific state, an institution's own staff or members — it is not automatically the
   general answer, however rich or well-developed it is.
2. **Separately and explicitly answer what the rest of the population — the ordinary majority not in that
   specific role or context — actually does, wears, believes, or experiences.** This usually requires genuine
   new synthesis, not reuse. `Real-World_Basis_Extrapolation_Method.md`, applied at the concrete level, is the
   primary tool for producing this general-population material rather than defaulting to whatever narrow thing
   is already written down.
3. **When both a general answer and a narrow/specific-context answer exist, present both, explicitly labeled** —
   the general baseline first, then the narrow case clearly scoped ("specifically for X, not the district
   default"), the way the corrected Cancer and Leo Fashion Findings now do.
4. **Cross-check against the same district's own Ordinary Daily Life finding** (Phase 5) — it explicitly
   establishes who actually lives there and what they actually do day to day. If a Fashion, Sensory Profile, or
   Religious Landscape finding contradicts that population picture, that's the same error surfacing again.

---

## Which categories this applies to, and the real exceptions

**Applies directly — check every time:** Fashion (the worst offender so far), Sensory Profile's "first
impressions" sub-field, Religious/Philosophical Landscape, Thematic Breadth Catalog, Robot-Specific Culture,
Visitor Experience. Ordinary Daily Life is already structurally designed to avoid this (it explicitly asks for
what's true *around* the district's headline function), but stay alert to it defaulting to one narrow archetype
anyway.

**Structurally exempt, by the category's own definition — don't force this discipline where it doesn't
belong:** Export Culture answers "what specific thing does this district send outward," which is legitimately
about naming specific goods, trades, or institutions (a craft workshop, a trained specialist role) — that's the
correct shape of an Export Culture Finding, not an error. Arcanet Culture can legitimately center on a specific
institution's use of the network (Cancer's Mother's Circuit is genuinely caregiving-robot-specific) *as long as*
the Finding also states the district's own general information-culture baseline separately and doesn't let the
specific institution stand in for it — Cancer's own Arcanet Finding already does this correctly, and is the
model to follow.
