## 1. What this phase actually asks for

Direct quote of the governing instruction: go through each district and work out what an ordinary resident's
actual day-to-day life is like — **daily routines, mundane concerns, personal struggles, and forms of personal
escapism/downtime** — **distinct from** whatever that district's own defining civic identity or institutional
purpose is.

**The developer's own governing example, given twice, worth repeating in full here since it's the entire test
this phase's output has to pass:** Scorpio residents cannot plausibly spend every waking moment in a death
ritual confessing their grief; people have lives outside a district's headline function, and those ordinary
lives are currently underexplored across the corpus. People don't wake up in the morning and grieve for twelve
hours, go home, go to bed, and repeat — that's the failure mode this phase exists to prevent, for every district,
not just Scorpio.

This applies equally to **human and robot residents** — a robot's version of "ordinary daily life, distinct
from institutional purpose" is exactly what Phase 8 will need as raw material.

## 3. Sources to read, per district

1. **The district's own headline civic identity/institutional purpose, stated plainly** — from the Hard Facts
   "Zodiac / Role" row and the existing Full_Extrapolation Findings — specifically so you know what to write
   *around*, not toward. This phase's entire job is contrast with this, not restatement of it.
2. **`District_Canon_Reference.md`'s Community Infrastructure section** — the richest existing source for
   plausible daily-life anchors: small offices for educational training, social cohesion mechanisms, named
   physical Additions. A resident's ordinary day plausibly moves through several of these without any of them
   being the district's headline function.
3. **`District_Refugee_Diaspora_Composition.md`** — a district's population isn't monolithic; different
   feeder-population residents plausibly have different ordinary-day textures, worth reflecting rather than
   writing one universal "the resident" archetype.
4. **The color-coded district map** — physical scale is real daily-life texture. A resident of a small,
   Hub-adjacent district (Gemini, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Aquarius, Scorpio, Aries) plausibly has most of their daily
   needs within a short distance; a resident of a huge outer-ring district (Sagittarius, Capricorn) plausibly
   faces real commute/logistics texture as an ordinary, non-thematic daily concern — this is exactly the kind
   of mundane detail the phase is asking for, not a thematic finding.
5. **Any existing Deep_Dive material not already folded into Full_Extrapolation** — Deep Dives sometimes contain
   grounded, specific character-level detail (named or unnamed) that's closer to "a day in this person's life"
   than the more abstract Findings format, worth mining directly.

## 4. Process

**Step A — State the district's headline function in one sentence, then deliberately write away from it.** This
is the single most important discipline of this phase. If a draft finding could be summarized as "[district]
residents do [headline function] all day, just described in more detail," it has failed the test — restart from
what the resident does *between*, *despite*, or *entirely unrelated to* that function.

**Step B — Cover all four required elements, not just routines.** Routines, mundane concerns, personal
struggles, and escapism/downtime are four genuinely distinct things — a district's finding shouldn't collapse
into only routines (a schedule) or only downtime (a hobby list). Concrete prompts for each:
- **Routines:** what does a resident's ordinary day actually contain, hour to hour, that has nothing to do with
  the district's headline function?
- **Mundane concerns:** what do residents worry about that isn't the district's defining wound or purpose —
  ordinary things like rent-equivalent obligations, a difficult neighbor, a minor health complaint, a delayed
  Arcanet repair?
- **Personal struggles:** small-scale, individual-level difficulty, distinct from the district's own
  established large-scale historical wound — something a specific person deals with, not the district as a
  whole.
- **Escapism/downtime:** what do residents actually do to relax or disengage, and does it draw on anything
  already established (Glitch-Coolant, a specific hobby-adjacent Community Infrastructure Addition, a
  district-specific pastime) rather than a generic "they watch entertainment" placeholder?

**Step C — Differentiate human and robot residents' ordinary days where the district's own population supports
it.** Not every district needs a fully separate robot-daily-life treatment, but where the district's established
robot population is substantial (per its Mega_Init "Who Lives Here" section), give it real, distinct
consideration rather than assuming an identical day to the human population.

**Step D — Use physical scale (map) for commute/logistics texture where relevant**, per Source 4 above — this is
one of the more concrete, easy-to-ground mundane-concern types available.

## 7. Rules and pitfalls specific to this phase

- **The single biggest failure mode is restating the district's headline function in more detail and calling it
  "daily life."** Every finding should pass the developer's own Scorpio test: would this reasonably be
  someone's entire day, every day, forever? If yes, it's wrong.
- **Don't write one universal resident archetype.** Different feeder-population residents (Source 3) plausibly
  have different textures.
- **Don't skip escapism/downtime** — it's the element most likely to get cut for space, and it's explicitly
  required by the governing instruction.
- **This phase feeds directly into Phase 8.** Write with an eye toward "what would a robot's version of this
  actually look like," even when focusing on the human population, since Phase 8 will need to draw on this
  material directly.
