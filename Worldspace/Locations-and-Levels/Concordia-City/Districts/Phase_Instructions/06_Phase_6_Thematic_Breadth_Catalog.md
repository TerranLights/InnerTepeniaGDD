# Phase 6 — Thematic Breadth Catalog

> **⚠ Read `00_RUNBOOK.md` first.** It is the mandatory entry point for all district and location
> culture work (binding per the project `CLAUDE.md`), and it opens with **LAW 0 — depth over speed**,
> which governs everything in this file.

**Purpose:** a district is built around a **theme**, not a **thing** — Cancer isn't literally wall-to-wall
caregiving facilities any more than a real-world "government town" is wall-to-wall government buildings. This
phase systematically catalogs the wider cast of places, things, people, and settings a district needs to feel
like a real, lived-in place — refracted through its theme, not restating it literally. It's the direct,
systematic follow-through on what Phase 5's daily-life routines already implied but didn't itemize, and it's
what gives Phase 8 (Robot-Specific Culture) an actual populated world to embed itself in.

**Read `00b_General_Population_Discipline.md` first** — this phase's own founding premise (theme, not thing) is
the same principle as that file's rule, applied to places/things/settings instead of Fashion specifically: don't
let one narrow ritual-specific or professional item stand in for the district's general texture.

---

## 1. What this phase actually asks for — and what it doesn't need to invent from scratch

**Not a blank page.** Every district already has a **Community Infrastructure & Social Life** pass
(`District_Canon_Reference.md`, brainstormed 2026-07-29, organized under three headers: **Additions** — named
physical spaces/institutions; **Small offices for educational training** — short phrases naming locally-taught
skills; **Social cohesion mechanisms** — named recurring rituals/traditions). This phase **extends** that
existing material with what it doesn't cover:

- **Named individual places and landmarks**, not just institution-types (Community Infrastructure names
  "major hospitals" as a category; this phase would name a specific one, if one doesn't already exist elsewhere).
- **Physical objects** — things residents would own, use, or encounter, distinct from named spaces.
- **People, as role/archetype placeholders only** — see the binding naming rule below.
- **Settings** — atmospheric/situational textures distinct from named landmarks (a specific kind of street
  corner, a recurring situation, not a building with a name).

Check each target district's existing Community Infrastructure section first. If an idea belongs under
Additions/Small-offices/Social-cohesion and isn't there yet, that's arguably still Phase 6 material worth
adding, but the priority is the four categories above, which that existing pass doesn't cover.

---

## 2. Binding rule: no invented names for "people" entries

**Developer instruction, 2026-08-16, binding.** Every "people" entry in this phase gets a role/archetype
placeholder only — "a veteran repair-shop owner," "a recurring streetside vendor," "the neighborhood's informal
mediator" — **never an invented proper name.** The developer wants to name these figures personally once the
roles themselves exist. This is distinct from, but philosophically consistent with, the Compilation Guide's own
pre-existing convention of proposing Notable Figures as explicitly labeled placeholder/proposed rather than
presented as settled — the difference here is that Phase 6 people-entries stay unnamed permanently pending the
developer's own naming pass, not just provisionally unnamed pending review.

---

## 3. Prerequisites

Phases 1, 2, 3, and 5 should ideally be done first — Architecture and Sensory Profile (Phase 1) tell you what a
place would look/sound/smell like before you name it; Religious/Philosophical Landscape and Fashion (Phase 2)
and Arcanet Culture (Phase 3) give texture to what kind of places/things would plausibly exist; Ordinary Daily
Life (Phase 5) directly implies many of this phase's entries already (a resident's routine has to happen
somewhere, among some people, using some things) — this phase is the systematic follow-through on exactly that
implied material.

## 4. Sources to read, per district

1. **`District_Canon_Reference.md`**'s existing Community Infrastructure & Social Life section, in full — the
   direct foundation this phase extends. Re-read it specifically looking for what it *doesn't* cover (individual
   named landmarks, physical objects, people, atmospheric settings) rather than re-deriving what it already has.
2. **Phase 5's own new Ordinary Daily Life Finding(s)**, once written — mine directly for implied but unnamed
   places/things/people/settings.
3. **`District-Inspirational-Influences.md`** and **`Real-World_Basis_Extrapolation_Method.md`** — this phase's
   **primary derivation technique**. Web-research each district's own Primary/Secondary/Supporting real-world
   picks at the concrete level (named buildings, rituals, roles, tools, sub-spaces), not just their general
   category, and fuse specific details against established in-fiction fact. See that method file's own worked
   example (Cancer's Ospedale degli Innocenti "wheel of the innocents" and Epidaurus's abaton) for exactly how
   this should look in practice — both are ready-to-use Phase 6 candidates for Cancer, not yet written into its
   Full_Extrapolation.md.
4. **The color-coded district map** — adjacency matters for this phase specifically: a place or setting near a
   district's border with a specific neighbor plausibly shows some influence from or interaction with that
   neighbor, which is a legitimate source of texture Phase 6 can use that pure in-district reasoning wouldn't
   surface.
5. **All prior-phase Findings for the target district** (Phases 1-5, whichever are done) — the direct raw
   material this phase organizes and makes concrete.

## 5. Process

**Step A — Re-read the existing Community Infrastructure section and Phase 5's Ordinary Daily Life finding,
listing every implied-but-unnamed place/thing/person/setting before inventing anything new.** This is the
"formalize before inventing" discipline already proven in Phase 1 (Cancer's Architecture) — expect real,
recoverable material here for every district, not a blank start.

**Step B — Run the Real-World Basis Extrapolation Method against the district's full Inspirational-Influences
pick list**, prioritizing Secondary/Supporting-tier picks (per that method's own note, these tend to go uncited
once a Primary pick anchors the district, making them the most likely to still hold unused material). Produce a
list of concrete real-world details fused against the district's established character before drafting final
entries.

**Step C — Organize findings under the four target categories** (named places/landmarks; physical things;
people-as-placeholders; settings), not as one undifferentiated list — this mirrors the existing Community
Infrastructure section's own three-header discipline and keeps the result usable for later quest-design/level-
design passes the way that section already is.

**Step D — Name places and things specifically; leave people as role placeholders.** Per the Compilation Guide's
"naming forces specificity" principle for the former, and the binding rule above for the latter.

**Step E — Check the map for border-adjacency texture** where the target district shares a wedge boundary with
a neighbor whose own character might plausibly bleed across — flag this explicitly if used, since it's a real
but easy-to-overuse technique (don't force every district to have cross-border content if nothing genuinely
suggests it).

## 7. Output format and placement

Same convention: new Roman-numeral Finding(s) in `{Name}_Full_Extrapolation.md`, tagged `(District Culture
Development Plan, Phase 6)`, **Proposed:** framing. Given the four-category structure, a single Finding with
four clearly labeled subsections (matching the Community Infrastructure section's own three-header format,
extended to four) is likely the most usable and consistent shape across all 13 districts — prefer this over
scattering entries across many small Findings, for consistency with the existing Community Infrastructure
precedent this phase extends.

---

## 8. Rules and pitfalls specific to this phase

- **The no-names rule for people is binding, not a suggestion** — re-read §2 before writing any people-entries.
- **Don't re-derive what Community Infrastructure already covers.** Check first; extend, don't duplicate.
- **Don't skip the Real-World Basis Extrapolation Method.** This phase is exactly what that method was built
  for — skipping it and inventing from pure imagination wastes the district's own already-populated
  Inspirational-Influences file.
- **Don't force border-adjacency content** (Step E) where nothing genuinely suggests it — a real but
  easy-to-overuse technique.
