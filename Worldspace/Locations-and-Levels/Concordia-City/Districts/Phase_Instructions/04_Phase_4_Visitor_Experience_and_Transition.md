# Phase 4 — Visitor Experience / Visitor-to-Resident Transition

**Purpose:** close a confirmed-absent category with genuinely no district-level precedent — cities have this
because Tepenia has real outsiders (Upper Earth, or simply someone from a different city) arriving; districts
need an intra-Concordia translation of the same concept before any per-district content can be written.

---

## 1. What this category actually asks for, and its district translation

Template §27 (Visitor Experience): *"what it's like to arrive as an outsider; what you notice first; how the
city feels from the outside looking in; the sequence of impressions; what visitors tend to take away."*
Template §28 (Visitor-to-Resident Transition): *"how someone stops being a visitor and becomes a local; what
marks the shift; whether there is a ceremony or threshold; how long it typically takes; what it feels like when
it happens."*

**District translation, finalized here (was left as a one-time definition task in the Plan):** a district's
"visitor" is not an outsider to Tepenia — it's **a resident of a different Concordia district, or a
freshly-arrived refugee before diaspora placement has assigned them a home district.** Concordia doesn't have
Federation-style citizenship boundaries between its own districts, so "visitor" and "resident" status here is
social/experiential, not legal — what actually marks someone as belonging to a district is a mix of daily
presence, participation in its Community Infrastructure/Social Cohesion mechanisms (Phase 6 territory), and
plain time. Two distinct visitor types exist and should usually get separate treatment:

1. **A cross-district visitor** — someone who lives elsewhere in Concordia, arriving for a specific reason
   (work, a companion/relationship, an errand, curiosity). Their "outsider" status is mild and temporary; they
   already understand Concordia's own baseline norms, just not this specific district's.
2. **A pre-placement arrival** — someone freshly displaced, not yet assigned a district via the diaspora
   affinity process (`City_Refugee_District_Affinities.md`). Their outsider status is much deeper — they may be
   experiencing Concordia itself for the first time, not just this one district.

Write both where a district's own established character makes both plausible; some districts (the Hub
especially, see below) may be dominated by one type almost entirely.

## 3. Sources to read, per district

1. **`District_Refugee_Diaspora_Composition.md`**, target district's section — the closest existing precedent
   for "how someone arrives here," even though it's framed as population-level composition rather than
   individual experience. Mine it for texture, not just statistics.
2. **The color-coded district map** — genuinely central to this phase. A district's physical position (tiny
   Hub-adjacent wedge vs. huge outer-ring giant with a highway ramp) determines *how* most visitors actually
   arrive: someone entering Concordia through Sagittarius or Capricorn's highway ramp experiences those two
   districts' outer arc as their literal first contact with the entire city, regardless of which district
   they're ultimately headed to — a physically real "first impression" distinct from anything a Hub-adjacent
   interior district could offer. Reaching an inner-ring district requires physically transiting through
   middle/outer districts first, which is itself part of the transition experience for a pre-placement arrival.
3. **Phase 1's own Architecture/Sensory Profile Findings** (if done) — direct source material for "what you
   notice first."
4. **`District_Canon_Reference.md`**'s Community Infrastructure section — an existing named space (Cancer's Open
   Door Hour, Welcome Plaza-style customs elsewhere) may already function as a de facto visitor-integration
   mechanism without being labeled as one.
5. **The Hub's own material specifically** (`District_Megasheets/13_Hub/`) — per the Source Index, the Hub's
   diaspora is almost entirely "overflow," people who didn't fit their home district and drifted to the neutral
   crossroads by elimination. This makes the Hub's own Visitor Experience / Transition finding probably the
   single most load-bearing of all 13 — its whole identity runs through people arriving without a natural first
   destination, which is close to definitionally *about* this phase's subject matter.

## 4. Process

**Step A — Decide which visitor type(s) apply, per district, before writing anything.** Not every district
needs both. A small, socially tight-knit Hub-adjacent district might mostly see cross-district visitors; a
gateway district (Sagittarius, Capricorn) or the Hub itself might be dominated by pre-placement arrivals.

**Step B — Answer "what you notice first" using the district's own established Sensory Profile/Architecture**
(Phase 1) rather than inventing new sensory material — this phase should feel like a continuation of Phase 1,
viewed through a newcomer's eyes, not a separate sensory pass.

**Step C — Define the actual threshold, concretely.** Per the template's own prompt ("whether there is a
ceremony or threshold; how long it typically takes"): does the target district have (or should it have) a
specific, nameable marker — participation in a specific Community Infrastructure custom, a length-of-residence
convention, a recognized social signal — that marks the shift from visitor to resident? Cancer's own Open Door
Hour is a plausible candidate mechanism already sitting in its Community Infrastructure material; check for an
equivalent in the target district before inventing one from nothing.

**Step D — For pre-placement arrivals specifically, connect to the diaspora-affinity mechanism.** A district's
own established feeder-city composition (`District_Refugee_Diaspora_Composition.md`) implies what a
newly-arriving population's own first-contact experience would plausibly feel like, given what they're already
culturally primed to expect versus what they actually find.

## 6. Output format and placement

Same convention: new Roman-numeral Finding(s) in `{Name}_Full_Extrapolation.md`, tagged `(District Culture
Development Plan, Phase 4)`, **Proposed:** framing. Given this category covers two distinct sub-topics (Visitor
Experience, Visitor-to-Resident Transition), either combine them into one Finding with two clearly labeled parts
or write two adjacent Findings — whichever reads more naturally for that specific district; no fixed rule either
way, follow the shape the material itself suggests.

---

## 7. Rules and pitfalls specific to this phase

- **Don't default to a single generic "visitor" template repeated 13 times.** Step A's visitor-type decision
  exists specifically to prevent this — a district dominated by pre-placement arrivals should read very
  differently from one that mostly sees curious cross-district visitors.
- **Don't invent a legal/citizenship mechanism.** Concordia's districts don't have that kind of boundary; keep
  the visitor/resident distinction social and experiential, per the finalized translation in §1.
- **The Hub is not "just another district" for this phase** — treat its finding with the weight the Source Index
  and this file both flag it as carrying.
