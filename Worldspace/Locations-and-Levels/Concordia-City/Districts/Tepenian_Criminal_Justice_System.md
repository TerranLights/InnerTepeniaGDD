# Tepenian Criminal Justice System

**What this is:** a first-pass design for what happens to a convicted criminal in Concordia — a genuine gap
identified 2026-07-24 while discussing player-instigated violence (see
`Hostility/Player-Instigated_Violence_-_Understandable_Reasons.md`). No file anywhere previously answered
this; Libra's own Megasheet and Vision Notes mention judges, arbitrators, and treaty law, but nothing about
actual sentencing. **This is a working design to be addressed progressively, not a finished or fully decided
system** — several open questions are flagged at the bottom rather than resolved here.

**The core discovery that grounds this whole design:** both Sagittarius (`District_Canon_Reference.md`'s
Inhabitants section) and Pisces (same) already list **"exiles from stricter districts"** among their
established populations — an unexplained fact sitting in the canon before this file existed. Nobody had ever
said *why* those exiles ended up there or what they were exiled *for*. This system exists to answer that
question, not to invent a new mechanic disconnected from what's already written.

**The founding-condition parallel, worth stating explicitly:** Tepenia's entire existence began as an exile
sentence — the Falkland Treaty exiled robots and their human allies to Antarctica. A criminal justice system
built on internal exile isn't importing a real-world prison trope; it's the exact same mechanism the whole
nation was founded on, applied one level down, inside the nation it produced.

## Two-Tier Severity Model

### Tier 1 — Petty / Culturally-Offensive Crimes → District Exile → Self-Relocation

**The likely overlap, worth noting directly:** this tier's actual content is probably drawn straight from
`Hostility/District_Hostile_Actions.md`'s own per-district trigger lists — those describe the *informal,
immediate-violence* response when a local catches someone in the act. Tier 1 of this system is what happens
instead when the offender is caught, survives, and faces institutional judgment rather than a mob: formal
exile from their home district specifically, not from Concordia as a whole.

Having no standing anywhere else, a district-exiled offender **self-relocates** to whichever of the two
existing overflow destinations actually fits them:
- **Pisces** — matches its own established ethos exactly: "no interest in where you came from... significant
  interest in what you need and what you're willing to trade for it." Takes anyone.
- **Sagittarius (the Frostlands)** — matches its own established self-reliance/frontier ethos: a place that
  measures you by what you can actually do out in the ice, not by where you came from or what you did there.

**The characterful part:** which of the two a specific offender chooses says something real about them —
dissolution/underworld vs. frontier self-reliance — a natural companion-origin hook (a recruitable
character's backstory could be exactly this: exiled from District X for a Tier 1 offense, chose Pisces or
Sagittarius, and that choice defines who they became afterward).

**Not every Pisces or Sagittarius resident is an exile, confirmed 2026-07-24 via a concrete example:** Heather
Wendell (`Characters/Dolls/.../Heather Wendell/README.md`) lives in Sagittarius by genuine choice — drawn
there on a near-spiritual level upon arrival, not sentenced there. "Exiles from stricter districts" describes
*part* of these two districts' populations, established directly in `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s own
Inhabitants sections — it was never meant to describe every resident, and this system shouldn't be read as
retroactively criminalizing anyone already living there.

### Tier 2 — Serious Crimes → Supervised Dangerous Labor Conscription

Established by the developer directly, 2026-07-24: more serious crimes are sentenced to **heavily-supervised,
closely-surveilled dangerous work in Virgo or Aries** — specifically the kind of work that *native,
non-criminal Virgoan/Arian workers themselves* would consider extremely dangerous, not merely ordinary duty.
Native Virgoan/Arian supervisors keep close, careful watch over conscripted offenders throughout.

**Why exile doesn't fit here, and labor conscription does:** exile is closer to "let them go, no longer our
problem" — appropriate for a lower-severity, non-violent offense. A genuinely dangerous offender can't simply
be released into the Frostlands or Pisces to fend for themselves; that solves nothing and controls nothing.
Supervised, surveilled labor keeps a serious offender under direct, ongoing institutional control, which is
what a serious crime actually calls for.

**Why Virgo and Aries specifically:** both districts are already built around the idea that essential,
life-risking labor is a source of civic virtue and identity (Aries' "running hot" status culture; Virgo's
tireless, underappreciated toil). Assigning convicted offenders to the *most* dangerous version of this work
— beyond what even native workers take on voluntarily — uses existing district identity rather than
inventing a disconnected penal-labor system from scratch.

**Open dramatic tension, not resolved here:** native Virgo/Aries workers take real pride in choosing their own
risk voluntarily (Aries especially — refusing a hot shift is a real social statement). Criminal conscripts
being assigned that same risk *without having earned it the district's own way* could create genuine friction
with native workers — or, alternatively, could become a redemption arc, where a conscript who survives and
performs well earns something closer to real respect over time. Worth developing as an actual narrative
thread rather than deciding the tone unilaterally here.

### Tier 3 — Truly Abhorrent Crimes → Forcibly "Volunteered" as Aquarius Test Subjects

Established by the developer directly, 2026-07-24: the truly, truly abhorrent — utterly unfathomable,
horrific crimes — result in the offender being forcibly "volunteered" as a test subject in Aquarius.
**Applies equally to human and robot offenders** — this tier is defined entirely by the severity of the
crime, not by the offender's nature. It is not a robot-specific consequence, and shouldn't be read as one.

**Why this fits without needing to be invented from nothing:** Aquarius already has a real, established
ethical grey zone around consent and experimentation — ethical review boards that are "real institutions that
everyone participates in, takes seriously, and routinely routes around," a district that genuinely believes
it's helping and is "not malicious... something potentially more dangerous: certain," and residents who are
already "walking proof-of-concept demonstrations of ideas that are five years from widespread application, or
that will never be approved at all." A forced-test-subject pipeline for the worst offenders slots into
existing district identity rather than requiring new invented infrastructure.

**The tonal note worth flagging, not resolving:** Tier 3 is the point where the system stops pretending to
honor consent at all — a sharp contrast with the deliberate care taken in the Robot Personhood Complication
below, where even Tier 2 robot offenders get a real (if fraught) choice. Whether Tepenian society itself feels
any conflict about that contradiction — a system that prides itself on Libra's "fairness" and takes robot
personhood seriously enough to litigate the 2318 Jeju-do ruling, yet abandons that principle entirely at the
severest tier — is worth developing as actual thematic content rather than glossing over.

## The Robot Personhood Complication

Robots have held legal personhood since the 2318 Jeju-do ruling (`project_long_night_war_inciting_incident`
and related canon) — which means Scorpio's existing rebirth/reconfiguration technology (already established,
used post-war on trauma and identity-fracture cases) cannot simply be used *on* a convicted robot offender
without consent. That would be an actual rights violation the game's own themes wouldn't let pass quietly.

**The more interesting framing:** present reconfiguration as a genuine, live in-world controversy rather than
a clean third sentencing option. It could be offered as a *choice* — reconfiguration instead of exile or
labor conscription — with a real robot-rights argument (echoing Capricorn's own suppressed robot-rights
undercurrent) that "choose this or lose everything" was never real consent to begin with. Neither side of that
argument needs to be declared correct; the tension itself is the content.

## Why There's No Clean, Codified System — Libra's Own Murkiness

Libra's Suspended Compact (`District_Canon_Reference.md`) already establishes that the district has operated
under permanent, never-formally-rescinded emergency powers since the Long Night War — "technically the
emergency provisions are still active... technically this is within the law." This gives a clean, in-fiction,
already-established reason Tepenia doesn't have a clean written penal code at all: serious cases are handled
individually "under emergency provisions," which is exactly the kind of quietly unsettling non-answer Libra's
established character already produces elsewhere.

## Open Questions — To Be Addressed Progressively

- Where exactly the Tier 2/Tier 3 line falls — what specifically qualifies as "truly abhorrent" versus merely
  "serious," and who draws that line (Libra? a specific court? case-by-case under the Suspended Compact's own
  emergency-provisions logic, consistent with the rest of this system).
- Whether Tepenian society itself feels any real tension about Tier 3's abandonment of consent, given the
  same system's genuine care about robot personhood elsewhere (the Robot Personhood Complication) — worth
  developing as actual thematic content, not smoothed over.
- Whether Tier 1's specific offense list should be drawn directly and systematically from
  `District_Hostile_Actions.md`'s per-district triggers, or developed as its own separate list.
- The actual tone of the Virgo/Aries conscript-labor tension (resentment vs. redemption vs. both,
  situationally) — flagged above, not resolved.
- Whether reconfiguration-as-choice ever actually gets exercised in game content, or stays background lore
  texture establishing that the controversy exists.
- Whether any of this ever surfaces as actual playable content (a Tier 1 companion origin, a Tier 2
  conscript-labor questline in Virgo or Aries) or stays worldbuilding depth without a direct playable hook.
