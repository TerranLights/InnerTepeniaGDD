# District Refugee Diaspora Composition & Cultural Transplants

**Written 2026-07-31.** Companion piece to `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s own "Community Infrastructure & Social Life" sections (Additions / Small offices for educational training / Social cohesion mechanisms, brainstormed 2026-07-29) and to `Worldspace/Locations-and-Levels/Outside-World/Tepenian-Federation/Locations/Cities/City_Refugee_District_Affinities.md` (the source for which of Tepenia's 35 outer cities send refugees to which Concordia districts, top-3 ranked). This file goes one layer deeper: for each of Concordia's 13 districts, which outer cities its diaspora population actually traces to, roughly how large each contributing community is relative to the others, and — for the significant contributors — **specific cultural items that population would plausibly have "brought with them"**: concrete things drawn from each source city's own established Additions/Social-cohesion-mechanisms/Small-offices lists that a displaced community would recreate or adapt in its new district home, now that the original physical setting is gone.

## Methodology

**Weighting.** For each city, its top-3 district picks (per `City_Refugee_District_Affinities.md`'s Master Table) are weighted 1st place = 3, 2nd place = 2, 3rd place = 1, multiplied against that city's own Census I combined population (`Official_Population_Census.md` Section II — used because, unlike Census II, it covers all 35 outer cities including Kunlun, Dome Fuji, Byrd, and Vostok). The four Stage 2 Overrides (Scorpio, for the destroyed/ruined cities of Zukelli, Belgrano, Palmer City, and Casey; Gemini, for Zukelli specifically) are weighted 2 each, additive on top of each affected city's own separate Stage-1 top-3 — not a replacement or a renormalization of it. **These weighted scores are relative-ranking figures, not a literal population count** — nothing in this project establishes the exact percentage split of a city's refugees across its own top-3 picks, so treat the percentages below as "roughly this much bigger/smaller than that other contributor," not as census-grade precision.

**Selecting transplant items.** Only cities clearing roughly 3–4% of a district's total weighted score get a dedicated entry; smaller communities exist in every district but are too marginal for individual treatment here. For each significant contributor, items are drawn directly from that city's own Community Infrastructure file, filtered for genuine portability — a specific festival, craft practice, culinary tradition, or named social ritual can travel with displaced people; a rookery, a glacier, a specific highway junction, or anything else inseparable from local geography cannot. Each item is written to show how it lands and adapts inside its new district's own already-established character, not as a clean copy-paste — some transplants are a near-perfect fit (the source city's values already resembled the district's own), others are visibly adapted, softened, or made to serve a different purpose once they arrive somewhere without a dominant culture already primed to receive them.

**Two special cases.** Scorpio's four largest contributing cities (Casey, Zukelli, Belgrano, Palmer City) are there via the Stage 2 Override, not ordinary cultural affinity — the district's entry below treats them accordingly, as an account of how each community's own specific character of loss is shaping Scorpio's institutions from the inside, not a festival-and-craft list. The Hub's diaspora is, per its own established lore, almost entirely "overflow" — people who didn't fit their home district and drifted to the neutral crossroads by elimination — except for Sayowa, the one outer city whose own pre-war culture *was* the crossroads; the Hub's entry below treats Sayowa's transplants as a natural, confident fit and the other three as adapted-to-neutrality imports.

## District 1 — Cancer (The Sanctuary)

Cancer's outer-city diaspora is dominated by a cluster of caregiving-, hospitality-, and memory-centered cities: roughly a quarter traces to **Esperanza**, close behind it **Mawson** at just over a fifth (rolling in the small, undedicated Palmer City/Port Lockroy/Dome Fuji tail below), **Zukelli** at around one-sixth, **Cape Adare** around 13%, **Lazar** at roughly 11%, **Abowasa** at 9%, and a smaller but still meaningful thread from **Scott** at just over 3%. Smaller communities from Palmer City, Port Lockroy, and Dome Fuji exist within the district too, but at well under 2% each they're too marginal for dedicated treatment here.

### Esperanza (~24.6%)
Esperanza is destroyed, and its diaspora carries two things that land almost frictionlessly in a district built entirely around caregiving and grief. The **Caregiver-Teacher Halls** — Esperanza's long-lived robot caregivers and teachers who raised generations of the city's children, the actual durable carriers of "being from Hope" identity — fold naturally into Cancer's existing **Paired Creches**. Esperanza-descended caregiving robots, already practiced at holding a dislocated population's continuity when human record-keeping fails, become sought-after teaching presences within Cancer's neighborhood nurseries, no reinvention required. Separately, the ongoing practice of diaspora members asking whether specific family names survive in the fragments of the **Birth Registry Archive** merges into Cancer's **open-air memorial circles**: Esperanza-descended residents use the circles' voice-note/token ritual specifically to search for lost family names, giving an existing Cancer mechanism a distinct, recognizable Esperanza inflection.

### Mawson (~22.0%)
Mawson's identity is built on hospitality — specifically the honeymoon-destination culture around a mountain-overlook walk undertaken by newlywed couples regardless of season. The geography obviously doesn't travel, but the ritual shape of it does: Mawson-descended households recast it as a "First Walk," a newlywed or newly-reunited-family procession through Cancer's **quiet "listening gardens,"** folded into the district's existing **Open Door Hour** custom of signaling welcome. It reads as a graft rather than an invention — Cancer already prizes gentle, curved, non-institutional spaces for exactly this kind of tender threshold moment, so Mawson's arriving families found a home for the custom almost by accident.

### Zukelli (~16.5%)
Zukelli is destroyed, and its diaspora's grief is sharper than most — many watched the city's destruction happen from across the water in Janbogo before eventually settling further into Cancer. Zukelli's own famous restaurant-and-performance-space model, cited as the city's model for its whole food-and-music-together hospitality tradition, resurfaces inside Cancer's **Broth Nights**: a recurring venue-night that blends the potluck format with live, informal music performance, functioning less as pure entertainment and more as a running memorial to a lost restaurant culture — squarely in keeping with Cancer's stated belief that "the dead are maintained," extended here to a dead city's civic life rather than an individual. The broader idea of **communal meals as the absolute center of civic life** reinforces Cancer's existing communal broth kitchens directly, with a recognizable "Zukelli table" becoming a known fixture within them.

### Cape Adare (~13.1%)
Cape Adare is destroyed too, but unlike Zukelli its core cultural export is documentary rather than culinary: the **Heritage Documentation Archive**, where centuries of preservation work on Borchgrevink's Hut survived even though the hut itself did not. That instinct — that a structure can be lost while its record endures — slots directly into Cancer's **Second Chair Workshops** and open-air memorial circles, with Cape Adare-descended residents specifically known for treating family and city history documentation with the same rigor their ancestors gave the hut. Separately, Cape Adare's arrival/departure meal rituals — a gateway city's own version of hospitality, marking comings and goings rather than routine sustenance — invert neatly once the diaspora reaches Cancer: having once staffed the entry point *to* Antarctica, they now staff a version of the entry point *into* a new home, folding the custom into Cancer's **Open Door Hour**.

### Lazar (~11.4%)
Lazar's defining civic story is **Coalescence Day**, commemorating the historical merger of two founding settlements into one city. That "many becoming one" narrative maps directly onto Cancer's own identity as an integration zone built to absorb disparate, traumatized populations into a single home — Lazar-descended households treat the day as a template rather than a foreign import, quietly attaching it to Cancer's **Habit-Keeping Evenings** as an annual note on how blended households came to be blended in the first place.

### Abowasa (~9.0%)
Abowasa's **Household Suppers** — the central social mechanism carrying the community's own daily give-and-take between two originally separate founding programs — merge into Cancer's communal broth kitchens without much adaptation needed. The transplant is smooth because the underlying logic is identical: shared food is how two formerly separate groups become one group, which is exactly what Cancer's entire district exists to accomplish for its refugee population.

### Scott (~3.4%)
Scott's contribution is small but strikingly on-the-nose: the Hut Point Remembrance, where residents of Scott and Fort McMurdo gather, unmandated, to lay candles and personal tokens in memory of St. Robert. Scott-descended residents arriving in Cancer recognize the district's own open-air memorial circles almost immediately as the same kind of ritual, and layer their St. Robert observance onto the existing practice as a specific, minor annual addition rather than a separate institution.

## District 2 — Taurus

Taurus draws the largest and most varied outer-diaspora among the districts covered by this file. **Lazar** alone accounts for roughly a quarter of it, with **Cape Adare** (~15%) and **Mirny** (~13.5%) close behind, followed by **Abowasa** (~10%) and **Mawson** (~10%), then a second tier of **Davis** (~8%), **Zhongshan** (~6%), **Zukelli** (~4%), **Scott** (~4%), and **Sanay** (~3%). Port Lockroy and Signy contribute smaller threads below the 3% cutoff and aren't treated individually.

### Lazar (~26.3%)
Two Lazar institutions transplant well here, both tied to continuity and record-keeping — Taurus's specialty. Coalescence Day, the merger-of-two-settlements observance, becomes a natural addition to Taurus's own **Genealogy Registry Halls**: Lazar-descended families with roots in both the Old Core and the Overflow District add their merger story as a documented household chapter, the same institutional impulse Taurus already applies to robot service lines. The Unbroken Watch, Lazar's observance of its own continuous operational record, merges into Taurus's **Anniversary of Service Ceremonies** — Lazar households extend the ceremony's existing robot-activation framework to cover household and neighborhood continuity milestones as well.

### Cape Adare (~15.0%)
Cape Adare's Heritage Documentation Archive — the instinct to formally preserve an object's or a structure's full history even after the structure is gone — reinforces Taurus's own **Heirloom Provenance Offices** almost exactly, giving that office a recognizable Cape Adare-descended specialty in exhaustive documentation. Separately, Cape Adare's acoustic folk music gatherings, unhurried, unamplified, and "belonging to everyone equally," fold into Taurus's **Quiet Hour Storytelling** — both traditions are built around restrained, intimate performance inside a culture with real noise limits, so the fit needs almost no adjustment.

### Mirny (~13.5%)
Mirny's Dual-Table Suppers — Russian and Chinese dishes served side by side, deliberately kept distinct rather than fused, per Mirny's own "coexistence, not fusion" ethos — land inside Taurus's **Newcomer Welcome Kitchens**, where longtime residents already teach new arrivals food tradition. Mirny-descended households become known within that institution specifically for maintaining two visibly separate culinary lines under one roof, rather than blending into Taurus's usual single-tradition household cooking — a distinguishable variant within an existing structure rather than a wholesale replacement. More loosely, the reality-tested rigor of Mirny's windwright apprentice trials — work judged against the actual wind, not a panel's opinion — echoes in Taurus's **Household Insulation Ateliers**, where Mirny-descended instructors apply a similarly exacting, judged-against-outcome standard to insulation apprenticeship.

### Abowasa (~10.4%)
Abowasa's Joint Founding observance sits comfortably alongside Lazar's Coalescence Day inside Taurus's Genealogy Registry Halls, reinforcing the Halls' function as a place where blended-origin households of several different kinds get formally recorded rather than treated as anomalies. Its Household Suppers tradition folds into Taurus's own **Long Stew Rotation**, with Abowasa-descended households contributing pots that carry the same "originally separate, now inseparable" story their ancestors told about the city's two founding programs.

### Mawson (~9.7%)
Mawson's honeymoon-walk custom finds an unusually apt structural home in Taurus specifically because Taurus's earliest architecture was deliberately engineered for bonded human-robot pairs who chose to remain on the surface, with layouts prioritizing family units and private courtyards. Mawson-descended households transplant the walk as a "First Walk" through those same private courtyards for newly bonded pairs settling into a Taurus household — a rare case where the destination district's own founding design already anticipated the exact need the transplant fills.

### Davis (~7.7%)
Davis is the one clear friction case in Taurus. Its Quiet Credit — an informal practice of pointedly acknowledging team members' uncredited contributions, part of Davis's triple-reinforced "enabler, not protagonist" identity — sits awkwardly against Taurus's more overtly proud heirloom-display culture (Heirloom Show-and-Tell Evenings exist specifically to showcase, not downplay). Davis-descended households still push the practice into Taurus's Genealogy Registry Halls, formally noting which family member or household robot quietly kept things running, but it reads as a deliberate, slightly foreign correction to Taurus's habits rather than a natural fit — exactly the kind of effortful transplant a less naturally hospitable cultural landing produces.

### Zhongshan (~6.2%, rolling in the small, undedicated Port Lockroy/Signy tail below)
Zhongshan's Dumpling Arguments — "a low-stakes outlet in a culture that otherwise doesn't argue in public" — merge easily into Taurus's already-established, self-consciously superior cuisine culture, giving households a specific, named venue for culinary pride disputes that Taurus's stubborn self-regard was already primed to enjoy. The Mending Houses garment-repair tradition folds directly into Taurus's existing heirloom-repair co-ops as a companion craft: garment lineage alongside furniture lineage.

### Zukelli (~4.2%)
Zukelli's pre-war "Crossing" tradition — informal visits with Janbogo that blurred who was hosting whom — maps almost exactly onto Taurus's own **Unannounced Visit Circuits**, where households take informal turns being "the stop" for a given week's drop-ins. Zukelli-descended residents graft their version on with minimal adjustment; the underlying social logic (mutual, informal, host-role-blurring visiting) is already identical.

### Scott (~3.9%)
Scott's contribution here is narrower and craft-based rather than ritual-based: its precision equipment calibration and maintenance training folds into Taurus's own structural-maintenance education for multi-generational houses, with Scott-descended residents known for bringing an unusually exacting, calibration-grade standard to ordinary household upkeep — a small but recognizable specialty rather than a new institution.

### Sanay (~3.1%)
Sanay's own quiet, deliberately understated observance of continued function after the Long Night War introduces a quiet counter-note inside Taurus's otherwise proud "stay-put" festivals. It's a minority current, not a replacement: a small number of Sanay-descended households mark their own persistence privately rather than festively, a modest variation that stands out precisely because it declines the celebratory tone Taurus's stay-put culture usually assumes.

## District 3 — Leo

Nearly a quarter of Leo's outer-diaspora population traces to **Casey** (23.9%), with **Shirayuki** close behind at almost a fifth (18.8%). **Janbogo (the city)** and **Zukelli** each contribute around a seventh (13.9% and 13.4%), **Mawson** and **Dumont d'Urville** each around a fifteenth (7.7% and 7.2%), and smaller but still meaningful communities descend from **Denison** (5.7%), **Palmer City** (5.3%), and **Juan Carlos** (4.1%).

### Casey (~23.9%)
Splinters — Tepenia's largest, most famous bar, already known well beyond Casey's own borders — gives its diaspora every reason to recreate it rather than let the name fade into a ruin's memory. In Leo, the ambition is less about physical scale and more about reviving Splinters Set Nights' genre-spanning format (Classic Rock, Jazz, Blues, Acoustic Folk rotating through one stage) inside the district's own open-mic amphitheaters — a natural fit for a district with no audience/performer distinction to begin with. Junction Toasts, Casey's understated recognition for a dispatcher or driver who got someone through danger, filter into Leo's own tiered recognition economy at a noticeably lower register than the district is used to: Casey's diaspora carries "quiet, non-demonstrative reticence even into loss" even here, so its toasts stay small and private, a muted counter-current running alongside Leo's more performative status theater rather than folding cleanly into it.

### Shirayuki (~18.8%)
Glacier-Pop Living Rooms — the informal, friends-and-neighbors performance spaces where Shirayuki's massive amateur music scene actually happens — map almost exactly onto Leo's own **Understudy Halls**, the district's existing proving-ground venues for not-yet-recognized performers; the two traditions likely merge rather than compete. The Ashiato Ateliers, where residents assemble and personalize homemade garments, slot directly into Leo's own "basic costume & personal-brand augmentation" training offices, giving that existing entry a specific craft identity it lacked. Institute Placement Nights, the mentorship gatherings connecting graduating students into performance circles, find a home inside Leo's **Lineage Houses** — giving the district's blood-lineage-style artistic seriousness a second, parallel credentialing track through formal instruction.

### Janbogo, the city (~13.9%)
Majyao's Original Teahouse is the most direct precedent of all Leo's contributors — its own keeper is already established as having relocated to Concordia, with the original site preserved as civic memory back home. That's effectively a transplant story Janbogo's own file has half-written already; a recreated teahouse in Leo plausibly becomes exactly the kind of small, personally-run venue that climbs Leo's recognition ladder over a generation. Janbogo's teaware & coffee craft ateliers likely persist as a boutique craft supplying Leo's existing bars and bistros.

### Zukelli (~13.4%)
Zukelli's famous restaurant-and-performance-space — a dedicated stage built directly into a restaurant — is close to a plug-and-play fit for a district that already runs bars and bistros as performance venues and already blurs performer and audience. A recreated version emerges less as memorial and more as a functioning restaurant-with-stage in Leo's own idiom. The pre-war "Crossing" tradition (informal visits blurring who was hosting whom) has no geography left to anchor it, but the underlying instinct — easy, unannounced visiting — plausibly softens Leo's more status-conscious **Golden Ring Walks** at the edges.

### Mawson (~7.7%)
The honeymoon-overlook pilgrimage can't travel intact — Leo has no mountain view. But the underlying practice, a pilgrimage timed to a dramatic light event, has an obvious substitute in a district whose "reverence for light is not metaphorical" and whose Dimming Vigil already treats controlled light and dark as sacred. Mawson honeymooners in Leo plausibly redirect the trip toward the **Candlelight Halls** — giving Mawson's honeymoon-destination identity a ready-made new venue for the exact kind of occasion it already built its civic reputation on.

### Dumont d'Urville (~7.2%)
The downtown Eateries-with-Stages model — "New Orleans at 1/20th scale," live music as ordinary infrastructure rather than special-occasion spectacle — is close to a direct match for Leo's bars and bistros, giving that entry a specific stylistic identity: small, packed, informal venues. Dumont d'Urville refugees are likely disproportionately represented among the operators of Leo's smaller, less prestigious venues — a register closer in spirit to the district's own historically disadvantaged "intimate" cluster from the Star War than to its grand tradition.

### Denison (~5.7%)
Denison's own file already lists survival-narrative and extremity literature as a dedicated small office for educational training, built on its defining ritual: reciting wind statistics loudly and proudly. Displaced into Leo, that verbal-boasting tradition has an obvious outlet in the district's open-mic amphitheaters and Understudy Nights, alongside the percussive music Denison built specifically to be heard "over" the wind — repurposed in Leo to compete with, rather than defer to, the district's own famous noise and heat.

### Palmer City (~5.3%)
Needle Down, the citywide dusk-to-dawn live-music observance spanning Palmer City's full genre range, is the most legible import for a district that already treats performance as sacred infrastructure — a diaspora-scaled version inside Leo's Candlelight Halls functions as both tribute and calendar addition. The Little Burgundy Quarter's jazz identity plausibly clusters wherever in Leo already leans toward the district's more intimate "Losing House" aesthetic.

### Juan Carlos (~4.1%)
Juan Carlos's own defining gathering tradition remains deliberately unnamed by developer direction, but its form — a private, guest-listed, argument-driven evening organized around food, drink, and storytelling — likely surfaces in Leo as a smaller counter-current to the district's public, applause-driven culture, most plausibly inside Leo's **reputation-trading cafés**, where status can already be lent or gifted.

## District 4 — Scorpio

Nearly thirty percent of Scorpio's diaspora traces to **Casey**, a quarter to **Zukelli**, and just over a fifth to **Belgrano**. A meaningfully smaller but ordinary cultural-affinity population comes from **Vostok** (7.7%), with **Palmer City** (6.7%), **Marambio** (5.7%), and **Scott** (3.8%) rounding out the rest.

Unlike Scorpio's neighbors in this file, the four largest contributors — Casey, Zukelli, Belgrano, Palmer City — aren't here because their pre-war culture resembled Scorpio's confrontational-transformation ethos; per `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s own Scorpio Development Notes, none of the four is Scorpio-coded on its own terms. They're here because the Archive of Final Confessions is the one Concordia institution built to receive their specific kind of mass-casualty civilizational grief, and each brings a genuinely distinct **character of loss** rather than a home culture to recreate. What follows for these four isn't a festival-and-craft list — it's an account of how each community's grief has been shaping Scorpio's own institutions from the inside, as long-term survivors-in-residence.

### Casey (~29.8%)
Casey's own established diaspora temperament — quiet, non-demonstrative reticence even into loss — is compounded by a harder wound: per Scorpio's own Development Notes, Casey's grief-character is "the surprise of not having been a primary target," destruction that arrived without even the grim clarity of having been deliberately chosen. That combination plausibly makes Casey's diaspora among the heaviest users of Scorpio's **Everyday Confession Booths** rather than the formal Archive itself — lower-stakes spaces suited to loss that resists a clean narrative — and disproportionately present in the district's **Silence Booths** and silent mask processions, where saying nothing is the entire point.

### Zukelli (~25.0%)
Scorpio's own notes describe Zukelli's grief-character as "unmediated shock" — a meaningful share of its diaspora watched their own city's destruction happen in real time, in daily sightline from Janbogo's waterfront, before ever reaching Concordia. That kind of immediate, witnessed trauma doesn't fit Scorpio's own "descent, integration, emergence" mask-phase pipeline, which assumes a processable arc. Zukelli's arrival, concentrated in the same post-war years as the district's own Identity Collapse Epidemic (c. 2812–2820) — when Scorpio was developing emergency techniques with no precedent — is plausibly part of what forced those techniques to exist at all; Zukelli's presence may be structurally responsible for some of Scorpio's least clinical, most improvised practices.

### Belgrano (~21.3%)
Belgrano's grief-character — "archival mission" — tracks exactly with the disciplined, duty-roster record-keeping already established as the city's defining trait. Scorpio's own Inhabitants list already includes "confession-recording archivists" as a distinct robot type; Belgrano's diaspora is the most plausible human source of that same instinct applied to grief rather than machinery — treating the Archive's intake with the same "the archive is a duty roster" discipline Belgrano once applied to Rastra maintenance schedules.

### Vostok (~7.7%) — ordinary Stage-1 fit, treated normally
Vostok's presence is a genuine cultural affinity rather than a trauma-driven one — its "legend living in someone else's house" identity (a subglacial archive so vast no single researcher understands it whole) resonates naturally with Scorpio's own culture of secrecy and partial disclosure. The Distributed Archive Reading Rooms, where researchers each hold and cross-reference their own partial slice of an incomprehensible archive, fold easily into Scorpio's Archive Reading Nights; Vostok refugees are likely overrepresented among the Archive of Final Confessions' own custodial staff. Window Courtesy — checking whether someone is currently "in their window" before approaching directly — maps almost seamlessly onto Scorpio's own elaborate secrecy code, where residents already signal what can and cannot be discussed publicly.

### Palmer City (~6.7%)
Palmer City's grief-character — "civilizational symbolism" — is qualitatively different: it wasn't just a city, it was Tepenia's own founding-era proof of what the exile generation had built. Its diaspora's confessions inside the Archive are plausibly the single biggest driver of the tension Scorpio's own notes already flag as unresolved — "whether an archive receiving this volume of acute loss can maintain its clinical rebirth orientation at all, or whether it is slowly transforming into something closer to a war memorial." Scorpio's own **Archive Reading Nights** are the clearest existing mechanism where that instinct could already be playing out.

### Marambio (~5.7%)
The thinnest cultural match here, and Marambio's own "functional, not self-reflective" dual-mode airfield-and-shipyard discipline is a poorer fit for Scorpio's introspective register than any other contributor. What plausibly does travel is the Comandante's Operations Hall's core trait — operational discipline that outlasted the founding population's own presence — giving Marambio's small diaspora a quiet, persistent foothold inside Scorpio's **Underground Rebirth Annexes**: a stubborn administrative competence rather than a therapeutic tradition of its own.

### Scott (~3.8%)
The Hut Point Remembrance — where Scott and Fort McMurdo residents lay candles, flowers, and personal tokens in memory of St. Robert, unmandated but genuinely felt — is a ready-made ritual form that plausibly surfaces as an informal tributary tradition alongside Scorpio's own **Emergence Welcomes**: a quiet, token-laying gathering for those who want to mark a loss without a full transformation process.

*(All four destroyed/ruined contributors — Casey, Zukelli, Belgrano, Palmer City — are not interchangeable, and Scorpio's own Development Notes explicitly flag that they may develop distinct relationships worth dramatizing; the material above gives each of those four relationships its own specific shape.)*

## District 5 — Aries — The Power Core

Aries's diaspora is a near-even three-way split among **Denison** (~39%), **Troll** (~35%), and **Belgrano** (~26%) — no long tail to speak of. Notably, all three source cities already share Aries's own core identity: hardened, essential-infrastructure work performed under crisis conditions by people who take blunt pride in enduring it, which makes most of these transplants unusually smooth rather than effortful.

### Denison (~39.0%)
Denison is destroyed, but its most portable cultural trait is verbal rather than physical: the practice of **reciting wind statistics loudly and proudly** — a spoken tradition that compensated for the city's own severe-but-architecturally-invisible conditions. It transplants almost seamlessly into Aries's **Running Hot Boards**, the district's existing public tally of who's taken the most dangerous shifts: Denison-descended workers bring the same boasting instinct, simply substituting shift-output and radiation-exposure figures for wind-speed numbers, into a district that already runs on exactly this kind of status talk. Denison's percussive, rhythmic music meant to be heard "over" harsh conditions folds into Aries's **never-dark canteens** as a specific musical identity — apt for a district that "never goes dark and never goes quiet."

### Troll (~34.9%)
Troll's Cross-Faction Dispatch Handoffs — the tense, necessary daily coordination between three competing factions that keeps freight moving because no single faction can fully function without the others' cooperation — reads almost as a direct precursor to Aries's own shift-crew culture, where crew loyalty is paramount but the grid itself still requires everyone's cooperation regardless of internal politics. Troll-descended dispatchers bring a refined instinct for exactly this kind of forced cooperation into Aries's **Per-Crew Mess Halls** and **Hot Shift Send-Offs**, reinforcing structures that already existed rather than introducing anything foreign. Runway Day, Troll's civic celebration of the airfield's founding or expansion, becomes a template within Aries's **Machine-Naming Ceremonies**, extended to mark milestones in the power core's own infrastructure growth.

### Belgrano (~26.1%)
Belgrano's Boneyard Maintenance Rotations and Maintenance Rotation Handoffs — the informal, organized survival system that kept the city functioning once its institutional command structure collapsed — merge almost without friction into Aries's existing shift-handoff culture; both are built on the same "necessity produces organization" logic Aries already runs on. Garage Nights, Belgrano's informal, competence-driven gathering built around the idea that "for anything with an engine, there's reliably somebody who can fix it," becomes a natural sibling to Aries's own machine-devotion culture (the **Named Machine Registries**, **Machine-Naming Ceremonies**): Belgrano-descended mechanics become the district's go-to specialists for named-equipment repair, since both cultures already treat their machines with the same hands-on reverence — Belgrano's aimed at Rastras and aircraft, Aries's at the power core.

## District 6 — Capricorn (The Yards)

**Neumayer** (22.9%) and **Sinheung** (19.6%) together account for over two-fifths of Capricorn's outer diaspora. **Rothera** (~12.6%, rolling in the small, undedicated Sanay/Byrd/Kunlun tail below) and **Troll** (11.6%) are substantial secondary contributors, followed by **Marambio** (10.4%), **Davis** (7.1%), **Belgrano** (6.5%), **Fort McMurdo** (5.4%), and **Sejong** (3.9%).

### Neumayer (~22.9%)
The Instrument-Craft Workshops — precision engineering and calibrated-tool-making, treated as genuinely admired craft — integrate almost seamlessly into Capricorn's guild structure, effectively arriving as a ready-made new specialty guild fitting the spire-and-status system exactly as naturally as its existing heavy-industrial trades. Data Milestone Gatherings, Neumayer's real social occasions tied to project completions rather than food, map directly onto Capricorn's own weekly "proof-of-work" exhibitions. After-Hours Sound Venues plausibly take root as an alternative to Capricorn's guild-hall dining culture (which only opens "after a day's quota is met"), giving that existing post-quota window an actual musical identity.

### Sinheung (~19.6%)
Shared-Dish Suppers — communal meals explicitly built so "everyone's dish is visibly part of the spread," echoing the fabrication floor's own individual-contribution-collectively-assessed ethos — land inside Capricorn as almost a philosophical statement: a visible, edible version of the district's own merit-board culture. Production-Floor Work Songs slot directly into Capricorn's existing "people working on some machinery/welding project together" mechanism, giving that largely wordless custom an actual soundtrack. Chamber Watch Toasts, marking a new fabrication chamber's completion, likely merge with Capricorn's own **Floor-Climb Celebrations**.

### Rothera (~12.6%, rolling in the small, undedicated Sanay/Byrd/Kunlun tail below)
The Standing Works — Rothera's observance of continued post-war industrial function, read as quiet pride rather than dramatic resilience — fits Capricorn's achievement-through-endurance culture closely enough to likely be absorbed into the district's existing **Merit Board Vigils**; Rothera's diaspora brings, above all, a story of decentralized survival into Capricorn's culture of legible, measurable achievement.

### Troll (~11.6%)
Cross-Faction Dispatch Handoffs — the tense daily coordination Troll's three rival airfield factions perform to keep freight moving, since none can fully function without the others — is a striking match for Capricorn's own unresolved internal tension around the Narrow Door and its passed-over-workers' undercurrent. Troll's diaspora arrives fluent in formalized cooperation between parties who fundamentally distrust each other; that competence finds real use inside Capricorn's own **Guild Departure Mediation Offices** and **Guild Binding Witness Circuit**.

### Marambio (~10.4%)
Bulk cargo handling as shared civic labor — the volume-driven dockworker tradition defining Marambio's shipyard identity — folds directly into Capricorn's own proof-of-work culture, giving it a specifically maritime-industrial flavor. The Comandante's Operations Hall's discipline, a founding-era standard that outlasted its own population, resonates with Capricorn's guild-continuity culture; Marambio's diaspora likely gravitates toward Capricorn's most tradition-bound guilds.

### Davis (~7.1%)
The Quiet Credit — researchers deliberately acknowledging team members' uncredited contributions — is close to a direct match for something Capricorn's own culture already half-suppresses: the **Undervalued Workers' Quiet Archive** and its own passed-over workers' **Quiet Solidarity Signals**. Davis's diaspora, arriving with an openly-practiced version of the same instinct Capricorn keeps hidden, plausibly gives that underground tradition its first genuinely public-facing counterpart.

### Belgrano (~6.5%)
Close to a native fit rather than an adaptation. The Rastra Garages transplant essentially unchanged, absorbed into Capricorn's existing informal machinery-project custom — Belgrano's own low-key, competence-driven Garage Nights and Capricorn's welding-project gatherings are already, structurally, the same social glue.

### Fort McMurdo (~5.4%)
Founders' Shift — a work-appreciation observance where crews are publicly recognized for output rather than narrated history — is close to a plug-and-play match for Capricorn's Floor-Climb Celebrations and Merit Board Vigils, sharing the same underlying value: visible output as the basis for public recognition.

### Sejong (~3.9%)
Sejong's own Boundary Zones — architecturally transitional, not hard-walled, districts where a dozen absorbed national communities' neighborhoods met — suggest a modest transplant: Sejong's small diaspora, drawn from a city whose whole civic skill was negotiating shared space between wary neighbors without hard walls, is likely quietly overrepresented among the mediators inside Capricorn's Guild Departure Mediation Offices.

## District 7 — Aquarius (The Labs)

Aquarius's outer-diaspora population is dominated by three Mirny subnet cities that together account for nearly three-quarters of its weighted total: **Halley** alone supplies well over a third, with **Shirayuki** and **Sinheung** close behind. **Vostok** (~10.2%) and **Marambio** each contribute roughly a tenth, and **Juan Carlos** and **Kunlun** round out the list at just above three percent apiece — small enough to be minority communities within the district rather than defining ones, but present enough to leave a mark.

### Halley (~36.4%)
Halley's entire civic identity is built around Position Day — the annual survey-and-record ritual tied to relocating the city's ski-mounted platform structures ahead of the sea ice that will eventually claim their current site — and Old Position Photography Walks, the community practice of documenting an abandoned position before it's lost for good. Both travel intact into Aquarius, because the district already runs on the same emotional logic: things are built, they fail or are superseded, and the failure gets treated as worth recording rather than as embarrassment. Old Position Photography Walks lands almost without modification onto Aquarius's own **Prototype Graveyards (with visitor access)** — Halley transplants become some of the district's most natural curators of decommissioned experiments. Position Day's meticulous survey habit feeds directly into the district's **Data Archivist Repositories**.

### Shirayuki (~19.7%)
Shirayuki's whole migration pattern — young people arriving for the Institute and staying for the informal arts culture that grows up around it — maps cleanly onto Aquarius's own **University of Concordia Dormitory Clusters** and its transient, politically active student population. Institute Placement Nights recreate themselves in Aquarius as an unofficial extension of **Research Cluster Potlucks**, with Shirayuki-descended mentors doing for incoming Aquarius idealists what their home institution once did for them. Glacier-Pop Living Rooms resurface as an analogue to Aquarius's own **Prototype Show-and-Tell Nights**: the same instinct to showcase unfinished, unpolished work in someone's actual living space rather than wait for a sanctioned venue.

### Sinheung (~17.9%)
Shared-Dish Suppers slot almost seamlessly into Aquarius's **Research Cluster Potlucks** — both are meals organized around a shared technical undertaking rather than a neighborhood. Chamber Watch Toasts translate into something Aquarius badly needs given its own culture of failed multi-year utopias: a ritual for marking the rare moment a difficult, long-gestating project actually works, sitting comfortably alongside **Cross-Generation Utopia Storytelling**.

### Vostok (~10.2%, rolling in the small, undedicated Dome Fuji tail)
Archive Cross-Reference Nights — researchers comparing their own partial understanding of an archive nobody comprehends in full — are close enough in spirit to Aquarius's **First Principles Salons** and anonymous idea-drop walls that Vostok transplants tend to gravitate straight into the **Underground Research Annexes**, bringing a native comfort with incomplete knowledge and collaborative triangulation that Aquarius's more individually-certain researchers often lack.

### Marambio (~9.5%)
Marambio no longer exists, but its Founders' Flight observance — commemorating the exile generation's own decision to fly out onto Seymour Island rather than wait — travels as pure story rather than physical setting, resonating with Aquarius's own culture of visionaries betting everything on an uncertain next attempt. The Comandante's Operations Hall's outlasting institutional discipline echoes into Aquarius as a small counter-current: Marambio-descended residents are disproportionately drawn to the **Contamination Response Staging Posts**, bringing operational rigor to a district that otherwise prizes brilliance over procedure.

### Juan Carlos (~3.2%)
Juan Carlos's own defining social institution — the hosted, argument-driven gathering tradition the developer deliberately left unnamed — requires almost no adaptation to fit Aquarius's 24-hour "first-principles" debate dens; it is, structurally, the same activity, and the small Juan Carlos community folds into that existing infrastructure rather than building anything new.

### Kunlun (~3.1%)
Kunlun's Scientist-Devotee Shared Watches — astronomers and Ice Cold Buddhism devotees working the same instrument during the same event for entirely different reasons, without friction — is close kin to Aquarius's own uneasy coexistence of **True Believer Gatherings** and the pragmatists who live with a Failed Utopia's aftermath. Kunlun transplants recognize the pattern immediately and tend to become informal mediators between Aquarius's visionaries and its skeptics.

## District 8 — Libra (The Government District)

Libra draws the widest and most evenly distributed diaspora base of any district covered here — eleven cities each contributing a meaningful share, none dominant, which is fitting for a district whose entire identity rests on formal balance between many parties. **Zhongshan** supplies the largest single share at just under a fifth, with **Halley**, **Mirny**, and **Sejong** following, then **Juan Carlos** (~8.6%); **Cape Adare**, **Janbogo**, **Neumayer**, **Sinheung**, and **Abowasa** each hold a smaller but still distinct footprint, and **Palmer City** closes the list just above the cutoff.

### Zhongshan (~19.2%)
Zhongshan's Long Record — the Mirny subnet's most complete surviving pre-Split-Brain archive — gives its diaspora a natural home in Libra's **Precedent Archives**; Zhongshan-descended archivists bring a centuries-deep habit of custodianship that fits Libra's own reverence for the written past. More usefully for daily life, Dumpling Arguments — Zhongshan's designated, low-stakes outlet for disagreement in an otherwise non-confrontational culture — recreate themselves inside Libra as something close to a plainer, lower-stakes cousin of Libra's own precedent-and-implication-coded disputes: a place where Zhongshan transplants can simply argue about something small and direct, a genuine relief valve in a district where every other disagreement has to pass through layers of proxy and precedent.

### Halley (~14.6%)
Halley's institutionalized habit of engineering/logistics documentation and collective decision-making process-writing — the bureaucratic backbone behind Position Day — is, in effect, a smaller-scale version of what Libra does at the level of an entire government. Halley transplants integrate easily into the **Precedent Archives** and the review culture around the district's crisis-management chambers, bringing a genuine comfort with process-for-its-own-sake that most new arrivals to Libra have to be taught.

### Mirny (~13.6%)
Mirny's Dual-Table Suppers — Russian and Chinese dishes served side by side, deliberately kept distinct rather than fused — is close enough to Libra's own formal balance-circle negotiations, where opposing parties sit equidistant until a ruling is reached, that Mirny transplants often describe the two as versions of the same idea: two things that don't merge, held in a stable, witnessed arrangement. It becomes a recognizable, informal counterpart practiced at Libra dinner tables long before anyone sits down at a formal negotiation.

### Sejong (~9.7%)
Sejong no longer exists, but its Neighbors' Accord — the anniversary marking the land-use agreement that let a dozen national communities coexist on one small island, deliberately kept separate from any claim the underlying tension was ever resolved — is close to a textbook precedent for everything Libra does. Sejong-descended residents gravitate toward the **Precedent Archives** with an unusual clarity, since their own home city's founding document did exactly what Libra's founding compact still hasn't managed: negotiate lasting coexistence honestly, without pretending it erased the friction.

### Juan Carlos (~8.6%, rolling in the small, undedicated Fort McMurdo/Kunlun tail below)
Juan Carlos's own half-joking self-nickname — "the Federation's memory" — lands with an almost uncomfortable resonance inside Libra, a district built entirely around functioning as the city's institutional memory. Its diaspora gravitates toward the **Precedent Archives**, carrying forward the archival instinct of a city that was destroyed specifically for holding too much of it.

### Cape Adare (~7.5%)
Cape Adare's Heritage Documentation Archive reinforces Libra's own **Treaty Archive Vaults** instinct that a record can outlive the thing it records. Its diaspora also brings arrival/departure meal rituals, the city's gateway-hospitality tradition marking comings and goings; inside Libra's checkpoint-heavy environment, this resurfaces as a small, human counterweight to the district's otherwise cold, procedural way of greeting incoming envoys.

### Janbogo, the city (~6.6%)
The custom of hosting someone who's had a hard week without them having to ask — food or drink simply arriving — is a deliberate softness that Libra badly lacks. Janbogo transplants recreate small versions of this hospitality inside the district's otherwise rigidly precedent-coded social world, offering a rare space where a tired aide doesn't have to signal anything through process to be looked after.

### Neumayer (~6.3%)
Neumayer's Instrument-Craft Workshops and its "Measured, Not Debated" precision culture translate naturally into Libra's own procedural exactitude — Neumayer transplants often end up drafting the fine print. Its Founders' Instrument observance, treating inherited research equipment as an ongoing mission rather than a museum piece, resonates with Libra's own reverence for inherited institutional legacy.

### Sinheung (~5.4%)
Sinheung's Hills Accord, the observance marking ongoing coexistence with its Larsemann Hills neighbors, is a smaller-scale precedent-and-treaty tradition that folds easily into Libra's own commemorative calendar — a minor but genuine addition to a district that already marks the passage of political agreements as civic occasions.

### Abowasa (~5.2%)
Abowasa's Joint Founding — the shared civic observance marking the moment two originally separate settlement programs became one town — is Libra's own founding myth (the post-Falkland negotiations) in miniature, and Abowasa transplants recognize it as such. The observance integrates without friction into Libra's calendar of treaty-adjacent commemorations.

### Palmer City (~3.3%)
Palmer City's founding-day observance, marking the day its exiles arrived the same day the Falkland Treaty was signed, is the smallest but most on-the-nose transplant in the district — a founding-day observance built around treaty timing, arriving in the one district built entirely around what a treaty means. Its community is too small to establish anything beyond quiet, private observance, but the resonance is immediate.

## District 9 — Gemini (The Circuit)

Gemini's diaspora is unusually concentrated and unusually charged: **Janbogo** and **Zukelli** together account for roughly half its weighted population, and Gemini's own established lore already names this exact pairing as one of the district's most significant ongoing social tensions — Janbogo-city survivors and Zukelli refugees living side by side, watching the same water from two very different sides of the Long Night War. **Esperanza** is nearly as large a contributor as Janbogo. **Sejong** and **Princess Elisabeth** add smaller but real communities, **Sayowa** sits just above the cutoff, and a token thread from **Neumayer** (~0.1%) rounds the composition out to its full total.

### Janbogo, the city (~30.0%)
Janbogo's own Community Infrastructure file records that the city's single most historically significant landmark, its most famous teahouse, is remembered today only by the name of the keeper who relocated to Concordia before the war. That keeper's lineage is exactly the kind of concrete anchor Gemini's diaspora content should build on: a Janbogo teahouse tradition transplanted wholesale, becoming one of the district's own informal information hubs, since a teahouse that already dealt in rumor, gossip, and travelers' news back home slides naturally into Gemini's culture of triangulating truth from competing accounts. Janbogo's Smuggled Book Reading Rooms likewise transplant intact into Gemini's **Shadow Network Access Points** — an already-existing tradition of openly welcoming otherwise-suppressed information finding its most natural possible new home.

### Esperanza (~28.7%)
Esperanza's diaspora carries something unusually well-suited to Gemini's central civic project. The Birth Registry Archive fragments, and the ongoing, emotionally live thread of diaspora requests to know whether specific family names survive within them, are structurally the same activity as Gemini's own **Great Corruption Recovery Archive**: searching a damaged, partial record for pieces of a lost whole. Esperanza-descended residents are disproportionately drawn into Gemini's data archaeology work — the district's ongoing recovery mission gives their inherited grief an actual, useful outlet.

### Zukelli (~19.2%)
Zukelli's presence in Gemini is already fully accounted for in the district's own established lore, down to the Zukelli Memory Circles it lists as a native social cohesion mechanism. What's worth adding is the *positive* half of what Zukelli brought with it, alongside the grief: the city's pre-war identity was built on communal meals as the center of civic life, and music and food treated as adjacent expressions of the same hospitality instinct. That tradition resurfaces in Gemini not as a memorial but as a living practice — small food-and-music venues run by Zukelli transplants, giving the district's refugee population somewhere to be together that isn't only about processing loss.

### Sejong (~9.9%)
Sejong's Hangul Instruction Halls, which maintained literacy and cultural continuity for a population that grew far more diverse than its founding nation, resonate with Gemini's own **Triangulation Schools** — both are institutions built to keep a linguistically and informationally mixed population legible to itself. Sejong transplants have a natural affinity for teaching newer arrivals the district's own skill of triangulating between competing accounts.

### Princess Elisabeth (~8.7%)
Princess Elisabeth's own dual American/Japanese demographic tradition of exchanging food, language, and custom between two communities finds an odd but genuine kinship with Gemini's Twin Channels: two parallel versions of the same thing, run side by side rather than merged. Princess Elisabeth transplants are unusually comfortable with the idea that a place can hold two simultaneous, equally valid versions of itself.

### Sayowa (~3.4%)
Sayowa's small community brings its own cooperative, multi-party junction-management instinct — the same ethic that keeps its real highway crossing running without any single party fully owning it. That translates loosely into Gemini's shift-change information-network handoffs; Sayowa transplants recognize the underlying pattern of cooperative dual-system management immediately, even without ever having worked an Arcanet node themselves.

### Neumayer (~0.1%)
A token thread rather than a real community, but worth naming rather than folding silently into the rounding: a handful of Neumayer-descended residents, drawn less by cultural affinity (Neumayer's own top picks lie elsewhere) than by Gemini's information-recovery mission itself, apply the same "Measured, Not Debated" documentation habit that defines Neumayer's Instrument-Craft Workshops to the painstaking, precise cataloguing work inside the **Great Corruption Recovery Archive**.

## District 10 — Pisces (The Markets)

Pisces's diaspora is heavily lopsided: **Shirayuki** accounts for the overwhelming majority, close to nine-tenths, with **Dome Fuji** making up the remainder at roughly one-eighth. No other city clears the threshold.

### Shirayuki (~87.7%)
The Ashiato Ateliers — informal studios where residents assemble, alter, and personalize homemade garments — transplant naturally into Pisces given the district's own fluid-frame, identity-reconfiguration culture, but the landing isn't a clean copy: filtered through Pisces's origin-blind, no-questions ethos, the Ashiato tradition takes on a more clandestine function, with alterations increasingly aimed at obscuring identity rather than simply expressing it, and stalls for the practice settling into corners of the district's shifting alley markets rather than fixed studios. Glacier-Pop Living Rooms merge cleanly into Pisces's own shifting alley markets and dream-tech dens, since both are unofficial, non-institutional performance spaces that can appear and vanish. The one genuinely awkward transplant is Institute Placement Nights — Shirayuki's open, name-based mentorship tradition. Pisces has no interest in anyone's credentials or origin, so the practice can't survive intact; it re-emerges reworked into something closer to the district's own secret-sharing circles, where newcomers are still connected to a community, but only through traded vulnerability rather than open introduction — a noticeably foreign adjustment for Shirayuki arrivals used to a warmer, more transparent welcome.

### Dome Fuji (~12.3%)
Dome Fuji's Ice Cold Buddhism doesn't merge with Pisces's own syncretic religion so much as run alongside it, contributing distinct devotional practices to an already-porous spiritual landscape. The Crossing — each devotee's individually timed arrival-anniversary reenactment, walked in robes to their own sacred site — adapts into Pisces via the district's own Wandering Shrine, with Dome Fuji-descended devotees performing their own robed anniversary walk to wherever the shrine has currently relocated, treating its unpredictable movement as an oddly fitting echo of the plateau crossing's original remoteness. Deepest Cold, "the closest thing to a universal, mandatory communal event" back home, introduces a genuine point of friction into Pisces's quiet syncretic gatherings: Pisces's own religion has no canonical texts and is transmitted person-to-person, entirely unscheduled, while Deepest Cold is fixed-date and universal. Dome Fuji-descended devotees keep observing it as such, and it persists as a small, recognizably foreign fixed point inside an otherwise deliberately informal spiritual calendar.

## District 11 — Sagittarius (The Frostlands)

Nearly two-fifths of Sagittarius's outer diaspora traces to **Denison** alone (39.6%). **Troll** (17.7%) and **Byrd** (14.0%) together add roughly another third, with smaller communities from **Dumont d'Urville** (8.4%), **Vostok** (7.2%), **Signy** (7.0%), and **Rothera** (6.1%) rounding out the rest.

### Denison (~39.6%)
Denison's own file already lists survival-narrative and extremity literature as a dedicated small office for educational training, built on its defining ritual — reciting wind statistics loudly and proudly as a way of verbally compensating for what the city's withdrawn architecture kept invisible. That tradition has no clearer destination anywhere in Concordia than a district where storytelling is already the primary cultural currency and "a story doesn't need to be literally true to be valuable — it needs to be true about something." Denison's diaspora plausibly becomes some of Sagittarius's most sought-after storytellers, slotting into the existing **Storyteller's Circuit** and **Elder Interview Nights**. Wind-rated gear worn as a recognized "quiet flex" fits just as naturally into Sagittarius's own explorer-guild meritocracy, close enough to Denison's own quiet-flex culture that the two likely merge into the district's existing **Guild Status Challenges**.

### Troll (~17.7%)
Troll's fractured, three-faction airfield isn't a tradition Sagittarius would adopt wholesale, but it's exactly the kind of real, lived dilemma the district's **Debate Hall Open Nights** were built to host. Troll's diaspora brings concrete, non-abstract material into Sagittarius's philosophical culture — real stories about keeping a shared, contested resource functioning when nobody fully trusts anybody else.

### Byrd (~14.0%)
St. Richard's Marker — the modest memorial visited quietly before undertakings nobody can be certain will hold, informally adopted across all five of Byrd's guilds — is close enough in function to Sagittarius's own **Third Expansion Memorial Waystation** that Byrd's diaspora likely treats the two as the same practice. The Grounded Wings' mothballed-but-genuinely-maintained hangar complex — hope kept in ready condition rather than left to decay — resonates with Sagittarius's own **Jury-Rig Recovery Workshops** and **Hope Engine Maintenance Circuit**.

### Dumont d'Urville (~8.4%)
The naturalist-observation tradition can't travel with its subject, but the underlying practice — long, patient, first-hand observation — fits neatly into Sagittarius's own open-sky observatories for long-range prospecting, a useful skill transfer rather than a lost custom.

### Vostok (~7.2%)
Archive Cross-Reference Nights — comparing partial understanding of an archive nobody comprehends in full — echo Sagittarius's own **Knowledge Preservation Lodges**, where elder scouts are paired with mentees precisely because the district's oral tradition means entire bodies of practical knowledge die with individual explorers. Vostok's diaspora, already practiced at systematically pooling incomplete knowledge, is a natural fit for that preservation crisis.

### Signy (~7.0%)
Signy's own historic open-boat survival commemoration is, at its core, exactly the kind of story Sagittarius's culture prizes. Even without the original setting to observe it at, Signy's diaspora plausibly keeps retelling it through the **Storyteller's Circuit**, sitting comfortably alongside the district's other genuine survival narratives.

### Rothera (~6.1%)
Rothera's decentralized-survival story — a city that made it through the war because no single strike could take out everything at once — travels as narrative rather than institution, fit for the district's **Debate Hall Open Nights** as a case study in what resilience looks like when it isn't a single hero's doing.

## District 12 — Virgo (The Undergrid)

Virgo is the largest and most crowded district in this whole file, and its diaspora reflects that: twelve contributing cities, none dominant, several of them converging independently on almost the same cultural instinct Virgo already embodies — quiet, unglorified, essential labor. **Davis**, **Belgrano**, **Casey**, and **Sanay** (~11.2%, rolling in the small, undedicated Rothera/Signy/Sayowa/Port Lockroy tail below) lead, each above a tenth of the weighted total; **Zhongshan**, **Neumayer**, and **Princess Elisabeth** follow closely; **Halley** and **Mirny** hold smaller but real shares; and **Fort McMurdo**, **Byrd**, and **Dumont d'Urville** — the three smallest, each between roughly three and five percent — are grouped together below rather than given full separate treatment.

### Davis (~13.3%)
Davis is arguably the single cleanest cultural match in this entire file. Its Harvest Quiet — a deliberately unshowy seasonal acknowledgment that Davis's greenhouse harvest feeds the wider Federation, treated with the same anti-glory ethos the city applies to everything — and the Quiet Credit, the informal practice of researchers making a point of acknowledging teammates' uncredited contributions, don't need adaptation to land in Virgo; they need almost none, because Virgo's entire civic identity ("We Keep You Alive" posters that are not ironic, a district that received better equipment allocations instead of memorials for war work nobody witnessed) is the same value system at a different scale. Davis transplants fold directly into the **Documentation Vaults**, and Harvest Quiet becomes an unofficial but widely observed second half of Virgo's own **"Another Day Above Ground" Morning Circles**.

### Belgrano (~12.3%)
The Rastra Garages and Garage Nights — specialist mechanic culture where "for anything with an engine, there's reliably somebody who can repair it" competence gets swapped and shown off socially — recreate themselves in Virgo almost unmodified, sitting comfortably alongside the district's own **Junction Box Galleries**: two cultures that both turned utilitarian repair work into something worth doing beautifully. Belgrano's Maintenance Rotation Handoffs are close enough to Virgo's own **shift-overlap "junction parties"** that Belgrano transplants often can't tell you which tradition they actually grew up with; the two describe the same social structure, just with engines instead of tunnels.

### Casey (~11.4%)
Casey no longer exists, and its diaspora carries its home city's quiet, non-demonstrative reticence into loss rather than performing grief outright — a temperament that fits Virgo's own gallows-humor stoicism well. Junction Toasts — informal recognition for a dispatcher or driver who quietly got someone through a dangerous wind window safely, with no ceremony beyond the toast itself — is a near-perfect match for Virgo's whole unacknowledged-heroism culture; Casey transplants have quietly extended the custom to maintenance crews who prevented disasters nobody outside Virgo ever heard about. Road Story Circles give Virgo's own written **Documentation Reading Nights** a spoken counterpart.

### Sanay (~11.2%, rolling in the small, undedicated Rothera/Signy/Sayowa/Port Lockroy tail below)
Sanay's own quiet, deliberately understated observance that explicitly declines to frame mere survival as exceptional describes Virgo's own culture almost word for word. Sanay transplants have quietly deepened the district's existing **"Another Day Above Ground" Morning Circles**, bringing a whole prior tradition built around refusing to celebrate endurance as though it were remarkable.

### Zhongshan (~9.8%)
The Mending Houses — dedicated workshops where garment repair is practiced and respected as genuine craft, not diminished labor — reinforces Virgo's own conviction that maintenance work deserves the same respect as invention. Zhongshan transplants have established small Mending-House-style workshops throughout the district, a natural sibling tradition to the existing **Junction Box Galleries**.

### Neumayer (~9.6%)
The Instrument-Craft Workshops slot directly into Virgo's own precision tunnel repair and diagnostic systems training offices; Neumayer transplants bring an obsessive calibration culture that matches Virgo's meticulous documentation instinct almost exactly. Its After-Hours Sound Venues give Virgo's own working class bars and **Surface-Brightness Decompression Rooms** a specific musical inflection.

### Princess Elisabeth (~8.7%)
Princess Elisabeth's Founders' Turbine — an observance where the actual maintenance and inspection of infrastructure *is* the ceremony, with no separate symbolic layer added on top — may be the single closest one-to-one cultural match anywhere in this file to Virgo's own defining belief that the work itself, done well, is the only tribute that matters. Its small diaspora recognizes Virgo's "We Keep You Alive" posters as literally the same value system they grew up with, and its Maintenance Corps Hall tradition folds naturally into Virgo's own **Undergrid Family Registries**.

### Halley (~5.6%)
Position Day's meticulous survey-and-record culture lands in Virgo as reinforcement for the **Documentation Vaults**: Halley transplants recognize instantly that if Virgo doesn't record its own labor, nobody else will, exactly the logic that has kept Position Day's records alive as possibly the only surviving trace of Halley's own lost history.

### Mirny (~5.2%)
Mirny's Rough Bar Nights — an unpolished, function-over-comfort drinking culture built on real kinship expressed without much ceremony — merges almost invisibly into Virgo's own working class bars. Its Quarry Return Toasts, informal recognition when a crew makes it back safely from harsher routes, converges independently on the same instinct as Belgrano's Garage Nights and Casey's Junction Toasts: a district-wide pattern of small, unceremonious rituals marking that dangerous, unglamorous work went right.

### Fort McMurdo, Byrd, and Dumont d'Urville (~5.1%, ~4.3%, ~3.5% combined)
These three smaller contributors each add a distinct but minor thread rather than a full separate tradition. Fort McMurdo's Founders' Shift reinforces the Documentation Vaults' preference for results over mythology. Byrd's Foundation Keepers guild, with its structural/seismic monitoring and trained acoustic listening for ice-sheet stress, is close enough to Virgo's own tunnel-inspection expertise that Byrd transplants integrate almost without friction into the **Undergrid Family Registries**. Dumont d'Urville's naturalist observation-blind tradition — a quiet, careful, contemplative watching practice extended openly to newcomers — lends Virgo's **Surface-Brightness Decompression Rooms** an unhurried, attentive style of shared silence that wasn't quite there before.

## District 13 — Concordia Central Hub (Axis Mundi)

The Hub's diaspora composition needs to be read differently from the other twelve districts. Per the Hub's own established lore, all but one of Tepenia's outer cities send Hub residents as **overflow** — people who didn't fit their home community's own natural district and drifted to the neutral crossroads by elimination, not because the Hub itself matched their home culture. The one confirmed exception is **Sayowa**, whose own pre-war civilizational identity *was* the crossroads; it is the sole outer city that produces Hub-born, not Hub-arrived, diaspora. **Lazar** supplies well over a third of the Hub's weighted population, with **Princess Elisabeth**, **Esperanza**, and **Casey** following, and **Sayowa** closing the list at just above five percent — small in raw numbers, but structurally the most important entry on this list.

### Lazar (~41.2%)
Lazar's loud, commercially confident culture doesn't transplant into the Hub unchanged — it gets smoothed down. Junction Market Nights, Lazar's holographic-ad-heavy highway-convergence commerce, arrive diminished into something closer to the **Overflow Welcome Office**'s small merchant kiosks: neutral, transactional, catering to whoever's passing through rather than performing for a home crowd the way Lazar's own markets do. What survives with more integrity is Lazar's everyday habit of moving between the Old Core's institutional reserve and the Overflow District's commercial energy as two adjacent moods rather than a rift. That specific skill, arriving already practiced, becomes exactly the raw material the Hub trains explicitly as **multi-district cultural literacy**: Lazar transplants often pick up the Hub-born habit of professional detachment faster than anyone else, because they were already fluent in code-switching between two civic registers before they ever left home.

### Princess Elisabeth (~26.9%)
Princess Elisabeth's own dual American/Japanese demographic exchange tradition doesn't fit the Hub's thirteen-district scale, so it dilutes: the specific bicultural custom gets generalized into practice for the Hub's own multi-district cultural literacy — working fluency in thirteen districts' norms rather than two. Its Founders' Turbine ethos — maintenance-as-ceremony, with no separate symbolic layer — lands oddly well in a district whose own Bridge Memorial has never once supported an official ceremony format through council review; Princess Elisabeth transplants are unusually comfortable with commemoration that has no formal shape, since that's the only kind their home culture ever practiced.

### Esperanza (~14.8%)
Esperanza's diaspora arrives carrying an identity built by robot caregivers and teachers, not bloodlines — the actual durable carriers of "being from Hope." In the Hub, that specific place-identity has nowhere to attach, so it gets repurposed: Esperanza-descended caregivers gravitate toward the **Overflow Welcome Office**, informally orienting new arrivals much the way their own caregivers once inducted children into a home identity — except now there's no home identity to induct anyone into, just intake. The emotionally live thread of wondering whether a family name survives in the fragments of the lost Birth Registry Archive has no natural venue in the Hub either, so it has quietly attached itself to the **Bridge Memorial Reading Rooms** — a space built for an entirely different kind of loss, repurposed by Esperanza transplants because it was the closest thing available.

### Casey (~11.8%)
Casey's waystation hospitality — meals doubling as information exchange between travelers and locals — is, on its face, almost too good a fit for a transit district. But per the Hub's overflow logic, it doesn't land as genuine warmth; it professionalizes. The intimate, small-scale version becomes something closer to the Hub's own **Grand Transit Exchange People-Watching Hours**: bigger, more anonymous, stripped of the personal stakes a Casey waystation actually carried. Road Story Circles survive in form but shift in content — Casey transplants keep the habit of telling stories aloud instead of just filing them, but the material drifts from road stories toward the Hub's own **Backroom Deal Debrief Suppers**, professional gossip standing in for travelers' tales.

### Sayowa (~5.3%) — the one genuine fit, not an adaptation
Sayowa is the one entry on this list that needs no adaptation at all, and the Hub's own canon already reflects this directly — **Sayowa-Style Crossroads Mentorship** is listed as a native Hub social cohesion mechanism, not an import that had to be explained. Sayowa's home-grown cooperative, duty-oriented junction-management ethic — keeping a shared piece of infrastructure running among multiple parties who all have a stake and none of whom fully owns it — is structurally identical to what the Hub needs from its own overflow population and rarely gets, because everyone else arrived having failed to be something else first. Sayowa transplants arrived already knowing how to be a crossroads person. That confidence is exactly why the Hub's own Development Notes flag Sayowa-descended residents as candidates to become the district's most articulate theorists of its own rootless identity, rather than merely its most numerous small community.

## A note on scope

Smaller communities exist below the roughly 3–4% cutoff in every district covered here — this file follows the same convention throughout, treating them as present but too marginal for a dedicated entry. Placeholder figure names encountered in source material (the unnamed keeper of Janbogo's teahouse, Zukelli's restaurant proprietor, and others referenced only by role) were deliberately not reused as named individuals above, consistent with the standing project rule that AI-proposed Notable Figure names require re-flagging every time they resurface in new content.
