# District Refugee Diaspora Composition & Cultural Transplants

**Written 2026-07-31.** Companion piece to `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s own "Community Infrastructure & Social Life" sections (Additions / Small offices for educational training / Social cohesion mechanisms, brainstormed 2026-07-29) and to `Worldspace/Locations-and-Levels/Outside-World/Tepenian-Federation/Locations/Cities/City_Refugee_District_Affinities.md` (the source for which of Tepenia's 35 outer cities send refugees to which Concordia districts, top-3 ranked). This file goes one layer deeper: for each of Concordia's 13 districts, which outer cities its diaspora population actually traces to, roughly how large each contributing community is relative to the others, and — for the significant contributors — **specific cultural items that population would plausibly have "brought with them"**: concrete things drawn from each source city's own established Additions/Social-cohesion-mechanisms/Small-offices lists that a displaced community would recreate or adapt in its new district home, now that the original physical setting is gone.

**Cross-referenced 2026-08-02** against the 2026-07-31 Community Infrastructure expansion for Palmer City, Shirayuki, Mawson, and Lazar (see `project_community_infrastructure_expansion_pass` memory) — each of those four cities' newly-added Additions/Social Cohesion Mechanisms were checked against every district entry where that city appears as a named contributor, and folded in as additional transplant material wherever a genuine fit existed. 10 of 12 eligible entries (Mawson→Cancer/Taurus/Leo, Lazar→Cancer/Taurus/Hub, Shirayuki→Leo/Aquarius/Pisces, Palmer City→Leo) gained new material; 2 were deliberately left untouched — Palmer City→Scorpio is a grief-driven Stage 2 Override entry, not ordinary cultural affinity, so hospitality/nightlife material doesn't apply there, and Palmer City→Libra is explicitly framed as "too small for anything beyond quiet private observance," which new grand-venue material would have undercut.

## Methodology

**Weighting.** For each city, its top-3 district picks (per `City_Refugee_District_Affinities.md`'s Master Table) are weighted 1st place = 3, 2nd place = 2, 3rd place = 1, multiplied against that city's own Census I combined population (`Official_Population_Census.md` Section II — used because, unlike Census II, it covers all 35 outer cities including Kunlun, Dome Fuji, Byrd, and Vostok). The four Stage 2 Overrides (Scorpio, for the destroyed/ruined cities of Zukelli, Belgrano, Palmer City, and Casey; Gemini, for Zukelli specifically) are weighted 2 each, additive on top of each affected city's own separate Stage-1 top-3 — not a replacement or a renormalization of it. **These weighted scores are relative-ranking figures, not a literal population count** — nothing in this project establishes the exact percentage split of a city's refugees across its own top-3 picks, so treat the percentages below as "roughly this much bigger/smaller than that other contributor," not as census-grade precision.

**Selecting transplant items.** Only cities clearing roughly 3–4% of a district's total weighted score get a dedicated entry; smaller communities exist in every district but are too marginal for individual treatment here. For each significant contributor, items are drawn directly from that city's own Community Infrastructure file, filtered for genuine portability — a specific festival, craft practice, culinary tradition, or named social ritual can travel with displaced people; a rookery, a glacier, a specific highway junction, or anything else inseparable from local geography cannot. Each item is written to show how it lands and adapts inside its new district's own already-established character, not as a clean copy-paste — some transplants are a near-perfect fit (the source city's values already resembled the district's own), others are visibly adapted, softened, or made to serve a different purpose once they arrive somewhere without a dominant culture already primed to receive them.

**Two special cases.** Scorpio's four largest contributing cities (Casey, Zukelli, Belgrano, Palmer City) are there via the Stage 2 Override, not ordinary cultural affinity — the district's entry below treats them accordingly, as an account of how each community's own specific character of loss is shaping Scorpio's institutions from the inside, not a festival-and-craft list. The Hub's diaspora is, per its own established lore, almost entirely "overflow" — people who didn't fit their home district and drifted to the neutral crossroads by elimination — except for Sayowa, the one outer city whose own pre-war culture *was* the crossroads; the Hub's entry below treats Sayowa's transplants as a natural, confident fit and the other three as adapted-to-neutrality imports.

## District 1 — Cancer (The Sanctuary)

Cancer's outer-city diaspora is dominated by a cluster of caregiving-, hospitality-, and memory-centered cities: roughly a quarter traces to **Esperanza**, close behind it **Mawson** at just over a fifth (rolling in the small, undedicated Palmer City/Port Lockroy/Dome Fuji tail below), **Zukelli** at around one-sixth, **Cape Adare** around 13%, **Lazar** at roughly 11%, **Abowasa** at 9%, and a smaller but still meaningful thread from **Scott** at just over 3%. Smaller communities from Palmer City, Port Lockroy, and Dome Fuji exist within the district too, but at well under 2% each they're too marginal for dedicated treatment here.

### Esperanza (~24.6%)
Esperanza is destroyed, and its diaspora carries two things that land almost frictionlessly in a district built entirely around caregiving and grief. The **Caregiver-Teacher Halls** — Esperanza's long-lived robot caregivers and teachers who raised generations of the city's children, the actual durable carriers of "being from Hope" identity — fold naturally into Cancer's existing **Paired Creches**. Esperanza-descended caregiving robots, already practiced at holding a dislocated population's continuity when human record-keeping fails, become sought-after teaching presences within Cancer's neighborhood nurseries, no reinvention required. Separately, the ongoing practice of diaspora members asking whether specific family names survive in the fragments of the **Birth Registry Archive** merges into Cancer's **open-air memorial circles**: Esperanza-descended residents use the circles' voice-note/token ritual specifically to search for lost family names, giving an existing Cancer mechanism a distinct, recognizable Esperanza inflection. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Esperanza's Founding Birth Anniversary — January 7th, marking the 1978 first-birth record the entire founding compact was built around — attaches quietly to Cancer's Paired Creches: Esperanza-descended households and caregiving robots use the date each year to informally trade notes on the newest human infants and freshly-activated robots being raised side by side, a fitting echo of a holiday built around a single, sacred first birth.

### Mawson (~22.0%)
Mawson's identity is built on hospitality — specifically the honeymoon-destination culture around a mountain-overlook walk undertaken by newlywed couples regardless of season. The geography obviously doesn't travel, but the ritual shape of it does: Mawson-descended households recast it as a "First Walk," a newlywed or newly-reunited-family procession through Cancer's **quiet "listening gardens,"** folded into the district's existing **Open Door Hour** custom of signaling welcome. It reads as a graft rather than an invention — Cancer already prizes gentle, curved, non-institutional spaces for exactly this kind of tender threshold moment, so Mawson's arriving families found a home for the custom almost by accident. The Welcome Plaza's own civic instinct — a dedicated public space built entirely around greeting whoever arrives — reinforces the same graft from the other direction: Mawson-descended residents don't just recreate the honeymoon walk, they bring an entire hospitality-first orientation that treats Open Door Hour less as one custom among many and more as the whole point of a home. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Mawson's Unbroken Watch — an observance of the city's own uninterrupted operational continuity — merges easily into Cancer's Habit-Keeping Evenings: Mawson-descended families and companions extend the same continuity instinct from a city's operational record to a household's preserved routines, treating an unbroken chain of care for the departed as their own small-scale version of a watch that has simply never stopped. The Welcome Feast — Mawson's own standing custom of greeting new arrivals and honeymooning couples alike with a shared meal, treating both as fundamentally the same kind of guest — folds directly into Open Door Hour's existing signaling-of-welcome function, giving Mawson-descended households a specific, food-centered ritual to enact what the custom already gestures toward.

### Zukelli (~16.5%)
Zukelli is destroyed, and its diaspora's grief is sharper than most — many watched the city's destruction happen from across the water in Janbogo before eventually settling further into Cancer. Zukelli's own famous restaurant-and-performance-space model, cited as the city's model for its whole food-and-music-together hospitality tradition, resurfaces inside Cancer's **Broth Nights**: a recurring venue-night that blends the potluck format with live, informal music performance, functioning less as pure entertainment and more as a running memorial to a lost restaurant culture — squarely in keeping with Cancer's stated belief that "the dead are maintained," extended here to a dead city's civic life rather than an individual. The broader idea of **communal meals as the absolute center of civic life** reinforces Cancer's existing communal broth kitchens directly, with a recognizable "Zukelli table" becoming a known fixture within them. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** The pre-war "Crossing" tradition with Janbogo — informal visits that blurred who was hosting whom — resurfaces among Zukelli-descended residents as an unofficial mutual check-in custom layered onto Cancer's Open Door Hour, particularly among households who share the specific trauma of having watched the city burn from across the water; the visiting no longer crosses a strait, but the instinct to simply show up on someone who shares your grief's doorstep, unannounced, survives intact.

### Cape Adare (~13.1%)
Cape Adare is destroyed too, but unlike Zukelli its core cultural export is documentary rather than culinary: the **Heritage Documentation Archive**, where centuries of preservation work on Borchgrevink's Hut survived even though the hut itself did not. That instinct — that a structure can be lost while its record endures — slots directly into Cancer's **Second Chair Workshops** and open-air memorial circles, with Cape Adare-descended residents specifically known for treating family and city history documentation with the same rigor their ancestors gave the hut. Separately, Cape Adare's arrival/departure meal rituals — a gateway city's own version of hospitality, marking comings and goings rather than routine sustenance — invert neatly once the diaspora reaches Cancer: having once staffed the entry point *to* Antarctica, they now staff a version of the entry point *into* a new home, folding the custom into Cancer's **Open Door Hour**. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** St. Carsten's Landing — February 17th, marking the day the first deliberate overwintering on the continent began — finds a natural home in Cancer's open-air memorial circles: Cape Adare-descended residents treat it as an annual occasion to formally add names and tokens honoring the city's own lost heritage-keepers, extending a saint's-day observance built around endurance into the district's broader vocabulary for maintained memory.

### Lazar (~11.4%)
Lazar's defining civic story is **Coalescence Day**, commemorating the historical merger of two founding settlements into one city. That "many becoming one" narrative maps directly onto Cancer's own identity as an integration zone built to absorb disparate, traumatized populations into a single home — Lazar-descended households treat the day as a template rather than a foreign import, quietly attaching it to Cancer's **Habit-Keeping Evenings** as an annual note on how blended households came to be blended in the first place. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Lazar's Cross-District Moods — the ordinary daily movement between the Old Core's institutional reserve and the Overflow District's commercial energy, experienced as two adjacent moods rather than a rift — offers Lazar-descended households in Cancer a ready-made framework for something the district needs constantly: holding grief and ordinary life as two adjacent moods within the same day rather than a contradiction, reinforcing Cancer's own hard-won belief that comforting someone and simply getting on with the day can coexist without either one being false. Old Core Archive Reading Days — Lazar's own scheduled tradition of residents coming to learn their specific neighborhood's history rather than only the city's headline founding story — reinforces Cancer's open-air memorial circles from an unexpected angle: Lazar-descended residents bring an institutional habit of treating "which specific street, which specific household" as worth formally remembering, not just the big Coalescence Day narrative, a granularity of memory-keeping Cancer's own circles already value.

### Abowasa (~9.0%)
Abowasa's **Household Suppers** — the central social mechanism carrying the community's own daily give-and-take between two originally separate founding programs — merge into Cancer's communal broth kitchens without much adaptation needed. The transplant is smooth because the underlying logic is identical: shared food is how two formerly separate groups become one group, which is exactly what Cancer's entire district exists to accomplish for its refugee population. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Abowasa's Neighborhood Music Nights — small household and neighborhood gatherings keeping the city's shared folk tradition alive — graft onto Cancer's own Lullaby Nights with almost no adjustment: Abowasa-descended families simply widen the audience for what was already an intimate, caregiving-adjacent musical custom, and Cancer's recognized lullaby-masters have started quietly absorbing a few Abowasa tunes into the shared civic repertoire.

### Scott (~3.4%)
Scott's contribution is small but strikingly on-the-nose: the Hut Point Remembrance, where residents of Scott and Fort McMurdo gather, unmandated, to lay candles and personal tokens in memory of St. Robert. Scott-descended residents arriving in Cancer recognize the district's own open-air memorial circles almost immediately as the same kind of ritual, and layer their St. Robert observance onto the existing practice as a specific, minor annual addition rather than a separate institution. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Scott's informal border crossing — the practically porous, unmandated line between Scott and Fort McMurdo treated as an everyday social fact — lands almost invisibly in Cancer, whose own cultural expectation that neighbors have unlocked access to each other's homes already normalizes exactly this kind of casual boundary-crossing; Scott-descended residents don't so much introduce the custom as simply recognize it, one more confirmation that Cancer was already primed to receive them.

## District 2 — Taurus

Taurus draws the largest and most varied outer-diaspora among the districts covered by this file. **Lazar** alone accounts for roughly a quarter of it, with **Cape Adare** (~15%) and **Mirny** (~13.5%) close behind, followed by **Abowasa** (~10%) and **Mawson** (~10%), then a second tier of **Davis** (~8%), **Zhongshan** (~6%), **Zukelli** (~4%), **Scott** (~4%), and **Sanay** (~3%). Port Lockroy and Signy contribute smaller threads below the 3% cutoff and aren't treated individually.

### Lazar (~26.3%)
Two Lazar institutions transplant well here, both tied to continuity and record-keeping — Taurus's specialty. Coalescence Day, the merger-of-two-settlements observance, becomes a natural addition to Taurus's own **Genealogy Registry Halls**: Lazar-descended families with roots in both the Old Core and the Overflow District add their merger story as a documented household chapter, the same institutional impulse Taurus already applies to robot service lines. The Unbroken Watch, Lazar's observance of its own continuous operational record, merges into Taurus's **Anniversary of Service Ceremonies** — Lazar households extend the ceremony's existing robot-activation framework to cover household and neighborhood continuity milestones as well. The Old Core Archive Halls — Lazar's own dedicated historical-records institution, distinct from its Unbroken Operations Hall's narrower operational focus — is close to a native fit for the Genealogy Registry Halls: Lazar-descended archivists arrive already practiced at treating full civic and neighborhood-level history, not just service records, as worth formally preserving, giving the Halls a genuine specialty in exactly the broader documentation Taurus's own culture already reveres. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Junction Market Nights — the recurring commercial-and-social gatherings held at Lazar's own highway-convergence markets — arrive in Taurus considerably quieter than they left home: Lazar-descended households fold the impulse into an expanded, livelier version of the Doorstep Tea Exchange, since Taurus's strict noise ordinances won't tolerate the original's full holographic-advertisement clamor, leaving a recognizably Lazar-flavored but noticeably subdued version of the custom. Sector Nights — Lazar's own neighborhood-scale answer to the fact that a megacity can't realistically share one citywide market rhythm — actually transplants more comfortably than its citywide parent: a smaller, quieter, sector-scale gathering is a closer match to Taurus's noise-conscious culture from the start, and some Lazar-descended households favor recreating Sector Nights specifically over the louder Junction Market Nights for exactly that reason.

### Cape Adare (~15.0%)
Cape Adare's Heritage Documentation Archive — the instinct to formally preserve an object's or a structure's full history even after the structure is gone — reinforces Taurus's own **Heirloom Provenance Offices** almost exactly, giving that office a recognizable Cape Adare-descended specialty in exhaustive documentation. Separately, Cape Adare's acoustic folk music gatherings, unhurried, unamplified, and "belonging to everyone equally," fold into Taurus's **Quiet Hour Storytelling** — both traditions are built around restrained, intimate performance inside a culture with real noise limits, so the fit needs almost no adjustment. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Cape Adare's arrival/departure meal rituals — a gateway city's own hospitality tradition marking comings and goings rather than daily sustenance — complete something Taurus's porch-circle culture already half-does: Taurus already answers every door and produces tea for arrivals, but rarely marks a guest's departure the same way, so Cape Adare-descended households become known specifically for never letting a visitor leave without something to eat, filling in the half of the ritual Taurus's own hospitality culture was missing.

### Mirny (~13.5%)
Mirny's Dual-Table Suppers — Russian and Chinese dishes served side by side, deliberately kept distinct rather than fused, per Mirny's own "coexistence, not fusion" ethos — land inside Taurus's **Newcomer Welcome Kitchens**, where longtime residents already teach new arrivals food tradition. Mirny-descended households become known within that institution specifically for maintaining two visibly separate culinary lines under one roof, rather than blending into Taurus's usual single-tradition household cooking — a distinguishable variant within an existing structure rather than a wholesale replacement. More loosely, the reality-tested rigor of Mirny's windwright apprentice trials — work judged against the actual wind, not a panel's opinion — echoes in Taurus's **Household Insulation Ateliers**, where Mirny-descended instructors apply a similarly exacting, judged-against-outcome standard to insulation apprenticeship. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Mirny's Quarry Return Toasts — informal recognition when a crew makes it back safely from the harsher southern extraction routes — fold into an expanded version of the Doorstep Tea Exchange: Mirny-descended households add a small toast whenever a family member returns from dangerous or extended work, a natural extension of Taurus's own genealogical seriousness about who left, who came back, and what it cost them.

### Abowasa (~10.4%)
Abowasa's Joint Founding observance sits comfortably alongside Lazar's Coalescence Day inside Taurus's Genealogy Registry Halls, reinforcing the Halls' function as a place where blended-origin households of several different kinds get formally recorded rather than treated as anomalies. Its Household Suppers tradition folds into Taurus's own **Long Stew Rotation**, with Abowasa-descended households contributing pots that carry the same "originally separate, now inseparable" story their ancestors told about the city's two founding programs. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Abowasa's Neighborhood Music Nights fare less smoothly here than in Cancer: Taurus's fierce noise ordinances mean the shared folk tradition survives only in a hushed, doorway-adjacent form, absorbed into Quiet Hour Storytelling's already-established habit of performance pitched low enough that only those near the threshold can hear — a genuine adaptation rather than a clean transplant, audible proof that Abowasa's communal instincts and Taurus's quiet-hour discipline don't fully agree.

### Mawson (~9.7%)
Mawson's honeymoon-walk custom finds an unusually apt structural home in Taurus specifically because Taurus's earliest architecture was deliberately engineered for bonded human-robot pairs who chose to remain on the surface, with layouts prioritizing family units and private courtyards. Mawson-descended households transplant the walk as a "First Walk" through those same private courtyards for newly bonded pairs settling into a Taurus household — a rare case where the destination district's own founding design already anticipated the exact need the transplant fills. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Mawson's Unbroken Watch — an observance of the city's own uninterrupted operational continuity — merges into Taurus's own Anniversary of Service Ceremonies alongside Lazar's version of the same custom above; Mawson-descended households extend the same continuity instinct specifically to newly bonded pairs, treating a couple's own unbroken future together as a household-scale echo of a city's operational record that has simply never stopped. Anniversary Returns — Mawson's own custom of honeymooning couples informally returning for milestone anniversaries, treated as minor local celebrities when they do — gives that same Anniversary of Service Ceremonies tradition a second, genuinely native-feeling wing: Mawson-descended households don't just borrow Taurus's genealogical seriousness about milestones, they arrive already practiced at formally marking them.

### Davis (~7.7%)
Davis is the one clear friction case in Taurus. Its Quiet Credit — an informal practice of pointedly acknowledging team members' uncredited contributions, part of Davis's triple-reinforced "enabler, not protagonist" identity — sits awkwardly against Taurus's more overtly proud heirloom-display culture (Heirloom Show-and-Tell Evenings exist specifically to showcase, not downplay). Davis-descended households still push the practice into Taurus's Genealogy Registry Halls, formally noting which family member or household robot quietly kept things running, but it reads as a deliberate, slightly foreign correction to Taurus's habits rather than a natural fit — exactly the kind of effortful transplant a less naturally hospitable cultural landing produces. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Not every Davis custom lands as awkwardly as the Quiet Credit. Harvest Quiet — the deliberately unshowy seasonal acknowledgment of the greenhouse harvest feeding the wider Federation — threads quietly into the existing Doorstep Tea Exchange hours, one of the only moments in Taurus's calendar genuinely built around understatement rather than display, giving Davis-descended households at least one custom that needs no correction at all.

### Zhongshan (~6.2%, rolling in the small, undedicated Port Lockroy/Signy tail below)
Zhongshan's Dumpling Arguments — "a low-stakes outlet in a culture that otherwise doesn't argue in public" — merge easily into Taurus's already-established, self-consciously superior cuisine culture, giving households a specific, named venue for culinary pride disputes that Taurus's stubborn self-regard was already primed to enjoy. The Mending Houses garment-repair tradition folds directly into Taurus's existing heirloom-repair co-ops as a companion craft: garment lineage alongside furniture lineage. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Zhongshan's Tea Ceremony Suppers — the unhurried, long-meal tradition where care is expressed through preparation rather than words — slot almost seamlessly into Taurus's own extraordinary preserved-food culture: Zhongshan-descended households become known within the Preserve Cellars and Fermentation Vaults for the same patient, quietly expressive approach to a shared meal, a value Taurus already holds as central to what makes its cuisine the best in the city.

### Zukelli (~4.2%)
Zukelli's pre-war "Crossing" tradition — informal visits with Janbogo that blurred who was hosting whom — maps almost exactly onto Taurus's own **Unannounced Visit Circuits**, where households take informal turns being "the stop" for a given week's drop-ins. Zukelli-descended residents graft their version on with minimal adjustment; the underlying social logic (mutual, informal, host-role-blurring visiting) is already identical. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Zukelli's habit of treating music and food as adjacent expressions of the same hospitality instinct arrives as a genuinely foreign note inside the Newcomer Welcome Kitchens, where food instruction is normally a quiet, single-tradition affair; Zukelli-descended teachers now layer soft, unamplified accompaniment into cooking lessons, a livelier touch that Taurus's noise ordinances only barely accommodate and that longtime residents still regard as a distinctly Zukelli flourish rather than house style.

### Scott (~3.9%)
Scott's contribution here is narrower and craft-based rather than ritual-based: its precision equipment calibration and maintenance training folds into Taurus's own structural-maintenance education for multi-generational houses, with Scott-descended residents known for bringing an unusually exacting, calibration-grade standard to ordinary household upkeep — a small but recognizable specialty rather than a new institution. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** The Hut Point Remembrance travels into Taurus mostly as a private, household-scale gesture rather than a district event: Scott-descended families fold a small candle-and-token observance for St. Robert into their own heirloom culture, placing the tokens beside the specific chair or object that already carries a lost family member's presence, so the saint's-day tradition reads less like an import and more like an extension of a value — that specific objects hold specific people — Taurus already lives by.

### Sanay (~3.1%)
Sanay's own quiet, deliberately understated observance of continued function after the Long Night War introduces a quiet counter-note inside Taurus's otherwise proud "stay-put" festivals. It's a minority current, not a replacement: a small number of Sanay-descended households mark their own persistence privately rather than festively, a modest variation that stands out precisely because it declines the celebratory tone Taurus's stay-put culture usually assumes. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Sanay's Founders' Bedrock — commemorating the founding generation's decision to build directly into stable rock — resonates unusually well with Taurus's household-scale insulation expertise: Sanay-descended residents within the Household Insulation Ateliers frame their structural know-how as a direct inheritance of that same founding instinct, one of the few Sanay customs that reinforces Taurus's own "stay-put" pride rather than quietly declining it, in contrast to the more understated Standing observance already noted above.

## District 3 — Leo

Nearly a quarter of Leo's outer-diaspora population traces to **Casey** (23.9%), with **Shirayuki** close behind at almost a fifth (18.8%). **Janbogo (the city)** and **Zukelli** each contribute around a seventh (13.9% and 13.4%), **Mawson** and **Dumont d'Urville** each around a fifteenth (7.7% and 7.2%), and smaller but still meaningful communities descend from **Denison** (5.7%), **Palmer City** (5.3%), and **Juan Carlos** (4.1%).

### Casey (~23.9%)
Splinters — Tepenia's largest, most famous bar, already known well beyond Casey's own borders — gives its diaspora every reason to recreate it rather than let the name fade into a ruin's memory. In Leo, the ambition is less about physical scale and more about reviving Splinters Set Nights' genre-spanning format (Classic Rock, Jazz, Blues, Acoustic Folk rotating through one stage) inside the district's own open-mic amphitheaters — a natural fit for a district with no audience/performer distinction to begin with. Junction Toasts, Casey's understated recognition for a dispatcher or driver who got someone through danger, filter into Leo's own tiered recognition economy at a noticeably lower register than the district is used to: Casey's diaspora carries "quiet, non-demonstrative reticence even into loss" even here, so its toasts stay small and private, a muted counter-current running alongside Leo's more performative status theater rather than folding cleanly into it. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Casey's Road Story Circles — the informal oral tradition of travelers' tales swapped at waystations — surface in Leo as a spoken-word undercurrent inside the open-mic amphitheaters rather than a marquee act: unhurried, conversational storytelling slotted between musical sets, matching the diaspora's own quiet register rather than competing for Leo's brightest stage time.

### Shirayuki (~18.8%)
Glacier-Pop Living Rooms — the informal, friends-and-neighbors performance spaces where Shirayuki's massive amateur music scene actually happens — map almost exactly onto Leo's own **Understudy Halls**, the district's existing proving-ground venues for not-yet-recognized performers; the two traditions likely merge rather than compete. The Ashiato Ateliers, where residents assemble and personalize homemade garments, slot directly into Leo's own "basic costume & personal-brand augmentation" training offices, giving that existing entry a specific craft identity it lacked. Institute Placement Nights, the mentorship gatherings connecting graduating students into performance circles, find a home inside Leo's **Lineage Houses** — giving the district's blood-lineage-style artistic seriousness a second, parallel credentialing track through formal instruction. The Battle Halls are arguably the single most natural Shirayuki transplant anywhere in this file: a venue built specifically for direct, competitive performer-vs-performer showcases lands inside a district that already runs on tiered public recognition and status theater as though it had been designed for Leo from the start, rather than adapted to fit it. Street Corner Stages fill a real gap in the district's own pipeline, too — Understudy Halls assume a performer ready to be watched, but Leo previously had no equivalent to a genuinely lower-stakes first stage; Shirayuki transplants bring one, and Corner Stage Debuts becomes the honest starting point Leo's own recognition economy never had before. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Ashiato Trade Circles — the informal gatherings where residents trade, alter, and pass along homemade garments — layer naturally onto Leo's own basic costume & personal-brand augmentation training offices, turning what's already a functional craft pipeline into a recurring social occasion, with garments changing hands as much for status-signaling as for fit. Battle Nights, the competitive counterpart to Glacier-Pop's own house shows, reinforces the Understudy Halls transplant directly: Shirayuki-descended performers bring genuine rivalry and reputation-keeping instincts that Leo's own status-conscious culture absorbs immediately, no adaptation required.

### Janbogo, the city (~13.9%)
Majyao's Original Teahouse is the most direct precedent of all Leo's contributors — its own keeper is already established as having relocated to Concordia, with the original site preserved as civic memory back home. That's effectively a transplant story Janbogo's own file has half-written already; a recreated teahouse in Leo plausibly becomes exactly the kind of small, personally-run venue that climbs Leo's recognition ladder over a generation. Janbogo's teaware & coffee craft ateliers likely persist as a boutique craft supplying Leo's existing bars and bistros. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Teahouse Story Circles — Janbogo's oral tradition of swapping anecdotes about specific teahouses, hosts, and small kindnesses during hard winters — transplant naturally into a recreated teahouse's back room, giving Leo's own storytelling culture a distinctly domestic, unhurried register that stands out against the district's louder performance venues.

### Zukelli (~13.4%)
Zukelli's famous restaurant-and-performance-space — a dedicated stage built directly into a restaurant — is close to a plug-and-play fit for a district that already runs bars and bistros as performance venues and already blurs performer and audience. A recreated version emerges less as memorial and more as a functioning restaurant-with-stage in Leo's own idiom. The pre-war "Crossing" tradition (informal visits blurring who was hosting whom) has no geography left to anchor it, but the underlying instinct — easy, unannounced visiting — plausibly softens Leo's more status-conscious **Golden Ring Walks** at the edges. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Zukelli's own defining civic instinct — communal meals as the absolute center of social life, food and music treated as adjacent expressions of the same hospitality impulse — reinforces rather than merely echoes the restaurant-with-stage transplant above; Leo's bars and bistros already blur the same line, so Zukelli's diaspora reads less like newcomers adapting and more like a community that already belonged there.

### Mawson (~7.7%)
The honeymoon-overlook pilgrimage can't travel intact — Leo has no mountain view. But the underlying practice, a pilgrimage timed to a dramatic light event, has an obvious substitute in a district whose "reverence for light is not metaphorical" and whose Dimming Vigil already treats controlled light and dark as sacred. Mawson honeymooners in Leo plausibly redirect the trip toward the **Candlelight Halls** — giving Mawson's honeymoon-destination identity a ready-made new venue for the exact kind of occasion it already built its civic reputation on. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** The Unbroken Watch, Mawson's observance of its own longest-of-any-subnet-city continuous operation, resurfaces in Leo as an informal honor paid to the district's own longest-running acts and unbroken mentor lineages — a natural graft onto Leo's Mentor-Lineage Reunions, though Mawson's honorees are civic institutions and Leo's are individual careers, so the borrowed observance reads as visibly repurposed rather than a clean transplant. Anniversary Returns, Mawson's own custom of treating returning honeymoon couples as minor local celebrities, is a more natural fit for Leo than anywhere else this diaspora contributor lands: a district that already runs on a tiered public-recognition economy has an immediate, unforced place for "notable local figures who return periodically to be recognized," and Mawson-descended households find the custom needs almost no translation at all.

### Dumont d'Urville (~7.2%)

Zhongshan transplants have established small Mending-House-style workshops throughout the district, a natural sibling tradition to the existing **Junction Box Galleries**. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Standing Stone Gatherings, informal community meetups around Zhongshan's own bedrock outcrop, echo naturally in Virgo's tunnel-adjacent junctions — Zhongshan transplants gravitate toward impromptu meetups at the district's own load-bearing structural points, gathering where the rock holds the weight.

### Neumayer (~9.6%)
The Instrument-Craft Workshops slot directly into Virgo's own precision tunnel repair and diagnostic systems training offices; Neumayer transplants bring an obsessive calibration culture that matches Virgo's meticulous documentation instinct almost exactly. Its After-Hours Sound Venues give Virgo's own working class bars and **Surface-Brightness Decompression Rooms** a specific musical inflection. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Founders' Instrument, commemorating the decision to treat inherited equipment as an ongoing mission rather than a relic, needs no adaptation in Virgo — it reads as simply one more articulation of "We Keep You Alive," and Neumayer transplants apply the same instinct to the district's own Documentation Vaults.

### Princess Elisabeth (~8.7%)
Princess Elisabeth's Founders' Turbine — an observance where the actual maintenance and inspection of infrastructure *is* the ceremony, with no separate symbolic layer added on top — may be the single closest one-to-one cultural match anywhere in this file to Virgo's own defining belief that the work itself, done well, is the only tribute that matters. Its small diaspora recognizes Virgo's "We Keep You Alive" posters as literally the same value system they grew up with, and its Maintenance Corps Hall tradition folds naturally into Virgo's own **Undergrid Family Registries**. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** The Crossing, marking Princess Elisabeth's dual American/Japanese demographic character, has almost no natural anchor in Virgo's more uniform labor culture — it survives thinly, mostly as a private family custom among the small diaspora rather than a district-wide practice.

### Halley (~5.6%)
Position Day's meticulous survey-and-record culture lands in Virgo as reinforcement for the **Documentation Vaults**: Halley transplants recognize instantly that if Virgo doesn't record its own labor, nobody else will, exactly the logic that has kept Position Day's records alive as possibly the only surviving trace of Halley's own lost history. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Old Position Photography Walks, documenting an abandoned position before the sea claims it, extend naturally into Virgo's own Documentation Vaults — Halley transplants photograph the district's decommissioned tunnel sections the same way, recording what's being abandoned before it's lost for good.

### Mirny (~5.2%)
Mirny's Rough Bar Nights — an unpolished, function-over-comfort drinking culture built on real kinship expressed without much ceremony — merges almost invisibly into Virgo's own working class bars. Its Quarry Return Toasts, informal recognition when a crew makes it back safely from harsher routes, converges independently on the same instinct as Belgrano's Garage Nights and Casey's Junction Toasts: a district-wide pattern of small, unceremonious rituals marking that dangerous, unglamorous work went right. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Relay Room Vigils, quiet gatherings near relay hardware during any stretch when the subnet connection is genuinely at risk, mirror Virgo's own culture of watching over critical infrastructure under duress — Mirny transplants fold naturally into the district's tunnel-monitoring crews during genuine emergencies.

### Fort McMurdo, Byrd, and Dumont d'Urville (~5.1%, ~4.3%, ~3.5% combined)
These three smaller contributors each add a distinct but minor thread rather than a full separate tradition. Fort McMurdo's Founders' Shift reinforces the Documentation Vaults' preference for results over mythology. Byrd's Foundation Keepers guild, with its structural/seismic monitoring and trained acoustic listening for ice-sheet stress, is close enough to Virgo's own tunnel-inspection expertise that Byrd transplants integrate almost without friction into the **Undergrid Family Registries**. Dumont d'Urville's naturalist observation-blind tradition — a quiet, careful, contemplative watching practice extended openly to newcomers — lends Virgo's **Surface-Brightness Decompression Rooms** an unhurried, attentive style of shared silence that wasn't quite there before. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Fort McMurdo's Founders' Shift already covers that community above; new to this trio, Byrd's St. Richard's Marker — the cross-guild ritual for facing a call nobody can yet be certain will hold — resonates with Virgo's own daily exposure to structural risk, and Dumont d'Urville's St. Jules Day, the public outdoor reading of surviving journal fragments held in the wind deliberately, gives Virgo's Documentation Reading Nights a windswept, ceremonial counterpart it didn't have before.

## District 13 — Concordia Central Hub (Axis Mundi)

The Hub's diaspora composition needs to be read differently from the other twelve districts. Per the Hub's own established lore, all but one of Tepenia's outer cities send Hub residents as **overflow** — people who didn't fit their home community's own natural district and drifted to the neutral crossroads by elimination, not because the Hub itself matched their home culture. The one confirmed exception is **Sayowa**, whose own pre-war civilizational identity *was* the crossroads; it is the sole outer city that produces Hub-born, not Hub-arrived, diaspora. **Lazar** supplies well over a third of the Hub's weighted population, with **Princess Elisabeth**, **Esperanza**, and **Casey** following, and **Sayowa** closing the list at just above five percent — small in raw numbers, but structurally the most important entry on this list.

### Lazar (~41.2%)
Lazar's loud, commercially confident culture doesn't transplant into the Hub unchanged — it gets smoothed down. Junction Market Nights, Lazar's holographic-ad-heavy highway-convergence commerce, arrive diminished into something closer to the **Overflow Welcome Office**'s small merchant kiosks: neutral, transactional, catering to whoever's passing through rather than performing for a home crowd the way Lazar's own markets do. What survives with more integrity is Lazar's everyday habit of moving between the Old Core's institutional reserve and the Overflow District's commercial energy as two adjacent moods rather than a rift. That specific skill, arriving already practiced, becomes exactly the raw material the Hub trains explicitly as **multi-district cultural literacy**: Lazar transplants often pick up the Hub-born habit of professional detachment faster than anyone else, because they were already fluent in code-switching between two civic registers before they ever left home. The single cleanest transplant in this entire file belongs here, though: the **Transit Spine**, Lazar's own internal rapid-transit network and the small, genuine social world — regular faces, familiar routines, unofficial etiquette — that grew up around commuting through a megacity too large to cross on foot, lands inside the Hub's own **Grand Transit Exchange** as something closer to coming home than adapting. Lazar-descended residents don't just use the Grand Transit Exchange, they recognize it; the Hub's own transit-hub identity is the one thing about the district that needed no explanation at all for people who spent their whole lives navigating Tepenia's single largest transit network. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** The Unbroken Watch, Lazar's observance of its own continuous operational record, cannot survive intact in a district defined by everyone's home continuity having been broken by arrival there — it dilutes into something more private and generalized, Lazar transplants marking their own personal streak of "still standing" rather than the city's, a quieter echo of a proud civic record with no civic body left to attach to. Transit Spine Commuter Culture fares far better: its own unofficial etiquette — the small, unspoken rules of shared movement through a space nobody owns — transplants into the Grand Transit Exchange's own people-watching hours almost without adjustment, giving Lazar transplants a genuine, functioning social world inside the Hub's transit infrastructure rather than the diminished, hollowed-out version most of their other customs become here.

### Princess Elisabeth (~26.9%)
Princess Elisabeth's own dual American/Japanese demographic exchange tradition doesn't fit the Hub's thirteen-district scale, so it dilutes: the specific bicultural custom gets generalized into practice for the Hub's own multi-district cultural literacy — working fluency in thirteen districts' norms rather than two. Its Founders' Turbine ethos — maintenance-as-ceremony, with no separate symbolic layer — lands oddly well in a district whose own Bridge Memorial has never once supported an official ceremony format through council review; Princess Elisabeth transplants are unusually comfortable with commemoration that has no formal shape, since that's the only kind their home culture ever practiced. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Founders' Turbine, already the anchor of the fit described above, is worth naming explicitly as the mechanism itself — it survives the Hub's diminishing effect better than most imports precisely because it requires no audience or civic body to keep its meaning, only work done well, which the Hub's transient population can still reliably provide.

### Esperanza (~14.8%)
Esperanza's diaspora arrives carrying an identity built by robot caregivers and teachers, not bloodlines — the actual durable carriers of "being from Hope." In the Hub, that specific place-identity has nowhere to attach, so it gets repurposed: Esperanza-descended caregivers gravitate toward the **Overflow Welcome Office**, informally orienting new arrivals much the way their own caregivers once inducted children into a home identity — except now there's no home identity to induct anyone into, just intake. The emotionally live thread of wondering whether a family name survives in the fragments of the lost Birth Registry Archive has no natural venue in the Hub either, so it has quietly attached itself to the **Bridge Memorial Reading Rooms** — a space built for an entirely different kind of loss, repurposed by Esperanza transplants because it was the closest thing available. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** the unwritten founding compact — historically reinforced through oral tradition and lived practice rather than a formal document — has nothing left to attach to in the Hub, so it thins into instinct: Esperanza-descended caregivers still orient new arrivals through relationship and unwritten trust rather than paperwork, and that informal, relationship-first style is what people actually remember about the Overflow Welcome Office, even though intake there is otherwise pure procedure.

### Casey (~11.8%)
Casey's waystation hospitality — meals doubling as information exchange between travelers and locals — is, on its face, almost too good a fit for a transit district. But per the Hub's overflow logic, it doesn't land as genuine warmth; it professionalizes. The intimate, small-scale version becomes something closer to the Hub's own **Grand Transit Exchange People-Watching Hours**: bigger, more anonymous, stripped of the personal stakes a Casey waystation actually carried. Road Story Circles survive in form but shift in content — Casey transplants keep the habit of telling stories aloud instead of just filing them, but the material drifts from road stories toward the Hub's own **Backroom Deal Debrief Suppers**, professional gossip standing in for travelers' tales. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Junction Toasts, informal recognition for a dispatcher or driver who got someone through real danger safely, have no genuine object in the Hub — nobody here crosses anything as literally dangerous as a Casey wind window. The ritual survives only as a hollowed-out phrase, "made the crossing," used sardonically by Casey transplants working the Grand Transit Exchange desks about surviving an unusually chaotic shift — the shape of the toast kept, its original stakes gone.

### Sayowa (~5.3%) — the one genuine fit, not an adaptation
Sayowa is the one entry on this list that needs no adaptation at all, and the Hub's own canon already reflects this directly — **Sayowa-Style Crossroads Mentorship** is listed as a native Hub social cohesion mechanism, not an import that had to be explained. Sayowa's home-grown cooperative, duty-oriented junction-management ethic — keeping a shared piece of infrastructure running among multiple parties who all have a stake and none of whom fully owns it — is structurally identical to what the Hub needs from its own overflow population and rarely gets, because everyone else arrived having failed to be something else first. Sayowa transplants arrived already knowing how to be a crossroads person. That confidence is exactly why the Hub's own Development Notes flag Sayowa-descended residents as candidates to become the district's most articulate theorists of its own rootless identity, rather than merely its most numerous small community. **Social Cohesion Mechanism:** Sayowa's own Crossing observance — marking its role as the point where the Mawson subnet, Concordia, and the Halley subnet all meet — needs no translation at all inside the Hub; it reads as a more natural civic anniversary for the Hub itself than anything the district has managed to establish on its own, and Sayowa transplants are already informally treating it as a de facto Hub-wide observance, unmodified.

## A note on scope

Smaller communities exist below the roughly 3–4% cutoff in every district covered here — this file follows the same convention throughout, treating them as present but too marginal for a dedicated entry. Placeholder figure names encountered in source material (the unnamed keeper of Janbogo's teahouse, Zukelli's restaurant proprietor, and others referenced only by role) were deliberately not reused as named individuals above, consistent with the standing project rule that AI-proposed Notable Figure names require re-flagging every time they resurface in new content.
