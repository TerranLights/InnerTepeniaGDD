# Cancer (Pre-War) — Mega-Init

**Step 1 of the Pre-War District Megasheet compilation process.** Companion document to the existing,
post-war `District_Megasheets/01_Cancer/Cancer_Mega_Init.md` — not a replacement for it. Reconstructs
Cancer as it stood during the Second Interwar Period (roughly 2564–2812), when Tepenia was a full,
functioning country, before the Long Night War. Synthesizes `Deep_Dives/01_Cancer_Deep_Dive.md`'s own
Stage 1 finding — that Cancer's founding population was traumatized by the *original exile*, not the
Long Night War, which was still 250 years in the district's future — and extends that finding into its
own full pre-war treatment. Compiled 2026-07-16.

**Scope note:** the post-war Cancer's two single most defining wounds — the Overcrowding Decision
(c. 2813) and the Rationing of Grief (c. 2818) — are both explicitly post-war and do not exist in this
document. Nor does the specific motto "No One Left Behind," which the post-war Mega-Init itself already
flags as "a *later*, war-era motto," not a founding-era phrase. This is not a light edit of the post-war
file; it's the district's own genuinely different pre-war identity — a 250-year-old caregiving culture
already fully mature in its own right, long before the war gave it a second, harder wound to absorb.

---

## The One-Line Pitch

*A sanctuary built to receive the original exile's own trauma, that spent two and a half centuries
turning "catch whoever arrives with nothing resolved" into a living, permanent civic institution — never
once tested by mass triage, because that test doesn't happen until the very end of this period.*

## Who Lives Here, and Why

Early caregivers, integration specialists, and the first generation of Cancer's own trained comforters —
people who chose, or were assigned in the founding years, to receive Tepenia's newly-exiled population
and help them physically and emotionally stabilize. Robots: early caregiving and companion models, still
crude by later standards, hydroponic bots, and the first creche units built specifically to receive
newly-activated robots arriving with no established identity yet formed. Cultural lean, present from
founding: Feeling/Compliant Enneagram types — emotional labor and duty to the vulnerable are founding-era
values, not something the district grew into later.

Per the Cancer deep dive's own Stage 1 finding, this population's wound is the original exile — forced
expulsion from Upper Earth, the Falkland Treaty's own coercive terms, and whatever the earliest robots
and human allies actually lost in being pushed off-world entirely. Crucially, per the task's own
observation about this district, that founding wound never fully closes: Tepenia's exile and
colonization process does not stop after the founding decades. Across the whole 250-year interwar
period, Cancer continues receiving new arrivals — continuing waves from Upper Earth, newly-activated
robots (activation itself is an ongoing process, not a one-time founding event), and a steady trickle of
broken or disillusioned frontier settlers washing back from Sagittarius' own expansion efforts. Cancer's
core pre-war role is not "war orphan shelter." It is the district permanently responsible for
integrating whoever Tepenia's own ongoing growth produces with nothing yet resolved — a role that never
has an off season, for 250 straight years, well before any war exists to give it a harder test.

Scorpio's own pre-war Mega-Init (`District_Megasheets_PreWar/04_Scorpio/Scorpio_Mega_Init.md`)
independently confirms the other half of this picture: Cancer and Scorpio are sibling founding-era
districts, both built in direct response to the identical original wound, through opposite methods —
Cancer nurtures it, Scorpio confronts it. Both institutions are already fully mature, functioning civic
fixtures well before the war ever becomes a live question for either district's residents.

## What It Feels Like

The curved, gentle, green-and-white architecture and the soft ambient haze from dense hydroponic
cultivation are already fully present — none of this atmosphere is war-produced, since it was built into
the district's original design brief for exactly the reason it still serves in the post-war period
(psychological comfort for people arriving with nothing settled). What's different from the post-war
district: the memorial tradition — specific chairs kept at dinner tables, a companion robot continuing a
deceased human's routines — is present and already old by the end of this period, but reads as ordinary,
accumulated domestic practice rather than the raw, still-active wartime grief culture the post-war
district carries. There is no Rationing of Grief to resent Libra for yet. Doors are unlocked, care is
available without needing to be triggered, and the district's slow, unhurried pace is the same pace the
post-war city will later recognize — Cancer didn't develop its rhythm during the war. It brought that
rhythm to the war.

## Personality (Enneagram Read)

**Feeling / Compliant / Reactive (or Positive Outlook)** — unchanged from the post-war read. Per the
Cancer deep dive, this dual register (Reactive fitting raw triage-adjacent stakes, Positive Outlook
fitting the healing-garden and bonding-ceremony side) is present from founding, since the district's
core caregiving function — and the tension between crisis response and cultivated calm — predates the
war by centuries.

## Worth Your Attention

The single most load-bearing fact carried over from the Cancer deep dive is that Cancer's founding
wound and the Long Night War are two separate historical events, roughly 250 years apart — the district
was not invented by the war, it was *tested* by the war, for the first time, after two and a half
centuries of practicing the same caregiving mission on a population the war had nothing to do with. The
Cancer/Scorpio sibling relationship is this document's own single biggest extension: giving Cancer's
half of that founding-era partnership the same full pre-war treatment Scorpio's own document already
gave its half.
