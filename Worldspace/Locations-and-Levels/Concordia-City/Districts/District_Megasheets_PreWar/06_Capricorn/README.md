# Capricorn (Pre-War) — Mega-Init

**Step 1 of the Pre-War District Megasheet compilation process.** Companion document to the existing,
post-war `District_Megasheets/06_Capricorn/Capricorn_Mega_Init.md` — not a replacement for it.
Reconstructs Capricorn as it stood during the Second Interwar Period (roughly 2564–2812), before the
Long Night War. Synthesizes `Deep_Dives/06_Capricorn_Deep_Dive.md` and, critically,
`Deep_Dives/06b_Capricorn_Alternative_Conditions.md`. Compiled 2026-07-16.

**Scope note — this is the deliberately unresolved district.** Capricorn's own central pre-war
conflict — whatever specific injustice gets exposed and buried around 2761 — is not yet settled. Seven
documented possibilities currently exist: the original Capricorn's core injustice [mechanism TBD — 4 contenders shortlisted, see `06b_Capricorn_Alternative_Conditions.md`] (robot-labor undervaluation)
and six full alternatives in `06b_Capricorn_Alternative_Conditions.md` (company-town economic captivity,
guild patronage over merit, hereditary trade caste, deliberate scarcity of advancement, constant
productivity surveillance, built-in obsolescence anxiety). This document does not pick one. Per
developer direction: treat the specific c. 2761 event as **not yet having happened** in this document's
own content, while building Capricorn's founding-to-2761 history so that *any* of the seven remains
equally plausible as what actually gets exposed. `06b`'s own "shared pattern" note already does most of
this work — four of the six alternatives (patronage, caste, scarcity, obsolescence) already share one
underlying shape: a founding-era practical necessity, calcified over ~200 years into entrenched
structure, exposed and buried around 2761. This document is built at exactly that level of
generality.

---

## The One-Line Pitch

*A district that had to make fast, centralized decisions about who does what and who gets what during
the earliest, most desperate years of exile — decisions that were genuinely necessary at the time — and
then spent two centuries never quite getting around to revisiting them once the crisis that justified
them had long since passed.*

## Who Lives Here, and Why

The same core population the post-war district describes, minus two centuries of war-era additions:
early master-crafters (arriving first, not necessarily most skilled), the first generations of
apprentices and foundry workers, early quality-control and resource-allocation figures making
fast, necessary calls under genuine founding-era scarcity. Robots: early industrial and assembly-line
models, cruder than their later counterparts, already doing the bulk of the district's heaviest and most
repetitive physical work from the very start. Cultural lean, present from founding: Thinking + Instinctive
+ Assertive + Competency — discipline and measurable achievement aren't traits Capricorn grew into later,
they're the district's founding survival strategy from day one.

---

## What It Feels Like

The same driven, unhurried-but-purposeful industrial register the post-war district carries — constant
machinery sound, a mixed human-and-robot workforce visibly building the infrastructure the rest of
Concordia depends on — is already fully present pre-war, since Capricorn's whole civic character was
forged in the earliest, most materially desperate years of exile, not war-shaped. What's different is
what the district is actually building: not wartime production quotas, but the literal domes,
foundations, and infrastructure that make the rest of Concordia's districts physically possible in the
first place. Whatever specific unfairness eventually gets exposed around 2761 is, at this point in the
district's own history, still simply "how things are done" — unquestioned, not because anyone's
actively hiding it, but because the arrangement is old enough that its own founding justification has
faded from living memory while the arrangement itself never went anywhere.

## Personality (Enneagram Read)

**Thinking + Instinctive / Assertive / Competency** — unchanged from the post-war read. This is the
district's own founding-era personality, present from the earliest construction years, not a trait
produced by the war or by wartime production pressure.

## What's Actually Open

## Worth Your Attention

This document's single most load-bearing design choice is what it deliberately does *not* say: the
exact nature of Capricorn's founding-era arrangement and its eventual c. 2761 exposure are both held at
the precise level of generality that keeps every one of the seven documented possibilities equally
alive. This isn't indecision — it's a genuine structural finding already present in `06b`'s own "shared
pattern" note, applied consistently: four of the six alternatives, plus the original Audit, already
describe the same underlying shape (founding-era necessity → 200-year calcification → documented,
buried exposure). Whichever specific version the developer eventually picks, this document's own
Capricorn history will already be compatible with it, requiring no rewrite — only a single pass to make
the deliberately abstract parts concrete.

# Capricorn (Pre-War) — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** open questions from `Capricorn_Mega_Init.md` (this same pre-war folder) given proposed
answers, where those answers don't require resolving which of the seven documented c. 2761 possibilities
actually happens. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Written
2026-07-16.

**Scope note, carried from the Mega_Init:** the central question (Capricorn's core injustice [mechanism TBD — 4 contenders shortlisted, see `06b_Capricorn_Alternative_Conditions.md`] vs. one of the six

## I. Demonym

**Proposed: Capricornian.** Unchanged from the post-war naming — no reason for a pre-war district to use
a different demonym than its later self.

---

## II. The Spire System's Own Founding-Era Origin

**Proposed:** the literal, floor-level-equals-status architecture already exists from Capricorn's
earliest construction years, and its origin is genuinely independent of whichever specific allocation
injustice is eventually chosen. Vertical status marking is administratively convenient on its own
terms — it's a visible, legible way to signal rank in a district built from nothing, useful for exactly
the same reasons regardless of *how* rank actually gets assigned underneath it. This means the spire
system itself isn't evidence for or against any of the seven options; it's neutral, load-bearing
infrastructure that whichever mechanism eventually wins would simply express through, the same way a
thermometer shows a temperature without caring what's heating the room.

---

## III. Capricorn's Founding-Era Relationships to the Districts It Built

**Proposed:** the same core relationships the post-war district holds — dependency ties to Aries
(power), Virgo (the infrastructure Capricorn's own domes and foundations sit on top of), and construction
obligations to Cancer and Taurus (whose earliest sheltering and residential infrastructure Capricorn
quite literally built) — are already fully formed by the end of the construction boom (late 2560s–2580s).
Capricorn's own established civic pride in "building the domes that made other districts possible" isn't
a later, self-serving reframing; it's a literal founding-era fact, true from the very beginning and
independent of anything the eventual c. 2761 reveal turns out to say about how the district treats its
own workforce.

## Worth Your Attention

Section II is the most structurally useful proposal here — it resolves a piece of Capricorn's own
physical architecture without touching the central unresolved question at all, by identifying that the

treating as a template for handling the rest of Capricorn's pre-war texture as this document develops

# Capricorn (Pre-War) — Cross-Reference Synthesis

**What this is:** the deliberate cross-referencing pass across this pre-war Megasheet's own Mega-Init and
Full Extrapolation, plus the existing post-war Capricorn Megasheet and `06b_Capricorn_Alternative_
Conditions.md`, looking for connections between facts that live in different documents and were never
actually placed next to each other before. This document stays purely descriptive. Written 2026-07-16.

---

## Finding 1: Capricorn's own calcification pattern is a structural cousin of Libra's already-established "Perpetual Emergency"

**Combining:** `06b`'s own alternative #5 (constant productivity surveillance), which explicitly names
itself "a close structural cousin of Libra's own established 'Perpetual Emergency' pattern (an
emergency measure that outlives the emergency)" + `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s own Libra entry, which
independently establishes that Libra has not gone more than three years without an active emergency
council session across its entire history.

**2nd-order effect:** the underlying shape — a genuine, temporary crisis-era measure that never gets
formally revisited once the crisis passes, simply because nobody with the power to end it has any
incentive to — isn't unique to whichever specific option Capricorn eventually adopts. It's already an
independently-confirmed pattern in a completely different founding-era district, run by a completely
different logic (Libra's procedural control vs. Capricorn's production efficiency).

**3rd-order effect:** this means Capricorn's own pending c. 2761 exposure, whichever specific form it
takes, isn't a Capricorn-specific character flaw — it's the same general Second Interwar Period failure
mode (emergency measures outliving their emergency) that this project's own research has now confirmed
independently in at least two founding-era districts.

**4th-order effect:** worth checking for this same pattern explicitly when building out the remaining
pre-war district Megasheets — "a founding-era crisis measure nobody ever formally ended" is looking like
a genuine, recurring Second Interwar Period civic shape, not a coincidence specific to Capricorn and
Libra.

---

## Finding 2: The spire system's neutrality (Full Extrapolation §II) means Capricorn's pre-war *appearance* is already fully fixed, independent of its pre-war *substance*

**Combining:** this document's own Full Extrapolation §II (the spire system's visibility is
administratively independent of whatever mechanism actually assigns rank underneath it) + the post-war
Megasheet's own flagged, unresolved tension between the developer's Vision Notes (status not perceptible
from street level) and `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s established "status lighting readable from the
street."

**2nd-order effect:** because the spire system's physical form is already settled and option-neutral,
that existing post-war street-level-visibility tension is *also* fully independent of which of the seven
c. 2761 options gets chosen — resolving one doesn't require or block resolving the other.

**3rd-order effect:** this means the two open Capricorn questions (which c. 2761 option, and whether
status is street-visible) can be worked on in either order, or in parallel, without either constraining
the other — a genuine simplification for whoever picks this back up later.

---

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across Both Findings

Both findings describe the same underlying discipline: separating what's true about Capricorn
regardless of the unresolved central question from what genuinely depends on it. Finding 1 shows that
even the *shape* of Capricorn's eventual crisis (a forgotten emergency measure) is already independently
supported by Libra's own confirmed pattern, without needing the specific mechanism settled. Finding 2
shows that a real piece of the district's physical architecture can be fully resolved without touching
the central question at all. Together, they suggest more of Capricorn's pre-war texture is actually
option-neutral than the Mega_Init's own initial "What's Actually Open" list might have suggested.

---

## Worth Your Attention

Finding 1 is the more significant of the two — it's the first piece of external validation that
Capricorn's own deliberately-unresolved central conflict is telling a plausible, well-grounded story
regardless of which specific version wins, since the same underlying shape (crisis measure outliving its
crisis) is already independently confirmed elsewhere in Concordia's own founding-era history.
