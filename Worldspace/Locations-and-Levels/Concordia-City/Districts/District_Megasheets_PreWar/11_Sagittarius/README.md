# Sagittarius (Pre-War) — Mega-Init

**Step 1 of the Pre-War District Megasheet compilation process.** Companion document to the existing,
post-war `District_Megasheets/11_Sagittarius/Sagittarius_Mega_Init.md` — not a replacement for it.
Reconstructs Sagittarius as it stood during the Second Interwar Period (roughly 2564–2812), when Tepenia
was a full, functioning country, before the Long Night War. Synthesizes `Deep_Dives/
11_Sagittarius_Deep_Dive.md`'s own Stage 1 material, which the deep dive itself already isolates as
existing "before the war ever touched it." Compiled 2026-07-16.

**Scope note:** this is the most directly-transferring of this batch's three districts. Sagittarius'
post-war defining character — self-inflicted rather than war-inflicted, opportunity-seeking rather than
trauma-processing — was already fully formed centuries before the war arrived, per the deep dive's own
explicit Stage 1 / Stage 2 split. The Third Expansion Collapse and the Siligel Rush and Bust (c. 2772–
2777) both predate 2812 outright. Only "Long Night War Frontier Defense" and the "Post-War Resource
Scarcity" pressures are removed; nearly everything else carries over with light adaptation.

---

## The One-Line Pitch

*A district that never needed the exile's original wound to become who it is, and never needed a war to
prove that its own defining values could cost sixty-three lives and still not shake the "push further"
ethos loose — a frontier civilization already two centuries deep into answering the hard questions about
expansion, cost, and what's owed to the people who pay for it, entirely on its own terms.*

## Who Lives Here, and Why

Rugged explorers, prospectors, long-range scouts, frontier settlers, homesteader families, traders,
caravan merchants, archaeologists, adrenaline-seeking adventurers, exiles from stricter districts —
unchanged from the post-war population, since none of it was ever produced by the war. Robots: rugged
cold-weather navigation models, mobile survey and resource-scanning units, caravan escort and defense
frames, optimistic "hope engine" personalities designed for morale in isolation, plus the occasional
recovered or jury-rigged unit from a lost expedition — a category of loss Sagittarius has been
accumulating since its earliest founding years, not one the war introduced. Cultural lean, present from
founding: Instinctive (with Thinking undertones) + Assertive + Positive Outlook Enneagram.

Per the deep dive, Sagittarius is one of three districts (with Taurus and Capricorn) whose founding logic
was never a response to the original exile wound at all — it exists because crowded domes will always
produce a population that wants opportunity and space more than safety, and the cold turns philosophy
into a practical survival tool rather than an intellectual hobby. This is already fully true in the
district's earliest decades, not something the intervening centuries deepened into existence.

---

## What It Feels Like

Vast, open stretches of frozen wasteland, sprinkled with living arrangements and occasional Rastras —
genuinely wild territory, clustered into isolated clans rather than one continuous settlement, each
cluster operating independently but bound by a shared, unspoken understanding that they have each
other's backs. Most Rastras are parked; a small handful are out actively driving. The district reads as
generally quiet and sparse, punctuated by real bursts of activity. None of this atmosphere is
war-produced — it's the direct, lived consequence of building a frontier civilization from nothing in a
lethal environment, present from the district's earliest habitable years. Soundscape: wind, constant and
audible, with Rastras plowing directly through it during operation.

---

## Personality (Enneagram Read)

**Instinctive (with Thinking undertones) / Assertive / Positive Outlook** — unchanged from the post-war
read. Raw survival against the ice, expansion drive, philosophical confrontation with the wilderness;
bold pioneering, independence from central control; optimistic idealism, reframing harsh conditions as
adventure or philosophical growth. This is the district's own founding-era personality, not a trait the
war produced or sharpened.

## What's Actually Open

- **Pace** — not directly confirmed; whether there's real urgency to movement given the cold, or a
  different rhythm entirely, applies just as much pre-war as post.
- **What actually defines a "clan"** — family lineage, shared profession, or (per the post-war Full
  Extrapolation's own proposal) shared risk-history — already an open, live question in the founding era,
  since clan structure would have needed to form early, not late.
- **The living-arrangement structures' own detail** (materials, permanence, size) beyond being clustered
  and expedition-derived.
- **Who refines Sagittarius' extracted precursor materials into finished siligel** — the deep dive's own
  proposed answer (Capricorn) is a pre-war mechanism from the moment the Rush begins, c. 2772; worth
  carrying into this document's own Full Extrapolation.
- **Whether the oral-tradition preservation crisis has already claimed anything specific** by the end of
  this period — a live, ongoing risk throughout the interwar centuries, not a post-war development.

---

## Worth Your Attention

The single most load-bearing fact carried over unchanged from the post-war Megasheet is that Sagittarius'
bitterness toward central authority was never generic frontier resentment to begin with — it's a specific,
repeatable grievance about proving the district's own value and not being credited for it. Pre-war,
that grievance has exactly one confirmed instance (the Siligel Rush's stranded political power). The
post-war district's version of the same grievance has two (the Rush, then the war's own caravan
logistics). This document is therefore the origin point of a pattern that only fully compounds once the
war arrives — worth reading as the "before" half of a two-part story whose second half doesn't exist yet.

# Sagittarius (Pre-War) — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question raised in `Sagittarius_Mega_Init.md` (this same pre-war folder)
given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what the post-war Megasheet
already establishes rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or
rejection piece by piece. Written 2026-07-16.

---

## I. Pace Already Has Two Speeds, Well Before the War Ever Raises the Stakes Further

**Proposed:** unchanged from the post-war district's own established logic, since it was never a
war-produced trait — clan life at rest moves at the deliberate, conserving rhythm of people managing
scarce energy and long isolation, while anyone actually out on the ice shifts immediately into sharp,
practiced urgency, because the cold makes hesitation lethal regardless of which century it happens in.
This dual rhythm is present from the district's earliest habitable years, not something that hardened
later.

---

## II. Clans Form Around Shared Risk-History From the District's Earliest Generations

**Proposed:** a clan is less a family tree or professional guild chapter and more a group bound by common
expeditions, common near-misses, and — once it happens — the shared memory of the Third Expansion
Collapse or a smaller-scale version of the same kind of loss. This makes clan membership closer to shared
survival history than heredity from the very start, since the earliest founding-era expeditions would
have produced exactly this kind of bonding well before there was a settled, multi-generational population
for bloodline-based clans to form around in the first place.

---

## III. Living Arrangements Descend Directly From Expedition Equipment, Not Designed Residential Architecture

**Proposed:** clan housing plausibly descends from forward operating shelters, incrementally reinforced
and expanded in place rather than replaced with something more permanent-looking — true from the
earliest construction years onward, since Sagittarius' founding population arrived as expedition crews,
not settlers with residential blueprints. Every cluster reads as a well-established base camp rather than
a town, a self-image the district has held since before it had the population to be anything else.

---

## IV. Capricorn Refines Sagittarius' Extracted Materials Into Finished Siligel From the Rush's Own Opening Years

**Proposed:** per the deep dive's own reasoning, Capricorn — already Concordia's established
heavy-manufacturing hub by the time the Siligel Rush begins, c. 2772 — is the only district with the
industrial capacity on record to plausibly be the actual refiner. This gives Sagittarius' own resentment
a specific mechanism from the very start of the boom: Sagittarius absorbs the Rush's full volatility (the
discovery, the windfall, the depletion, the stranded political power) while Capricorn captures the more
stable, ongoing value of turning Sagittarius' raw extraction into finished product. The grievance is not
retrospective, invented after the war gave Sagittarius a second reason to resent central districts — it's
live and current for roughly the last thirty-five years of this document's own timeframe.

---

## V. The Oral-Tradition Preservation Crisis Is Already Underway by the End of This Period

**Proposed:** given the district's own oral-only knowledge economy and the two founding-scale events it
has already lived through by the war's arrival (the Third Expansion Collapse, the Siligel Rush and Bust),
it's plausible the specific engineering detail behind the Collapse's structural miscalculation is already
fading from precise memory among the generation active at the end of this period — remembered in broad
strokes, not exact cause, by anyone who wasn't personally present for the original disaster. This isn't a
new, post-war problem; it's a slow-burning one already several decades into its own life by 2812.

---

## VI. Demonym and Notable Figures

**Demonym, proposed: Sagittarian.** Unchanged from the post-war naming — no reason for a pre-war district
to use a different demonym than its later self.

**Notable Figures, proposed:**

  *(placeholder, unnamed)* — one of the eleven-day stranding's survivors, whose account of what expansion

- **An early Rush-era resource administrator, watching the boom's political power concentrate and then

## Worth Your Attention

Section IV remains the standout proposal, carried directly from the post-war document's own reasoning —
it converts an unexplained "resource competition with Capricorn" into a specific, mechanically grounded,
already-live grievance roughly thirty-five years before the war would ever give Sagittarius a second,
compounding reason to feel it.

# Sagittarius (Pre-War) — Cross-Reference Synthesis

**What this is:** the deliberate cross-referencing pass across this pre-war Megasheet's own Mega-Init and
Full Extrapolation, plus the existing post-war Sagittarius Megasheet and the already-completed pre-war
Scorpio, Capricorn, Leo, and Pisces Megasheets, looking for connections between facts that live in
different documents and were never actually placed next to each other before. This document stays purely
descriptive. Written 2026-07-16.

---

## Finding 1: Sagittarius and Capricorn are both non-wound-response founding districts organized around ambition, pointed in opposite directions

**Combining:** this pre-war Mega-Init's own confirmation that Sagittarius' founding logic was never a
response to the exile wound at all, but opportunity-seeking with philosophy as a side effect of the
stakes + Capricorn's own pre-war Mega-Init's parallel confirmation that its founding logic was a
practical, ambition-driven arrangement for allocating status and reward during the earliest construction
years, equally independent of the exile wound.

**2nd-order effect:** both districts channel the same underlying founding-era instinct — that the
newly-arrived exile population's energy is better spent building toward something than processing what
was lost — but Capricorn directs that instinct centripetally, toward the center, building the domes and
foundations the rest of Concordia stands on, while Sagittarius directs it centrifugally, toward the edge,
pushing outward into territory nobody has secured yet.

**3rd-order effect:** this mirrors the "confrontation over avoidance" pattern Leo's own pre-war
Cross-Reference Synthesis already found between Leo and Scorpio (the same civic instinct, aimed at
opposite audiences) — except here the shared instinct is ambition rather than confrontation, and the
opposite poles are direction (inward/outward) rather than audience (internal/external). Between the two
findings, Concordia's founding generation appears to have organized itself around a genuinely small set
of underlying strategies, expressed in mirrored, opposite-facing pairs district by district.

**4th-order effect:** worth checking explicitly whether Taurus, the deep dive's own third non-wound-
response district alongside Capricorn and Sagittarius, completes a similar mirrored triangle rather than
standing alone — a question for whichever pre-war Megasheet eventually covers Taurus.

---

## Finding 2: Capricorn's buried c. 2761 exposure and Sagittarius' Siligel Rush are separated by roughly a decade, with Capricorn's stable gain beginning just as its own founding-era system finishes being definitively not revisited

**Combining:** Capricorn's own pre-war Mega-Init dating (a documented exposure "around 2761," met with
dispute and quiet burial) + this document's own Full Extrapolation Section IV dating (the Siligel Rush
beginning c. 2772, with Capricorn as the established refiner of Sagittarius' extracted materials).

**2nd-order effect:** Capricorn's own calcified allocation system — whichever of the seven documented
possibilities it turns out to be — survives its own buried 2761 exposure with roughly a decade to spare
before a completely unrelated windfall (Sagittarius' Rush) begins flowing new, stable value directly into
Capricorn's own production line.

**3rd-order effect:** this means Capricorn enters the most economically fortunate stretch of its own
pre-war history at almost exactly the moment its own internal system proved, and then buried proof, that
it wasn't delivering what it claimed — a specific, checkable near-coincidence that neither district's own
existing material currently notices, since Capricorn's pre-war document deliberately doesn't narrate the
2761 event's aftermath, and Sagittarius' own material has no reason to track Capricorn's internal
politics.

**4th-order effect:** if the eventual choice among Capricorn's seven possibilities involves robot labor
specifically (the Merit Board Audit or the obsolescence-anxiety option), this timing becomes sharper
still — Capricorn's robot workforce would have absorbed both the burial of their own exposed grievance
and an immediate, unrelated increase in the value flowing through their own production line, with nothing
connecting the two for them any more than it currently connects for either district's documentation.

---

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across Both Findings

Both findings place Sagittarius in direct, previously-undrawn relation to Capricorn — Finding 1 at the
level of founding-era civic strategy (ambition, mirrored and inverted), Finding 2 at the level of a
specific, dated near-coincidence in their economic relationship. Together they suggest Capricorn's own
pre-war story is more entangled with Sagittarius' than either document currently states outright: the
same district that Sagittarius already resents for capturing stable value from a one-time windfall is
also, on this document's own timeline, a district quietly consolidating an entirely separate and
unrelated form of unrewarded proof at almost the same moment.

---

## Worth Your Attention

Finding 2 is the more significant of the two — it's a genuine, dated near-coincidence between two
districts' own separately-developed pre-war Megasheets, discovered only because both now exist in enough
specific chronological detail to be placed side by side. Worth flagging as exactly the kind of connection
this whole pre-war Megasheet project is positioned to keep finding as more districts get their own
treatment.
