# Concordia — Full Extrapolation (Ultra-Megasheet, Step 2)

**What this is:** every open question about Concordia as a whole given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established across all 13 district Megasheets and Concordia's own top-level Specs rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Concordia_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-10.

---

## I. Each district's own specific theory of how to build a true city

The Mega-Init's own central reframe — that all 13 districts share one goal (a genuine, thriving city) and differ only on method — was established in the developer's own 2026-07-06 Vision Notes session, but no individual district's specific belief was ever stated. Proposed here, derived directly from each district's own already-established founding logic:

- **Cancer believes a city becomes real the moment it takes care of its most vulnerable, unconditionally.** A true city is proven not by what it builds, but by who it refuses to let fall through the cracks.
- **Taurus believes a city becomes real through accumulated, unbroken domestic continuity.** A true city is one where families can simply stay, generation after generation, without ever needing to flee it.
- **Leo believes a city becomes real through shared culture and undeniable collective experience.** A true city has things worth celebrating together — spectacles that prove its people, human and robot alike, are fully alive and fully seen.
- **Scorpio believes a city becomes real only once it stops flinching from what it actually is.** A true city has confronted its own worst history directly, rather than managing it from a comfortable distance.
- **Aries believes a city becomes real through relentless, uncomplaining production.** A true city simply doesn't stop — it generates the literal power that lets everything else in it exist at all.
- **Capricorn believes a city becomes real through visible, measurable achievement.** A true city's people can point to something they built with their own hands and say, plainly, that they made it.
- **Aquarius believes a city becomes real by inventing itself anew.** A true city breaks from inherited constraint and designs better systems from first principles, even at real risk of failure.
- **Libra believes a city becomes real through fair, stable process.** A true city is one where everyone can trust the rules apply equally and disputes resolve without violence.
- **Gemini believes a city becomes real through shared knowledge and connection.** A true city is one where every part can actually talk to every other part, informed rather than isolated.
- **Pisces believes a city becomes real by having room for everyone the other theories fail.** A true city isn't complete until it has somewhere for the people who don't fit anywhere else to still belong.
- **Sagittarius believes a city becomes real by pushing outward.** A true city has a frontier still worth exploring — proof it hasn't stopped growing, hasn't settled for what it already has.
- **Virgo believes a city becomes real from the ground up, literally.** A true city is one whose infrastructure works reliably enough that people can stop thinking about survival and start thinking about living.
- **The Hub believes a city becomes real by having somewhere all the other theories can actually meet.** A true city needs genuinely neutral ground — a place where no one has to compromise their own theory just to stand in the same room as everyone else's.

Read together, these are not thirteen arbitrary positions — they are thirteen legitimately defensible theories of what makes an urban settlement into an actual city, none of them wrong, all of them incompatible enough in practice that 250 years hasn't reconciled them. This is, itself, the clearest possible demonstration of the Mega-Init's own central claim.

## II. Official district names — correcting a stale open question, and proposing the rest

`Specs/Concordia.md`'s own Open Questions section lists seven districts as still lacking official names: Cancer, Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aries, Capricorn, Libra. Cross-checked against `District-Inspirational-Influences.md`'s own bracketed subtitles, this list is stale — **Capricorn already has a working name ("The Yards") and Libra already has one ("The Government District")**, both in consistent use across multiple project documents; they were simply never promoted to the formal "Official Name" field in `District_Canon_Reference.md`. Gemini's "Janbogo Subnet Nexus" and Sagittarius' "the Frostlands" are in the same position — informally universal, never formalized.

That leaves five districts genuinely still needing a name proposed from scratch, plus one (Leo) where an in-world name already exists in the text but hasn't been promoted either:

- **Cancer, proposed: The Sanctuary.** Directly matches its own founding designation as "the primary sanctuary and integration zone" — the plainest, least invented option available.
- **Taurus, proposed: The Hearth.** Matches its own domestic-continuity founding logic and its established heirloom culture more precisely than a generic "residential district" label would.
- **Leo — already has one: The Golden Ring.** Already established and used repeatedly throughout Leo's own Megasheet as the district's central feature; proposed here simply to promote it to the formal district name rather than treating it as a sub-location within some other, unnamed whole.
- **Scorpio, proposed: The Veil.** Matches the engineered fog, the guarded chambers, and the district's whole culture of privacy calibrated by depth rather than outright secrecy.
- **Aries, proposed: The Core.** Matches its own status as the literal power core of the city, and echoes the geothermal/nuclear "core" terminology already used throughout Aries' own material.

## III. Pre-war Concordia's own character

**Proposed:** before the Long Night War, when Concordia was merely one settlement among many rather than the last one, it plausibly held a reputation as the remote, unglamorous inland posting — the place people were assigned to rather than the place they chose. Palmer City's own established status as Tepenia's cultural capital implies Concordia held no comparable cultural prominence beforehand; its founding population (per `Specs/Concordia.md`) were people who "directed or chose" the inland posting, language that reads as practical assignment rather than aspiration. Concordia's pre-war reputation was very plausibly closer to Aries' or Capricorn's own established "unglamorous but essential" self-image today, projected across the whole city rather than one district within it — a place known for being solid, remote, and slightly beneath notice, right up until being remote turned out to be the single most valuable thing about it.

## IV. Who gets built or activated in Concordia, versus who arrives as a refugee

**Proposed:** Calethina's Lab — the game's own primary activation site for newly sentient robots, physically located at the Cancer/Taurus/Capricorn corner — gives Concordia a genuine, mechanical answer to this open question rather than requiring an arbitrary ruling. Robots currently active in Concordia are disproportionately likely to have been built and activated there directly, simply because the city hosts the activation infrastructure. Humans, by contrast, are overwhelmingly refugee-descended, arriving from destroyed or ruined coastal cities across every subnet — Concordia has no equivalent "human nativity" infrastructure the way it does for robots. This gives Concordia a genuinely distinct demographic asymmetry: its human population is a mosaic of arrival, its robot population disproportionately born in place — worth keeping in mind for any Notable Figures work going forward, since a robot NPC's plausible origin story defaults toward "built here," while a human NPC's defaults toward "arrived from somewhere that no longer exists."
