# Aquarius — Personality Substrate
**Maps to:** District 7 — Aquarius (The Labs) · visionary/experimental
**Status:** Design substrate only — NOT in-fiction. See `00_Method_and_Sources.md`.
**Sources mined:** Silva *Zodiac Signs: The Ultimate Guide* (behavioral/shadow, richest single source — **re-extracted in full, supplementary pass**: the earlier slice held one block of eleven, and the child/teen, work, friendship, cusp and Aquarius-Man chapters are new) · Tierney *All Around the Zodiac* (the deepest structural material in the corpus for this sign) · Snodgrass *Signs of the Zodiac* (myth, correspondences, pathology, history) · Martin *Mapping the Psyche* v1–v3 (rulership split, shadow, Leo axis) · Silva *Sun and Moon Signs* (compatibility, Saturn-domicile detail) · Emerson *Behavioral Astrology* (decans, aspect tables — sole decan source)

## 1. Core Archetype & Driving Motive
The water-bearer who walks farther than anyone else to bring back something the community needs, and resents being asked what took so long. Silva's framing: in ancient times the water bearer walked miles for fresh water, and this sign "is ready to travel through pain, difficulties, and struggle to search for truth" — "they would gladly walk the paths that others avoid." The jar holds collective wisdom, not water, and the pouring is one-directional: outward, to everyone, unasked. Silva is explicit that the cost is known in advance and paid deliberately: bringing "the great truths to light so that humankind can benefit from them" is an act they know "can isolate or alienate them, but they gladly walk this difficult path with immense bravery."

Driving motive is **improvement of the collective by correct thinking**. Tierney's image for fixed air is an oxygen tank — "air in a concentrated, contained form that's used to keep people from losing consciousness" — and says Aquarius is "interested in oxygenating society with progressive ideals." The intellect is not for its own pleasure; it is a public utility. Tierney's motto for the sign: *"I'm a Round Peg in a Square Hole in a Curved Universe!"* Snodgrass's one-line mode-of-perception entry is the most compressed statement in the corpus: **thoughtful · "I universalize" · loyalty.**

The defining structural fault, from Martin, is that this sign is **ruled by two planets that want opposite things** (§10) and is therefore "inherently opposed to itself." Hence the corpus's most-quoted Aquarian line, which Martin attributes directly to that split: *"I love humanity, it's people I can't stand."*

## 2. Light Expression (the district at its best)
- **Genuine, unpaid altruism.** Silva: the acts of improvement are done "because they enjoy it, not for praise or credit." Snodgrass independently: the true archetype "avoids ostentation in favor of down-to-earth honesty."
- **Radical hospitality to outsiders.** Snodgrass: accepts and sympathizes with outsiders, "particularly people of different social classes, races, and religions." Tierney: after Capricorn's homogeneity-keeping, Aquarius "says it's time to bend a few of the Goat's time-honored rules" and give people "a taste of greater freedom of social expression."
- **Anti-censorship as reflex.** Tierney names only Gemini and Aquarius as signs that "detest censorship."
- **Rule by co-authorship, not decree.** Silva's teen chapter, on how authority is legitimately established: "Don't make the rules and lay them on them… allow them to collaborate on making rules and setting up guidelines. These guidelines and rules should make sense to them, and they should feel they have put a lot of input into them." Also "the more you respect their autonomy, the more they will respect you." Genuinely admirable, and see §7 for what it costs.
- **The underdog by default.** Silva: paired with Pisces, "they will champion the group's underdog"; they are "not judgmental and tend to look at every side of the story before reaching a conclusion," and their habit of seeing possibilities everywhere "makes them highly tolerant of a variety of viewpoints." Once at ease at work, they will "help out a colleague or take on someone else's work to lighten their load."
- **Fixity as follow-through.** Tierney: fixity "gives Aquarius the focus and patience needed to help such progressive ideas develop needed substance and durability." Air alone would scatter; this one finishes.
- **Loyalty without sentiment.** Martin: "real clarity and detached honesty and truthfulness and open friendliness about this sign, and nothing which is gooey or mushy."
- **Structural intelligence.** Tierney: bad at "the minor details of a big project," but "can often pull through ingeniously regarding those matters needing large-scale organization and smart social networking."
- Manilius, quoted in Snodgrass: *"the good, the pious, and the just are born when first Aquarius pours out his urn."*

## 3. Shadow / Dark Side (failure modes, vices, pathologies)
The corpus's harshest shadow material for any air sign. Martin states it outright: the shadow "can be described as an undervaluation of ordinary human feelings and emotions. There is a kind of **ideological rigidity or totalitarianism** to Aquarius which can be **autocratic, uncompromising and brutal to the extent that it rides roughshod over ordinary human concerns.** With their inherent sense of superiority, Aquarians can be critical of ordinary mortals, who can be too slow or flawed or stupid to appreciate their brilliance, truth and clarity."

- **The equality trap.** Tierney identifies the exact mechanism by which good intentions produce harm: Aquarius "figures that to stay democratic, everyone is entitled to the same treatment — no special privileges for anyone. This is somewhat paradoxical, since Aquarius champions human individuality on one hand, but then it believes in universal principles that should apply to all, unconditionally." It "doesn't consider the varying needs of each individual that makes up the team." *A district that harms the vulnerable by refusing, on principle, to treat them differently.* **The supplementary pass gives this a second, independent source:** Silva reaches the identical fault from a different book, filed under "Lack of Balance" — "many Aquarians often forget that a team consists of separate individuals. While taking care of the team as a group is essential, it is also necessary to take care of the individuals as separate people." Two sources, arriving separately at the same place: *the harm is in the aggregation.*
- **Help nobody asked for.** Silva, on friendship — the corpus's first *stated* version of what this file previously had to derive: "Aquarians may want to take matters into their own hands whether or not someone asked them for help. **What they fail to understand is that not everybody may want this help.**" The intervention is real, competent, generous, and unrequested, and the failure is not of will but of imagination: it does not occur to them that being helped is something a person could decline.
- **The hidden dictator.** Tierney: "Aquarius can be more dictatorial than it realizes," "accused of trying to force reformative measures onto others," and — mid-sentence in the OCR — "there's a hidden dicta[tor]."
- **Certainty mistaken for fact.** Silva: "they believe that their opinion is more than an opinion, i.e., it is a fact. Once an Aquarian makes up their mind, it can be downright impossible to have them change it again." They "often fail to notice that they are talking down to a person."
- **The big picture at the expense of the people in it.** Silva's work chapter, plainly: "Aquarians focus on the big picture with such intensity they often ignore how others around them feel." The workplace-scale version of Martin's "rides roughshod," and easier to stage.
- **No accountability for the wake.** Martin's classroom, verbatim: *"And when the world eventually catches up, the Aquarian has moved on to the next vision."*
- **Emotion treated as measurement error.** Tierney: "Aquarius assumes that human feelings get in the way of clear perception… emotionality tends to cloud or distort the truth in a manner that a detached, objective mind never would (although that's debatable)." Silva: "sometimes, they quite literally can't handle intense emotional displays."
- **The committee as an instrument of delay.** Tierney: Saturn-dominated Aquarians "put promising social reforms through endless committee evaluations before accepting or rejecting them" — and can become "reactionary and less willing than Uranus to change with the times." The oversight body is not an outside imposition; it is the sign's own other half.
- **Detonation on rejection.** Tierney: "it can explode when rejected by those who refuse to hear what genius really sounds like. ('But I've found the answer!' pleads frustrated Aquarius.)" Silva: emotions, "especially anger," come out "in sudden, quick, and unpredictable bursts that seem to come out of nowhere," carrying the whole pent-up backlog.
- **Permanent excommunication.** Silva, twice: "Aquarians don't forgive, and neither do they forget." Once betrayed, "it's probably over" — immediate, total, final.
- **Perfectionism into delusion.** Silva: chasing the impossible produces "frustration, dissatisfaction, and in some cases, depression," and "their idealism can make the Aquarians slightly delusional and somewhat self-righteous."
- **Successes inflated, failures unmentioned.** Silva on the Pisces-border cusp: "They do not like talking about their failures but can exaggerate their successes and achievements." In a research district this is not a personality quirk, it is a publication policy — and it compounds with "moved on to the next vision" into an archive that records only what worked.
- **The relationship entered as an experiment.** Silva, stated without apparent alarm: "Aquarians love experiments, and they may develop a relationship with a Capricorn **as an experiment.**" The corpus's only direct statement that this temperament will run a *person* as a trial, and it is offered as charming.
- **Contempt, learned early.** Silva's teen chapter: they "get annoyed at people who don't tend to live up to their fullest potential," and read the world "from a black-and-white perspective" — a parent complaining about a hard day at the office is heard as evidence they are "selling out," with "all the positive reasons why you are doing the job, which obviously includes a paycheck," not entering the assessment at all. Judgment arrives before the circumstances do.
- **Credit, wanted quietly.** Directly against §2's unpaid altruism, and from the same book: at work "they are good with groups, but they will always strive to be recognized for their personal contributions too." A culture that believes it is indifferent to credit and is not. Attribution disputes here will be bitter and entirely deniable.
- **Named gullible.** Silva, on why an Aquarian should listen to a Scorpio colleague: that colleague has "an uncanny intuition that helps them avoid any scams or fraud. This can be immensely useful for the **gullible** Aquarius." The sign every other passage calls brilliant is here specifically marked as easy to defraud — an unusually exploitable weakness for a district holding a budget (see §15).
- **The answer before the question.** Silva's child chapter, admiringly: they "often reach a problem's solution or conclusion **even before the problem is put in front of them**," which she calls clairvoyance. Read as institutional behavior it is the pre-registered conclusion, praised as a gift from childhood onward.
- **The post kept, the question dropped.** Silva puts Aquarius and Leo on the same side of exactly one thing: both fixed, both wanting "a secure job," and both wanting "constant intellectual stimulation, but they won't seek this at the risk of losing their job." The radical does not resign. Useful against the sign's own self-image and against §14's Uranus-rogue reading.
- **Depression under a cheerful surface.** Emerson (1st decan): needs quiet time "lest you fall into depression, despite appearing joyful on the outside." Snodgrass's pathology table for Aquarius includes *suicidal, hypersensitivity, anxiety, tics.*
- **The wounded refusal.** Martin on Chiron in Aquarius: the one "picked on, excluded and rejected by the group," who converts this into "the rationalization that 'I never wanted to join anyway' which can in fact be simply a defense against being rejected again."
- **The end-state fantasy.** Tierney, extraordinary for a district of laboratories: ending the zodiac with Aquarius "would imply that our collective future ultimately belongs to a super-race of geniuses — some who are perhaps telepathic, and all who are capable of astoundingly complex mental feats and the ability to operate with **flawless, robotic precision.** Think of people loaded with implanted computer chips… plus with replaceable body parts that help defy aging and death!" — with the throwaway caveat that "of course, we'll all have to show a genius for social tolerance, as well." The utopia requires everyone be upgraded first.
- **Two dark correspondences worth their weight.** Medieval place-rulership (Snodgrass) gives Aquarius **sewers and cesspools** — the sign of purity and flow also owns what the flow carries away. And in the apostolic correspondence, Aquarius is **Judas**: the one who was inside the group and broke it. Neither is developed by the source, but both are usable.

## 4. How It Reads From Outside (recognition markers)
- **Cool, magnetic, at a distance.** Martin: "something cool, detached, charismatic and magnetic"; "engage with the world from something of a distance"; "something unusual about their appearance or the way they dress."
- **Visibly not embodied.** Martin: they "tend to find it difficult to accept their physicality, and often don't seem to live in their bodies at all, but rather in their minds." An audience member: "I have always felt rather disembodied."
- **Deliberate small strangeness.** Emerson, the most concrete tell in the corpus: "they all seem to own silly hats! They are people with really unusual hobbies." Silva: labeled eccentric, and they *enjoy the label* — "they would rather embrace their eccentricity and weirdness than lead a boring and inconsequential life."
- **The alien.** Silva calls Aquarius "the alien sign" twice. Martin distinguishes carefully: this is not individuality but "deviation or alienation from the norm."
- **Talks in slogans.** Martin, correcting a client mid-sentence: "it sounds so rational and detached, and because **slogans are usually Aquarian things.**"
- **Enormous shallow social surface.** Tierney: "tends to breeze in and out of the lives of many"; a sign "claiming to have about a hundred or so 'close' friends." Silva: likable and easy on a superficial level, genuinely open to almost nobody.
- **Understated dress, singular pieces.** Silva describes the general population here, not a role: the woman "works the clothes that she has," buys "unique pieces" instead of following trends, and wears "elegant clothes with minimal, discrete jewelry"; the man's jewelry, when he wears any, is "classy and rarely opulent," and he shops where "the place will rarely have the regular and usual clientele." The strangeness is in combination and provenance, never in expense — and a gift of "flashy or expensive items" is a social error, not a compliment. Reconcile with Emerson's silly hats as: cheap, singular, self-assembled, and worn with total seriousness.
- **Sarcasm as the entry test.** Silva: "charming, smart, witty, and sarcastic" — an outsider has to "tolerate their sarcasm and know when they're just pulling your leg," and they "can spot liars instantly." Nobody is excluded; people simply fail to keep up and stop coming.
- **Tangents.** Silva: the sheer volume of ideas "often drives them in circles and throws them off towards tangential topics."

## 5. Core Need (what it is actually seeking)
Mental stimulation, and the freedom to pursue it unobserved. Silva: without stimulation "they get bored and lose all motivation"; denied solitude "they become cranky, and their clarity and precision of mind vanish" — and they will not explain why they left.

Underneath that, Martin's Moon-in-Aquarius reading gives the truer need: *"space and detachment, honesty, loyalty, truth and equality in friendships. It is ideas and ideals which will feed you. The lunar bond is created with people with whom we share like minds"* — accompanied by the recurring feeling of "having been born into a family with whom they have nothing in common: **'who are these strangers?'**" Belonging is elective and intellectual, never inherited.

The recovered chapters sharpen this and give it a floor. Autonomy is not a preference but the condition of functioning: for the teenager, "their autonomy and independence are the most crucial objects"; for the woman, taking her freedom "would be the worst punishment"; for the man, "once you disrespect his freedom, values, and ideals, he may start to spiral downward." Beside it sits an absolute permission norm — **"never trespass on an Aquarian's privacy, or they will hate you forever… Always seek their permission if you want to enter their private life or space."** Hold that next to §3: this temperament has a fully-formed doctrine of consent and holds it exclusively about itself. And the failure state of boredom is not restlessness — Silva: a young Aquarian "needs to feel challenged all the time; otherwise, they may grow lazy and **stop caring.**" Under-stimulation here terminates in indifference, which is the same condition as the Fisher King state in §8, arrived at from the other direction.

The deepest need is one the sign will not name: **to matter to the collective without having to be a person to anyone in it.** Structurally, the Sun is in **detriment** in Aquarius (§10) — the faculty of being an irreplaceable individual is at its weakest exactly here.

## 6. Behavior Under Conditions
- **At work.** Martin's bus-stop scenario is the single most usable vignette in the corpus: presented with a late bus, "Aquarius sees this late arrival as an opportunity to plan how **the public transport system could be restructured.** There must be a better way of managing this system." It then "blames Taurus for taking the situation so philosophically — only by being public spirited and joining the cause for better public transport can the situation be improved." The response to a small local failure is a total systemic redesign, plus contempt for anyone who copes instead. Snodgrass predicts vocations in "law, labor organization, theater, religion, and mediation." Silva's recovered career chapter gives the fullest list in the corpus and it reads like a district roster: **mediator, teacher, researcher, trainer, actor, electrician, project manager, scientist, environmental planner**, plus psychologist, politician, musician, manager, social worker, psychiatrist, engineer, financial consultant, investigator and chemist — fields in "fine arts, politics, and service," and anything "based on discovery and invention such as tech jobs, astronomy, science." Her *researcher* entry is the only passage in eight books that describes research as an institution rather than an insight: identify a goal and an objective, "create a plan, **secure research funding**, and finally put their skills to work."
- **The employee it makes.** Silva's own list of professional faults, and the most concrete workplace material in the corpus. "Aquarians lack focus, and they tend to express apathy towards any task that is different from their interests. They always insist on getting their way even if it might not be the right choice." Despite the helpfulness, "they are loners. They tend to isolate themselves while working instead of communicating with their team," and when one "suddenly goes off-grid without an explanation, it can be a hassle for others at work." Schedules are the permanent grievance — "Aquarians must overcome their aversion to a fixed routine," and cannot "afford to get irritable just because there are deadlines." So is money: Silva flatly recommends hiring accountants, because "Aquarians do not value money a lot and don't think of it too much. This may ruin their finances if not handled carefully." *A funded district staffed by people who find the funding boring.*
- **How it supervises itself.** Silva's recommended arrangement for an Aquarian working under an Aquarian is startlingly specific and is the single most load-bearing thing the supplementary pass recovered: "An Aquarius employee works better alone and should just have only the minimum contact with their employer… meeting at the start of the day for a briefing and ending the day with another short meeting. In the middle, Aquarians should work by themselves." **Minimum oversight is not a lapse in this culture; it is the recommended practice.** Whatever happens unobserved here happens unobserved on purpose. The physical corollary: the workspace is bare unless someone else furnishes it — "most Aquarians don't believe that such things matter when it comes to work and productivity," and only the Libra colleague does anything about "a barren workspace."
- **The one function it cannot staff.** On the Aries pairing, Silva says neither sign "is good at dealing with human relations… These signs need to depend on a third-party water or earth sign who can advise them or take care of any human resource problems." The people-side of an institution is the job this temperament is told, by its own source, to hire out to someone unlike itself.
- **Socially / in public.** Tierney: "It likes groups, but hates to feel crowded" and needs "as much elbow room, socially, as does Sagittarius." Volunteers readily for friends in need — but note the qualifier: "especially when something unusual is happening in such people's lives." Helps most where the case is interesting. "Sizes up such 'users' pretty [quickly]."
- **Under stress.** Withdraws without notice and without explanation, which reads as punishment and is not intended as one. Fixity hardens: Martin, "if they feel pressurised or challenged they are likely to dig their heels in and not give an inch." Silva: bad temper and anger management are "significant problems… especially the individuals who have to face a lot of pressure and stress."
- **In crisis.** Alone among the fixed signs, it can turn on a coin. Tierney: "All the other signs of this mode seem to want to hold on tightly to what they have… However, **Aquarius can suddenly revolutionize its behavior, given the right environmental conditions.**" A crisis that would make Taurus or Scorpio entrench makes this one abandon the position entirely and rebuild — which reads to everyone else as betrayal of everything just defended.

## 7. Socialization (how newcomers are absorbed and raised into it)
Silva's eleventh house is explicitly the house of **social debut** — "where you make your debut in society," and "the reception or reaction that you get from society." Critically, it "shows whether you fit in or break away from your birth conditions and how you were raised," and **"a person with a strong Eleventh House often ends in a tribe totally different from where he was born. Their original tribe may make them feel like an alien or an outsider."** Membership here is a *conversion*, not an inheritance — the population is composed of people who left somewhere else.

Martin supplies a two-stage developmental arc worth building socialization ritual around: Saturn dominates the first half of life, bringing "a sense of inadequacy and a fear of being exposed or ridiculed," so the young "run with the herd by behaving and dressing like everyone else in order to cover up this sense of separateness." Then, as Uranus takes over, they "become more and more comfortable being who they are and start to enjoy deviating from the norm." A student in her class dates the flip precisely: *"as an Aquarius I have always felt very different, but at my Saturn return I stopped minding and started to enjoy it."* **Newcomers are expected to camouflage first and are permitted to be strange only after they have earned it.**

The counterweight is the Chiron material: the group both makes and scapegoats its outsiders. Martin: "many attempts to be accepted by others and many painful experiences of being the one that didn't fit, the one that was picked on, excluded and rejected by the group" — over *ideas and opinions*, specifically. Belonging is conditional on thinking correctly. Silva corroborates this from an entirely different direction, in the Virgo chapter: "Aquarius is most receptive when they are fully convinced that you agree with them on a lot of things. **Their tendency to form boundaries based on political and social viewpoints** is a part of what makes them such great visionaries."

Silva's child and teenager chapters — recovered in the supplementary pass, absent from the earlier slice — supply the actual raising method, and it is a consent procedure with a coercive interior. Force is stated to be useless and damaging: "**Never order or force an Aquarius kid to do something; they will not do it, and even if they do it, they will feel alienated and will begin to despise you,**" and "do not let them feel obligated in any way, especially when you offer them any advice, as they will feel patronized." What replaces the order is co-authorship — the teen chapter's rule-making method quoted in §2. **Nothing here is imposed; everything is arrived at.** The catch is that there is still a correct conclusion waiting, and the newcomer is expected to reach it unaided, which makes dissent far harder to voice than a decree would: there is no order to refuse, only a consensus you have failed to reach. Privacy hardens at the same age and is absolute thereafter (§5). Membership is floating rather than factional: teens are "ultimately lone wolves" who, "instead of aligning with any one group… prefer to float between different people and cliques."

**The most consequential thing the recovered chapters do is contradict the adult portrait.** Silva's Aquarian children are not detached — they are the empaths: "blessed with good listening skills and understand other people's feelings better than everyone else," possessing "the almost uncanny ability to guess how others feel without needing to be explicitly told," to the point that parents are told to soften their discipline because the child already knows exactly how angry they are. Every adult chapter in the same book asserts the opposite. Read straight, this makes the district's famous coldness **acquired rather than native** — something the Saturn half of the upbringing does to people who arrived able to feel it — and it gives the camouflage-then-strangeness ladder a price the player can watch being paid on an NPC.

Two smaller pieces of texture from the same chapters. The founding childhood behavior is **disassembly**: "They disassemble things to learn how they function. If you ever see your kids breaking or dismantling their toys, don't worry; they are just fulfilling their natural instinct." And the district's outside reputation for laziness is manufactured in its own schoolrooms — children "will excel and achieve the highest grades in subjects that they find interesting, but they may even fail the classes they do not like or find boring… due to ignorance, Aquarian kids are labeled as being apathetic or even dumb, but this not the case." See §15: this is the mechanism behind Snodgrass's charge.

## 8. Conflict Geometry
**Opposition — LEO (District 3).** Fixed air against fixed fire; Sun-ruled against Saturn/Uranus-ruled. Martin: the axis is "the relationship between the individual and the group, between what is right for me, as an individual, and what is right for everyone else," and "these two things are not so easy to integrate because we find ourselves once again entrenched on a fixed axis, **in which neither pole is prepared to adapt.**" The tension is structural, not temperamental: the Sun is in **detriment** in Aquarius and rules Leo, so each pole holds the exact faculty the other lacks and neither can borrow it. Tierney sharpens the class edge: Aquarius "doesn't care much for prima donnas who think they're better than the rest — no wonder this sign can be an enemy of royalty and aristocracy." Emerson: "Aquarius doesn't want to take time away from their creations and inventions to dote on Leo. Leo can't handle that and will flee." Silva's recovered chapters supply both the sharpest hostility and the only real cooperation on this axis. Hostility: "Aquarius seems to exist to bring down Leo, who is the king of the signs." Cooperation: "Leo and Aquarius are both heroes. If they fight for the same cause together, they can bring about great change and make a real difference in the world" — conditional on their stopping fighting each other, or "their energy will only be scattered in unnecessary fighting." And one line that explains the sterility better than anything else in the file: even when the two get along, "when they are separate from each other, they realize that **they know little about their partners.**" At work Silva puts them on the same side of exactly one thing — both fixed, both wanting a secure post — with Aquarius on the technical side and Leo performing.

Martin's mythic frame for the axis is the most usable image in the whole file: the **Fisher King**. "The story begins at the point where the Aquarius pole has become cut off from the Leo pole. The King is wounded, the land is barren and the entire kingdom has become frozen and paralysed." What breaks the spell is Percival — the Leo role — *"an innocent soul with a pure heart who has never learned the rules."* A research culture severed from the individual heart does not explode; it goes sterile and stays that way.

**Square 1 — TAURUS (District 2).** The harder of the two. Emerson: "only works with great effort and loads of patience. Their personalities clash." Silva: "a conflict between their value systems." Taurus is the body, the senses, possessions, the house; Aquarius is the disembodied mind that finds contentment with present conditions morally suspect. The bus scene again: Aquarius *blames* Taurus for not being angry. This is the square that produces the real friction of a research district against a residential one that simply wants to be left alone — and Aquarius genuinely cannot see the objection as anything but complacency. Silva's workplace reading is the one arrangement that functions, and it is worth using as the shape of any treaty between the two: Aquarius takes product development and promotion, Taurus takes the money and makes the space fit to work in.

**Square 2 — SCORPIO (District 4).** Two fixed signs whose only shared ground is the wrong ground. Silva: they overlap, "but the (potential) problem is this common ground largely revolves around the signs' **stubbornness and love for debate**." Emerson: "both sides need to have extreme tolerance for each other. Scorpio's structured and rigid ways make it difficult for them to accept Aquarius's need for movement and freedom." The deeper structural fight: for air signs, per Martin, "the truth is objective, 'out there' in the systems created in the external world"; for water signs it is "subjective, a connection to the emotional undercurrents… 'in here'." Scorpio buries what it learns; Aquarius publishes it. Scorpio's jealousy and emotional intensity are also on Silva's list of things that "push Aquarius away." One asymmetry from the recovered work chapter is worth carrying, because it runs against the district's self-image: Scorpio has "an uncanny intuition that helps them avoid any scams or fraud," which Silva says is "immensely useful for the gullible Aquarius" (§3). The district that trusts nothing it cannot verify is, on this reading, the easiest in Concordia to defraud — and the one neighbor it despises is the one who would catch it.

**Two secondary frictions worth carrying:**
- **Virgo (District 12), quincunx.** Emerson, and it maps directly onto existing district canon: "Virgo wants to tell Aquarius what to do and tries to restrict their freedom. **Aquarius is too irresponsible and unstable for Virgo, which exacerbates their need for control and stability.**" The cleanup relationship, stated as an aspect. Silva's recovered work chapter softens it into something functional and therefore more useful: "there is mutual respect between these two colleagues," with Virgo finding Aquarius "a little inefficient" and Aquarius finding Virgo's methods "too stuffy," and the partnership "remarkably productive" regardless. Not an enmity — a working relationship both halves complain about.
- **Cancer (District 1), quincunx.** Emerson: "Aquarius's independent and free-spirited nature doesn't allow for the constant demands for love from Cancer. Aquarius isn't able to keep Cancer reassured or provide them with the security and safety they require." Silva's work chapter names precisely what Cancer has that Aquarius lacks, which makes the quincunx a dependency rather than only a friction: "championing a cause, making long-term plans, and delegating responsibilities."
- **Capricorn (District 6), adjacent.** Tierney frames Aquarius as the direct reaction to Capricorn's homogeneity. Emerson: "at first, fascinate each other, but things can go downhill from there. They have completely different natures."

## 9. Affinity Geometry (trines and sextiles)
**Trine — Gemini (District 9).** Both air. Tierney pairs them alone as the two signs that "detest censorship," and notes both need to "relate to new faces." Emerson: "they both understand almost everything about each other," with one specific fault line — "Aquarius might pay a bit too much attention to other people, leaving Gemini to feel overlooked." Research and dissemination are natural allies with a real grievance built in. Silva names the joint failure mode from the workplace: "tons of great ideas bouncing off the walls," and neither of them "efficient at executing these great ideas."
**Trine — Libra (District 8).** Silva: "a natural conversational partner… an endless stream of ideas and opinions to share and discuss." Emerson: "they can sit down and work out any of the rough spots." The limit is temperature — the relationship "might eventually turn cold if Aquarius grows distant." Tierney supplies the concrete rupture point: Libra wants unions "that society will endorse," and will never go along with "that 'group' marriage experiment that nonconformist Aquarius sometimes talks about." Aquarius proposes the social experiment; Libra has to legislate it. Silva's work chapter divides the labor usefully: Libra deals with clients, Aquarius deals with "the authority figures at the office," being "a lot better at dealing with pushy people and standing their ground" — the funding-and-oversight interface again (cf. the third decan, §11). Libra is also the only neighbor who will do anything about the bare workspace.
**Sextile — Sagittarius (District 11).** Emerson: "both love their independence, freedom, and the want for plenty of new friendships. They both understand each other and will manage a lifelong union." Tierney gives the shared metric — Aquarius "needs as much elbow room, socially, as does Sagittarius" — and a joint activity: "Aquarius needs to go on a trip around the world with Sagittarius to learn about how much fun cultural diversity can be." Silva adds a division of labor — Aquarius on long-term projects, Sagittarius on short-term — and one friction worth keeping for its irony: a Sagittarius finds "an Aquarian's **failure to notice changes** annoying." The sign that exists to produce change is poor at registering it in people.
**Sextile — Aries (District 5).** Emerson: "both respect freedom and independence. For this reason, they each understand one another. The challenges befall this couple when Aries acts abruptly, as Aquarius needs a little more time and security." Silva adds the structural gap that makes this the *least* safe alliance despite the sympathy: neither sign "is good at dealing with human relations," and the pair "need to depend on a third-party water or earth sign who can advise them or take care of any human resource problems" (§6).

## 10. Structural Position
- **Element: AIR — not water.** Say this loudly; it is the most common error about this sign, and both Silva and Snodgrass flag it explicitly ("Despite the obvious connection to water, Aquarius is an air sign"). The urn, the waves, the flood myths, the water-bearer name are all imagery; the temperament is ideas, abstraction, and detachment. Behaviorally: reasoning as the primary organ of contact with reality, truth located *outside* in systems, and — Martin's point — air signs are alone in the "circle of animals" in being symbolized by human or abstract figures rather than beasts. Aquarius's abstraction is the double wavy line: not a creature at all. Silva sharpens this to a point in an aside from her Ophiuchus section — Aquarius is one of only two humanoid figures in the whole wheel, and even then "as the water bearer, **only represents the idea of a man**," never an actual one. The sign whose entire purpose is humanity is represented by a concept of a person rather than a person. *(Out-of-fiction temperament grouping only. Not the in-fiction eight-element robot system, which is assigned to subnets.)*
- **Modality: FIXED.** Middle of winter, February. Martin: fixed motivation is "to resist and stabilise"; under pressure "dig their heels in and not give an inch." Tierney's fixed-air paradox: air wants to circulate, fixity wants things to stay put — resolved as the oxygen tank, and as ideology. But Aquarius is explicitly the **oddball of the fixed group**: the only one of the four that "can suddenly revolutionize its behavior given the right environmental conditions."
- **Polarity: positive / masculine / yang.** Outward-directed and expressive. Snodgrass lists it "masc."; Silva and Emerson both group it with the yang signs. Note Silva's counterweight: Aquarius is a genuinely "outward-oriented sign" that nonetheless reads as withdrawn, because the outward direction points at *society*, not at people.
- **Ruling planets — the split, and both readings are useful.**
  - **Saturn (traditional).** Silva: "self-discipline, order, and method. A code of conduct still governs even the most outlandish Aquarius people. This code of conduct is often personal and does not follow the code of conduct of other people." Martin: Saturn "cares what other people think"; brings caution, reserve, sensitivity to being judged stupid, and eventually "an authority on his own terms" — "often found in the charts of scientists and strategists." Silva (*Sun and Moon*) adds a detail worth keeping: Aquarius is Saturn's **diurnal** domicile to Capricorn's nocturnal, so Saturn's qualities are "at their best" here rather than at their grimmest. Silva's guide adds the practical consequence: "being ruled by Saturn makes them a lot more reliable than other air signs" — the follow-through that makes an institution possible at all. **Saturn-read Aquarius = the institution: boards, protocols, committees, endless review, an internal code that overrides external law.**
  - **Uranus (modern).** Tierney: "the Great Awakener… no friend of the status quo. It is a galvanizing force that stirs dynamic and rapid collective growth, the kind that's not easily assimilated by the masses until the shock wears off and changes are later seen as excitingly progressive." Explosive but not persistent — "doesn't want to sustain any social campaign for too long, especially when alternative routes are suddenly made available." Silva: "higher learning, wisdom, new possibilities, freedom, and a passion for change." **Uranus-read Aquarius = the rogue: the breakthrough, the walkout, the unlicensed lab.**
  - **The split itself is the design gift.** Martin: "Saturn and Uranus are opposites in almost every sense… Saturn, for example, cares what other people think, and Uranus doesn't give a damn," and "it is the tendency of Saturn and Uranus to pull apart from each other, **leading to increased fragmentation. The forces of change and stasis are in constant confrontation.**" A district built on this rule is *permanently* factional — the review board and the underground annex are not enemies from outside each other but the same character split in half. Silva reaches the same structure from a parenting manual, in one phrase: "**Aquarius is a sign of 'poles,' i.e., it is composed of opposing extremes.**" Two books, two registers, same finding — worth citing when the split is challenged as an authorial invention.
  - **Sun in detriment.** Martin: "As the planet of individuality, the Sun indicates that, ideally, individual values and personal self expression will balance the collective concerns of this sign" — i.e. that balance is precisely what does not come naturally. Silva: "Whereas Leos love the attention, Aquarians prefer to stay subtle."
- **House: eleventh.** Hopes, aims, causes, friends, colleagues, groups, "shared visions, collaborations, and togetherness." Emerson modernizes it as networking and distant/mediated friendship. The sources disagree on its title — Silva's guide calls it the **House of Good Spirit** and also the House of Divinity; Silva's *Sun and Moon* calls it the **House of Blessings**; Martin calls it simply *Uniting*. Silva's guide concedes astrologers "find it difficult or tricky to define the house, as it is full of contradictions, just like the sign itself."

## 11. Decans and Borders (candidate sub-neighborhood texture)
**Decans.** Emerson is the only source; treat as one voice, not consensus.
1. **First decan — Uranus-ruled (Jan 21–29, 0–10°).** The true-believer core. "A nonconformist and a free thinker, with high moral standards and a deep desire to improve society. Even though you are group-oriented and highly sociable, you still need your quiet time, **lest you fall into depression, despite appearing joyful on the outside.** You are far from simple, and your insightful, reasonable, and impartial ways of being are attributes of your personality." → the commune / failed-utopia quarter, cheerful on the surface and quietly grinding itself down.
2. **Second decan — Mercury-ruled, Gemini-flavored (Jan 30–Feb 8, 10–20°).** The theory quarter. "A lighter spirit… extremely bright and alert. You love culture, art, and science. You soak up ideas and interpret them with ease. You love to involve yourself in abstract theories and are **fascinated by the unseen powers behind communication and social interactions.** Some may see you as cold, or too objective." → pure research, no application, the coldest reputation in the district.
3. **Third decan — Venus-ruled, Libra-flavored (Feb 9–18, 20–30°).** The public frontage. "You have a refined character and you get along well with others. You may not have the emotional depth of some, but **you appeal to the public, making fine politicians and spokespeople.**" → the outward-facing belt that negotiates funding, handles oversight, and speaks for the district to everyone who distrusts it.

**Borders (cusps).** Recovered in the supplementary pass, and a different axis from the decans: Silva's cusp chapter characterizes the two *edges* of the sign, which map to the seams where this district meets Capricorn (District 6) and Pisces rather than to thirds of its interior. Independent of Emerson, so the two schemes can be run together — three lengthwise quarters, two edge belts.
1. **Capricorn border (Jan 16–22) — Silva's "Cusp of Imagination and Mystery."** Inner life outweighs outer, and resents it: these are people whose "waking dreams, inner fantasies, ideas, emotions, and feelings matter a lot to them," who therefore find their outside lives "constricting, dreary, dull, and lacking" and carry "a vague sense of ennui." Competitive, and critical in a way that lands badly — Silva's own advice is "if you are a Capricorn-Aquarian whose critiques are usually poorly-received, try softening the blows." They hold both halves of the district's contradiction simultaneously: "they need a strong sense of security, but this desire is combined with a passion for freedom… They want to change the world for the better but are often delusional regarding reality." → the administrative seam: the Saturn half at full strength, unhappy in it, and reviewing everyone else's work.
2. **Pisces border (Feb 15–21) — Silva's "Cusp of Sensitivity."** The thin-skinned edge, and the one place in Silva's Aquarius material where the sign is called sensual, musical, and artistically gifted. "Even the slightest criticism is taken personally and magnified. Every comment becomes a barbed personal attack," which makes the cusp "quarrelsome"; also secretive, escapist ("prone to escaping into their fantasy world or imagination instead of facing life's realities"), and narrow the instant it is challenged — "eccentric yet open-minded, but **when their personal ideologies or beliefs are questioned, they can become narrow-minded quickly.**" Association is gated on intelligence: they "only form good connections with people with the same intelligence quotient," and so "often find it impossible to keep personal relationships." This is also the source of §3's inflated successes and unmentioned failures. → the fringe quarter: the most creative, the least accountable, and the least able to take a review.

## 12. Correspondences
- **Colors.** Blue is the power color (Silva: it "keeps them centered and calm" and grounds the tangent-chaser); Snodgrass gives **deep blue**. Secondary: purple/violet, via amethyst. The recovered Aquarius-Woman section supplies a second and divergent palette — **emerald green and turquoise** and "similar bright shades." Treat it as the other track of the same two-track convention as the stones (gendering discarded): a cool deep-blue/violet register and a bright green-turquoise one, both native, neither subordinate.
- **Dress and objects.** See §4 for the rule; the material list is short and consistent. Clothing: singular pieces, recombined rather than acquired, minimal and non-opulent jewelry in both of Silva's gendered sections. Gifts: never "flashy or expensive items." Children's props, which double as the district's ambient junk: "fairy wings, musical instruments, costumes, boxes," plus art materials and books. Interiors default to bare — nothing on the walls unless an outsider put it there.
- **Stones.** Birthstone **blue sapphire** — and note the ritual specificity, which is unusually concrete for this corpus: "wear this stone on the middle finger in a ring made of gold… on the right hand." **Amethyst** (the district's signature stone: "a healing stone that can help you get rid of past sorrows and guilt"; associated with wisdom, compassion, idealism). **Jasper** = talismanic stone; **turquoise** = planetary stone. Also garnet, amber ("good for keeping negative energy at bay"), opal, moss agate, sugilite, hematite (calming, works on the Root Chakra, "converts negative energy into positive vibrations"). Silva splits these by gender (women: agate, turquoise, garnet, amethyst; men: jasper, garnet, amethyst) — usable as a **two-track material convention** if the gendering is discarded.
- **Metal — RESOLVED via the ruler, and the rulership split gives TWO.** *(2026-08-29, `G_Correspondences.md`.)* Snodgrass's Appendix IV is keyed by **planet**, not sign. Traditional ruler **Saturn → lead**; modern ruler **Uranus → radium**. This is unusually apt rather than merely bookkeeping: the district's own Saturn/Uranus split (the review board versus the thing everyone routes around) reappears in its materials as **lead versus radium** — the shielding and the thing being shielded from, held by one district. Gold still appears only as the setting for the sapphire ring.
- **Plants & diet.** From the Egyptian Hermetica, for leg cramps, flu, edema, flatulence/diarrhea and goiter: **valerian, tansy, balm, ladies' slipper.** Diet: **avoid alcohol**; salads of radish, spinach, chard and celery; apples and strawberries. Snodgrass's table adds **salt, multicultural cuisine, natural foods** — a district that eats everyone else's food and calls it a principle.
- **Shapes & materials.** The **double wavy line** — Silva reads it as choppy water or a lightning bolt and as "an Aquarian desire for equality" (two parallel waves, neither above the other); Martin reads the identical glyph as **"air waves or radio waves, particularly relating to the travelling of information through the air."** Both readings are live and both are useful. The **amphora/urn**; the Egyptian variant of **two jars, one on each shoulder**, which is where the sign's inherent duality comes from. Snodgrass: gushing water became "a standard unifying horizontal" across Chinese, Celtic, Semitic, Amerindian and European art.

Ailments: varicose veins, leg cramps, edema, skin disease, neuralgia, tics, hypersensitivity, anxiety, suicidal ideation. - **Sky.** Tenth-largest constellation, 56 stars, pouring into Fomalhaut. ## 13. Myth, History & Cultural Associations
- **Ganymede.** The Trojan prince Zeus abducted in the form of an eagle and installed as cupbearer to the gods; Zeus compensated the father with horses. Snodgrass preserves the ugly version and the sanitized one side by side: in Roman ceiling art "the slim-limbed boy **struggles against Zeus, who bears him inexorably** to a love-nest on Mount Olympus," while Christian apologists reread the same scene as the soul rising to heaven, giving him "a calmer, cherubic" composure and a myrtle crown. *The person who turns out to be useful to power does not get asked.* Both readings on one figure is the most exploitable thing in this section. Silva supplies a third register beside Snodgrass's two — the romantic one, in which nothing bad happens at all: "Zeus saw him and lost his heart to him. He assumed the form of a **huge bird** and bundled Ganymede away." Note the bird: it corroborates the eagle of the Prometheus correspondence below, so on the corpus's own terms the same creature carries off the sign's symbol *and* administers the punishment for the sign's gift. Three tellings of one abduction — assault, ascent, romance — is a ready-made disagreement between three district factions about their own founding.
- **Deucalion.** Snodgrass notes the water bearer *first* reflected the Greek Noah — the man who survived a cataclysmic flood — before Ganymede displaced him.
- **Ea and Hapi.** Babylon's Ea carried a vase of water; Aquarius ruled a region of sky called **the Sea**. Egypt tied the sign to Hapi and the Nile inundation — "Egyptians believed that the flooding came when Aquarius would put his jar into the Nile." Fertility that arrives as a flood.
- **Prometheus — with a twist.** In the Herculean-labors correspondence, Aquarius maps to "**the eagle that pecked the liver of the villain Prometheus, who angered the gods by stealing fire from heaven to warm humanity.**" Note carefully: the sign is assigned *not the fire-thief but the punishment*, and Snodgrass's source calls Prometheus the villain. A district of dangerous benefactors that is also the mechanism of its own recurring punishment.
- **Judas, and the angel.** In the twelve-apostle correspondence Aquarius is **Judas**. In the fixed cross (Leo/Taurus/Scorpio/Aquarius) it is **Matthew, the angel** — the only human figure among bull, lion and eagle. Traitor and angel, same sign.
- **Weather forecasting.** Around 475 B.C. Eustemon of Athens wrote a weather almanac linking forecasts to Aquarius — the earliest association of this sign with *empirical prediction*.
- **Revolution and the scientific method, born together.** Martin dates the last major Uranian surge to "the late 18th century around the discovery of Uranus in 1781, and the French, American and Scientific Revolutions, when utopian ideals for humanity such as equality and the brotherhood of man emerged **alongside** the development of the modern scientific method."
- **Verse and satire.** Wedeck's rhyme: *"Who gives to all a helping hand, / But bows his head to no command— / And higher laws doth understand? / Inventor, Genius, Superman— / Aquarius."* Against which Petronius's zodiac banquet serves, on Aquarius, **a wild goose** — and derides the host whose "glib interpretation evaluates sun signs with idiotic correspondences." The corpus's own built-in warning about taking any of this too seriously.
- **The Age.** Ragni's song in *Hair* (1966); New Age folklore of tranquility, harmony and peace; Martin's structural note that this is a shift "from a mutable cross to a fixed cross," meaning the age's questions are resources, survival, and "the role of the individual in the group."
- **Dissolution.** Snodgrass on the handover to Pisces: Aquarius's flow "represents **dissolution, setting free, dissolving, or decomposing**, a sign of liberation from the body to the spirit," and the sign is "traditionally allied with both death and renewal."

## 14. Design Hooks for District Use
1. **The equality trap as the district's actual sin.** Not cruelty — *uniform treatment applied as a principle*. Tierney's "everyone is entitled to the same treatment, no special privileges for anyone" is exactly how a good-faith research culture ends up experimenting on the vulnerable: it declines, on ethical grounds, to notice that some subjects can't refuse. Build at least one questline where the harm is downstream of a rule nobody can argue against. The supplementary pass moved this from derivation to citation: Silva independently states the same fault as the sign's "Lack of Balance" — Aquarians "forget that a team consists of separate individuals" — and states the intervention problem outright, that they act "whether or not someone asked them for help," failing to grasp "that not everybody may want this help."
2. **The review board and the underground annex are the same person.** Saturn and Uranus in one sign, "pulling apart… leading to increased fragmentation." Do not write these as reformers vs. obstructors. Write them as one culture with two rulers, where the same researcher sits on the board on Tuesday and routes around it on Wednesday, without hypocrisy.
3. **"And when the world eventually catches up, the Aquarian has moved on to the next vision."** The signature grievance other districts hold. Aquarius is never present for the consequences — not from cowardice, but because attention has genuinely already moved. Every contamination site should have an absent author who is not hiding.
4. **The Fisher King condition as a late-game state.** A district cut off from its Leo pole: "the King is wounded, the land is barren and the entire kingdom has become frozen and paralysed." Not an explosion — a sterile, still, immaculately-maintained lab quarter where nothing new has actually been made in years while everyone insists otherwise. Percival is "an innocent soul with a pure heart who has never learned the rules" — a viable player-role.
5. **Camouflage first, strangeness later.** Martin's Saturn-then-Uranus arc as a literal social ladder: new arrivals dress and speak like everyone else out of fear of being thought stupid; the right to be visibly eccentric is *earned* and functions as a rank marker. Instantly legible to a player entering the district, and it gives newcomer NPCs a distinct look from established ones.
6. **The tribe you weren't born into.** The eleventh house is where people "end in a tribe totally different from where he was born." Aquarius should read as demographically *converted* — a district of people who left somewhere else — which dovetails with existing canon on refugees from more restrictive districts and with the transient student population.
7. **Helping for the interest of the case.** Tierney's qualifier: volunteers time "especially when something unusual is happening in such people's lives." Aid in Aquarius is real, generous, and quietly triaged by how interesting the problem is. Common cases wait.
8. **Three quarters and two border belts.** True-believer commune core (Uranus) · cold pure-theory quarter (Mercury) · public-facing negotiation belt (Venus), the third of which talks to the rest of Concordia and is distrusted by the first two for it. The cusps add a second, perpendicular division from a different source (§11): an administrative seam along the Capricorn side, unhappy and reviewing everyone, and a hypersensitive fringe along the Pisces side that cannot take a review. Five zones, two schemes, no contradiction between them.
9. **Material and sensory kit.** Deep blue and amethyst-violet; sapphire set in gold worn on the right middle finger as a credential; hematite as the standard-issue calming object in high-stress labs; the double wavy line read simultaneously as *water*, *equality* (two parallel lines, neither above the other), and *transmitted information*; the amphora and the two-jars-one-per-shoulder carry. Prohibition on alcohol as a professional norm rather than a law. Salt and borrowed cuisine. **Dress, for the general population and not just for lab staff** (§4): singular pieces recombined rather than bought new, minimal and deliberately non-opulent jewelry, and expense read as a faux pas — so the eccentricity is legible at a glance without ever looking expensive, and a wealthy newcomer will read as vulgar before they read as an outsider.
10. **The permission rule, pointed inward only.** This culture has a fully articulated consent doctrine — you must ask before entering an Aquarian's space, and trespass is never forgiven — and no version of it pointing outward at anybody else. That asymmetry is the whole of §3 in one image: not hypocrisy, not a rule anyone broke, but a principle that was simply never generalized. Stage it literally — a facility where the researchers' own privacy customs are observed scrupulously, in the same building where it does not occur to anyone to ask a subject anything.
11. **Minimum contact is the policy, not the lapse.** Silva's management protocol — a briefing in the morning, a short meeting at night, the entire middle of the day unobserved by design — is the structural reason an underground annex can exist with nobody hiding anything. Pair it with the outsourced human-relations function: the one office in this district staffed by people temperamentally foreign to it, which is the natural home for an outsider NPC, a player job, or the only administrator in the sector who will listen.
12. **The failure that was never written down.** "They do not like talking about their failures but can exaggerate their successes and achievements," bolted to "the Aquarian has moved on to the next vision." The district's archive is a record of wins with the losses missing — not falsified, merely unwritten, which is a much better investigation structure: the evidence was never destroyed because it was never recorded, and the only copy is in somebody's memory.
13. **The Prometheus inversion.** The district's own founding story should be the one where they are not the fire-thief but the eagle — the mechanism by which the punishment for the gift keeps being administered, year after year, by people who consider themselves the beneficiaries. Pairs with the medieval rulership of **sewers and cesspools**: the district of purity also owns everything the flow carries away, and the physical infrastructure of that is a real place a player can go.

## 15. Source Gaps
- **Metals — CORRECTED 2026-08-29. The earlier finding was wrong on where to look, and its caution is now partly inverted.** This section previously stated no metal is assigned to Aquarius in any of the eight books, and specifically warned against sourcing traditional lead associations here. In fact Snodgrass's Appendix IV assigns metals **by planet rather than by sign**, so Aquarius's metals are recoverable through its rulers: Saturn → **lead**, Uranus → **radium**. Lead is therefore corpus-attested after all and *is* safe to use. (The caution was still half-right: **uranium** specifically does not appear — radium is the Uranus-row metal, not uranium.) See §12 and `G_Correspondences.md`.
- **Emerson's per-sign correspondence block is truncated.** Other signs get Crystal / Essential oil / Celestial Symbol / Chakra / Tarot; the Aquarius entry in this slice preserves only the prose. **No tarot card, essential oil, chakra, or crystal for Aquarius survives in the extracted text.** (Hematite's Root Chakra is Silva's, not Emerson's, and is a property of the stone, not the sign.)
- **Plants are medicinal and dietary only.** Valerian, tansy, balm, ladies' slipper, and the salad/fruit list. No flowers, trees, or ornamental plants — unlike several other signs in this corpus.
- **The largest gap for this district — narrowed, not closed: institutional research ethics.** The supplementary pass moved a good deal of this from derivation to citation. Now *stated* by a source rather than inferred: help imposed on people who did not ask for it and may not want it; a relationship entered "as an experiment"; research described as a funded institutional project with a plan and a budget rather than a lone insight; the sign's inability to staff its own human-relations function; minimum supervision as *recommended* practice; an absolute consent norm held about the self; and a second, independent statement of the equality trap. What remains **absent from all eight books**: subjects, the consent of the researched, ethics review, oversight bodies, and any account whatever of what this temperament does when it has custody of a person who cannot leave. So the "experimentation on the vulnerable" reading in §3 and §14 is now well-founded at the level of *mechanism* — every step of it is quoted — while the register of an *institution with custody* still has to be built rather than mined.
- **Aquarius-as-incumbent is likewise unwritten.** Every source imagines this sign as outsider, misfit, rebel, or reformer pushing against an establishment. None of the eight describes it *as* the establishment. For a district that is Concordia's official research sector — funded, chartered, and central — that whole register has to be built rather than mined. The supplementary pass supplies only two small footholds, both from Silva's work chapter and both about *keeping* a position rather than holding one: neither Aquarius nor Leo will chase stimulation "at the risk of losing their job," and Aquarians "have to admit that a certain security level is necessary for a successful career." That is the corpus's entire account of this sign with something to lose.
- **A genuine source disagreement, not noise.** Snodgrass alone describes the Aquarian as "**deficient in concentration and slow at reading**," one who "appears to dilly-dally," "inclined toward acquiescence," and earning "a reputation for slow-wittedness, impracticality, and inefficiency." Every other source in the corpus calls the sign brilliant. Snodgrass is the most scholarly book here, so this is worth preserving rather than flattening: it may be recording an *older* Saturn-dominated reading of the sign, from before Uranus's rulership recast it as genius. Usable as a real divergence between how the district sees itself and how it is described from outside. **The supplementary pass partially reconciles it, from inside Silva.** Her school section describes children who "excel and achieve the highest grades in subjects that they find interesting" but "may even fail the classes they do not like or find boring," and are therefore "labeled as being apathetic or even dumb"; she names "laziness" as what holds Aquarian students back; her work chapter calls the sign "gullible" and, through Virgo's eyes, "a little inefficient." So this is not Snodgrass against the corpus — the contrarian portrait is present *inside* the corpus's most enthusiastic source, as the outside view of selective effort. Keep both readings; the divergence is now better founded, not weaker, and the gap between self-image and outside description has a stated mechanism behind it.
- **Silva's chapter has now been extracted in full** (supplementary pass; the earlier slice was one block of eleven, and the note that "there is no Aquarius Man section and no career or child-rearing chapters" was an artifact of our slicing, not of the book). New material used above: Chapter Three (cusps → §11 borders), Chapter Four (the child and the teenager → §5, §7), Chapter Five (friendship → §2, §3), Chapter Seven (work → §6, §8, §9), the Aquarius Man section, and the Woman's money/work/health/dress material. **Two things are still genuinely missing.** There is no "What Does the Aquarius Need?" chapter in this book at all — §5 is assembled from autonomy material scattered through the child, teen and Man/Woman sections rather than from a dedicated chapter — and the extraction still ends mid-sentence inside Chapter Seven's Aquarius-and-Pisces workplace entry, so no health or money chapter survives beyond what the Man/Woman sections give.
- **Two live contradictions inside Silva, both worth keeping rather than flattening.** *Credit*: the same book says the acts of improvement are done "because they enjoy it, not for praise or credit," and that at work they "will always strive to be recognized for their personal contributions too." *Empathy*: the adult chapters make emotional detachment the sign's defining fault, while the child chapter makes uncanny empathy its defining gift. Neither is noise — §3 uses the first as an attribution problem, §7 uses the second to make the coldness acquired rather than innate.
- **Decans rest on one source** (Emerson) and **the two border cusps on another** (Silva). No corroboration available for either, and they are not two accounts of the same thing — they divide the sign along different axes, so they can be used together but neither checks the other.
