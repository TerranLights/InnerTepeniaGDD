## 1. What the Elements Actually Group

The four groupings do not sort the twelve signs by *what they want* — that is the Enneagram layer, held in `Regional-Characteristics/district_by_Enneagram_group_series.md`. They sort by **how experience is processed before it becomes a want**: which channel of information a temperament trusts, and which it discounts.

Martin frames it as two opposed pairs on a truth axis. **Fire and earth contest personal truth**: for fire the truth is subjective and needs no proof — "a religious experience or personal conviction, which is absolutely real to the individual but which cannot be, and does not need to be, proved, explained or justified"; for earth the truth is objective but equally personal, "arising from earth's own experience, via the five senses, of the outside world. If earth doesn't like the smell, taste or colour of something, then that is that." **Air and water contest collective truth**: for air it is "out there in the systems created in the external world," for water it is "in here," in the emotional undercurrents "which belong to us all."

Why four, and why three signs each: the twelve-sign frame is built so that each element contains exactly one of the three modalities (see `B_Modalities.md`). An element is therefore never uniform — it appears once as an initiator, once as a holder, once as a distributor. Martin: "Each of the elements becomes progressively more 'mature' in the order of the zodiac signs." Tierney adds the sequence rule: fire always opens each cycle and water always closes it, with earth immediately following fire in every case — "perhaps fire's expression reaches a point where it becomes overenergized in dangerous ways that run rampant. Fire then becomes a destructive force. Earth acts as" the brake.

Two conventions carried in from the sources and used below: fire and air are the **positive/yang/outward** pair, earth and water the **negative/yin/receptive** pair (Silva, Emerson); and Emerson's crude but load-bearing formula, **"fire needs air to burn; earth can't live without water."**

Martin's element-*absence* rule is the most directly usable structural note in the whole file: a chart with no earth cannot manufacture one — "we cannot manufacture an earth function for ourselves if none of the planets are there." Applied to districts, this says a district missing a temperament does not develop a weak version of it. It develops a **substitute**, visibly bolted on, and the substitute never fully works.

## 2. Fire — Aries, Leo, Sagittarius

**Mode of operation:** acts first, on subjective conviction, and treats the world as material for a personal drama. Tierney: fire is "our human ability to move bravely out into the world according to the spontaneous impulses of our desire," and daredevils "are born of the fire element." Martin: fire signs "have an innate sense of their own unique specialness, which explains why they have a tremendous faith in themselves and why they will take the kind of risks that other elements shrink from."

**Good at:** starting things nothing else would start. Contagious warmth; morale as an actual output. Recovery — "even when they crash and burn, fire signs normally have the faith to pick themselves up and carry on." Risk-taking that is not bravado but genuine unconcern with odds.

**Characteristic blindness:** actual facts. Martin: "'Objective truths', such as the statistical odds against winning the lottery, are meaningless to fire signs and often a cause of incomprehension, frustration and impatience." Also the body — "many fire signs have an active dislike of their bodies, which are too confining, too restrictive." And other people's interiors: fire "can appear to trample on other people's feelings and sensitivities without even noticing," with "nothing devious or malicious about it" — which is precisely why it never gets corrected.

**Shadow / failure mode:** the vision goes unfed and the element distorts rather than dies — Martin names the three distortions exactly: "violent and destructive, in the case of Aries, demanding and jealous in the case of Leo, or arrogant and patronising in the case of Sagittarius." Underneath is the corpus's most counterintuitive fire finding: **fire has no stamina.** "Fire signs can be very vulnerable and tend to have a curious lack of stamina. They can easily be overwhelmed by the resistances which the world throws at them, and it is not unusual to meet fire signs who seem to have been 'put out'."

**Needs from others:** fuel and containment, which are the same thing. "Fire cannot burn unless it is consuming some kind of matter, such as coal or wood, both of which belong to the earth element. This explains both the repulsion and attraction that exists between fire and earth." Martin's summary: "it is the practical harnessing of the element of earth which supports and hones and shapes the true potential of the fire signs."

**Internal differentiation:** *Aries* (cardinal) is primal and fastest — "demands the fastest results and can be quite impatient when time seems to drag," with "a very short attention span, but that's okay, since it doesn't need a lot of time to react." Tierney's image is a pilot light left permanently on. *Leo* (fixed) is contained fire — "the steady glow of logs in a fireplace"; "the most dependable of the fire signs, and one that is capable of great loyalty," but living "at the centre of his own myth," instinctively expecting "to be waited on, served and cleaned up after." *Sagittarius* (mutable) is fire distributed — "a lightning-instigated, raging forest fire… ablaze, triggered by a bolt from the blue, that quickly spreads far and wide." Most mature, most restless, and the one that mistakes its own conviction for the general truth: "it claims to seek the ultimate reality underlying anything, but it has a gullible, credulous nature."

## 3. Earth — Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn

**Mode of operation:** verifies through the five senses, then builds something that lasts. Tierney: earth is "savvy when it comes to understanding the material world and figuring out what can and cannot be accomplished with available resources," running on "an instinctual know-how that is not based on mere intellectual knowledge." It is the element that makes ordinary systems keep working: "Earth is the custodial element of life, dutifully straightening up the world whenever it gets out of control."

**Good at:** perseverance, endurance, realism, delivery. Manifesting — turning intention into an object. Resource accounting. Also, uniquely, the body: earth signs "unlike the other elements tend not to repress or undervalue the body's needs for proper nourishment, rest and exercise."

**Characteristic blindness:** that the tangible layer is the only real layer. Tierney: earth signs "steadily focus on this most evident level of experience, yet they run the risk of assuming that this tangible" world is all there is. Consequently it cannot see the point of anything unproven, and will not sit in a room "endlessly discussing concepts and theories."

**Shadow / failure mode:** calcification, then cynicism. Martin: "a marked lack of enthusiasm, hope, faith and optimism, which can result in a kind of cynical disappointment with life, a sense that the world has 'let them down'." Silva names the structural version: earth "will prefer to settle for a life where they don't feel happy or appreciated enough — emotionally, intellectually, or financially — just to maintain the sense of security they've created for themselves." And the enforcement version: earth keeps chaos at bay "mainly by devising and enforcing necessary rules and regulations that ensure order," which is custodianship one step from policing.

**Needs from others:** disruption it did not choose. Silva is explicit that the remedy is the element earth gets on worst with: "one of the biggest challenges for any Earth sign is dealing with sudden change, [which] creates a lack of compatibility with the Air element… but that's also why Taurus needs the Air elements to grow and prosper." It also needs water for permission to feel anything about what it has built.

**Internal differentiation:** *Taurus* (fixed) is the most static sign in the frame — "no other sign is as entrenched in its attitudes," and stubbornness has it "tackling things the slow, hard way" out of "deep, internal resistance… especially when it is being manipulated by external forces." *Virgo* (mutable) is earth made workable — "potter's clay: solid and somewhat heavy, but malleable"; the regulator, whose job "is to regulate the body's various systems so that none of them become prematurely worn out from unhealthy overstimulation," designing "a dimmer switch that will help tone things down and save energy from being wasted." *Capricorn* (cardinal) is the only earth sign that will leave the known: "the most willing to venture forth into unknown territory to take a few calculated risks, something Taurus and Virgo have trouble doing… the only earth sign with the momentum to achieve prominence on a grand scale."

## 4. Air — Gemini, Libra, Aquarius

⚠ **Aquarius is an air sign.** The water-bearer imagery misleads constantly; Snodgrass states it flatly — "Despite the obvious connection to water, Aquarius is an air sign." Libra is mis-sorted the same way for the opposite reason: Martin warns Libra "is often automatically assumed to be a Water sign" because it rules relationships, and it is not. Both errors, if they reach a district draft, will produce exactly the wrong culture.

**Mode of operation:** abstracts, compares, articulates, and keeps a deliberate gap between itself and the material. Tierney: air is "the brainiest of all the elements, the one that loves to live life in its head and to create brilliant ideas to share with" others, and its altitude is the point — "the higher up we go, the more widely we can observe the terrain below. Details may seem less sharp," but the pattern appears. Martin: air signs are "the most civilized, most social, detached, abstract, rational and, in fact, the most 'human' of all the signs" — and note that they are the only ones with human or abstract emblems rather than animals.

**Good at:** perspective, negotiation, arbitration, standards. Language discipline — "not a big fan of gibberish, air demands that language be clear and precise if real communication is truly our objective." Holding two opposed positions at once without collapsing into either.

**Characteristic blindness:** its own feelings, and other people's. Air will discuss emotion expertly as a subject: air signs "can tell you a great deal about all the different theories of emotion and feelings, but this can often be a very clever defense against actually having to feel anything for themselves." Martin's panel anecdote is the compact version — asked whether the soul exists, air concluded it did not, "because its existence had not been sufficiently proven according to the air signs' criteria of objective, collective truth," and "it is not uncommon for the air signs to talk about things they know nothing about."

**Shadow / failure mode:** rationalizing a problem out of existence instead of solving it. Martin: "Shaking hands after a dispute attends to the form but does nothing to resolve the emotional undercurrent of the problem." When the suppressed material does surface it arrives as possession, not expression — the client "extremely embarrassed by her emotional outburst" who says *I don't know what came over me*. Air therefore "will run away if there is any danger of things getting messy."

**Needs from others:** access, and something real to be accountable to. Tierney's political note is the sharpest thing in the corpus on air: "In countries where access to certain information is forbidden by the ruling parties, or where illiteracy prevails, the expanded potential of the air signs is impeded. The air in each of us evolves the more we're free to know things; censorship thus thwarts air's natural development." Air also fears one specific thing — "social isolation, something that all air signs dread."

**Internal differentiation:** *Gemini* (mutable) is air at its most self-directed — "more self-involved with its knowledge-gathering than are the other air signs," skimming "the surface before moving on to something new," and actively unable to sit with emotion: it "quickly wants to take its feelings apart to figure them out — anything to avoid merely feeling feelings on their own terms." *Libra* (cardinal) forces issues into the open and trades: "there is always a quid pro quo where Libra is concerned; nothing is given unconditionally by Libra because this upsets the balance." *Aquarius* (fixed) is the anomaly of its own modality — where Taurus, Leo and Scorpio hold on by force, "Aquarius can suddenly revolutionize its behavior given the right environmental conditions," because "air signs aren't into attachments that much." Tierney's image for fixed air is an oxygen tank: air under pressure inside a container. Its fixity lives in *ideals*, not possessions — "fixed ideas and ideals, or, perhaps, the structured intellect or rigid thinking."

## 5. Water — Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces

**Mode of operation:** absorbs. Water reads what was not said and disregards the boundary it was not invited through. Tierney: "Its instinct is to ignore all boundaries and instead to quietly seep into otherwise inaccessible places that are hidden from view." Martin: water signs "tend to absorb the emotions and feelings behind what is being said rather than the words themselves," and are "extremely sensitive to atmosphere, picking up subtle feelings about places and people."

**Good at:** reading hidden motive — "an advantage when it comes to perceiving another's hidden motivation. They are drawn to what is secretly going on below the surface of people's observable behavior." Unconditional support: "if you are upset or in pain, water can offer support, no matter what you have done or why you are feeling that way," precisely because "unlike air, they will not try and rationalize the problem away." Endurance under psychological load — "its deep, uncanny understanding of human nature suggests it has the power to withstand much turmoil before collapsing." Imagination, which Tierney assigns to water outright.

**Characteristic blindness:** its own edges. "A lack of boundaries can also imply poor self-preservation instincts. Water signs can be reluctant to fend off the dominating influences of others; they are not fighters by nature." Also proportion — without a container it has no shape and no perspective, producing "a kind of emotional chaos, or getting waterlogged, like drowning in one's emotions."

**Shadow / failure mode:** two, and they are opposite ends of one mechanism. Outward: water "can get sucked into" someone else's turbulence, or *become* the turbulence — "sometimes they become the troubled ones who pull others deeply into their vortex." Inward, and far more useful for design — Tierney: "Water signs, when repressed for long periods, suffer psychological damage, as their injured parts sink deeper within to slowly fester and become toxic." Water does not discharge a grievance. It stores it, and the store degrades.

**Needs from others:** a container and a filter. Tierney prescribes the filter explicitly, as borrowing from the other three: water "needs to learn to devise a better filtering system by borrowing a few natural defenses that the other elements use — such as fire's self-interest, earth's territorial streak," and air's detachment. It also needs solitude, which the corpus treats as a genuine requirement rather than a preference: "water signs crave solitude," and cultures that read that as pathology and tell water types to "get out more" are doing damage.

**Internal differentiation:** *Cancer* (cardinal) is the demonstrative one — "the most demonstrative of the water signs" and, thanks to cardinality, unable to conceal: "it can't easily hide what it's really going through, at least not for long, unlike more inscrutable Scorpio or Pisces." It is also tidal; Martin: when the tide goes out "you will get nothing but the shell. You can knock on the shell but there is nobody there." And it sets a reciprocal condition that becomes a whole social rule — "Cancer expects to hear only what it can handle emotionally." *Scorpio* (fixed) is "a very deep, dark, still well"; fixity "helps stabilize water's emotionality, cutting down on mood swings and panicky reactions," so it "can appear amazingly composed in the face of impending danger" and "does a marvelous job when handling crisis situations." Its function is purgative: where something is "emotionally polluted or distorted, they will feel compelled to bring it to the surface in order that it can be eliminated." It also makes one specific error — "the mistake of equating vulnerability with weakness." *Pisces* (mutable) is "the chameleon of the zodiac, taking on the shape and colour of its environment," so inclusive that Martin opens by saying it is "very difficult to define, or to get any kind of intellectual grip on"; the only water sign "capable of grand, abstract overviews of life."

## 6. How the Elements Wear On Each Other

The value of this section is that the sources are specific about *mechanism* — what each element does to the other, not merely whether they get on.

**Fire ↔ Earth — the fuel/smother pair.** Ask what earth does to fire and Martin's audience answers immediately: "Puts it out. Smothers it, suffocates it." The paradox is that fire *needs* what smothers it, because coal and wood are earth. So the friction is never about goals; it is about pace and permission. What earth finds intolerable, behaviorally, is fire's exemption-seeking: fire will not do the administrative substrate of its own plan, and treats being asked to as an insult — Martin's example is a tax return, which "is an affront to fire, which doesn't see the need to justify its existence to anyone or to obey the rules which, after all, only apply to ordinary mortals." Fire also does not clean up. Martin's party image is exact and reusable: the Virgos "going round cleaning the ashtrays or in the kitchen washing up, while the fire signs are in full flow." What fire finds intolerable is being metered — Tierney: "fire signs find earth signs to be classic party poopers, but earth signs demand that we shut down for a while to conserve our vitality." Silva's Taurus/Sagittarius verdict is the pair at its worst: "scorched earth is the best way to describe what happens," because the archer sees possessions as "a chain… an anchor, and they hate that." And Silva's warning on the long game: earth signs "often have the potential to restrain [fire], but even they might feel it's a lost cause after a while." Earth gives up on fire before fire gives up on itself.

**Fire ↔ Air — the combustion pair, with one hidden knife.** The formula is real: air feeds fire, and Silva's images are combustive — "sparks, combustion… this can create a fire that burns intensely forever. But it can burn itself out"; fire and air together make "a fire tornado." Structurally they share polarity, tempo, and a tolerance for individualism, and air supplies fire with the planning fire lacks. The friction is narrow but sharp, and it runs one way: **air is the element that can kill a fire idea with a fact.** Martin: "Fire's subjective vision and enthusiasm can easily be crushed by air's rational analysis of the actual facts." Fire's counter is not argument but exit — asked what fire does when air starts theorizing, Martin says fire "would get impatient and just leave air to carry on debating with itself, because fire is subjective and doesn't really care what other people think." So the pair does not fight; it produces a lot of energy and a lot of abandoned conversations.

**Fire ↔ Water — the mutual-annihilation pair, and the one that starts best.** Both sides lose something they need. Water douses: Martin, "water can douse the heat and enthusiasm"; Silva, Pisces is "a true water sign that likes to put a flame out." Fire dehydrates: "there's also the risk that a fire sign could make the water sign feel dried up, that they may not receive the nourishment they need." Behaviorally, fire's offense is volume and exposure — Silva: "the Scorpio might be exhausted by Fire signs because of their ultra-grand, larger-than-life aura," and fire's honesty arrives without a filter into a temperament that runs entirely on filtration. Tierney gives fire's side as contempt: fire "has trouble respecting" a water sign that "worries too much about the threat of a potentially shaky future. The Crab can sometimes be so spineless." And the danger is that this is the pairing with the most attractive opening: "Cancers frequently jump into relationships with fire signs because the feelings and emotions at the start are too intense to turn down." High initial heat, sustained mutual depletion — the most useful friction shape in the file for a long-running district feud.

**Earth ↔ Air — the pair that talks past each other without ever raising its voice.** No hostility, no chemistry, no comprehension. Silva's Libra/Capricorn line is the whole mechanism in one sentence: "Libra considers their mind of great value, and Capricorn doesn't care about words if they don't see results." Martin's earth response to a theory session: earth "would not see the point of sitting around endlessly discussing concepts and theories." Air experiences earth as unbearably slow and literal; earth experiences air as "the fastest and most unpredictable" element and as an unreliability dressed in confidence — Silva calls the Capricorn/Aquarius mismatch "a different pace because of their different elements," Capricorn "thorough and slow," Aquarius "unreliable and slightly flaky." Silva's practical ruling is worth keeping: Libra and earth can "get along fine, certainly enough to get a job done," but long-term "these signs will ultimately remain a mystery" to each other. This is the pairing that produces *procedural* conflict — committees, standards disputes, unratified agreements — rather than grievance.

**Earth ↔ Water — the natural fit, with a specific trapdoor.** Same polarity, complementary lack. Silva: "Water and Earth are elements that go together naturally, offering mutual benefits… [water] can offer the emotional support that Earth signs need so they can comfortably share their feelings. As for the Earth sign, they have more than enough patience to deal with [water's] moodiness and complex nature. They are of the Earth; and like the Earth, they will always be there." Earth supplies water the container it cannot generate; water supplies earth the interior life its own realism suppresses. The trapdoor is that earth is not equipped for what it unlocks: Silva, on water softening reserved earth signs, "in doing that they may open a box of emotions they weren't prepared to handle." Second-order risk: two receptive elements in agreement produce comfort and no correction, which is how earth's cynical settling and water's festering storage both go unchallenged.

**Air ↔ Water — the opposition, and the corpus's best single exchange.** Martin's dialogue with a mother whose son is a water sign is the most reusable friction material in the file. She complains she cannot get a straight answer. Martin: "You won't get a straight answer because your son is a water sign. You will get deflection, deviation and changing the subject and you will get a lot of other stuff, but what is really happening is you are not getting the answer. This is not accidental, but an example of a watery defensive" maneuver. Air experiences this as evasion; water experiences the demand itself as intrusion. From the other direction, what water finds intolerable is that air *solves the feeling by explaining it* — air will "rationalize the problem away, which can be a way of ignoring and undervaluing the feelings." And Silva records that air's limit is not attitude but capacity: air signs "quite literally can't handle intense emotional displays and requirements from others." Yet the two are also each other's medicine — Silva: "air signs create a strong foundation for water to communicate their feelings," and water is the only element that can make air's shadow accessible, since "the shadow for the air signs will be the element of water." The pairing's characteristic outcome is a relationship that works excellently until it becomes serious, then fails on the exact axis that made it work.

**Fire ↔ Fire.** Instant recognition, matched tempo, and no ballast. Silva: "fire signs burn bright together, and although they may not be lifelong friends, they can have a great time." The failure is structural rather than personal — two things that both need to be the center: "you both wish to be the alpha… it is like playing a game of hot potato," and one submits "only through gritted teeth." Silva's own escalation note: rivalry "might not always be bad if the rivalry is healthy and productive. When taken to unhealthy extremes, though, rivalries tend to spiral into conflict." Nothing in the pairing supplies a stopping mechanism.

**Earth ↔ Earth.** Genuinely compatible and genuinely closed. Silva: two earth signs "can walk at the same pace. While other signs may feel like this couple moves too slowly, it goes exactly as needed" — and the tell is external: "onlookers from the fire or air signs can rarely bear the flow of conversation between two earth signs. However, for these two signs, it is a completely enjoyable experience." Silva elsewhere: "a double earth sign is likely going to exhaust some people." Earth with earth does not generate friction; it generates a shared reality other temperaments cannot enter.

**Air ↔ Air.** Endless stream of ideas, endless deferral. Silva on the double-air combination is unusually blunt: "the catch here is that you avoid feeling. For you, it is easier to talk, draw, and materialize it than go through each of your emotions completely." Two air positions can debate indefinitely without either being obliged to conclude — Martin's panel, doubled. The pairing produces excellent analysis and no decisions with emotional cost attached.

**Water ↔ Water.** Total comprehension, no differentiation. Silva: "when two water signs are together, it feels as if they become one. They are not even aware of their internal differences anymore because they mirror everything they do and agree with it" — and the volume warning: "too much water and you can have excessive flooding." The specific behavior is non-verbal deadlock: two water types "can figure out what the other person needs without them saying a word, but these signs together are likely to have standoffs full of pouting and sulking when they don't see eye to eye." A dispute between two water temperaments never becomes an argument, so it never gets resolved and never ends.

## 7. The Enclosed-City Filter

**Derived, not sourced.** The books assume an open world with weather, distance, and exit. Concordia is sealed, dark for months, and metered against a single shared heat and power budget. Running each temperament through that changes the odds substantially, and the derivation is directly usable.

**Fire is the temperament enclosure punishes hardest — and it is punished in a physically literal way.** Martin says fire's replenishment channels are sunlight ("people with an emphasis on fire really need plenty of sunshine, since the sun… actually restores their energy levels and their vitality"), plus "physical exercise, travel and adventure." A sealed polar city removes the first two outright and rations the third. Combine that with the corpus's counterintuitive finding that fire "has a curious lack of stamina" and can be "put out" by accumulated obstruction, and enclosure predicts not extinguished fire but **distorted fire** — Martin's three distortions arriving early and permanently. Note the mapping consequence, because it is not obvious: two of the three districts in this grouping sit exactly where enclosure is survivable for them. The Power Core is where the heat budget is *made* rather than merely spent, and the Frostlands' entire function is the outside. Leo has no such outlet — which predicts a very specific culture: performance with nowhere else to perform, reputation as the only currency, and feuds nobody can walk away from because there is no away.

**Earth becomes more viable, and more dangerous.** A single shared budget is a resource-accounting problem, which is the one problem earth is natively equipped for — "figuring out what can and cannot be accomplished with available resources." Virgo's dimmer switch, scaled up, is literally a load-management policy. Earth's characteristic blindness — that only the tangible layer is real — costs far less in an environment where the tangible layer genuinely is the binding constraint. The cost lands elsewhere: enclosure hands earth a permanent, unanswerable justification for control, and the corpus already flags that earth keeps chaos at bay "mainly by devising and enforcing necessary rules and regulations." Custodianship with no exit and no appeal is administration sliding toward coercion. Second failure: Silva's "settle for a life where they don't feel happy or appreciated enough, just to maintain the sense of security" stops being a choice when there is nowhere to settle *for* instead.

**Air is the least physically strained and the most politically exposed.** Air's medium is language and information, which travel a sealed city at effectively no thermal cost; and enclosure permanently solves the one thing "all air signs dread," social isolation. But Tierney's censorship rule bites hard here: a sealed city has exactly one information regime, and "the air in each of us evolves the more we're free to know things; censorship thus thwarts air's natural development." Air's viability in Concordia is therefore not a function of the dome but of who controls the record — which puts the Circuit, the Government District, and the Labs on a shared vulnerability none of them can solve locally. Air's specific enclosure pathology is that nothing ever forces closure: everyone remains permanently reachable, so the debate never has to end, and Libra's quid pro quo becomes an unclosable ledger with people you will never stop dealing with.

**Water gets the environment it reads best and the one it survives worst.** In a sealed city the atmosphere genuinely *is* collective and shared, which makes water the only temperament reading the instrument correctly. But water "takes in other people's energies indiscriminately" and has "poor self-preservation instincts" — and enclosure removes both distance and the solitude the corpus says water actually requires. The decisive line is Tierney's: repressed water's "injured parts sink deeper within to slowly fester and become toxic." Grievance in a sealed city has nowhere to drain, so it accumulates, and water is the element that stores rather than discharges. That single mechanism should generate three different district answers to one question — *what do you do with what cannot be expelled?* — and the Sanctuary, Scorpio, and the Markets should each have solved it differently and incompatibly. Add Tierney's cultural note as a city-wide pressure: "in heavily industrialized nations, where a pragmatic, materialistic, even scientific intellect is highly prized, it's our water-sign experience that suffers." Concordia is an engineered survival machine — that condition at maximum. Water is structurally undervalued citywide, by the city's own design logic, and that is an engine rather than a flaw.

**One further derivation worth keeping.** Fire is the only one of the four whose defining requirement is *literally metered* in this setting. Heat and power are quantified, allocated, and contested every day. That converts an out-of-fiction temperament grouping into a real in-fiction political axis — how much of the budget goes to things that burn rather than things that hold — without the zodiac, the elements, or any astrological framing ever surfacing in the fiction. Use that route.

## 9. Source Gaps

- **The fire-exhaustion passage — RESOLVED 2026-08-29, and it IS genuine Tierney.** The extracting agent correctly reported that this passage was absent from `_Elements.txt` and declined to attribute it. That was the right call on the evidence available, but the cause was a slicing defect, not a bad citation: the Elements keyword pattern matched `\belemental\b` and phrases like "fire sign," and this passage uses neither — it says "elements" (plural) and refers to fire by bare name. It has since been located and verified in the source text. The full passage, with OCR artifacts cleaned and marked:

  > "…in the process of exuberantly expressing who they are, fire types can deplete the energy of others. They can indulge in too much of a good thing, and in an uncontained manner. We tend to assume that fire people give of themselves in abundance — they warmly and generously radiate their light and heat — but they also need fresh energy sources to refuel themselves. It's exhausting to be around people who colorfully flare out all the time, who happily expend themselves, but then consume some of your vitality just to keep their own blaze going. Fire is thus a very demanding, even draining, force to be around for long periods of time. It can wear out other less-energetic elements — most so earth and water, who prefer cooler temperatures, anyway."
  > — Tierney, *All Around the Zodiac*

  **This is safe to quote as Tierney.** Note it also directly corroborates the "fire has no stamina" finding in §2 — fire is draining to others *and* needs external refuelling, which is the same mechanism seen from both sides. Note further that the OCR is heavy here (`demanding,evendraining`, `canwear`, `blazegoing`, `elementhas`), so quote from the cleaned version above rather than re-extracting raw.

- **Slicing lesson worth carrying forward:** thematic keyword slices can miss passages that discuss a topic without using its keyword. If this folder is ever extended, prefer over-inclusive patterns (here, `element` unanchored rather than `\belemental\b`) and accept the noise.
- **Snodgrass contains at least one outright element error**, and it should not be trusted unverified on classification: "Because Capricorn is a water sign associated with fresh and salt water, it profits from a conciliatory connection that tempers Saturn's harshness." Capricorn is earth in every other source in the corpus. Snodgrass appears to be reasoning from the sea-goat's tail. Snodgrass remains excellent on correspondences, myth, and topography — use it there, not for classification.
- **Snodgrass also dissents sharply on water's temperament**, describing the water triad as "persuaders and fun-lovers" who "easily motivate others and enjoy close personal relationships." Every other source in the corpus describes water as private, withdrawn, solitude-craving, and non-verbal. Recorded rather than flattened; Martin and Tierney are the stronger sources here and §5 follows them.
- **Air-to-air friction is under-served.** The corpus is rich on every cross-element pairing and on water/water and earth/earth same-element dynamics, but has almost nothing structural on two air temperaments in sustained conflict beyond "they will get on well and talk a lot." §6's air/air entry is thinner than the others and is partly inferred from air's documented avoidance behavior.
- **No source addresses element behavior under confinement, resource scarcity, or environmental extremity.** The books assume an open world with exit available. §7 is entirely derived, and is flagged as such in place.
- **Topography correspondences are one line long and stop at Gemini.** Snodgrass gives "Aries tends toward the desert, Taurus to the plains, and Gemini to pairs of crags or peaks" and the extraction truncates before the remaining nine. If per-district landscape texture is wanted, that passage needs re-extraction from the Snodgrass source directly.
- **Nothing in the corpus discusses an element's relationship to a thirteenth, unassigned member.** The Hub's exclusion from this system is structural and correct, but it is unsupported by source and rests on the reasoning in `00_Method_and_Sources.md`.
