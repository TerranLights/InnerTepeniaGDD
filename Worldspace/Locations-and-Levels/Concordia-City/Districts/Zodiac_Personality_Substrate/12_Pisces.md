# Pisces — Personality Substrate

**Maps to:** District 10 — Pisces (The Markets) · black market/underworld
**Status:** Design substrate only — NOT in-fiction. See `00_Method_and_Sources.md`.
**Sources mined:** Silva *Ultimate Guide* (behavioral bulk, shadow, decans, cusps) · Snodgrass (myth, correspondences, pathology, topography) · Martin *Mapping the Psyche* v1–v3 (Jungian depth, Virgo axis, rulership split) · Tierney (modality/element structure, the sharpest shadow material in the corpus) · Silva *Sun & Moon* (rulership, compatibility) · Emerson (decans, aspect tables, the twelfth-house crime line)

## 1. Core Archetype & Driving Motive

The last sign — the one that dissolves what the other eleven built. Silva calls its house "the house in which the self is deconstructed"; Silva's *Sun & Moon* names it flatly the **House of Sacrifice**; Emerson lists its domain as "emotional and mental health, **addiction, crime**, fears, sleep, and **secrets**." Tierney supplies the mechanism most useful here: when Taurus is taught that it owns nothing and that its possessions must eventually be "recycled at a later date," that job is **assigned to Pisces**. This is a district that exists to take apart what other districts consider settled property — goods, identities, obligations, memories — and put the components back into circulation.

Driving motive is **not** acquisition. Martin: Pisces is "the part in all of us that never wanted to incarnate in the first place and longs to return to formlessness and oblivion"; "'Reality' is often too difficult, harsh, brutal and restrictive." The motive is *release from having a fixed shape* — and the market is the machinery that sells that release to other people while running on it internally.

One structural note that earns its place at the top: as the last sign, Pisces "represents the cumulative experiences of all the other 11 signs," which Silva calls "both a tremendous burden and an extravagant gift." The Markets are the one district that holds a working fragment of every other district — not as an archive but as inventory.

## 2. Light Expression (the district at its best)

- **Reads people without being told anything.** Silva: Pisces can talk someone off a ledge "understanding what's going on, emotionally, with other people, without being apprised of the situation. They just know." No interrogation, no file, no analysis.
- **Genuine redistribution.** Silva's Pisces child: "'Me' and 'mine' aren't part of Pisces Baby's vocabulary… they believe that everything should be shared… no one should go without while others have more than they need." Snodgrass's period doggerel: "Who prays and serves… **And feeds the beggar at the door**." The compassionate-smuggling half is not a veneer over the predation; the corpus makes it primary.
- **Nobody is turned away.** Tierney: "Their door is always wide open, letting anything enter." As virtue: the only place that will still transact with you after everywhere else has stopped.
- **Refusal of degrading work as a principle, not laziness.** Silva: "Hard work is just backs being broken to make other people wealthy, and Pisces doesn't play that." "The moral universe of the Pisces bends toward justice."
- **Beauty as a peak function, not decoration** — Venus is *exalted* here. Martin: acute sensitivity to "music, dance, art, fantasy, film, fashion and romance." The district's aesthetics are its most potent export.
- **Sees worth in what everyone else discarded.** The single most district-relevant thing in Silva's needs chapter, and it is filed under *romance*, not commerce: "a trip to the second-hand store… Beautiful vintage clothes inspire and light Pisces up and even second-hand clothes nobody else wants, Pisces sees the potential to make something beautiful out of even the lowliest polyester leisure suit. **The thrill of the hunt** satisfies their curiosity and the thrill of finding what they didn't even imagine they needed… even more so." Salvage as pleasure rather than as trade. The same taste shows up in the home — "outsider" art by makers "who normally get little attention," and rescued animals. This is the nearest thing the corpus has to a native market instinct, and it is a *scavenger's* instinct, not a merchant's: the reward is the find, not the margin.
- **Vindicated foresight.** Silva: Pisces "proposes a solution that sounds impossible or impractical. It is ignored until someone higher up the food chain clues in, and then Pisces is proven right." Reliably correct, reliably too early, reliably uncredited.

## 3. Shadow / Dark Side (failure modes, vices, pathologies)

The corpus's Pisces shadow is unusually specific and lands almost entirely on a vice district. The recovered material roughly doubles it, and the addition is all of one kind: this sign's aggression is **social and retaliatory, never acquisitive**. It does not take things from people. It ends people. See the prey/predator note in §15.

- **No filter, by construction.** Tierney: the Fishes "seem to lack the psychological filters needed to reject that which is unhealthy to body, mind, and spirit. Their door is always wide open, letting anything enter — including all that is undesirable and even **sabotaging on a soul level**." The same open door is the light and the shadow; there is no version with a filter.
- **…except that the filter exists and fires early — it just does not close the door.** The recovered Silva flatly contradicts Tierney here, and the contradiction is the useful part. "Pisces has a sixth sense for problem people, toxic people, narcissists, **exploiters**, and anyone else who doesn't have the best of intentions. They can smell it, and if they smell it on you, you're over"; "**a Pisces can spot a phony a mile away**." Both are true at once in practice: the detection is excellent and the *exclusion* only applies to the inner circle. Anyone can transact at the door; almost nobody gets past it. The district knows precisely who is robbing it and serves them anyway, because trading with the untrustworthy is the business and the vetting is reserved for intimacy.
- **Absorbs and cannot afterward source it.** Tierney: "What it vividly feels inside itself typically stems from outside sources." Martin: planets here are "psychic sponges" — take on the mood of whoever walks in "without knowing why." A district that cannot tell which of its convictions were originally its own.
- **Deception that begins as accommodation.** Martin on Mercury in Pisces: "everything is relative and the truth can be anything you want it to be. If you want black to be white, then that is fine." An audience member calls it lying; Martin's counter is the important part — he is "more likely to respond to you on an emotional level… by telling you what you want to hear." Fraud here does not start as a plan. It starts as courtesy and hardens.
- **Unable or unwilling to discriminate.** Martin: "you can have whichever piece of it you would like, since it doesn't matter either way." A merchant with no fixed price and no fixed truth, for the same reason.
- **Victimhood as leverage.** Martin, the single most usable shadow line in the slice: "**addiction to vulnerability, weakness and self-sacrifice which can be extremely masochistic**," coupled with "a refusal to establish healthy boundaries or to take any kind of responsibility." Emerson's second decan warns explicitly of "**leeching on to other people**."
- **Bottomless need presenting as generosity.** Martin on Moon in Pisces: "there are no boundaries or limits to our neediness… forever hungry and thirsty, feeling that our needs can never be fulfilled," ending in addiction, and in "driving others away, since they can become **drowned** by our neediness." Neptune is "where we are always thirsty, where we always want more."
- **The sanctified con.** Tierney: "Pisces can con itself into believing its own grandiose, spiritual hype — **who's to prove otherwise?** — while using its charisma to…"; types perceive themselves as "cosmic ambassadors from higher dimensions." The self-deception comes *first*; the fraud on others is downstream and sincere. Also the "unreal yearning for sainthood" through which "the ego comes out of hiding and swells out of proportion."
- **Escape as the standing answer.** Neptune "rules drugs and alcohol and any addictions which help us lose ourselves." Silva: Pisces are "either a teetotaler (having learned their lesson about booze) or a madcap dancing on the pool table" — no moderate register — and drink to "dull the energies coming at them."
- **Ego-dissolution mistaken for virtue.** Tierney: "to just senselessly undergo an ego-disappearing act — and confusing it with true spirituality — is a major blind spot of this sign."
- **The permanent cold — with one key, and it is not restitution.** Silva: cross Pisces deliberately and "you will encounter a wall of utter silence… **You have died to them. You are no more. You are an ex-person.** When the fish goes cold, it stays cold." The fuller text adds the exact terms of re-entry, and they matter for a district: unintentional offense "will thaw out eventually," but "**Pisces only gives second chances to those who ask for them**." Not paid for, not worked off, not adjudicated — *asked for*. Someone who does not grasp that they need to ask is simply gone: "your phone number has been permanently lost and… you're blocked on every conceivable social media channel." And a grudge "can endure until the Apocalypse if you don't clear things up."
- **Idealization, then the shutdown that makes you come to them.** The underrated first stage. Silva: Pisces connects with people they have "read" as safe, which "can lead to the idealization of the people they have connected with," and then "they will respond poorly to perceived slights" — a canceled meeting, an uncalled call — by shutting down and "forcing the person who's disappointed them to make the first move." Ordinary friction is converted into an obligation on the other party. The district does not complain; it withdraws service and waits.
- **The blacklist: unappealable, evidence-optional, and it travels.** This is the corpus's only real sanctioning mechanism and it is not institutional at all. Silva: "People with less than noble intentions coming into proximity with a Pisces will feel two holes burning into them from across a crowded room… Without a word, Pisces will know that these people are **not on the menu for them or for anyone they know**. That knowledge is **not subject to amendment**." Elsewhere: "if you get out of line, **she'll make sure to tell the others**." Note the sequence Silva actually describes — read on sight, then "Pisces only cuts the cord **once there's evidence that they were right in their intuition**" — so there *is* a probationary interval, but the verdict was reached before it started and the evidence is confirmatory, never exculpatory. And Silva frames the whole thing as innocent: "It's not even personal with Pisces. With the fish, it's all about **damage control**."
- **Reputation as the weapon of choice.** Where other signs retaliate materially, this one retaliates socially. Cross Pisces romantically and "you may even discover that you're **widely known as a douchebag or a trollop**." Silva is explicit that the response is deliberate: read any hint of deception "and they will be shattered, and **they will make sure that you share in at least a small portion of the pain you've caused**." The Markets' enforcement arm is gossip with perfect distribution and no right of reply.
- **The permanent grievance posture.** Silva's "Cousin Bob" is the most useful shadow portrait in the recovered material precisely because he has none of the sign's compensating virtues: "Bob is always right, even when he's wrong. Bob will fight you over a perceived slight, faster than you can blink. Bob is always demanding apologies for those perceived slights" — over things "that may or may not have happened." Silva's diagnosis is that "the only aspect of Bob which is even vaguely Piscean is his **pathological sensitivity**" — "like an open wound, the slightest glance can set him off." Proof from inside the corpus that the sign's shadow can present as pure aggrieved aggression with the empathy stripped out entirely.
- **The single detonation.** Pisceans "internalize negative emotions… Retaining those feelings is corrosive," then discharge disproportionately — Silva's third-grade anecdote ends with the bully throwing a chair and Pisces answering with a baton to the head, *and Pisces being the one sent home*. Absorb, absorb, absorb, detonate once, lose the standing the absorption bought. Silva's third decan exists partly to bleed this off; see §11.
- **Self-recrimination underneath all of it.** Silva: Pisces "labors under something of an **inferiority complex**," running on "neurotic self-critique and self-recrimination," parsing its own sentences afterward for the wrong word. The confidence is borrowed from whoever is currently reassuring it — which is the same open door as everything else, pointed inward.
- **The fall taken for someone else, and the price never recovered.** Silva's teapot story: a friend breaks a prop, Pisces raises her hand and claims it to spare him a second expulsion, is disbelieved, and is expelled from the drama class for the rest of high school — after which "she abandoned any hope of involving herself in acting or dramatic pursuits, as the hurt never healed." Silva's verdict: "**Noble, yes. Smart? No.**" The self-sacrifice is real, uncoerced, and permanently costly, and the person it protected pays nothing.
- **No external brake.** Silva on two Pisces together: "the tendency of the two to become completely lost in each other, to the detriment of all else… severe negativity, as **they feed off one another in a toxic loop**." Only an earth-sign moon reliably stops it. A whole district of this temperament has no native corrective; the correction has to come from outside its walls, which is exactly what it is organized to keep out.
- **Compassion fatigue, expressed as coldness.** Silva: deprived of solitude, "they can become **cold, intolerant, and exhausted** from all the human energies swirling around them"; "energy vampires are Piscean kryptonite," and ordinary social load that reads as innocuous to anyone else is "an **existential threat**." The district's warmth has a duty cycle, and past it the same faculty runs in reverse.
- **Chronic loss of the things that matter operationally.** Silva files it as comedy — the friend who has lost three phones in two years — but the substance is not comic: leaving a phone in a taxi, leaving keys in plain sight and forgetting them in five minutes, a tendency that "can cost them jobs, friends, and lovers," and being taken "in stride, usually **laughing off the loss**." A district running on secrecy whose native failure mode is misplacing the sensitive item and finding it funny.
- **When the loyalty is not returned.** Silva, bluntly: intensity and loyalty unreciprocated leaves Pisces "**morose to the point of suicide**." The corpus's darkest single line about the sign, and it is about being loved badly, not about being wronged.

## 4. How It Reads From Outside (recognition markers, first impressions)

Hard to grip and hard to locate. Martin: Pisces rising people "are normally so well camouflaged" and "can be particularly hard to get hold of, since they tend to change shape all the time"; polarized here, engaging with it is "like trying to get a grip on a piece of soap," and the outsider risks getting "**Pisced**" — walking out confused, muddled, and unable to say what was agreed. No clear boundary between the district and its surroundings; you are inside before you crossed anything.

**It does not approach you.** Silva's party portrait: Pisces sits in one chair all night and waves once — and the room comes to *him*; "will socialize with those willing to socialize with them, preferably making the first move." Not the life of the party, "most certainly the soul." What it is doing while it sits there is not passive: "don't take it personally if you catch Pisces observing you from the other side of the room. **They'll probably wave at you, smile, and then go back to observing you. They don't care if you notice.**" Being watched openly and without embarrassment is the district's first impression.

**Vulnerability on display is not weakness on display.** Silva's chapter epigraph is Rihanna's "*it's tougher to be vulnerable than to actually be tough*," and Silva's gloss is that Pisces wears vulnerability on the sleeve "from a place of strength" — a sign "with little interest in cloaking its feelings." Outsiders consistently misread the exposure as an opening. **And the read fails in both directions:** Silva's "Cousin Bob" — gruff, incurious, permanently disgruntled, a man nobody would ever identify as this sign — is one, and the tell is only the pathological sensitivity underneath (§3). Not everyone in the Markets looks like the Markets. Snodgrass adds the career-impeding surface read: "**indiscretion and slovenliness**," and "a popular winner of hearts, the archetype never lacks for friends, but often loses supporters." Emerson: "mysterious, magical, and **sometimes frightening**… fantastic illusionists; the **Houdinis** of the celestial realm," and to outsiders "delusional, or at the very least, flaky." Visual register: flowing and diaphanous, sea-green through teal, blue, lilac, mauve, magenta; embroidery; "wearing your heart on your sleeve in your favorite color."

## 5. Core Need (what it is actually seeking)

To be **let in on** — to belong to something without being filtered by it. Martin: Pisces "describes our sense of belonging to and being protected by the whole"; Chiron there is "existential loneliness, a feeling of being cut off from the source of support, a kind of **emotional exile**… as if the universal umbilical cord has been brutally severed. We feel particularly alone and have no alternative but to become **heroic**." That last clause is the district's engine: the wound is not treated, it is *converted into a service offered to others*. Emerson: "helping other people who have suffered trauma alleviates your personal suffering." The district launders its own grievance through its clientele — which is why its charity and its racket are the same gesture and cannot be separated by watching.

Silva's *What Pisces Need to Thrive* chapter names six needs directly, and it is the most concrete want-list in the corpus for any sign. Read as district requirements:

- **Reciprocity, which it never gets.** The load-bearing one. Pisces is "the **agony aunt** of the zodiac," and "it's often the case that when Pisces needs a shoulder to cry on, it's not there." Silva names the mechanism: "the otherworldly aura of spiritual intelligence associated with Pisces often misleads people into believing that they'll figure it out on their own — and that they're **emotionally superhuman**." The district is universally relied on and structurally un-helpable, because being the place everyone comes to for help is the exact reason nobody thinks to bring it any.
- **Someone to say it did the right thing.** "Pisceans need **cheerleaders** who tell them when they've done something right… that they're good people with good intentions and worthy of all the compassion they show others." Against the inferiority complex of §3, external validation is not a vanity — it is the only source of the self-assessment the district cannot generate internally. Whoever supplies it holds real leverage.
- **A creative outlet, or it rots.** "Without a locus for their endless ideas and inspirations, Pisces can become **stagnant, resentful, and even depressed**"; the same person in repetitive work "can seem lazy," "losing job after job." Silva's needs chapter, not the work chapter, is where the laziness gets explained: it is a starved faculty, not a missing one.
- **The hunt.** Filed by Silva under romance and quoted in §2 — the second-hand store, the vintage find, the thing nobody else wanted. Note what the need actually is: not the object and not the profit, but *finding what they didn't even imagine they needed*. A market that exists to be walked through.
- **Withdrawal, on its own schedule.** "Like a battery that has run down… They often have to recharge their batteries and recycle their energy." Silva is specific that the solitude is *processing*, not rest: Pisces withdraws "to analyze what they've learned in their social interactions, about themselves and about other people." Denied it, "Pisces may rebel," and turns cold (§3).
- **Honesty about identity, as the entry price.** "When you fail to model honesty and integrity with the sensitive fish, you will find yourself **cut off**." Silva's formulation is worth keeping exact because it is not about truthful statements: "**if you can't be true to yourself, you'll never be true to others**, and that is fundamentally dishonest." Consistent with §7 — the offense is misrepresenting what you are, not lying about facts.

And a seventh Silva names but does not count: **to be believed.** "Not taking Piscean intuition seriously is a major source of frustration for this sign." The closing formulation is the cleanest statement of the core need in the corpus — what Pisces needs "more than anything is **the freedom to swim in its eternal figure eight**, both pulling and pushing, coming and going, being and not being." Not freedom *from* anything. Freedom to keep circulating without being made to settle into one shape.

## 6. Behavior Under Conditions

- **At work.** Needs to believe in it or it does not happen; Silva insists the famous "laziness" is really **boredom**, and that a job Pisces isn't emotionally connected to produces someone "staring out the window instead of doing what he or she is being paid to do." Procrastinated items "creep down the Piscean 'to do' list until it drops right off the page." Adapts instantly — "wherever Pisces go is home… Easily bored, Pisces love it when you move the goalposts." As a boss: no micromanagement, reads what each person needs, uniquely quick to spot boredom and bullying — but glazes over at "performance reviews and payroll" in ten seconds and "may even slip out the side door" rather than deliver bad news or fire anyone. Silva's warning to subordinates: "**Just don't try to drive the agenda. That Pisces tail may well come out to slap you back into line.**" Bookkeeping is structurally bad here: Mercury is in **detriment**, and Martin notes Piscean speech is "sensitive to the feel and atmosphere of words rather than to their actual concrete meaning." Agreements are atmospheric, not written. Silva states the dependency this creates outright: "what the Pisces boss needs more than anything is **administrative support to get the boring end of the job done**. When this is in place, a Pisces boss is inspirational, productive, and exceptional." The district cannot keep its own books and must import the function — which is the Undergrid, and see §8 for what that costs it.
- **What it is natively good at selling.** Silva's own career list is short and unexpectedly on the nose for a market quarter: filmmaker, counselor, caregiver, **charity** ("driving their ability to bring in those donations"), and **marketing** — "Pisces love to bring the unseen to life and **creating a persona** for a commercial brand is exactly that kind of magic." Persona-fabrication and donation-extraction, named as the sign's two commercial excellences. Also: Silva recommends "**consulting or freelancing**" over employment. Independent operators, not a firm.
- **Socially / in public.** Semi-social; watches. Silva calls people-watching "Pisces' most beloved party game" — noting who to meet, "who to avoid like the plague, and who might be having a bit of a rough time and need a little support." That is simultaneously the district's warmth and its intelligence apparatus. Extremely selective about who is let past the outer layer; "operates on trust," and only the innermost circle sees the home. The vetting is explicit and heavy: "Pisces will **vet you harder than a political party's machinery will a candidate for public office**… They need to be 100% convinced that their emotional investment in you will not blow up." Silva notes the cost of doing this in public — "others can interpret their reticence as **coldness or snobbery**."
- **After exposure.** Not a stress response; the standing operating rhythm. Every foray out is followed by mandatory withdrawal, and the withdrawal is where the analysis happens (§5). "Even a night out dancing can be difficult for Pisces to recover from. They come home over-stimulated and unable to sleep." Deprived of the cycle, it gets "a little cranky," then cold. Anything the district learns in a room is not acted on in that room.
- **Under stress.** Flees rather than confronts: "Pisceans tend to splash as far away as possible if a confrontation looms," and "may just get up and leave without warning." Retreats to a bolt-hole (Martin: "a public library, or a retreat, or a weekend fishing on the banks of a river"). Snodgrass's escalation ladder is worth keeping intact: "retreating to the shore for fishing and contemplation, **joining a monastery**, erecting a house on a hill, or **traveling to an undisclosed destination**." Substance use as boundary-substitute. Silva adds the boredom variant, which for a district is a desertion risk rather than a mood: "a bored Pisces is a listless, uninspired Pisces who is **likely to bolt**."
- **In crisis.** Three exits, all absolute, none proportionate. The **cold door** (§3 — excommunication, re-entry only on request, never on payment); the **single detonation** (§3 — disproportionate, self-damaging, once); or the **blacklist** (§3 — quiet, distributed, permanent, and the one it reaches for most often because it costs nothing and can be denied). Note that only the second is visible from outside, which is why the district reads as harmless right up until it doesn't.

## 7. Socialization (how newcomers are absorbed and raised into it)

By **emotional contagion, not instruction**. Silva: Pisces children "cleave to your emotions to reinforce their connection to you," and parents are told to "temper your emotions around them" — the young take the room's mood as their own content. Learning is **visual and imitative**, never rule-based. "Don't fence me in" is the childhood mantra; unstructured time, small groups or solitude, no molds. What is actively taught: advocacy — first to stand up for someone being bullied, "won't stand for meanness, lying, or manipulation of any kind."

Four structural notes for a district:

- **The absolute prohibition is misrepresenting who you are — not fraud.** Silva: "be exactly who you say you are. Pretending to be something or someone you're not will earn you the eternal contempt of Pisces." A place that sells forged goods and edited memories can still treat false *identity* as the one unforgivable offense, and that is not a contradiction the sources need help with. The recovered needs chapter states the underlying principle in a form worth quoting to an NPC: "**if you can't be true to yourself, you'll never be true to others**, and that is fundamentally dishonest."
- **Membership is an audition, and there is no appeals process.** Silva's language is not metaphorical about this: newcomers are vetted "harder than a political party's machinery will a candidate for public office," and "**once you've passed the audition**, you'll find that Pisces is as loyal and nurturing a friend as you could ever hope to find." Fail it and the sanction is graded: bold and aggressive types get soft exclusion — "they won't slam the door in your face, but they probably won't invite you inside, either" — while "manipulation, lying, unfair gossip, character assassination… will slam that door in your face before you know it." No rule is published, no test is announced, and the criteria are read off you rather than asked.
- **The sanction is exclusion, and it is framed as hygiene rather than punishment.** Silva: ostracism "is not done to be cruel or to punish. This is done by Pisces, **to protect themselves and those around them**… it's all about damage control." That framing is the thing to build with — a district that excommunicates constantly, sincerely does not experience itself as punitive, and will be genuinely puzzled by anyone who calls it cruel.
- **Newcomers first try to pass as respectable.** Martin: with Pisces rising "we are born into an environment where practical skills and an orderly, precise and businesslike approach to the world were highly valued. For many years we may try to define ourselves according to the way we have been mirrored" — the district's children imitate the *opposite* district's virtues first and only later find their own shape.

**The formative injury is being disbelieved after taking someone else's fall.** Silva's teapot story (§3) is a socialization story more than a shadow one: the young Pisces protects a friend by claiming his damage, is disbelieved by an authority who "not only knew better — he didn't like the boy," is expelled from the thing she loved, and never returns to it. She "never regretted preventing her friend from getting in trouble again," only the loss. That is the district's coming-of-age in one incident — the covering is not regretted, the institution that punished it is never re-entered, and the person covered for is never billed.

## 8. Conflict Geometry

**Opposition — Virgo (District 12, The Undergrid, maintenance/service).** Martin's Virgo–Pisces axis is the best-documented opposition in the whole corpus: order/chaos, control/trust, form/content, the practical and imaginal worlds, realities/dreams, **service and devotion**, sickness/health, accepting and judging. She frames it as the grail legend — Pisces asks "*whom does the grail serve?*", Virgo answers "*he who serves the grail*." **Why the tension is structural:** Mercury rules Virgo and is *in detriment* in Pisces, so the two districts are running on the same faculty at opposite settings — one is precision, accounting, and error-detection as a way of life; the other is the sign in which precision, accounting, and error-detection literally cannot function. Neither is a bolt-on. Martin's polarization warnings map straight across: an over-Virgo Undergrid becomes "consumed by duty and drudgery," longs to escape, and **attracts more and more disruption into its life**; an over-Pisces Markets becomes so diffuse there is nothing to negotiate with, and is eventually "forced to engage with the world in practical ways, something we may bitterly resent." Note the parasitic sweetener: Virgo is drawn to Piscean types *and equally to "those who are vulnerable or sick, because then they can use all their practical talents to care for their partner"* — an Undergrid that needs the Markets to stay broken.

**The asymmetry — and this is the single best structural find in the recovered material.** Silva describes the same relationship twice, once from each side, and the two accounts do not match. From Virgo's side it is one of the zodiac's rare genuinely good oppositions: "*Pisces is your polar opposite… This match is one of the few* [that works]. *You are both service-oriented and modest… Pisces offers compassion and spiritual support while you offer hands-on assistance… they are a match made in heaven. Pisces works to soothe and calm you while you keep Pisces grounded in the real world.*" From Pisces' side, in Silva's own Pisces volume, Virgo appears on the list of **signs to avoid**: "the virgin is someone the fish should avoid… they will quickly be at odds and feel **suffocated**," subject to standards that "seem unfair and unduly harsh," left "unwelcome, unloved, and disrespected," and the whole thing is "disorienting and **soul-crushing** for the sensitive fish." Silva does soften it elsewhere — Virgo "may be able to reign in a few of Pisces' 'less desirable' traits, organizing and tempering them" — but the valence gap holds.

That is not a corpus error to be reconciled; it is the relationship. **The Undergrid experiences the Markets as its best and most fulfilling partnership. The Markets experience the Undergrid as the thing slowly killing them.** Both are sincere, both are correct about their own experience, and neither can hear the other's version. It also explains why the district keeps importing the ledger-keeping it cannot do itself (§6) and why every such arrangement ends badly without either side understanding what went wrong.

**Square 1 — Sagittarius (District 11, The Frostlands, frontier).** The harder of the two, because it is a **sibling quarrel over shared property**: Jupiter traditionally rules both. Silva: "While Jupiter finds its diurnal domicile in Sagittarius, Pisces is where the mighty planet rests **at night**." Same inheritance, one worked openly and declaratively, one worked nocturnally and inward. Tierney gives the doctrinal split: Sagittarius is convinced with "all the fiery self-assurance it can muster" that it is on the right track, and "any contradictory details that crop up and challenge one's faith is **for another sign (Pisces) to fret over**." The frontier exports certainty; the market makes its living in the gap between the certainty and the fact. Silva adds the texture: Sagittarius's directness and speed feel "**vulgar**" to Pisces, and Pisces reads Sagittarius's non-commitment as an insult; conversely Pisces looks like a millstone. The recovered text supplies the line that sharpens the whole square: "**For all the hippie-dippy vibes of Pisces, people born under this sign are, by nature, conservative. Pisces thinks before speaking and looks before leaping.**" The Markets are not the reckless quarter and the Frostlands are; the district that *looks* lawless is the cautious one, and it finds the frontier "impulsive, appearing wild and reckless." Free irony, and it reframes the border: the Markets' objection to the Frostlands is not moral, it is that they move too fast to be read.

**Square 2 — Gemini (District 9, The Circuit, information).** Both mutable, both shape-shifters, incompatible on what shape-shifting is *for*: Gemini disperses into words and circulation; Pisces dissolves into atmosphere and concealment. Opacity is the Markets' only real asset and circulation is the Circuit's, so every Circuit success is a Markets loss by definition. Silva: Gemini "flip from one priority to another at the drop of a dime," which disorients and depletes; Gemini "regard their sexuality as a toy — and they may include sensitive Pisces in that column, much to Pisces' detriment"; "Gemini will appear fly-by-night to Pisces, Pisces will seem a bit of a millstone to Gemini." Emerson is blunt: "not very likely to last… They will both end up **relentlessly complaining about each other**." Silva *Sun & Moon* names the specific collision — Piscean privacy vs. Gemini sociability — "can easily come to blows." Silva's Gemini volume adds the view from the Circuit, which is the more useful direction: Gemini finds Pisces "**holding them back due to their demanding personality**," and reacts to Piscean intensity by backing off — which "will cause Pisces to feel insecure… and make them even **needier for reciprocation and validation**," which repels Gemini further. A documented escalation loop rather than a static dislike. Also flagged: Gemini's humor "can be possibly offensive for Pisces, as it can unintentionally hurt their sentiments." The Circuit wounds this district casually and without noticing, which is worse for relations than deliberate hostility would be.

## 9. Affinity Geometry (trines/sextiles, plus the collaboration exception — natural allies, and why)

- **Trine — Cancer (D1, The Sanctuary).** "If Pisces has something like a twin in the rest of the zodiac, it's Cancer." Cancer supplies the anchor and the planning Pisces will not do; Pisces supplies what Cancer's guardedness cannot reach. Silva's friendship chapter gives the division of labor precisely — "**Cancer bringing the ideas and Pisces bringing the imagination to transform them**," and Cancer teaching Pisces "that doing one thing at a time is probably more effective than doing, say, twenty." **The documented failure mode is financial**: Emerson — "great at supporting each other emotionally. The issue here is the lack of practicality with their finances… neither wants to bear the financial responsibility." A refuge district and a market district can run a very effective informal welfare pipeline and go broke doing it.
- **Trine — Scorpio (D4).** The security relationship. Scorpio "can intuit the motivations of others, tempering Pisces' trusting nature with an ability to identify red flags," and is "committed by nature to protecting those nearest to them." In exchange Pisces draws the guarded sign out; Emerson notes Pisces "**softens Scorpio's lack of impulse control**." Silva: cocooned together they are "a mystery to others… Other people are weird!" — and their joint projects are "often in the realm of **personal or public politics**," with Scorpio plotting the course while "Pisces rides shotgun, looking for the esoteric, hidden details Scorpio may have overlooked." Tierney flags the risk: only Scorpio matches Pisces for "extreme, divergent expression" and for reaching such "gloomy inner depths," so this pairing amplifies rather than moderates.
- **Sextile — Capricorn (D6, The Yards).** Manufacture meets fence. Capricorn "can help Pisces **articulate and materialize** many of their philosophical concepts"; Pisces opens the Yards' interior life. Silva's friendship chapter assigns the roles explicitly: "**Pisces is in charge of this relationship, managing its emotional aspects, while Capricorn puts their nose to the grindstone**" — the Markets handle the relationship, the Yards handle the work. Friction is money: Capricorn is "skittish about the Piscean tendency to generosity and charity," Pisces feels inhibited by Capricorn's care around money.
- **Sextile — Taurus (D2, residential).** The customer base and the ballast — "the bull will provide Pisces with grounding to keep them from losing sight of reality," and Taurus is "practical and will serve as a **great advisor** to the fish." Conditional: works "so long as Taurus doesn't attempt to change Pisces." Documented irritant in the other direction — "Pisces will frustrate the bull with the notorious fishy forgetfulness" (§3).
- **Quincunx — Leo (D3, cultural) and Libra (D8, Government). The collaboration exception, and it is a real structural finding.** Silva files both as *poor* relationships and *excellent* collaborations, and says so in the same chapter. Leo socially: "polar opposites," "Leo's monolithic ego grates on sensitive, humble Pisces," who eventually reads the "continual proclamations of superiority" as "the smoke and mirrors they are" and walks away. Leo operationally: "**pair them on a project at work or in the community, and watch the hostile sparks turn into the right kind of fireworks**" — and the reason is a clean fit of appetites. "Leo is perfectly willing to do all the heavy going, while Pisces plugs along in the background. **Pisces couldn't care less about fame and fortune, while Leo lives for them, so putting the lion out front is a winning strategy.**" Libra is the same shape, less dramatic: Libra "do[es] better on the front end of things than sensitive Pisces, especially if we're talking customer service or prickly clients… while **Pisces keeps the back end moving along** as it should." Both are front-of-house arrangements. The Markets do not want the counter; they want whoever is standing at the counter to be someone else, visibly, and to enjoy it.
- **Adjacent (semi-sextile) — Aquarius (D7, The Labs) and Aries (D5, The Power Core).** Martin warns adjacent signs "have absolutely nothing in common," and Silva agrees on the *personal* register — Aquarian "cool detachment will puzzle a Pisces." But the recovered material makes Aquarius Pisces' **best collaborator** on anything humanitarian: "Aquarius can facilitate the visionary prognostications of Pisces… With logical Aquarius at the controls, Pisces may dream their visions into being." Silva's Aquarius volume divides it the same way from the other end — "Aquarius should stick to the analytical aspect of a project while **Pisces can deal with any social niceties**," and Aquarians who invest in the relationship find "the latter will work much harder." Labs supply the rigor, Markets supply the face and the vision. Silva's cusps remain directly usable as *border-neighborhood* texture: the Aquarius border is the **Cusp of Sensitivity**, where the district finally gains "strong boundaries that people take seriously" and intuition sharpens into "a weapon"; the Aries border is the **Cusp of Rebirth**, alpha meets omega, where the district stops dissolving into tears and "put[s] up the proverbial dukes."

## 10. Structural Position

- **Element — Water** *(out-of-fiction temperament grouping only; never a district label — see the element-collision constraint in `00`)*. Tierney's cycle logic: "fire always starts each cycle and **water always completes it**." Snodgrass on the temperament: empathy and sensuality, and — usefully for a market — "**as persuaders**… the watery three easily motivate others." The medieval Arab scheme files Cancer/Scorpio/Pisces as the cold, wet North.
- **Modality — Mutable.** Martin: motivation is "to adapt, mediate and reconcile"; more interested in the journey than in arriving; "tend to **avoid conflict at all costs**." Tierney: the mutable signs "weaken the mighty resistances that fixed signs put up to avoid being altered. **Solid structure starts to lose its form.**" That is a functional description of a black market operating on a planned city.
- **Polarity — Negative / yin / receptive** (Snodgrass: *fem.*). Energy is drawn inward and answered rather than projected. Consistent with §4: the district does not solicit; it receives.
- **Rulers — Jupiter (traditional/personal) + Neptune (modern/transpersonal); Venus exalted; Mercury in detriment.**

**The two fish, bound and swimming in opposite directions.** Emerson: the symbol "represents their persona, which glides back and forth between reality and fantasy." Martin: "two fish or dolphins **yoked together**." Silva registers the standard reading — "two fish appear to swim in opposite directions, indicating **tension** in the character" — and then explicitly rejects it for a better one: "I see them as swimming in an **eternal loop**, ever-rotating around the mysteries of the universe, penetrating and illuminating them," restated in her closing chapter as the "**eternal figure eight**, both pulling and pushing, coming and going, being and not being." Keep both, because they are two different districts: opposition (a charity wing and a predation wing straining against each other) versus **circulation** (one closed loop with no fixed direction, in which the same goods, favors, and people come around again). The second is the better market model and the first is the better conflict model. The tether is not metaphor in the sources — Pisces' alpha star is **Alrisha, "the cord"**; Alpherg is a double star "accenting the tether that joins the two fish"; and the myth gives the reason: **Venus tethers herself to Cupid while fleeing Typhon so they will not be separated in the water.** The binding was an emergency measure taken by someone protecting a child, and it outlived the emergency. Directly usable: the district's compassionate wing and its predatory wing are not factions that could be separated by policing one — they were tied together while running from something, by someone who meant well, and the knot is now load-bearing. Tierney supplies the behavioral proof that both live in one body: "world-renowned humanitarian by day, chronic alcoholic by night — perhaps no other sign, except Scorpio, is capable of such **extreme, divergent expression**." Alchemical gloss, Lambsprinck's *De lapide philosophico* (1625): "**The Sea is the body; the two fish are spirit and soul.**" And the monster is still in frame — the Greeks read Alpherg as the head of **Typhon**, lying near the northern fish's tail.

**Traditional (Jupiter) vs. modern (Neptune) — two different districts.** Take them as two economies sharing streets rather than as one blended tone.
- *Jupiter Markets*: Martin — "the potentially joyful, ecstatic '**dolphin**' nature." Abundance, festival, generosity, faith, the beggar fed at the door, mutual aid, genuine surplus. Trade is real and the goods are good; it is illegal, not fake.
- *Neptune Markets*: Martin — "drugs and alcohol and **any addictions which help us lose ourselves and escape from the world**"; "longs to return to formlessness and oblivion." A **dissolution economy**: narcotics, memory editing, identity erasure, augmentation that removes rather than adds. The product is the removal of a self. Tierney marks Neptune the more dangerous of the two: it "is more prone to **absorb** its surroundings, and the people in them, than is Pisces," and "self-deception tied to misplaced faith may be stronger in Neptunians," who chase "'miracle' cures and other quick, magical fixes" where Pisces proper "helps us become more accepting of what we cannot change."
- *Venus exalted*: beauty operates at maximum strength here — and Martin notes Venus in Pisces equally describes "dissatisfaction or disappointment or **deception** in relationships." The district's most beautiful offering is also its most reliable lie.
- *Mercury in detriment*: no functioning ledger, no reliable inventory, no precise contract. Enforcement, accounting, and audit fail here for structural reasons, not corruption.

## 13. Myth, History & Cultural Associations

- **The tether has a reason.** Venus and Cupid, ambushed by the fire-dragon Typhon, flee into water — the one element Typhon must avoid — become fish, and **she ties herself to her son so they cannot be separated**. Al-Sufi records the yoked pair mirrored in the sky. The Greek variant: the fish are rewarded with a place in heaven for saving Aphrodite and her child.
- **The fish are not fish.** Silva: in the Greek telling the pair are "**Ichthyocentaurs**" — "the upper body of a human, the forelegs of a horse, with the rest of the body consisting of a fish's tail," also called **sea-centaurs**, the term first attested in the 12th century (Ioannes Tzetzes). The same beings appear in Syrian myth attending the sea-birth of **Astarte**. Worth having: the district's own emblem is a composite creature, three things joined and passing as one animal, and outsiders who think it is a fish have not looked closely.
- **Submerge and come out someone else.** Silva traces the fish-symbol's water lineage to the rite of baptism, where "the christened is submerged under the water — representing the waters from which the earth was born — **emerging as a new creature**." Strip the religion and what is left is exactly the Neptune Markets' core service: a procedure you enter as one person and leave as another, with the old identity considered dead rather than hidden. The precedent for the district's identity trade is a *sacrament*, not a crime — which is the right tone for how the district thinks about it.
- **The fish as a safe-house mark.** The Christian *ichthys* was an acronym (*Iesous Christos Theou Uios Soter*) and, per Snodgrass, "an **unassuming marker for a safe house**, the symbol welcomed followers of the new religion to fellow converts." A sign in plain view that means nothing to outsiders and everything to the initiated — a black market's ideal iconography, already historical.
- **Secrets and mysteries.** "Like the Orphic mysteries that grew out of the dismemberment of the singer Orpheus, **Pisces governs occult rituals and secrets**." The sign "represents mystic fusion or epiphany."
- **The completion labor is a ransom.** Pisces "governs the completion of Hercules's labors" and corresponds to his **rescue of Hesione from the Trojan sea monster** — the last task is buying a hostage back.
- **The substitute apostle.** In the medieval twelve-apostle mapping Pisces is **Matthias** — the replacement, elected to fill the seat Judas vacated. (Aquarius is Judas.) The one who takes the traitor's place.
- **Sacrifice / dissolution / cycle's end.** Martin: the entire sign occupies the lunar **Balsamic phase**, "about letting go of the current cycle and preparing for the future… so it involves **surrender and sacrifice**, but is also a period of **gestation** for the future cycle." Martin's plant cycle: the fruit decomposes in Capricorn and "**the seed is released in Pisces** for the next growth cycle." Capricorn/Aquarius/Pisces = *revaluing, reorganising, releasing*. Tierney: the sign symbolizes "the less-observable, **underground waters** of spirit that feed new life potential ready to incarnate."
- **The scapegoat register.** Snodgrass's period doggerel — "Who prays and serves, and prays some more; / And feeds the beggar at the door— / And weeps o'er love lost long before? / **Poor Pisces**" — and Petronius' satirical banquet, where the vulgar host serves "on Pisces **two mullets**" and glibly assigns vices to each sign (Snodgrass explicitly derides him). Both are records of the sign *being mocked from outside*, which is itself the material: a quarter everyone quotes jokes about.
- **Escape literature named by Snodgrass:** Hurston's *Their Eyes Were Watching God* and Morrison's *Beloved* — "the near-tragic story of a plantation slave who flees her role as a breeder of more slaves." Flight from being used as a resource.
- **The age that is ending.** The vernal equinox sat in Pisces for roughly two thousand years (the constellation was once called the *First Point of Aries*) and is now precessing out. Martin frames the Age of Pisces as devotion (Pisces) plus practical service (Virgo) — loaves and fishes. Tierney notes Tibetans "have long been enthralled by the otherworldly, mystical realms of Pisces." Snodgrass's geography table appears to give **Portugal**, though the OCR is column-scrambled and this is not certain.

## 14. Design Hooks for District Use

1. **The recycler's mandate.** Build the Markets as the city's designated dissolution point — Tierney's "job assigned to Pisces" — where other districts' goods, debts, identities, and memories are broken into components and recirculated. Not "stolen goods sold cheap"; *matter and identity taken apart on purpose*, with the Yards and the Undergrid quietly dependent on it.
2. **The knot, as an actual institution.** Charity wing and predation wing bound by a founding emergency — someone tied them together while fleeing something, to keep from losing a child. Make the tether a specific historical event the district still commemorates, and make cutting it a real, costed, losable player choice rather than a moral formality.
3. **One unforgivable crime, and it isn't fraud.** Forged goods, edited memories, and counterfeit provenance are ordinary trade. **Misrepresenting who you are** ends you — permanently, silently, with no appeal ("you are an ex-person"). Player consequence: an alias burned here is burned forever, everywhere in the district, and nobody will explain why.
4. **The one key is asking.** Silva's fuller text supplies the exception to the cold door, so make it a mechanic rather than a mercy: the district gives "second chances **only to those who ask for them**." Not bribes, not favors, not quest completion — the player has to *request* readmission, out loud, without leverage, and be told nothing about whether it worked. Unintentional offenses thaw on their own; deliberate ones never do. A player who reflexively tries to buy their way back proves the point and stays exiled.
5. **The blacklist is the government.** This is the district's actual governance, and it is not an institution. Sanction is exclusion, propagated by word of mouth ("she'll make sure to tell the others"), reached on sight, confirmed by evidence that is only ever confirmatory, and "**not subject to amendment**." No court, no charge, no record, no appeal — and everyone involved sincerely experiences it as **damage control** rather than punishment. Make the player's standing a hidden value they can lose without a notification, discoverable only through doors that stop opening. The district should be genuinely bewildered that anyone calls this unfair.
6. **Ask both sides about the Undergrid and get two different relationships.** Build the Virgo/Pisces asymmetry (§8) as literal contradictory testimony: Undergrid NPCs describe the arrangement as their most rewarding partnership, in warm and specific terms; Markets NPCs describe the same arrangement as suffocating, humiliating, and slowly fatal. Neither is lying. No document settles it. A player trying to mediate has nothing to mediate *with*, and that is the point.
7. **The wine press and the cord.** Use Pisces' own star-names as district heraldry rather than inventing signage: *Alrisha* (the cord), *Torcularis* (the northern wine press), *Revati* (the rich), and "the Circlet." A knot-and-ring mark cut into a doorframe reads as decoration to outsiders and as a safe-house mark to initiates — exactly the *ichthys* precedent.
8. **Two economies, one street.** Run Jupiter and Neptune as separately-priced overlays on the same map: the ecstatic surplus bazaar (real goods, real generosity, festival) and the dissolution trade (narcotics, memory clinics, subtractive augmentation, identity erasure). A quest that helps one damages the other, and no NPC frames this as good vs. evil.
9. **No ledger, by design.** Mercury in detriment: nothing here is written down accurately. Investigating this district by records is a dead end by construction — the player must read atmosphere, faces, and who is avoided at a gathering (Silva's "most beloved party game"), which is exactly the skill the district itself runs on. Extend it with the dependency: the Markets *import* their bookkeeping, so the only accurate records of this district exist in the Undergrid, held by people the Markets resent and cannot do without.
10. **The compassionate fixer whose kindness is the hook.** Martin's "addiction to vulnerability, weakness and self-sacrifice" plus Emerson's "leeching": a genuinely generous NPC whose generosity creates dependence and who is not consciously aware that this is what she does. She should be *right* that she is helping. The exploitation is emergent, not planned.
11. **The sanctified con.** Tierney's "who's to prove otherwise?" — a figure selling transcendence who fully believes his own claim. Not a cynic. Player cannot expose him by proving he knows he's lying, because he doesn't.
12. **The single detonation.** A district faction that absorbs insult after insult with unnerving patience and then answers one grievance with wildly disproportionate force — and is punished for the response rather than for the accumulation, exactly as the baton anecdote ends. The third decan is where this is *routinized* (§11): a sub-neighborhood that exists to bleed the pressure off in controlled amounts, and whose absence would make the detonations more frequent, not fewer.
13. **Newcomers arrive imitating the Undergrid.** Recruits show up over-neat, over-precise, quoting maintenance-district manners, because that's what they were told respectable looks like. The district's coming-of-age is unlearning it. Free, legible visual storytelling for NPC tenure.
14. **The find, not the margin.** Build trade here around Silva's thrill of the hunt (§2, §5): stalls that will not tell you what they have, inventory that is never listed, and a culture that rates a seller by what they *turned up* rather than what they cleared. Haggling over price reads as vulgar; haggling over provenance is the real conversation. This is what makes the Markets a market without making its people merchants, and it is the one commercial instinct the corpus actually supports.
15. **Someone else at the counter, and they love it there.** The Leo and Libra collaboration pattern (§9): the district's public faces are systematically *imported* — loud, credit-hungry, front-of-house operators from the cultural and government quarters who take the visibility and the risk while the Markets work the back end. A player who traces a Markets operation to its apparent head has found the wrong person, and the wrong person does not know they are a screen.

## 15. Source Gaps

*Revised after the supplementary Silva pass (~27k words, all 8 blocks). Four gaps closed or downgraded, two conflicts resolved, the rest stand.*

**Closed or downgraded**

- **~~The corpus does not support "Pisces = merchant."~~ Downgraded — there is a real commercial instinct, and it is a scavenger's.** The core claim still holds: unconcerned with money, "not a materialistic person," bad with finances, "couldn't care less about fame and fortune." But the recovered needs chapter supplies what the first pass lacked — **the thrill of the hunt**, the second-hand store, the vintage find, "the potential to make something beautiful out of even the lowliest polyester leisure suit," and the reward being *the find itself*. Silva also names **marketing/branding** ("creating a persona… is exactly that kind of magic") and **charity fundraising** as two of the sign's five listed career excellences, and recommends **freelancing over employment**. That is enough for a market of independent operators trading in salvage, provenance, and manufactured personas. What remains unsupported is *margin* — nothing in the corpus makes this sign want profit. Build acquisition-free commerce; do not manufacture a profit motive.
- **~~Deliberate predation is genuinely thin.~~ Resolved into a type: the aggression is real, and it is social rather than acquisitive.** The recovered material roughly doubles the predator column and gives it a consistent shape — the unappealable, self-propagating blacklist ("not on the menu for them or for anyone they know… not subject to amendment"); reputation destruction as retaliation ("widely known as a douchebag or a trollop"; "she'll make sure to tell the others"); explicit intent to hurt back ("they will make sure that you **share in at least a small portion of the pain** you've caused"); the idealization-and-shutdown maneuver that forces the offender to move first; grudges lasting "until the Apocalypse"; and Cousin Bob, an entirely aggrieved, apology-demanding, physically retaliating Pisces with the empathy removed. **The prey framing still holds on the economic axis and only there** — this district is robbed, undercharged, uncredited for its ideas ("ignored until someone higher up the food chain clues in"), takes falls that are not its own, and is bullied from childhood. It just does not answer any of that with theft. It answers with exile. Prey economically, predator socially, and the two are the same faculty (the read) pointed in different directions.
- **~~Zero institutional or governance material.~~ Partly closed.** There is now a described sanctioning mechanism (the blacklist, §3), a described membership process (the audition and vetting, §7), a described sanction rationale ("damage control," not punishment), and described terms of readmission ("second chances only to those who ask for them"). What is still absent is anything about **territory, succession, or dispute resolution between two insiders** — every source only ever describes Pisces judging an outsider. Escalation *within* the district remains pure derivation.
- **~~Decan ruler conflict.~~ Resolved.** Silva names the third decan "Pisces-**Scorpio**" and assigns **Mars**; Emerson assigns **Pluto**. Both agree on the sub-sign; they simply pick different rulers of Scorpio — the same traditional/modern split as Jupiter/Neptune one level up. Not an error to adjudicate. Run both as overlays (§11).
- **Leo/Pisces contradiction resolved.** Silva and Emerson were describing different registers, and Silva says so herself in the recovered friends/enemies chapter: Leo is one of two signs Pisces "should stay away from" *personally*, **and** one of Pisces' best *collaborators* — "pair them on a project… and watch the hostile sparks turn into the right kind of fireworks." Emerson's "wonderful match" is the working relationship; Silva's "steam roller" is the private one. Both true. Same pattern applies to Libra and Aquarius (§9).
- **Virgo/Pisces contradiction resolved — as an asymmetry, not a reconciliation.** Silva's Virgo volume calls the opposition one of the zodiac's few genuinely good ones; Silva's Pisces volume lists Virgo among the signs to avoid, "disorienting and soul-crushing." Both are the corpus reporting the same relationship from opposite ends. Treat the mismatch as the content (§8, §14/6).

**Still open**

- **Emerson's twelfth-house line is still carrying enormous weight.** "Addiction, crime, fears, sleep, and secrets" remains the *only* place in the corpus that connects this sign to crime as such. One sentence, one source — and the supplementary pass added nothing to it. The criminality of the Markets is a district premise the substrate supports only obliquely, through dissolution, secrecy, unreliable records, and salvage.
- **Compatibility contradictions, still unresolved.** Silva's own two books still disagree on Aries (poor match in the Pisces chapter, "may make for a fun Pisces fling"; "Aries is most compatible with Virgo, Sagittarius, and **Pisces**" in *Sun & Moon*). Emerson still rates Sagittarius/Pisces "a fantastic match" despite it being a **square** in his own aspect table.
- **No metal, no formal shape, no architecture.** The Snodgrass correspondence row is OCR column-scrambled; the geography entry (Portugal) is probable, not certain; there is still no architectural material for Pisces anywhere in the corpus, unlike several other signs. The supplementary pass added interior décor (murals, cushions, vintage, outsider art, painted walls in non-water colors) but nothing structural.
- **Tierney's most useful sentence is still truncated mid-clause** — "using its charisma to…" — cut off in the source text. The Silva supplement does not contain it; the rest of that thought is not recoverable.
- **Heavy Christian/mystical loading, now slightly heavier.** Roughly half the myth material is Christological (ichthys, Matthias, the Age of Pisces, loaves and fishes), and the supplement adds baptism. Usable structurally, but it must be *stripped of religious specificity* before any of it reaches a Tepenian page — the district needs a secret mark on a doorframe and a procedure you come out of as someone else, not a church.
- **Corpus-internal contradiction on the filter, deliberately left standing.** Tierney: no psychological filter, the door lets everything in. Silva: an unerring sixth sense for exploiters, phonies, and narcissists that fires on sight. Both are quoted in §3 and reconciled there by scope (detection is universal, exclusion applies only to the inner ring), but that reconciliation is *derivation* — no source states it.
