# Decans — Sub-Neighborhood Texture
**Status:** Design substrate only — NOT in-fiction. See `00_Method_and_Sources.md`.

**Source strength: WEAK — the weakest slice in this folder.** Only **two of the eight books mention decans at all**
(Emerson; Silva's *Ultimate Guide*). Snodgrass, Tierney, all three Martin volumes, and Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs*
contain **zero** occurrences of "decan" or "decanate" — there is no independent corroboration anywhere in the corpus.
Emerson gives every sign roughly **three to six sentences** and nothing more. Silva gives a real chapter to **four
signs only** (Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Pisces) and skips the other eight. Date ranges **contradict each other between
sources and are internally inconsistent within each source**. Section 3 — the application — is the part of this file
worth reading; sections 1 and 2 exist so a future reader knows how little is actually here.

---

## 1. What a Decan Is

Each sign spans 30° of the zodiac; each is divided into **three 10° segments**, called a *decan* or (older spelling,
used interchangeably by Silva) a *decanate*. Silva traces the word to Greek *dekanoi*, "separated by ten days," from
an Egyptian 360-day calendar of twelve 30-day months each split into three ten-day sections. Both sources are
emphatic on the same point: **a decan does not change the sign, it refines it.** Silva: "the decan of a zodiac sign
doesn't alter the fundamental characteristics of the sign. Instead, it only refines and personalizes the zodiac's
general traits… You are special and different from them while they are special and different from you." The planet
attached to a decan is a **sub-ruler**, explicitly not a replacement — Silva's own analogy is "kind of like a Vice
President."

**The two schemes named in the brief are not actually competing in this corpus — they are one scheme stated twice.**
Both books describe (a) each decan carrying a **planetary sub-ruler**, and (b) each decan being **flavored by another
sign of the same temperament family**. But (a) is derived from (b): the sub-ruling planet *is* the ruler of the
same-family sign. Silva states the generating rule outright: "Each sign's decan shares a Triplicity with the zodiac
sign in the same order they appear in the Zodiac. So, for example, Aries first decan is Aries and Mars rules it. Leo
is the second decan, and the Sun rules it."

**So the operative scheme is:** decan 1 = the sign blended with **itself** (double-strength, own ruler); decan 2 = the
sign blended with the **next** sign of its temperament family; decan 3 = the sign blended with the **remaining** sign
of that family. The corpus presents this as *the* scheme and never acknowledges that any alternative exists.

> Reminder on the second binding constraint: the four classical elements are an out-of-fiction grouping of *signs*
> only. Never write "the Fire district." See `00_Method_and_Sources.md` and `A_Elements.md`.

### Where a second source exists (Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Pisces only)

Silva's four decan chapters are the only places in the corpus with any real depth. What they add beyond the table
(the Virgo and Pisces material is quoted more fully in §3, where it is put to work):

- **Virgo** — the only sign given **keywords and constellations**: 1st *Analyze* (Crater, the cup of Apollo —
  "emotional generosity"); 2nd *Efficient* (Hercules, "who wins a victory over evil"); 3rd *Discriminate* (Boötes, the
  bear driver or old herdsman — "the symbol of using wisdom"). Also: 1st is warm and witty but admits few — "not
  everyone gets a ticket into the inner chambers"; 2nd is "perceived as focused and persistent when, in truth, you are
  mutable and versatile"; 3rd runs on approval.
- **Libra** — Silva's text is near-identical to Emerson's, to the point of shared phrasing; **it is not independent
  corroboration.** It adds only "very You-Oriented," "paradoxical," and "rays from both Saturn and Uranus" on the 2nd.
- **Scorpio** — the only sign given **decan names and tarot cards**: 1st *Intensity* (Five of Cups; grief, loss,
  anger); 2nd *Transmutation* (Six of Cups; peace, forgiveness, harmony); 3rd *Manifestation* (Seven of Cups; finding
  where you fit in, soul searching, self-examination). 1st: "the purest of Scorpios… enhanced in good and bad ways"; a
  secretive loner with "bulldog-like tenacity," and — usefully — "the one just learning how to make use of your
  energies… you might make a few mistakes." 2nd: "death and resurrection," a felt higher purpose, craving mystery and
  the occult, possibly a healer, possibly "a bit of a God complex," cunning "used to gain control over other people."
  3rd: never shows pain openly — "but you never forget what caused it… When time and chance present themselves, you
  will exact revenge, and do so generously."
- **Pisces** — 1st: "the decan of imagination and intuition," driven by "philanthropy and service to their community,"
  "born to be caretakers." 2nd: "Pisces at its most balanced, emotionally"; wacky and eccentric; comfortable alone;
  "transformative thinkers, they build better mousetraps"; "Don't dream it, be it!" 3rd, under Silva's Mars reading:
  "the last decanate in the zodiac's last sign… forces of nature," "dynamic information processors," empaths,
  "visionaries, with a strong orientation toward the practical," with fate more active in their lives than planning.

## 3. Design Application — three sub-neighborhoods per district

This is what the file is for. The data above is thin; **the structure it implies is not.**

**The pattern.** A district splits into three areas that share one core character and differ in how that character is
expressed:

1. **The pure core** — the district doing its own thing at double strength, with nothing diluting it. Oldest, most
   orthodox, least apologetic. Because nothing softens it, this is also where the district's **shadow is most naked**.
   Silva's phrasing is the whole brief: "the purest… enhanced in good and bad ways," and "just learning how to make use
   of your energies… you might make a few mistakes."
2. **The blend** — the same motive pursued through a *neighboring* district's method. Not softer; **redirected**. This
   is the quarter that the borrowed-from district finds legible, and therefore where cross-district ties actually form.
3. **The outward face** — the district turned toward the rest of the city. The corpus makes third decans consistently
   the most versatile, most articulate, most public-facing ("extremely versatile, more so than any other decan";
   "appeals to the public"; "the most humanitarian") **and the most compromised** ("never let anyone control you" to the
   point of ruined relationships; "absolute refusal to admit when you're wrong"). The pure core usually regards this
   quarter as sold out. That disagreement is free internal politics.

**The useful constraint:** decans 2 and 3 are not arbitrary. Each names **two specific other districts** whose methods
the sub-neighborhood borrows. That converts "invent a sub-area" into "answer a question with only one correct answer."

**What this does NOT license.** The spatial reading — core inward, third decan outward — is a *design decision*, not
anything the corpus says; decans are degrees and dates, not geography. And none of the scaffolding surfaces in play:
no sub-neighborhood is ever named after a decan, a planet, a constellation, or a tarot card, and no character ever
refers to any of it.

### Worked example — District 6, The Yards *(Capricorn → Taurus → Virgo)*

- **1. The foundry core (Saturn).** "Determination and patience are legendary"; "dictatorial, rigid, and inflexible";
  capability greater than the confidence it projects. The original master-tier workstations and the oldest spires. This
  is where **The Narrow Door** was calibrated and where it is most fiercely defended; the merit boards are largest here
  and the passed-over undercurrent is most bitter, because this is the ladder that will not move.
- **2. The guild-family belt (Venus).** Same work ethic, but it "makes time for rest, relaxation, and artistic
  pursuits" and "slowly takes in new experiences." Read as: craft treated as heritage rather than competition — guild
  dinners, binding ceremonies, domestic comfort earned and displayed. The quarter Taurus finds intelligible, and the
  natural site of cross-district guild ties. Its failure mode is exactly the source's: it will not adopt a new method.
- **3. The audit quarter (Mercury).** "Restless… a great need for efficiency and functionality… a scientific mind…
  evidence empirically backed." Quality control, process engineering, the only part of The Yards with the vocabulary
  and data discipline to argue that the meritocracy is miscalibrated — and the only part that talks to The Undergrid.
  The independent review of c. 2761 would have been staffed from here. The core reads it as disloyal; it reads the core
  as sentimental. **The district's existing canon fault line, now localized on a map.**

### Worked example — District 1, The Sanctuary *(Cancer → Scorpio → Pisces)*

- **1. The old clusters (Moon).** "Limitless energy and compassion," and "a tendency to be very jealous." Unlocked
  doors, kept chairs, lullaby masters, the memorial walls. Also the smother-lock at full strength, and where the
  survivors of the Overcrowding Decision live and still argue.
- **2. The clinical quarter (Pluto).** "Unapproachable and reserved to the point of seeming emotionless, when you are
  actually the exact opposite"; "a great therapist, psychologist, or **detective**." Professionalized care where warmth
  is deliberately withheld because the work requires it — and the natural home of the **Mother's Circuit**, since the
  "detective" note is precisely a network that maintains a collective portrait of who is at risk. Scorpio respects this
  quarter; Cancer's own core distrusts it as cold.
- **3. The intake edge (Neptune).** "Tireless," "born nurses, though sometimes moody," "fast-changing feelings that
  don't have a logical explanation." Where new refugee waves land: over-extended, boundary-less, run by volunteers
  rather than rosters. Where the district's burned-out caregivers are actually produced, and its most porous border
  with The Markets.

### Worked example — District 10, The Markets *(Pisces → Cancer → Scorpio)*

- **1. The dream-den heart (Neptune).** "Wandering through life like a leaf in the wind"; "loves comfort more than
  money"; "less assertive than the other two — be careful that you don't get taken advantage of." Dissolution proper:
  nothing enforced, nothing defended, and therefore the most exploited part of the district. The Flood Memorial Dens
  belong here.
- **2. The residential Markets (Moon).** "In need of security, both spiritually and financially… the most loyal and
  sweet… careful of clinginess." The half of Pisces that is a neighborhood rather than a trade — families, mutual aid,
  the informal networks that made the gray economy survivable rather than predatory. Cancer would recognize this
  quarter as kin. Every other district's account of Pisces leaves it out entirely.
- **3. The hard edge — and here the source conflict is a gift.** Under Emerson's **Pluto**: "a vindictive and jealous
  streak when compared to other Pisceans" — the syndicate quarter, the part with teeth and a long memory for injury.
  Under Silva's **Mars**: "forces of nature… dynamic information processors… visionaries with a strong orientation
  toward the practical… often gifted in science and technology" — the fixers and interface specialists who actually
  build the dream-tech. **Take both.** A technically brilliant quarter that never forgets a grudge is more interesting
  than either reading alone, and the corpus genuinely supports both.

### Worked example — District 12, The Undergrid *(Virgo → Capricorn → Taurus)*

The best-sourced district here — the only one with keywords and constellations.

- **1. The diagnostic core (Mercury; *Analyze*).** Insights "considered as prophetic, when they are simply results of
  acute observation"; "not everyone gets a ticket into the inner chambers." The deep-tunnel quarter of the undergrid
  families, closed to outsiders, where the district's total knowledge of the city actually lives. The guarded entry maps
  exactly onto the district's existing social aristocracy, and the "prophecy" line explains why the rest of Concordia
  finds Virgo uncanny: it is only observation nobody else bothered to do.
- **2. The rota quarter (Saturn; *Efficient*).** "Plans in advance to avoid mistakes"; "perceived as focused and
  persistent when, in truth, mutable and versatile"; "the kind of perfectionist that suffers unnecessarily when you fall
  short of your own expectations." Shift supervision, maintenance-cycle planning, contracts and specifications. **The
  Year of Silence was organized from here** — working to rule requires knowing exactly what the rule says. Also where
  the burnout is most self-inflicted.
- **3. The surface-facing quarter (Venus; *Discriminate*).** "**Approval is your major motivator**"; connoisseur of
  expression; "perfect use of color represented in your appearance or surroundings"; "recognized for your diplomacy and
  sensitivity"; "finds no joy living under limitations." Liaison crews who go up and face the districts they serve —
  and the home of the **decorated junction-box galleries** already in canon. The source reframes those galleries: they
  are not decoration, they are a bid for acknowledgment from a district that never receives any. The exact wound the
  canon names, localized in one neighborhood.

## 4. Caution

**This scheme is weakly sourced and is offered as an OPTIONAL structuring device, not a canon requirement.** A district
does not have to have three sub-neighborhoods. Some districts will read better as one continuous place, or as two, or
as a dozen. Where the three-way split *does* help — level layout, giving a large district visual and tonal variety
without fracturing its identity, or locating an existing canon conflict on an actual map — use it. Where it would force
an invention nothing else supports, drop it. Nothing downstream depends on this file, and no district canon has been
modified to fit it.

## 5. Source Gaps

**Coverage**

- **Two sources out of eight.** Emerson and Silva's *Ultimate Guide* only. Snodgrass, Tierney, Martin v1/v2/v3, and
  Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs* have **zero** hits for "decan" or "decanate." No cross-checking is possible.
- **Silva covers four signs, not twelve** — Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Pisces. The other eight single-sign books in the
  omnibus have no decan chapter. So eight of twelve signs rest on Emerson alone, at three to six sentences each.
- **Silva's Libra chapter shares wording with Emerson's** closely enough that it should not be counted as a second
  independent source for that sign.
- **Ophiuchus / the Hub has no decan material at all.** The scheme is 12 × 3 by construction. District 13 gets nothing.

**Internal inconsistencies and outright errors**

- **Dates conflict between sources.** Virgo is offset by two days (Silva Aug 22–Sep 1 / Sep 2–12 / Sep 13–22 vs Emerson
  Aug 24–Sep 3 / Sep 4–13 / Sep 14–22). Scorpio is offset by one (Silva Oct 23–Nov 2 / Nov 2–11 / Nov 12–22 vs Emerson
  Oct 24–Nov 2 / Nov 3–12 / Nov 13–22). Neither book acknowledges the other scheme.
- **Silva's Scorpio boundaries overlap themselves** — November 2 is assigned to both the first and second decan.
- **Emerson's own ranges are unequal** where 10° segments should be equal: Aquarius 1st spans nine days, Sagittarius 3rd
  nine, while Aries/Taurus/Gemini/Leo 3rd span eleven. Silva waves this away — "this isn't accurate because not every
  month is exactly 30 days, but the math is the same" — and never resolves it.
- **Pisces 1st is given as February 19–29**, a range that does not exist in three years out of four. Unaddressed.
- **Ruler conflict, Pisces 3rd:** Emerson says **Pluto**, Silva says **Mars**. Both are naming Scorpio's ruler — modern
  versus traditional — but neither acknowledges the other, and Emerson's own paragraph then explains the decan's traits
  "since you are influenced by Neptune," which is neither of them. Its heading also states the ruler twice.
- **Ruler-listing conflict, Scorpio:** Emerson gives single modern rulers (Pluto, Neptune); Silva gives traditional +
  modern pairs (Mars and Pluto; Jupiter and Neptune). Silva's Libra 2nd likewise says "rays from both Saturn and
  Uranus" while its own heading reads "Ruler – Uranus."
- **Degree typo:** Silva's Libra 3rd decan is printed as "10° to 30°." It should read 20°–30°.
- **Silva's Scorpio chapter refers to "a Scorpio born in the fifth decan."** There is no fifth decan.
- **Emerson's explanation of the scheme is muddled:** "if you are a Libra and you were born in the first ten days… Libra
  is your planetary sub-ruler." Libra is a sign, not a planet.

**Missing entirely**

- **No shadow depth.** Decan entries are overwhelmingly positive-trait lists with one or two vices appended. Nothing
  here approaches the shadow material in the per-sign files, which is where this folder's real value sits.
- **Extra layers exist for a subset only.** Keywords and constellations for **Virgo alone**; decan names, tarot cards,
  and tarot keywords for **Scorpio alone**. Silva asserts as a general rule that "each decan is represented by a
  constellation" — and then supplies constellations for exactly three decans out of thirty-six.
- **Nothing on decan boundaries.** Both books discuss sign-cusps at length and neither says anything about what happens
  at the edge between two decans of the same sign — the obvious question for a sub-neighborhood border.
- **No alternative scheme is acknowledged.** The corpus presents the triplicity/same-family scheme as the scheme, never
  as one option among several. Whether other decan systems exist historically is a question these eight books do not
  answer, and this file should not be read as claiming they do not.
