# Correspondences — Material Texture Reference

**Status:** Design substrate only — NOT in-fiction. See `00_Method_and_Sources.md`.

**Why this file exists:** Four separate per-sign extraction agents independently reported that their
correspondence data (stones, metals, plants, colors, shapes, pathology) was truncated or corrupt in their
slices — Cancer reported *no stone, metal, or plant surviving anywhere in the corpus*; Aries, Sagittarius,
Aquarius and Pisces each reported the metal column missing. This file is the systematic fix: one
cross-checked table built from the two books that actually carry correspondence data properly, checked
against every per-sign file's own quotations. **Most of those reported gaps turned out to be slice
artifacts, and are closed here.**

**Source reliability — read before using any cell:**

1. **Emerson's per-sign blocks are misaligned in places.** Verified in this slice: the CANCER block prints
   `Element: Air` where Emerson's own prose two lines above says "Cancer is a cardinal water sign." The
   GEMINI block prints `Celestial Symbol: Deer`. Those are the two hard corruptions.
2. **The warning attached to the source slice is itself partly wrong.** It claims Cancer's block "carries
   Jade / peppermint / root-chakra values that actually belong to CAPRICORN." In the full text those three
   values sit inside **Capricorn's own block, where they belong**. The bleed the Cancer agent saw was
   caused by *its slice boundary cutting mid-block* and pulling the following sign's lines in — not by the
   underlying text. Same for the reported "missing Tarot line": **all twelve Tarot entries are present**
   here. Treat per-sign corruption reports as slice-local until confirmed against this file.
3. **Snodgrass Appendix IV is planet-keyed, not sign-keyed.** Pushing it through rulership to reach a sign
   generates apparent "conflicts" that are artifacts of the method. Where Appendix IV and a sign-keyed
   source disagree, that is two systems, not one corrupted one. Both are recorded; neither is deleted.
4. **Nothing here is backfilled from general astrological knowledge.** Every cell is traceable to Emerson's
   block, a Snodgrass appendix, or a Snodgrass/Silva/Martin quotation already captured in a sibling file.
   Gaps are left as gaps.

**Confidence legend used throughout:**

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **✓✓** | Two or more independent sources agree |
| **✓** | One source, uncontradicted anywhere in the corpus |
| **⚠** | Contested — another source gives a different value; both recorded |
| **✗** | Demonstrably corrupt; correction given where derivable |
| **—** | No corpus data. Do not invent. |

## Per-Sign Detail

### Aries
- **Emerson:** Mars · Fire · head · red · black candle · basil · amethyst · rosemary oil · Ram · solar plexus · Libra · The Emperor.
- **Metal:** **iron** (Mars, App. IV). Mars also carries **bloodstone** and **flint** as jewels — available as an alternate mineral if amethyst is wanted elsewhere.
- **Stone — resolved in Emerson's favor.** Amethyst appears twice across the twelve blocks (Aries, Pisces), which looks like corruption. It is not: **Snodgrass's own per-sign table independently gives Aries amethyst** (quoted in `01_Aries.md`). Two sources agree. The Appendix IV assignment of amethyst to *Jupiter* is a planet-keyed statement in a different system and does not overturn it. Silva separately offers diamond and garnet for Aries — three-way disagreement, kept apart.
- **Plant — contested.** Emerson says basil; Snodgrass's per-sign table says **flower sage, tree thorn**. Basil appears in no Snodgrass herb list. Note that **Emerson assigns sage to Cancer**, so sage is claimed by two signs across two sources. Unresolvable here.
- **Candle: black — flagged.** The only candle in the twelve that contradicts its own sign's color. Black is Saturn-coded in Appendix IV ("black stones"). No correction derivable; treat as suspect.
- **Snodgrass Appendix V:** eating *hurried, finicky*; symptoms *excessive force, hot, dry skin, inflammation*; afflictions *sleep disturbances, brain irregularities, vertigo, inflamed eyes and gums, scalp disease, fever, fainting*. Confirms head rulership independently.
- **Appendix II (Ari):** overhead 10/30 · highest altitude **4/31 ✗ (impossible date — April has 30 days; source corruption)** · brightness 6.344, 7th · area 441.39 sq° (11th) · 28 stars, 4 major. Second-smallest zodiac constellation.
- **Appendix I:** 1664 — Hooke discovers **Mesarthim**, Aries's gamma star.
- **Beyond Emerson (from sibling files):** topography **desert**.

## Design Notes — using correspondences for district texture

Concordia is a **sealed city on the Antarctic ice**. Every correspondence below has been run through that
filter. The zodiac itself remains out-of-fiction: residents do not know why their district works in copper.

**1. The metals are the real palette; the gems mostly are not.** Appendix IV's metal column now covers all
twelve signs and it is the single most usable output of this file — gold, silver, copper, quicksilver, iron,
tin, lead, radium, tungsten, platinum, lithium, plutonium. In a sealed city every metal is **reclaimed**, so a
district's signature metal is really a statement about what it pulls out of the waste stream and who controls
that stream. Note that four of the twelve are *not* craft materials: radium (Aquarius) and plutonium
(Scorpio) are instrumentation and reactor context, never surfaces; tungsten (Pisces) belongs in filaments and
heat, platinum in catalysis. Treat those as **what the district handles**, not what it decorates with.

**2. Antarctic enclosure inverts the value of stones.** Antarctica's geology is under two kilometers of ice
and there is no gem industry. But a robot city can *grow* minerals: diamond, emerald, sapphire, garnet and
quartz are all synthesizable, while **opal** (slow silica accretion) and **jade** (metamorphic mass) are not.
So the Earth hierarchy flips — **Leo's diamond is cheap and Cancer's opal is precious.** A Leo district that
gilds everything in synthetic diamond is being gaudy with a commodity; a Cancer household with one opal has
an heirloom that cannot be replaced. Obsidian (Scorpio) is the interesting middle case: volcanic glass is
locally plausible on this continent in a way nothing else on the list is.

**3. The plant list splits cleanly by cost, and the split is free characterization.** Short-cycle hydroponic
herbs — **mint, peppermint, sage, parsley, basil, marjoram, catnip, lemon balm** — grow anywhere in the city
for nothing. Everything else on the list needs flagging: **saffron** (Sagittarius) is 150,000 hand-picked
flowers per kilogram and in a sealed city becomes pure flaunting; **sunflower** (Leo) is a tall,
light-hungry, heliotropic crop under lamps in a place with no sun — a trophy, which is perfectly Leo;
**frankincense** (Taurus) is arid-upland tree resin, import-only, and collides with Taurus's own attested
dislike of synthetic substitutes; **kava kava** (Aquarius) is a three-to-five-year Pacific root crop and a
psychoactive, so it lands in pharmacology or contraband, not the herb garden. And any correspondence naming
a **tree** — Aries's thorn, Pisces's willow, Virgo's nut-bearing trees, Scorpio's blackthorn — has a volume
and decades problem: a single specimen tree in this city is a civic monument, not landscaping.

**4. Four of the twelve essential oils are citrus, and none of them can grow here.** Lemon (Leo), sweet
orange (Sagittarius), lime (Aquarius), grapefruit (Pisces). Citrus is a tree with a long juvenile period and
a large light budget. This is a feature: it forces a decision per district between **import** (who is paying
freight from 4,000 km away, and why), **synthetic ester** (cheap, slightly wrong, and a class marker), or a
**prestige orangery** under lamps. What a district's air smells like becomes an economic statement rather
than a decorative one. This is the highest-yield finding in the file for cheap sensory writing — but note
the oil column is also the *least* reliable, so treat these as prompts, not canon.

**5. Rewrite Appendix V pathology as the epidemiology of a sealed city.** The affliction lists survive
completely and they translate almost mechanically into infrastructure. Gemini's asthma/pneumonia/pulmonary
list is a **ventilation** problem; Taurus's laryngitis/bronchitis/swollen-glands list is **airborne
particulates**; Aquarius's varicose veins, edema and leg cramps are **standing labor**; Capricorn's knee
degeneration and dislocated joints are **ladder-and-crawlspace work**; Virgo's parasites, colic and
peritonitis are a **water-loop contamination** story; Capricorn's dry mucosa and Aries's dry skin are
**cold-dry recycled air**. A district's clinic queue is now derivable without inventing anything. **Gap
worth naming: the corpus has no entry anywhere for light deprivation**, which is the actual defining
Antarctic pathology. That one has to be built from scratch, for every district.

**6. Translate pathology into frame failure, keeping the organ logic.** These districts are robot-majority
and the appendix is human medicine, but the mechanisms transfer better than expected. Cancer's
**alcoholism** maps onto glitch-coolant dependency. Scorpio's mechanism — "in tight control of excreta, the
type engulfs the body with toxins" — is literally a closed waste loop poisoning its own body. And
Capricorn's symptom **"crystallization"** is the best single word in the appendix: mineral deposition in
joints and lines is a real robot fault and a real district metaphor at the same time. Keep the project's
vocabulary: **frame** and **body**, never "chassis."

**7. Use the Appendix IV Governance column to set what a district *does*.** It is fully intact and it names
occupations directly: Sun → business executives and authority figures (Leo); Moon → workers and public
figures (Cancer); Mercury → intelligentsia (Gemini, Virgo); Venus → ambassadors and social leaders (Taurus,
Libra); Mars → military leaders and weapon makers (Aries, and Scorpio traditionally); Jupiter → judiciary,
investors and financiers (Sagittarius, Pisces traditionally); **Saturn → executives of state *and the poor*
in the same cell** (Capricorn, Aquarius traditionally) — that internal contradiction is a district design in
itself; Uranus → transportation and social workers (Aquarius); Neptune → demagogues and organized labor
(Pisces); Pluto → idealists (Scorpio).

**8. Shapes give an architectural grammar, but do not build canon on it.** The Form/Shape column is a
reconstruction. Used as a tiebreaker on a facade brief it is fine: circles and arcs for Leo, broken lines for
Capricorn, sharp angles mixed with curves for Aquarius, angles-and-curves for the Venus districts, short
lines for the Mercury districts. Pair it with the *sign*-keyed geometry from the per-sign files, which is far
better attested — Virgo's hexagon/honeycomb, Libra's two horizontal lines and bilateral symmetry, Gemini's
paired figures, Pisces's circlet, Scorpio's fish hook, Sagittarius's arrow.

**9. Food is the cheapest characterization available, and the enclosure sharpens all of it.** Appendix V's
eating habits are intact for all twelve. Under the Antarctic filter they re-rank: **Pisces's "bubbly drinks"
become the one food correspondence that is *cheaper* here than on Earth** — CO₂ is a life-support scrubber
byproduct, so carbonation is nearly free and a Pisces market floods with it. **Taurus's "loves potatoes"** is
the perfect closed-loop staple. **Sagittarius's "red meat"** is the most expensive line in the appendix — it
means either livestock volume nobody can spare or vat culture, and either answer characterizes the Frostlands.
**Aquarius's "natural foods"** is a political claim in a city that synthesizes most of its calories.
**Scorpio's "condiments and hors d'oeuvres"** is import-dependent. **Libra's "prefers ornate place settings"**
isn't about food at all — it is a craft tradition in tableware, which pairs with copper.

**10. Do not use Appendix III.** See above. Geography-to-sign mapping would import national stereotype
straight into district culture.
