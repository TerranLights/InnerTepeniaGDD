# Planetary Rulerships & Dignities — the District Capability Model
**Status:** Design substrate only — NOT in-fiction. See `00_Method_and_Sources.md`.
**Sources mined:** Clare Martin, *Mapping the Psyche* v1 (**dominant** — the only source with a complete, internally consistent per-sign dignity table, plus the psychological mechanics of detriment and fall); Martin v2 (house rulers, personal/transpersonal ruler distinction); Martin v3 (scattered confirmations); Silva *Zodiac Signs: Ultimate Guide* (moderate — Capricorn dignities in detail, Libra/Pisces exaltations, plus several errors, see §7); Silva *Sun and Moon Signs* (modern-only ruler list); Snodgrass, Tierney, Emerson (**nothing usable** — their hits in this slice are keyword-match artifacts on the ordinary English words "ruler" and "exalt").

---

## 1. What Rulership Claims

A sign's **ruling planet is its governing faculty** — the one capacity the sign is organized around, exercises without effort, and cannot see as a choice. Martin: the ruler is the planet "which is said to function strongly and be particularly comfortable and 'at home' in the sign."

For district design this converts directly: **the ruling planet is what the district is institutionally built to do.** Not what it values, not what it says about itself — what its bureaucracy, its labor, and its daily habits are actually shaped to produce. It is the district's default answer to every problem, applied whether or not the problem calls for it.

The assignment is not arbitrary in the sources; it is a symmetrical scheme. Martin: the rulers "were allocated by the Greeks in a symmetrical and balanced proportion," distributed outward from the Sun in order of observed orbital speed — Sun to Leo (the hottest point of the year), Moon to Cancer, then Mercury (Gemini, Virgo), Venus (Taurus, Libra), Mars (Aries, Scorpio), Jupiter (Sagittarius, Pisces), and slowest, at the coldest and darkest point of the year, Saturn (Capricorn, Aquarius). Every planet but the two lights rules a pair — one active sign and one receptive — which is why **the same faculty produces two very different districts.** Mars in the Power Core is instinctive and loud; Mars in Scorpio is "the professional soldier rather than the football hooligan, the knife used with skill and precision by the surgeon" (Martin). Same capacity, opposite posture. Use this before inventing anything: two districts sharing a ruler are not redundant, they are the loud and quiet versions of one faculty.

## 2. The Traditional vs. Modern Split — and why BOTH are useful

Three signs have two rulers. The outer planets are, in Martin's phrase, "relatively recent newcomers... added to this model." She herself prefers traditional rulerships "because they lie within the boundary of Saturn, which helps us to stay grounded."

**Do not resolve this. Each split yields a genuinely different district; treat it as a design menu, or as a district's own history.**

| Sign / District | Traditional reading | Modern reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Scorpio → D4** | **Mars.** Disciplined, personal-scale force. An internal-affairs and forensic district: skilled operators, controlled aggression, surgical intervention on individuals. | **Pluto.** Collective purging. A district that periodically forces the whole city's buried material to the surface "so that it can be healed" (Martin) — civilizational scale, not personal. |
| **Aquarius → D7 The Labs** | **Saturn.** The city's standards body. Rules, structures, institutions built slowly for collective benefit. Conservative in method, radical in aim. Brings "a sense of inadequacy and a fear of being exposed or ridiculed." | **Uranus.** The refusal. "Uranus describes where in the chart we refuse to engage" (Martin) — disruption, invention, deviation from norm as a positive value. |
| **Pisces → D10 The Markets** | **Jupiter.** The "joyful, ecstatic 'dolphin' nature" (Martin). A gray economy of abundance, generosity, and plenty. | **Neptune.** Dissolution. The bolt-hole, "a place of escape and seclusion," and "the part in all of us that... longs to return to formlessness and oblivion." An economy of escape, intoxication, and self-erasure. |

Martin supplies a fourth use: **a sequence.** On Aquarius she describes Saturn dominating the first half of life (conformity, hiding the sense of separateness) and Uranus emerging later, until "people... become more and more comfortable being who they are and start to enjoy deviating from the norm." That is a district *history*, not a contradiction — The Labs founded as a standards bureau and matured into a deviance engine. The same trick works on Scorpio and the Markets.

Under the modern scheme the detriments shift too: Pluto's detriment falls in Taurus, Uranus's in Leo, Neptune's in Virgo. Not in the corpus explicitly, but forced by the opposition rule (§4) and worth using — it is the only thing the modern scheme adds to §5.

## 3. The Planets as Faculties

Stated behaviorally, for a civic institution rather than a person.

- **Sun** — Identity and named authority. The capacity to produce a center: a figure or institution that others organize around, with "personal authenticity, integrity and authority which is not dependent on the opinions of others" (Martin). Where the Sun is strong, someone will put their name on a decision.
- **Moon** — Care, belonging, and the emotional container. The capacity to house and feed people, to make somewhere feel like somewhere you are from. Martin's fourth-house language: "the quality of the nurturing we experienced... how safe we feel about belonging."
- **Mercury** — Analysis, record, transmission, verification. Objectivity, "analytical thinking," the partial and multiple truth, and the sense of humor that comes with holding more than one frame at once.
- **Venus** — Valuation and relatedness. What things are worth, and to whom. Martin: "an individual's values, talents and joys" — plus beauty, comfort, and, as ruler of Libra, "justice, diplomacy, equality." Also plain gentleness.
- **Mars** — Assertion, initiative, force. Standing up for oneself; starting things; the willingness to make a claim against someone. Two modes: instinctive and hot, or "highly trained and disciplined in the controlled use of energy and aggression."
- **Jupiter** — Meaning, scale, and generosity. "Keep the wood in mind as well as the trees." The capacity to say what the work is *for*, and to extend belonging past the immediate clan "to community, society, country, to ancestors and back into history."
- **Saturn** — Limits, structure, time, law. "Lord of time." Discipline, patience, the deferred reward, the enforceable boundary, the concrete track record. The faculty that says *no* and makes it stick.
- **Uranus** — Refusal and disruption. Foresight, invention, the challenge to the status quo, "led from the head rather than" the body. The capacity to not participate.
- **Neptune** — Dissolution and devotion. Compassion, mercy, imagination — and the erasure of boundaries between self and other, gift and debt, real and imagined. Escape as an institution.
- **Pluto** — Purge and regeneration. Bringing to the surface what "has become polluted, poisonous or taboo" so it can be eliminated. Power, and the abuse of it: "otherwise we become power demons and poison both ourselves and others."

## 4. The Four Dignities

- **Domicile (rulership).** The faculty is native and free. It costs the district nothing and is therefore invisible to it — nobody in the district experiences it as a choice or a skill. This is why districts are worst at explaining their own competence.
- **Exaltation.** The faculty is a guest of honor: amplified, celebrated, given more weight than it can carry. Distinct from domicile precisely because it is *not* native — it is performed, with a slight excess, and the district is proud of it in a way it is never proud of its ruler. Institutionally: the thing the district over-invests in and puts on its letterhead.
- **Detriment.** The sign opposite the planet's home. The faculty **works against the grain** — it is not absent. Martin is emphatic on this and it is the single most useful correction in the slice: told that Venus in Aries "doesn't work well there because it is in its detriment," she answers that on the contrary "this is a powerful Venus because she knows what she wants." The faculty survives, but only by being reshaped into the local idiom. **Institutional read: the district will perform this function only by disguising it as something else.**
- **Fall.** The sign opposite the planet's exaltation. Weakest, and — the operative word — **least trusted.** Martin's term is "debilitated." Where detriment is distortion, fall is *contempt*: the district has learned to look down on this capacity. **Institutional read: this is the function the district would sooner outsource, defund, or moralize against than perform itself.**

Martin's whole method is that detriment and fall are the **developmental task**, not the verdict: "focus on the conscious integration of... the planets which are traditionally in their detriment and fall." For district design that means the deficit is not a static flaw to note once. It is where the district's drama lives — every attempt to import, fake, or finally develop the missing faculty is a story.

## 5. COMPLETE DIGNITY TABLE — all twelve signs

| Sign | District | Domicile (trad.) | Modern | Exaltation | Detriment | Fall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aries | 5 — The Power Core | Mars | — | Sun | Venus | Saturn |
| Taurus | 2 — Taurus | Venus | — | Moon | Mars | *(none)* |
| Gemini | 9 — The Circuit | Mercury | — | *(none)* | Jupiter | *(none)* |
| Cancer | 1 — The Sanctuary | Moon | — | Jupiter | Saturn | Mars |
| Leo | 3 — Leo | Sun | — | *(none)* | Saturn | *(none)* |
| Virgo | 12 — The Undergrid | Mercury | — | **Mercury** | Jupiter | Venus |
| Libra | 8 — The Government District | Venus | — | Saturn | Mars | Sun |
| Scorpio | 4 — Scorpio | Mars | Pluto | *(none)* | Venus | Moon |
| Sagittarius | 11 — The Frostlands | Jupiter | — | *(none)* | Mercury | *(none)* |
| Capricorn | 6 — The Yards | Saturn | — | Mars | Moon | Jupiter |
| Aquarius | 7 — The Labs | Saturn | Uranus | *(none)* | Sun | *(none)* |
| Pisces | 10 — The Markets | Jupiter | Neptune | Venus | Mercury | **Mercury** |

**Verification.** Every row was checked mechanically against the two generative rules — *detriment = the sign opposite the planet's domicile*, *fall = the sign opposite the planet's exaltation* — across all six opposition axes (Aries–Libra, Taurus–Scorpio, Gemini–Sagittarius, Cancer–Capricorn, Leo–Aquarius, Virgo–Pisces). Both rules hold for all twelve rows with no exceptions. The table also matches Martin v1's twelve per-sign headers cell-for-cell. **Only two cells are not attested anywhere in the corpus and are derived from the rules alone: Mercury's exaltation in Virgo and Mercury's fall in Pisces** (see §7).

Three structural facts fall out of the completed table, and all three are load-bearing for §6:

1. **Virgo is the only sign whose ruler is also its exalted planet.** Mercury twice. The Undergrid has one faculty at maximum and no second.
2. **Pisces is the only sign where one planet holds both detriment and fall.** Mercury twice, the other way. The Markets have one faculty at minimum, doubly weakened.
3. **Exaltation and fall trade across the axis.** Aries and Libra swap Sun and Saturn; Cancer and Capricorn swap Jupiter and Mars; Virgo and Pisces swap Mercury and Venus. Taurus and Scorpio go one way only (Moon exalted / Moon fallen). **Each of these districts' amplified faculty is exactly its opposite's despised one** — a ready-made supply relationship that neither side can accept gracefully. Gemini/Sagittarius and Leo/Aquarius have no exaltation traffic at all; their opposition is pure ruler-versus-detriment.

## 6. District Capability Readings

**D5 — The Power Core (Aries).** Structurally strong at force and initiative (Mars domiciled) and at producing named individual authority (Sun exalted — someone always steps forward and signs). Structurally deficient in valuation (Venus in detriment) and in limits, patience, and time (Saturn in fall). **Characteristic institutional failure: it cannot price or ration what it produces, and it cannot maintain a schedule.** Power gets allocated to whoever pushes hardest, because the district has no faculty for weighing competing claims — and preventive maintenance is deferred until failure, because deferred reward is the capacity it despises most. Note the geometry: both missing faculties are concentrated in District 8, where Venus is at home and Saturn is exalted. The Power Core structurally cannot do what the Government District is structurally best at, and cannot hear that from it.

**D2 — Taurus (residential/stability).** Structurally strong at holding and increasing value (Venus domiciled) and at care and belonging (Moon exalted — the strongest domestic capacity in the table). Deficient in assertion (Mars in detriment); **it is the only district with no fall at all**, meaning there is nothing it actively despises. **Characteristic institutional failure: it can hold value but cannot defend or reallocate it.** It will not evict, will not strike, will not initiate a claim, and will absorb encroachment rather than answer it — Martin's cure is exactly the missing planet: Mars would give it "the determination to stand up for its own values and to put itself and its own values first when necessary." Having no fall makes it the city's donor district: it never refuses on principle, only slowly, and neighboring districts have learned this.

**D9 — The Circuit (information).** Structurally strong at transmission, translation, and analysis (Mercury domiciled). Deficient in meaning and scale (Jupiter in detriment). Neither exaltation nor fall — nothing here is revered and nothing is taboo. **Characteristic institutional failure: it can tell you everything and cannot tell you what matters.** Its institutions issue briefings, never judgments; every claim gets equal airtime, and the district has no mechanism for ruling one account truer than another. It equates more information with better decisions and produces paralysis by parity. The absence of any exaltation *or* fall is the deeper problem: a district with no sacred and no forbidden is one the rest of the city cannot predict, and therefore does not trust with anything.

**D1 — The Sanctuary (Cancer).** Structurally strong at care and shelter (Moon domiciled) and — the sharper point — at *expanding who counts as family* (Jupiter exalted; Martin: the sense of belonging "reaches beyond the personal clan to greater connections to community, society, country"). Deficient in limits (Saturn in detriment) and self-assertion (Mars in fall). **Characteristic institutional failure: an admissions system with no denial mechanism, permanently over capacity.** The Jupiter exaltation makes this worse rather than better — the district is ideologically committed to taking more, and the one faculty that could cap intake is the weakest present. With Mars in fall, nobody advocates for themselves: grievances are always filed on behalf of someone more vulnerable than the complainant, and aggression can only route through a dependent.

**D3 — Leo (cultural/performative).** Structurally strong at identity and at generating a center (Sun domiciled) — everything organizes around one visible figure or institution. Deficient in structure, time, and the deferred reward (Saturn in detriment). No exaltation, no fall. **Characteristic institutional failure: no succession, no archive, no maintenance budget — institutions last exactly as long as their founder.** It can stage a spectacular annual festival and cannot write a building code. Martin's developmental note is the whole design brief for the district's arc: only "over time and through real concrete achievements" does its authority stop depending on applause. Under the modern scheme, Uranus is also in detriment here, which adds a second edge: it cannot metabolize a genuine outsider, so dissent leaves rather than reforms.

**D12 — The Undergrid (Virgo).** The most technically capable district in the table and the narrowest: Mercury is both its ruler and its exalted planet, so analysis is native *and* amplified, with nothing to counterweight it. Deficient in scale and meaning (Jupiter in detriment) and in self-valuation (Venus in fall). **Characteristic institutional failure: it can report the exact condition of every component and cannot say which system is worth saving** — it will perfect a subsystem that should have been decommissioned, on schedule, to specification. Venus in fall is the crueler half: the district cannot price its own labor or claim credit, reads exploitation as normal working conditions, and dresses chronic self-neglect as professionalism. Martin: the missing planets "help to balance what can otherwise be an 'all work and no play' approach." Perfect maintenance logs, no budget authority.

**D8 — The Government District (Libra).** The best-equipped legislative apparatus in the city: Venus domiciled (valuation, equity, diplomacy) *plus* Saturn exalted (law, structure — Martin: "Saturn... is very comfortable in this sign, since Saturn also rules laws"). Deficient in force (Mars in detriment) and in named individual authority (Sun in fall). **Characteristic institutional failure: it writes excellent law it cannot enforce, and cannot produce a person willing to be personally accountable for a decision.** Enforcement is delegated outward — usually to the Power Core, its opposite, where Mars is at home — and responsibility is diffused through committee until no name attaches to any outcome. The Sun in fall is why: committee is not this district's ideal, it is its only option, because a single accountable authority is the one thing it cannot generate. Silva's note that Libra will "dither, even in the face of real danger" is the emergency version.

**D4 — Scorpio (psychological/transformative).** Structurally strong at investigation, controlled force, and elimination (Mars domiciled in its disciplined mode; Pluto under the modern reading). Deficient in gentleness and valuation of others (Venus in detriment) and in care (Moon in fall). **It has no exaltation at all** — no faculty here is amplified beyond its native strength. **Characteristic institutional failure: world-class diagnosis with nowhere to convalesce.** It will identify what is wrong with you accurately and has no institution for recovery; its welfare provision is procedural, and its care workers burn out because care in this district is delivered as endurance. Martin's read is exact: integrating "Venus and the Moon, the feminine planets of gentleness, peace and caring which are in their detriment and fall" is what would "soften the otherwise brutal effectiveness of this sign." Comfort here is always an import — most naturally from D2, whose Moon exaltation is precisely this district's fall.

**D11 — The Frostlands (Sagittarius).** Structurally strong at meaning, scope, conviction, and the long journey (Jupiter domiciled). Deficient in detail, record, and objectivity (Mercury in detriment). No exaltation, no fall. **Characteristic institutional failure: it mistakes conviction for verification.** Expeditions are superbly motivated and badly provisioned; records are anecdotal; it will state a conclusion with total confidence and cannot show its work. It plans at the scale of the whole frontier and loses people to a detail nobody checked. It cannot run a bureaucracy, because paperwork is exactly where its single deficit lives — and Martin names the fix as the acknowledgment "that truths can be partial and multiple," which is the concession this district finds hardest.

**D6 — The Yards (Capricorn).** The strongest executive combination in the table: Saturn domiciled (structure, time, discipline) plus Mars exalted (Silva: Mars's "power and propulsion... blend uniquely with the single-minded and steady work ethic," aggression stabilized into perseverance). Deficient in care (Moon in detriment) and in meaning and generosity (Jupiter in fall). **Characteristic institutional failure: it can build anything and cannot say what for, so it substitutes the deadline itself as meaning.** Moon in detriment means welfare provision is not underfunded but structurally absent, and workers treat needing help as a disqualification — Martin on this placement: "self-sufficient... more comfortable looking after others than being looked after." Jupiter in fall means it will not fund anything speculative, which strangles its own future capacity while feeling like prudence.

**D7 — The Labs (Aquarius).** Structurally strong at collective structure (Saturn) or at refusal and invention (Uranus) — pick per §2, or run it as the district's history. Deficient in individual identity and worth (Sun in detriment). No exaltation, no fall. **Characteristic institutional failure: it cannot credit an individual, cannot protect a dissenter, and will spend a person on a program without registering that anything was lost.** Ethics review fails here not through malice but because the faculty that would notice a single subject's claim is the weakest one present. Martin's corrective is the design target: "individual values and personal self expression will balance the collective." Note the axis — the Sun it lacks is at home in D3, and D3 lacks the Saturn that this district rules; the two are each other's only real supply, and neither will ask.

**D10 — The Markets (Pisces).** Structurally strong at valuation and desire (Venus exalted — the highest valuing capacity in the table) and at abundance or dissolution depending on ruler (Jupiter / Neptune). **Uniquely, one faculty is doubly deficient: Mercury sits in both detriment and fall here** — record, measurement, and verification at the lowest state the system can express. **Characteristic institutional failure: it can price desire better than any district in the city and cannot keep a ledger.** This is not dishonesty chosen; verification simply has no institutional home, so contracts are personal, provenance is unrecoverable, and disputes resolve by reputation and story rather than record. Under the Neptune reading, add the specific hook: it dissolves the distinction between a gift, a loan, and a debt, which is exactly how people become bound to it. And note the axis — D12, its opposite, holds Mercury doubly *strong*. **The Undergrid and the Markets are structural opposites on the single faculty of verification**, maximum against minimum, which is the cleanest inter-district conflict the dignity system produces.

## 7. Source Gaps

- **Two derived cells.** No source in the corpus states Mercury's exaltation in Virgo or Mercury's fall in Pisces. Martin's own per-sign tables omit both (her Virgo block lists ruler/detriment/fall only; her Pisces block lists ruler/exaltation/detriment only). Both are standard traditional attributions and both are forced by the opposition rule, but they are **derived, not attested** — and they happen to be the two cells that generate the Undergrid/Markets verification axis in §6, so flag them if that reading ever gets challenged.
- **Four districts get only a two-term reading.** Gemini, Leo, Sagittarius, and Aquarius have no traditional exaltation *and* no traditional fall; Taurus has no fall; Scorpio has no exaltation. This is a genuine feature of the classical system, not an extraction failure — but it means D9, D3, D11, and D7 have a thinner capability model than the other eight, and their readings above lean correspondingly harder on ruler-versus-detriment and on the *absence* itself.
- **Modern exaltations are absent or unreliable.** The corpus asserts one — Silva, in passing, has Scorpio exalting Uranus — with no support anywhere else and no accompanying scheme. Not adopted. If modern exaltations are ever wanted for D3/D7/D9/D11, they will have to come from outside this corpus.
- **Degrees of exaltation are entirely absent.** The traditional system fixes exact degrees (Sun at 19° Aries, and so on). Not a single degree appears in the slice.
- **The rest of the classical dignity scheme is missing.** Triplicity rulers, terms/bounds, and faces are never mentioned; only decan sub-rulers appear (see `E_Decans.md`). "Peregrine" — a planet holding no dignity at all, which would be the natural label for most planets in most districts — is never named. If a finer-grained capability model is ever wanted, this is the direction, and it needs new sources.
- **Silva contains a flat error that contaminates its dignity statements.** It states that "a planet will be exalted when placed in its home sign (domicile), which means it will be strongest," conflating the two dignities outright. Every Silva dignity claim was therefore checked against Martin before use. Silva also gives **Pluto as a modern co-ruler of Aries** (a real minority position, but unsupported elsewhere in the corpus and not adopted here), and contains an obvious typo, "With Saturn in Aries, folks are intellectually gifted," in a passage plainly about Saturn in Aquarius.
- **Martin v1's Leo block prints "Detriment: Saturn" twice** with no exaltation or fall line — an OCR or typesetting duplication, not a second attribution.
- **Tierney, Snodgrass, and Emerson contributed nothing.** Their passages in this slice matched on the ordinary words "ruler" and "exalt" — Roman emperors, Athena Parthenos, Nebuchadnezzar — not on planetary dignity. Tierney's sole hit is a corrupted Aries fragment that never mentions a planet. This is a keyword-matching artifact of the slicing method, not a claim that those books are silent on the topic; if more rulership material is needed, they should be re-sliced on planet names rather than on "ruler."
