# Leo — Personality Substrate
**Maps to:** District 3 — Leo · cultural/performative
**Status:** Design substrate only — NOT in-fiction. See `00_Method_and_Sources.md`.
**Sources mined:** Silva, *Zodiac Signs: The Ultimate Guide* (heavy — behavioral depth, shadow); Tierney (heavy — element/modality mechanics, ego architecture); Snodgrass (heavy — myth, stars, correspondence, pathology); Martin, *Mapping the Psyche* v1 (heavy — Jungian depth, humiliation material), v2 (Leo–Aquarius axis, fifth house, Leo rising), v3 (light — fixed cross, cycle position, generational counter-current); Silva, *Sun and Moon Signs* (moderate); Emerson, *Behavioral Astrology* (moderate — decans, pairwise conflict).

## 1. Core Archetype & Driving Motive
Fixed fire under the Sun. Not "wants attention" — **needs to be witnessed being itself**, and cannot tell the difference between being unwitnessed and being wronged. Snodgrass's keyword pair is `I create / assurance`. Tierney's motto: *"The Show Must Go On — starring [me]."*
The engine is not vanity but a conviction of birthright: the Lion "feels it was born to shine brightly and enjoy elevated status. It behaves as if first-class treatment from the Cosmos is its birthright" (Tierney). Martin puts it as living at the center of one's own myth — "the special one, the unique one, the 'divine child' of the zodiac," for whom "the most important world is the drama in which he plays the leading role."
Critically, the motive is **not self-sufficient**. Tierney: Leo "cannot live and breathe only for itself. It must pour its vitality into others — preferably an audience of loyal fans." The district is structurally dependent on an audience it also holds in contempt. Cycle position (Rudhyar, via Martin v3): the **bud** — not the seed, not the flower; the swollen, pre-open, self-advertising stage. Key verb: *expressing*.

## 2. Light Expression (the district at its best)
- **Talent-spotting as reflex.** "Besides being proud and having a strong sense of self-worth, Leos find worth in others. If you have a virtue you are neglecting or a talent you aren't developing, a Leo will be sure to point it out and help you make the most of your qualities" (Silva, *Sun & Moon*). A district that finds people.
- **Morale infrastructure.** Tierney: a "wonderful morale booster." Silva: Leo may not organize the team but will motivate everyone in the room; "what they lack in administrative skills, Leos make up in their ability to inspire a crowd."
- **Event-manufacture.** "This sign tries to create events from the smallest moments in their life" — housewarming, welcome, farewell, all of it becomes an occasion. A district that ritualizes ordinary life.
- **Fixed-fire endurance.** Tierney: the most dependable of the fire signs; "Leo's flame is not easily extinguished once ignited." Emerson notes the Sun never goes retrograde — hence a reputation for consistency and loyalty.
- **Genuine generosity, and a working honesty code.** Keeps promises, pays debts, and will refuse a commitment up front rather than let you build false expectations.
- **Liberty as a value.** Martin: prefers "to be leaned on than to lean on." Silva on Leo as parent: "Since Leos are never used to being enclosed or on a shelf, they understand the importance of liberty."
- **Dignity under exposure.** Snodgrass's chosen exemplar of Leo *at his best* is Joseph Merrick — a man whose whole life was spectacle imposed on him, praised for maintaining "dignity and decorum, expressing himself courteously and generously to his many admirers." That inversion is the district's best self.

## 3. Shadow / Dark Side (failure modes, vices, pathologies)
- **The substitution.** Martin, the load-bearing failure mode: the Leo who does not actually believe in himself "will be unable to shine or radiate from his own heart and may become desperate for the approval of others and demand recognition and praise as a substitute for a lack of genuine self worth." Applause replaces the thing applause was supposed to confirm.
- **Humiliation is the mortal wound.** Martin: "The worst thing that can happen to a Leo is humiliation, which is an absolutely devastating experience." Repeated humiliation in childhood makes them shut their hearts. They are "vulnerable to being crushed, to having their innate sense of specialness destroyed." Silva's version: correct a Leo *in public* and you are permanently in their bad books; correct them in private and they will take it.
- **People as scenery.** Tierney, the sharpest line in the corpus: "It's not too clear if the Lion sees others as anything but extensions of itself, sort of like props on a stage. Leo may think, 'Everyone's supposed to make me look good, and those who do so best get my love the most.'"
- **Exile by omission.** Silva: someone who "looks, dresses, or performs better than them can be a serious threat, and Leos would rather keep them away than fear the absence of attention." The rival is not attacked; the rival is simply not booked. Nothing is on record.
- **Arrogance experienced from inside as confidence.** Silva: "Leos don't realize their pompousness and arrogance as they believe it to be self-confidence." Tierney: Leo indulges arrogance "often without realizing it (as Leo won't give itself bad reviews)."
- **Sycophancy corruption.** Snodgrass: "Among flatterers, the lion is thought to glow with self-importance and to shower sycophants with favors." Patronage runs the place, and patronage is measurably bought with praise — even though Leo believes itself immune, having a genuinely sharp nose for fake compliments.
- **Contempt for service, contempt for thrift.** Silva: Leo "would absolutely hate the idea of serving others," will snap at a rude customer at the cost of the job, and "will judge people who are unable to afford or give them expensive objects." A partner who can't afford gifts can lose their respect outright.
- **Refusal of the dirty work.** Tierney: after every party "there's always lots of clutter and trash left for somebody to clean up afterwards. The Lion is not particularly interested in doing what's required to restore such order," and dislikes "ordinary tasks that demand keeping a low profile."
- **Complacent belligerence.** Snodgrass: "In conflicts with an adversary, the invincible Leo is said to become complacent while noncombatants fight the wars. When pressed into military service, the traditional lion strides to the front in a show of might. At the moment of conquest, Leo typically shows no mercy." Also: "pomposity, attention seeking, and **victimization of the vulnerable**"; "indolence and insensitivity to weaklings or followers."
- **Fighting because the fight started.** Silva (*Sun & Moon*, Leo Moon): "you know that people are afraid of you, so you use this as an advantage"; Leo "will continue to fight only because 'they had already started fighting in the first place.'"
- **Hidden insecurity that leaks as noise.** Same source: when a Leo doesn't know something, the insecurity spikes and the display gets louder. "If no one ever says anything, you will immediately start thinking something is odd."
- **Overexposure burns bystanders.** Tierney: unchecked output "can consume too much energy from others, who eventually suffer exhaustion from overexposure to Leo energy — it's like getting a nasty sunburn."
- **Binary health.** Snodgrass: robust to the point of exercising "to the limits of endurance," but in illness "the lion moves to the other extreme into collapse." No middle gear.
- **Materialism as identity maintenance.** Chronic overspending; will empty savings for an item that reads as status. Even broke, "they will find a way to shop."

## 4. How It Reads From Outside (recognition markers)
Entrances are staged: "Whenever Leos enter a social gathering, they imagine a red carpet being rolled out for them." Martin: recognizable by carriage — walking "as if they had a crown on their heads," sometimes with something catlike and delicate in the gait. Snodgrass: preening, worldliness, "ostentatious dress and hairstyles"; sturdy frame, regal posture, imposing expression, silken hair, prominent eyes. Tierney's self-advice: *"Always wear something in bright red — that way they can't miss you."*
Architecturally the tell is refusal of context: Leo-designed buildings are "the loudest, most exquisite, or attention-grabbing on any street… less likely to blend within the context unless that is specifically suggested by the client" (Silva). Also legible: honorifics and titles insisted upon; guests expected to dress well but **not better than the host**; departure from any room where attention has dried up.

## 5. Core Need (what it is actually seeking)
Silva's own list: attention/spotlight, **respect** (must be genuine — fakeness is detected instantly and punished with withdrawal), passionate love, true friendship, luxury.
But the corpus's deeper answer is Martin's: the actual goal is "personal authenticity, integrity and authority which is **not dependent on the opinions of others**." Everything in section 3 is what the district does while it hasn't got that yet. Snodgrass adds the resilience mechanism: "In moments of despondency, the lion is said to quell doubt by renewing faith in self" — i.e., the district's only self-repair tool is more self-belief, which is also the thing that broke.

## 6. Behavior Under Conditions
- **At work.** Positions itself near whoever confers status; makes its own work conspicuous; sulks or throws tantrums when uncredited. Finds the smart route over the hard one — "will either trick their subordinates into completing a task or find a simpler solution." Charms the decision-maker and takes the promotion over a better performer. Refuses subordinate roles categorically; a job with a permanent supervisor above it is intolerable. Prone to laziness and procrastination out of boredom, yet still delivers on time.
- **Socially / in public.** Show-stealer, master connector, huge network and very few real intimates ("a pack of acquaintances, but few bosom pals" — Snodgrass). Hosts compulsively. Flirts with servers for free drinks and for the small hit of notice. **Leaves early if under-attended; stays all night if fed.**
- **Under stress.** Withdraws entirely. Silva: "when work and socializing become too extreme for Leos, they will often disappear and stay at home for a couple of days until they have their energy back." The district goes dark, then reopens louder. Even in retreat it is starting something new within a day.
- **In crisis.** Two documented modes, and they conflict. Tierney: on real failure "Leo is crushed and heartbroken (it may roar with hurt and anger when it doesn't win the top prize it seeks)." Snodgrass: strides to the front as display, then shows no mercy at the moment of victory. Martin's warning condition: if Leo stops believing in itself or "tries to take the easy way, then everything starts to go wrong" — collapse is total, not graduated.

## 7. Socialization (how newcomers are absorbed and raised into it)
Absorption is fast and shallow by design — "Leos just need to 'click' with others, after which they are your friend or at least a contact." The inner circle is very small and entered only by people who can hear the worst as well as the best. Best route in is **not** a private meeting (the calendar is full); it is catching attention inside a group and being invited inward from there.
Raising is by praise, and the corpus is specific about what praise must be aimed at: reward honesty and conduct, **not appearance** — "otherwise, they will grow up paying more attention to the way they look instead of the way they behave." Correction is delivered privately, always; public correction pierces pride and is not recovered from. Tasks must be framed as important, never menial — the young "need to feel important and worthy of the tasks," and a visible checked-off list supplies the accomplishment. Energy must be given a channel or it goes into tantrum. The characteristic pathology to inoculate against: the belief "that they have to be perfect to be loved and respected," which untreated leads to burning out "even if it means sacrificing food, sleep, and inner peace." Snodgrass adds the parental form: showing off the child's handiwork, report cards and trophies to guests, and summoning the family to perform — contentment while the child is small, conflict when the child becomes a rival.

## 8. Conflict Geometry
**Opposition — Aquarius (District 11).** Structural, not temperamental. Martin: this is the axis of "the relationship between the individual and the group, between what is right for me, as an individual, and what is right for everyone else," and it is a **fixed** axis "in which neither pole is prepared to adapt." The tension is currency-level: Leo's entire economy runs on recognition, and Aquarius categorically declines to spend that currency — it "hardly cares about anyone's opinions and doesn't feel the need to be validated" (Silva). Tierney: "no wonder this sign is as far away from Leo as a sign can get." Emerson's blunt version: Aquarius won't stop working on its inventions long enough to dote, "Leo can't handle that and will flee." Martin's mythic frame is the Fisher King: the Aquarius pole cut off from the Leo pole leaves the king wounded and the land frozen, and only the untutored heart (Percival) thaws it. Note the poisoned edge: the anti-privilege position is *also* an internal Leo position (see §13, Uranus generation).
**Square 1 — Taurus (District 2).** Tierney flags this explicitly: "It's Taurus that poses a problem for the Lion, since these signs square each other (they share the same mode, but involve less compatible elements)." Same fixity, incompatible tempo. Both want luxury, both want notice, both refuse to yield — "extremely difficult for both signs to yield, which could grow grudges" (Silva). The specific irritant is that Taurus's quiet, self-contained pride reads to Leo as a *rebuke* rather than a difference: Tierney notes that unassuming humility "is not what Leo thinks will help it make its big splash." Emerson: "Leo is commanding and opinionated and Taurus accommodates" — until accommodation stops, which it does, permanently.
**Square 2 — Scorpio (District 8).** Same fixity again, and the deadliest asymmetry in the corpus. Tierney: "Leo may indulge in its dramatic, royal pout when its pride has been wounded, but **Scorpio aims for the jugular**, at least on those psychological levels where it knows it can do more permanent damage." Leo's weapon is display; Scorpio's is durable injury — Leo cannot win a fight whose scoreboard is invisible. The clash object is different too: Scorpio does not contest the spotlight, it contests **power**. Silva: "there is a balance between these signs until one gets too hungry for power," after which neither will concede, "which is nearly impossible" to resolve. Emerson calls it flatly non-viable long-term.
**Adjacent friction worth carrying (semi-sextiles, not squares).** *Virgo (District 6)* is where the corpus is unexpectedly rich: Tierney frames Virgo as the sign that cleans up after Leo's party and whose entire program "seems to be an adverse reaction to Leo's self-promotional excesses," refining or redefining how the Lion expresses ego. Virgo also holds the accusation that "publicity-crazy Leo sells parts of its soul to the world in return for temporary notoriety." Emerson: "Leo needs their ego stroked regularly, and Virgo isn't much of a stroker." Sources disagree — Silva reads the pair as unusually mature and balanced, with Virgo happily grooming the star it does not want to be. *Cancer (District 4)* finds Leo's need for validation "insincere, shallow, and inauthentic," and Leo finds Cancer's brooding unbearable against its own sunny default.

## 9. Affinity Geometry (trines/sextiles — and the price of each)
- **Aries (trine).** Tierney: "Aries feeds the fires of self-interest in Leo. The Ram's way of looking at life appeals to Leo." Price: Aries wants *power*, Leo wants *center* — and each is jealous of the other's social life; Aries's brutal honesty is exactly the register Leo cannot take.
- **Sagittarius (trine).** The corpus's strongest pairing. Both perform an acceptable self for the world, and with each other "they can easily let the guard down and show their true selves" — bonding equally over adventure and over a warm cup of tea, comfortable in silence. Price: Sagittarius periodically needs to be *absent*, which Leo reads as withdrawal of regard.
- **Libra (sextile).** Mutual admiration and complementary function: Leo goes to Libra for taste ("book, music, and movie recommendations, given their impeccable taste in art"), Libra borrows Leo's glamour and, per Emerson, "will stroke Leo's ego until the cows come home." Price: Libra is mortified when Leo "snaps at waiters or growls at bartenders"; Leo is enraged by Libra's indecision and simply buys both.
- **Gemini (sextile).** Tierney: Gemini "stimulates Leo to be creatively versatile while it encourages playfulness." Silva's version is better: Leo wants its inner child out and cannot get it out alone; Gemini can. Price: Leo is too high-maintenance to clean up the mess, and Gemini's short attention span grates.

## 10. Structural Position
- **Element: Fire** — classical four-element temperament grouping, out-of-fiction only; **no relationship whatsoever to the in-fiction 8-element robot elemental system assigned to subnets.** Behaviorally (Tierney): treats its own needs as urgent by default, and does not require anyone's approval before acting. Snodgrass: "combustive… radiant with magnanimity, intuition, and self-confidence."
- **Modality: Fixed** — motivation is "to resist and stabilise" (Martin). Doesn't get pushed; digs in; enormous stamina and loyalty; "one-track-minded and unwilling to change in midstream, even when it's plainly advantageous to do so." Tierney's image is the operative one: fixed fire is not a wildfire but **the steady glow of logs in a fireplace** — contained flame, which "can burn more intensely and deeply." Seasonally, fixed is mid-season at full extremity, where "changes occur less often… the days can seem almost boringly similar to one another." Caveat Tierney flags: unlike its fixed siblings, "Leo can sometimes have a problem with waiting to be gratified."
- **Polarity: Positive / yang / active** — outward-directed, expressive, initiating contact rather than receiving it.
- **Ruler: the Sun. Detriment: Saturn.** Sun = ego, willpower, need for recognition, integrity, self-dignity. Tierney's institutional reading: the Sun is "the 'big cheese' of the solar system, with all the planets — like loyal subjects — revolving around this central power source." Useful distinction he draws: pure solar types "are not necessarily gleeful, and they view themselves more as authority figures in commanding positions," whereas Leo *plays* and clowns to steal the scene. Saturn in detriment means restraint, limit, and structure sit badly here — Saturn-in-Leo people "must go the extra mile when trying to find balance," and over-correct into self-criticism or inhibition.
- **House: Fifth** — recreation, play, creative self-expression, romance, children, gambling, pleasure. Not the house of *work* produced; the house of what makes you glad to be alive.
- **Fixed cross:** Leo–Taurus–Scorpio–Aquarius; in Christian correspondence the four directions and four evangelists, with Leo as **Mark, the lion of God**.

## 11. Decans (candidate sub-neighborhood texture — Emerson, single-source)
1. **Sun-ruled (Jul 23 – Aug 1).** Self-reliant, warm, sees itself as number one, "usually satisfied in life, but sometimes you can become totally dependent on others' approval." The pure case — and the most fragile.
2. **Jupiter-ruled (Aug 2 – 12).** "Conservative and traditional when it comes to principles, beliefs, and values," with "great regard for spirituality and education." Lucky. Gambler or at minimum a risk-taker. The old-money / conservatory / high-church quarter.
3. **Mars-ruled (Aug 13 – 23).** "The most chivalrous and ambitious of all decans," with "the unique ability to drive people crazy with your absolute refusal to admit when you're wrong." Self-righteous, and it costs them their circle. The militant, duel-prone quarter.
**Edge zones (Silva, cusps).** *Cancer-side border:* introverts converting into extroverts, insecure inside and masking it with a playful spirit, "emotionally blocked at times," swinging top-of-the-world to depressed overnight. *Virgo-side border:* perfectionists with creative flair who "will readily serve you, but with a flair of pride, almost with a portrayal of obligation" — the directors, stage managers and technicians rather than the stars.

## 12. Correspondences
- **Colors:** orange and yellow-gold (Snodgrass); bright red as the deliberate visibility signal (Tierney); warm palette generally, and where the dress is dark the accent must carry the heat.
- **Metal:** **gold**, and specifically "incorruptible, eternal fire" (Martin). Alchemically the lion is "the primitive, untransmuted form of the alchemical gold" — the raw feedstock the work refines into the *filius philosophorum*; Lambsprinck's *De lapide philosophica* (1625) shows lion and lioness as the two sides of that primal substance.
- **Shapes:** the constellation is "a sickle-shaped cluster with a triangular tail," also called the Scythe, the Diamond of Virgo, and the Spring Triangle — "one of the most clearly defined shapes in the heavens." **Sickle + triangle** is the usable geometry. Heraldically: crowned lion, forepaws grasping sword, orb, fleur-de-lis, or scepter; gold field, lion often bold red; hybrid variants have leafy mane and tail, barbed claws, knotted tail, finned or scaled body, elongated tongue.
- **Named lights (all Snodgrass, all excellent for institution names):** **Regulus** — bluish-white, brightest and hottest, marks the **heart**, "augments its natural glow with light from two companion stars," one of the four Persian Royal Stars, Hindu *Mahga*, "the mighty." **Denebola** — blue, at the tail, in Hebrew tradition ominous: "the judge or the lord who cometh." **Adhafera / Alserpha — "the funeral pyre,"** a double star. **Algenubi** — yellow, at the mouth. **Zosma** — the back, "a blend of violet, blue, and yellow." **Leo Minor**, the lion cub (Hevelius, 1600s), principal star Praecipua. **Leonid meteors** peak every **33 years** — spectacular in 1799, 1833, 1866, 1966.
- **Topography:** mountains (compare: Libra to palaces and estates, Cancer to rivers and parks).
- **Body:** back, heart, and liver (modern scheme); shoulders in Manilius's older Roman scheme; left eyebrow in the facial division. Jung specifically mapped "susceptible hearts in people born in Leo."
- **Pathology:** impaired circulation and heart disease as the catastrophic end-state, especially in old age; plus leg cramps, high blood pressure, seizures, back pain, palpitations, lung infection. Prescribed: magnesium phosphate; bran, oats, peas; salads of lettuce, sorrel, dill, fennel; citrus, cocoa, mint. Emerson adds cooling lotions and iced hyssop tea. Silva's dietary angle is sun-dried and sun-ripened food — tomatoes, grapes, apricots, chilies, hot peppers, dates. Snodgrass's one-line eating note: **"needs pampering."** Dark, dank rooms and oppressive climates are described as anathema; recovery happens in sunlight.

## 13. Myth, History & Cultural Associations
- **The Nemean lion.** Hercules's first labor; he "strangled the beast and skinned it in one mighty pull on its pelt," then wore the hide as a symbol of courage and invincibility. Snodgrass notes the sky arrangement: "Hercules resides far from Leo. The lion appears to steer clear of his old adversary." A defeated monster whose skin became the hero's costume, and a permanent avoidance between them.
- **Knem and the flood.** Egyptians worshipped the constellation because it coincided with the Nile inundation — destructive, and the source of the year's fertility. "For this benefit, Egyptians were willing to welcome the season, despite its liabilities."
- **Sekhmet and Re.** The lion-headed goddess is the scirocco, "the hot, murderous desert wind that quickly depletes the body of moisture. Armed with fire-tipped arrows, she shot her victims through the heart." Her male equivalent is Re, the sun god, "giver of strength… and antidote to the death-dealing goddess." One heat, two faces.
- **The sphinx.** The Esneh zodiac ran Virgo → Leo; "the union of maiden with lion produced the sphinx, a term meaning 'closely united.'"
- **Thrones and gates.** Tutankhamen's coronation throne carries lion-head medallions at the seat/leg juncture, all four legs ending in clawed feet, "suggesting firm contact with the earth and suitable preparations for self-defense or attack." The Lion Gate at Mycenae pairs lions on the lintel. Counter-image: after a Hittite alliance of 1300 B.C., sculptors carved "suppliant lions stretched out submissively beneath the feet of the winged gods" — the lion as *subjugated* emblem.
- **How you tame it (medieval bestiary).** "He is quieted by hearing a sweet musical voice; when he bathes himself he becomes so gentle that a child might sit on him and lead him everywhere." Also the medieval paradox of lion and maiden: "the civilizing force of purity over brutality."
- **Aesop, and the district's real honor code.** In "The Lion, the Mouse, and the Fox," Leo is outraged that the mouse ran over his mane and ears and disturbed his nap; the moral is **"Little liberties are great offenses."** In "The Lion and the Three Bulls," he beats three attackers by separating them and taking them one at a time. In "The Lion and the Boar," the two combatants pause to breathe, notice the vultures already gathering for the loser, and agree it is better to patch up the quarrel than to make dinner for vultures. In "The Lioness," she answers rivals boasting of litter size with three sons, "but each was a thoroughbred."
- **Religion and taste.** "The lion is said to prefer splendor and a 'Te Deum' or praise anthem over quiet faith and concealed stewardship," predisposing Leo to "the arts, haute couture, high-church religion and evangelism, and theater." Taoism inverts all of it: **in Taoism, the lion represents emptiness.**
- **The internal counter-current.** Martin's generational reading of Uranus in Leo (1955–62): a cohort that "doesn't believe in privilege or entitlement," attacks inherited elitism and undeserved status, and whose task is "to forge a new recognition that every single person is special and unique in some way" — punk as the visible form. Its own trap: on principle, its members will decline promotion over others and thereby erase themselves.
- **One dark artist datum.** Rossetti buried his poems with his wife and, in 1870, had her body exhumed to retrieve and publish them — recorded by Martin under a solar eclipse in Leo.

## 14. Design Hooks for District Use
1. **The 33-year cycle.** Build the district's defining showcase on the Leonid period rather than an annual calendar. A generational event most residents see once or twice. Entire careers are timed to be at their peak when it comes, and there is a permanent underclass of people who peaked in the wrong decade.
2. **Regulus, Adhafera, Denebola as venue architecture.** The great house is the *heart*, and it is deliberately flanked by two lesser satellite houses whose function is to make it look brighter. The career-ending house is the "funeral pyre" (a double venue — you burn in one and are watched from the other). The critic's box sits at the tail: "the judge, the lord who cometh."
3. **The hide economy.** Status objects are things taken off defeated predecessors — the retired star's coat worn by her successor, recycled regalia, borrowed billing. And Hercules's other lesson: the district keeps a standing, permanent distance from whichever party once humbled it, and no one will say why.
4. **"Little liberties are great offenses."** Codify it. Public correction is a duel; private correction is protocol and is accepted gracefully. Outsiders read the district's reactions as wildly disproportionate; inside they are exactly proportionate. This one rule generates most of the district's violence.
5. **Sunburn tourism.** A place people adore for three days and cannot survive for three weeks. Design the consequence: a short-stay economy, a seasonal labor class that burns out and rotates, and a body of people who service the district but cannot live in it.
6. **The cleanup deficit as politics.** The district structurally cannot clean up after itself. Whoever does — an adjacent district, a night crew, an under-caste of stagehands and haulers — holds real leverage and a real grievance. This is a concrete, non-malicious source of inter-district hostility.
7. **Blacklisting by omission.** The signature local injustice leaves no evidence: the too-talented newcomer is never denounced, only never booked. Any investigation into it must run on absence rather than record.
8. **Patronage that believes it can't be bought.** Patrons genuinely have sharp fakeness detectors and genuinely shower favors on flatterers. Both are true at once. The comedy and the tragedy of the district live in that gap.
9. **De-escalation by music and washing.** From the bestiary: song quiets the lion, and a bathed lion can be led by a child. Give the district a real, working, non-violent riot protocol built on those two things — and make its failure cases specific.
10. **Robust or collapsed, nothing between.** Apply the pathology at district scale: institutions here run at full endurance until they don't, then fail totally and at once. No graceful degradation, ever.

## 15. Source Gaps
- **No gemstone or mineral correspondence survives in this slice.** Snodgrass's correspondence table is truncated after the color column (orange, yellow-gold), and Emerson's per-sign Crystal / Essential oil / Chakra / Tarot block for Leo is absent — the two visible blocks belong to Cancer and Aquarius. Only the **metal (gold)** is attested. The traditional ruby/peridot/sardonyx associations are *not* in this corpus; do not import them as if mined.
- **No flower or plant correspondence.** Only dietary and medicinal plants survive (bran, oats, peas; lettuce, sorrel, dill, fennel; citrus, cocoa, mint; hyssop; sun-dried produce). No sunflower, marigold, or celandine here.
- **No tarot card for Leo** in the slice.
- **Corpus defect.** Silva's *Ultimate Guide* Leo chapter opens with a misfiled header block — lucky numbers, "Ruling planet: Jupiter," and a bullet list describing Pisceans. That is Pisces material printed under a Leo heading; discard it. Leo's ruler is the Sun, per four independent sources.
- **Tierney is OCR-damaged** throughout (word-joins, dropped spaces, scrambled column order); his "Famous Leo Types" list is unreadable.
- **Two live contradictions, left unresolved on purpose.** (a) *Courage:* Snodgrass says "boldness in Leo is an illusion: the lion roars at danger in a vain show of courage," while Silva and Emerson call it the bravest sign outright. (b) *Grudges:* Silva says Leos forgive easily and don't hold grudges — then, elsewhere in the same book, that they forgive but never forget and will re-raise it; Emerson says the fixed modality means they "hold tightly to resentments, shunning anyone who has been disrespectful." Both readings are usable; pick per sub-neighborhood rather than flattening.
- **Everything is written at individual scale.** No source addresses Leo as a governing body, an economy, or a civic institution. All district-level institutional design here is extrapolation and should be labeled as such downstream.
- **Thin on non-rival peers.** Nearly all peer-to-peer material is framed as competition. The corpus barely describes how this temperament relates to an equal it does *not* perceive as a threat.
- **Silent on poverty.** Every luxury and generosity claim assumes means. The corpus never describes this temperament without money — the nearest datum is that even when broke it will find a way to buy.
- **Decans are single-source (Emerson) and thin**, roughly three sentences each, with no corroboration. Treat as suggestive only.
