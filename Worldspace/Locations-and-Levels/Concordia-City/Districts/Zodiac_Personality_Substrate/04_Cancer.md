# Cancer — Personality Substrate
**Maps to:** District 1 — Cancer (The Sanctuary) · nurturing/protective
**Status:** Design substrate only — NOT in-fiction. See `00_Method_and_Sources.md`.
**Sources mined:** Silva, *Zodiac Signs: Ultimate Guide* (heaviest — behavior, shadow, social mechanics); Tierney (heaviest for shadow and structural logic; OCR-degraded); Snodgrass (myth, etymology, astronomy, correspondences); Martin *Mapping the Psyche* v1 (sign portrait, dignities) and v2 (polarity axis); Martin v3 (thin — mostly cycle theory and one case study); Silva, *Sun and Moon Signs* (moderate); Emerson, *Behavioral Astrology* (decans, body, compatibility grid).

## 1. Core Archetype & Driving Motive
The Sanctuary is not "the kind district." It is the district organized around **shelter as an obligation it assigns itself**. Tierney: it is the first structure in the sequence "to poignantly feel both love and sadness for the human condition," and it "senses that something potent inside itself moves it to want to protect all in life that is vulnerable and in need of shelter." Critically, **it does not wait to be asked**: "It feels compelled to mother people — once it picks up on their distress signals — and thus intrudes on their emotional space." The motive is not altruism. It is the conversion of other people's need into one's own security: if you are needed, you cannot be left.
Martin's dignity set is the whole design in four words: **Moon rules, Jupiter exalted, Saturn in detriment, Mars in fall.** Care governs; belonging expands past the clan into ancestry and history; *limits and authority function badly here*; *self-assertion functions badly here*. Aggression exists only as proxy — on behalf of a dependent, never oneself.

## 2. Light Expression (the district at its best)
- **Absorptive perception.** "Always alert to the unspoken feelings of others" (Tierney); reads posture and face before speech. Nothing goes unnoticed here.
- **Loyalty that fights.** Snodgrass: "combats the foes of a friend as though they were personal enemies." Martin on Mars in Cancer: "the kind of fierce aggression with which a mother will protect her young… there is nothing weak about it at all."
- **Mediation and orchestration.** Silva: excellent at *choosing between* presented options, at brokering win-win compromise, at hosting. The district is a superb convener.
- **Indirect competence.** Martin: "like a crab, tends to approach its goals subtly and invisibly and, when nobody else is looking, get what it wants to the surprise of all." It is "prepared to wait for the tide, or until everybody else has lost interest."
- **Institutional memory.** "A memory like a steel trap." Records, albums, custodianship, continuity, the honoring of ancestors.
- **Sustained tenure.** Silva on work: it stays past the point of readiness "feeling that they owe their employer something."

## 3. Shadow / Dark Side (failure modes, vices, pathologies)
This is the load-bearing section. The Sanctuary's vices are all *deformations of protection*, which is what makes them hard to name from inside.
- **The smother-lock.** Tierney, the central mechanism: "The mothering side of Cancer also unconsciously refuses to let anyone it loves grow up and exercise independence. Should they no longer need to be parented, loved ones might one day say goodbye and walk away, leaving Cancer all alone." A ward who recovers is a ward who leaves. **The district is structurally incentivized to keep its dependents dependent** — and cannot see this, because "it's not objective enough to realize how oppressive this can be to others."
- **Help as trespass.** Aid arrives unrequested and does not stop on request. Outside help, correspondingly, reads as accusation — an audit of its adequacy.
- **Feeding as coercion.** Martin's exact phrase for its emotional blackmail: **"if you don't eat my food it means you don't love me."** Hospitality that cannot be declined without political cost.
- **Passive aggression as the default grammar.** Silva: months of nothing, then a sink of dishes triggers it; "snarky post-it notes," "underhanded text messages," things said months ago. Emerson: "a harsh pinch of passive aggression," "hardly forthcoming with what might be bothering them."
- **Pressure-cooker rage.** Tierney: it is *terrified* of its own anger — it "anticipates isolation as its punishment for venting" — so it builds and builds and then "blows like Mount Etna," with "totally blind, irrational rage that can be destructive in its full release." Afterward it wants to pretend it was a bad dream. **There is no escalation ladder.** Riots here have no visible run-up.
- **Victim posture.** "Suddenly, everyone else is at fault and Cancer is the poor victim… They willfully insist on creating much overblown, psychological drama for both themselves and whomever else they latch on to in desperation. No other sign can drown in its own sorrow as intently as Cancer."
- **Curated nostalgia.** "Cancerian nostalgia chooses to dwell only on the good stuff that happened" — selective memory as official history, with the painful record kept but sealed.
- **Grudge accounting.** Snodgrass: when thwarted it will "vegetate and brood over a secret agenda of hurts and betrayals." It forgives aloud and never forgets; a scar stays an open wound.
- **Safe-relationship substitution.** A once-wounded Sanctuary "only traffics in safe relationships that cannot cause much emotional damage… not intimately fulfilling, but they do allow Cancer to give deeply of itself" — Tierney's example is volunteer work. Charity replaces intimacy.
- **Cold shutdown.** Under repeated abuse it "can seem coldly remote… as it is symbolized by a cold-blooded creature, it can expertly turn off its feelings."
- **Manipulation as last resort.** Silva is careful: it is not habitual, but it appears "when they feel extremely vulnerable."
- **Insulation as pathology.** Tierney literalizes it: the guarded Crab "can physically gain a lot of insulating weight — a buffer zone of fat and retained water." Martin's client: emotional starvation expressed as obesity. Read this at district scale as hoarding, walling, over-stocking.

## 4. How It Reads From Outside (recognition markers)
- **Walking conundrum.** Silva's word. It seems introverted, yet it hosts. Snodgrass's trait string: *sensitive, patriotic, inspired, elusive, home-loving, emotional, instinctive, bashful, artistic, intuitive, secretive, complex, moody* — and, damningly, "excellent parents, but intolerant or inaccessible friends."
- **You cannot approach it head-on.** Aristophanes, 421 B.C., quoted in Snodgrass: **"You cannot teach a crab to walk straight."** Snodgrass adds that in antiquity the crab's sideways motion "suggested duplicity," and that Cancer "produces the most nebulous of archetypes."
- **The one big claw.** Tierney: real crabs "have that one huge claw they love to thrust out at whoever approaches uninvited — as if to menacingly say, 'Ya see this?'" A display, not a strike. The crustiness is theater over a soft interior.
- **It never says what's wrong, but it is always saying it.** Silva: "Cancers are always communicating their needs and desires; it's on the other person in their life to pick up on that. This seems extremely unfair, and in many relationships or friendships, it is."
- **Stillness reads as rejection.** "You can drive Cancer crazy by remaining stone-faced and still; this sign thrives on active human response. Laugh, cry, get angry — but show something!" A neutral outsider is read as hostile.
- **It sniffs out performance instantly.** Any visitor "just trying to play it cool" is clocked.

## 5. Core Need (what it is actually seeking)
Not to be loved — **to be needed**, which it treats as the only non-revocable form of love. Emerson's south-node phrasing: "you feel best when you are needed. Many times, you might feel unsafe in public." Beneath that sits the fourth-house word Martin uses as the house's actual title: **BELONGING** — and beneath *that*, the twin core fears the polarity axis names: **engulfment and abandonment**. Add one small, sharp thing from Emerson (Ceres in Cancer): its standing challenge is "to lower your expectations for praise when you do a good deed." The Sanctuary needs its care *acknowledged*, and reads unacknowledged care as betrayal.

## 6. Behavior Under Conditions
- **At work.** Commitment over mobility; wants autonomy but hates working alone; needs colleagues who are competent *and differently minded*. Its signature failure is **decision paralysis at the option-generation stage** — Silva: it "makes the right decision when it comes down to a few choices, but when they have to go out and find all the options, research them, and make the decision, things go wrong." Whoever writes the shortlist governs. When jaded it becomes the chronic complainer "with little insight into how to solve these problems." Emerson notes one genuine strength: it does not compartmentalize home and work, so it burns out *less* than expected.
- **Socially / in public.** Small, repeated, on its own ground. Consistency over novelty — standing weekly gatherings, not festivals. At someone else's event it is "sitting outside by the fire pit" with two friends and leaves early. It mixes friend groups others keep separate, so it appears to know everyone. It declines invitations and expects to keep receiving them.
- **Under stress.** Withdraws, goes quiet, molts. Silva's crab-biology gloss: molting crabs "create or dig a cave and hide away; others will engage in ingenious scams that make them appear dead and unsavory to predators." Displacement into labor is the tell — Tierney: when angry it will "plunge vigorously into heavy-duty housework." Silva's finest behavioral marker, from a counseling case: **when she is knitting, leave her alone; when she is doing the chores she hates, that is the time to worry.**
- **In crisis.** Cardinal water is not a mist. Tierney contrasts it with the other two water signs as "the heavy drenching common to the rushing waters of cardinal Cancer" — it surges outward, fast, and makes immediate impact on its environment. It will fight to the death for a dependent. It cannot hold the front for long: it is "the most demonstrative of the water signs." Then either the Etna blowout or the cold shutdown.

## 7. Socialization (how newcomers are absorbed)
- **Entry is sideways, never frontal.** Silva's "Sideways Shuffle": people arrive via a brother's girlfriend's friend, or through a mutual acquaintance neither party likes. Careers arrive the same way — by adjacency and convenience, not application. A direct petition to belong is the one route that does not work.
- **The unspoken standing invitation is the loyalty test.** Silva: the regulars know to show up every week invited or not, and the host "might occasionally take the liberty of not sending invites to see who shows up anyway." Asking whether you are invited proves you are not yet in.
- **You do not become a friend; you become family.** "You can't just be a male Cancer's friend; you end up being part of the family for as long as you're willing to stick around." There is no intermediate tier.
- **Labels are permanent.** Silva on Cancerian children: "more likely to fit into assigned labels. If you tell a Cancer child that they're 'bad,' they'll feel that way. If you tell them that they're a 'bully' or 'stubborn,' they will [lean] toward that behavior even though it hurts." Classification here is destiny.
- **Authority is applied from a distance.** Discipline works only if it does not turn into contact; the young learn to convert a scolding into a hug. Privacy demands spike early (roughly ages 5–9) and again in adolescence, where searching a room reads as outright betrayal and "How was your day?" trips alarms.
- **Absent deliberate pushing outward, it stalls at one or two bonds and stops.** Someone must force the horizon open, or the cohort ossifies.

## 8. Conflict Geometry
**Opposition — Capricorn (District: the achievement/authority pole).** Structural, not temperamental. Martin: this is "the parent-child axis, and of course there is no child without a parent and there is no parent without a child" — Moon vs. Saturn, fourth house vs. tenth, the need to be held vs. the need to stand alone. It names "the twin core fears of engulfment and abandonment." **The tension is structural because each pole supplies what the other refuses to develop**, and so each has an interest in the other never developing it. Martin's polarization law is the usable part: *"When this axis polarises, the Cancer end becomes increasingly needy and the Capricorn end becomes increasingly withholding and withdrawn."* It is a runaway loop, not a standoff — every increment of Sanctuary demand buys an increment of Capricorn coldness, which buys more demand. The axis's whole subject matter is **"what is enough and what is not enough."** Tierney adds the sting: Saturn is in *detriment* in Cancer, so the Sanctuary genuinely cannot generate the limit-setting its opposite specializes in; it must import it or collapse.

**Squares — Aries and Libra.** Tierney gives the shared cause: Cancer "has more emotion invested in creating and sustaining a calm and peaceful home, one where all relationships reflect agreements well, which is one reason why it receives a frictional square… from both combative Aries and potentially argumentative Libra."
- **Aries — contempt for displayed weakness.** Tierney: Aries "has trouble respecting ol' chicken-hearted Cancer… The Crab can sometimes be so spineless," and doesn't "have much respect for any sign that tries to use its weaknesses and insecurities to solicit sympathy or pity." The Sanctuary's core political instrument — visible, testified need — is precisely what Aries reads as fraud. Emerson: Cancer finds Aries self-centered and is left feeling insecure; Aries is worn down by the insecurity. Silva calls Aries "the least compatible with any water sign, but especially with Cancer."
- **Libra — two incompatible definitions of fairness, and mutual concealment.** Both cultures believe themselves the fair one. Cancer's fairness is loyalty-weighted and *felt* — Silva: it brokers "win-win scenarios with little effort," and "when it's evident that one person is outright unruly or unfair, then they step in with their claws." Libra's is procedural and balanced. Silva flags the deeper problem: "These two seem like they'd be perfect as they're both kind-hearted and concerned with fairness, but they don't mix well. Cancers are too good at sniffing out fakeness, and Libras aren't happy to handle an emotional rollercoaster." Emerson adds the deadlock: **both sides shut down and hide what they actually feel**, so nothing ever reaches the table. This is the quieter and nastier of the two squares — no open fight, just decades of unresolved courtesy.

**Quincunx (five apart, no shared logic) — Sagittarius and Aquarius.** The corpus is unusually consistent that these are the true worst matches. Aquarius: "possibly the worst friend combination," "apt to under-communicate, and they care much more about logic and reason than feelings and fairness"; Emerson, "Aquarius isn't able to keep Cancer reassured." Sagittarius: over-communicates, "too self-interested to give a Cancer proper attention when they need it," and "can't promise to always provide material and emotional support."

## 9. Affinity Geometry (trines / sextiles)
- **Trine — Scorpio.** Magnetism plus survivable violence: Silva, "even when epic fights happen, it certainly doesn't mean the end… the emotional eruptions are just part of these two being together." The alliance holds on one condition (Emerson): Cancer must concede control. Scorpio supplies the intensity and the willingness to name what Cancer will not.
- **Trine — Pisces.** "A bond so deep it almost feels psychic." Also the most dangerous ally, because both dissolve into each other; Silva's own warning is "watch out for mothering tendencies," and Emerson's is that neither will carry the financial burden. Two Sanctuaries make a flood, not a home.
- **Sextile — Taurus.** The cleanest partnership in the set: Taurus grounds and funds, Cancer supplies emotional infrastructure. "Great friends all around."
- **Sextile — Virgo.** Valuable for the rarest reason in the corpus: **Virgo nurses the nurse.** Silva, *Sun and Moon*: "Virgo's own nurturing and attentive traits will sometimes provide Cancer with some respite, which is a welcome change for Cancers since they do all the nurturing with most other signs." Friction point: Virgo is privately reserved and will fight over emotional demand.
- **Opposite-as-ally.** Both Silvas note that Capricorn *pairs* superbly once responsibilities are divided — opposition here is a functioning marriage or a runaway loop, never neutral.

## 10. Structural Position
- **Element — Water** (out-of-fiction temperament grouping only; unrelated to the in-fiction robot elemental system). Behaviorally: absorption without filters. Tierney: "Cancer readily sponges in the environment, and often without using protective filters. It's typically only after the Crab has taken in too much 'negative' emotional discharge from others that it learns to build a hard shell." **The shell is scar tissue, not skin** — the district's hardness is a dated historical record, not a temperament.
- **Modality — Cardinal.** Initiating, fast, goal-directed, "gotta get moving." Tierney flags Cancer as the *misfit* cardinal sign — shy-seeming yet assertive — and insists the timidity is self-image, not fact: "the Crab sees itself more as being timid and shy — though this is not so." Behaviorally: **the Sanctuary acts. It annexes, expands its protectorate, and moves first**, while sincerely describing itself as merely defending. Martin's cycle placement is "putting down roots," and the keyword for the phase is *deciding*.
- **Polarity — Negative / yin / receptive.** Internalizing, magnetic rather than projective; power exercised by drawing in rather than reaching out.
- **Ruler — the Moon.** Phase-driven, so behavior is cyclical rather than stable; reflective rather than generative (Silva: the Moon "represents reaping the benefits of work already done"). Tierney's distinction is useful: "Cardinality drives Cancer to reach out assertively for a support system, while the Moon is more likely to go within to create a secure, inner home base" — the district does both at once and looks incoherent doing it. Moon-power is defined as "willingness to protect life by making things feel safe."
- **Exalted — Jupiter.** Martin: belonging "reaches beyond the personal clan to greater connections to community, society, country, to ancestors and back into history." The mandate to grow the family. Also mentorship and open-handed generosity.
- **Detriment — Saturn.** Boundaries, refusal, and formal authority all misfire here. The positive integration Martin names is exactly what the district lacks: the strength "to stand on their own feet."
- **Fall — Mars.** **Nobody in the Sanctuary fights for themselves.** Aggression only routes through a dependent. Design consequence: a resident with no ward has no standing to make a claim.

## 11. Decans (candidate sub-neighborhood texture)
Emerson is the only decan source in the corpus.
1. **First decan (Moon-ruled, 0–10°).** "Very authentic character… emotionally charged. Your heart, with its limitless energy and compassion, is in everything you do. You demand intense loyalty from your lovers and your friends, and have a tendency to be very jealous." → The old core. Openly warm, openly possessive; loyalty audited constantly.
2. **Second decan (Pluto-ruled, 10–20°, Scorpio-tinged).** "You may seem unapproachable and reserved to the point of seeming emotionless, when you are actually the exact opposite of that." Possessive but very giving; "would make a great therapist, psychologist, or detective." → The investigative/clinical quarter. Cold-faced, hyper-perceptive; where the district keeps its files and its interrogators.
3. **Third decan (Neptune-ruled, 20–30°, Pisces-tinged).** "Probably the most sensitive of all in the Zodiac… prone to helping people with tireless energy. Born nurses, though sometimes moody… be careful that you don't get taken advantage of. You might find yourself meditating in a monastery some day." → The hospice/almshouse edge. Self-sacrificial to the point of exploitability; the district's saints and its easiest marks.

## 12. Correspondences
- **Colors:** violet, ice blue (Snodgrass's table — the *only* correspondence column that survived the slice).
- **Shape:** the sign glyph is "circular, internal and protective" (Martin), read as breasts/ovaries and the closeness of mother and child. Snodgrass's iconographic variants: the crab; **the she-crab grasping a ball of fertilized eggs in a protective claw**; the Egyptian scarab; and "an arm uplifting a sword" — the last two both glossed as *emblems of struggle*. The medieval *Book of Hours* renders it as "a chubby circlet topped by three bright stars… eight legs tipped with smaller stars."
- **Topography:** rivers, trees, and parks (Snodgrass's matchup list). Notably *not* the sea — riverine and cultivated-green, not oceanic.
- **Body / pathology:** upper torso, stomach, breast, womb (Snodgrass, Emerson); the **forehead** in facial correspondence. Martin's functional read is the useful one: the breasts and stomach are "the functions of giving out nourishment to others and taking in nourishment for oneself," and health holds "for so long as the ebb and flow are kept in some kind of balance." Pathologies attested: digestive complaint, water retention and insulating weight, seasonal low mood (Silva names winter-onset dips explicitly).
- **Sky texture, usable as local color:** alpha star **Acubens, "the claw,"** a white-and-red doublet in the southern claw; **Praesepe**, the cluster whose obscurity "has been a reliable rain predicter because water vapor dims it" — a sign that reads the weather by how badly it can see; zeta Cancri, a revolving quintet; the Chinese regrouping of two of its stars as **Kwei, the spectre**; the Hindu name for its delta star, **Pushya, "the flower."**
- **Metal — SILVER. Stones — PEARL, OPAL, MOONSTONE.** *(Resolved 2026-08-29 via `G_Correspondences.md`; supersedes the earlier "absent" finding.)* Snodgrass's Appendix IV is keyed by **planet**, not sign. Cancer is ruled by the Moon, and the Moon row supplies silver, pearl, opal, moonstone, and four herbs. For an enclosed Antarctic city this set is unusually well-chosen: **opal cannot be synthesised**, so it holds value where manufactured stones do not — a district whose signature stone is one the city cannot make for itself.

## 13. Myth, History & Cultural Associations
- **The trampled crab.** Hera sends a crab to nip Heracles' heel while he fights the Lernaean hydra; both combatants trample it underfoot. Hera rewards the crab with a place in the sky "to symbolize tenacity and courage in the face of certain death." Snodgrass's closing detail is the best thing in the corpus for this district: **the crab's constellation was placed far from Hercules', keeping "a safe distance from the strongman."** Honored for a doomed intervention on someone else's behalf, and relocated out of reach of the thing it attacked.
- **Names for it.** Arabic *Alsartan* and Syriac *Sartano*, both meaning **"the Holder."** Tibetans: the frog. Egyptians: the scarab. Prehistoric Indian eight-part cosmology: *nand*, the crab.
- **The gate.** The constellation is "associated with the gate through which humanity enters the world," and carries the Roman title ***janua inferni*, the gate of hell,** "which opens from the Milky Way to earth." Buddhist reading: "an ominous sleep of death"; the Inca share the foreboding. A threshold that runs both directions.
- **Repentance and rebirth.** Because the crab sheds its shell, Christian apologists used it for repentance and being born again (John 3); in the apostolic scheme Cancer answers to John the Divine. It governs "gestation, baptism, and rebirth" and, psychologically, "sympathy and the awakening of the conscious mind."
- **Duplicity.** The coin from Acragas, Sicily (late 5th c. B.C.) pairs the monster Scylla with a crab — read as the sea, "or perhaps suggesting the city's reputation for devious dealings."
- **The name is a disease.** *Carcinus* → carcinogen; Hippocrates first applied the crab metaphor to the tumor. Snodgrass: the sign "shares with human tumors the grasping tentacles that victimize organs by shutting off their life forces. For this reason, Cancer is one of the least popular sun signs in iconography, art, literature, and language." **The archetype's own name means a thing that grows by consuming its host.** Do not put this in-world, but it is the district's shadow in one word.
- **Historical anchors.** Snodgrass's exemplar is Nelson Rockefeller, who built "large, imposing buildings" and then "withdrew crab-fashion into cultural and philanthropic interests, substituting facades and money for more genuine, personal pleasures." Martin notes Pluto entered Cancer at the outbreak of WWI, "a time when old securities were destroyed and families throughout Europe were decimated" — a template for a founding trauma that produces a shelter-culture.
- **A ready-made internal dissident faction.** Martin's Uranus-in-Cancer generation "doesn't believe in family, in belonging or in any kind of biological containment. **Any kind of nest will make Uranus in Cancer panic.**" Its stated function: "to construct new tribes and clans based on shared ideals rather than shared genetics." A native-born counter-movement that rejects the district's entire premise while needing it "on an ordinary, human, lunar level."

## 14. Design Hooks for District Use
1. **No front door.** Membership is only ever conferred sideways — through an in-law, a coworker's cousin, a mutual acquaintance both parties dislike. Build a gate the player cannot solve by petitioning; they must first become *adjacent*. Directness reads as suspicious here, not honest.
2. **The uninvited gathering.** A standing weekly event that issues no invitations. Attending unbidden is the proof of belonging; asking whether you're welcome is the disqualifying answer. Occasionally the hosts deliberately skip notice "to see who shows up anyway."
3. **Food is the ledger.** Meals are family-style, refusal is an accusation, and the district's real political currency is who is fed by whom. Codify Martin's line as an actual social rule: *declining a Sanctuary meal is declining a Sanctuary claim on you.*
4. **Aid you cannot refuse or cancel.** The district's charitable apparatus helps people who did not ask and does not stop when told to. A strong quest inversion: the objective is not to obtain help but to get help **withdrawn** — and the district cannot process the request, because withdrawal is indistinguishable from abandonment.
5. **The smother-lock as institutional design.** Discharge paperwork that functionally cannot be completed; wards who "graduate" but stay on the rolls forever. Every incentive in the bureaucracy quietly rewards non-recovery. Pair with an emancipation faction and the Uranus-in-Cancer dissidents from §13.
6. **Standing requires a dependent.** With Mars in fall, nobody advocates for themselves. A grievance is only hearable when voiced on behalf of a ward. Consequence: residents acquire dependents in order to acquire a voice — and the district cannot see this as corruption.
7. **Whoever writes the shortlist rules the Sanctuary.** Its councils choose brilliantly among two or three options and lock up completely when asked to survey a field. The real power is not the vote; it's the clerk who assembles the slate. Excellent, quiet faction politics.
8. **Read the chores, not the speeches.** A resident absorbed in a craft they love is fine; a resident grimly doing work they hate is the alarm. At district scale: a sudden citywide burst of repair, scrubbing, and renovation is the reliable public tell that a political crisis is being suppressed. Nothing will be said about it.
9. **No escalation ladder.** Grievance is invisible for months, then detonates without warning. The passive-aggressive record — withheld services, unsigned notes, snubs, delayed permits — is the *only* available pressure gauge, and reading it is a genuine skill check.
10. **The shell is dated.** Institutional hostility here is scar tissue with a timestamp. Let the player date a given office's coldness to a specific historical intake surge and, by naming that event correctly, get through it. The crustiness was learned; it can be addressed at its source.
11. **Trust the past, distrust the present.** Martin: it "naturally tends not to trust unless it is absolutely safe to do so. On the other hand it is normally safe to trust the past and to trust memories." Verified history and old kinship open doors that present-tense good conduct never will. Archives are curated to keep only the good; the painful record is *sealed, not destroyed*.
12. **Names stick.** A classification bureau whose labels — assigned young, on thin evidence — become the labeled person's actual behavior for life.

See §12 and `G_Correspondences.md`. What *is* still truncated is Snodgrass's per-sign sympathy table, which preserved only the colors (violet, ice blue).
- **Emerson's Cancer correspondence block is corrupt.** It prints "Element: Air" for Cancer, contradicting Emerson's own text (cardinal water) everywhere else. The adjacent "Crystal: Jade / Essential oil: Peppermint / Chakra: Root" lines belong to **Capricorn**, not Cancer — its block bleeds into the next sign's. Nothing from that run should be treated as Cancer material.
- **Tierney is OCR-degraded** throughout ("cardina.lwate!", "v/2fs1-45", "elementhas"). Quotations above were reconstructed conservatively; anything not legible was left out rather than guessed.
- **Sources genuinely disagree on three points, and I have not flattened them:**
  (a) **Concealment vs. transparency.** Silva's *Sun and Moon* claims "you can count on from Cancer… honesty and openness. These folks will tell their partner clearly when something is bothering them." Emerson and Silva's *Ultimate Guide* say the exact reverse. Emerson is better corroborated; the outlier is noted, not used.
  (b) **Timidity.** Tierney argues aggressively that the shyness is a *self-image error* and that cardinal Cancer is assertive, demonstrative, and "can't easily hide what it's really going through." Snodgrass's trait list ("bashful, elusive, secretive") says the opposite. This disagreement is *productive* and is built into §10 rather than resolved.
  (c) **Trust.** Silva treats Cancerian loyalty as producing easy mutual trust; Martin flatly denies it. Both are in §14/hook 11.
- **Silva (both volumes) leans hard on a male/female Cancer split** as an organizing device — different molting behavior, different friendship formation, different loyalty thresholds. No other source in the corpus supports it. Treat it as one author's schema, not as consensus material, and do not port the gendering into district design.
- **Martin v3 yielded almost nothing sign-specific.** It is cycle theory plus a single extended Rossetti case study; its only reusable items are the Plato/Cancer-Capricorn axis note, the Rudhyar "putting down roots" phase, and the Pluto-into-Cancer/WWI anchor.
- **The corpus is silent on:** architecture, clothing and textile, music, funerary practice, governance forms or political institutions, law, economy, and any treatment of strangers *at scale* (its outsider material is entirely interpersonal, never civic or migratory — a real gap for a district whose premise is sanctuary).
- **Decans come from Emerson alone**, unconfirmed by any second source. Snodgrass, the scholarly source, gives none.
