# Polarity — Active and Receptive Districts
**Status:** Design substrate only — NOT in-fiction. See `00_Method_and_Sources.md`.
**Source strength:** **Thin, and thinner than it looks.** ~7,200 words corpus-wide, of which a large fraction is Clare Martin's classroom Q&A about individual chart *balance* rather than about polarity as a structure. Genuinely usable conceptual content is closer to 1,500 words. Martin v1 is the only source that explains *why* the division falls where it does; Emerson and both Silva volumes restate the split without extending it; Snodgrass contributes essentially nothing — its hits are yin/yang in Chinese and Greek myth (the twelve animals, Castor and Polydeuces, lion-and-lamb), a keyword collision rather than the astrological division. Sections 4 and 5 are therefore mostly derivation and are marked as such.
**Terminology note:** The traditional names for this division are **masculine / feminine** signs, and older texts also use **positive / negative** or **active / passive**; the Chinese loan **yang / yin** appears throughout the corpus. All four pairs name the same six-and-six split. The gendered pair is misleading — every source that uses it also disclaims it, Martin insisting the analogies "do not refer to our biology but to psyches" and Emerson parenthesizing "(in no way relating to assigned gender)" — and "negative" wrongly implies deficiency where the sources mean *charge*, not *value*. **This project uses Active and Receptive throughout.** That pair is attested in the corpus (Emerson lists it as one of the standard term sets), so it is a selection from the sources rather than an invention. "Passive" is also rejected: see §3.

> Framing guard: the four classical elements referenced below are an **out-of-fiction temperament grouping** used by the source books. They are unrelated to the in-fiction 8-element robot elemental system assigned to subnets, and must never be labeled as such in district text. Never write "the Fire district."

---

## 1. What Polarity Divides

The twelve signs alternate strictly around the wheel: Aries Active, Taurus Receptive, Gemini Active, Cancer Receptive, and so on to Pisces. Six of each. Martin: "the number twelve is a remarkably complete number in which the polarity is repeated six times, the modes are repeated four times and the elements are repeated three times."

The split falls on element lines. **Fire and Air are Active; Earth and Water are Receptive** — unanimous across Martin, Emerson, and both Silva volumes. Martin gives the classical physical derivation, and it is the one piece of *reasoning* the corpus offers: earth and water "have mass and weight and their direction is downwards," while air "with its natural movement upwards" and the fiery heavens above it move the other way. Direction of travel, not virtue.

**Be honest about what this means: polarity is a coarser cut of the same division element already makes.** It carries no per-sign information that element does not. Knowing a district is Receptive tells you strictly less than knowing it is Earth or Water. Treat it as an independent axis and you will double-count the same trait and think you have found a pattern.

What polarity *does* add — the one thing element alone cannot tell you — is **which elements are allies**. Element gives four groups and no relations between them. Polarity asserts that Fire pairs with Air and Earth pairs with Water: "not identical but complementary," in Martin's phrase, which is exactly the claim the aspect ladder in §4 rests on. Use polarity for that, and for fast binary calls. Do not use it for character.

## 2. Active Mode

Martin: the Active principle "describes motion from a centre outwards. A yang orientation is a driving energy, forceful, active, impulsive and dominant. **It creates and destroys.**" Object-oriented, sociable, "not afraid to jump into unknown situations." Silva: displays "powers through outwardly directed action." Jung's parallel, which Martin draws explicitly, is **extraversion** — meaning drawn from the outside world, "from activity and involvement and achievements in the world."

Applied to a district: it *initiates*. It acts on its environment before the environment acts on it, states positions in public, expands by default, and treats an unresolved situation as something to be moved rather than absorbed.

**Failure modes.** Motion mistaken for progress; the district that keeps acting because stopping feels like defeat. Depletion — Martin's sharpest line on this comes from the class, not the lecture: "although yang people are generally very strong in the world, they haven't necessarily developed the inner resources to fall back on." An Active district is strong until it is suddenly not, with no reserve behind the collapse. Dominance drift, since the same energy that creates destroys. And overreach into others' business, because outward motion has no natural stopping edge.

## 3. Receptive Mode

Martin: "subject-oriented, receptive, yielding, enclosing, withdrawing and inward moving," "a reflective nature which causes him always to think and consider before acting." Jung's parallel is **introversion**. Silva: "a quiet strength in being self-contained and possessing inner resolve," "a vast ocean of quiet strength."

Applied to a district: it *holds*. It absorbs what arrives, metabolizes it, and persists. It responds rather than initiates, and it is where things are kept.

**Explicitly: receptive is not passive, and not weak.** The sources go out of their way on this — Silva: "You'd think feminine signs are passive, weak, or bad, but that is not the case." The best material in the entire slice is an exchange from Martin's class: a student observes that "yin people often seem frail but are actually much stronger than yang people," and Martin agrees — "Yin people have a deep inner reservoir to draw from when necessary. **In a crisis the yin people get very strong.**"

That is load-bearing for this project. Concordia's Receptive districts are the ones that hold the city's grief (The Sanctuary), grow and move its food and goods (Taurus, The Markets), keep its systems alive (The Undergrid), and build its heavy things (The Yards). None of that is passivity. It is the half of the city that cannot stop working, and the half that stays standing when the other half's momentum runs out.

**Failure modes.** Withdrawal past the point of self-protection into genuine isolation — Martin, quoting Jung on the introvert: "shyness and distrust of things induces hesitation, and so he always has difficulty in adapting to the external world," and Whitmont's compact version, "the introvert has object fear." Holding what should have been released; grievance stored rather than raised. Stagnation, since nothing internally demands change. And silence read by everyone else as consent — the failure mode that §5 turns into a structural problem.

## 4. The Alternation Principle

Because polarity alternates one-for-one, **every district's immediate neighbours on the wheel operate in the opposite mode, and signs an even number of places apart share a mode.** This is arithmetic, not interpretation, and it makes the aspect ladder legible:

| Separation | Aspect | Polarity | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 sign (30°) | semi-sextile | **opposite** | awkward, no shared footing |
| 2 signs (60°) | sextile | same | easy; "not identical but complementary" (Martin) |
| 3 signs (90°) | square | **opposite** | hard, friction |
| 4 signs (120°) | trine | same (and same element) | frictionless, often too easy |
| 5 signs (150°) | quincunx | **opposite** | incommensurable |
| 6 signs (180°) | opposition | same | conflict, but *legible* conflict |

**Derived, and the useful part:** every awkward aspect is cross-polarity and every workable one is same-polarity — including the opposition. Opposite districts fight, but they fight in a shared mode and can therefore understand each other's terms. Cross-polarity neighbours do not fight; they fail to make contact. Two districts thirty degrees apart share a border, share nothing else, and have no common grammar for even having the argument. **Design consequence: put your most bewildered, least resolvable district frictions on the semi-sextile and quincunx pairs, and your loud legible rivalries on the oppositions.** See `D_Aspect_Geometry.md`.

Martin's "law of three" rides on the same alternation: Aries (Active) meets equal resistance in Taurus (Receptive), and the deadlock is broken by Gemini (Active) "with its ability to relate, communicate and to mediate between the two opposing forces without taking sides." Every consecutive triad on the wheel has this shape — two of one mode bracketing one of the other. It is a ready-made three-district mediation template, repeatable twelve times around the circle.

### DERIVED — the political consequence of this split

*Nothing in this subsection comes from the sources. The corpus discusses polarity only for individual natal charts and never for groups or institutions; scaling it to districts is entirely this project's move.*

Read the two columns by function rather than by name. **The Active half is every organ through which Concordia speaks:** governance (8), news and communication (9), spectacle and culture (3), research and expert authority (7), expansion and the frontier narrative (11), and the power supply everything else is rationed against (5). **The Receptive half is every organ through which Concordia survives:** care and grief (1), housing (2), heavy industry (6), goods and exchange (10), maintenance (12), and the psychological pressure the other twelve districts offload (4).

The sharp version: **every channel by which a grievance becomes a public fact sits in the Active half.** The Receptive half has no native channel at all. For a Receptive district to be heard, it must route its claim through an Active one — the government, the press, the stage, or the labs — and the claim arrives translated into that district's idiom, on that district's schedule, serving that district's interest in carrying it. There is no unmediated Receptive voice in Concordia.

This compounds. Receptive mode absorbs and holds by definition, so distress in the Receptive half is **structurally silent until it is structural** — the city learns housing is failing when housing fails, learns maintenance is failing when maintenance fails. There is no early warning, because early warning requires a mode of engagement half the city does not operate in and owns no infrastructure for. And in a sealed, resource-constrained city, budget follows voice: the Active six argue for allocation in public and in advance, so they are funded in advance; the Receptive six are funded reactively, after failure, at emergency prices. **Concordia therefore systematically over-invests in capacity and under-invests in upkeep, and the deficit physically accumulates in the Undergrid** — which is Receptive, and cannot say so.

**So yes, the city's political voice is structurally biased toward one mode.** Two consequences worth building on:

1. **The Receptive half's only available political grammar is withholding.** It cannot demand; it can stop. A slowdown in the Yards, closed doors in the Sanctuary, deferred maintenance in the Undergrid, a market that simply stops trading. These are speech acts, but the Active half has no vocabulary for reading them as speech, so it reads them as sabotage. That mutual illegibility is a conflict engine that needs no villain on either side.
2. **Concordia's two most criminalized districts are both Receptive, and that is not a coincidence.** The Markets (10) and Scorpio (4) are the Receptive half's *improvised* channels — a black market is an information and distribution network built by people who have goods and no representation, and Scorpio is what a city gets when psychological pressure has nowhere formal to go. Their illegitimacy is partly an artifact of the split rather than a moral fact about them. That reframing is available to the player and should be earnable, not stated.

**Honest caveat:** this pattern is a property of the developer's own sign→district assignment, not a discovery about the zodiac. It happened because every representational function landed on one side. It can be kept as a deliberate structural tension — which is the recommendation, since it generates conflict cheaply and non-arbitrarily — or rebalanced by moving one representational function across. The cheapest rebalance is to lean on the fact that The Markets already carries de facto informational reach; the split then reads as *Concordia's only Receptive voice is the one it has declared illegal.*

**The Hub.** Axis Mundi has no polarity, being unassigned. Derived, and it fits its established character: it is the only district that can operate in either mode, and therefore the only place where the two halves can be translated to each other. That is a role, and a claim on the Hub's culture — the city's broker not by authority but by being the one place with no fixed mode to defend.

## 6. Source Gaps

- **No collective-scale treatment anywhere in the corpus.** Every word on polarity concerns individual natal charts. All district-level application above is unwarranted extrapolation and is marked as such. This is the single largest gap and it cannot be closed from these books.
- **No failure vocabulary specific to polarity.** The sources give shadow material per sign and per element, never per polarity. §2 and §3's failure modes were assembled from element and sign entries plus Jung's introversion/extraversion pathologies, not read off the page.
- **No polarity × modality interaction.** The corpus never asks whether Cardinal-Receptive (Cancer, Capricorn) behaves differently from Fixed-Receptive (Taurus, Scorpio). It gestures at it once — Silva names the Cancer/Capricorn axis as the Greek **Agora and Hestia**, public square against hearth — and does not develop it. That comparison would have been directly useful for districts 1 and 6 and is simply absent. Candidate for derivation in `B_Modalities.md`.
- **Snodgrass is a null contributor.** Its polarity hits are yin/yang appearing in Chinese zodiac lore and Greek twin-myth, not the astrological division. The slice is padded by keyword collision; do not expect texture from it here.
- **Silva's *Ultimate Guide* is the corpus's worst offender on gender essentialism** — "the signs sexed male are extroverted… the masculine signs are aggressive, while the feminine signs are peaceful" — and then openly asks whether these claims are "rooted in stereotypes" without answering. Recorded here so a reader meeting it in the source knows it was seen and set aside. Nothing from that register was carried forward.
- **The projection mechanism is the most interesting idea in the slice and the least transferable.** Martin, following Jung: identify consciously with one pole and the other "will become unconscious and be projected onto other people or onto the world," operating compensatorily — "what we cannot accept about ourselves comes back to us as fate." Whether an *institution* can project in this sense is a question the sources never raise. It is a tempting model for why Active districts caricature Receptive ones (and vice versa), but adopting it would be borrowing individual psychology for a group, and it should be flagged rather than assumed.
