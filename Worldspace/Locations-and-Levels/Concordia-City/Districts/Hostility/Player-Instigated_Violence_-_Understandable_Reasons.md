# Player-Instigated Violence — Understandable (Not Justifiable) Reasons

**What this is:** a companion piece to `District_Hostile_Actions.md` and `District_Hostile_Justifications.md`,
but addressing the opposite direction. Those two files explain what makes *locals* turn hostile in reaction
to something the player did, and what in-character justification the player might have for doing it anyway.
This file addresses a "murderhobo" playstyle directly — the player instigating violence *first*, unprompted —
and asks what in-world, internally-consistent reasons could make that behavior make sense as an expression of
who this specific character is, without pretending it's morally justified. The Baldur's Gate 3 comparison:
Dark Urge doesn't make murder *right*, it makes murder *make sense* as an expression of that character's
actual nature. Written 2026-07-24, at the developer's request — none of this is decided or locked in; it's a
set of candidates to draw from.

---

## Whole-Game-Level Candidates

These are setting-wide, build-legible reasons a character could plausibly instigate violence across any
district, not tied to one location's specific culture.

1. **The low-Humanity build, taken at face value.** Humanity is an existing MACHINE stat governing emotional
   intelligence, empathy, and social connection, selectable down to 1 at character creation
   (`Character_Creation_Overview.md`). A 1-Humanity build doesn't need a new system invented to explain
   violence-first behavior — it's already the mechanical explanation. Violence isn't sadism here; it's simply
   what the world looks like from inside a character whose empathy circuit barely runs. The cleanest anchor,
   because it costs nothing new — it's leaning into what character creation already permits rather than
   inventing an origin mechanic from scratch.
2. **Bridge Unit activation instability.** The player is activated by Calethina at the exact moment she's
   mid-collapse (the Planetary Split Brain, pre-Triage-Protocol-discovery chaos). A rushed or imperfectly
   calibrated activation is a clean, in-fiction reason a given build's Nerve/Humanity calibration could run
   hot from minute one — not evil, just imperfectly booted, mirroring her own condition rather than being an
   unmotivated character quirk.
3. **Residual player-mechanic echoes, from the Fragmentation Matrix.** This term already exists (flagged
   Launch-exclusive in `Early_Access_vs_Launch_Content_Split.md`, never fully detailed mechanically). A
   heavily re-specced character accumulating Grief could manifest literal intrusive violent impulses —
   an abandoned configuration reasserting itself, structurally identical to how Dark Urge's true nature keeps
   trying to resurface. Especially strong because it ties a murderhobo playstyle directly to *how much the
   player has re-specced*, giving it its own build-legible cause rather than floating free of any system.
4. **Unresolved personhood uncertainty.** The player's own nature (human? robot? bridge-thing?) is unsettled
   at game start — a direct tie-in to the game's own north-star creative question about robot consciousness
   (`user_creative_principles`). Violence-first as the behavior of someone who hasn't yet decided whether
   *anyone's* life, including their own, carries the weight a "real" person's would.

**Still open:** whether any of these four should actually become a real character-creation option (an
origin/background choice, closer to how BG3 implements Dark Urge mechanically) or stay purely as roleplay
justification with no mechanical hook. Not decided here.

## Per-District Candidates

Proactive, not reactive — each grounded in that specific district's own established psychology per
`District_Canon_Reference.md`, not a generic reason copy-pasted across all thirteen.

### 1. Cancer (The Sanctuary)
- Re-enacting the Overcrowding Decision's own triage logic preemptively — "better to remove a burden before
  it becomes one," a chilling mirror of the district's own buried history.
- Paranoid preemption against the Mother's Circuit's informal surveillance network — striking before it
  "gets to you."

### 2. Taurus
- Class-preemption against "warm side" political privilege — striking before the Cold Side/Warm Side divide
  inevitably works against you.

### 3. Leo
- Inherited Star War bitterness — striking symbols or descendants of the winning performance house before
  the losing house's century-old grievance dies out entirely.

### 4. Scorpio
- "The only way through is through," weaponized — forcing someone else's confrontation or transformation
  without consent, a twisted extension of the district's own core therapeutic philosophy.

### 5. Aries
- "Running hot" internalized past all reason — manufacturing conflict to prove you're not soft, since
  backing down is the one truly unforgivable thing in this district's own value system.

### 6. Capricorn
- Meritocracy taken to its logical extreme — eliminating a rival is just "earning your spire floor" by other
  means.

### 7. Aquarius
- The district's own genuine-but-dangerous certainty — preemptively "neutralizing" an obstacle to a greater
  research goal, echoing its real historical pattern of ethical shortcuts justified by results.

### 8. Libra
- The Suspended Compact's own logic, turned outward — "technically this is still an emergency," borrowing
  the government's own permanent-exception reasoning to justify striking first.

### 9. Gemini
- The Speed Problem, applied to violence — treating a fast, decisive strike as inherently better than a
  slower, verified response, the district's own institutional pathology turned lethal.

### 10. Pisces
- "Everything eventually dissolves" — striking first because betrayal is inevitable here anyway, so better
  to be the one who acts.

### 11. Sagittarius
- Frontier kill-or-be-killed calculus — genuinely justified by the setting's own logic that expansion
  decisions out here are already, unavoidably, life-or-death.

### 12. Virgo
- The Year of Silence, escalated — "you'll only listen if it costs you something real," an extreme extension
  of the district's one precedent for making itself heard.

### 13. Concordia Central Hub (Axis Mundi)
- Principled rootlessness taken nihilistic — belonging nowhere becomes owing nothing to anyone's rules at
  all.

## Open Questions

- Whether any whole-game-level candidate becomes an actual mechanical origin/background option, or stays
  pure roleplay justification.
- Whether per-district candidates should ever surface in-fiction (an NPC who half-suspects your reasoning) or
  stay entirely unspoken, internal-to-the-player-character framing.
- How this interacts with the Reputation Matrix and Fragmentation Matrix — a murderhobo run presumably tanks
  both, but whether either system has anything *specific* to say about this playstyle (beyond the obvious
  reputation collapse) hasn't been explored.
