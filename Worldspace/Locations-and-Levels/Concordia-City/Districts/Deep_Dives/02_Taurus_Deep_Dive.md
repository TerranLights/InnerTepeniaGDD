## Finding — A genuine anachronism in Historical_Pressures.md's founding-era bullet

`Historical_Pressures.md`, Taurus: "Early Post-Falkland Settlement (late 2560s)... stable, long-term homes for bonded human-robot pairs who chose to stay on the surface **rather than risk space colonization**."

Orbital/space colonization was not a founding-era (late 2560s) capability anywhere in established Tepenian history — it's a much later Second Interwar Period development (Census I is explicitly "pre-orbital era," Census II "orbital era," and orbital infrastructure's own established build-out happened in stages long after 2564). Nobody in the late 2560s was choosing surface life "rather than" space colonization, because space colonization didn't exist yet to choose against.

**This phrase belongs one bullet later.** The very next entry, "Long Night War Evacuation Waves (early 2800s)," correctly uses the real version of this same idea: "As many Tepenians fled to space via Amundsen Tower, a large number of families and bonded companions who could not or would not leave were redirected here." `District_Canon_Reference.md` — the authoritative synthesis — already silently dropped the anachronistic phrase from its own founding-era bullet; `Historical_Pressures.md` just never got the same correction applied. Same pattern as Finding 3 (Calethina's Lab): the older file is stale, the newer one already fixed it.

**Suggested fix:** delete "rather than risk space colonization" from `Historical_Pressures.md`'s founding-era bullet, matching `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s already-clean version.

## Stage 1 — What Taurus Actually Became, Across 250 Years, Before the War Ever Touched It

With the anachronism set aside, Taurus' actual founding population is simpler and more interesting than the erroneous framing implied: ordinary bonded human-robot pairs from the original exile, given a deliberately planned, well-insulated residential sector, prioritizing family units and private courtyards. No dramatic choice against an alternative — just people building a home.

**What 250 years of that specifically produces:** a community whose defining trait, per its own established culture, is "stubborn resistance to destabilizing change." That's not a trait a population develops by facing constant crisis — it's a trait a population develops by facing almost *none*. Taurus' heirloom culture (a grandparent's chair as "non-negotiable furniture"), its cross-generational robot-family genealogies (a robot present in the same household for four generations outranking a newer, more capable model), its noise ordinances and quiet-hours enforcement — all of this reads as exactly what happens when a community gets 250 years of relative continuity to accumulate tradition, generation after generation, with nothing forcing them to adapt. Taurus didn't become conservative *despite* an easy 250 years. It became conservative *because of* one.

**The Insulation Schism as a genuinely pre-war (Stage 1) event, not a war outcome.** The "Cold Side"/"Warm Side" social stratification — insulation materials repeatedly diverted to Capricorn "at multiple points during resource shortages," with residence tracking almost exactly to "when your family arrived and how much political capital they had" — reads as an ordinary Second Interwar Period phenomenon, not something the war caused. This is worth stating explicitly as Stage 1 material: Taurus' internal class structure (Established vs. Coldsiders, in `District_Unity_of_Opposites.md`'s own framing) was already fully formed *before* 2812, a genuine 250-year-old stratification, not a war-era wound.

## Stage 2 — How the War's Fallout Landed on That Already-Settled Community

This is where the existing material (Long Night War Evacuation Waves, the Bonded Pair Registry Collapse of 2812, the Stockpiling Culture's Dark Side) actually belongs — a second, later force acting on a community that, per Stage 1, had spent 250 years getting very good at *not changing*, and had never developed any cultural muscle for handling disruption.

**Why the war hit Taurus specifically hard, given Stage 1:** a community whose entire 250-year identity was built around continuity had, by definition, never practiced adapting to anything. When the evacuation waves and the Bonded Pair Registry Collapse actually arrived, Taurus' only available cultural response was to dig in harder — "hold the line" isn't a new trait forged by the war, it's the same 250-year-old resistance-to-change reflex, deployed defensively for the first time against something that could actually break it, using the only tool the culture had ever needed before.

**A genuine new stratification worth naming: Coldsiders and Unverified may overlap.** The Bonded Pair Registry Collapse (2812) created a new post-war underclass — the Unverified, whose bonds could never be re-verified, existing in a limbo the culture refuses to acknowledge. This is a *second*, separate stratification system layered on top of the pre-existing (Stage 1) Cold Side/Warm Side divide. Nothing currently states whether these two populations overlap, but it's a natural, well-grounded question: are Coldsiders — already the lower-political-capital population before the war — disproportionately likely to also be Unverified after it, compounding into a genuinely entrenched double-underclass? This isn't stated anywhere currently and would be a natural, earned elaboration rather than an invention.

## The Double Result

Taurus' present-day "hold the line" identity isn't something the war created. It's 250 years of uninterrupted domestic permanence, meeting the first real disruption that permanence ever had to survive, and responding the only way it knew how — not by adapting, because it had never needed to, but by gripping tighter. The tragedy latent in Taurus, that the existing docs don't quite name outright: a culture whose whole value was continuity just had the least practice of any district in Concordia at surviving actual change, precisely because it had spent two and a half centuries being so good at not needing to.

## What I did not find

No contradiction in Taurus' relationship to Cancer, Virgo, Capricorn, or Pisces as currently written — those hold up under closer inspection, and the Cancer-to-Taurus refugee pipeline (already established) reads consistently as a 250-year-old pattern, not a new post-war one.
