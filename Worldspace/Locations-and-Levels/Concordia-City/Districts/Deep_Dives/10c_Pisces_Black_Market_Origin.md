# Pisces — Black Market Origin: Confirmed Mechanism

**Purpose:** resolves the item flagged in `TODO.md` on 2026-07-07 ("Pisces' Black Market Origin — deliberately deferred") — Pisces' already-established "Long Night War Black-Market Lifeline (early 2800s)" bullet in `District_Canon_Reference.md` states a fact (Pisces became the shadow economy that kept the city alive) without a mechanism. This file supplies one.

**Status: CONFIRMED, 2026-07-29.** Not yet promoted into `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s Pisces entries — held here as the confirmed reference, same discipline as the Non-Malice Audit's own dedicated files before their combined promotion pass.

## Full candidate set considered

Five distinct possibilities were developed and compared:

- **P1 — Always Black:** no turning point; the market was always illicit from founding. Rejected as too thin — answers nothing, gives no scene to build.
- **P2 — Later Founding, Legitimate-to-Black:** the market starts ordinary, turns black at a later crisis. Costly on its own — requires softening already-written founding color (smugglers and "chemical or digital escape" are already in the founding text).
- **P3 — Gray-to-Black:** founding stays exactly as written (already home to smugglers and escape-seekers); the war's desperation hardens an existing sympathetic gray economy into real criminal syndicates and addiction cycles. **Used below.**
- **P4 — One Root Cause, Two Mysteries:** the Amundsen Tower's destruction (2812) triggers both the market turning black and The Flood. Elegant, but requires moving The Flood's already-fixed c. 2771 date.
- **P5 — The Founding-Era Enforcement Gap:** Libra's founding-era policing capacity, genuinely scarce, was never extended to a low-status district like Pisces, and the gap was never revisited. A Continuity and Stability Act provision. **Used below.**

Five combinations of these were compared (nAlpha through nEpsilon); **nBeta (P3 + P5)** was confirmed as both the strongest overall and the most in-world characteristically consistent — zero cost, ties into the same Act thread as four other districts, and matches the exact "became... when official supply lines collapsed" timing already written into Pisces' own war-era canon.

## CONFIRMED — nBeta, plus a concrete anchor and an Act-evidence payoff

### A. Founding (late 2560s) — unchanged

Pisces settles exactly as already written: an unofficial overflow zone for those who didn't fit early planning — failed artists from Leo, trauma cases too intense for Scorpio, smugglers, and those seeking chemical or digital escape. Its porous architecture and lower enforcement priority allow informal networks to flourish from day one. No population color is rewritten.

### B. The founding-era enforcement gap

During the same earliest, most desperate exile years, Libra's own policing and enforcement capacity was genuinely scarce and had to be triaged — directed toward whichever districts and crises seemed most urgent at the time. Pisces, already a low-status overflow zone nobody had specifically planned for, simply never made that list. This wasn't a decision *against* Pisces — nobody sat down and chose to let it run wild — it was an ordinary, defensible crisis-era allocation choice, never revisited once the emergency eased, because nobody with the standing to redirect enforcement resources ever prioritized doing so. This is a surviving provision of the same **Continuity and Stability Act** already confirmed for Aries (a stability-review protocol), Libra (a records-retention clause), and Cancer (a compliance formula) — this time granting enforcement discretion with no built-in review.

### C. Second Interwar Period (2564–2812) — a genuine, contained gray economy

With no real oversight for 250 years, Pisces' founding-era networks don't need to hide or stay small to survive — but without a citywide crisis forcing them to scale into something essential, they grow only organically into a real, known, locally-embedded gray economy: sympathetic, low-stakes, community-serving. Smuggling and escape-tech are an ordinary fact of neighborhood life here, tolerated by omission rather than anyone's active choice — not yet the syndicate-run shadow economy the war-era text describes.

### D. Long Night War (early 2800s) — the hardening

Official supply lines collapse citywide. Per the already-established mechanism in `National_Economy_and_Currency.md`, the Amundsen Tower's destruction kills both the unified grid backing the national currency and the Arcanet network that verified it, in the same catastrophic instant. Pisces — uniquely positioned by 250 years of unsupervised network-building — is the one place in the city able to instantly absorb and service the resulting desperate demand. This is where the war-era text's own word choice earns its verb: the war doesn't invent Pisces' capability, it "embeds" real criminal syndicates and addiction cycles into networks that were, until this point, genuinely gray rather than genuinely dark.

### E. Post-war

Libra's repeated crackdowns fail — not from newly-discovered reluctance, but because the same 250-year-old enforcement gap never actually closed, now compounded by genuine citywide dependency (per the already-established "too many other districts secretly relied on its services" text). The Tolerance Pact, whenever its own status is resolved, becomes a natural eventual formalization of an arrangement that was functionally already true.

### F. The concrete anchor — the Triage Directive

Somewhere in Libra's own administrative archive — plausibly the already-established Treaty Archive Vaults — sits a genuine, physical, dated founding-era document: an emergency enforcement-priority allocation from the earliest exile years, listing which districts and concerns Libra's scarce early policing capacity would be directed toward. Pisces isn't on it. Not struck from it, not targeted by it — simply never added, because nobody drafting it in the chaos of the earliest years thought to. Nobody has revisited this document in two centuries. It's exactly the kind of thing a player finds while investigating something else entirely in Libra's own records, or that a long-standing Pisces resident has heard rumored to exist but never actually seen.

### G. The Continuity and Stability Act's first findable evidence

This is the anchor's real payoff. The Act currently exists only as an inferred pattern — confirmed as the shared root behind several districts' quiet power, but never yet given an actual, discoverable, in-world physical form. If the Triage Directive explicitly cites its own authority as deriving from emergency enforcement-discretion "under the Continuity and Stability Act" (name possibly redacted, abbreviated, or bureaucratically obscured in-document, per the developer's own future call), it becomes the first tangible artifact anyone in-world could actually hold that proves the Act is real — not just a pattern a sufficiently thorough investigator notices, but a literal piece of paper. This is the natural kickoff point for the "connect the dots" mystery already flagged as worth developing via Gemini's data archaeologists, and it belongs to Pisces first.

### H. Bonus thread — the Scorpio parallel

Pisces' own established "nobody officially acknowledges needing us" identity is explicitly shared with Scorpio in `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s own Cross-District Notes ("The districts nobody officially acknowledges needing are Pisces and Scorpio. Both of them know this."). nBeta's mechanism — quietly indispensable, never officially valued, benefiting from the gap between those two facts — gives that shared line a real, derived cause for Pisces, closely paralleling (without needing to be identical to) Scorpio's own now-confirmed custody-dispute chain. The two districts' companion NPCs could plausibly recognize the same shape in each other's history without requiring any literal shared document between them.

### I. The Cross-District Notes line, resolved

The bonus thread in Section H pointed at a real, standing problem: `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s own Cross-District Notes used to close with *"The districts nobody officially acknowledges needing are Pisces and Scorpio. Both of them know this. Both of them use it."* "Use it" implied active leverage — a district deliberately wielding its own unacknowledged status against someone. That was already flagged during the original non-malice audit pass as worth rechecking once either district's material got reworked, since it read as the same family of problem as the Aries/Libra items. Both districts now have: Scorpio's Forbidden Archive War is a chain of accidental custody (founding-era overflow → a dual-appointed custodian who died → an institutionalized redundancy nobody re-examined); Pisces' own Black Market Origin, confirmed above, is a founding-era enforcement gap nobody ever closed. Neither district actively wields anything against anyone in either mechanism — both are passive, structural, and never revisited, exactly the non-malicious shape the whole audit requires.

**Five candidate replacements were considered for "Both of them use it":**
1. *"Neither has ever had to change"* — the most mechanically precise option, directly restating the "never revisited" shape both confirmed mechanisms actually share.
2. *"Both of them have quietly built around it"* — kept a sense of agency without implying leverage, but read as too abstract on its own. **Dropped by developer request.**
3. *"Neither one minds"* — short and wry, matching the line's original conspiratorial tone, but risked undercutting Scorpio's more serious, confrontational register.
4. *"Both of them are better off for it"* — plain, unambiguous, states the passive benefit directly, if a little flat standing alone.
5. *"Neither one is in a hurry to be found"* — atmospheric, preserves the original's air of mystery, without implying either district is actively concealing anything — neither is hiding; neither has ever been looked at closely enough to need to.

**Confirmed: options 1, 3, 4, and 5 combined**, treated as four distinct facets of the same idea — mechanism (1), evaluative outcome (4), tone (3), and atmosphere (5) — rather than as competing alternatives, folded into one flowing closing sentence instead of a checklist of separate claims. **Already promoted directly into `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s Cross-District Notes** (unlike the Black Market mechanism itself, which is still pending its own promotion pass): *"Neither has ever had to change, and both are better off for it — neither one minds, and neither is in a hurry to be found."* Full tracking also recorded in `Cross_District_Non_Malice_Audit.md`'s own "Cross-District Notes entry" section.
