## Stage 1 — What Capricorn Actually Became, Across ~200 Years, Before the War Ever Touched It

**A structural, not necessarily malicious, root cause for the merit system's own bias.** The Narrow Door (c. 2761) found Capricorn's rating system had undervalued robot workers "for decades" relative to humans performing equivalent work — a genuine deviation from Tepenia's established national baseline ("egalitarian, skewed in favor of robots"). Worth asking why *this* district specifically produced the exception: a meritocracy has to decide what to measure and how, and if Capricorn's founding-era metrics (output counted in ways that map naturally to human labor — hours, physical exertion, visible exertion) were designed early, before the country's own now-standard egalitarian norms had fully crystallized, the system could have baked in a bias from day one without anyone needing to actively intend it — a measurement tool that simply never learned to see how robots actually contribute (speed, precision, tirelessness, parallel task-handling), rather than active discrimination. This doesn't excuse it, but it explains something the existing text doesn't: not villainy, just 200-year-old tools nobody updated.

**Why this went uncorrected for 60+ years after being formally documented.** The Narrow Door happened in 2761. The present day is ≈2822–2827. That's over sixty years of a *documented, independently-verified* injustice sitting quietly buried, in a city whose own governance district (Libra) is explicitly built around fairness and process. The most plausible reason, given what's already established: Capricorn's own economic indispensability. `general_problems.md`'s own Capricorn section already states "the district is the city's primary producer... if Capricorn's production ever collapses... the entire city would face severe material shortages very quickly. This gives the district significant hidden leverage." That's the same shape of leverage Aries already uses to resist accountability (the implicit blackout threat) — Capricorn's own version is quieter, but the mechanism is the same: too economically essential to actually discipline, even once the injustice is on the record.

## Stage 2 — How the War's Fallout Landed on That Already-Mature, 200-Year-Old System

**The Wartime Production Deaths are plausibly the same broken metric, hitting a different population at higher stakes.** The established fact: wartime production quotas were "mechanically achievable and humanly lethal," known to be excessive by leadership, publicly framed as heroic sacrifice. Read against Stage 1's proposed root cause — a 200-year-old measurement system built around human-centric assumptions that never got recalibrated — this isn't a separate wartime failure. It's the same underlying flaw (a metric system that was never actually well-calibrated to either population's real limits, just optimized to look achievable on paper) manifesting differently depending on the stakes: undervaluation when the cost is merely economic and reputational (robots, peacetime), literal death when the cost is wartime production targets pushed past human physical limits (humans, 2812). One broken system, two different harms, depending entirely on what was being measured against whom.

**The Calethina Problem persists across both stages** — Capricorn built the district's infrastructure and produces many of the robots the Lab activates, but has never held jurisdiction over it. This reads as a continuous, ongoing grievance rather than a single dated event, present from the Lab's own founding-era establishment straight through to today.

## New conflict thread — Capricorn's own passed-over robot workers build the bodies that become persons somewhere else

Capricorn's construction and assembly-line workforce plausibly includes the actual production of robot frames later activated at Calethina's Lab — meaning some of Capricorn's own workers passed over by The Narrow Door have quite literally built the physical bodies that go on to be recognized as full persons, the moment they leave Capricorn's own jurisdiction and reach a lab Capricorn has never controlled. This is a sharper, more specific version of the district's own established grievance than The Narrow Door alone: not just "the system doesn't measure what it claims to," but "we build the thing that gets recognized and rewarded once it's somewhere else, while we're still stuck behind our own district's narrow door." Worth developing as a specific, personal radicalizing thread for individual members of Capricorn's Recalibration Underground, distinct from the more abstract, city-wide grievance the review itself represents.

## New findings — informed by the 2026-07-31 diaspora composition pass

**Sinheung supplies nearly a fifth of Capricorn's diaspora (~19.6%), and its arrival may be the specific demographic mechanism nudging the district's suppressed Production Death Memorial Corrections tradition toward the surfacing the deep dive above already predicts.** The diaspora file connects Sinheung's own soberly-observed Founding Day — reckoning honestly with what the Jeju-do allocation actually cost rather than celebrating it — to Capricorn's underground **Production Death Memorial Corrections**, noting Sinheung's diaspora is "plausibly overrepresented among the grassroots voices keeping that quietly dissenting Capricorn tradition alive." Capricorn's own Cultural Texture already states that this undercurrent of dissent "is real and currently dismissed" but "will eventually surface, and when it does, The Narrow Door of 2761 will be its primary exhibit." Read together, this gives that prediction an actual causal mechanism rather than a vague inevitability: a specific, culturally primed population — used to naming a founding cost publicly as a matter of civic honesty, not scandal — arriving in large numbers is a plausible trigger for exactly the surfacing the district's own notes already anticipate, rather than dissent simply reaching a random breaking point on its own. Worth treating Sinheung's diaspora's continued growth or consolidation as a rough timeline peg for when that surfacing becomes politically live.

**Neumayer is Capricorn's single largest diaspora population (~22.9%), and its entire cultural identity — precision measurement, calibration, "Measured, Not Debated" — is arriving inside the one district whose defining flaw is a measurement system nobody ever recalibrated.** The diaspora file describes Neumayer's Instrument-Craft Workshops integrating "almost seamlessly into Capricorn's guild structure," effectively becoming a new specialty guild in its own right. But Capricorn's own confirmed mechanism for The Narrow Door (per `06b_Capricorn_Alternative_Conditions.md`) is precisely a calibration failure: a promotion system "calibrated during the founding crisis to conditions that no longer hold, and was never recalibrated once they changed." Neumayer's diaspora — over a fifth of the district's outer population, credited elsewhere in this project with the patience and rigor behind the Amundsen Tower's own schematics — is a population structurally built to notice exactly this kind of stale-calibration problem faster than anyone raised inside Capricorn's own settled, self-satisfied meritocracy. If any Neumayer-descended engineers eventually sit on a review committee or guild council, they arrive with the specific professional instinct to ask when the review windows were last actually re-measured against real conditions — a question apparently nobody native to Capricorn has asked in over sixty years. Worth flagging as a plausible in-world route to the Narrow Door's actual technical diagnosis, rather than only its political exposure.

**Troll's diaspora (~11.6%) arrives fluent in exactly the skill Capricorn's own passed-over workers most need and don't have: formalized cooperation between parties who fundamentally distrust each other.** The diaspora file grounds Troll's Cross-Faction Dispatch Handoffs — daily coordination between three rival airfield factions who all depend on each other's cooperation despite the distrust — inside Capricorn's **Guild Departure Mediation Offices** and **Guild Binding Witness Circuit**, and separately notes Troll's diaspora is "already fluent in watching an official narrative eclipse an older, truer one," plausibly drawn to the district's suppressed Undervalued Workers' Quiet Archive over its louder public ceremonies. Capricorn's own Recalibration Underground — the passed-over-workers' undercurrent this deep dive's own earlier "New conflict thread" already develops as a radicalizing thread tied to the Calethina Lab grievance — currently has no established mechanism for actually negotiating anything with Capricorn leadership; it only compares notes quietly. Troll's diaspora, arriving already practiced at exactly this kind of forced, mutually-distrustful negotiation under real stakes, is a plausible source of the Underground's first genuine negotiators, rather than merely sympathetic bystanders — worth developing as a specific character type for whenever the Underground moves from grievance to actual demand.

**Rothera's diaspora (~12.6%) imports a story of decentralized survival into a district whose core structural flaw is a single, fixed bottleneck — and the two ideas are close to direct opposites.** The diaspora file notes Rothera's Standing Works observance, folded into Capricorn's **Merit Board Vigils**, brings "a story of decentralized survival into Capricorn's culture of legible, measurable achievement" — Rothera survived the war specifically because "no single strike could take out everything at once." Capricorn's own confirmed Narrow Door mechanism identifies the opposite design as the district's actual root problem: "a fixed, genuinely limited number of master-tier workstations, a real capacity constraint nobody ever prioritized funding an expansion for," where "every rare vacancy becomes a high-stakes decision." A population whose entire civic identity is proof that redundancy beats concentration arriving inside a district organized around one narrow, centralized point of advancement is a live ideological contrast, not just a cultural curiosity — Rothera-descended voices inside the Merit Board Vigils are structurally positioned to be the first to suggest the actual fix isn't better patronage but more workstations, spread wider, since that's the literal lesson their own home city's survival already taught them.

**Marambio's diaspora (~10.4%) gravitates specifically toward Capricorn's "most tradition-bound guilds" — which, per the district's own confirmed mechanism, are exactly the guilds most likely to still be running on the Narrow Door's uncorrected, decades-stale calibration.** The diaspora file connects Marambio's Comandante's Operations Hall — a founding-era operational standard that outlasted the city's own population — to Capricorn's guild-continuity culture, predicting Marambio's diaspora "likely gravitates toward Capricorn's most tradition-bound guilds." But those are the same guilds whose "earliest master-crafters built the system around their own working rhythms" and where "masters have always sponsored apprentices who feel familiar to them" — the guilds where the Narrow Door's patronage-through-familiarity problem would be most entrenched, not least. Marambio no longer exists to send anyone a second wave of arrivals, which means this specific diaspora population — already vulnerable as refugees of a destroyed city — is disproportionately settling into the exact institutional pocket least likely to recognize an unfamiliar working rhythm as worth sponsoring. Worth treating as a quiet, structural double-vulnerability distinct from the more visible robot-specific mismatch the Narrow Door's own mechanism describes: destroyed-city human refugees, drawn by genuine cultural affinity into the guilds worst-positioned to actually welcome them.
