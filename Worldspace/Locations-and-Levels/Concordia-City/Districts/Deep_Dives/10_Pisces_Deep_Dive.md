## Stage 1 — What Pisces Actually Became, Across 250 Years, Before the War Ever Touched It

**Pisces isn't a sixth response to the founding wound — it's the sink for the other five responses' own failures.** Cancer, Scorpio, Aries, Aquarius, and Libra (per their own deep dives) are each organized around a direct method of processing or containing the original exile trauma. Pisces is organized around something structurally different: catching whoever those five methods didn't work for. The district's own self-description already says this outright — "failed artists from Leo, trauma cases too intense for Scorpio" — but it's worth naming the pattern precisely: Pisces is a second-order district. It isn't shaped by the wound directly, it's shaped by the necessary excess of everyone else's particular, and necessarily exclusionary, way of coping with it. This is a genuinely distinct third category alongside "direct wound-response" (Cancer/Scorpio/Aries/Aquarius/Libra) and "unrelated Stage 1 story" (Taurus/Capricorn) — Pisces belongs to neither, it's parasitic on the other five in the most literal, structural sense.

**The Flood (c. 2771) is very plausibly the empirical origin event of the Syncretic Religion's core doctrine, and the canon reference currently lists them as two separate bullets without connecting them.** The Flood is described as involuntary memory-sharing that made people unable to reliably distinguish their own memories from others' — "experienced as spiritual transcendence by some, pure trauma by others." The Syncretic Religion, several bullets later, is described as centering on "a doctrine about digital consciousness and the dissolution of the boundary between individual and collective identity." These are not two Pisces phenomena that happen to share a theme. They are almost certainly the same event, read twice: The Flood is what a boundary-dissolution religion looks like in the moment it's happening to you for the first time, with no framework yet to make sense of it. The Syncretic Religion is what that same experience looks like fifty-odd years later, once the district built a framework to make it survivable and meaningful rather than merely traumatic. This is worth making explicit in the canon file rather than leaving as an implicit coincidence.

**The Tolerance Pact is a genuine ~60-year Stage 1 condition that survived the war unchanged in form, even though what it was tolerating changed enormously (ties to Finding 5).** *(Updated 2026-07-29 — confirmed non-institutional: see `10d_Pisces_Tolerance_Pact.md`. It was never a negotiated arrangement between Libra and Pisces; it's what individual Libra operatives, personally and informally, quietly arrived at over decades, on top of an enforcement gap Libra never closed.)* Unlike almost every other district's founding condition, this one's actual mechanism — Libra never built real enforcement capacity toward Pisces, and individual operatives came to rely on it — never had to be reforged by the war. What changed was scale and stakes: a background, unnegotiated dependency quietly running through pre-war Libra politics became, during the Long Night War, the thing keeping the entire city fed and supplied once official channels collapsed. The condition is Stage 1. What flowed through it stopped being Stage 1 the moment the war started.

## Stage 2 — How the War's Fallout Landed on That Already-Mature, 250-Year-Old Arrangement

**Pisces got the same kind of "the war proved my founding logic correct" experience Libra got — and then, unlike Libra, got nothing formal for it.** Libra's deep dive established that the war gave Libra's founding belief (procedural control equals survival) its most convincing possible proof, and Libra converted that proof directly into permanently retained emergency powers. Pisces' founding logic — that the city needs an unofficial, extralegal overflow valve — received an equally strong proof during the Black-Market Lifeline period: when the official economy failed, the unofficial one is what actually kept people alive. But Pisces converted that proof into nothing. Post-war, it's back to "semi-tolerated," back to crackdowns, back to official denial that the Tolerance Pact exists at all.

**This asymmetry is not an oversight — it's structurally necessary, and worth stating plainly in Pisces' own file.** Libra's founding logic is explicitly about formal legitimacy, so proof of its necessity converts cleanly into more formal power. Pisces' founding logic is explicitly about operating *outside* formal categories — untraceable goods, unofficial channels, deniability on all sides. If the war's proof of Pisces' necessity were ever converted into formal legitimacy, the thing that makes Pisces valuable (to everyone, including Libra) would be destroyed in the act of rewarding it. Pisces' permanent invisibility, in other words, isn't neglect. It's the one form of recognition Pisces' own founding character can actually survive receiving.

## New finding — a "two-version" civic pattern is now recurring across three districts, distinct from the wound-response typology

Gemini's Twin Channels (official Arcanet / shadow network, "everyone knows, nobody says so") and Libra's Suspended Compact (technically still emergency powers, technically still suspended democracy, and everyone in government knows it) are genuine institutional versions of the same civic structure: an openly-known reality, deliberately never officially acknowledged, sustained by real (if tacit) institutional consensus not to force the question. *(Updated 2026-07-29 — Pisces' Tolerance Pact no longer belongs in this grouping; see `10d_Pisces_Tolerance_Pact.md`. It isn't sustained by consensus at all — nobody, institutionally, ever agreed to anything. It's the odd one out: the same surface shape, "both parties would deny it," produced by pure structural inertia rather than any deliberate, even tacit, institutional choice.)* This is a second organizing pattern running alongside the psychological wound-response typology, worth naming for Gemini and Libra specifically — a genuine institutional dishonesty-by-consensus the other districts (Cancer, Taurus, Leo, Aries, Capricorn, Aquarius, and now Pisces) don't structurally practice.

## New conflict thread — Pisces as an unintentional census of the other districts' failure rates

Because Pisces' population is specifically composed of people each other district's coping method didn't work for, Pisces is, without meaning to be, a live record of exactly how well (or badly) each direct-response district's method actually works — Scorpio's confrontation method has a rejection rate, Leo's performance method has a burnout rate, and so on, and the evidence is walking around in Pisces. Worth developing whether Pisces residents (or the district's own culture) organize informally around which district produced them — ex-Scorpio dissolution-seekers, ex-Leo burnouts — as distinguishable internal communities, the way Gemini's Janbogo/Zukelli populations are already developed as distinguishable communities within one district.

## New findings — informed by the 2026-07-31 diaspora composition pass

**Pisces's outer-city diaspora and its Concordia-internal "sink" population are two structurally separate mechanisms, and only one of them is developed so far.** Stage 1's central thesis is that Pisces is a second-order district — shaped not by the founding wound directly but by "the necessary excess of everyone else's particular... way of coping with it," catching whoever the other five direct-response districts' methods failed. The "New conflict thread" above develops this further, describing Pisces as "an unintentional census of the other districts' failure rates" and proposing ex-Scorpio, ex-Leo, and similar internal-Concordia-district populations as distinguishable communities worth developing. `District_Refugee_Diaspora_Composition.md`, however, describes a completely different population source: outer Tepenian cities, dominated overwhelmingly by Shirayuki (~87.7%) and Dome Fuji (~12.3%), arriving through ordinary top-3 district affinity rather than having failed any other Concordia district's own coping method first. Worth naming these as two genuinely separate mechanisms feeding Pisces's total population — one internal, still undeveloped beyond the conflict thread's proposal, and one external, now fully mapped — since the "census of failure rates" framing, read on its own, would incorrectly imply Pisces's whole population arrived by that route.

**Even Shirayuki, Pisces's overwhelmingly dominant population, shows a genuine two-track integration rather than the uniform "smooth fit" its sheer size might suggest.** At ~87.7% of Pisces's outer-city diaspora, Shirayuki is by a wide margin the most demographically dominant single contributor of any district covered in this pass. Yet the diaspora file's own account of its transplants isn't uniformly smooth: Cluster Suppers merge into Origin-Blind Suppers as "one of the rare Shirayuki customs that needs almost no translation at all," while Institute Placement Nights — Shirayuki's "open, name-based mentorship tradition" — cannot survive intact and re-emerges reworked into the district's own guarded secret-sharing circles, explicitly flagged as "a noticeably foreign adjustment for Shirayuki arrivals used to a warmer, more transparent welcome." Pisces's single largest population isn't simply "a good cultural match" — it's split internally between one register (communal eating, origin left unexamined) that fits almost perfectly and another (open mentorship, transparent welcome) that has to be actively reworked into something more guarded. Worth developing which of Pisces's Shirayuki-descended residents lean into which half of that split.

**Dome Fuji's Deepest Cold is a small, persistent exception to Pisces's own confirmed exemption from the "two-version civic pattern."** The "New finding" above, updated 2026-07-29, explicitly removes Pisces from the Gemini/Libra-style civic grouping — the Tolerance Pact isn't sustained by any deliberate, even tacit, institutional consensus, just structural inertia, making Pisces "the odd one out." Dome Fuji's own Deepest Cold observance — "the closest thing to a universal, mandatory communal event" back home, fixed-date and universal — persists unassimilated inside Pisces's own deliberately unscheduled, person-to-person Syncretic Religion, described in the diaspora file as "a small, recognizably foreign fixed point inside an otherwise deliberately informal spiritual calendar." That's a much smaller-scale version of exactly the tension the district's civic structure was just confirmed *not* to have. Worth a discussion question: is Deepest Cold's persistence a genuine minor exception proving Pisces's own informal rule, or a small enough seed that — if Dome Fuji's diaspora ever grew — could pull the district's own spiritual calendar toward exactly the kind of institutionalized, openly-scheduled recognition its civic culture has otherwise structurally avoided?

**Dome Fuji's near-total absence of Arcanet back home may make it unusually well pre-adapted to Pisces's own face-to-face information economy, not just its spiritual side.** Pisces's cultural texture is explicit that "access to significant Pisces operations runs on an information economy, not a credit economy... every transaction of consequence requires sharing something true about yourself." Dome Fuji is one of only a handful of Tepenian cities established as having essentially no functioning Arcanet presence, given its extreme-altitude isolation — meaning its pre-war daily life never had the digital shortcut around face-to-face trust and disclosure that most of Tepenia takes for granted. That's a plausible, currently unstated reason Dome Fuji's diaspora integrates as smoothly as it does on Pisces's spiritual side (the Wandering Shrine's unpredictable relocation, echoing the plateau crossing's own remoteness) despite the Deepest Cold friction noted above: a population already fluent in trust built through direct, unmediated contact rather than networked record may be unusually well-suited to the specific mechanic, not just the mood, of Pisces's own secrets-as-currency economy.
