# Libra — Developer Vision Notes

**Purpose:** A running record of the developer's own creative vision for this district — distinct from `District_Canon_Reference.md` (established facts) and the district's own two-stage deep dive (`Deep_Dives/08_Libra_Deep_Dive.md`). This file captures open questions asked, answers given, and anything newly established through direct conversation, so it can be referenced later and folded into the more formal Megasheet process when ready.

**Session date:** 2026-07-09

---

## Facts and factions presented at the start of this session

Recap of what was already established going in: Libra ("The Government District") is Concordia's diplomatic/governance district — a place that genuinely believes in fairness and has spent two centuries accumulating the institutional power necessary to enforce it, without quite reckoning that those two things aren't the same. Established texture: everything moves slowly, deliberately, by design (congenitally incapable of responding to anything in under six weeks), an unwritten language of political implication, a deliberately intimidating refined aesthetic (perfect symmetry, polished surfaces, rank signaled through clothing detail), and Harmony Plaza — perfectly, almost uncannily symmetrical. Also carries the Suspended Compact, the quietly-known secret that the district's own democratic provisions have technically been "suspended" under emergency powers since the war.

---

## The developer's vision

**Official — genuinely, thoroughly official, both in the people and the physical space.** Confirmed as both at once, not one or the other: the politicians and staff carry themselves with real conviction, and the place itself (architecture, presentation) reinforces the same feeling.

**No sense of superiority or condescension from the people there — instead, a sense that they sincerely believe in what they're doing.** Whether that sincerity reads as reassuring (competent, dedicated people who care) or unsettling (true believers who might not question themselves) was left genuinely uncertain — the developer described it as "hard to tell... might be a mix of both," not resolved one way or the other.

**Pace: brisk — like people are about to be late for something.** Worth flagging directly against the district's own established six-week-minimum institutional decision process: individual staff moving briskly through their day isn't necessarily a contradiction of the slow, deliberate review cycle itself, but it's a real nuance worth reconciling later rather than smoothing over. It's possible both are true simultaneously — busy, hustling people serving an institutionally glacial system.
