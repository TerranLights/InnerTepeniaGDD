# Cancer — Developer Vision Notes

**Purpose:** A running record of the developer's own creative vision for this district — distinct from `District_Canon_Reference.md` (established facts) and the district's own two-stage deep dive (`Deep_Dives/01_Cancer_Deep_Dive.md`). This file captures open questions asked, answers given, and anything newly established through direct conversation, so it can be referenced later and folded into the more formal Megasheet process when ready.

**Session date:** 2026-07-09

---

## Facts and factions presented at the start of this session

Recap of what was already established going in: Cancer is Concordia's nurturing/protective sanctuary district, designated as such from the beginning of the Second Interwar Period (late 2560s) — built to receive the exile's own traumatized founding population (war orphans, injured refugees, newly activated robots needing stabilization), not created as a response to the Long Night War. Established texture: architecture with no straight lines (they read as institutional), doors left unlocked within residential clusters as a warmth-and-vulnerability norm, an elaborate memorial tradition where the dead are maintained rather than simply remembered (specific chairs kept at dinner tables, a robot companion continuing a deceased human's daily routines for years), lullabies as a serious, mastered art form, a care-debt parallel economy in place of credits, and the Mother's Circuit — caregiving robots' informal shared emotional-data network that has drifted into something resembling surveillance. Also carries a hidden wound: the Overcrowding Decision (c. 2813), a post-war triage crisis its own archivists destroyed the records of.

---

## The developer's vision

**Curved, gentle corridors in a white-and-green palette.** The green isn't paint — it's actual living plantlife woven directly into the architecture. Confirmed as serving a dual purpose: partly functional (tied to the district's hydroponic systems) and partly for the psychological comfort of it, both at once, not one or the other.

**A soft, hazy, fog-like light with no actual fog.** This isn't a deliberate engineering choice or designed lighting effect — the impression is that the air in Cancer is simply like that, an ambient, unremarked quality of the district rather than something anyone built on purpose.

**The quality of care extended to strangers, confirmed specific.** Walking through Cancer as an unhurt, unknown visitor, people don't approach offering help — but there's a strong, felt impression that they would if you were hurt. Both humans and robots make genuine eye contact and wave as you pass. Care here isn't performative or hovering; it's present and available without needing to be triggered.

**A soundscape built around the district's own lullaby culture, made tangible.** While walking, there's someone singing softly in a nearby house — audible but far enough away that the words can't be made out. Not staged or centralized; just part of the ambient texture of the place.

**Pace: slow, but not extremely so.** Explicitly not "island slow" — a deliberate, unhurried rhythm, but not lethargic or empty. "Slow enough."
