# Aries — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Aries given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Aries_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-09.

---

## I. Aries operates in two distinct registers: steady baseline competence, and sudden, total crisis urgency

**Proposed:** given the established culture of overclocked heroism and "push it until it breaks" engineering, Aries' pace isn't one fixed thing — normal operation reads as steady and deliberate, competent people doing demanding but routine work, while a crisis (an overclock event, a cascade-failure risk) flips the entire district into total, all-hands urgency. The Overclock Martyrs and the Black Silence are both, definitionally, examples of the second register. The district's baseline Vision Notes impression (people absorbed in their own obligations, nobody rushing) describes register one; register two is what everyone in Aries is quietly, permanently braced for.

---

## II. Heat and noise intensity scale with proximity to the actual power generation

**Proposed:** consistent with the district's own established "extreme temperature gradients," the heat and industrial ambience confirmed in the Vision Notes plausibly aren't uniform — areas closest to the actual geothermal taps and reactor cores would run hotter and louder, while administrative and support areas further from the core generation would be comparatively (though still noticeably) milder. This gives the district genuine internal geography rather than one flat environmental condition throughout.

---

## III. Quiet self-respect extends to helping visitors — efficiently, not warmly

**Proposed:** given Aries' established "duty, not pride" ethos and blunt, unglamorous honesty, a worker encountering a visitor who visibly needed help would very plausibly act immediately and competently — this is, after all, a district built entirely around emergency response — but without warmth or extended engagement. Help would be rendered practically, then the worker would return to their own obligations. Consistent with the Vision Notes' own confirmed impression that nobody stops to acknowledge passersby: the exception is a genuine need, not a social greeting.

---

## IV. Demonym and Notable Figures

**Demonym — flagged, not simply defaulted.** The obvious formation, "Arian," carries an unfortunate real-world homophone (Aryan) that makes it a poor choice regardless of etymology. Proposed alternate: **Ariesian** — slightly awkward, but unambiguous, and worth deliberately choosing over the more natural-sounding option for this specific reason.

**Notable Figures, proposed:**
- **A leading voice of the Political Faction** *(placeholder, unnamed)* — someone who believes Aries' blackout leverage should have been exercised more decisively by now, given the district's outsized real power relative to its actual deference.
- **A senior Safety Engineer** *(placeholder, unnamed)* — someone who has spent years documenting preventable deaths within the Overclocker culture, viewed by the dominant culture as necessary but unglamorous, exactly the role `District_Unity_of_Opposites.md` already establishes as keeping the martyrdom culture viable long-term.

## V. Architecture (Phase 1)

**Proposed.** Aries's architecture is organized by a single principle: **everything is arranged around access to
the thing that must never stop.**

**Baseline:** heavy industrial fabric — poured structure, exposed run, and service galleries wide enough to move
equipment through at speed. **Nothing here is finished in the decorative sense**, and this is not austerity; it
is that a surface which cannot be opened quickly is a liability. Panels come off. Walls have hatches. **The
district looks unfinished to visitors and is in fact highly optimized.**

**The thermal gradient is the district's real geography** (Finding II): proximity to generation determines
everything — how hot a block runs, how loud it is, how it is built, and who lives there. **Near-core housing is
not slum housing.** It is where the people who must reach the plant fastest live, and living there carries real
standing, which is exactly inverted from how a visitor reads it.

**Purpose-built:** muster points at fixed intervals, deliberately over-provisioned; **hard-boundary
compartmentation**, since an enclosed city cannot let a fire in a generation hall become a fire in a district;
and **the galleries** — the wide service runs that double as the district's main social space (Finding XII).

**What is conspicuously absent: any building whose purpose is to make a decision.** Aries has no chamber, no
forum, no hall of records built for deliberation. Its administrative functions occupy converted service space.
**A district that cannot weigh competing claims (Finding XVIII) never built anywhere to do it**, and nobody has
ever noticed the omission because nobody has ever needed the room.

## VI. Sensory Profile (Phase 1)

**Proposed. Sound:** a continuous low mechanical floor that residents stop hearing within weeks and visitors
cannot stop hearing at all. **The significant sound is a change in it** — Ariesians read the district's health
by ear and will stop mid-sentence at a shift in pitch that a visitor did not register. **The Black Silence is
named for the one thing that has never otherwise happened here**, and the name is exact: what was terrifying
was not a noise.

**Smell:** hot metal, mineral steam, lubricant, and ozone near the heavy runs. Clean rather than foul — this is
a maintained industrial environment, not a decaying one — with a distinct hot-dust note nearer the core that
residents can date their childhood by.

**Feel:** heat as the district's permanent physical fact, and **the gradient is felt within a single walk**.
Air is dry where Scorpio's is wet. Surfaces are warm to the touch. **Vibration is constant and is the thing
residents actually miss when they leave** — several accounts of Ariesians sleeping badly elsewhere in Concordia
for want of it.

**First impressions:** competent, busy, and unwelcoming without any hostility. Per Finding III, nobody
acknowledges a passerby, and **a visitor in genuine trouble is helped immediately, efficiently, and without
warmth**, after which the helper returns to work. Visitors consistently misread this as coldness. It is the
opposite — it is the district's actual ethic operating exactly as intended.

## VII. Export Culture (Phase 1)

**Proposed.** Aries's exports are the most materially consequential in Concordia and the worst-compensated,
and Finding XVIII explains why.

**(1) Power itself**, which is not a cultural export but is the fact under everything else — including the
district's blackout leverage and its inability to convert that leverage into terms (Finding XVIII).

**(2) Marked components and consumables.** **Fused from Solingen, researched for this pass:** the real
protection there is a **legally-defined designation** requiring that every essential manufacturing stage be
performed inside the city, enforced by an ordinance that specifies precisely when the name may be used — and
blades were marked from the 16th century with *"me fecit Solingen"*, **"Solingen made me," the object speaking
in the first person about where it came from.**

Aries has the same institution. **A component carrying the district's mark is trusted absolutely across
Concordia**, the mark attests that every stage was done inside the district by a marked trade, and **the mark is
worded from the object's side, not the maker's.** See Finding XVIII for why this matters structurally: **the
mark is the only form of valuation Aries is actually capable of.** It cannot price what it makes. It can certify
what it is. Someone else sets the number.

**(3) People who are good in an emergency**, on loan rather than permanently — the district's most requested
export after power, and the one it gives away most freely.

## VIII. Religious/Philosophical Landscape — the load, and the flame that went out (Phase 2)

**Proposed. Applying the Naming technique** (`02_Phase_2` §8): Aries already uses religious register for
industrial facts, in canon, without anyone naming what it asserts. **"The Overclock Martyrs"** is not a metaphor
the district reaches for occasionally; it is what these people are called. **"Duty, not pride"** is its own
stated ethic.

**The premise, stated as a resident would agree with it:**

> **The load is real, it does not care whether anyone is ready, and someone has to take it. Whoever takes it is
> not owed an explanation and does not ask for one.**

That is the district's actual theology, and naming it does the usual work of making unrelated facts into one
thing: it explains why help is rendered to strangers instantly and coldly (Finding III), why the Safety
Engineers are simultaneously respected and unglamorous, why nobody in Aries describes what they do as brave, and
why the martyr culture is not recruitment — **nobody is persuaded into it, because the premise holds that
persuasion is not involved.**

**Worth being precise, because it is easy to write this wrong:** this is not a death cult and Aries is not
morbid. The overwhelming majority of the district's work is done safely by people who go home. The martyrs are
few, remembered exactly, and **the district's own view is that each one represents a failure of something
upstream** — which is the position the Safety Engineers hold and why they are tolerated rather than fought.

**The shrine object, and it is the best thing in this pass.** *(Fused from Baku's Ateshgah and Yanar Dag,
researched for this pass.)* The real site is a fire temple built on a natural gas vent whose flames were held
sacred and burned perpetually — **until 1969, when they went out, because the surrounding gas extraction had
depleted the reserve that fed them. The eternal flame is now maintained on a piped supply.**

**Aries has exactly this.** A perpetual flame that predates the district's build-out, which **the district's own
extraction extinguished**, and which is now kept burning on a deliberate feed. Everyone knows. It is not
concealed and there is no pretence that the flame is what it was.

**What it means is genuinely contested inside the district, and should stay contested:**
- **The majority reading** is honesty — *we keep our debts where we can see them.* The fed flame is a standing
  admission, and putting it out would be a lie of omission.
- **A real minority reading** is that maintaining it is the evasion: that a flame you are feeding is a monument
  to nothing, and the district keeps it lit because extinguishing it would require someone to say out loud what
  was spent.
- **The Tally** (Finding XV) hold the second view loudly, which is one reason they are disliked.

**No marginal imported belief is proposed.** Aries's premise has no room for one — it makes no cosmological
claim to disagree with, and offers no consolation to compete against.

## IX. Fashion (Phase 2)

**Proposed. General population first, per `00b`** — and the failure mode to avoid here is obvious and specific:
**most Ariesians are not overclockers.** The Overclock Martyrs are a small, remembered minority. Writing the
district's dress from them would be Leo's performer error in a different costume.

**The baseline is heat-rated working clothing and it is worn by nearly everyone, nearly all the time**:
flame-resistant layers, close-cut so nothing catches, heavy footwear, sleeves that fasten. **Ordinary residents
wear working dress off-shift too**, not from affectation but because the district is hot, the gradient is real,
and there is no cultural occasion that calls for anything else. **Aries has no formal wear**, and visiting
delegations find this either refreshing or insulting depending on temperament.

**The mark is the whole of the district's ornament** *(Solingen fusion, Finding VII)*. A worker's trade and
stage-qualification are worn plainly — at the collar, on the cuff — and **these are read fluently by every
resident and are invisible to outsiders.** This is the district's entire status vocabulary: not rank, not
wealth, but *what you are qualified to be responsible for.*

**Consistent with `District_Armor_Augmentations_and_Protection.md`'s Aries set** as the armored descendant of
ordinary heat-rated working dress, rather than the general population's civilian wardrobe being derived from
combat gear.

**One deliberate exception, correctly scoped:** the trades most exposed to heat maintain heavier, more visibly
specialized kit. **That is one profession's version and not the district's default** — stated explicitly here
because this is precisely where this file would otherwise repeat a recorded project-wide error.

## X. Arcanet Culture — operational truth, and nothing else (Phase 3)

**Proposed.** Aries's relationship to Arcanet is unlike the four districts covered so far and is defined by one
requirement: **the district's information has to be correct right now, and stale information here kills people.**

**Its Arcanet use is overwhelmingly telemetry and operational state** — plant condition, load, thermal margin,
who is where, what is isolated. **Near-real-time and near-total.** Within the operational domain, Aries is the
least private district in Concordia: what you are doing, what you have isolated, and what you signed for is
visible to anyone who needs it, and nobody experiences this as surveillance.

**And almost nothing else is recorded.** Aries keeps poor institutional memory outside the operational layer —
few narratives, thin history, little correspondence. **This is not carelessness; it is the district's time-deficit made
infrastructural** *(substrate: Saturn in fall)* (Finding XVIII): a district with no faculty for time does not naturally build archives.
**Whatever Aries knows about its own past, it knows because someone remembers it or because a choir sings it**
(Finding XIV).

> **The permit is the district's single most important document, and its handling is the district's greatest
> weakness.** *(Fused from Piper Alpha and the Cullen findings, researched for this pass.)* The real disaster's
> mechanism was that **a pressure safety valve had been removed by one crew and the incoming crew did not know**
> — a permit-to-work system that existed, and was, in the inquiry's own words, **"habitually departed from."**
>
> **Aries has a permit system and it is habitually departed from**, and — critically — **not by rebels.** It is
> departed from by competent, conscientious people under time pressure, each of whom believes this specific
> exception is justified, and each of whom is usually right. **The failure is never the exception. It is the
> handover** — what one shift knows and the next does not. This is Aries's characteristic catastrophe, it needs
> no villain, and it is the direct operational expression of a district that runs on individual initiative
> and has no faculty for continuity *(substrate: Sun exalted, Saturn in fall)*.

**Relationship to Gemini:** Aries is a heavy consumer of bandwidth and a negligible contributor of content. To
Gemini it is a district that transmits enormous volumes and says nothing.

## XI. Visitor Experience and Visitor-to-Resident Transition — with the conflict geometry written in (Phase 4)

**Proposed.** *(Conflict geometry is written into this phase per the Mode B rule, `00e` §11b.2, rather than
deferred to a substrate section.)*

**Visitor experience:** unwelcoming without hostility, per Finding III. **The single thing a visitor must
understand is that they are a hazard** — an unqualified body in a space where everyone else's position and
competence is known. Escort is normal, is not suspicion, and is explained to nobody.

**Visitor-to-resident transition: the mark.** *(Solingen fusion.)* Residency in Aries's own terms is **being
qualified to be responsible for something.** The transition is neither social nor temporal — it is the moment a
newcomer holds a stage-qualification and can sign for work. Before that they are present; after it they are
counted.

**This is unusually meritocratic and unusually brutal in the same motion.** Nobody cares where an arrival came
from, what their history is, or who vouches for them — the qualification is the whole of it, and it is
genuinely open. **And a resident who cannot hold a qualification has no route to standing at all**, which is
the same fact seen from the other side (Finding XIX).

### Conflict geometry

- **The Government District — the district Aries is set against, and the one it cannot do without.** *(Substrate
  §8; `F_Rulerships.md` §5-6.)* **Both of Aries's structural deficits are concentrated there** — valuation and
  patient limit-setting are precisely what that district is best at. **So Aries is dependent on its own opposite
  number for the two capacities it lacks, and being set against it is exactly what prevents it from
  accepting them.** It cannot price its own output; the Government District can, does, and is resented for it.
  Aries experiences this as being valued by people who have never carried anything. The Government District
  experiences Aries as incapable of stating terms. **Both are correct**, which is why this has never resolved
  and is the most consequential relationship in either district's politics.
- **The Sanctuary and the Yards — the two hardest frictions, and they are not the same kind.** With **the
  Sanctuary**, the friction is quiet and mostly invisible: two districts whose entire self-understanding is
  *taking on burden for others*, with no shared vocabulary for it — one absorbs slowly and permanently, the
  other absorbs suddenly and totally, and each privately finds the other's version self-indulgent. With **the
  Yards**, it is loud and constant, because the Yards is built on exactly the patient, scheduled discipline
  Aries structurally cannot sustain, and the two have to work together on physical infrastructure daily.
  **The Yards thinks Aries is reckless. Aries thinks the Yards is slow. Neither is wrong.**
- **Natural allies: Leo and the Frostlands.** Leo is the most comfortable relationship Aries has — both are
  loud, both act first, and Leo is one of very few districts that finds Aries's manner unremarkable. The
  Frostlands shares the frontier register. **The Circuit and the Labs** are easy working partners rather than
  cultural ones.

## XII. Ordinary Daily Life (Phase 5)

**Governing test applied:** nobody in Aries is in a crisis most days. Finding I already establishes that the
district's baseline is *steady, deliberate, competent work* and that crisis is the second register. **This
Finding is about the first one**, which is the great majority of the district's actual life.

**Routines.** Shift work dominates and structures everything — meals, sleep, childcare, social life all
organized around rotations rather than around a day. **The galleries** (Finding V) are the social spine:
residents eat, wait, argue and pass time in the wide service runs, and a gallery at shift change is the closest
thing Aries has to a town square. **They also remain live equipment routes, and both facts are held at once by a
practice the district is genuinely proud of: on the clearing signal a full gallery empties completely, in
seconds, without anyone hurrying** — plates lifted, benches folded, the whole room against the walls before the
sound has finished. Children learn it before they learn to read. **Visitors who have seen it once describe it
more often than anything else in the district**, and Ariesians find that faintly puzzling, since it is simply
what you do. *(Added 2026-08-29 per the Review Panel — the Line Maintainer's objection that a run you must move
equipment through at speed cannot also be a dining hall.)* Many households run on staggered shifts and see each other in overlaps.

**Mundane concerns.** Whether a qualification renewal will come through before a rotation. The permanent
low-grade domestic problem of heat — keeping food, keeping things from warping, keeping a room cool enough to
sleep in near the core. Somebody's kit going missing from a gallery locker. **The specific irritation of a
component being sent back by another district over a paperwork question**, which Ariesians find both baffling
and insulting.

**Personal struggles.** A worker whose qualification lapsed after an injury and who has not found a way to say
so. Someone who is competent, reliable, and simply not fast in the second register, and who knows the district
has no word for that but has definitely noticed. A parent on opposite rotation from their child for a year at a
time. **Someone who thinks the Tally are right and cannot say so at work.**

**Escapism and downtime.** Choir, overwhelmingly — see Finding XIV, and it is genuinely the district's main
leisure activity rather than a ceremonial one. Beyond that: gallery card games with long-running scores, sleep
as a serious cultural priority, and travel to Leo for a night by people who would not admit to enjoying it.

## XIII. Thematic Breadth Catalog (Phase 6)

**Extends `District_Canon_Reference.md`'s Community Infrastructure. Real-World Basis note:** four picks
researched at concrete level for this pass, selected by difference rather than tier.

**Named places and landmarks:**
- **The Fed Flame** — the perpetual flame the district's own extraction extinguished, now maintained on a
  deliberate supply (Finding VIII). *Fused from Ateshgah/Yanar Dag.* Small, unimpressive, and the most

- **The Long Gallery** — the widest of the service runs, and the district's de facto civic space. Not built as
  one; nobody has ever proposed making it official, and any proposal to would be resented.
- **The Marking Hall** — where stage-qualifications are conferred and recorded. *Fused from Solingen's guild
  structure, where forging, hardening, grinding and polishing were each separate crafts with separate guild
  privileges.* Aries's trades are likewise split by stage rather than by plant, and each has its own mark.
- **The Silence Board** — the district's memorial, listing the Overclock Martyrs and the dead of the Black
  Silence. Plain, exhaustively accurate, and updated with the same care as a permit.

**Physical things:** a **stage-mark** worn at collar or cuff (Findings VII and IX); a **permit** as a physical
signed object with a handover procedure (Finding X); a **choir book** belonging to a specific choir and its
specific incident (Finding XIV); **heat-rated working kit** with a personal repair history.

**People (role/archetype placeholders):** a Safety Engineer *(already canon)*; a marking-hall examiner whose
signature is the district's real gatekeeping instrument; a choir secretary who administers the bereavement fund
and is therefore, quietly, one of the most powerful people in the district (Finding XIV); a gallery cook.

**Settings:** the gallery at shift change; the ten minutes of a handover, which the district treats with more
formality than any ceremony it has; the Fed Flame late, when the arguments happen.

## XIV. Cuisine, Music, and Arts — the district that sings its dead (Phase 7)

**Proposed.**

**(a) Food.** Aries eats on rotation, not on a day. **The canteen and the gallery are the district's real
kitchens**, and household cooking is comparatively rare — not from poverty but because shift patterns make a
family meal a scheduling problem. Food is hot, dense, salted heavily *(a district that sweats)*, served fast,
and available at every hour because there is no hour at which nobody is working.

**The one genuinely local practice: food is kept warm and waiting for whoever comes off a bad shift**, without
anyone being asked and without it being remarked on. **This is the district's affection**, and it is close to
the only form it takes publicly.

**(b) Robot cuisine and siligel.** Siligel provision here is **continuous rather than scheduled** — available at
every gallery station, unregulated, and taken standing. The local peculiarity is that **Aries makes no
distinction whatsoever between human and robot catering arrangements**: both populations are fed on the same
logic, at the same points, on the same non-schedule, because the district organizes intake around *shift
position* rather than around what is being consumed. **Glitch-Coolant** sits on the working-class potency side
and is drunk after shift in the galleries, in company, quickly.

**(c) Music — the choirs, and this is the district's real cultural institution.** *(Fused from Welsh coalfield
choral culture, researched for this pass.)* The real tradition is unusually specific and every part of it
transfers: choirs formed **out of the enforced idleness of unemployed miners**; rooted in **participatory
harmonized singing** rather than performance; carrying the memory of named disasters; and — the detail that
makes it — **the Ynysowen choir founded *after* the Aberfan disaster, as part of rehabilitation and fundraising.**

**Aries's choirs are founded after incidents, named for them, and outlive everyone who founded them.**

- **Membership is general and enormous.** This is not a specialist pursuit; a large fraction of the district
  sings, and a choir is attached to a plant or a stage-trade rather than to a neighborhood.
- **The repertoire is commemorative and the accuracy matters.** A choir's own incident is sung correctly — names
  in order, what actually happened — and getting it wrong is a serious offense. **Aries keeps thin archives
  (Finding X); the choirs are where its history actually lives**, and this is the single most important
  consequence of the finding.
- **And the choirs collect.** *(Directly from the real practice, where singing raised funds as well as spirits.)*
  Each choir administers a bereavement fund for the families of its incident's dead. **This is Aries's actual
  compensation mechanism**, and Finding XVIII explains why it has to be: a district that cannot price what it
  loses cannot institutionally compensate for it, so the money moves through singing instead. **The choir
  secretary is therefore one of the quietly most powerful people in the district**, and nobody has ever
  described the arrangement as a welfare system.

> **Cross-district contrast, stated inline so the next reader does not merge these** *(Gate 6b)*. **Aries and
> Taurus both have general-population singing, and they are not the same institution.** Taurus sings **quietly,
> indoors, in close harmony, at conversational volume, about lineage** — the continuity of a household line.
> Aries sings **loudly, massed, in public, about a specific day and a specific list of dead.** Taurus sings a
> line; Aries sings a date. Neither could use the other's repertoire, venue, or volume. *(For completeness: Leo
> barely plays and listens expertly; Scorpio speaks simultaneously so that nobody is individually audible.)*

**(d) Arts.** Beyond choir, Aries's making is **functional and marked** — tools, fittings, and repairs executed
better than they need to be, signed with a stage-mark. **The district's aesthetic principle is that a thing
should be legible to the person who has to work on it next.** Ornament that obscures a fastening is not just
ugly here, it is faintly immoral. The one exception is memorial work, which is plain to the point of severity
and is the only thing in Aries anybody would call beautiful without embarrassment.

## XV. Human-Robot Relations, Counterculture, and Private Life (Phase 7)

**Proposed.**

**(a) Human-Robot Relations — the local inflection is that the load does not fall evenly, and the district's own
ethic prevents it from examining that.** The axis that matters in Aries is not species; it is **who takes the
load.** But the two are not independent, and this is the finding.

**A robot can absorb thermal, radiological and atmospheric exposures that would kill a human.** In a district
whose defining practice is stepping into conditions to keep the supply up, that means **the overclock burden
falls disproportionately on robot workers** — not by policy, not by anyone's decision, but because the person
who *can* go in is the one who goes in, every time, and going in is what the district honors.

**And Aries's own premise forecloses the examination** (Finding VIII): *whoever takes the load is not owed an
explanation and does not ask for one.* An ethic built to prevent recrimination also prevents accounting.
**Nobody in Aries is exploiting robot workers. Nobody in Aries is looking, either**, and the district would
experience the question as an insult to the people it commemorates.

**The Overclock Martyrs are therefore disproportionately robots**, and the Silence Board says so plainly to
anyone who reads the names and knows how to read them. **The Tally read them. It is most of why they exist.**

**(b) Counterculture — the Tally.** *(Derived from Step C per Mode B: what does the district require of
everyone, and who will not give it?)*

**Aries requires that the load be absorbed without being counted.** "Duty, not pride" forbids the complaint, and
the premise forbids the invoice. **The counterculture is the people who keep books anyway.**

The Tally maintain unofficial ledgers: what Aries supplied and what it was actually paid; what deferred
maintenance cost and when; who died, in what conditions, and how the load was distributed by population. **They
are not a strike movement and they do not want the blackout leverage used** — that is the Political Faction
(canon), and the two are frequently confused and mutually irritated. **The Tally want the numbers to exist.**

- **The mainstream finds them distasteful rather than dangerous**, and the reason is precise: **counting sounds
  like complaining**, and complaining is the one thing the district's ethic actually forbids. To say *this cost
  us more than we were paid* is heard as *I want to be thanked*, which no Ariesian will tolerate being heard as.
- **"The Tally" started as a mild insult and has been adopted.**
- **They are performing the district's own missing faculty as an unofficial practice** (Finding XVIII), which is
  what makes them structurally interesting rather than merely oppositional — and it is also why the Government
  District quietly finds them the most legible people in Aries, which does the Tally no favors at home.

> **Cross-district contrast** *(Gate 6b)*. **Aries's counterculture is the only one of the four that *adds* a
> practice rather than refusing one.** Taurus's decline the record; Leo's decline recognition; Scorpio's decline
> the silence — all three are refusals. **The Tally insist on doing something the district does not do at all.**
> Different in kind, not just in subject.

**(c) Private Life.** The integration bargain is the plainest in Concordia: **qualify, and you are in, fully and
without reference to anything else about you.** No vouching, no history, no cell, no record of where you came
from — Aries is genuinely the least interested district in Concordia in a newcomer's past, and for people
arriving from places that hold their history against them, that is not a small thing. **Several of Concordia's
quieter diaspora populations are here for exactly that reason.**

**What it costs:** everything private is genuinely private, because nobody asks — including when someone is
struggling. **The district that will run into a fire for a stranger will not ask a colleague of ten years how
they are**, and does not experience this as a contradiction. Both behaviors come from the same premise.

**Generationally:** children grow up on rotation, raised substantially by the galleries and by whoever is off
shift, which produces an unusually self-sufficient and unusually unsentimental adult population — and a
recurring, rarely-voiced complaint from Ariesians who left, that they were well looked after and never once
asked.

## XVI. Municipal Holidays (Phase 7)

**Proposed — three.**

**1. The Reading** *(annual, solemn, choir-led, and the district's most important day).* The Silence Board is
read aloud in full — every name, by the choir attached to each incident. It takes a long time and the length is
the point. **No speeches.** The district's premise (Finding VIII) forbids anyone drawing a lesson from it out
loud, and attempts by other districts to send dignitaries have gone badly.

**2. The Shutdown** *(irregular, scheduled months ahead, and genuinely a carnival).* When a plant goes down for
planned maintenance, the district throws a party. **The joke everyone is in on is that a district which cannot
schedule anything treats a successfully scheduled outage as a triumph** — and it is, because a planned shutdown
is the one occasion on which Aries beat its own worst tendency (Finding XVIII). Galleries are cleared, the
choirs sing non-commemorative material for once, and it is the loudest, least dignified, most enjoyed event in
the district's year. **Other districts are invited and generally do not understand what is being celebrated.**

**3. Marking Day** *(quarterly, small, extremely emotional).* New stage-qualifications are conferred. Brief,
undecorated, and the closest thing Aries has to a rite of passage — for many residents the day they became a
person the district counts (Finding XI). **Families come. Nobody makes a speech about it either.**

## XVII. Robot-Specific Culture — the capstone (Phase 8)

**Scope note**, as with the four completed districts: works from Aries's established robot material plus
Tepenia-Wide Robot Culture Canon, not a full *Robot Universals* triage.

**Lens:** theme/role-based — Aries's robot population organizes around the district's defining vocation,
**being the one who can go in.**

**Finding 1 — The overclock burden falls disproportionately on robots, and the district's own ethic prevents it
from noticing.** *(Inheritance: genuinely emergent, robot-only. Iceberg: Deep Culture.)* See Finding XV(a). This
is the anchor, and it is the most uncomfortable finding in the pass.

**Finding 2 — Robots are the district's continuity across the handover, which is exactly where Aries fails.**
*(Inheritance: genuinely emergent. Iceberg: Deep Culture.)* Finding X establishes that Aries's characteristic
catastrophe is the shift handover — what one crew knows and the next does not. **A robot who does not go off
shift is the institutional memory the district structurally lacks**, and long-serving robot workers function as
the real permit continuity regardless of what the paperwork says. **This is load-bearing and undocumented**: the
district's actual safety margin is substantially a robot memory, and no procedure anywhere acknowledges it.

**Finding 3 — Kinship is rooted in having been present at the same incident.** *(Inheritance: adapted pool-draw.
Iceberg: Deep Culture.)* Per the Kinship reminder to start from the actual shared experience: not shared
activation, model, or plant — **the specific event.** Which means robot Kinship in Aries maps exactly onto the
choirs (Finding XIV), and robot workers are disproportionately choir members for that reason.

**Finding 4 — The mark is held identically by both populations, and this is Aries's real egalitarianism.**
*(Inheritance: adapted pool-draw. Iceberg: Surface over Deep.)* A stage-qualification is a stage-qualification.
There is no robot register and no human register. In a city where most districts have *some* differential
practice, **Aries's total indifference on this point is conspicuous** — and it comes from indifference rather
than principle, which is very much in character.

**Consistency checks:**
- **Human-Robot Relations Baseline:** consistent with the egalitarian, robot-skewed baseline. Finding 1 is an
  asymmetry of **physical capacity and consequent exposure**, not of standing — and the district's failure is
  one of attention, not of respect.
- **Fragmentation Matrix:** Aries's Bond/Grief tagging is **not confirmed in this pass** and is deliberately not
  guessed at. Flagged.
- **Robot Elementals & Solar Symbols:** unconfirmed for districts, as with the previous four.

**Swap Test:** tested against **Leo** — chosen per the sharpened rule as the partner most likely to survive:
Aries's own natural ally, same temperament family, comparably loud, comparably status-conscious. **Findings 1-3
fail the swap immediately and correctly.** Leo has no equivalent of the overclock burden, no handover-critical
operational continuity, and its Kinship is already established as running on shared *recognition-climb* rather
than shared incident. **Finding 4 is the weakest** — indifference to population in a qualification system could
plausibly be written elsewhere — and holds here only because it follows from a district-wide indifference that

## XVIII. Capability profile — Aries cannot price what it gives away, and both of its deficits live next door (Substrate)

**Proposed — the pass's primary generator, and the first complete four-term capability row in the project.**
*(Substrate: `F_Rulerships.md` §5-6 D5 row.)* Taurus had one absence, Leo two, Scorpio no amplified faculty.
**Aries has all four terms filled**, which makes it the first district whose profile is a balance rather than a
gap — and the balance is brutal.

- **Structurally strong at force and initiative.** It acts, immediately, without needing authorization.
- **Structurally strong at producing named individual authority.** *Someone always steps forward and signs.*
  Aries never has a leadership vacuum in a crisis, ever.
- **Structurally deficient in valuation.** It cannot weigh competing claims against each other.
- **Structurally deficient in limit, patience, and time.** Deferred reward is the capacity it despises most.

**The characteristic institutional failure follows precisely: it cannot price or ration what it produces, and it
cannot maintain a schedule.** Power is allocated to whoever pushes hardest, because there is no faculty for
adjudicating between two legitimate claims. Preventive maintenance is deferred until failure, because the whole
value of preventive maintenance is realized later.

**Which explains, at a stroke, three things already in canon that have never had a mechanism:**

1. **Why the blackout leverage is never used.** Canon has a Political Faction arguing Aries should have
   exercised its enormous real power by now, and canon has the district deferring anyway. **The reason is not
   timidity — it is that Aries cannot work out what its leverage is worth.** It can supply and it can cut off.
   It has no middle setting, because the middle setting is a negotiated price and pricing is the faculty it
   lacks. **A district that cannot name a number cannot bargain, so it does the only two things it can do.**
2. **Why the Overclock Martyrs exist.** Not a values choice and not recruitment — **it is what a district with
   no capacity for deferred reward produces under load.** The martyr culture is the human cost of the district's
   scheduling deficit, arriving one person at a time.
3. **Why the Safety Engineers are "necessary but unglamorous."** They are doing the one thing the district
   structurally cannot do, on its behalf, permanently. **They are not disliked. They are simply illegible** —
   the district has no register in which their work reads as heroic, because their success looks like nothing
   happening.

**And the geometry makes it worse in a way no other district has faced.** *(This is the genuinely new structural
shape.)* **Both of Aries's missing faculties are concentrated in a single other district — the Government
District — where valuation is at home and patient limit-setting is amplified.** The one place in Concordia that
is structurally excellent at exactly what Aries lacks **is the district Aries is set against.**

**So Aries depends on its own opposite number for the two things it cannot do, and that very antagonism is
precisely what stops it accepting them.** The Government District prices Aries's output because somebody must, and Aries
experiences this as being valued by people who have never carried anything — a complaint that is emotionally
true and structurally beside the point. **Neither district is behaving badly. The arrangement is simply
unbearable to one of them and invisible to the other.**

**The one valuation Aries *can* perform is attestation** (Findings VII, IX, XI). It cannot say what a thing is
worth; it can say, with total authority, what a thing **is** — who made it, to what standard, at which stage.
**The mark is a district's entire economic vocabulary compressed into a statement of fact.** Someone else
supplies the number, and that dependency is the deficit in its most everyday form.

**Shadow proportion** (`00d`): **Aries works, and the city is alive because of it.** Its people are competent,
decent, and will help a stranger instantly. Its baseline is steady rather than frantic. **The shadow is that a
district which cannot price what it gives away also cannot notice what it is losing** — not in power, in people
— and its own ethic forbids the accounting that would reveal it. *(Discoverability: invisible on arrival; it
surfaces only by comparing what Aries supplies against what it receives, over time, deliberately — which is
exactly what the Tally do and why they are unwelcome.)*

## XIX. Gate 9 finding — Aries can only recognize a need that arrives as an emergency (Substrate)

**Proposed. Produced by the asymmetry check** (`00c` Gate 9), run first against the pre-Plan findings per
`00e` §11b.

**Finding III establishes that a worker encountering a visitor in visible need acts immediately and
competently, then returns to their obligations.** It wrote the emergency case only. **The reverse case is
unwritten and is the more common one.**

**Aries has no register for slow need.** A person who is not in danger but is not managing — a lapsed
qualification after an injury (Finding XII), a household on opposite rotations for a year, someone who is
reliable but simply not fast in the second register — **presents nothing the district's machinery is built to
detect.** It is not that Aries ignores them. **It is that the district's entire apparatus for recognizing that
something is wrong is calibrated to sudden change**, in exactly the way its residents read the plant by
listening for a shift in pitch (Finding VI).

**And Finding I's two registers complete the trap.** The district flips into total urgency for a crisis and
returns to steady competence afterward. **There is no third register**, and slow deterioration lives precisely
in the gap between the two.

**Combined with Finding XVIII this is the district's specific cruelty, and it requires nobody cruel:** the same
time-and-patience deficit that defers maintenance until failure defers *people* until failure. Aries will respond
magnificently to a collapse it could have prevented, in a person exactly as in a plant, **and will experience
its own response as evidence that the district takes care of its own.**

**Design hook, no villain required:** a resident visibly deteriorating for two years in full view of colleagues
who like and respect them, none of whom has done anything wrong, and every one of whom would run into a fire for
them tomorrow.

## XX. Contradiction reconciled, and the shape of the district's future (Substrate)

**Proposed.**

**The contradiction.** The substrate places Aries in the **initiating** modality — the one that opens, rushes
in, clears out the old, and does not endure. Canon says something that looks incompatible: Aries's baseline is
*steady, deliberate, competent, routine* (Finding I), and its residents are unhurried and absorbed in
obligations, not charging about.

**Both are true, per the both-are-true shape** (`00e` §7 — *one disposition, two objects*): **Aries initiates
against events, not against time.** It does not start projects, campaigns, or reforms; it starts *responses.*
The steadiness of the baseline is not a contradiction of the initiating temperament — **it is what an initiating
temperament looks like while it is waiting**, which Finding I already described exactly without naming it as
such: *"what everyone in Aries is quietly, permanently braced for."*

**Which yields the district's real limitation, and it is not a moral one: Aries cannot begin anything that does
not already have a cause.** It has no mechanism for initiating an unforced change — no faculty for deciding, in
a calm year, to do something differently. **Every change in Aries's history has been downstream of an incident**,
including its choirs (Finding XIV) and, on the Tally's reading, its entire safety apparatus. This is why the
Political Faction has never converted its argument into action, and why the district's reformers are
structurally dependent on something going wrong.

**Mined from the substrate's source gaps** *(per the 2026-08-29 rule that a hole in the sources is often the
district's mechanism)*: the corpus's material on this temperament is almost entirely about *individuals acting*,
with **near-nothing on what it does while nothing is happening.** That absence is the district's own condition.
**Aries has an extraordinarily well-developed culture of response and an underdeveloped culture of ordinary
time**, which is precisely why its leisure (Finding XII) is either commemorative or nothing, why the Shutdown
(Finding XVI) is celebrated so disproportionately, and why "duty, not pride" gives residents no vocabulary at
all for a good year.

## XXI. Panel-derived — the district forbids counting the living and meticulously counts the dead (Review Panel)

**Proposed. Raised by the Devout**, who takes Finding VIII's premise seriously and noticed that the district
violates it in public, permanently, and has never remarked on this.

**The premise says: whoever takes the load is not owed an explanation and does not ask for one.** That is why
the Tally are found distasteful (Finding XV) — counting sounds like complaining, and complaining is the one
thing the ethic forbids.

**And yet the Silence Board is an exhaustive, meticulously maintained account.** Every name. What happened.
Updated with the same care as a permit (Finding XIII), read aloud in full once a year with no speeches
(Finding XVI). **The district that will not let you count what a living person gave keeps a perfect ledger of
what the dead did.**

**Neither practice is going to yield, and the reason is that they are not actually in conflict from inside.**
The distinction the district draws without ever articulating it is that **an account of the dead cannot be a
request.** A living person's ledger is heard as a claim — *pay me, thank me, notice me.* A dead person's cannot
be, so it is safe, and the district pours into it all the exactness it forbids itself everywhere else.

**Which reframes the Tally entirely, and this is the finding's real value:** they are not importing an alien
practice into Aries. **They are extending a practice the district already performs, to people who are still
alive** — and that is precisely why the objection to them is so strong and so hard for anyone to articulate.
The mainstream is not defending the absence of records. It is defending the rule that **you may be counted once
you can no longer benefit from it.**

**Design consequence:** the most dangerous thing a Tally member can say in public is not a number. It is *"read
his name while he can still hear it."*

## XXII. Panel-derived — the choirs are the district's archive, and archives drift (Review Panel)

**Proposed. Raised by the Long-Lived Robot**, from the one position in the district that can check.

Finding X establishes that Aries keeps almost no institutional memory outside its operational layer. Finding XIV
establishes that its history therefore lives in what the choirs sing, that each choir's own incident is sung
correctly, and that **getting it wrong is a serious offense.**

**Both are true and they cannot stay true across 250 years.** An oral tradition with no external record to check
against does not stay accurate; it stays *coherent*, which is a different property. Names shift position for
metre. A confused sequence resolves into a clean one. A death that was nobody's fault acquires a cause, because
a cause is easier to sing.

**Nobody is lying, and the seriousness about accuracy is genuine** — which is what makes this work. Each
generation sings faithfully what it was taught, and the drift is invisible from inside precisely because
fidelity to the teaching is the thing being policed. **The district has confused faithfulness with accuracy,
and has no instrument that could tell them apart.**

**Except one.** A robot who was present at an incident and is still present now can hear the difference between
what is sung and what happened. **This is an uncomfortable position and most of them stay in it quietly**, for
reasons that are entirely in character: correcting a choir means telling a district that keeps no records that
its only record is wrong, in the one register where accuracy is a point of honour, about its own dead. Some have
done it. It has not always gone well.

**And it compounds Finding XV(a):** the overclock burden falls disproportionately on robots, so robots are
disproportionately *in* the incidents the choirs sing — which means the population most likely to notice the
drift is also the population most likely to be the subject of it, and least positioned to object.

## XXIII. Panel-derived — three practical holes the panel found in a district that had already passed QA (Review Panel)

**Proposed. Grouped rather than given separate Findings**, because each is a discrete gap rather than a thesis.
All three come from positions that had no counterpart among Gates 0-9.

**(a) Who does not go?** *(The Parent.)* Finding I establishes that a crisis flips **the entire district** into
total all-hands urgency, and Finding XII establishes that children are raised substantially by the galleries and
by whoever is off shift. **Those two cannot both hold during a crisis.** If everyone goes, the children are
alone.

**So somebody is designated not to go, and Aries has never written down who.** The answer is genuinely
interesting because it cuts against the district's whole ethic: being the person who *stays* is, by the
district's own premise, the one role that cannot be honoured — you did not take the load. **Proposed: the
designation exists, rotates, is not volunteered for, and is quietly one of the most resented duties in the
district** — and the people who hold it are disproportionately the ones the district has already stopped
counting on for register two (Finding XIX). Not resolved here beyond flagging it; it wants a real answer.

**(b) A lapsed qualification is a loss of standing, and nothing describes what that means.** *(The Worker Past
Their Peak.)* Finding XI states that qualification **is** residency — holding a stage-mark is what converts a
person from present to counted. Finding XII mentions, in passing, a worker whose qualification lapsed after an
injury. **Read together, that person has been decounted**, and the file says nothing about what that actually
does to them socially, materially, or administratively. **Is there an emeritus mark? Does the mark revert, or is
it withdrawn? Who takes it?** Given Finding XIX, the likeliest answer is the worst one — that nothing formal
happens at all, and the person simply stops being addressed as someone who signs. Open, and load-bearing.

**(c) There is no unqualified working population in this file, and there must be one.** *(The Newcomer.)*
Everything written assumes a marked, qualified workforce. **Every district this size has people doing necessary
work that carries no qualification** — hauling, cleaning, feeding, fetching. In Aries they would be the people
for whom the entire status vocabulary (Finding IX) is inapplicable, living in a district where standing *is*
qualification. **This is the single largest general-population hole the pass left**, and it went unnoticed by
Gate 2 because Gate 2 checks whether a finding over-generalizes from a narrow group — not whether a whole group
is missing.

## Worth Your Attention — updated 2026-08-29

**Standout: Finding XVIII — Aries cannot price what it gives away, and both of its deficits live next door.**

Aries is the project's **first complete four-term capability row** — two real strengths, two real deficits,
nothing absent — which makes it the first district whose profile is a balance rather than a gap, and the balance
is unforgiving. It acts instantly and always produces someone willing to sign; it cannot weigh competing claims
and cannot sustain deferred reward. **From which: it cannot price or ration its output, and it cannot keep a
schedule.**

What makes it the standout is how much standing canon it explains that previously had no mechanism. **The
blackout leverage is never used** not from timidity but because Aries cannot work out what its leverage is
worth — it can supply or cut off, and the middle setting is a negotiated price it has no faculty to name. **The
Overclock Martyrs** are not a values choice but the human cost of a scheduling deficit, arriving one person at a
time. **The Safety Engineers** are illegible rather than disliked, because their success looks like nothing
happening.

**And the geometry is a genuinely new structural shape.** Both of Aries's missing faculties are concentrated in
one other district — the very one it is set against — which is structurally excellent at precisely what Aries
lacks. **Aries depends on that district for the two things it cannot do, and being set against it is exactly
what prevents it accepting them.** Neither district behaves badly; the arrangement is simply unbearable to one
and invisible to the other. No previous district's deficit had a specific address.

**Runner-up: Finding XIV — the choirs, and what they turn out to be doing.** The Welsh coalfield research
supplied a tradition that transferred whole: choirs founded *after* named disasters, as rehabilitation and
fundraising, rooted in mass participation rather than performance. Dropped into Aries it does two things at
once. **It gives the district the history it otherwise has no archive for** — Finding X establishes that Aries
keeps almost no institutional memory outside the operational layer, so **its past lives in what the choirs
sing.** And **it turns out to be the district's actual compensation mechanism**: each choir administers a
bereavement fund, because a district that cannot price what it loses cannot institutionally compensate for it,
so the money moves through singing instead. **Nobody in Aries would call that a welfare system.**

**Also worth flagging: Finding XV(a).** The overclock burden falls disproportionately on robots, because the one
who *can* go in is the one who goes in — and the district's own ethic, that whoever takes the load is not owed
an explanation, forecloses the examination. **Nobody is exploiting anyone. Nobody is looking, either.** It is
the most uncomfortable finding in the pass and the district would experience the question as an insult to its
dead.

**The original standout still holds** — the demonym flag in Finding IV, which correctly refused the natural
formation "Arian" for its real-world homophone and chose the more awkward **Ariesian** deliberately. That was
the right call and remains so.
