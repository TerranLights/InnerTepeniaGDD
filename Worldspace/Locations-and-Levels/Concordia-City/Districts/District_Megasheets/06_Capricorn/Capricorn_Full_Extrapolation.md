# Capricorn — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Capricorn given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Capricorn_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-09.

---

## I. The status lighting is real, but legible mainly to people who already know the code

**Proposed:** reconciling the flagged tension between canon's "status lighting readable from the street" and the Vision Notes' own "hierarchy not perceptible from where I'm standing" — both can be true at once if the lighting system functions the way any deeply internal social code does: technically visible to anyone, but only actually *readable* to residents fluent in Capricorn's own specific visual grammar. A longtime Capricorn resident would glance at the skyline and instantly know exactly who outranks whom; a newcomer or outside visitor would see attractive, varyingly bright buildings without perceiving the encoded hierarchy at all, the same way Gemini's light-tracks plausibly carry meaning only a local could decode. The lighting is genuinely "readable from the street" — just not to a street-level newcomer without the cultural key.

## II. "Driven" describes workers and atmosphere as one inseparable thing, by design

**Proposed:** Capricorn's meritocratic culture doesn't distinguish between individual ambition and systemic pressure — the merit system was built specifically so that external expectation and internal drive reinforce each other until they're functionally the same thing. This is why the developer's own Vision Notes description of "driven" reads as a single quality rather than two separable ones (a driven person in a driven place): in Capricorn, that's not really two facts, it's one. This is a genuinely different resolution than Leo's own personal-vs-general ambiguity (a deliberately cultivated craft) — Capricorn's version isn't a performance, it's what happens when a district successfully makes systemic pressure indistinguishable from personal ambition for 250 years running.

## V. Architecture — the perimeter is the civic virtue (Phase 1)

**Proposed.** Capricorn's architecture is organized around **thresholds**: what you may enter, and what that
entry says about you.

**Baseline:** heavy, permanent, deliberately over-built construction — *things designed to outlast their
builders.* Stone-analogue and structural metal, minimal ornament, and an unusual amount of the district's
material budget spent on things nobody sees: foundations, load paths, tolerances. **A Yards building is
over-engineered as a moral position**, not a practical one.

**The spires and the status lighting** (Finding I) are the district's vertical grammar, already canon. What this
Finding adds is that **the lighting is architectural rather than decorative** — the encoded hierarchy is built
into what a structure *is*, so a change in standing is a change to a building. See Finding XIX for what that
does to a person.

**Enclosure as the organizing form** *(substrate §14.12 — the sign's ruled place is the fortress)*: gatehouses,
walls, permit thresholds, controlled approaches. **The Yards has more doors that check you than any district in
Concordia**, and residents do not experience this as suspicion — a threshold is how a place knows who is
responsible for what is inside it.

**Numbering is load-bearing** *(substrate §12)*: bays, shifts, workstations, and gates are numbered on a fixed
scheme that everyone knows by heart, and **the number is a location in the hierarchy as much as in space.**
Saying which bay you work is saying something quite precise.

**What is conspicuously absent: anywhere designed for people not currently working.** No commons, no
unstructured interior public space of any size. Idleness is destabilizing here rather than restful *(substrate
§6)*, and the district never built for it — which is not austerity but something closer to a blind spot.

## VI. Sensory Profile (Phase 1)

**Proposed. Sound:** industrial, but *rhythmic* rather than chaotic — the district runs on cycles, and residents
read the time of day by which processes are audible. **The significant sound is a cycle running late.**
Underneath it, the Yards is quieter than visitors expect: this is a district where people work in silence
together for long stretches, and where **talk happens over a shared task rather than instead of one** *(substrate
§14.8)*.

**Smell:** cut material, lubricant, cold stone, and the particular dry-dust smell of a place that is swept
constantly. **Cleanliness is conspicuous** — not for hygiene but because mess is evidence of a process not under
control.

**Feel:** cool. The Yards runs colder than Concordia's average and residents are proud of it; heat is treated as
waste. Hard surfaces, deliberate wear-paths worn into floors over 250 years and left unrepaired, because the
wear is a record.

**First impressions:** orderly, unwelcoming, and **legible only in part.** A visitor sees a well-run place and
correctly senses a hierarchy without being able to read it (Finding I). The district is courteous — genuinely
so — and maintains an unmistakable distance. Nobody is unfriendly. Nobody invites you anywhere either.

## VII. Export Culture (Phase 1)

**Proposed.** The Yards is Concordia's builder, and its exports are the least glamorous and most depended-upon
in the city.

**(1) Built structure and structural work** — the physical fabric of other districts. **Where Aries supplies
power, the Yards supplies the thing the power runs through.**

**(2) Qualified people, and this is the real export.** *(Substrate §6: the five native occupations — teacher,
manager/organizer, accountant, executive, programmer — all share the shape of **imposing order on someone else's
material**.)* The Yards exports **organizers**: people other districts bring in to make a chaotic undertaking
finish on time. Its own self-image, stated plainly, is that other places *cannot organize themselves without us*
— which is both slightly insufferable and substantially true.

**(3) Standards.** The district's specifications, tolerances, and grading schemes are used across Concordia,
often by districts that resent them. **A Yards standard is a genuine export good**, and the district gives them
away free — which reads as generosity and functions as influence, and the Yards has never examined which it is.

## VIII. Religious/Philosophical Landscape — naming the creed, without touching the Door (Phase 2)

**Proposed, and deliberately provisional** per the Plan's own instruction.

**Applying the Naming technique** (`02_Phase_2` §8): the search is for something the district already believes,
in religious register, unnamed. Capricorn has a great deal of *named* apparatus — the Merit Establishment, the
Recalibration Underground, the Narrow Door itself — and underneath all of it sits one unstated proposition that
none of them argues about:

> **Standing is paid for. A thing you did not pay for is not yours, and cannot be made yours by being given.**

That is the creed, and naming it does the usual work of turning unrelated facts into one thing:

- **It explains why the district has no charity and does not experience the absence as a lack.** A gift would
  produce unearned standing, which by the creed is not standing at all. *(Substrate §3: "Nothing is a gift;
  everything is an entry.")*
- **It explains why the Recalibration Underground demands the system apply to everyone rather than demanding
  the system be abolished** (already canon, `District_Unity_of_Opposites.md`). They are not heretics. **They
  believe the creed more literally than the Establishment does.**
- **It explains why recognition-denial is catastrophic here rather than merely disappointing.** *(Substrate
  §5.)* Dues paid without standing arriving is not a reward withheld — **it is the disproof of the only theory
  of worth the district has.** The district's dangerous people are not shortcut-seekers; they are people who
  paid in full.
- **And it explains the district's characteristic kindness**, which is real: *"they usually have a very good
  reason for doing so"* *(substrate §14.16)*. The Yards will genuinely **ask** why you broke a rule, and
  genuinely weigh the answer. What it will not entertain is that the rule was wrong. **Fair hearing inside a
  fixed frame** is the most accurate one-line description of Capricorn justice.

**Held against the moral-policing shadow, that last pair is the district's live internal argument, not a
contradiction** — and it should stay unresolved.

**No marginal imported belief is proposed**, for the structural reason the substrate supplies: this temperament
*"cannot comprehend the magic of life or any beliefs of a certain kind… only trust in rational thought, hard
work, and real results."* An imported faith has nothing here to attach to.

## IX. Fashion (Phase 2)

**Proposed. General population first.** The failure mode to avoid is writing the district from its spire elite;
**most Capricornians are working trades, and the spire is a small population.**

**Baseline:** dark, hard-wearing, precisely maintained. *(Substrate §12: brown, khaki, black, purple; **yellow
and red are actively avoided** — which is worth noting, since those are exactly the Leo and Aries palettes, the
two districts the Yards grinds against most.)* Garments are repaired rather than replaced and the repair is
neat, not decorative — **the opposite of Leo's visible mend and of Taurus's ornamental one.**

**The grade-mark is the whole ornament.** Qualification, trade, and grade are worn plainly and read fluently by
every resident. **Understated elegance is the explicit local aesthetic** — *"the Lion seeks fame while a
Capricorn's main goal is a fortune… a Capricorn enjoys understated elegance."* Expense is invisible by
preference: good material, no display.

**One genuine and unremarked local detail** *(substrate §12 — the sign governs the knees, and bone and joint
degeneration is its canonical pathology)*: **older Yards workers walk badly**, and the district reads a limp as
a service record rather than as a disability. Knee supports, canes, and braces are ordinary, unhidden, and
carry a quiet prestige nobody would admit to. **Fashion here includes how you walk.**

## X. Arcanet Culture — the record is the district's real product (Phase 3)

**Proposed.** Capricorn's information culture is **documentary**, and it is unlike the four covered so far.

**Everything is recorded, and the record is procedural rather than personal.** Not Taurus's lineage, not
Scorpio's confession, not Leo's status broadcast — **the Yards records *processes*: what was specified, what was
built, to what tolerance, by whom, when, and whether it passed.** Its archive is enormous, dull, and the most
reliable body of fact in Concordia. Other districts come here to settle disputes about what actually happened.

**The favor ledger sits underneath it, informally** *(substrate §3)*. Nothing is a gift; every accommodation is
an entry that will be called. **The formal record and the favor ledger run in parallel and never touch**, and
the district would be genuinely offended by the suggestion that the second one is a record at all.

**Privacy norms:** work is entirely public, private life entirely private, and the boundary is sharp and
uncontested. Your grade, your hours, your pass rate are open. **Nobody asks about anything else** — which
visitors read as respect and which is closer to disinterest.

**The scheduling apparatus is the district's proudest institution**, and per the substrate the most important
thing to know about it is **why it is never revisited**: not neglect, not defense of an advantage, but that
*continuity is what safety feels like here* **(substrate §14.14)**. Anyone proposing recalibration is heard as
proposing instability by people who are not lying about how it feels. *(This is deliberately written as the
emotional condition around the Narrow Door without asserting which mechanism the Door is.)*

**Relationship to Gemini:** the Yards is a heavy producer of records and a light consumer of news. To Gemini it
looks like an enormous archive that never asks anything.

## XI. Visitor Experience and Visitor-to-Resident Transition — with conflict geometry (Phase 4)

**Proposed.** *(Geometry written into this phase per the Mode B rule.)*

**Visitor experience:** courteous, escorted, and quietly assessed. **The Yards is the easiest district in
Concordia to visit and the hardest to be taken seriously in.** You will be helped efficiently and you will not
be asked back.

**Transition: test-and-admit, slowly** *(substrate §7, §14.13)*. What earns entry is **demonstrated skill,
kept promises, and showing up over time** — *"display your skills to them, since they are attracted to such
things."* The distance is not coldness; **the distance is the test.** Maintenance is a duty: you are expected to
keep showing up, remember dates, and not disappear. **Someone who vanishes for a year has not paused their
standing; they have spent it.**

**The thirty-year threshold, and it is a real institution** *(substrate §15, the Saturn return)*: the district
marks a **coming-of-age at roughly thirty** — not majority, not qualification, but the point at which someone is
considered to have *become* an adult of the Yards. Before it you are an apprentice however skilled; after it
your judgment is your own. **It is the district's most emotionally significant threshold and it is entirely
undramatic**, marked by a change in what people ask you rather than by any ceremony.

### Conflict geometry

- **The Sanctuary — the district Capricorn is set against, and the axis is *parent and child*.** *(Substrate
  §8.)* The question both districts are asking from opposite ends is **who feeds whom, and what counts as
  enough.** The documented failure mode is a spiral: *"the Cancer end becomes increasingly needy and the
  Capricorn end becomes increasingly withholding and withdrawn,"* each escalation confirming the other's
  diagnosis. **And the structural cruelty is exact: the Yards' weakest faculty is care, and its opposite number
  has made care a civic institution.** The one district best equipped to supply what the Yards structurally
  lacks is the one it is least able to accept it from. *(The substrate's cleanest image for the pair is worth
  keeping: **the marketplace and the hearth** — the world outside the door and the fire inside it.)*
- **The two hardest frictions are both fellow initiators, and they are not the same fight.** With the **Power
  Core**, the argument is **whether earned rank is legitimate at all** — Aries defines itself against a system
  that makes people obedient, and pays for its defiance with worse standing, which the Yards reads as proof and
  Aries reads as freedom. With the **Government District**, the trap is that they *look* compatible: shared
  competence, shared favor ledgers, easy at the level of ideas — and then **the Yards cannot bear that the
  Government District will not decide.** Procedure against deliberation.
- **Three-way differentiation — how a decision gets made** *(applying `00e` §6; all three are initiators, so
  the axis is real)*: **the Power Core** — someone steps forward and signs, immediately. **The Yards** — the
  procedure decides, and the schedule decides the procedure. **The Government District** — deliberation
  continues. **Each finds the other two negligent**, and none of them is wrong about the others' failure mode.
- **Natural allies:** **Taurus** (shared material realism; and the corrective — *"great at reining you in when
  you're going too hard"*) and **the Undergrid** (instant mutual recognition, natural division of labour, with
  a real risk of mutual suffocation). **Scorpio** is the one relationship where trust genuinely runs deep, with
  the substrate's own caution attached: these two **tyrannize when paired**, and isolate together.

## XII. Ordinary Daily Life (Phase 5)

**Governing test applied:** the Yards is not a district of grim strivers. **Most residents are competent people
doing skilled work they are good at, among people they have known for decades**, and the prevailing mood is
closer to dry satisfaction than to pressure.

**Routines** are shift-structured and stable to a degree that would feel oppressive elsewhere and reads here as
security. **The workplace is the social unit** — the substrate's sharpest daily-life note is that this
temperament *only actually talks while working on something together* **(substrate §14.8)**, and Yards
friendships are consequently built sideways, over years, at adjacent benches. **A Capricornian invitation is
almost always an invitation to do something**, never to sit and talk; a proposal to just have a meal reads as
faintly pointless.

**Humor is the district's most underrated feature and it is load-bearing.** *(Substrate §2: dry humor is "the
ace up Capricorn's sleeve and the secret to their awe-inspiring perseverance.")* The Yards is **much funnier
than any other district believes**, in a register outsiders often fail to detect as humor at all. It is how the
place gets through things.

**And the age curve is inverted, which is the most charming true thing about the district** *(substrate §14.10 —
"born old, then young")*: **the apprentices are grim and the retirees are the funniest people in the room.**
Seriousness is front-loaded here and joy is the second half of life. **A young Capricornian is more burdened
than an old one**, and both know it.

**Mundane concerns.** Whether a grade review lands this cycle or next. A tolerance dispute with another
district's inspector. The knee. Somebody's apprentice who is good but will not be seen. **A schedule change
proposed by a district that does not have to live with it.**

**Personal struggles.** A worker whose dues are paid and whose standing has not arrived, waiting with a patience
that is starting to curdle *(substrate §3, §14.11)*. Someone who has realized they do not actually want the
next grade and has no vocabulary for saying so. A parent who cannot get their teenager to stop being right
about things.

**Downtime** is structured: courses, projects, collaborative games, the district's serious and long-running
craft societies. **Unstructured leisure is a genuine local difficulty** — see Finding V.

## XIII. Thematic Breadth Catalog (Phase 6)

**Real-World Basis note:** two picks researched at concrete level for this pass, chosen by difference.

**Named places and landmarks:**
- **The Two Founders** — a paired memorial, and the best single object the substrate produced. *(Fused from
  Pricus and Cronus, substrate §13, §14.9.)* **Two statues. One honors a founder who repeatedly restored an
  earlier configuration at his successors' expense, until he stopped. The other honors a founder who kept his
  own position by consuming the people who would have replaced him.** Both are maintained with total sincerity.
  **The district can tell you, in detail and without irony, why both were good men** — and the accounts it gives
  are not obviously wrong. *(This is deliberately compatible with all six Narrow Door conditions and asserts
  none of them.)*
- **The Grading Hall** — where qualifications are conferred and reviewed; the district's most-visited building
  and the one it is proudest of.
- **The Long Bays** — the oldest working floors, worn into visible paths, deliberately never resurfaced.
- **The Gate Rows** — the permit thresholds; the district's characteristic street form is an approach to a
  checkpoint that is not hostile.

**Physical things:** a **grade-mark**; a **specification** as a physical signed document with a tolerance and a
name on it; a **favor-ledger entry** *(substrate §14.3)* — informal, unofficial, and kept by families and halls
rather than by the district; **knee braces and canes**, unhidden.

**People (role/archetype placeholders):** a grading examiner; a sponsor who can state honest, detailed, entirely
proxy-based grounds for every sponsorship he ever made *(substrate §14.1)*; **an apprentice-handbook author**
whose kindly, well-meant advice is the most damning document in the district *(substrate §14.2 — see Gate 7,
deliberately unused here)*; a retiree who is very funny.

**Settings:** the ten minutes before a grade review; a bench conversation that has been running for thirty
years; the Two Founders on a maintenance day.

## XIV. Cuisine, Music, and Arts — the guild with the feast stripped out (Phase 7)

**Proposed — and the unifying insight comes from the research, not the substrate.**

> **The fusion that organizes this whole Finding.** *(From medieval guild practice in the Low Countries,
> researched for this pass.)* A real guild bundled **three** things into one institution: **a craft standard**
> (apprenticeship, tolerances, quality control, the right to practice), **mutual aid** (support for members
> sick, disabled, or old; provision for widows and surviving children; funding for funerals and burials), and
> **shared meaning** (patron saint, devotional life, the ceremonial and social dinners the guild met for).
>
> **The Yards has the first and neither of the other two** — and Finding XVIII says exactly why: aid requires
> the faculty it is weakest in, and feast-and-devotion requires the one it is second-weakest in. **Nobody
> stripped them out. They never developed.**
>
> **The Yards is a guild with the burial fund and the patron's day missing, and it has never noticed the shape
> of the hole**, because it has no comparison. This is the most useful single sentence about the district's
> culture, and it does not require the Narrow Door to be resolved.

**(a) Food.** Functional, plentiful, cheap, and taken at the workplace. Canteens by bay; food is fuel and
timing, and the district is genuinely good at it in the way it is good at logistics. **What the Yards does not
have is a feast** — no calendar meal, no occasion at which the district eats together as a district. *(Note the
substrate's own dietary correspondence, milk over wine, and read it as temperament: this is not an abstinent
culture, it is an unceremonious one.)*

**The one real food custom is small and telling:** you feed the person coming off a shift that went badly, and
you do not mention why. **Care here is delivered as provision without acknowledgment**, because acknowledgment
would make it a favor, and a favor is an entry.

**(b) Robot cuisine and siligel.** Siligel provision is **scheduled, standardized, and audited** — the Yards is
the district most likely to have a specification for it, and its siligel is regarded across Concordia as
reliable and dull. **Glitch-Coolant** sits on the working-class potency side, is drunk after shift in small
known groups, and the district's rule about it is not moral but procedural: *never in a way that affects
tomorrow's grade.*

**(c) Music.** The Yards' native music is **work-timed** — cyclical, structured, functional in origin, and
performed by ordinary residents rather than specialists. Its serious register is **long-form and slow**, music
built the way the district builds anything, valued for construction rather than expression. **Nobody here
improvises in public.**

> **Cross-district contrast, stated inline** *(Gate 6b)*. Five districts now have general-population music and
> none can borrow another's: **Taurus** sings quietly indoors about lineage; **Leo** barely plays and listens
> expertly; **Scorpio** speaks simultaneously so nobody is individually audible; **Aries** sings massed and
> loud about specific dead; **Capricorn** performs long, constructed, cyclical pieces where **the merit is in
> the building and error is audible.** A Yards performance is judged the way a tolerance is judged.

**(d) Arts.** **Craft, and the district would dispute that this is art at all.** Its aesthetic principle is that
**a made thing should show its construction** — visible joinery, exposed structure, no facing that conceals how
something is held together. Concealment reads as either incompetence or deception. Literature: the
**specification** and the **service record**; the district's genuinely admired written form is a technical
account so clear that a stranger could rebuild the thing from it.

## XV. Human-Robot Relations, Counterculture, and Private Life (Phase 7)

**Proposed.**

**(a) Human-Robot Relations — the local inflection is that the grade is the whole answer, and this is nearly
the district's best feature.** *(Substrate: the district's status vocabulary is qualification, and nothing
else.)* In the Yards **there is no robot register and no human register** — a grade is a grade, a specification
is a specification, and the district is genuinely and unsentimentally indifferent to which population holds
which. Several of Concordia's robot populations regard the Yards as the fairest district in the city, and
**they are largely right.**

**The inflection, and it is uncomfortable:** the same indifference that makes the grading fair makes the
district **unable to notice population-specific harm.** Its instruments measure output and tolerance. They do
not measure who is being worn out, and the district's own creed says the question is improper anyway — *if the
grade is met, what else is there to ask?* **A robot working conditions no human could sustain is, by every
instrument the Yards possesses, simply meeting spec.**

Nobody is exploiting anyone. **The district has built the most impartial measurement system in Concordia and
has no instrument that could detect this**, and would consider building one an insult to the fairness of the
first.

**(b) Counterculture — and the honest finding here is that the Yards already has one, in canon.** The
**Recalibration Underground** *(`District_Unity_of_Opposites.md`)* is the district's counterculture, and Step C
confirms rather than replaces it: what the Yards requires of everyone is that **standing be earned through the
system**, and the Underground's position is that *the system should apply to everyone it claims to measure.*
**They are not refusing the demand. They are insisting on it.**

> **Cross-district contrast** *(Gate 6b)*. Taurus's counterculture declines the record; Leo's declines
> recognition; Scorpio's declines the silence; Aries's **adds** an accounting the district cannot perform.
> **Capricorn's demands the district's own stated rule be enforced literally** — the only one of the five whose
> program is *more* orthodox than the mainstream's, which is precisely why it cannot be dismissed as heresy and
> precisely why it is so irritating.

**What this pass adds is the second, unorganized critic** *(substrate §14.15)*: **the district's adolescents.**
Capricornian teenagers are constitutionally disposed to say plainly that something is unjust — *"a strong sense
of what is right and social justice"* — and they are the one demographic the district's own
respect-must-be-earned logic gives **no standing whatsoever.** **The complaint is available decades before
anyone will hear it.** That is a better and more painful shape than a district with no internal dissent, and it
requires no villain: the people ignoring them are correct by the district's own rules.

**(c) Private Life — and here the Pullman research supplies the institutional form.**

> *(Fused from Pullman, Illinois, researched for this pass.)* The real company town's mechanism: **to work for
> Pullman you had to live in Pullman's houses, with rent deducted from wages.** When the 1893 panic forced
> wages down by an average of 28 percent, **rents were not reduced** — because the housing was **an investment
> requiring a return**, not a provision that could flex. The rents were already 20-25% above the local average.
> The federal commission afterward found the paternalism itself partly to blame.

**The Yards houses its workers, and houses them well.** Solid, warm, durable, better than several districts
manage. **And the housing sits on the district's books as an asset**, because that is the only category the
Yards has for it — the district's care deficit *(substrate: Moon in detriment)* means welfare provision is not underfunded here,
it is **structurally absent as a concept**, so provision gets accounted the only way the district knows how to account for anything.

**The consequence, which requires nobody cruel:** when things tighten, **the provision cannot flex, because
flexing it means writing down an asset.** There is no ledger category for *reduce this because they are
struggling.* And the creed (Finding VIII) closes the loop from the other side — **asking for relief is asking
for something unearned**, which is the one request the district's culture cannot process. **So the request is
rarely made, which the district reads as evidence that it isn't needed.**

**The integration bargain, then:** you get genuine security, real housing, a fair grade, and a place that will
never ask you an intrusive question. **What you cannot get is help**, and the district does not know that this
is what it is offering.

**Generationally:** *"many Capricornians cannot relate to young children"* is a real and admitted limit
*(substrate §6)*, and it lands on the district's teaching institutions. **The Yards trains apprentices superbly
from about sixteen and is poor with anyone younger** — childcare here is the least-developed institution in a
district that is excellent at everything else, and nobody has ever put it that way.

## XVI. Municipal Holidays (Phase 7)

**Proposed — three, and the district's own paucity here is the finding.** *(Substrate §15 records "no festival,
calendar, food, or ritual material" as a real and standing source gap. Per the 2026-08-29 rule, the hole is
itself the answer: **the Yards has fewer observances than any district in Concordia**, and Finding XIV explains
why.)*

**1. The Reading of Standards** *(annual, dry, and genuinely important).* The district's specifications and
tolerances are reviewed and reissued. Attendance is a duty, the content is tedious, and residents take it
seriously in a way outsiders find inexplicable. **It is the closest thing the Yards has to a civic liturgy**,
and its flatness is deliberate — the substance is the ceremony.

**2. Turning** *(at the year's hinge; sober, reflective, private).* *(Fused from the substrate's Janus reading —

## XVIII. Capability profile — it can build anything and cannot say what for (Substrate)

**Proposed — the pass's primary generator.** *(Substrate: `F_Rulerships.md` §5-6, D6 row.)*

**Shape: complete four-term row** — the second in the project after the Power Core, and per the typology
(`00e` §5) the question a complete row asks is *what does the balance cost?*

- **Structurally excellent at structure, time, and discipline.**
- **Structurally excellent at sustained applied force** — aggression stabilized into perseverance. The substrate
  calls this pairing **the strongest executive combination in the table**, and that judgment should be taken at
  face value: **the Yards can execute anything Concordia can specify.**
- **Structurally deficient in care.**
- **Structurally deficient in meaning and generosity.**

**The characteristic institutional failure follows exactly: it can build anything and cannot say what for, so
it substitutes the deadline itself as meaning.** A completion date is not a constraint here; **it is the answer
to the question of why.** Which is why the Stand-Down (Finding XVI) is the district's only real festivity —
finishing is the only thing the Yards has that *means* something, so it is the only thing it can celebrate.

**The two deficits, run out:**
- **Care:** welfare provision is not underfunded, it is **structurally absent as a category**, and workers treat
  needing help as a disqualification. See Finding XV(c) for the institutional form.
- **Meaning:** it will not fund anything speculative — **which strangles its own future capacity while feeling
  like prudence.** *(Substrate §14.6: the ceiling that reads as caution. Deliberately left as texture rather
  than asserted as the Narrow Door's mechanism.)*

> **And the deficits are differently located, which is new.** *(Applying the addressed-vs-diffuse question from
> `00e` §5.)* **Care is *addressed*: it is at its strongest in the Sanctuary — the district the Yards is
> structurally set against.** So that deficit has a name, a location, and a permanent grievance attached, and
> the two districts spiral (Finding XI). **Meaning is *diffuse*: it sits in districts the Yards has no strong
> relation to at all.**
>
> **The consequence is precise. The Yards knows it has a problem with care** — it argues about it, resents being
> told, has a whole relationship organized around it. **It does not know it has a problem with meaning**, because
> there is nobody across the table saying so. **The addressed deficit produces politics; the diffuse one
> produces a silence nobody can hear.** The Power Core had two addressed deficits and Taurus had a diffuse one;
> **Capricorn is the first district with one of each, and the asymmetry is the finding.**

**Shadow proportion** (`00d`): **the Yards works, and works superbly.** It builds what the city needs, it
measures fairly, it houses its people well, its grading is the least corrupt instrument in Concordia, and its

## XIX. Gate 9 finding — there is no way to stop here that does not read as failing (Substrate)

**Proposed. Produced by the asymmetry check** (`00c` Gate 9), run first against the pre-Plan findings per
`00e` §11b — **the fifth consecutive district where the failure was in 2026-07-09 material.**

**Finding II establishes that Capricorn's merit system was built so that external expectation and internal drive
became functionally indistinguishable**, and treats this as a genuine achievement — which it is. **It wrote only
the working case.**

**The reverse is unwritten and follows immediately: if pressure and ambition are indistinguishable, so are
*losing motivation* and *failing*.** A resident who wants to stop — who has enough, who is tired, who has
reached the grade they actually wanted — **has no vocabulary in which to say so that does not parse as
underperformance**, to others or to themselves. The district cannot hear "I have enough," because its creed
(Finding VIII) holds that standing is paid for continuously and **nothing establishes that the paying is ever
done.**

**And the substrate names exactly what is missing, from the other side.** Its "what this temperament needs"
material is not a list of achievements but **a list of permissions** — permission to value one's own judgment,
permission to take daily unproductive time, **permission to hope**, and last and sharpest, **permission to
stop.** *"Needs to stop criticizing themselves all the time,"* and should ask whether they would say those things
*"to someone they loved."*

**Combined with Finding XVIII this is the district's specific cruelty, and nobody administers it:** the Yards has
built the fairest measurement system in Concordia and **has no instrument that reads "enough."** Its
grading tells you where you stand. It cannot tell you that you may stop climbing, because the district has no
concept under which that would be a legitimate result.

**Design hook, no villain required:** a resident at a good grade, respected, secure, who quietly stopped wanting
the next one eleven years ago, and has never found a way to say it that does not sound like giving up — least
of all to themselves.

## XX. Contradiction reconciled, and the collective gap (Substrate)

**Proposed.**

**The contradiction, and the substrate flags it as its own most valuable disagreement.** The corpus contains
both a **workaholic** portrait — exhaustion as evidence of virtue, refusing to stand down, the briefcase balanced
on a lapboard rather than resting — and a flat statement that this temperament **leaves when the work is
finished**, *"even if colleagues are still at their desks."*

**Both are true, per the both-are-true shape** (`00e` §7 — *one disposition, two objects*): **the district
measures a task; its promotion system measures a shift.** A Capricornian is sovereign over their own
deliverables and punctual to other people's appointments — completion-oriented in their work and
presence-oriented in how their work is seen. **The worker finishes and goes. The observer records who was
present.** Both behaviors come from the same source: the task is the thing you owe, and visibility is the thing
that is graded.

> **This is deliberately written as far as it goes and no further.** The gap between the two is plainly where
> the Narrow Door lives, and the substrate supplies a great deal of material about it — including the district's
> own advice literature, which tells the disadvantaged worker to *"work at looking more interested in their
> work"* rather than telling anyone to widen the window they are observed through. **That material is recorded
> in Gate 7 as available and deliberately unused.** Finding III reserves the choice, and this pass does not
> take it.

**Mined from the substrate's own §15 Source Gaps**, per the rule that a hole in the sources is often the
district's mechanism:

- **"The largest real gap — collective/crowd behavior."** Every source describes an individual; nothing
  describes a *population* of this temperament governing itself, mourning, striking, or celebrating. **Applied:
  that absence is the district's own condition.** The Yards has an extremely well-developed vocabulary for
  individual standing and **almost none for collective action** — which is why its counterculture takes the form
  of demanding the existing rules be applied (Finding XV(b)) rather than of organizing anything, and why the
  district has so few observances (Finding XVI).
- **And the one genuine derivation the gap forces, which is worth having:** *under idleness this temperament is
  destabilized rather than rested.* **A work stoppage in the Yards would be hardest on the strikers.** Not
  through hardship — through the loss of the structure that makes them feel safe. **Any Yards labor beat should
  be built on that**, and it is a far more interesting obstacle than management resistance.

## XXI. Panel-derived — the Yards has no venue for anything that is not work (Review Panel)
