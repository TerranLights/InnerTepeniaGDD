# Hub (Axis Mundi) — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about the Hub given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Hub_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-10.

## I. The remaining six Bridge Memorial readings, carried forward from the deep dive's own proposal

The deep dive already proposed readings for the six districts the canon file left blank, derived directly from each district's own established founding logic, explicitly offered for review rather than locked in. Preserved here as the Extrapolation's own first proposed answer, since they complete a canon-file table that currently stops at six of twelve:

- **Taurus** (domestic continuity, "hold the line"): **surrender** — leaving through the Tower is giving up the one thing Taurus values above all else, the home you stay and defend.
- **Capricorn** (earned status through output): **squandered investment** — enormous collective labor and production lost the moment people left rather than staying to keep building.
- **Aquarius** (escape through radical reinvention): **a parallel experiment** — a different kind of escape attempt, run at civilizational scale, using orbit instead of social restructuring.
- **Libra** (procedural control equals survival): **proof the system held** — neither tragedy nor heroism, just evidence that an emergency evacuation process, properly managed, worked as designed.
- **Gemini** (official version / real version): **the truest Twin-Channels object in the city** — an official face that says nothing, and a real, unspoken meaning every resident already knows and never states aloud.
- **Pisces** (dissolution as transformation): **the ultimate dissolution** — the boundary between staying and leaving, between the city and everyone it lost, finally and completely erased.

## II. A visible "Hub person" is marked by neutral, mixed-signifier dress rather than any single distinguishing trait

**Proposed:** given the Vision Notes' own confirmed detail that visitors read as visibly different from each other (reflecting district origin) while genuine Hub people are still distinguishable within that mix, the most plausible marker isn't a single garment or symbol — it's the absence of any one district's visual language, deliberately assembled from small, mismatched pieces of several. A Hub-born resident's clothing, accessories, or robot-frame styling plausibly draws a little from many districts and commits fully to none, the sartorial equivalent of "principled rootlessness" — recognizable specifically by *not* reading as any one place, in a district where everyone else, by definition, does.

---

## III. Pace is even and unhurried, deliberately calibrated to put every visitor at ease regardless of their home district's own rhythm

**Proposed:** consistent with the district's own established professional-detachment culture and mediator function, Hub-born residents plausibly cultivate a consciously neutral pace — neither Aries' urgency nor Sagittarius' loitering nor Gemini's organized chaos, but something closer to practiced evenness, because a mediator who visibly rushes or visibly lingers signals something about how they're reading the situation. The Hub's pace, like its culture, is a professional performance of neutrality rather than a spontaneous rhythm.

---

## IV. Sayowa-descended Hub residents become the district's quiet theorists, while Hub-arrived-by-elimination residents become its most sincere true believers

**Proposed:** per the canon file's own flagged open question, it's plausible both halves of the asymmetry are true simultaneously, in different roles. Sayowa refugees arrived already fluent in being a crossroads people, and plausibly gravitate toward the roles that require actually articulating what Hub identity means — council facilitators, mediators, the people who can explain the Hub to itself. Hub-arrived-by-elimination residents, who came to the Hub having failed to belong somewhere else, plausibly hold the identity more sincerely and defensively, since "principled rootlessness" is the first identity that ever actually worked for them. The Hub's culture isn't absorbed from Sayowa or independently reinvented — it's articulated by one group and most fervently believed by the other.

## V. Demonym and Notable Figures

**Demonym, proposed: Hub-born** (for those born in the district) **and Hub-arrived** (for everyone else who settled there) — rather than a single unified demonym. Given the canon file's own explicit two-tier distinction between these populations, and the deep dive's own finding that they carry a genuinely different relationship to the district's core identity, collapsing them into one word would erase a distinction the district's own material treats as load-bearing.

**Notable Figures, proposed:**
- **A senior Sayowa-descended council facilitator** *(placeholder, unnamed)* — one of the district's most articulate theorists of Hub identity, per Section IV, specifically because they arrived already fluent in being a crossroads person rather than having drifted there by elimination.
