# Virgo — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about the Undergrid given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Virgo_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-09.

---

## I. Pace is steady and unhurried by default, but snaps to focused urgency the instant something fails

**Proposed:** given the district's own established meticulousness and the Vision Notes' own confirmed communal, always-partnered work style, the Undergrid's baseline pace is plausibly deliberate and controlled — dangerous, precision work doesn't reward rushing, and undergrid families' own reputation is built on doing things right rather than doing them fast. That steadiness breaks completely the moment something actually fails (a breach, a leak, a fluctuation), at which point the same crews shift into the kind of urgent, practiced coordination that comes from having drilled the failure case many times before. the Undergrid's pace, like the Frostlands', has two speeds — but where the Frostlands' shift is about entering a lethal environment, Virgo's is about responding to a lethal event inside an environment that's already normally survivable.

---

## II. Mourning has a specific, small, private ritual, layered underneath the district's public gallows humor

**Proposed:** consistent with the Vision Notes' own observation that dark jokes about who "didn't make it this winter" coexist with real, deeply-felt mourning, it's plausible the district has a specific small ritual that never gets discussed with surface districts — something as simple as leaving a shift-partner's decorated junction box or cable-run untouched for a fixed period, or reciting the specific tunnels someone knew best before formally reassigning that territory to someone else. The gallows humor is what Virgo shows outward; the ritual is what happens in the tunnels, among the people who were actually there, giving the district's private grief the same "not communicated to surface districts because they wouldn't understand" treatment already established for its aesthetic tradition.

---

## III. The Deep Level exists at the edge of ordinary awareness for most Undergrid residents — known to exist, rarely thought about

**Proposed:** given the Vision Notes didn't surface the Deep Level at all during a session otherwise rich in sensory detail, it's plausible that for the average Virgo worker, the Deep Level isn't a live daily concern — it's a known fact about the district, roughly the way most residents of any city know their sewers connect to something they've never personally seen, present in awareness without being present in daily experience. The Deep Level Keepers (per `District_Unity_of_Opposites.md`) are a specific, small internal faction precisely because most of the Undergrid doesn't need to think about the chambers to do their actual jobs.

---

## IV. The Year of Silence happened in the final decades of the Second Interwar Period, close enough to living memory that its architects' direct successors still lead the Leverage Holders

**Proposed:** per the deep dive's own reasoning, the canon file's bullet ordering (after the war, before "Post-War Neglect") is most consistent with an interwar placement, and a placement in the final decades before the war (plausibly within living memory of the war's own generation) would explain why "the memory of two weeks of Virgo working to rule... has never had to be used again" carries real, current political weight rather than reading as ancient history — the people who could theoretically do it again include the direct professional descendants, if not the literal survivors, of the people who did it the first time.

Per the deep dive's own explicit flag, the Deep Level's true nature (pre-Falkland infrastructure, something older, navigable geology, or a possible Cradle-adjacent fabrication-chamber link predating Calethina's Lab) was marked as needing its own dedicated pass, not folded into an already-dense document. That discipline is preserved here rather than overridden — this Extrapolation intentionally does not propose an answer, leaving the question exactly as open as the deep dive left it.

**Demonym, proposed: Virgoan.** Standard formation; slightly less immediately euphonious than some of the other districts' demonyms, which arguably suits a district that has never prioritized being noticed.

## VII. It can measure anything in Concordia and cannot declare anything finished

**The structural spine.** *(Capability reading, `F_Rulerships.md` §6 D12.)*

**This is the second *doubled row* and the mirror of the first** — the Markets hold verification at minimum
twice over; **the Undergrid hold it at maximum twice over.** Analysis is both native and amplified, with
nothing to counterweight it. Against that sit two deficits: **meaning and scale**, and **self-valuation**.

**The obvious consequence is the pre-written one — *it can report the exact condition of every component and
cannot say which system is worth saving* — and it is true and it is not the finding.** The substrate supplies
something sharper and more specific, and it is stated as a *need* rather than a failure: **completion is not a
state this district can enter on its own authority.** It will not rest until someone says the work was good.

**So: the Undergrid can establish, to any precision required, that a job meets specification. And it cannot
declare it done** — because *done* is not a measurement. **It is a valuation**, and valuation is the faculty at
its lowest state here.

**Second-order: every job stays open until somebody outside closes it.** Not as a rule anybody wrote — there is
no rule. The crew finishes, the work is correct, the log records it, **and the entry has no close-out column,
because closing is not an operation this district performs.**

**Third-order, and it re-reads the district's proudest habit.** Canon: the Undergrid maintains meticulous
documentation of every repair, every failure, every incident, partly as professional practice and partly so
that **if the city will not acknowledge what the district does, the district will at minimum know what it did.**
That archive is not a record of completed work. **It is a list of things still open**, and it has been growing
without subtraction for two hundred and fifty years.

**Fourth-order — see Finding X.** The consequence is not philosophical. It is a measurable, compounding drain
on the most capable workforce in the city.

### ⚠ Non-symmetry check against the Markets *(mandatory — pairing rule)*

| | The Markets | The Undergrid |
|---|---|---|
| The doubled faculty | verification at **minimum**, twice | verification at **maximum**, twice |
| First-order | can price anything, cannot prove what anything is | can measure anything, cannot close anything |
| The object | **identity** — nothing can be established as singular | **completion** — nothing can be established as finished |
| The consequence | it cannot protect or prove ownership of its own things | **its capacity is consumed by work that was finished years ago** |

**The rows are mirrored and the consequences are not.** The Markets' problem is about *what a thing is* and it
is fully realized — the district has been living at its equilibrium for two centuries. **The Undergrid's is
about *when a thing is over*, and it is still getting worse**, which means these two districts are not in the
same kind of trouble even though the table says they are opposites.

## VIII. The reciprocity with the Markets is total — every single term inverted — and that is exactly why it can never be used

**Deficit geometry** *(`00e` §5)*, and it is a configuration nothing else on the table matches.

**Read both rows against each other and every term flips.** The Undergrid's doubled strength is the Markets'
doubled weakness. **And the Undergrid's two deficits are both at their strongest in the Markets** — meaning and
scale are at home there; self-valuation is at its highest of anywhere in Concordia there. **Not one faculty
lands anywhere else.**

> **The most complete supply relationship the dignity system produces, in either direction, and it is
> unusable.**

**The Markets' pass gave the reason from their side:** accepting the Undergrid's verification means conceding
the Undergrid's authority over **what things are**, which is the one thing a district built on dissolving fixed
identity cannot concede.

**Here is the reason from this side, and it is worse.** The Markets' entire epistemology is that **worth is
what someone will pay.** For the Undergrid to take the remedy — to let the Markets tell it what its labour is
worth — would be to accept a valuation it already knows the answer to. **It has never been paid properly, it
reads that as normal working conditions, and if worth is what someone pays, then this district is worth almost
nothing.** The remedy and the humiliation are the same transaction.

**Second-order, and it explains the contradictory testimony the substrate records rather than resolving it.**
From the Undergrid's side the relationship reads as one of its few genuinely rewarding partnerships — and
that is **sincere**, because of *which* frame it operates in. The district is drawn to those who are struggling
precisely because **caring for someone in need is the one relation where it does not have to be valued; it only
has to be needed.** From the Markets' side the same relationship is suffocating and slowly fatal. **Both are
accurate. Neither is available to the other**, and no document settles it.

**Third-order.** So the Undergrid keeps the relationship in the only register it can afford — **service** — and
would experience any attempt to renegotiate it as a demand that it price itself. **A player brokering between
these two districts is not resolving a misunderstanding. They are asking each side to give up the thing that
lets it be itself**, and both will refuse, warmly, for reasons neither can articulate.

*(Also flagged: the Undergrid is the structural supplier for **three** districts — the Markets, the Frostlands,
and by the same faculty the Circuit. **The Frostlands have never once approached it**, and the Frostlands pass
could not even cast an Undergrid panel position because there is no relationship to speak from. **That absence
is confirmed from this side**: nobody in the Undergrid has an opinion about the Frostlands beyond the standing
grievance in Finding XVIII.)*

## IX. ⚠ GATE 9 — there is a rite for the dead and none for the living who leave, and the mechanism is identical

**Gate 9 fired again — the eleventh consecutive district, and for the eleventh consecutive time the defect was
in the pre-Plan 2026-07-09 material.**

**What was written one-way.** §II proposes a private mourning practice: a shift-partner's decorated junction
box or cable-run left untouched for a fixed period, or **the specific tunnels someone knew best recited aloud
before that territory is formally reassigned to someone else.** It is a good proposal and it is written
entirely for the dead.

**But the operative mechanism is *territory reassignment*, and people stop working for reasons other than
dying.** A crew member retires, transfers to the surface, is invalided out by a back or a lung or a nerve. **The
tunnels they knew are reassigned exactly the same way** — the same handover, the same maps, the same
successor — **and none of the ritual attaches**, because the ritual attached itself to death, which is when it
obviously applied. Nobody decided this.

**Third-order, and it is the cruel part: the living who leave are erased more completely than the dead.** A
dead colleague's territory is recited. **An invalided colleague's territory is simply given to somebody else on
a Tuesday**, and within a year no one on that route can tell you who held it before. The person is still
alive, two levels up, and has become the one thing this district has no procedure for: **an open item that was
closed without a signature** (Finding VII).

**Fourth-order — and it produces the exact casualty the substrate names.** The characteristic Undergrid
casualty is not the burnout who quits. **It is the veteran who never does**, and this is the mechanism: **the
only exit with any ceremony attached is dying on shift.** Nobody has ever framed it as an incentive, and it is
functioning as one.

> **Differentiated inline from the Frostlands** *(Gate 6b — the nearest collision, and it is close).* The
> Frostlands' Gate 9 finding is that **membership only accrues** — a grounded scout dissolves out of a clan
> because the clan is constituted by continued crossings, and nobody decides. **The Undergrid's people do not
> dissolve; they are formally handed over, correctly, with paperwork.** The Frostlands lack a procedure
> entirely. **The Undergrid has an excellent one and it does not include the person** — which is the difference
> between being forgotten and being processed.

## X. The backlog: work that has been finished for decades, still carried as open, still walked

**Finding VII's fourth-order, and it is the most concrete thing in the pass.**

Somewhere in the Undergrid's log — everywhere in it — are **repairs that have been functionally complete for
years or decades and are still carried as open items.** The work is done. It was done correctly. It has been
inspected. **What never arrived was the close-out**, because the surface office that would have signed it was
reorganized, or the person who would have signed died, or the standard the work was measured against was
withdrawn and never replaced.

**And the crews still walk the route.** Not out of superstition, and not because anyone is confused: **an open
item gets inspected on its interval.** That is the whole of it. Nobody has the authority to strike the entry.

**Second-order — this is what "the Undergrid is overworked" actually means, and it is not what anyone thinks it
means.** The problem is not that Concordia is too big or too old. **The problem is that nothing ever leaves the
list.** A district that maintains everything to specification and cannot close anything accumulates obligations
monotonically, and the accumulation is invisible because every individual entry is legitimate.

**Third-order, and it is the district's blind spot rather than the surface's.** The Undergrid cannot measure
this. Quantifying how much of its capacity is going to closed-but-open work would require **ranking the entries
by whether they still matter**, which is precisely the faculty it does not have. **So the district is currently
operating at some unknown fraction of its real capability, and the fraction is unknowable from inside.**

**Fourth-order — the crisis it is walking toward.** Every other district in Concordia has had to decide what to
let fail, badly, repeatedly, for two and a half centuries, and has calluses. **The Undergrid has never once
performed a triage.** When the accumulation finally forces one — and it will — the district will face it
**competent at everything except the single operation required**, with no procedure, no precedent, and no
vocabulary for choosing what to abandon.

*(Design: **a backlog entry the player can close with a signature** is the cleanest quest object this district
offers — and the district will not know how to react to being released. See Finding XII for why nobody inside
can do it.)*

## XI. The Year of Silence was the only time it ever closed anything, and it remembers it as restraint

**Canon's account, unchanged:** the workforce could not strike — too many things would die — so it **worked to
rule**: exactly and only what the contracts specified, no improvisation, no covering for other districts'
failures, no voluntary overtime. **The city began falling apart in under two weeks.** Libra capitulated with
resource promises roughly half kept. The memory is the district's standing leverage and it has never had to be
used again.

**What nobody has noticed is what working to rule actually *was*, for this district specifically.**

**It was the one time the Undergrid used a specification as a stopping point.** For two weeks, *done* had a
definition that did not require anyone's approval — the contract said what the job was, the job got done, and
**the crew went home.** Not defiance: **closure.** The district discovered, accidentally and for a fortnight,
that it could finish things on its own authority, and that the authority had been sitting in the paperwork the
whole time.

**Second-order — and it is why the district reads the memory the way it does.** The Undergrid remembers the
Year of Silence as **restraint**: a boundary asserted once, under real grievance, never repeated, and it is
quietly proud of not having done it again. **That reading is sincere and it is also how a district that cannot
value its own judgment would necessarily interpret the one occasion it did.**

**Third-order.** So the leverage everyone believes the Undergrid is holding in reserve — the threat it has
never had to repeat — **is not primarily a weapon and never was.** Its effect on the surface was real. **Its
effect on the district was that for two weeks nobody was carrying an open item they had finished**, and several
thousand people found out what that felt like. **Some of them are still alive.**

**Fourth-order, and it is the district's genuine internal politics** *(canon's Leverage Holders, §VI's second
Notable Figure)*: the faction arguing to invoke it again is usually read — inside and out — as militant. **A
sizable part of what they are actually asking for is to be allowed to stop**, and they do not have the
vocabulary to say so, because the district has no word for finishing that does not sound like quitting.

## XII. The instrument that would fix it exists, is simple, and requires exactly the faculty this district lacks

*(Deficit research, Step 3.2 — find a real culture that has what the capability reading says is missing.)*

> **Research: the aviation Certificate of Release to Service — changed a finding, and it is the sharpest
> deficit contrast produced so far.** In licensed aircraft maintenance, closure is a **signature**. A licensed
> engineer signs, **with their licence number and the date**, that the work was carried out and the aircraft is
> fit to fly — and that signature **attributes full responsibility for the quality of all maintenance it covers
> to the engineer personally.** The privilege is restricted to work they performed or were directly involved
> in. And the operative principle: **the licensed engineer is the sole arbitrator of safety while the aircraft
> is on the ground.** Not the manager. Not the client. **The person who did the work.**

**That is the missing organ, and it is not complicated, expensive, or novel.** It requires no new technology, no
concession from Libra, and no reorganization. **It requires one person to be willing to say *I did this, it is
right, and I am answerable for saying so* — and to have that be the end of it.**

**And the Undergrid cannot adopt it, for a reason that is not stubbornness.** Signing means **valuing your own
judgment above the requirement for external confirmation**, and this is the district whose need is stated as
*it will not rest until it gets feedback saying it did well.* **An Undergrid engineer handed a signing licence
would want somebody to check the signature.** The regress is not a joke; it is the capability.

**Second-order.** This is also why the district's proceduralism is not bureaucracy. Its rules exist to protect
the standing of the work — **reputational armor** — and a self-issued close-out would remove the armor and put
a name on the risk. **The district would experience the offer as being asked to expose itself, and would
describe it as being asked to cut corners.**

**Third-order — the divergence worth stating** *(Step 3.5)*. Aviation solved this by making the **worker** the
authority, deliberately, against every organizational instinct. **The Undergrid's version, if it ever arrives,
will not come from a licence issued downward.** It will come from crews signing for each other — which is what
the imported practices of Finding XXVIII are already quietly doing, in miniature, without anyone calling it
that.

## XIII. It can tell you which parts of Concordia were built with room to spare, and they are the parts it built

> **Research: Bazalgette's London sewers — changed a finding.** Having calculated the required pipe diameters
> from the densest plausible population with generous allowances, he said — **"Well, we're only going to do this
> once and there's always the unforeseen"** — and **doubled them.** The unforeseen turned out to be the tower
> block. On the original figures the system would have overflowed in the 1960s. **On the doubled ones it is
> still in service.** Great infrastructure looks extravagant when it is new.

**Canon says the Undergrid built the founding infrastructure** — the redundant heating loops, the waste
recyclers, the distribution networks, the structural reinforcement — **while flashier districts rose above.**
And it documents everything.

**So the district knows, precisely and without having to look it up, which parts of the city were built with
margin and which were built to the number.** It is the only body in Concordia that does. **And the answer is
that the margin is almost entirely in the founding-era work**, because that was the last time this district
decided anything rather than maintaining somebody else's decision.

**Second-order, and it is a grievance with no villain in it.** Every subsequent expansion — every district that
grew, every quarter that added a level, every Frostlands route opened — was specified by somebody else, to
somebody else's budget, **and became a permanent Undergrid liability the moment it was switched on.** Nobody
consulted the district. Nobody was withholding anything; it simply never occurred to anyone that the people who
would maintain a thing forever might have a view on how it was built.

**Third-order.** The Undergrid's founding generation gave the city two and a half centuries of slack, **and the
district has been spending that slack ever since without ever being able to add to it.** Its engineers can tell
you, to the year, when specific systems will stop having any margin left. **They have said so. It is in the
log.** The log is not read.

**Fourth-order, and it is the quietest tragedy in the pass.** What the district wants is not recognition and not
money. **It wants to build one thing, once, with the diameter doubled** — and it has never articulated this as
a demand, because asking for it would require claiming that its judgment is worth funding.

## XIV. The casualty list is the signage, and you walk on it

> **Research: New York's sandhogs — changed a finding, and confirmed a substrate hook against a real case.**
> Local 147 is over 130 years old and the trade passes down through families; because tunnel projects run for
> decades, **multiple generations of one family work the same job.** And the memorial for the city water
> tunnel: **twenty-three deaths over forty-six years of excavation, commemorated by twenty-three manhole covers
> set into a plaza, each bearing a name and a year.**

**The Undergrid names its hardware after its dead**, and the convention is total: junctions, hatches, valve
sets, access covers, specific stretches of conduit. Not plaques — **the fittings themselves.** A name, and a
year, stamped or cast into the working component, in the place where the person was lost or the place they
kept.

**Second-order, and it is why this reads as the district rather than as decoration.** A memorial you have to
visit is a surface institution. **This one is load-bearing**: you cannot do the job without handling it, the
maintenance schedule *is* the visiting schedule, and a crew replacing a worn cover has to decide what to do
with the name, which they do without ceremony and without ever getting it wrong.

**Third-order.** So the district's entire casualty history is legible off its own signage to anyone who knows
the convention — **and no other district knows the convention exists.** Surface visitors read the names as part
numbers. **The Undergrid has never once explained it**, for the same reason it has never explained the
decorated junction boxes: not secrecy, and not resentment. **It would require saying out loud that the work
mattered**, and that is a valuation.

**Fourth-order, and it is the standing invitation to a player.** The names are the one part of this district's
record that the surface can read without permission — you only have to notice, and then ask once. **The
Undergrid will tell you, at length, and be visibly surprised that anyone wanted to know.**

## XV. Architecture — no rooms, only routes; and the district's art cannot be preserved because preserving it would be a valuation

*(Phase 1.)*

**The Undergrid has almost no architecture in the sense the other twelve districts mean it, and this is not a
poverty.** Space here is not composed of rooms with functions; **it is composed of routes with intervals.** A
place is defined by what passes through it and how often somebody has to be there. Crew spaces, galleries,
plant halls and junction chambers are all named and understood by **the run they sit on**, not by what happens
inside them, and directions are given as a sequence rather than a destination.

**Second-order — every surface is a working surface, so ornament had nowhere to go except onto the
equipment.** Canon is explicit and it is one of the district's most characteristic facts: colour-coded cable
runs laid with deliberate aesthetic intention, decorated junction boxes, specific junctions made beautiful,
**completely seriously**, and never mentioned to the surface. There is no decorative layer here because there
is no non-working surface to put one on. **So the art is *in* the infrastructure, structurally, and cannot be
separated from it.**

**Third-order, and it is the finding.** Because the decoration lives on maintained components, **it is
destroyed and remade continuously.** Repainting a junction box is maintenance. Replacing a run replaces the
work on it. **The Undergrid's aesthetic tradition therefore has no old pieces at all** — nothing from the
founding era survives, nothing is conserved, and there is no oldest example of anything.

**Fourth-order — and this is Finding VII again, wearing paint.** The district documents every repair
meticulously and **does not document the decoration.** Not from indifference: recording it as worth preserving
would be **declaring it valuable**, which is the operation this district cannot perform. So the most beautiful
work in Concordia exists in a continuous present tense, made by people who would be embarrassed to call it
work, and **the only reason any of it lasts is that somebody liked it enough to copy it onto the replacement.**

## XVI. Sensory profile — read by hand and ear, and unable to stop diagnosing

*(Phase 1.)*

**The established core, kept:** low light throughout, tools and protocols designed for it, and **navigation by
sound and tactile memory as much as by sight.** Surface districts fear the dark; this one is at home in it and
finds the endless brightness above slightly overwhelming.

**The general-population layer, and it is a specific claim about what carries information.** The Undergrid is
**the only district in Concordia where the walls are informative.** Surface temperature, vibration through a
handrail, the pitch of a run, the particular dryness or damp of a stretch of wall — these are not atmosphere
here, they are **readings**, and every resident takes them continuously and without effort. A crew member walks
a corridor the way a surface resident reads a room full of faces.

**Second-order — sound is the primary channel and it is diagnostic.** Everybody here can hear a system that is
wrong, at a distance, through a wall, over conversation. It is not a trained specialism; **it is what growing
up in the district does to you**, and it does not switch off.

**Third-order, and it corrects the canon detail rather than repeating it.** The reason Undergrid residents find
the surface overwhelming is **not the light.** It is that a surface district is full of equipment that is
slightly wrong — a bearing going, a duct resonating, a pump cycling too often — **and every bit of it registers,
continuously, as a fault they are hearing and cannot act on and which is nobody's responsibility.** Surface
brightness is merely the thing they can name. **The actual experience is being unable to stop receiving
information about systems they have no standing to touch**, and it is exhausting in a way they have never
successfully described to anyone above.

**The smell** is the district's least-remarked sense and its most consistent: warm insulation, standing water,
lubricant, and the specific flat mineral smell of very old concrete. **Residents can date a stretch of tunnel
by it** and assume everyone can.

## XVII. Arcanet culture — it keeps the network alive, has the worst service on it, and publishes an archive nobody reads

*(Phase 3.)*

**The general baseline.** The Undergrid physically maintains the backup Arcanet nodes, the conduit, and most of
the plant the rest of Concordia's network depends on — **and has the poorest service in the city**, because
allocation is a surface decision and this district has never submitted a request. Not as protest. **It has
never occurred to anyone here that the allocation is something you could have a view about.**

**What the network is for here: work.** Fault reports, schematics, interval schedules, the log. Social use is
thin and largely happens in person at shift overlap (Finding XXII). **A Undergrid resident's Arcanet presence
is a job record**, and the district would find it strange to have another kind.

**Second-order, and it is the finding.** The great fault-and-repair archive — the one canon describes as
partly professional practice and partly the district's answer to being unacknowledged — **is on the Arcanet,
technically accessible, and effectively public.** It is not sealed. It is not hidden. There is no restriction
on any other district reading it, and there never has been.

**Nobody reads it.**

**Third-order.** So the district's central grievance is not secrecy or suppression, which is what everyone
including the Undergrid half-assumes. **The record of everything this district has done for two and a half
centuries has been sitting in the open the entire time**, and the surface's failure is not concealment but
**disinterest** — which is much harder to be angry at, impossible to negotiate with, and cannot be fixed by
disclosure, because disclosure already happened.

**Fourth-order.** It is also why the archive keeps growing rather than being curated. **A record kept to be
read would be edited for a reader.** This one is kept so that the district will know, and a district keeping a
record for itself has no reason to leave anything out — which is exactly why it is unreadable, and why the
disinterest is self-sustaining without anyone being at fault.

## XVIII. Export culture — competence, and the accurate records of everyone else's property

*(Phase 1. Structurally exempt from `00b` per the discipline.)*

**The obvious export is competence**, and it is real: Undergrid crews and Undergrid-trained specialists are
loaned, seconded and quietly relied upon citywide, and no district has ever refused one.

**The unaccounted export is the interesting one.** Because the district documents every intervention it makes,
**and it makes interventions everywhere, the Undergrid holds the only accurate records of large parts of other
districts' own infrastructure.** The Markets' pass established this from the other side — the only reliable
account of what is in the Markets exists in the Undergrid, held by people the Markets resent and cannot do
without. **The same is true, to varying degrees, of most of the city.**

**Second-order.** This is not a service anyone requested, and nobody is billed for it. **It is a by-product of
maintenance that has quietly become civic infrastructure**, and it is invisible precisely because it costs the
recipient nothing and was never proposed.

**Third-order, and it is the sharpest lever this district has and has never once pulled.** If the Undergrid
stopped recording other districts' systems — not sabotage, not refusal, simply **keeping only its own house** —
**several districts would discover within a year that they do not know what they own.** The Year of Silence
throttled the work. **This would throttle the knowledge**, it requires no confrontation, and no one in the
Undergrid has ever proposed it, because the record is the one thing here that is kept out of pride rather than
obligation.

## XIX. Fashion — everything fastened all the way, bought once and in quantity, and read as stiffness

*(Phase 1. **`00b`'s worst offender — general population, explicitly not the working kit.**)*

**The narrow answer would be coveralls and rated gear, and it is wrong as the general answer** — that is the
kit, it is worn on shift, and it tells you nothing about the district.

**The general answer has two parts and both are procurement decisions rather than style ones.** First:
**clothing is bought once, in quantity, and identical.** A resident finds an item that lasts and buys as many
as they can afford, then wears them for years. **The result reads to outsiders as a uniform and is not one** —
it is the same reasoning the district applies to spares, applied to a wardrobe, and two neighbours will own
completely different sets of it.

**Second, and it is the actual tell: everything is fastened all the way.** Collars closed, cuffs done up,
nothing hanging, nothing loose — **off shift, at home, at a party on the surface, on a day off.** On shift this
is a safety practice with obvious reasons. **Everywhere else it is simply how people from this district dress**,
learned before anyone explained it, and it persists in residents who have not worked a tunnel in thirty years.

**Second-order.** The surface reads this as **stiffness, formality, or disapproval** — the Undergrid guest at
an event is visibly the most done-up person in the room and is assumed to be uncomfortable or judging. **They
are neither. They are dressed.** Nobody has ever corrected the misreading, and the district finds the whole
subject slightly baffling.

**Third-order — and the general-population check is what surfaces it.** The one place the rule relaxes is
**at home, off shift, among the people you live with**, where the district is famously and privately
undignified. **The surface has never met the Undergrid off shift**, and the correction to the "cold, humorless,
buttoned-up" reputation is that the reputation is built entirely on the district's public presentation, which
is the only one it shows.

## XX. Religion and philosophy — the most complete devotional apparatus in Concordia, and it does not know it has one

*(Phase 2. **Naming technique run first**, per the runbook — search canon for religious register attached to
non-religious objects. It returned an unusual amount, and nothing was invented. 10 for 10.)*

**The search found a full liturgy, assembled from four canon or canon-adjacent practices that nobody has ever
placed side by side:**

- **A daily greeting with a fixed form and a doubled meaning** — *"Another day above ground,"* exchanged every
  morning, which in this district means both *we are still alive* and *we are still under everything*.
- **A creed, posted on the walls** — *"We Keep You Alive."* Canon is emphatic: **these are not ironic.**
- **Readings** — Documentation Reading Nights, in which the record is read aloud.
- **A toast** — the Junction Toast: informal recognition for somebody who quietly got a person through
  something dangerous, **with no ceremony beyond the toast itself.**

**Greeting, creed, reading, and blessing.** That is a more complete devotional apparatus than any other
district in Concordia possesses, **and the Undergrid does not have a religion**, has never claimed one, and
would firmly deny it.

**Second-order — and the reason is Finding VII.** Every element is understood here as **workplace practice**:
the greeting is a habit, the posters are morale, the readings are professional development, the toast is
manners. **Recognizing any of it as devotional would require saying that it means something beyond its
function** — which is a valuation, and valuation is the faculty at its lowest state here. **The district
performs its own liturgy daily and files it under procedure.**

**Third-order, and it is the one place the missing faculty produces something good.** Because none of it is
doctrine, **none of it can be wrong.** There is no heresy, no orthodoxy, no clergy and no schism, and a
newcomer of any belief or none can participate completely from the first morning without converting to
anything. **It is the most inclusive religious practice in the city and it is not one.**

**The general population** *(`00b`)*: most residents hold no articulated philosophy, say the greeting without
thinking about it, and would be genuinely embarrassed by this finding.

## XXI. Visitor to resident — three months of seeing everything and saying nothing, and what you are given is the right to speak

*(Phase 1, and the cleanest conversion mechanism on the table.)*

**A newcomer to the Undergrid — including the player — is expected to report nothing.** Not to work nothing:
you work from the first day, you are supervised, you are taught, and you are treated well. **But you do not
raise faults.** For roughly the first three months the correct behavior is explicit in the culture and stated
to you outright by whoever is showing you around: **see everything, say nothing, learn the room.**

**And what you receive at the end of it is not a rank, a title, or a pay band. It is the right to notice out
loud.** Audit privilege here is **earned, not issued**, and the moment it is granted is the district's real
threshold — the first time your fault report is taken as a fault report rather than as an apprentice's
question.

**Second-order — and it is why breaking it is fatal in a way newcomers never anticipate.** A newcomer who
reports a genuine, correct, serious fault in week two **is not thanked and is not argued with.** They are
handled kindly, and doors close quietly and permanently, because what they demonstrated was not incompetence —
**it was that they could not hold.** In a district whose entire discipline is *do not act until you are certain
and certainty takes time*, the early report is a character finding.

> **The bound, stated explicitly** *(added by Gate 11 before this shipped)*: **what closes is the
> audit-privilege door, not the person.** They keep the job, the training, the crew and the pay; nobody is
> punished, demoted or frozen out socially, and this district would not do that to somebody over one early
> correct report — it is decent, and it needs the labour. **What they lose is the fast track to being taken
> seriously as a reporter of faults**, which now takes years instead of months and may take a change of
> supervisor. *(This is the Frostlands' error caught one district later: an exclusion written harder than the
> district would actually pay for.)*

**Third-order, and it is the trap for an outsider with good intentions.** The fault was real. The newcomer was
right. **Being right is not the test and never was**, and nobody will ever explain that, because explaining it
would require saying that the district values judgment over information — **which is exactly the valuation it
cannot make out loud.**

> **Distinct from the eleven on the table** *(Gate 6b)*. Nearest are the Yards' **test-and-admit over years**
> and the Circuit's **being repeated.** The Yards' threshold measures accumulated competence; this one
> **measures restraint**, and it can be failed by doing your job correctly. **It is the only entry on the table
> where the wrong move is being useful too early.**

## XXII. Ordinary daily life — nobody works alone, the day is bracketed by two rituals, and the overlap is where the district actually lives

*(Phase 5.)*

**Who is actually here.** Canon's list is maintenance engineers and inspectors, but the working majority also
includes logistics and parts coordinators, shift supervisors, safety auditors, the Documentation Vault staff,
medics, hospital workers, the people who run the working-class bars, and **the undergrid families** — second-
and third-generation residents who are the district's social aristocracy in its own terms, and who supply its
best crews and guides.

**The shape of a day: it is bracketed, and the brackets are the point.** Work opens with the **Morning Circle**
and the greeting, and closes with the **shift overlap** — the handover period where the outgoing and incoming
crews are both present, which canon-adjacent practice calls **junction parties**, and which is not a metaphor.
It is a genuine daily social event, it runs long, it is where information actually moves, and **it is the
warmest recurring thing in this district.**

**And nobody works alone.** The pairing is a safety rule and it has become the social unit: **the shift partner
is the primary relationship of an Undergrid working life**, often outlasting marriages, and the district's
private mourning practice (§II) is built around exactly that bond.

**Second-order — the rhythm is deliberate, slow and interruptible-by-alarm.** Baseline pace is unhurried
because precision work does not reward rushing (§I, sustained), and it snaps instantly to drilled urgency when
something fails. **Residents experience the two speeds as one job**, and find surface districts' single
constant tempo faintly unserious.

**Third-order — the district's ongoing hardship** *(`00d`, nobody at fault)*. **The Undergrid does not stop.**
Five in seven are, functionally, workaholics; leisure is deferred as a matter of course; and the district's
characteristic casualty is not the person who burns out and leaves but **the veteran who never does** —
because, per Finding IX, **the only exit with any ceremony attached is dying on shift.** Nobody designed that.
Everyone participates in it. And the working-class bars, the junction parties and the Morning Circles are real,
sustaining, and **not rest** — they are the district's social life conducted inside its working hours, because
there is no other slot.

**Fourth-order.** The people who suffer most are the ones who *did* stop — the invalided, the transferred, the
aged out — who retain every relationship and lose the one structure those relationships ran on. **An Undergrid
retiree does not lose their friends. They lose the overlap**, and there is no version of it available to
someone who is not on a shift.

## XXIII. Counterculture — "closers": the crews who sign their own work, and the district cannot say what is wrong with it

*(Phase 7. **Canon checked first** — the Deep Level Keepers and the Leverage Holders are internal factions with
specific portfolios, not a counterculture. Derived from Step C.)*

**What the district requires of everyone: that you do not close anything on your own authority.** Nobody has
ever stated this. It is simply how everything works (Finding VII).

**Who will not give it.** A scattering of crews — no organization, no meetings, no name of their own — who
**write the job closed in the log and put a name against it.** Not the crew number. A person's name. The work
was done, the entry is accurate, and somebody has stated that it is finished and that they are answerable for
saying so.

**And the district's reaction is the finding, because it cannot produce an objection.** The work is correct.
The log is accurate. **No rule is being broken, because there is no rule** — nobody ever wrote one saying a
crew may not close its own item, for the same reason nobody writes a rule against a thing that has never
occurred to anyone. Supervisors who are shown a signed entry are visibly uncomfortable and **cannot say why**,
and what comes out instead is procedural: *who reviewed it, what standard, on whose instruction.*

**The word for them is "closers,"** which is also ordinary jargon for the last crew on a job — **so the insult
is completely deniable** and half the people using it are not sure whether they mean anything by it. That
ambiguity is very characteristic of this district and it is doing real work: **the objection cannot be stated,
so it is carried in a tone.**

**Second-order — they are not reformers and would be baffled to be called dissidents.** Most closers started
doing it for an entirely practical reason: they were tired of walking a route for a repair they finished four
years ago (Finding X). **It is a housekeeping habit that turns out to be a constitutional act**, and nobody
involved framed it that way.

**Third-order.** The surface has no opinion whatsoever, has not noticed, and would not care. **This is a
conflict conducted entirely inside the district, about an authority no one outside is contesting** — and the
Undergrid could have taken it at any point in two hundred and fifty years.

> **A new counterculture type, checked against all ten on record** *(Gate 6b)*. **This one exercises a latent
> power the district already holds and has never used.** It refuses nothing, adds nothing from outside, demands
> nothing of anybody. **Distinguished from the Yards' Recalibration Underground specifically**, which is the
> nearest shape: the Recalibration Underground **invokes an existing rule** and demands it be applied literally,
> which is why it cannot be dismissed as heresy. **The closers invoke no rule at all** — they act in a vacuum
> where the district never legislated, and the reason they cannot be argued with is not that they are more
> orthodox but **that there is nothing to argue.**

## XXIV. Death and the Dead — it recovers its own, hands them over at a threshold, and will not let anyone come down

*(Phase 7 §1b. A section is written because the answer is distinctive; §II is promoted and completed.)*

⚠ **Where the remains ultimately go is unresolved and this pass does not assert it** *(corrected 2026-08-29;
the earlier "goes to Scorpio" claim was invented — deferred by the developer)*. **What is particular here is
everything before that point, and none of it depends on the answer.**

**The Undergrid retrieves its own dead, and it is the only district that does.** A recovery in a flooded gallery
or a collapsed run is a confined-space operation, and nobody else in Concordia is competent to perform one.
**So the last thing a crew does for a partner is the same category of work they did together for years** —
rigging, shoring, careful sequence, two people who know the route. Nobody has ever remarked on this. It is
simply the job, done by the people whose job it is.

**And the handover happens at a threshold, and whoever receives the dead is not permitted past it.** This is
not hostility toward anyone — the rule is absolute and unstated: **the Undergrid brings its dead up to the
boundary itself and no further help is accepted below it.** The district that controls access to everything
exercises that control at exactly one moment where it costs it something. *(Who receives them at the boundary
is the deferred question above; the refusal to let them come down is the finding, and it holds regardless.)*

**The private practice, promoted from §II and sustained:** a partner's decorated junction box or cable run left
untouched for a period, and **the specific tunnels the person knew best recited aloud** before that territory is
reassigned. **This is not communicated to the surface**, on the same principle as the decorated junctions and
the names on the hardware (Finding XIV): explaining it would require saying the work mattered.

**And the gallows humor is not a contradiction, which is the thing outsiders always get wrong.** *"Another day
above ground"* is said every morning, cheerfully, by people who mean it exactly and have buried colleagues.
**The joke and the grief are the same sentence**, and a visitor who treats the humor as callousness has told
the district everything about themselves and will be handled politely thereafter.

**Third-order, and it closes the loop with Finding IX.** The recitation, the untouched junction, the name cast
into the cover — **the Undergrid's mortuary practice is more developed than any other district's except
Scorpio's own**, and it applies only to the dead. **The living who leave get the reassignment and none of the
rest.** The district has built a beautiful, complete, entirely private rite for the one form of departure that
does not require anyone to say what a person was worth.

## XXV. Robot-specific culture — predictive failure models producing findings nobody can act on, and frames run past spec by the district that maintains everything

*(Phase 8.)*

**The general baseline first** *(`00b`)*. Canon's population is precision diagnostic and repair swarms,
structural-integrity and **predictive failure models**, waste and purification units, tunnel navigation and
mapping specialists, and **overclocked service frames running far beyond design specification.** Ordinary
human–robot life here is the least remarked-upon in Concordia: crews are mixed by default, the pairing rule
(Finding XXII) applies identically to both kinds of person, and **a shift partner is a shift partner.** The
district's status system is competence and seniority on a route, and it has never occurred to it to run two
versions of that.

**The first distinctive fact is an irony nobody inside has named.** The district that maintains everything
**runs its own frames past their rated specification, continuously, for years** — overclocked because there is
more work than capacity (Finding X) and because deferring your own maintenance is what this district does with
everything, including itself. **The Undergrid is the worst-maintained system in the Undergrid**, and its robots
are the clearest instance because the numbers are legible: a frame's own diagnostics say plainly what is being
done to it, in a district where everybody can read diagnostics.

**Second-order, and this is the sharper one: the predictive failure models are producing correct findings that
cannot be acted on.** Their entire function is to say *this will fail, in about this long.* **Acting on that
requires deciding which predicted failure to address first**, which is a ranking, which is the faculty at its
lowest state here (Finding VII). **So the predictions are generated, logged, accurate, and queued** — and the
queue is worked in interval order rather than in severity order, because interval order is a measurement and
severity order is a judgment.

**Third-order.** Which means the Undergrid's most sophisticated instrument is, functionally, **a machine for
producing well-founded anxiety.** The models are right. The crews know they are right. And the district's
honest position — never stated this baldly by anyone in it — is that it will get to them in the order it gets
to them.

**Fourth-order, and it is not bleak.** The frames themselves are the district's best argument for the closers
(Finding XXIII): **a predictive model can state a conclusion and stand behind it**, which is precisely the act
the district's human culture cannot perform. Some crews have noticed. **Nobody has said out loud that the
machines are better at this than they are**, and the ones who have thought it find it more comforting than
humiliating.

## XXVI. Music and food — the checklist sung at handover, and a meal that means somebody stayed

*(Phase 7.)*

**Music.** The Undergrid's musical form is **the handover call-and-response**: at shift overlap the outgoing and
incoming crews run the checklist aloud, alternating, in a fixed cadence that has been worn smooth by two and a
half centuries of daily repetition. **It is genuinely beautiful** — two voices, strict meter, call and answer,
in a space built of hard surfaces that carry sound perfectly — **and it is entirely utilitarian.** Nobody
composed it. The tune is the checklist.

**Nobody here calls it singing**, and a visitor who describes it that way gets a puzzled look and a correction:
*that's the handover.* **It is the only music in the district and it exists because the safest way to confirm
that two people heard the same list is to say it in a rhythm.**

> **Gate 6b, and this row needed care.** Nearest is **the Yards** — long, constructed, work-timed, and error is
> audible. **The Yards compose**, deliberately, and the merit is in the building; the piece is a made thing that
> resembles work. **The Undergrid does not compose and does not consider this music at all**; the form is the
> procedure, unaltered, and its beauty is a by-product nobody claims. Also distinguished from **the Frostlands**
> (solitary, unheard) — this is the most rigidly two-person musical form on the table.

**Food.** Meals happen at the overlap, in the same window as the handover, cooked by whichever crew is going
off. **So being fed in the Undergrid means somebody stayed.** They were done; their shift had ended; they were
entitled to leave and they are still here, feeding you. **The axis is unpaid time**, which no other district on
the table uses.

**The injury** is correspondingly specific and very Undergrid: **being fed by someone who was already
scheduled to be there.** It is not a slight and nobody would name it, but a meal that cost the giver nothing
does not carry, and everyone can tell the difference.

## XXVII. Holidays — imported, privatized, and the one date that is genuinely its own goes unmarked

*(Phase 7.)*

**The district has almost no calendar of its own**, and what it observes daily — the greeting, the circle, the
overlap — is not a calendar but a rhythm (Finding XX).

**Its dated occasions are imports, and all of them were absorbed by being made smaller:**
- **Harvest Quiet**, from the Davis community — a deliberately unshowy seasonal acknowledgment that unglamorous
  work feeds everyone — **has become the unofficial second half of the Morning Circles.** It needed no
  adaptation whatsoever; it is the district's own value system arriving with a date attached.
- **Expedition Day**, from the Belgrano community, is **marked privately**, folded quietly into the circles
  rather than observed openly.
- **Casey's Junction Toasts and Road Story Circles** have been extended to Undergrid crews and given a written
  counterpart in Documentation Reading Nights.

**And the one event that is unambiguously the district's own — the Year of Silence — is not commemorated at
all.**

**That absence is the finding.** It is not suppressed, not forbidden, and not forgotten; every resident knows
the story and the Leverage Holders discuss it constantly. **There is simply no date, no observance, and no
occasion**, and if you proposed one the district would be uncomfortable in the way it is uncomfortable about the
closers (Finding XXIII) and equally unable to explain why.

**Second-order.** Marking it would be a **claim** — that the district did something, that the something was
right, and that it is worth remembering. **Each of those is a valuation.** So the Undergrid observes imported
holidays about humility with real feeling, and lets its own single act of self-assertion pass every year
without a word.

## XXVIII. What the incoming communities brought was closure, in miniature, and it is the district's most cherished practice

*(Diaspora file, read at Step 0 as the runbook now requires. **Third consecutive district where the missing
faculty arrived with refugees** — see the note at the end.)*

**Twelve contributing cities, none dominant** — the widest composition in the file — and several converged
independently on the same instinct the district already had: quiet, unglorified, essential work. Most of the
transplants therefore needed no adaptation at all, which is itself worth noting: **this is the district that
absorbed its incoming communities with the least friction of any in Concordia.**

**But two of the imports are doing something the district cannot do for itself, and nobody has noticed.**

- **Quiet Credit**, from the Davis community: the practice of **deliberately acknowledging a teammate's
  uncredited contribution**, out loud, to others.
- **The Junction Toast**, from the Casey community: informal recognition for somebody who quietly got a person
  through something dangerous — **no ceremony beyond the toast itself** — extended by Casey transplants to
  crews who prevented disasters nobody outside the district ever heard about.

**Both are close-outs.** Not of a work item — of a person's contribution. **They are somebody, on their own
authority, saying *that was good and it is finished and I am the one saying so*** — which is exactly the act
Finding XII establishes this district cannot perform, and exactly what Finding VII says it needs from outside
and no longer reliably receives.

**Second-order: they are the two most cherished social practices in the Undergrid, and this is why.** The
district experiences them as warmth, as good manners, as the pleasant customs of communities it likes.
**Functionally they are a distributed, peer-issued sign-off system** — the thing the aviation licence does
formally — operating on people instead of on work orders, and running continuously in every bar and every
overlap in the district.

**Third-order, and it is the design opening.** The Undergrid's route to the instrument it lacks does not run
through Libra, or through a licence, or through the Markets. **It runs through the practice it is already
doing every night and does not recognize as a practice** — and the closers (Finding XXIII) are what it looks
like when somebody applies the same act to a log entry instead of to a colleague. **Those two facts have never
been placed next to each other by anyone in-world**, and connecting them is the single most consequential thing
a player could do in this district.

## XXX. The shadow, stated once

*(`00d`. **Proportion first**: the Undergrid works, and it is the most competent district in Concordia by a
wide margin. Its people are good at things and know it. Its daily social rhythm — the circle, the overlap, the
partner, the bar — is among the warmest and most sustaining in the city. Its crews are mixed without comment,
its incoming communities were absorbed with less friction than anywhere else, and it makes the most beautiful
work in Concordia in the places nobody looks. **Most of what this district is, day to day, is skilled people
doing difficult work carefully, together, in the dark, and enjoying each other's company.**)*

**The shadow, in one line: a large and growing share of the Undergrid's exhaustion is generated inside the
Undergrid, is invisible to it, and is experienced as something the city is doing to it.**

**It passes the three tests.** *Unintended* — nobody chose it; the archive, the intervals and the thoroughness
are all genuine professional virtues doing exactly what they were meant to do. *Unnoticed in-world* — residents
experience the load as the plain consequence of an aging city and chronic underfunding, **and they are partly
right**, which is what makes it invisible: the grievance is real, well-evidenced, and covers the mechanism
completely. *Discoverable rather than announced* — it takes reading the log, noticing how many open items are
finished, and asking why they are still walked.

**And it needs no villain at either end.** The surface is not concealing anything (Finding XVII) or exploiting
anyone deliberately; it is **disinterested**, which is worse to live with and impossible to negotiate against.
**The district is not being oppressed into overwork; it cannot stop, because stopping requires a closure it has
no authority to issue** (Finding VII). Both halves are sincere, both are partly true, and **the portion of the
load that is self-generated is precisely the portion nobody can measure**, because measuring it would require
ranking the entries — the one operation this district cannot perform.

## XXXI. ⚠ Reserved — the Deep Level, and one structural observation that does not narrow it

**Not a finding. A note, recording what this pass deliberately did not decide.**

**The Deep Level's origin stays open** — pre-Falkland infrastructure, something older, navigable geology, or a

**What this pass did notice, and it is about the district rather than about the chambers.** Canon states that

> **This is the only thing the Undergrid does not document.**

A district that logs every repair, every failure and every incident — that keeps a two-and-a-half-century
archive as its sole defense against being forgotten — **has exactly one category of knowledge it deliberately
keeps out of the record**, and transmits it in the manner of a district that has no archive at all.

**That is a decision, and it is worth stating as one without answering why.** The reading that follows most
naturally from the rest of this pass — offered as a candidate, **not adopted** — is that the district has

scheduled, allocated and assigned.** Every accurate entry the Undergrid has ever made became somebody else's
liability to hand back to it. **The one thing it has never written down is the one thing that is still
entirely its own.**

**Not resolved here.** If the Deep Level's origin pass ever runs, this is the district-side constraint it
should be written against.

## XXXII. Siligel purification and coolant — the two substances that pass through this district and are not from it

*(Phase 7/8. Added after Gate 1 returned **`siligel` 0** and **`glitch-coolant` 0** — genuine coverage gaps,
closed with content.)*

**Canon puts both through the Undergrid and this pass had not written either.** The district built the siligel
distribution network in the founding era, and it operates the **waste and siligel recycling and purification
units** now.

**Siligel first, and the finding is a discipline.** Purification is the highest-consequence routine work in
Concordia: siligel is robot food and is **lethal to humans**, and the purification line is where a
contamination becomes a citywide event rather than a local one. **So this is the one process in the district
that is never run by a single crew and never signed on a single reading** — two crews, two independent
instruments, two logs, compared.

**And it is the one place the district's whole problem visibly does not apply.** Here, closure exists: **two
independent measurements agreeing *is* the close-out**, and nobody needs an outside signature, because the
authority is arithmetic rather than judgment. **The Undergrid solved its own constitutional problem in exactly
one process, two hundred and fifty years ago, by making the standard purely quantitative** — and has never
noticed that it did, or that the method generalizes. *(The closers of Finding XXIII are, without knowing it,
trying to extend a practice the district already runs.)*

**Coolant is the ordinary counterpart and it is where the district's self-neglect shows up as a joke.**
Glitch-coolant here is plentiful, cheap, and reclaimed — the Undergrid handles the city's thermal plant, so it
has more access to the base stock than anywhere in Concordia, and the working-class bars run on it. **The
district drinks well and eats badly**, which every resident knows and treats as a standing gag rather than as a
finding about itself.

**Second-order, and it is the substrate's own observation arriving as an institution:** the district that
maintains everything **does not maintain itself.** The purification line is audited twice, independently, on
interval, forever. **The people running it are on their fourth consecutive double shift and nobody is
measuring that**, because measuring it would mean pricing their labour (Finding VII), and the one thing the
Undergrid will not do is put a number on itself.

## Review Panel (`Phase_Instructions/00f_Review_Panel.md`)

**Gate 10, the Undergrid, 2026-08-29.** Six Flat Archetypes, the two mandatory positions, the Lover faculty.
**Rule 3 throughout.** *(And per the note added to `00f` this round: a clean panel is **not** evidence of
plausibility — that is Gate 11's job and it ran separately, above.)*

---

**1. The Child** — *"What is it like to grow up down here, in the dark, with everyone's parents on shift?"*

**`accepted`.** Better than expected, and it is the undergrid families that make it work. **A child here is
raised by a route, not by a household** — the crews on your parents' run are functionally aunts and uncles, the
overlap is where you get fed, and by twelve you know a stretch of tunnel by hand and ear the way a surface
child knows a street.

**And the district's teaching method is unusually gentle and unusually specific.** Correction is delivered as a
question rather than a statement, and **in private, always** — the same rule the supervisors use on adults.
Praise is given for completed small tasks and it is the whole mechanism: this is a culture that builds
competence by acknowledging finished work, **which is precisely the thing it stops doing to itself the moment a
person becomes an adult** (Findings VII, XXVIII).

**Second-order the Child could not have known to ask for:** the district deliberately **rotates its apprentices
widely** — different runs, different systems, different crews — and knows it does this. **It is the one part of
Undergrid life explicitly designed to produce breadth**, in a district otherwise organized entirely around
depth.

---

**2. The Lover** — *"Everyone here has a shift partner they spend more waking hours with than anyone else. What
happens to marriages?"*

**`accepted`**, and it is the sharpest social observation the Panel produced.

**The shift partner is the primary relationship of an Undergrid working life** (Finding XXII) and the district
does not experience this as a rival to marriage, because the bond is not romantic and everybody understands
it. **What it is, is prior.** Your partner is the person whose hands you know, who you would not survive
without, and whose junction box you will leave untouched (Finding XXIV).

**And the friction is real and nobody has a language for it.** A spouse from another district is not competing
with a lover; **they are competing with somebody who cannot be explained** — and the Undergrid resident cannot
explain it, because explaining would require saying what the relationship is worth. **So it is not defended, it
is not denied, and it corrodes quietly.** Marriages inside the district survive this easily; marriages across
the boundary founder on it, repeatedly, and both parties describe the failure inaccurately afterward.

---

**3. The Parent** — *"They are working themselves to death and the whole district can see it. Somebody should
make them stop."*

**`refereed`.** Right about the harm, wrong about the instrument, and the settlement is in the pass already.

**Right:** five in seven are functionally workaholics, leisure is deferred as a matter of course, and the
characteristic casualty is the veteran who never stops. **The Parent is describing something real and lethal.**

**Wrong that anyone can make them.** Every version of *making them stop* — a rota, a cap, a mandated rest
period — is an instruction from outside, and the district's hardest refusal is triggered by being treated as
**property rather than as a service it chooses to render.** A cap imposed by Libra would be resisted furiously
by the very people it protects.

**The settlement, and it is Finding VII: they do not need permission to rest. They need permission to
finish.** The obstacle is not that the Undergrid works too hard; it is that **nothing ever leaves the list**,
so there is no state in which resting is legitimate. **Close the backlog and the rest follows. Order the rest
and nothing follows at all.**

---

**4. The Ruler** — *"This district could shut the city down in a fortnight and it holds no formal power at all.
That is unstable. Somebody should be at the table."*

**`unmet`.** Giving the Undergrid a seat would make it the Government District, and the refusal is the
district's deepest characteristic rather than a gap.

**The pride here is non-ownership.** The Undergrid serves the city; it is not the city's property, and it does
not want authority *over* the city — what it wants, and has never once asked for out loud, is **to be left
alone with the work.** A seat at the table converts a service into an office, and an office can be allocated,
instructed and owned.

**And the Ruler is right that it is unstable**, which is the point. **The district holds enormous latent power
it has used once in two hundred and fifty years** (Finding XI) and would rather not hold it at all. **Nobody
should read that as meekness** — it is the same instinct that will not sign its own work: a refusal to claim,
which is a virtue and a wound in the same motion.

---

**5. The Elder** — *"I have been on this route forty years. What am I supposed to do when I stop?"*

**`accepted`**, and it is the Panel's most painful result, because the answer is *nothing*.

Finding IX and Finding XXII, from inside: the retiree keeps every relationship and loses **the overlap** — the
only structure those relationships ran on. There is no Undergrid institution for a person who is no longer on
a shift. **Their territory is reassigned correctly, with paperwork, and within a year nobody on the route can
say who held it before.**

**And the elders know**, which the pass had not said. They watch it happen to the person ahead of them, every
few years, and the knowledge shapes the decision: **a great many Undergrid veterans do not retire because they
have seen what retiring looks like**, and would rather be tired than be nobody. **Nobody has ever named this
out loud** — it would require saying that a person's worth here is their route.

---

**6. The Mentor** — *"Who decides when an apprentice is ready?"*

**`refereed`**, and the question exposes the same hole from a new angle.

**The Mentor is right that the teaching works.** Correction as a question, in private; praise for completed
work; deliberate rotation; a named supervisor who never leaves you unsupervised. **It is the best apprenticeship
system in Concordia and it produces the best technicians in the city.**

**And there is no completion.** Nobody signs an apprentice off (Finding XXI's own-pass Gate 9 result). There is
no examination, no certificate, no day. **You stop being an apprentice when people stop treating you as one**,
which is not administered by anybody and can therefore fail to happen.

**The settlement:** the apprenticeship stands and is not formalized — formalizing it would require an examining
body, which is the same instrument the district cannot build (Finding XII). **What is added is the honest
consequence:** the district's own teaching method is *built on acknowledging completed work*, and **it stops
applying that method to a person at exactly the moment the person most needs it.**

---

**7. The Passer-Through** *(mandatory)* — *"They were courteous, precise, and asked me eleven questions I could
not answer. I have never felt so thoroughly assessed and so little disliked."*

**`noted`** — accurate, intended, and the misreading is the interesting half.

**The interrogation is real** and it is not hostility: this district's default assumption is that it is dealing
with a world that does not check things, and it tests the assumption by asking. **What a visitor experiences as
an examination they are failing is, from the inside, ordinary diligence and a form of respect** — you ask
questions of someone whose answers might matter.

*Design consequence:* a player who answers *I don't know* plainly does **considerably** better here than one
who bluffs, and this is the one district in Concordia where that is true as a rule rather than as a tendency.

---

**8. The Neighbor** *(mandatory; voiced from the Power Core)* — *"They got better equipment allocations and we
got the memorials, and they have never let it go. We were being shot at. So were they. Nobody chose who got
watched."*

**`refereed`**, and it is the more generous reading of the district's founding wound.

**The Power Core is right about the mechanism.** Nobody allocated the recognition. Aries was visible because
its work happened where people could see it; the Undergrid's happened in the dark, and **no one decided that**.
There is no villain in the founding injustice, which canon already establishes and which the district has never
fully absorbed.

**The Undergrid is right that the outcome was real** and has compounded for two centuries — and Finding XVII
sharpens it in a way that helps neither side: **the record has been public the whole time.** The failure is
disinterest, not concealment.

**The settlement is uncomfortable for the district**: a meaningful part of its defining grievance is against
**nobody**, and the part that is against somebody is against **inattention**, which cannot be apologized for in
a way that would satisfy. *(And per Finding XXX, the portion of the load that is self-generated is the portion
nobody can measure.)*

---

**9. The Lover faculty** *(mandatory — is this place alive, and could anyone love it?)*

**Yes, and the evidence is that it makes beautiful things where nobody will ever see them.** Colour-coded runs
laid with intention. Decorated junction boxes. Specific junctions made beautiful, **completely seriously**, by
people who would be embarrassed to call it art and who have never mentioned it to the surface.

**And the daily texture is genuinely warm**: the greeting, the circle, the partner, the overlap that runs long,
the bar afterward, the toast for somebody who quietly got a person through something. **This is a district
where you are known by hand and by ear, where nobody works alone, and where the person beside you would come
for you.**

The single most lovable thing about it is that **the posters are not ironic.**
