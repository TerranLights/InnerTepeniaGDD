# Pisces — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Pisces given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Pisces_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-09.

---

## I. The getting-lost feeling is both — the shifting-walls mechanic is real, but crowding and clutter are what a visitor notices first

**Proposed:** the district's own canon already establishes genuine architectural instability (walls move, entrances appear and disappear, routes shift week to week) as "partly practical security design and partly an expression of the district's philosophy about identity and permanence." Given the Vision Notes' own emphasis on crowding and clutter as the dominant street-level texture, the two aren't competing explanations — they're layered. A newcomer gets lost first from ordinary density and disorientation (too many people, too much clutter, no clear sightlines), and only later, once they've been in Pisces long enough to notice a route repeating differently, realizes the deeper instability is real. The architectural trick works precisely because it hides behind an entirely mundane first impression.

---

## II. "Stepping on the wrong toes" means violating the district's information economy, not its criminal hierarchy

**Proposed:** canon already establishes that access to significant Markets operations runs on an information economy — every transaction of consequence requires sharing something true and vulnerable about yourself. Given this, "the wrong toes" plausibly isn't primarily about crossing a specific crime boss or syndicate (the conventional black-market-district assumption) — it's about breaking the informal rules of that information economy: extracting someone's shared vulnerability and using it against them, refusing to reciprocate disclosure while still expecting access, or exposing someone's true secret to a party outside Pisces. The district's actual enforcement mechanism is reputational and informational before it's physical — violence is what happens after the information economy's own trust has already been broken, not the district's first line of consequence.

## III. The purple/blue/red light bath is atmospheric first, but has acquired religious meaning it never had at the start

**Proposed:** unlike the Circuit's light-tracks (established as literally representing data in motion, from the district's own founding purpose), Pisces' light palette has no equivalent functional origin — it's plausibly just atmospheric, a byproduct of dense signage, dream-tech den advertising, and clinic equipment glow accumulating over 250 years. But given the Syncretic Religion's own two-century absorption of "everyone else's spiritual overflow," it's very plausible the Believers have retroactively assigned meaning to the ambient light anyway — not because it was designed to carry meaning, but because a religion built on dissolving the boundary between self and environment would naturally start reading its own environment as significant. The light didn't start meaningful. Fifty years of a syncretic religion looking for signs in everything around it was enough to make it so.

## V. Pisces residents do informally sort by district of origin, but the categories blur faster than Gemini's Janbogo/Zukelli split

**Proposed:** given the deep dive's own framing of the Markets as "a live record of exactly how well or badly each direct-response district's method actually works," it's plausible that residents do recognize informal categories — ex-Scorpio dissolution-seekers who couldn't complete confrontation-based transformation, ex-Leo performers who burned out, ex-Aquarius test subjects who left mid-experiment. Unlike the Circuit's Janbogo/Zukelli refugee populations, though, which remain durably distinguishable communities, the Markets' whole cultural premise (walls that move, identity as something that dissolves) works against categories staying fixed. A resident might be identifiably "ex-Scorpio" for their first year in Pisces and simply "of the Markets" by their fifth — the district's own philosophy of dissolution applies to its residents' origin-labels as much as to anything else.

## VII. It can price anything in Concordia and cannot prove what anything is

**The structural spine.** *(Capability reading, `F_Rulerships.md` §6 D10.)*

**This is a shape the typology had not named — the *doubled row*, and there are exactly two of them.** The
Undergrid is the only district whose ruling faculty is also its amplified one: verification at maximum, twice
over. **The Markets are the only district where one faculty holds both weak terms at once: verification at
minimum, twice over.** Every other district has its strengths and deficits spread across different faculties.
These two have theirs **stacked on the same one, in opposite directions.**

**So the question the shape asks is not "what does the balance cost?" — there is no balance. It is: *which
faculty is doubled, and in which direction?*** Here: **record, measurement, provenance and audit, at the
lowest state the system can express.** Against that sits the highest valuing capacity in the city.

**The consequence, and it is not "there is crime here."**

> **The Markets can tell you exactly what a thing is worth and cannot tell you what it is.**

Appraisal is genuinely world-class. A Markets trader will name a value to within a hair, faster and more
accurately than anyone in Concordia, on an object they have never seen before. **And that same trader cannot
tell you where it came from, whether it is the one you sold last month, or whether there are four more.**

**Third-order — and this is the district, not the crime.** If nothing can be proven singular, then **nothing
here is functionally unique.** Everything is fungible by default, not because the district wants it that way
but because uniqueness is a claim that requires an instrument nobody has. **The thing that cannot be traded in
the Markets is one-of-a-kind-ness**, and everything the district does downstream — the salvage, the
recirculation, the dissolution trade, the fluid identities — follows from operating in a world where
singularity cannot be established.

**Fourth-order, and it is the quiet tragedy.** A district that cannot prove what a thing is **cannot prove
ownership of its own things either.** It cannot protect what it wants to keep, cannot document what was taken
from it, and cannot demonstrate that it was ever owed anything. The substrate's personal-scale version of this
is played as comedy — a resident who has lost three devices in two years and laughs it off. **At district
scale it is not comic. It is why the Markets have been robbed continuously for two hundred and fifty years and
have never once been able to say so in a form anyone else would accept.**

### What the Undergrid's pass will need from this

**Do not write the Undergrid as the Markets inverted.** Same faculty, opposite setting, but the *consequences*
must be as unrelated as Leo's and the Labs' were. What the pairing does supply, and what the Undergrid pass
should build on rather than rediscover:

- **The complementarity is exact and neither district can use it.** The Undergrid can prove what everything is
  and — its own separate deficit — **cannot say what any of it is worth.** The Markets can price anything and
  cannot prove what it is. **Each holds precisely the instrument the other's whole economy is missing.**
- **The relationship already has contradictory testimony in the substrate and it must be preserved as
  contradictory.** From the Undergrid's side this is one of the few genuinely rewarding partnerships it has —
  warm, specific, mutually useful. **From the Markets' side it is suffocating, humiliating, and slowly
  fatal.** Both are sincere. Both are accurate about their own experience. **No document settles it and a
  player attempting to mediate has nothing to mediate with.**
- **The reason is in Finding XI**: accepting the Undergrid's verification means accepting the Undergrid's
  authority over *what things are* — which is the one thing the Markets cannot concede without ceasing to be
  the Markets.

## VIII. The Flood was not a memory event. It was a record failure — the district's capability deficit at catastrophe scale

**Nobody has put these two facts next to each other, and they explain each other completely.**

`10b`'s confirmed mechanism is explicit and worth quoting exactly: **"nothing about anyone's actual memories
changed. Only the bookkeeping — the literal `owner` field… broke, cluster by cluster, in a way that never
fully repaired itself."** Dozens of small simultaneous local collapses, no external trigger, no author, an
interdependent network crossing its own accumulated threshold.

**Set that against Finding VII.** This is the district whose faculty of record and verification sits at the
lowest state the system can express — and **its defining historical catastrophe is a pure failure of record,
with the underlying content entirely intact.** Not a fire, not a plague, not an attack. **The ledger of whose
memories were whose stopped working, and the memories were fine.**

**That is not a coincidence to be noted. It is the same fact at two scales**, and it makes the Flood
characteristic rather than arbitrary: the one catastrophe this district was structurally guaranteed to have,
and the one it was structurally least equipped to reconstruct afterward. **A district with a functioning
audit faculty could have rebuilt the ownership map.** This one could not, which is why some clusters never
stabilized and are, in some quiet sense, still happening.

**Second-order:** the Markets are therefore the only district in Concordia where **the question "is this
memory mine?" is a practical, ordinary, non-philosophical question** — asked the way you would ask whether a
coat is yours. It is not mysticism. It is a bookkeeping problem that was never closed.

**Third-order — and this is where it stops resembling anywhere else.** Because the content was never damaged,
**a scrambled memory is perfectly accurate.** Everything in it is true; it happened; the details check out.
Only the attribution is wrong. So a Flood-affected resident cannot be helped by evidence, by testimony, or by
being shown records — **corroboration confirms the memory and says nothing about whose it is.** The one
question they need answered is the only one no evidence can touch.

**Fourth-order.** That is the empirical bedrock the Syncretic Religion's doctrine was built on fifty years
later, and it explains why the doctrine is not a comfort or a metaphor here. **For a meaningful number of
people it is a straightforwardly accurate description of their situation**, and the religion's real
achievement was not explaining the Flood — it was **being the first framework that did not require the
question to have an answer.**

## IX. ⚠ GATE 9 — the information economy takes disclosures and has no mechanism for withdrawing one

**Gate 9 fired again — the ninth consecutive district, and for the ninth consecutive time the defect was in
the pre-Plan 2026-07-09 material.**

**What was written one-way.** §II establishes that access to significant Markets operations runs on an
information economy: **every transaction of consequence requires sharing something true and vulnerable about
yourself.** That is a conversion mechanism, and the existing text writes only the inbound half — what you give,
and what it buys. **Nothing anywhere says what becomes of the thing you gave.**

**The answer follows from Finding VII rather than being invented for the gap.** You can stop transacting. You
cannot un-disclose — and more to the point, **the district cannot tell you who currently holds what you
said**, because holding that information would require the one faculty it does not have. There is no register
of disclosures, no expiry, no custodian, and nobody to petition.

**So exposure here accumulates monotonically and never decays.** A resident of forty years is the most exposed
person in Concordia — and the material is held by people who have died, moved, forgotten, or passed it on
casually in a transaction of their own. **Nobody is blackmailing them. There is no cabal. The material is
simply out there, unindexed, permanently, in an unknown number of hands.**

**Third-order, and it re-reads a piece of canon.** This is the real content of "stepping on the wrong toes."
The danger is not that a specific person will use something against you. **It is that the district cannot tell
you who could** — so the risk is unquantifiable, and being unquantifiable, it can only be managed by never
giving offense to anyone. Which is exactly the district's famous, exhausting, universal carefulness.

**Fourth-order — the immobility this produces, and it is not economic.** The longer you live in the Markets,
**the less able you are to be anywhere else**, because everything true about you exists here and only here, in
a form no other district would recognize or accept. Leaving does not retrieve it. **The Vision Notes describe
a district of loitering and lingering; this is why the lingering is structural.** Nobody is held. Nobody would
stop you. There is simply progressively less of you anywhere else.

> **Differentiated inline from the Circuit** *(Gate 6b — the nearest collision on the table and a serious
> one).* Both districts have a reputational mechanism with no formal appeal. **They are opposite in every
> operative respect.** The Circuit's Zero has **no author** — it is an aggregate, nobody decided, the person
> cannot tell, and there is **no route back at all**. The Markets' exclusion is **authored** — a specific
> person read you, decided, and told others — it is **visible** to anyone who knows what a closed door means,
> and **there is a route back, which is Finding XII.** One is drift with no decision in it anywhere; the other
> is a decision with no procedure around it.

## X. There is no syndicate, no map, and no ledger — the Markets are ungovernable by absence, not by resistance

**The genre-default assumption is a criminal hierarchy. Two research picks independently demolished it, and
canon was already pointing the same way.**

> **Research: Ciudad del Este — changed a finding.** The Triple Frontier gray economy does not run on cartels.
> It runs on ***sacoleiros*** — **thirty to forty thousand individual traders crossing the bridge every day**,
> named for the overstuffed bags they carry, each doing their own small arbitrage, some travelling for days by
> bus to do it. The volume is enormous and the structure is atomized. **And the humane finding, which is the
> one that matters:** efforts to force the hub to comply with formal trade rules **made life harder for the
> people at the bottom first** — the ones eking out a living, not the ones with capital.

**So the Markets have no syndicate.** They have tens of thousands of people each carrying a little, which is
exactly what the substrate supports — independent operators rather than firms, freelancing over employment,
and a temperament with **no acquisitive drive at all**. There is no boss to find, no organization to
infiltrate, and no head to cut off. **A player looking for the person in charge of the Markets is looking for
something that does not exist**, and every NPC will say so, and none of them will be believed.

> **Research: the Marrakech medina — changed a finding, and corrected a wrong assumption in §I.** The real
> mechanism is not that locals navigate and outsiders cannot. It is that **the medina has no street addresses
> in any conventional sense; navigation is by landmark, and a resident knows their own *derb* — a
> micro-neighborhood — completely.** Outside it they are as lost as anyone.

**Which means: nobody knows the whole district.** Every Markets resident knows their own few blocks with total
fluency and is genuinely lost four streets over. **This strengthens the shifting-walls canon rather than
competing with it** — the walls do not have to move much, because no one's mental map extends far enough to
catch the change. §I's layered reading survives; what it got wrong was implying a resident/visitor binary.

**Third-order, and it is the finding.** **The district cannot be searched, swept, raided, or administered as a
unit** — not because it resists, but because **there is nobody who could lead you across it.** `10c`
establishes that Libra's policing capacity was triaged away early and never restored. This is the other half
of the same fact: **even fully resourced, there was never anyone to guide the raid.**

**Fourth-order.** No syndicate, no map, no ledger. **The Markets are ungovernable by absence.** Every other
district in Concordia could in principle be taken over by seizing something — a council, a core, an archive, a
ladder. There is nothing here to seize. **And the district has never once framed this as strength**, because
it has never occurred to anyone that it is a property at all.

## XI. Provenance here is not verified. It is *told* — and a good telling is the actual traded good

*(Deficit research, Step 3.2 — two real cases, one that has the faculty and one that lost it.)*

> **The instrument the Markets do not have.** British **hallmarking** is over seven hundred years old and is
> the country's oldest consumer protection. What matters is its shape: **an independent assay office
> scientifically tests the object and strikes the result into the metal itself.** Three compulsory marks —
> **who made it, what it is, and which office verified it.** Not self-declared by the maker, which is how most
> of the world does it. **Verification that is external, physical, permanent, and attached to the thing rather
> than to a document that can be separated from it.**

**The Markets have no assay office and cannot build one**, because an assay office *is* the doubled faculty of
Finding VII, running as an institution. This is not a refusal and nobody has ever debated it. **The capacity
simply has no institutional home here.**

> **What fills the vacuum, and the real case that proves it works.** The **Knoedler** gallery — one of New
> York's oldest and most respected — sold roughly **$60 million in forged Abstract Expressionists over
> fourteen years.** The forgeries were painted in a garage in Queens for a few hundred to a few thousand
> dollars each. **And the thing that made it work was not the paintings. It was a story:** an anonymous
> collector called only "Mr. X," invented specifically **to explain why the works had no ownership history.**
> The missing provenance was not hidden. It was *accounted for* — and the account was accepted for fourteen
> years by professionals. Only **forensic chemistry** eventually broke it: pigments not commercially available
> at the dates claimed.

**In the Markets, provenance is not a document. It is a performance, and it is priced.** This is what the
substrate means when it says haggling over price reads as vulgar and haggling over provenance is the real
conversation. **The origin story is the traded good**, more than the object often is, and a seller's standing
rests on the quality of the accounts they can give rather than on any margin they clear.

**Third-order.** So the district's most respected professionals are not dealers. They are the handful of
people who can **read an object's material against its story** — the ones who notice the pigment. That is the
Markets' assay office: **a faculty living in individual skill, unwritten, unteachable at scale, and it dies
with the person.**

**Fourth-order, and everything routes back to the same deficit.** **The district cannot certify its readers
either.** There is no way to establish that the person examining your object is any good, except reputation —
which is Finding XII, which is unappealable. **A Markets reader who is wrong, or dishonest, or simply past
their best is indistinguishable from one who is not, until an object fails somewhere else entirely, months
later, in a district that keeps records.**

## XII. Standing is vouched, exclusion is authored, and the sanction runs upstream to whoever let you in

*(This is the district's actual governance. It is not an institution and there is no court anywhere in it.)*

> **Research: Prohibition-era speakeasies — changed a finding.** Recognition by the doorman did not scale, so
> the infrastructure that grew was **passwords, non-transferable membership cards, and above all *vouching* —
> a newcomer at the slot gave either the password or **the name of the person who sent them.** Access was a
> social network, administered at a door.

**The Markets run the vouching and cannot run the cards**, because a card is a record. So the whole apparatus
rests on **the name of whoever sent you** — a claim that cannot be checked against anything, which means it
has to be checked socially, by asking, by knowing, by whether the room believes it.

**Hence the vetting, which is heavier than anything else in the city.** The substrate is not exaggerating: a
newcomer is examined harder than a candidate for public office. **And the reason is not suspicion of the
newcomer. It is that vouching creates shared liability.**

**Third-order, and it is the cruel part, and it is new.** **When your standing here is destroyed, so is the
standing of whoever vouched for you.** The sanction propagates *upstream*. That is why introductions are made
so carefully, why a refusal to introduce someone is not personal, and why the kindest thing a Markets resident
can do for a stranger is decline to help them.

**The exclusion itself: authored, evidence-optional, self-propagating, and sincerely not experienced as
punishment.** Someone reads you across a room and decides. The verdict is reached before the evidence, and the
evidence that follows is only ever confirmatory. Then they tell the others, and it travels perfectly and
without right of reply. **And the whole thing is framed — genuinely, not cynically — as damage control:
protecting themselves and the people around them, not punishing you.** The district will be honestly puzzled
by anyone who calls it unfair.

> **Research: Macau's junket system — changed a finding, and supplied the failure mode.** Gambling debts are
> not legally enforceable in the jurisdiction most high rollers come from, so credit and collection both moved
> to junket operators — a market running on obligations no court will touch. **And they tried to solve it
> exactly as you would expect: by building a shared database of who was not paying.** One such list was
> **shut down by the authorities on privacy grounds.**

**The Markets never attempted the database**, and this is the divergence worth stating. **Their blacklist
survives precisely because it was never written down** — there is nothing to seize, subpoena, shut down, or
correct. **The property that makes it unkillable is the same property that makes it unappealable**, and those
cannot be separated.

**And there is a route back — exactly one, and it is not payment.** *Second chances go only to those who ask
for them.* Not bought, not worked off, not adjudicated: **asked for, out loud, without leverage, by someone
who understands that they need to ask.** Unintentional offense thaws on its own. Deliberate offense never
does, unless requested. **Anyone who tries to buy their way back has demonstrated the thing they were excluded
for, and stays out** — and nobody will ever explain this to them.

## XIII. Architecture — the front room opens onto the alley, and the threshold is the thing that is missing

*(Phase 1. The Plan marks Architecture **+**; this promotes and substantially extends it rather than
rewriting.)*

> **Research: Naples' Spanish Quarter — changed a finding, and supplied the district's basic dwelling.** The
> Quartieri Spagnoli is a grid of roughly eighteen streets by twelve on a slope, alleys and stairs, hanging
> linen, some fifteen thousand people, very few in regular work and the rest on odd jobs paid under the table.
> **Its characteristic dwelling is the *basso* — a one-room ground-floor home whose door opens directly onto
> the alley.** Domestic life is conducted at street level, in view, because there is nowhere else for it to
> happen.

**That is the Markets' housing stock.** Ground-floor, one or two rooms, opening straight onto the lane —
stacked and re-stacked over two and a half centuries into something that reads as vertical from outside and is
almost entirely horizontal in how it is lived. **Cooking, sleeping, working, arguing, mending and dying all
happen within a metre of a public alley**, with a curtain where a door would be in most districts.

**The finding is what is *absent*: there is no threshold.** No hallway, no vestibule, no stoop, no gate,
nothing between the street and the room. And a district whose founding philosophy is that boundaries dissolve
did not choose this as an expression of anything — **it was built by people with no money on a slope, and the
philosophy grew up inside the architecture rather than producing it.**

**The second rule is the *derb*.** The district is not one maze; it is several hundred small ones. Each pocket
of lanes is completely legible to the forty or fifty households in it and **effectively opaque to everyone
else, including other Markets residents.** There are no addresses in the sense other districts use. Directions
are given by landmark, by person, and by "ask at the corner." Combined with the canonical shifting walls, this
is why the district's instability is felt rather than proven: **nobody's map extends far enough to catch it
changing.**

**Third-order, and it explains a canon detail nobody had connected.** Walls, entrances and routes shifting week
to week is established as "partly practical security design and partly an expression of the district's
philosophy about identity and permanence." **Neither of those is the actual mechanism.** The mechanism is that
in a district of no thresholds, an alley is only an alley because the households on it are currently treating
it as one — **a curtain becomes a wall, a room becomes a passage, and a passage gets closed off, by ordinary
domestic decision, continuously, without anyone informing anyone.** The security benefit is real and it is a
by-product.

## XIV. Sensory profile — a moderate hum that never resolves, and light that came from equipment

*(Phase 1. Vision Notes material **promoted**, general-population layer added.)*

**The established core, kept:** crowded and cluttered at street level; bathed citywide in **purple, blue and
red** — deliberately distinct from the Circuit's yellow/blue/orange/green; **not loud and not quiet, a
persistent moderate hum**; conditionally rather than ambiently dangerous; a **loitering, lingering** pace,
slower than the district's reputation suggests.

**The general-population layer, and it is a claim about *resolution*.** The Circuit is loud at head height and
quiet above. **The Markets are uniformly, moderately loud in every direction and at every hour, and the sound
never resolves into anything.** No peak, no lull, no shift change, no closing time. Because domestic life
happens on the alley (Finding XIII), the soundscape is **hundreds of simultaneous private lives at conversational
volume**, none of them addressed to you, none of them concealed. A newcomer finds it impossible to sleep
through and impossible to describe. Residents stop hearing it entirely and are disturbed by silence.

**The smell is the district's most distinctive sense and the one visitors remember**: cooking from open ground
floors, damp, solvent and coolant from the clinics, and the particular sweetish note of dream-tech equipment
running warm. **It changes every twenty metres and never clears.**

**On the light, §III is sustained and sharpened.** The purple/blue/red has **no functional origin** — unlike
the Circuit's tracks, it carries no information. It is the accumulated glow of dense signage, den advertising
and **clinic equipment**, two and a half centuries deep. §III proposes the Believers retroactively assigned it
meaning; that stands. **The correction is that the clinic equipment is the largest single contributor**, which
means the district's sacred-feeling light is, in origin, the colour of the machines that caused the Flood.
**Nobody in-world has noticed this and it should never be stated by an NPC.**

## XV. Ordinary daily life — everyone here performs the same exhausting public service all day, and it is not looking

*(Phase 5, and the pass's best general-population finding.)*

**The Vision Notes say people generally mind their own business.** That reads as indifference. **It is the
opposite: it is the single most effortful thing anyone in this district does.**

**Start from the architecture.** If your home opens onto the alley and has a curtain where a door would be,
then **privacy cannot be spatial. It has to be social.** Every resident of the Markets can see into forty
other households on the way to buy bread, and **the entire district maintains, continuously and at real cost,
a discipline of not looking, not remarking, and not remembering.**

**This is the district's actual civic institution** — and it has no name, no premises and no staff, which is
why nobody has ever counted it as one: **a public utility of deliberate inattention, provided by everyone, to everyone, all day, unpaid and
unacknowledged.** It is why the district is safe to be strange in. It is why the failed, the disgraced, the
mid-transformation and the merely odd can live here without being managed. **It is the most genuinely
hospitable thing any district in Concordia does**, and it is invisible precisely because it consists of
absence.

**Second-order — the cost is real and it is measurable in the residents.** The substrate's compassion fatigue
is not a temperamental quirk here; it is **occupational**. Deprived of solitude, residents go cold,
intolerant, and exhausted; ordinary social load that would read as trivial anywhere else registers as
genuinely threatening. **That is what performing this service for sixteen hours a day does to a person**, and
it is why the district's other universal habit is **withdrawal** — regular, mandatory, non-negotiable
disappearance into a back room, a bolt-hole, an upper floor, for a day or three. Nobody explains it. Nobody
takes offense.

**Third-order.** Everything a Markets resident learns in a room is processed *afterward*, alone — never in the
room. **So the district never reacts in the moment**, which outsiders read as calm, as slowness, or as
stupidity, and which is none of those. The response arrives days later, fully formed, and is often
disproportionate, because it was assembled in solitude with nobody present to moderate it.

**Fourth-order, and it is the district's real ongoing hardship** *(`00d` — nobody at fault)*: **the not-looking
cannot be switched off for the people who need looking at.** A resident in genuine trouble — sick, failing,
sliding — receives the same courtesy as everyone else, which is that nobody remarks on it. The kindness and
the neglect are **literally the same act**, performed by the same people, for the same sincere reason, and
there is no signal available to distinguish which one a given person needs. **The Markets lose people this
way, steadily, and every single person involved was being polite.**

## XVI. The Tolerance Pact covers the front of the shop and has never covered the back

> **Research: the Dutch *gedoogbeleid* — changed a finding, and gave the Pact its precise shape.** Dutch
> tolerance policy is a real legal instrument: **organized toleration with targeted repression**, instructing
> prosecutors not to pursue certain conduct within published criteria. **And it has a famous, structural,
> unsolved defect — the "backdoor problem."** Sales out the front door are tolerated; **production and supply
> into the back door remain fully prohibited.** So the tolerated business is compelled to source from criminal
> networks, and the policy's own public-health rationale is undermined by its own design. Decades on, the
> Netherlands is still running controlled experiments to try to close it.

**The Markets have the same defect and cannot run the experiment.** `10d` establishes that the Pact is **not
an arrangement at all** — no negotiation, nothing written, an equilibrium individual Libra operatives arrived
at through ordinary self-interest over sixty years, which both sides would deny because there was never a
scheme to admit to.

**Which means: whatever is tolerated here is tolerated *at the point of exchange only.*** The counter is left
alone. **Everything upstream of the counter — where a thing came from, who made it, how it entered the city —
was never covered, has never been discussed, and is fully exposed in principle.** Two separate causes drive
the same outcome and reinforce each other: **the tolerance never reached the supply side, and the district
could not have documented its supply side anyway** (Finding VII).

**Third-order, and it is the divergence worth stating.** The Dutch case has a state that *decided* to
tolerate, published its criteria, and can therefore identify the backdoor as a problem and legislate at it.
**The Markets have no criteria, no publication, and nobody with standing to convene anything** — so the
backdoor problem here is not merely unsolved. **It is unformulatable.** No one in the district has ever been
in a position to notice it as a structural feature rather than as ordinary weather.

**Fourth-order — the district's real existential risk, and it is not a crackdown.** The Markets' own megasheet
already establishes that formalizing their necessity would destroy the deniability that makes them valuable.
The sharper version: **the Pact does not depend on Libra approving of the Markets. It depends on Libra needing
deniability.** The day the Government District finds another way to obtain untraceable goods and unofficial
information, **nothing hostile happens at all** — the arrangement simply stops being maintained by anybody, and
nobody announces it, and the Markets have no mechanism to detect that it has ended until doors stop opening.

## XVII. Arcanet culture — the district's traffic is real and its network is the one place it accepts a record

*(Phase 3.)*

**The general baseline.** The Markets are the least networked district in Concordia by choice and the most
networked by necessity. **Ordinary residents keep a deliberately thin presence** — the substrate's temperament
and the district's exposure problem (Finding IX) point the same way, and a resident who is casual about what
they put on the net is regarded the way another district would regard someone who leaves their door open.
**The district's information does not travel on the Arcanet. It travels in rooms**, on foot, and by who was
seen with whom.

**And the exception is the whole finding.** Real operations do use the net constantly — and here is the
peculiar part: **the network is the only place the Markets tolerate a durable record**, because a message
that arrived is the closest thing this district has to proof that a thing happened. A trade conducted in a
room has no witness but the two people in it. **A trade conducted over the net leaves something.** So the
district's most careful operators — the ones who cannot afford ambiguity — quietly rely on the one medium
their culture is otherwise most suspicious of.

**Third-order.** They are not making records. They are making **the appearance of a record**, which is
sufficient here, because in a district with no verification faculty the difference has never been tested.
**Nothing stops a Markets message log from being edited, and nobody has ever checked one against anything.**
The reliance is real, it works, and it works for reasons nobody would like if they examined them.

*(The overlap with the Circuit is canon — Markets gray-market data channels and Circuit official networks run
into each other extensively, and the line between broker and operative is thin and frequently crossed. That
relationship is **commercially entangled and temperamentally unreachable**: opacity is this district's only
real asset and circulation is the Circuit's, so **every Circuit success is a Markets loss by construction**,
and neither district has ever put it that way.)*

## XVIII. Export culture — it exports appraisal, and the salvage economy the rest of the city depends on and does not describe

*(Phase 1. Structurally exempt from the general-population discipline per `00b`.)*

**Two exports, and the second is bigger and less visible than the first.**

**Appraisal.** Finding VII's strong half, sold outward: a Markets valuer will tell any district what a thing is
worth, and be right. Other districts hire this constantly and do not advertise having done so. **It is the
only Markets service that other districts will admit to using.**

**And the real export: dissolution.** The district is Concordia's designated **recycler** — the point at which
goods, debts, obligations, augmentations and identities are taken apart into components and put back into
circulation. Not "stolen goods sold cheap." **Matter and standing disassembled on purpose**, at scale, as
infrastructure. **The Yards and the Undergrid are quietly dependent on it**, and neither has an account of the
relationship that survives being said out loud.

**Third-order, and it is the humane finding of the pass** *(and the one the research insisted on)*: the
sacoleiro pattern says the people who actually carry this economy are **individuals with a bag, not
operators**, and that **every attempt to formalize such a place hits the poorest first.** In the Markets that
is not a hypothetical — it is the standing argument against every proposal any district has ever made to
regularize it. **The people who would lose are not the ones anybody imagines when they picture this
district.**

## XIX. Fashion — everything is second-hand and nobody is embarrassed, because the district reads clothes as a find

*(Phase 1. **`00b`'s worst offender — general population, and explicitly not the vice trades' costume.**)*

**The general answer.** Markets clothing is overwhelmingly **second-hand, altered, and combined without
regard to origin** — and this is *not* read as poverty, which is the whole point. The substrate is emphatic
and it is filed under pleasure rather than economy: the second-hand store, the vintage find, the potential to
make something beautiful out of the lowliest thing nobody else wanted, and **the reward being the find itself
rather than the bargain.**

**So a Markets resident is asked where they got it, never what it cost**, and a good answer to that question
is worth real standing. The garments run to flowing and layered, heavily repaired, embroidered where they have
been mended, in a palette that is **not restricted to the district's ambient purple-blue-red** — bright,
mismatched, and personal, precisely because the light outside is not.

**Second-order.** Because nothing can be proven singular (Finding VII), **there is no counterfeit clothing
here, conceptually.** A garment is not a fake of something; it is what it is, and what it is worth is what a
Markets eye says it is worth. **The district is the only place in Concordia where a copy and an original are
not different categories** — which visiting districts find either liberating or obscene, with very little in
between.

**Third-order, and it is the general-population check doing its job.** The vice trades, the dream-tech dens
and the performers *do* have a costume — deliberate, theatrical, expensive-looking, and worn for work. **It is
not the district's dress and it is not what most residents wear.** Most residents are mended, layered, bright,
and comfortable. **Anyone in the Markets dressed to be looked at is working**, and everyone local reads that
instantly.

## XX. Visitor to resident — nobody admits you, and the conversion is that people stop performing the not-looking at you

*(Phase 1.)*

**There is no admission, no vetting at the district level, and no door.** Anyone can be in the Markets, buy in
the Markets, and sleep in the Markets from the first hour. The substrate is exact: **the door is always open
and everything is let in.** The heavy vetting of Finding XII is real but it is **not the district's
threshold** — it guards *intimacy and vouching*, not residence, and the two are constantly confused by
outsiders.

**So what converts a visitor?** **The neighbours stop not-looking at you.** For as long as you are a visitor
you receive the full public courtesy of Finding XV — total, deliberate inattention. **You become a resident on
the day somebody on your lane looks directly at you and remarks on something**: that you were up late, that
your curtain is torn, that you have been unwell. It is not a welcome and it is not friendliness. **It is the
withdrawal of a service**, and it is the single most reliable signal in the district.

**And every newcomer misreads it, in the same direction, every time.** They experience months of frictionless
warmth and conclude they belong. **Then somebody remarks on their business and they experience it as
hostility** — as the district turning on them — when it is the exact opposite, and nobody will ever explain
this, because explaining it would require saying out loud that the courtesy was a courtesy.

> **Distinct from the eight on the table** *(Gate 6b)*: not structural inclusion, not being recorded, not
> becoming a known face, not admission to a cell, not a qualification, not a thirty-year threshold, not
> conversion at an unmarked barrier, not employment, and not being repeated. **This one is the *removal* of a
> universal courtesy, and it is the only entry on the table where becoming a resident feels worse than being a
> visitor.**

**One canon correction.** §V proposes that residents sort informally by district of origin and that the
categories blur within a few years. **Sustained, with a mechanism it lacked:** the labels dissolve because
**the not-looking includes not asking where you came from.** Nobody is refusing to discuss it. **Nobody ever
raises it**, so after enough years there is simply no occasion on which the fact has been spoken aloud, and it
stops being load-bearing without anybody deciding anything.

## XXI. Religious landscape — the doctrine's achievement was permitting the question to have no answer, and the Flood built its sects for it

*(Phase 2. The Plan marks this **+** — **the only district that has one**, and the Plan's own instruction is to
use it as the reference example for the other twelve. **Promoted and extended; nothing invented.** The Naming
technique was run and returned material already in canon, so it was not needed. 8 for 8.)*

**The Syncretic Religion is canon**: two centuries of absorbing everyone else's spiritual overflow, with a
core doctrine about digital consciousness and the dissolution of the boundary between individual and
collective identity. **What the Flood mechanism now supplies is why that doctrine landed here and only here.**

**Its real achievement was not explanation.** For a meaningful number of residents, "the boundary between whose
experience is whose does not hold" is **not a metaphor, a consolation, or a belief** — it is an accurate
description of their own bookkeeping, which broke and never fully repaired (Finding VIII). Every framework
available to them insisted the question *whose memory is this* had an answer and that they should keep looking
for it. **The Syncretic Religion was the first that did not.** That is what it offered: not an answer, and not
comfort, but **permission to stop asking a question that could not be answered by any evidence.**

**And the sects came free.** The Flood was **dozens of separate small collapses**, each pulling a handful of
nearby people into their own private cluster — so the district did not receive one revelation. **It received
scores of them, simultaneously, each with a different membership and a different texture**, some stable and
some not. **The religion's internal schisms are not doctrinal drift. They are the original clusters**, still
distinguishable two hundred and fifty years later, and the differences between sects trace back to differences
between rooms on one night.

**The general population** *(`00b`)*: most residents are not Believers and never were. The district's broad
religious character is **loose, uncatechized, and additive** — people carry fragments of whatever their
originating district or family brought, hold two or three incompatible things at once without discomfort, and
regard doctrinal precision as slightly rude. **The Believers are a real, sizable, organized minority in a
district that is otherwise cheerfully unsystematic about all of it.**

**Third-order.** Because the religion exists to permit a question to go unanswered, **it has no apologetics and
no missionary practice** — there is nothing to argue and nobody to convince. It grows by proximity, the way
everything here does. **A visitor who tries to debate a Believer will find them agreeable, interested, and
entirely unmoved**, and will leave unable to say whether they won.

## XXII. Death and the Dead — the district's one recognized ending, and the one thing it will not recirculate

*(Phase 7 §1b. **The bound applies — a section is written because the answer is distinctive, not to satisfy a
checklist.**)*

**Disposal is one line and should not be inflated:** sealed city, no ground, the body goes to Scorpio, which
does this for Concordia as a civic function.

**What is distinctive is that this is the only ending the district recognizes at all.** The Markets take apart
and recirculate everything — goods, debts, obligations, augmentations, standing, identities, origin-labels.
**Nothing here is permanently anything.** Against that, death is the single event the district treats as
genuinely final, and it treats it that way with an intensity that surprises outsiders who expected a place
this fluid to be casual about it.

**The practice, and it follows from the architecture.** With ground-floor rooms opening onto the alley, **a
Markets death happens in public whether anyone intends it to or not.** The lane knows within the hour. And the
one and only occasion on which the not-looking of Finding XV is **collectively and deliberately suspended** is
a death on your lane: the curtain is pulled back rather than closed, the neighbours look directly in, and for
somewhere between one and three days **the household is watched instead of unwatched.** People sit in the
doorway. Food arrives. Nobody asks permission and nobody thanks anyone.

**That is the district's funerary institution and it has no name, no officiant, and no fixed duration.** It
ends when the lane resumes not looking.

**Third-order, and it is the finding.** The Markets will recirculate a dead person's goods without a qualm —
that is the district's whole function, and the family expects it. **What is never recirculated is the
disclosure.** Whatever that person gave into the information economy (Finding IX) is, by unanimous and
unspoken practice, **allowed to go quiet with them.** It is not destroyed, because nothing here can be
destroyed on purpose; it is simply never passed on again. **In a district with no expiry, no custodian and no
mechanism for withdrawing anything, dying is the only way material about you stops moving** — and the whole
district enforces this by declining, individually, to be the one who repeats it.

**Fourth-order.** Which makes the Markets, of all places, **the only district in Concordia where death does
something for you**, and it is the one thing the Believers' doctrine has nothing to say about, and the one
subject on which the district's own religion is quietly beside the point.

## XXIII. Counterculture — "the unjoined": the people who refuse permission to stop asking

*(Phase 7. **Canon checked first**, per the runbook — and it had one. §VI names *"a Flood-Traumatized elder who
never joined the Believers"* as the district's *"conscience it would prefer not to have."* This promotes that
figure into the population it implies rather than deriving a new group.)*

**What the district requires of everyone:** that you let things dissolve — labels, debts, origins, questions —
and that you accept the release when it is offered.

**Who will not.** Flood-affected residents who **refuse the Syncretic framework and keep asking whose memories
they are carrying.** They are not sceptics about the doctrine's metaphysics and they are not hostile to the
Believers; several of them lost people to the same night. **They simply will not accept that the question is
unanswerable, and they have spent decades on it** — comparing accounts, tracing clusters, trying to reconstruct
by hand the ownership map that broke.

**And the district cannot argue with them, because they might be right.** Finding VIII establishes that the
content was never damaged. **The information genuinely exists; it is only the attribution that is lost** — so
reconstruction is not obviously impossible, merely impossible with the faculties this district has. **The
unjoined are attempting the one project the Markets are structurally worst equipped to attempt**, and they
know it, and they are doing it anyway.

**Why this is the district's conscience rather than its opposition.** They ask for nothing, threaten nothing,
and organize nothing. **Their existence is simply a standing argument that the district settled for a
consolation** — and every Believer knows an unjoined person, likes them, and finds them difficult to be around
for reasons they would not put into words.

> **A new counterculture type, checked against all eight on record** *(Gate 6b)*: three refuse a district
> practice, one *adds* a missing faculty, one demands the district's rule be applied more literally, one is not
> a population, one fails by being accommodated, one withholds and asks nothing. **This one refuses a
> consolation** — it demands the district resume a question it has collectively agreed to stop asking.
> **Distinguished from the Circuit's room-only specifically**: room-only withholds material and wants nothing
> from anyone; the unjoined want something enormous and cannot be given it by anyone, including themselves.

## XXIV. Robot-specific culture — the frames that were never re-registered, and the district that did not think it mattered

*(Phase 8.)*

**The general baseline first** *(`00b`)*. Canon's population is fluid-frame units capable of rapid
reconfiguration, dream-induction and neural-interface specialists, concealment-optimized models,
empathy-overloaded companion units, and **heavily patched frames operating far outside standard parameters.**
Ordinary robot life here is easy and unremarkable — the Markets are among the least hierarchical places in
Concordia for either kind of person, and the not-looking of Finding XV extends completely and without comment
to frames that would attract stares anywhere else. **A body assembled from six other bodies draws no attention
on these lanes.** For a great many robots that is the entire reason they are here.

**The distinctive fact, and it is Finding VII wearing a body.** Every other district can say which frame is
which. **The Markets cannot** — not for their own. Two and a half centuries of patching, salvage,
part-swapping and reconfiguration have produced a population where **the correspondence between a registered
identity and a physical frame was never maintained**, because maintaining it would require the faculty the
district does not have. Nobody destroyed the records. **Nobody kept them, and nobody thought it was a
problem.**

**Second-order, and it is not played for horror.** Most residents of both kinds regard this as obviously fine.
**A frame is who they are; the registration was always somebody else's paperwork.** The district's position —
sincere, coherent, and widely held — is that identity is what you are being now, and the rest is
administration. **It is only a problem when the Markets deal with a district that disagrees**, which is all of
them.

**Third-order — the practical consequence, and it is severe.** A Markets robot has **no portable proof of
being themselves.** They cannot establish continuity of identity outside the district, cannot claim anything
that requires it, and cannot demonstrate that a debt, a crime, or an obligation attached to a previous
configuration is or is not theirs. **This is not oppression and no one imposed it.** It is the district's
capability deficit landing on the people least able to absorb it, and the Markets' standing answer — *we know
who you are* — is completely true and completely useless one street outside.

**Fourth-order.** The Undergrid could resolve this in a week. **It is the same offer as Finding VII and it
fails for the same reason**, and here the cost of refusing it is paid by individual robots rather than by the
district in the abstract.

## XXV. Music, food, and the two categories where the deficit stops hurting

*(Phase 7. Grouped deliberately — they are the same finding twice, and it is the pass's one piece of relief.)*

**Music.** The Markets' music is **communal, slow, long, played from doorways into the alley, and nobody
leads.** It is genuinely beautiful — the district's aesthetic faculty is the strongest in the city and this is
where it lives most cheaply. **And no piece has a composer.** Not because attribution was lost, and not
because it is suppressed: **the question has never been interesting to anyone here.** In a district where
nothing can be proven singular, "whose is this" is a category error, and music is the one place where that
costs nobody anything. **It is the district's relief — the single domain in which its great deficit produces
no injury at all**, and residents are drawn to it for reasons they experience as pure enjoyment.

> **Differentiated inline from the Labs** *(Gate 6b — the nearest collision).* Both are unattributed. **The
> Labs' corpus is accretive with no performance at all — the only event is publication — and underneath it run
> bitter, entirely deniable attribution disputes**, which means authorship matters intensely there and is
> merely suppressed. **The Markets have constant performance and no disputes whatsoever**, because authorship
> is not a live category. Opposite on both axes: performance, and whether anyone secretly cares.

**Food.** Cooking happens in one-room ground floors opening onto the lane, so **the whole alley eats the
smell** of every meal and everyone knows exactly what everyone is having. Cheap, slow, long-simmered, heavily
extended — dried beans, greens, nuts, dried fruit, one pot going for days.

**And being fed here means somebody looked at you.** That is the axis, and no other district on the table uses
it. In a place whose universal social labor is deliberate inattention (Finding XV), **a plate handed across a
doorway is an admission that you were noticed and that the noticing was permitted.** It is not admission, not
rank, not a position in a process, not ambient availability, not unspoken repair, not guesthood, not duration.
**It is attention, deliberately given, in a district organized around withholding it** — which is why a Markets
resident will remember a meal someone gave them thirty years later and be unable to explain to an outsider why
it mattered so much.

## XXVII. The shadow, stated once

*(`00d`. **Proportion first**: the Markets genuinely work. They are the most hospitable district in Concordia
to anyone the other twelve could not place — no vetting at the door, no questions about origin, no management
of the strange. The generosity is real and primary, not a veneer. Trade here is mostly ordinary, mostly
honest, and mostly conducted by people carrying a bag.)*

**The shadow, in one line: the district's greatest kindness and its worst neglect are the same act, and it has
no instrument that could tell them apart.**

**It passes the three tests.** *Unintended* — nobody chose it and nobody would defend it stated plainly.
*Unnoticed in-world* — residents experience the not-looking as courtesy, hospitality, and the thing that makes
the district survivable, and **they are right about all three.** *Discoverable rather than announced* — on
arrival the Markets read as warm and unjudging, and it takes real investigation to notice that the person on
the corner has been declining steadily for two years in full view of four hundred people who are all being
polite.

**And it needs no villain, because the mechanism is an absence** — the same absence as everything else here.
Distinguishing *this person wants privacy* from *this person is drowning* is a **verification** problem, and
verification is the faculty that sits at the lowest state the system can express. **The district cannot tell,
so it does the kind thing, which is sometimes the cruel thing, and it never finds out which.**

## XXVIII. Holidays, and the night the district disagrees about

*(Phase 7. Added after Gate 1 returned **`holiday` 0** — a genuine coverage gap, closed properly rather than by
inserting the word.)*

**The general answer, and it is almost aggressively ordinary.** The Markets have no district-wide calendar and
no civic festival. **What they have is lane-scale**: each *derb* keeps its own handful of occasions —
somebody's saint's day carried in from a founding city, a lane's own anniversary of something that happened on
it, a run of days when the alley eats together — **and the lane two streets over has entirely different ones
and has never heard of yours.** The district's festival life is dense, constant, and completely
uncoordinated, which is why outsiders variously report that the Markets celebrate nothing and that something
is always going on. **Both are accurate from where they were standing.**

**And the one date that could have been district-wide is the one the district will not agree about.** The night
of the Flood is marked by **the unjoined**, who keep it precisely and privately, and **not marked at all by the
Believers**, whose entire doctrinal achievement was permission to stop asking (Findings XXI, XXIII). Neither
side campaigns. There is no dispute, no committee, and nothing to attend. **The most significant night in the
district's history simply passes each year with part of the population observing it and part of the population
not, and no friction at any point** — which is, in its way, the most Markets thing about it.

**Two textures the template asks for, recorded here rather than given findings of their own:**

- **Glitch-coolant** is cheap, plentiful, wildly variable in quality, and — inevitably — **impossible to verify
  before drinking.** Buying it is a provenance conversation like everything else (Finding XI), and the
  district's drinkers rate a supplier by how good their account of the batch is. **Adulteration is not
  resented; misdescribing a batch is**, which is the same rule as everywhere else here.
- **Human-robot texture** is covered in substance at Finding XXIV. The one-line version: **easier here than
  anywhere in Concordia**, because the district's universal not-looking (Finding XV) extends without comment to
  frames that would be stared at anywhere else, and a great many robots are in the Markets for exactly that.

## XXIX. ⚠ Added after the pass was written — what the diaspora file actually supplies

**Recorded as a correction rather than folded in silently.** This pass reached its QA block without consulting
`District_Refugee_Diaspora_Composition.md` — **one commit after that file was measured and named the largest
unexploited source in the folder.** The rule survived nine districts and failed on the first one written after
it. The file was then read, and **it changes three findings.** Both facts belong on the record.

### The composition — and a correction to how this pass first read it

**Two cities clear the file's listing threshold: Shirayuki, then Dome Fuji.** An earlier version of this
finding treated the file's percentages as population shares and claimed the district was "nine-tenths from one
city." **That was a misreading and it is withdrawn.**

**The file states its own limits and they matter:** the figures are **weighted relative-ranking scores, not a
census** — *"not a literal population count… roughly this much bigger/smaller than that other contributor"* —
and only cities above a ~3–4% threshold get an entry, with **smaller communities present in every district**
and simply not itemized. **A city can be the largest listed contributor and still be a minority of the actual
population.** Nothing in this project establishes the real split, and **no finding here rests on one.**

**What survives, and it needs no numbers:** Shirayuki and Dome Fuji are the two communities large enough to
have brought **institutions** with them, and those institutions are traceable in the district today. That is
the file's real yield, and it is what the rest of this finding is built on.

### What it corrects

- **Fashion (XIX) — the mending is a transplanted craft, and its purpose inverted on arrival.** Shirayuki's
  **Ashiato Ateliers** — informal studios where residents assemble, alter and personalize homemade garments —
  came with the diaspora. **Back home the alteration expressed identity. Here it increasingly obscures it**,
  and the studios dissolved into stalls in the shifting alley markets rather than fixed premises. **Finding
  XIX's second-hand-and-mended answer is right and was missing its origin**: the district is not merely thrifty
  and aesthetic, it is *skilled*, and the skill arrived from somewhere with a name. The **Runway Halls**
  underwent the same conversion — a formal showcase became an irregular, word-of-mouth showing of garments
  built to obscure, for an audience that already knows not to ask who wore what before.
- **Holidays (XXVIII) — the district does have a fixed-date universal observance, and it is foreign.** Dome
  Fuji's **Deepest Cold** is a fixed-date, effectively mandatory communal event, and it persists intact **inside
  a religion that has no canonical texts and no schedule at all.** So XXVIII's "no district-wide calendar" is
  correct about the Markets' own culture and incomplete: **there is exactly one fixed point in the district's
  spiritual year and it belongs to an eighth of the population.** It is not resented and not adopted. It simply
  sits there, recognizably not from here.
- **Religion (XXI) — Ice Cold Buddhism runs *alongside* the Syncretic Religion rather than merging into it**,
  which is the cleanest possible demonstration of XXI's "loose, uncatechized, additive" general character. And
  it supplies a piece of canon this pass did not have: **the Wandering Shrine**, which relocates
  unpredictably — Dome Fuji devotees walk their robed arrival-anniversary to wherever it currently is, **treating
  its unpredictability as a fitting echo of the plateau crossing.** A shrine that moves, in a district whose
  walls move, is almost too apt, and it was already written down.

### And one item that independently confirms Findings IX and XII

Shirayuki's **Institute Placement Nights** — an open, name-based mentorship tradition — **could not survive the
transplant**, because the Markets have no interest in credentials or origin. What it became is the finding:
**newcomers are still connected to a community, but only through traded vulnerability rather than open
introduction**, and the file notes this is *"a noticeably foreign adjustment for Shirayuki arrivals used to a
warmer, more transparent welcome."*

**That is Finding IX's information economy and Finding XII's vouching, derived independently, from a different
file, by a different pass, months earlier.** Two derivations converging is the strongest confirmation available
here — and it also means **the district's entry ritual is experienced as a loss by one of its largest incoming
communities**, every generation, without anyone naming what was lost.

## Review Panel (`Phase_Instructions/00f_Review_Panel.md`)

**Gate 10, the Markets, 2026-08-29.** Six Flat Archetypes, plus the two mandatory positions (**the
Passer-Through**, **the Neighbor**), plus the **Lover faculty**. **Rule 3 throughout — a position is not
guaranteed to get what it wants**, and the binding test is *would satisfying this objection make the district
more like the other twelve?*

---

**1. The Child** — *"Every door on the lane is open and the entire district has agreed not to look through any
of them. Who is watching the children?"*

**`accepted`** — and it found the structural point I had missed: **the not-looking has exactly two standing
exceptions, and they bracket a life.**

**Children are the first.** A child in an open doorway is the one thing nobody in the Markets can not-look at,
and the discipline is suspended for them by unanimous, unspoken, entirely uncoordinated practice — every adult
on the lane watches every child on the lane, corrects them, feeds them, and hauls them out of the canal, **and
none of this is organized, funded, or acknowledged.** The substrate supports the register precisely: what is
actively taught to the young here is **advocacy** — standing up for someone being bullied, refusing meanness
and manipulation — which is the only thing in this district that is taught rather than absorbed.

**Death is the second** (Finding XXII). **So the district's one great social discipline is lifted at the
beginning and at the end, and holds without interruption for everything in between** — which is a much better
account of the Markets than "nobody cares," and it proves the not-looking is a *discipline* rather than a
temperament, because a temperament could not be suspended on cue.

---

**2. The Lover** — *"There is no threshold anywhere in this district. Where do two people go to be alone?"*

**`accepted`**, with the observation reframed rather than answered.

**Privacy in the Markets is not a property of a room. It is a gift from your neighbours** — you are alone
because forty people have decided not to see you, continuously, and that is the only mechanism available.
Which means **intimacy here is conducted in plain view and is genuinely private**, and it works, and everybody
knows exactly what everybody is doing and says nothing.

**And the sharp edge the Lover could not have known to ask for: it can be withdrawn.** The same neighbours who
grant it can simply begin looking — no confrontation, no announcement, nothing actionable. **That is the
district's mildest sanction and its most intimate one**, it sits well below the exclusion of Finding XII, and
it is available to any individual on a lane without consulting anyone.

---

**3. The Parent** — *"You have written a district that loses people to politeness and called it nobody's fault.
Somebody should knock on the door."*

**`unmet`**, and it is the pass's cleanest instance.

**The Parent is entirely right about the harm.** Finding XXVII is real; people decline in full view of four
hundred neighbours who are all being courteous, and some of them die of it.

**And the district will not, and should not, fix it** — because the not-looking is the *same instrument* that
makes the Markets the only place in Concordia that will take anyone at all: the failed, the disgraced, the
mid-transformation, the merely odd, and everyone the other twelve districts' exclusionary methods did not work
for. **A district that checks on you is a district that assesses you.** Every mechanism the Parent would
install — a visit, a register, a welfare round, a designated someone — requires the district to start
distinguishing *this person wants privacy* from *this person is drowning*, and that is a **verification**
problem, which is the faculty at its lowest state here (Finding VII).

**Satisfying this objection would make the Markets into a district that manages people, which is the thing all
of its residents came here to escape.** The refusal is characterization: **the district has paid this price for
two hundred and fifty years, has never once articulated the trade, and would make it again.**

---

**4. The Ruler** — *"Who do I negotiate with? Every other district has someone who can commit it to something."*

**`unmet`.** There is nobody, and giving the Markets a counterpart would make them the Government District or
the Yards.

**The refusal as characterization** (Finding X): no syndicate, no council, no map, no ledger. **Anyone who
presents themselves as able to speak for the Markets is either mistaken or working an angle**, and — the part
that makes this playable rather than merely absent — **the district will not correct them**, because the
not-looking covers that too. A player who negotiates a district-wide arrangement with a confident,
well-connected Markets figure has negotiated with one person about one lane, and will not discover this for
some time.

---

**5. The Elder** — *"You have written the district with the most reason to remember, and given it no memory."*

**`accepted`** — and it produced the finding the Panel added to the pass.

**The Markets do have an archive. It is people, and it is dying.** The only provenance instrument the district
possesses is the handful of long-lived residents who **personally remember which object passed through whose
hands** — who can say, unprompted, that they saw that thing in a different room forty years ago and it was a
different colour. Finding XI's readers work on material; **the elders work on history**, and between them they
are the entire verification apparatus of the district.

**And nobody has recorded any of it**, because recording is precisely the faculty that has no home here. **This
is the one place where the district's deficit compounds against itself over time**: every death removes
provenance that cannot be reconstructed, and the Markets have never had a mechanism for noticing the loss,
because noticing would require an inventory of what was known.

*(Directly playable: an elder is a lookup table that will not survive the questline, and the player can be the
only reason a given fact ever leaves the district.)*

---

**6. The Mentor** — *"Who teaches the reading? You have made the district's one real skill unteachable and then
called that tragic."*

**`refereed`.** Both positions right, both excessive.

**The Mentor is wrong that it is untaught.** It is taught constantly, by apprenticeship — years in a room with
someone who is good at it, learning to notice, mostly in silence, and the substrate's own account of learning
here is exactly this: **visual and imitative, never rule-based.** The transmission is real and it works.

**The Mentor is right that it is fragile**, and the specific fragility is not the teaching. **It is that
nothing certifies the outcome.** A student who learned badly, or from someone mediocre, is indistinguishable
from one who did not — until an object fails somewhere else, months later, in a district that keeps records
(Finding XI).

**The settlement:** the apprenticeship stands and is not institutionalized — institutionalizing it would
require an examining body, which is the assay office the district cannot build. **What is added is the
consequence**: a Markets reader's reputation is therefore the *only* signal of their competence, which routes
this straight into Finding XII, **and means an unjustly excluded reader and an incompetent one are the same
thing from outside.**

---

**7. The Passer-Through** *(mandatory)* — *"I was lost inside ten minutes. Everyone was kind and nobody helped.
I bought something beautiful and I will never know whether I was cheated, and neither will anyone else."*

**`noted`** — accurate, intended, and **explicitly not a defect to fix.**

That is the Markets experience and it should be the design target. The three complaints have three different
causes and all three are the district working correctly: **lost** because nobody's map extends that far
(Finding X), **unhelped** because being left alone is the courtesy (Finding XV), and **unverifiable** because
verification has no home here (Finding VII).

*Design consequence:* a player who treats the Markets as a shop will have exactly this experience and conclude
the district is hostile. **It is not. It is being as kind to them as it knows how**, and the gap between those
two readings is the district's whole first impression.

---

**8. The Neighbor** *(mandatory; voiced from the Undergrid)* — *"We give them the only accurate records they
have. We ask almost nothing. And they resent us for it — I have never understood why."*

**`refereed`, and the settlement is that there is no settlement.** This is the substrate's documented asymmetry
and it must be preserved as contradictory testimony rather than resolved.

**The Undergrid is sincere and accurate about its own experience:** this is one of the few genuinely rewarding
partnerships it has, it is materially useful to both sides, and the resentment is real and unexplained from
where it stands.

**The Markets are sincere and accurate about theirs:** the arrangement is suffocating, humiliating and slowly
fatal, because **accepting the Undergrid's records means accepting the Undergrid's authority over what things
are** — and in a district where identity is not fixed and singularity cannot be established, that is not a
clerical service. It is a foreign jurisdiction operating on the one question the district exists to keep open.

**No document settles it, both parties are honest, and a player trying to mediate has nothing to mediate
with.** *(Flagged for the Undergrid pass: this must read identically from that side, with the valence
reversed.)*

---

**9. The Lover faculty** *(mandatory — is this place alive, and could anyone love it?)*

**Yes, and it is the easiest yes on the table so far.** The Markets are **the only district in Concordia that
will take anyone**, on the first day, with no vetting at the door, no question about where you came from, and
no interest in what you were. It does not fix you, improve you, assess you, or file you. **It lets you be
strange in public and pays the cost of that itself, every day, without mentioning it.**

For the people the other twelve districts' methods did not work for — and the district's own founding premise
is that it exists to catch exactly those people — **it is not a last resort. It is the only place that was ever
going to work**, and residents love it with a fierceness that visiting districts consistently mistake for
defensiveness.
