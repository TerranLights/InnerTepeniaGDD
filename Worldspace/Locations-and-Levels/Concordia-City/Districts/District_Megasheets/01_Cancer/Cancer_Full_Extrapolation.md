# Cancer — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Cancer given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Cancer_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-09.

---

## I. The plantlife is tended — and tending it is itself a form of care

**Proposed:** Cancer's woven-in plantlife is neither purely ambient/wild nor maintained by a dedicated invisible service class — it's tended visibly, in public, largely by people who are themselves recovering or convalescing. Given Cancer's established care-debt economy (favors, time, and emotional labor exchanged instead of credits), tending the district's own greenery is a recognized, low-stakes, genuinely therapeutic form of that labor — assigned to people who need something purposeful and gentle to do while healing, rather than to a specialized gardening staff. This makes the plantlife simultaneously functional (feeding the hydroponic systems), psychologically comforting (per the Vision Notes), and a visible, everyday demonstration of the district's whole "everyone contributes what they can" ethos. Proposed name for this informal practice: **the Green Ledger** — an unofficial, understood accounting of who's tending what, kept the same way the district's care-debt economy keeps track of everything else: informally, socially, never actually written down.

---

## II. The ambient haze is a side effect of scale, not design

**Proposed:** given the Mega-Init's own scale reference (Eden Project–sized biome cultivation, not small decorative garden rooms), the district's soft, fog-like light is the natural result of that much living plant transpiration raising ambient humidity throughout an enclosed environment. Nobody engineered it. It's what inevitably happens when a district this size is this densely alive with cultivated greenery — consistent with the developer's own instinct that "nobody intentionally engineered it this way, the air is just sort of like that."

---

## III. "No One Left Behind" extends to outsiders and strangers as a matter of founding logic, not policy

**Proposed:** Cancer's own founding population had nothing and belonged to no established community when the district was built to receive them — there was never an "us" with a prior claim for an "outsider" to be measured against. This is why the available-but-not-hovering quality of care, confirmed directly in the Vision Notes toward an unhurt, unknown visitor, plausibly extends to genuine strangers and outsiders more broadly, not just established residents. Where Taurus' hospitality is warm specifically at the household level and cooler toward strangers in the street, Cancer's founding logic never had a version of "our people first" to begin with — the district's whole reason for existing was catching people with no prior claim on anyone.

---

## IV. The overcrowding strain is real, but hidden behind institutional walls, not visible on the street

**Proposed:** Cancer's established chronic resource strain and overcrowding pressure concentrates specifically inside its care institutions — shelters, nurseries, clinics — rather than reading as general street-level density. This resolves an apparent tension between the district's documented capacity crises (the Overcrowding Decision, chronic caregiver burnout) and the Vision Notes' own "slow enough," uncrowded street-level impression: the district's genuine struggle happens behind closed doors, inside the institutions actually absorbing the pressure, while the curved, green, spread-out public architecture (per the Gardens by the Bay–scale reference) keeps the visible, walkable parts of Cancer feeling calm. This is, in miniature, the same pattern as Cancer's own established "visible virtue, hidden cost" wound — the district's worst moments have always been the ones it doesn't put on a memorial wall.

---

## V. A concrete case of the Withdrawn-culture friction: one specific resident, not a pattern

**Proposed, directly building on the revised Finding B in `Deep_Dives/01_Cancer_Deep_Dive.md`:** since the friction between Cancer's visibility-based care model and privacy-reserved cultures (Byrd, Zhongshan, Sanay, Neumayer) is established as rare rather than systemic, it works best as one specific character rather than a population-wide tension. Proposed, unnamed placeholder: a Zhongshan-descended caregiver who ended up in Cancer for a specific personal reason (a bonded partner's family, or a posting that became permanent) and who still practices Zhongshan's own non-prying norm even while working inside the Mother's Circuit — someone whose quiet discomfort with the district's whole informational-openness ethos is a genuine, earned personal story, not a district-wide reckoning.

---

## VI. The four internal factions don't have separate territory, but they do have recognizable gathering sites

**Proposed:** The Unlimited, The Triage Realists, The Trauma-Bearers, and The Surveillance Faction (per `District_Unity_of_Opposites.md`) coexist in the same physical space rather than occupying separate zones — Cancer is too small and too interdependent a caregiving apparatus for genuine spatial separation to make sense. But each plausibly has an informal, recognized site associated with it: The Surveillance Faction around whatever facility physically houses the Mother's Circuit's aggregated data (a genuine "monitoring room," proposed name **the Circuit House**); The Trauma-Bearers around a specific, quieter memorial space distinct from the district's general memorial walls, reserved for processing guilt rather than public grief; The Unlimited and Triage Realists less around fixed sites and more around specific, recurring decision points (whoever currently staffs intake triage on a given day/shift).

## VII. Demonym and Notable Figures

**Demonym, proposed: Cancrian.** Straightforward, consistent with how most Concordia district demonyms would likely resolve — though "Cancerian" (matching the zodiac spelling more directly) is a viable alternate, worth deciding between rather than defaulting silently.

**Notable Figures, proposed:**
- **The Zhongshan-descended caregiver** (Section V, placeholder/unnamed) — a genuine, small-scale personal story rather than a district-wide reckoning.
- **A founding-era archivist** *(placeholder, unnamed)* — whoever it was among Cancer's own archivists who made the decision to destroy the Overcrowding Decision's records; not framed as a villain, but as someone who made an unrecorded, unresolved choice that still shapes the district's relationship to its own worst moment.

---

## Worth Your Attention

Section IV — the overcrowding strain being real but hidden behind institutional walls rather than visible on the street — is the standout proposal here, because it resolves what otherwise reads as a genuine tension between Cancer's documented capacity crises and its own calm, unhurried street-level Vision Notes, without discarding either fact.
