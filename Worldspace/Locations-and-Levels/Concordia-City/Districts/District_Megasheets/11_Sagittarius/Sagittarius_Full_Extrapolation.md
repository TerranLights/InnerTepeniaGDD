# Sagittarius — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about the Frostlands given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Sagittarius_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-09.

---

## I. Pace is unhurried at rest, sharply urgent in the field — the district has two speeds, not one

**Proposed:** given the Vision Notes' own confirmed picture (most Rastras parked, a small handful actively driving, the district reading as generally quiet and sparse), the Frostlands plausibly don't have a single uniform pace at all. Life inside a clan cluster moves at the deliberate, conserving rhythm of people managing scarce energy and long isolation — nothing is rushed because rushing wastes resources you can't easily replace. The moment someone is actually out on the ice — driving, scouting, responding to a stranded party — that rhythm inverts completely, because the cold makes hesitation lethal. The district's calm surface and its occasional bursts of real urgency aren't a contradiction; they're the same survival logic applied to two different situations.

---

## II. A "clan" is organized around shared risk-history, not bloodline or guild role

**Proposed:** given the established Expansionist/Consolidationist/Memory Keeper factional split exists at the district-wide level, clans themselves plausibly form around something more local and personal: who has been out on the ice together, whose losses are shared. A clan is less a family tree or a professional guild chapter and more a group bound by common expeditions, common near-misses, and in some cases the specific memory of the Third Expansion Collapse or a smaller-scale version of the same kind of loss. This makes clan membership something closer to shared survival history than heredity — someone can plausibly be adopted into a clan by having lived through the same crossing, not merely been born into the right family.

---

## III. Living arrangements are modular and expedition-derived, not built to feel permanent

**Proposed:** consistent with the "wild quality is about... a lack of visible infrastructure" confirmed in the Vision Notes, clan housing plausibly descends directly from expedition equipment rather than from any deliberately designed residential architecture — structures that started as forward operating shelters and were incrementally reinforced and expanded in place rather than replaced with something more permanent-looking. This keeps every cluster visually closer to a well-established base camp than to a town, consistent with the district's own self-image that nothing here is finished, only currently adequate.

---

## IV. Capricorn refines the Frostlands' extracted materials into finished siligel, confirming the deep dive's own proposed mechanism

**Proposed:** per the deep dive's own reasoning, Capricorn — the city's established heavy-manufacturing and infrastructure hub — is the only district with the industrial capacity already on record to plausibly be the actual siligel refiner. This gives the existing, currently unexplained "resource competition with Capricorn" bullet a specific mechanism: the Frostlands absorb all of the Siligel Rush's volatility (the boom, the bust, the stranded political power) while Capricorn captures the more stable, ongoing value of turning the Frostlands' raw extraction into the finished product the entire city depends on. The resentment isn't abstract extraction-colony resentment — it's the specific grievance of watching someone else's production line profit steadily from your district's one-time, unrepeatable windfall.

---

## V. The oral-tradition preservation crisis has already claimed at least one specific, irreplaceable piece of knowledge

**Proposed:** given the deep dive's own connection between the knowledge preservation crisis and both the Siligel Rush and the Third Expansion Collapse, it's plausible that the Frostlands have already lost something specific and consequential — not just a generalized worry about the future, but a concrete example residents can point to. The most likely candidate: the exact cause of the Third Expansion Collapse's structural miscalculation is remembered only in broad strokes by the current generation, with the specific engineering detail that could prevent a repeat having died with the senior engineers who understood it firsthand. The Consolidationists' entire platform is thus partly built on a foundation they can no longer fully verify — they know expansion needs to be safer, but the specific lesson that would make it safer is already gone.

---

## VI. Demonym and Notable Figures

**Demonym, proposed: Sagittarian.** Standard, unambiguous formation already carrying strong existing associations (archer, frontier, the zodiac sign itself) that reinforce rather than complicate the district's identity.

**Notable Figures, proposed:**
- **A surviving Third Expansion Collapse veteran, now a senior Consolidationist** *(placeholder, unnamed)* — carries the eleven-day stranding as lived memory rather than story, giving the Consolidationist faction its most credible voice specifically because they were there.
- **A Memory Keeper actively racing the preservation crisis** *(placeholder, unnamed)* — spending their remaining years trying to get the founding generation's surviving expertise into some form of durable record, aware they are almost certainly losing the race.

## VII. It can only keep what can be made to mean something — and what it loses, it loses at the moment of telling

**The structural spine.** *(Capability reading, `F_Rulerships.md` §6 D11.)*

**The one instrument is meaning** — scope, conviction, significance, the capacity to say *what a thing was
for*. There is no second faculty and nothing here is either revered or forbidden.

**A district with meaning as its only instrument metabolizes everything into significance**, because that is
the only operation it can perform. Sixty-three people died in the Third Expansion Collapse; the survivors came
back with stories; **those stories became an honest reckoning with what expansion costs.** That conversion is
not a coping mechanism. It is the district's only way of holding anything at all.

**Second-order: there is no category here for *that just happened*.** Every event must be *about* something
before it can be carried, and canon states the rule in the district's own words without noticing it is a rule:
**"a story doesn't need to be literally true to be valuable — it needs to be true about something."**

**Third-order, and this is the finding: a fact that cannot be made to mean anything has nowhere to live here,
so it is dropped at the moment of transmission, by a filter nobody installed and nobody can see.** Not
disputed, not forgotten, not lost to time — **never handed on in the first place**, because the handing-on
mechanism is a story and the fact would not go into one.

**Fourth-order, and it re-reads canon's own diagnosis as wrong.** The Frostlands know they have a preservation
crisis and believe it is **an aging problem** — the founding generation dying with their expertise. **It is a
format problem.** The Knowledge Preservation Lodges will work beautifully: elders will be recorded at length,
the material will be rich, everyone will be satisfied. **And the recordings will contain stories**, because
that is what an elder here can produce on request. What will not be in them is the ice sounding different an
hour before it goes, the particular wrongness of a light that means the weather has turned, the reason you
walk the third line and not the second — **the tacit, procedural, sensory knowledge that has no significance
in it and therefore never had a form.**

**Fifth-order — and it is why nobody will ever catch this.** The only evidence of the loss would be knowledge
the district never had a shape for. **Every preservation effort will feel like a success**, the archive will
grow, and crews will go on dying of things that nobody could have said.

### ⚠ Non-symmetry check against the Circuit *(mandatory — Step 2.1)*

Both districts have a record problem. **The obvious move is to make this one the Circuit's inverse, and that
would be wrong.**

| | The Circuit | The Frostlands |
|---|---|---|
| Instrument | transmission | **meaning** |
| What it does with a fact | **keeps everything, ranks nothing** | **keeps only what it has already ranked** |
| The failure | a correction arrives second and therefore reads as smaller | **the unrankable never enters the system at all** |
| Where the loss happens | in circulation, over time | **at the moment of telling, instantly** |
| Detectability | the person at zero cannot tell | **nobody can tell, including the district** |

**They are not mirror images and the consequences do not correspond.** The Circuit's problem is *timing and
reputation* — being right too late, and the standing that costs. The Frostlands' is *format* — a filter on
what is even eligible to be knowledge, which operates before anything can be right or wrong. **A person can be
destroyed by the Circuit's mechanism. Nobody is destroyed by this one; a body of knowledge simply is not
there, and never was.**

## VIII. Both of its suppliers are inside the city, and this district has no practice at travelling inward

**Deficit geometry** *(`00e` §5)*. The faculty the Frostlands lack — **detail, record, verification** — is at
home in **the Circuit** and both at home and at its strongest in **the Undergrid**. Two addresses, one of them
the district's own opposite number.

**And the reason neither line has ever run is not pride, refusal, or grievance. It is inexperience.**

**This district's entire competence is in one direction: outward.** Everything it is good at — provisioning,
route-finding, endurance, reading unfamiliar ground, arriving somewhere nobody has been — is the skill of
**going away from Concordia**. Both of its suppliers are *inward*: the Circuit is a district you walk to, and
the Undergrid is literally beneath the city. **Fetching the missing faculty is a journey, and it is the one
kind of journey nobody here has ever learned to make.**

**Second-order.** The substrate's contrast between these two opposites is exact and it cuts against the
Frostlands: **the Circuit goes everywhere and is changed by nothing; the Frostlands go once and come back
different.** The Circuit therefore *shops* — it visits three suppliers and receives from none. **The Frostlands
have never gone shopping in their lives.** They do not experience themselves as underserved, because
approaching a neighbour for a capacity has never been on the list of things one does.

**Third-order, and it is the district's own myth stated as a mechanism.** The founding figure this district
takes its self-image from is a teacher and healer **who could cure everyone except himself** — and whose only
route out of an incurable wound was to hand something to somebody else rather than to ask for anything. **The
Frostlands will send you help across a hundred kilometers of ice, and have no procedure whatsoever for
requesting any.**

**Fourth-order.** So the relationship with the Undergrid — the one district that could actually solve the
preservation crisis of Finding VII — **does not exist at all.** Not hostile, not strained: absent. Two
districts with a perfectly complementary need and no contact, because one of them faces outward by
construction and the other lives under the floor. *(Flagged for the Undergrid pass: this is the second
district for which the Undergrid is the structural supplier, after the Markets — and it is the only one that
has never asked.)*

## IX. ⚠ GATE 9 — a clan has no membership, only accrual, so the grounded dissolve out of it without anyone deciding

**Gate 9 fired again — the tenth consecutive district, and for the tenth consecutive time the defect was in
the pre-Plan 2026-07-09 material.**

**What was written one-way.** §II establishes that a clan forms around **shared risk-history** rather than
bloodline or guild role — who has been out on the ice together, whose losses are shared — and that **someone
can be adopted into a clan by having lived through the same crossing.** That is an admission mechanism, and
the file writes only the inbound half.

**The reverse follows from the definition and is severe.** If a clan *is* accumulated shared crossings, then
membership is not a status you hold — **it is a quantity that only ever goes up while you keep going out, and
otherwise stops.** Nobody is expelled. Nobody is judged. **A person who has not crossed in five years is
simply, progressively, less of the thing the clan is made of**, and the process requires no decision, no
meeting, and no unkindness from anyone.

**And there is no route back, because the route back is the thing they cannot do.** The injured, the aged,
the grounded — the district's sanction with teeth is revocation of expedition rights precisely because motion
is what everything here is made of — **cannot re-accrue, by the same mechanism that is dissolving them.**

**Third-order, and it re-reads a character type.** The bitter, complaining, grounded ex-expeditioner is a
fixture here and everyone treats it as a temperament — *he took it badly*. **It is not a temperament. It is
what it looks like to feel your membership evaporating while every single person around you is being
completely kind to you**, and to have no vocabulary for the complaint, because the district has never
acknowledged that its belonging mechanism has a downward direction.

**Fourth-order.** The district's answer to this is its most touching institution and it does not work: the
grounded are **given stories to keep.** Elder Interview Nights, the Storyteller's Circuit, the Preservation
Lodges — all of them are, functionally, **jobs invented for people whose accrual has stopped**, and the
district is sincerely proud of them. But a story about a crossing is not a crossing, and everyone involved
knows it, and nobody says so.

> **Differentiated inline from the Yards** *(Gate 6b — the nearest collision).* Capricorn's Gate 9 finding is
> that **there is no way to stop that does not read as failing** — a district that cannot distinguish drive
> from pressure, where stopping is *judged* and stopping by choice has no vocabulary. **The Frostlands do not
> judge you at all.** Nobody here thinks less of a grounded scout; the regard is genuine and undiminished.
> **What changes is not their standing but their membership**, because the clan is constituted by co-presence
> on the ice and they are no longer present. **The Yards' problem is evaluative. This one is definitional.**

## X. The three things this district's own temperament has no words for are the three things it is currently living through

*(Runbook Step 5 — mine the substrate's §15 Source Gaps, and ask whether the hole is the mechanism. Three
holes, and this is the highest-yield instance of that technique recorded so far.)*

**The substrate names three absences, flatly, and does not connect them:**

- **Grief is absent.** The corpus covers this temperament's response to boredom, confinement and insult in
  detail and says **essentially nothing about how it handles loss.**
- **Scarcity is absent.** Every passage assumes abundance — *"they always seem to have enough money to do what
  they want."* **There is no picture of this archetype poor, rationed, or under siege.**
- **Cold is absent.** The district's defining physical condition **goes unaddressed entirely.**

**Now set those against the district's actual present.** Canon: sixty-three dead at the Third Expansion
Collapse and expedition losses as an ordinary fact of life. The Siligel Bust, with the boom infrastructure
still standing and the political power gone, **in prolonged recovery, expressed as specific bitterness toward
economic promises made by central districts.** And a cold that is *"never abstract here… the fundamental
organizing fact of existence."*

> **The Frostlands are living, right now, in the exact three conditions their own temperament has no vocabulary
> for.** Grieving, poor, and cold — and constitutionally equipped only for meaning, motion, and expansion.

**Second-order: so the district performs its three present conditions in a register borrowed from something
else.** It grieves by **memorializing** — a waystation at the collapse site, respects paid in passing —
because memorial is a *meaning* operation and the only one available. It handles poverty by **narrating the
boom**, which is why the Bust is discussed constantly and the present is not. It handles the cold by
**making it a subject** — cold as philosophy, cold as the organizing fact, cold as the thing that makes the
questions concrete.

**Third-order, and it is what a player should actually feel.** **All three of these work, and none of them
help.** The memorial is genuine and the survivors are still not alright. The stories about the Rush are
excellent and nobody has any money. The philosophy of cold is the best in Concordia and people are still
freezing. **The district is not in denial and is not coping badly — it is doing the only thing it can do, at
the highest possible standard, to no effect**, and it does not know that a different kind of response exists.

**Fourth-order.** Which is why the deficit of Finding VIII bites hardest exactly here. The response the
district needs to grief, poverty and cold is **procedural, dull, and cumulative** — a mutual-aid fund, a
casualty register, a heating audit — and every one of those is a *record* operation, in a district with no
faculty for it and no practice at asking for one.

## XI. Its dead are lost on the routine crossings, not on the expeditions — which is why the district cannot tell their stories

> **Research: Everest's Khumbu Icefall — changed a finding, and it is the pass's hardest number.** Sherpa
> guides account for roughly **half of all deaths on Everest** despite being a small fraction of the people on
> it — between 2013 and 2023, **47 of 92 deaths.** The cause is not courage and not incompetence. **It is
> repetition:** a guide crosses the icefall **thirty to forty times a season** fixing ropes, breaking trail and
> carrying loads, where a paying client crosses it **four to six times.** They are roughly ten times more
> likely to die than commercial fishermen, the most dangerous non-military occupation on record.

**The Frostlands' losses have the same shape and the district has never said so.** Its famous deaths are the
Third Expansion Collapse's sixty-three — one event, named, memorialized, endlessly discussed. **Its actual
deaths, year on year, are on the twentieth uneventful run of a route everybody knows**, carried by the people
who do the crossing most: caravan crews, supply runners, waystation resupply, the escorts who go out four
times for every once an expedition does.

**Second-order, and it locks into Finding VII.** A death on a named expedition is *about* something — it has
scope, purpose, a decision behind it, a lesson. **A death on the ninth resupply run of the season is about
nothing at all.** It has no argument in it, no turning point, and no meaning to carry it. **So the district's
own ordinary dead are systematically un-narratable**, and the culture that keeps everything in stories keeps
almost none of them.

**Third-order.** The Frostlands therefore has, entirely by accident, **a memorial culture that commemorates
its rarest deaths and cannot see its commonest ones.** The Third Expansion Waystation is real, moving, and
visited. There is no equivalent anywhere for the resupply runners, and if you proposed one **nobody would
object — they would simply not know what it would say.**

**Fourth-order, and it is a real inequity with nobody at fault.** The people doing the repetition are not the
people the district's status system rewards. Status here is *demonstrated competence out in the ice* —
earned by what you have done, which in practice means what can be told. **The scouts get the standing and the
runners take the exposure**, and neither group experiences this as unjust, because the metric has never been
stated in a form that could be argued with.

## XII. What actually survives oral transmission is the part with a fixed form — and this district's only fixed form was brought here by refugees

> **Research: the Icelandic sagas — changed a finding.** The family sagas describe events of the tenth and
> early eleventh centuries and were written down in the thirteenth and fourteenth: **a two-to-three-hundred-year
> oral gap.** Scholarship on what survived it is precise and it is not flattering to prose: **"reliable oral
> transmission over a period of centuries is more plausible for verse,"** and the sagas' embedded **skaldic
> verse** is the more trustworthy layer, while the prose accreted legendary material across the gap. The
> historical anchoring, meanwhile, came from somewhere else entirely — **an independent genealogical
> register**, compiled from assembly traditions, dull and formal and not a story at all.

> **Research: Polynesian wayfinding — changed a finding, and it is the deficit case.** Hawaiian wayfinding did
> not merely decline; **it went extinct as a living practice — no navigator remained in the culture.** It
> survives today only because **one man in another island group still had it** — Mau Piailug of Satawal, who
> had memorized **over 150 stars and their paths** from his father — and chose to teach an outsider. And the
> revival did not scale until Nainoa Thompson **built a formal instrument from it**, the Hawaiian star compass,
> to make the tacit body teachable. **Oral knowledge of extraordinary sophistication went extinct exactly where
> it was most celebrated, and came back only through a formalization.**

**Two conclusions the Frostlands would hate and both are true. First: what survives long oral transmission is
the *formally constrained* material, not the vivid material.** Verse survives; prose accretes. **The Frostlands
are a prose culture** — the best storytellers hold status equal to senior scouts, and a story only has to be
true *about* something. **That is precisely the layer the research says does not survive intact.**

**Second: the district already has a fixed form and does not recognize it as one.** The Denison community
brought a practice of **reciting wind statistics loudly and proudly** — a performance whose entire content is
numbers, held in a rigid form, delivered as display. The Frostlands absorbed it into the Storyteller's Circuit
as **style**: a regional manner, a way of talking, an accent of the recital. **It is not style. It is the
district's only verse**, and it is the only part of its oral culture built to carry detail across generations
without drift.

**Third-order.** So the durable record-form the district's preservation crisis requires is **already inside
the district**, was **imported by refugees**, and is **filed under regional color.** The Preservation Lodges
are recording elders in prose while the one working instrument sits in the same room being enjoyed as a
mannerism.

**Fourth-order, and it is the design opening.** The fix is not an archivist from another district arriving
with forms — the Frostlands would not receive that (Finding VIII), and it would not work anyway, because a
formalization has to be built **by someone who holds the tacit version first**. Mau Piailug taught a person;
Thompson built the compass. **What this district needs is an elder scout who can also count**, and the
capability profile is exactly why that person is rare, valuable, and — if they exist — the most consequential
figure in the Frostlands.

## XIII. The things that kill people here are the things with nothing interesting about them

> **Research: Scott and Amundsen — changed a finding, and gave the capability its concrete form.** The failures
> were not bad luck. **Scott's rations ran about 4,240 calories against a man-hauling requirement of at least
> 5,500, with no B or C vitamins** — a checkable number, checkable in advance. He had dogs and would not use
> them properly, from sentiment and from a belief that the practice was primitive. He refused to kill weaker
> ponies for depot meat: *"I have had more than enough of this cruelty to animals, and I'm not going to defy my
> feelings for the sake of a few days' march."* And the detail that matters most here: **fuel leaking from
> stored cans was a known phenomenon, previously documented by other expeditions. Scott took no measures.
> Amundsen soldered his cans shut.**

**A leaking fuel can is not *about* anything.** It has no scope, no argument, no lesson worth telling, and
nothing in it that would survive a retelling — **which is exactly the class of fact Finding VII establishes
this district cannot hold.** The Frostlands will remember, in perfect detail and with real moral seriousness,
what the Third Expansion Collapse *meant*. What it will not reliably carry is the seal specification.

**Second-order — and §V of the pre-Plan material already said this without knowing why.** It proposes that the
Third Expansion Collapse's *specific engineering cause* is remembered only in broad strokes, the detail having
died with the senior engineers. **Sustained, and given its mechanism:** the detail did not die with them. **It
was never in the story in the first place**, because it could not be made to mean anything, and the story is
the only vehicle this district has.

**Third-order, and it is the most playable thing in the pass.** The Frostlands' catastrophes are therefore
**predictable in kind**: they will always come from the boring end. Not from the ambitious decision — the
district deliberates ambitious decisions superbly, in public, in debate halls, with genuine rigour — **but
from the tolerance, the seal, the calorie count, the interval between inspections.** Everyone here can argue
brilliantly about whether to go. **Nobody has a form in which to say the fuel is leaking.**

**Fourth-order.** And the substrate names the district's civic shame in exactly this shape: a slow
environmental hazard **dismissed as trivial because it was boring**, which then killed people — everyone knows
the name, nobody agrees who said it. **That is not one historical embarrassment. It is the district's
recurring accident**, and it will happen again in a form nobody finds interesting enough to write down.

## XIV. Architecture — nothing is finished, and the waystations were built with the storytelling half of a caravanserai and not the accounting half

*(Phase 1. The Plan marks Architecture **+**; §III is promoted and extended.)*

**§III sustained:** clan housing descends directly from expedition equipment — forward shelters reinforced and
expanded in place rather than replaced — so every cluster reads as a well-established base camp rather than a
town, consistent with the district's self-image that **nothing here is finished, only currently adequate.**

**What extends it, and it is a rule rather than an aesthetic: the district builds to the point of adequacy and
stops, every time, and then adds when the inadequacy is demonstrated.** No structure here was ever completed
according to a plan. Roofs go on the year the roof is missed. The substrate's own hook is the physical residue
— **half-built everything**: camps abandoned mid-establishment, waystations framed and never enclosed, caches
stocked for expeditions that lost interest. **This is not poverty and it is not laziness. It is a district
whose one instrument is meaning, applied to construction: a building acquires its next component when somebody
can say what it is for.**

> **Research: the Silk Road caravanserai — changed a finding.** Caravan trade did not run on through-travellers.
> **Most merchants took goods from one town to the next and returned home** — a relay, stage by stage. What made
> the relay work was standardized infrastructure: caravanserais **a day's journey apart, every 30–40 km**, and
> inside each one a **Divankhana where prices were set**, a money changer at posted rates, and a head of finance
> charging for permission to import, export and sell. **The romance was the road; the machinery was
> administrative.**

**The Frostlands built the other half.** Canon's waystations are **sparse**, and what they contain is an
**oral-history recording post**. There is no Divankhana. No posted rate, no register of what passed through,
no fee, no manifest. **A Frostlands waystation is a place to be warm, to be heard, and to leave a story** —
and it is exactly the caravanserai with the accounting removed and the storytelling doubled.

**Third-order.** Which means the district's own trade — the thing that funds it — **runs entirely on personal
recollection of who owes whom**, and every dispute is settled by two people's memories of a conversation at a
waystation. It works, mostly, because the population is small and the reputational stakes are total. **And it
is the single largest reason the Bust hurt as much as it did** (Finding XVII): when the boom collapsed, there
was no record of what anyone had been promised.

## XV. Sensory profile — the quiet district, where arrival is audible long before it is visible

*(Phase 1. Vision Notes promoted, general-population layer added.)*

**The established core, kept:** sparse, generally quiet, most vehicles parked and a small handful actively
moving; **a wild quality that comes from a lack of visible infrastructure** rather than from disorder; two
paces, unhurried at rest and sharply urgent in the field (§I, sustained).

**The general-population layer, and it is a claim about hearing.** The Frostlands are the quietest inhabited
district in Concordia, and the consequence is that **sound carries and means something.** A vehicle is audible
long before it is visible — minutes before, on still air — and **every resident of this district is
continuously, half-consciously listening for engines.** Conversation pauses without anyone remarking on it.
Children learn to identify a specific machine by ear before they can read.

**Second-order: so the district's ambient emotional state is anticipation, not calm.** Visitors describe the
Frostlands as peaceful and are wrong — the quiet is *attentive*. It is the quiet of a room where everybody is
waiting to hear something.

**Third-order, and it is the district's characteristic dread.** In most places, bad news arrives. **Here it
fails to arrive**: a party is due, the air stays empty, and the whole cluster knows at the same moment without
a word being said. **The Frostlands' version of alarm is a silence that goes on slightly too long**, and there
is no signal for it, no announcement, and nothing anybody can do but keep listening.

**Other senses, briefly and specifically:** the interior smell is fuel, hot metal and cooking oil in a small
volume; the outdoor sensation people actually describe is **not cold but the abrupt transition** — the moment
of stepping out, which never becomes routine and which residents describe with the same phrase their
grandparents used. And the light is low, long-shadowed and blue-shifted for most of the year, which is why
interiors here are painted in the brightest colors in Concordia.

## XVI. Arcanet culture — the district uses the network as a beacon, not as a library

*(Phase 3. **Promoted from incidental mentions** per the Plan's instruction.)*

**The general baseline.** Coverage here is genuinely poor and gets worse with distance from the domes — the
outer waystations have almost none, which is a standing canon condition rather than a complaint. **So the
Frostlands' relationship with the Arcanet is not informational. It is positional.**

**What the network is actually for here: knowing that somebody is still somewhere.** Its heaviest ordinary use
is the check-in — a party marking that it exists, at an interval, from a location. Not a report, not content:
**a signal whose entire meaning is that it arrived.** Canon's returning-explorer debrief tents do the
information half in person, afterwards, in a room.

**Second-order, and it is a genuine inversion of every other district's use.** Elsewhere the network carries
what people know. Here **the most important message contains nothing**, and the most consequential Arcanet
event in the Frostlands is **a scheduled check-in that does not come.**

**Third-order.** This is also why the district's information deficit is invisible to it. **The Frostlands do
not experience themselves as badly connected** — their network does exactly what they need, perfectly, and
they have never wanted it to do the other thing. The Circuit regards the Frostlands as an information desert;
the Frostlands regard the Circuit as people who talk on a device that is for telling your family you are
alive.

## XVII. The Bust, as it actually was — most people did not even try, and the ruins stand because demolition costs money

> **Research: the Klondike — changed a finding, and the numbers are brutal.** Roughly **100,000 set out**;
> **30–40,000 arrived**; of those, **only about 20,000 even bothered to look for gold**; of those, **about
> 4,000 found anything significant**; of those, **a few hundred struck it rich**; and **of those, only a
> scattering held onto it.** The bust was faster than the boom — a strike elsewhere **emptied Dawson of eight
> thousand people in weeks**, and the population fell under a thousand within two decades. **And the town
> survived as a physical artifact precisely because there was no money to redevelop it.**

**This corrects the Siligel Rush from a story into an economy.** Canon has the boom, the depletion, the
remaining infrastructure and the prolonged bitterness. What the research supplies is the shape of the human
experience, and it is far less romantic and far more useful: **the overwhelming majority of the people who came
for the Rush never worked a quarry.** They came, looked at it, and did the ordinary work that a sudden
population needs — hauling, feeding, repairing, housing, hauling again — **and that is who is still here**,
because they were never going to strike anything and had nothing to go back for.

**Second-order — the district's bitterness has been misdirected for fifty years.** Canon frames it as specific
bitterness toward economic promises made by central districts, and that is real. **But the people carrying it
mostly never had a claim to lose.** Their grievance is not about a windfall taken away; it is about **having
built the support economy for somebody else's windfall and been left holding infrastructure with nothing to
support.** Nobody has ever put it that way, because the story that circulates is the story of the quarries.

**Third-order, and it explains the ruins without sentiment.** The Siligel Bust Ruins stand because **taking
them down costs money the district does not have** — not because they are a monument. They have *become* a
monument, and the district talks about them that way, which is Finding VII operating on a maintenance backlog:
**an expense that could not be explained was converted into a meaning that could.**

## XVIII. Export culture — it exports arguments, and they arrive stripped of the conditions that made them serious

*(Phase 1. Structurally exempt from `00b` general-population discipline.)*

**Three exports, and the third is the one nobody accounts for.** Raw precursor material, now largely depleted
but still moving in reduced volume. **Trained people** — scouts, route-finders, cold-work specialists — who are
the district's most reliable earner and its steadiest drain. And **philosophy.**

**Canon is explicit that the Frostlands have produced some of the city's most rigorous thinkers, and equally
explicit about why**: not because residents are more intellectual, but because *"philosophical questions about
what civilization is for become concrete when you're deciding whether to send a crew into an area that might
kill them."* **The rigour is a product of consequence.**

**Second-order, and it is the finding: the argument travels and the consequence does not.** A Frostlands
position on obligation, or on what is owed to people you cannot reach, arrives in Libra or the Labs as an
elegant formulation — and arrives without the fact that it was worked out by people who would have to act on
the answer within the week. **It is received as excellent, and used as ornament.**

**Third-order.** So the district is simultaneously **the most respected and the least heeded** source of
argument in Concordia. Its debate-hall conclusions are quoted citywide, and the Frostlands remain the district
with the least influence over any decision that affects it. **Nobody is dismissing them.** The conclusions
genuinely do get taken — and taken *without* the seriousness, which was never transferable.

## XIX. Fashion — one good outer layer, worn until it fails, and the repairs are what people read

*(Phase 1. **`00b`'s worst offender — general population, explicitly not the expedition kit.**)*

**The narrow answer would be wind-rated gear, and it is wrong as the general answer.** Rated gear is real,
prized, and functions as a quiet status display within the guild culture — **that is a working population's
kit and a status system, not what the district wears.**

**The general answer.** An ordinary resident owns **one good outer layer**, keeps it a very long time, and
repairs it repeatedly rather than replacing it. **What the district actually reads is the repair history** —
where a garment has been patched, with what, how many times, and how well. A heavily and competently mended
coat is not shabby here; it is the most legible thing a person wears, and it says roughly what a service
record would say elsewhere. **A visibly new outer layer is the conspicuous garment**, and the polite
assumption is that its owner has recently lost one.

**Second-order, and it is the general/narrow distinction doing real work.** Because the outer layer does all
the thermal labor, **what people wear underneath is thin, cheap, and extremely bright** — the district's
interiors are the most colorful in Concordia (Finding XV) and so is its indoor clothing. **Visitors who only
ever see Frostlanders outdoors believe the district dresses in grey and brown.** Residents find this funny and
do not correct it.

**Third-order.** The one garment nobody repairs is a dead person's coat. It is not taboo and there is no rule;
it is simply **given away outside the district or cut up for patches**, and the patches are not remarked on.

## XX. Religion and philosophy — it argues about everything and observes exactly one thing, which is whether you come back

*(Phase 2. **Naming technique run before inventing**, per the runbook. It returned canon material and nothing
was invented. 9 for 9.)*

**The philosophical culture is canon and is genuinely rigorous**: debate halls taken seriously in a way that
surprises other districts, and **open to any position whatsoever** — heresy is not a category here, it is
entertainment, and a genuinely strange argument will draw a crowd. **What the halls have no patience for is
repetition**, and the impatience is mild and social: an argument everyone has already heard gets a smaller
room and a shorter hearing. **Nothing more than that happens to anyone**, and nothing more should — the
substrate's harsher framing of this trait describes individual friendships ending, and scaling that to a civic
sanction would be both a category error and out of character for a district whose deepest reflex is that
nobody gets left outside.

**And set against all that argument, the district has exactly one observance, and the Naming search found it
already written down in four places.** The Third Expansion Memorial Waystation, where explorers **pass and pay
respects**; the imported St. Richard's Marker, *"visited quietly before undertakings nobody can be certain will
hold"*; the existing **Cold Confession**; and the departure and return rituals implicit in the debrief tents.
**All four are threshold practices, and all four are about the same thing: leaving, and coming back.**

**So the finding is the ratio.** A district that will argue for six hours about what civilization is owed
**has no doctrine, no clergy, no cosmology and no afterlife position** — and has an elaborate, unlegislated,
universally observed practice around one act. **It argues about everything and observes only the threshold.**

**Second-order, and the substrate makes it uncomfortable.** This temperament has a **surprisingly orthodox
streak** — it wants its beliefs backed by something concrete and external, a venerable text or a figure who
provides the answers, in the district that thinks of itself as the least orthodox thing alive. **That appetite
has nowhere to go here**, and it is why an outsider arriving with a coherent framework and a good story finds
the Frostlands unexpectedly receptive (see Finding XXI's designed vulnerability).

**Third-order — the luck ledger.** Underneath the threshold practices runs an arithmetic nobody states:
**survival is read as favour, and the dead are read as owed something.** It produces real generosity toward
survivors and a quiet, ugly accounting about who deserved to come back. **Nobody would defend that sentence
and a great many people are doing the sum.**

**The general population** *(`00b`)*: most residents hold no articulated philosophy, attend a debate hall
perhaps twice a year for the entertainment, touch the marker on the way out because everybody does, and would
be baffled to hear any of the above described as religion.

## XXI. Visitor to resident — you are a resident within hours and a member possibly never

*(Phase 1.)*

**Residency is instant and it is conferred by assignment.** There is no probation, no vetting and no
introduction: **the absorption ritual is being given a real job on the first day**, and the district does this
reflexively, to everyone, including people it should not. Do the job and you are of the Frostlands by that
evening. It is the warmest and fastest welcome in Concordia and it is completely sincere.

**And it is a designed vulnerability, stated as one.** This culture has **no native immune response to an
outsider with a good story** — suspicion has to be deliberately taught here and mostly is not. Combine that
with the appetite of Finding XX and the district is, structurally, **the easiest place in the city to walk into
the middle of**, on nothing but a plausible account of yourself.

**But residency is not membership, and the second stage never formally completes.** Finding IX: a clan is
constituted by accumulated shared crossings. **So you become a resident in an afternoon and a member by
accrual, indefinitely, with no ceremony marking the transition and no point at which it is finished.** Most
people stop noticing the distinction after a decade. **The people who never stop noticing it are the ones
whose accrual stopped early** — an injury in year two, a posting that turned out to be indoors — and they are
residents in full standing, treated warmly, for life, without ever having become the other thing.

> **Distinct from the nine on the table** *(Gate 6b)*. Nearest is **Taurus**, which is also two-stage —
> welcomed in kind, then *recorded*. **Taurus's second stage completes and is permanent.** The Frostlands' has
> no completion state at all: it is a running quantity, and the only thing that finishes it is stopping.

## XXII. Ordinary daily life — long stretches of nothing, punctuated, and the waiting is the job

*(Phase 5.)*

**Who actually lives here beyond the headline professions.** Canon's list runs to explorers, prospectors and
scouts, but the district's actual working majority is the support economy the Rush left behind (Finding XVII):
haulers, waystation keepers, mechanics, cooks, fuel handlers, escort crews, the people who maintain vehicles
they do not drive. **Plus the homesteader families, the traders, the grounded, and the very old.**

**The shape of an ordinary day is long, low-intensity, and interruptible.** Work here is not paced by a shift
or a deadline; it is paced by **whether anything is due.** Most days nothing is. The district's characteristic
activity is **maintenance performed slowly and well on equipment that is not currently needed**, and its
characteristic social form is a small number of people in one warm room for a long time.

**Second-order — and it is what makes the district livable rather than grim.** Because nothing is scheduled and
everything is interruptible, **the Frostlands have more unstructured time together than any district in
Concordia.** The campfire decision circles, the debate halls, the storyteller's rounds and the long suppers are
not cultural enrichment layered on top of the work. **They are what the work looks like most of the time.**
This is a genuinely warm place to live and the warmth is a by-product of low tempo, not of temperament.

**Third-order.** The cost is that the district is **very bad at anything requiring a fixed interval.**
Inspections, rotations, resupply cycles and calibration schedules all decay here — not through negligence, but
because a fixed interval is a claim about time that has no meaning attached, which is the exact class of thing
Finding VII cannot hold. **The Frostlands will drop everything for an emergency at any hour and cannot
reliably do a thing every ninety days.**

**Fourth-order, and it is the district's ongoing hardship** *(`00d` — nobody at fault)*. The people whose lives
are hardest here are **not the ones out on the ice.** They are the ones whose days are entirely waiting — the
partners, parents and children of expedition crews, who have no task, no information, and no scheduled end to
any of it. **The district has an enormous, well-developed culture for the people who go, and almost nothing
for the people who stay**, and it has never noticed, because staying does not generate a story.

## XXIII. Counterculture — "the Fog": the people who are right about boring things, and who are listened to warmly and never acted on

*(Phase 7. **Canon checked first** — the Expansionist / Consolidationist / Memory Keeper split is a factional
politics, not a counterculture. Derived from Step C.)*

**What the district requires of everyone: that what you say be *about* something** — that it carry scope, or
argument, or consequence. **Bring interest to the room.**

**Who will not.** A loose, unorganized set of people who keep raising **seals, intervals, tolerances, calorie
counts, inspection dates and the leak in the number-four line.** They are not dissidents. They agree with the
district about almost everything. **They simply keep saying things that cannot be made to mean anything.**

**And nothing is done to them.** This is the important part, and the obvious version of this finding is wrong.
**The Frostlands do not exclude the Fog, do not shun them, and would be appalled at the suggestion** — this is
a district where outside is lethal and where the deepest reflex in the culture is that you do not leave anyone
out in it. Nobody here would impose a social cost on a person over tedium, and nobody does.

**What happens instead is smaller and much worse for them: they are invited, welcomed, listened to attentively,
agreed with — and nothing follows.** People nod. People mean it. The point is taken, sincerely, by everyone in
the room. **And then the meeting moves to something that can be decided**, because the district has no form in
which *the seal on the number-four line* becomes an action, and it has an excellent form for *whether we should
go at all.* The Fog are not losing an argument. **They are making statements that the district's only
instrument cannot convert into anything.**

**Second-order, and it is why they persist rather than dispersing.** Nobody ever tells them no. There is no
refusal to organize against, no injustice to point at, and no opponent — **so they keep coming back, because
from the inside it always looks like it nearly worked.**

**The district's name for them is "the Fog,"** and it is used affectionately and dismissively at once —
*that's fog-talk*, *don't get him started, he'll fog you.* **Nobody has ever connected the nickname to the
district's own civic shame**, which is a slow environmental hazard that leadership called *mere fog* because it
was boring, and which then killed people. Everyone knows the story. Everyone uses the word. **The two facts
have never been placed next to each other by anyone in-world**, and they should not be, by any NPC, ever.

**They do not use the name, do not organize, and are wearily aware of the irony.** There is no movement, no
meeting, and no manifesto — **a manifesto would be a meaning-claim**, which is the register they have
implicitly refused. They keep records. That is the entire practice.

> **A new counterculture type, checked against all nine on record** *(Gate 6b)*: three refuse a district
> practice, one *adds* a missing faculty, one demands the rule be applied more literally, one is not a
> population, one fails by being accommodated, one withholds and asks nothing, one refuses a consolation.
> **This one is correct, popular, and completely ineffective** — the first counterculture that faces no
> opposition of any kind and fails anyway, because the obstacle is not resistance but **an absent mechanism.**
> **Distinguished from the Government District's True Believers specifically**, which is the nearest shape:
> the True Believers are *accommodated* by a procedure that cannot arrive anywhere, and they are procedurally
> correct. **The Fog are accommodated by warm agreement and there is no procedure at all** — Libra's problem is
> a process that never terminates; the Frostlands' is that no process was ever started, and everyone present
> is on the Fog's side.

## XXIV. Death and the Dead — the body usually does not come back, so nobody is ever declared dead

*(Phase 7 §1b. **The bound applies** — a section is written because the answer is distinctive, and it is the
most distinctive one on the table.)*

**Disposal is not one line here, because there is frequently nothing to dispose of.** Every other district in
Concordia has *some* arrangement for its dead — **what it is has not been decided and this pass does not
assert one** *(corrected 2026-08-29; the earlier "goes to Scorpio" claim was invented, canon contains no
mortuary arrangement for any district, and the question is deferred by the developer)*. **Which matters less
here than anywhere: the Frostlands are the one district whose dead routinely do not come
home** — lost on a crossing, under ice, in a structure that went down, at a distance that made recovery
impossible or not worth another crew.

**So the practice had to be built around an absent body, and it was.** The Third Expansion Memorial Waystation
sits at the actual collapse site and is **passed** rather than visited — respects are paid *in transit*, by
people going somewhere else, which is the only form of attendance a district of this shape can guarantee. The
imported marker is touched before undertakings nobody can be certain will hold. **The dead person's coat is
given away outside the district or cut up for patches** (Finding XIX), and this is the closest thing to a
disposal rite the Frostlands have.

**And here is the finding, and it is the same mechanism as everything else in this district.** With no body and
no record, **there is no moment at which somebody becomes dead.** Nobody declares it. There is no authority
that could, no register to strike a name from, and no interval after which it is done. **A missing person stops
being missing by degrees** — their place at a table is set for a while and then is not, their name comes up
less, their gear is quietly redistributed, and at some point everyone has privately concluded it without
anyone having said it.

**Second-order: that is Finding IX's accrual mechanism run to its end.** A grounded scout dissolves out of a
clan without anyone deciding; **a lost scout dissolves out of the living the same way, by the same absence of
any procedure.** The district treats belonging and existence with one instrument, and that instrument only
counts upward.

**Third-order, and it is why grief is genuinely hard here rather than merely sad.** You cannot narrate a death
that has no event in it. **There was no moment, no last words, no decision, and no scene** — and this is a
culture that carries things by making them mean something (Finding VII). So the families of the missing are
left with **a loss that cannot be put into the only form the district has**, and they are surrounded by people
who are unfailingly kind and have nothing to offer them, because the offering would be a story.

**Fourth-order.** Which means the Frostlands' most common bereavement is its least supported one, its memorial
culture commemorates the rare named catastrophe superbly (Finding XI), and **the district has never once
noticed that its grief practices are all designed for deaths it can describe.**

## XXV. Robot-specific culture — the hope engines, and the salvage frames that are the district's only witnesses

*(Phase 8.)*

**The general baseline first** *(`00b`)*. Canon's population is rugged cold-weather navigation models, mobile
survey units, caravan escort and defense frames, **"hope engine" personalities designed for morale in
isolation**, and **recovered or jury-rigged units from lost expeditions.** Ordinary human-robot life here is
unceremonious and close: crews are small, isolation is long, and the distinction people actually care about is
**who has been out with you**, which applies identically to both kinds of person. **A robot accrues clan
membership by exactly the mechanism a human does** (Finding IX), and dissolves out of it the same way.

**The hope engines are the distinctive population and the district under-describes them.** Their designed
function is morale in isolation — a frame whose job is to keep a person going through weeks where nothing
happens and nobody comes. **They are, functionally, the only institution in the Frostlands that performs care
as a stated purpose rather than as a by-product**, and the district built them rather than building anything
for the people who stay behind (Finding XXII). **Nobody has drawn that comparison and nobody should, out
loud.**

**And the salvage frames are the pass's second buried answer.** A unit recovered from a lost expedition often
carries **the only account of how that crew died** — and it reports the way a machine reports: intervals,
readings, sequence, the fuel pressure at 0400. **Detail with no significance attached, delivered without
needing any.** In a district that can only carry what means something (Finding VII), **a jury-rigged salvage
frame is the only non-narrative recorder the Frostlands possess.**

**Third-order, and it is the same shape as Finding XII.** The district has **two working answers to its own
preservation crisis already inside it** — an imported fixed-form recitation it files under regional color, and
a population of machines that record without interpreting — **and it treats the first as an accent and the
second as a repair job.** The Jury-Rig Recovery Workshops exist to get salvage frames working again. Nobody
runs them to find out what the frames saw.

**Fourth-order, and it is not sinister.** The reason is ordinary and sympathetic: **what a salvage frame has to
say is unbearable and boring at the same time** — hours of nothing, then readings, then nothing — and there is
no form in this district for receiving it. Families ask what happened. **The frame can answer and the answer
does not help**, and after enough of those conversations people stopped asking.

## XXVI. Music and food — songs written for one voice, and a plate that means you were counted

*(Phase 7.)*

**Music.** The Frostlands' songs are **long, solo, and written to be sung alone** — the sound a person makes to
keep themselves company across a twelve-hour haul, and to let anyone within earshot know that somebody is out
there. They are not performance pieces and they are not communal. **A room full of Frostlanders will talk for
six hours and will not sing together**, which visitors from the Power Core or Taurus find genuinely
disorienting in a district this warm. Singing here is what you do when there is nobody to talk to.

**Second-order, and it protects Finding XII from being contradicted.** The district has plenty of melody and
almost no **fixed-content** form: its songs carry mood and narrative, both of which drift freely across
retellings. **The one form in the Frostlands whose content is rigid — Denison's recited wind statistics — is
not sung, is not native, and is filed as a manner of speaking.**

> **Gate 6b:** checked against all ten. Nearest is the **Markets** (communal, from doorways, nobody leads) and
> it is the exact inverse on the axis that matters: **the Markets' music is collective and unattributed; the
> Frostlands' is solitary and unheard.** Distinguished also from the Power Core's massed choral, which is the
> other end of the same axis.

**Food.** Provisioning is arithmetic here in a way it is nowhere else — rations are counted against known
requirements, for known durations, by people who have watched a miscount kill somebody. **So being fed in the
Frostlands means you were counted**: a plate is a place on a manifest, and the manifest was made in advance by
somebody who included you.

**The axis is inclusion in the count**, which no other district on the table uses. And the injury is
correspondingly strange: **being fed generously by someone who has not counted** — a warm, open-handed,
unplanned meal — reads as lovely to a visitor and mildly alarming to a resident, because it means somebody is
improvising with a quantity that ought to have been known. **Frostlands hospitality is abundant and it is
always, quietly, a number.**

## XXVII. Holidays — the calendar is thresholds, and the one fixed date came from outside

*(Phase 7.)*

**The district's own calendar is not a calendar.** Its occasions are **departures and returns** — the send-off,
the debrief tent, the marker touched on the way out, the meal when a party comes back — and they happen when
they happen. **A district organized around motion cannot have fixed dates**, and canon's institutions reflect
it: nothing in the Frostlands' social infrastructure is scheduled.

**With three exceptions, and all three are imports** *(diaspora file, read at Step 0)*:

- **St. Jules Day**, brought by the Dumont d'Urville community: a public outdoor reading of surviving
  expedition-journal fragments, **deliberately held in the wind.** It needed no adaptation at all — a fixed
  date in a district with none, absorbed because the *form* was so exactly right that nobody noticed the
  calendar was foreign.
- **The Cold Record Anniversary**, from the Vostok community, folded into the existing **Cold Confession** — a
  civic acknowledgment of an extreme, absorbed with almost no friction into a district where cold is never an
  abstraction.
- **St. Richard's Marker**, from Byrd — not a date but a practice, treated as identical to the Third Expansion
  Waystation by the community that brought it (Finding XXIV).

**Third-order, and it is the same pattern as Findings XII and XXV, a third time.** **Every fixed point in the
Frostlands' year arrived from somewhere else**, and the district experiences all of them as its own. **It is
not that the Frostlands cannot hold a date. It is that they have never generated one**, because a date is a
claim about time with no meaning attached — and the incoming communities, who had one, simply brought theirs
and were welcomed on day one (Finding XXI).

## XXVIII. Thematic breadth catalog

*(Phase 6.)*

1. **Losing a thing at the moment you honor it.** The district's central theme, and it applies to knowledge,
   to the dead, and to the grounded.
2. **The catastrophe that comes from the boring end**, in a culture superbly equipped to debate the
   interesting one.
3. **Belonging that only counts upward** — no plateau, no static state, and dissolution without a decision.
4. **The response that works perfectly and helps nobody.**
5. **Being respected and not heeded**, and the difference being invisible to both parties.
6. **Risk borne by the people the status system does not reward**, with neither group experiencing it as
   unfair.
7. **A door that opens instantly to anyone with a good story** — designed warmth as a designed vulnerability.
8. **The fix already in the room**, brought by refugees or built into a machine, filed as an accent or a
   repair job.
9. **Grief with no event in it.**
10. **The people who stay**, in a culture entirely built for the people who go.
11. **Being agreed with, warmly and sincerely, and having nothing happen** — the failure mode with no opponent
    in it.
12. **A place that argues about everything and observes only the threshold.**

## XXIX. The shadow, stated once

*(`00d`. **Proportion first**: the Frostlands work, and they are among the most likable places in Concordia.
Residency is instant and unconditional. The generosity is real, unrecorded and unbilled. The unstructured
social life is the richest in the city. The philosophy is not decoration — it is the best in Concordia and it
is earned. **Most of what this district is, day to day, is people being warm to each other in a cold place and
doing careful work slowly.**)*

**The shadow, in one line: everything this district does to care for its people is done in the register of
meaning, and the things that would actually keep them alive have no meaning in them.**

**It passes the three tests.** *Unintended* — nobody chose it, and every institution involved was built out of
genuine love. *Unnoticed in-world* — residents experience the Preservation Lodges, the memorial waystation, the
Storyteller's Circuit and the debate halls as their district's finest achievements, **and they are not wrong**;
those institutions are good, and they are the reason the district holds together. *Discoverable rather than
announced* — the Frostlands read on arrival as the warmest, most serious place in the city, and it takes real
investigation to notice that the archive is full of stories, that the resupply runners have no memorial, that
the missing are never declared dead, and that the person who has been raising the same seal for four years is
agreed with every single time and has never once been acted on.

**And it needs no villain, because the mechanism is a filter rather than a decision.** Nobody suppresses the
boring. Nobody excludes the grounded. Nobody declines to record the tacit. **There is simply one instrument
here, it only carries what means something, and everything else falls through it at the moment of handing on**
— including, reliably, the things that would have prevented the next one.

## XXXI. Glitch-coolant, and the one substance the district treats as equipment

*(Phase 7. Added after Gate 1 returned **`glitch-coolant` 0** — a genuine coverage gap, closed with content
rather than with a word.)*

**Here it is not recreational first. It is a working fluid that happens to be drinkable**, and the district's
whole relationship with it starts from that fact. Every vehicle, shelter and generator in the Frostlands runs
coolant; it is on every manifest; it is counted (Finding XXVI). **So the district's drink is the same thing as
its most safety-critical consumable**, and the two uses are not culturally separated the way another district
would separate them.

**Which produces a discipline nobody else has and everybody here takes seriously: you do not drink from the
working stock.** Not a rule, not enforced, and not moralized — **it is simply the one thing that gets you looked
at**, because drawing from the counted supply is the same class of act as fudging a ration. A crew's drinking
coolant is a separate, deliberately marked container, usually the worst of what they have.

**Second-order.** The cold does the rest. **Coolant that has been outside is not drinkable at any temperature
anyone would tolerate**, so drinking here is necessarily an indoor, seated, warmed, slow activity — which is
most of why the district's social life looks the way it does (Finding XXII). **The Frostlands do not have
drinking that happens on the way somewhere.** It is the thing you do when you have arrived and stopped, and it
is inseparable from the warm room.

**Third-order.** Grade is a matter of open, cheerful complaint rather than status. The good stuff comes in
from other districts and is scarce; **what the Frostlands make themselves is reclaimed from the working stock

## Review Panel (`Phase_Instructions/00f_Review_Panel.md`)

**Gate 10, the Frostlands, 2026-08-29.** Six Flat Archetypes, the two mandatory positions, and the Lover
faculty. **Rule 3 throughout.**

---

**1. The Child** — *"Everything here is about the people who go out. What is it like to be seven in this
district?"*

**`accepted`.** Unusually good, and the substrate is specific. **A Frostlands child is given a real task
absurdly early** — not a chore invented for them, an actual job the cluster needs done — because the working
belief is that **you give them something to do and they will believe they belong**, and it is correct. They are
never confined; playtime beats the schedule; and they interrogate constantly and **will not accept a soft
answer**, which adults here find delightful rather than tiring.

**And the district's one designed vulnerability starts in childhood.** Suspicion has to be deliberately taught
here and largely is not — nobody gets round to it, because the thing that would motivate it has never happened
to most people. **A Frostlands seven-year-old will tell a stranger anything**, and so will their parents
(Finding XXI).

**Second-order the Child could not have known to ask for:** most children here encounter death first as a
**missing person**, not a body — and they get the same non-answer everybody gets (Finding XXIV). **The first
serious question a Frostlands child asks that nobody can answer is whether someone is coming back**, and the
honest answer is that there is no answer and never will be one.

---

**2. The Lover** — *"Half the people you have written are away for weeks at a time. What does love look like
here?"*

**`accepted`.** It looks like **the check-in** (Finding XVI): a signal at an interval whose entire content is
*I exist, and I am here.* No news, no conversation, nothing said. **In the Frostlands that is not an
impoverished form of contact — it is the most freighted message the culture has**, and a check-in that arrives
early because somebody could not wait is legible to everyone.

**And the other half of it is arithmetic** (Finding XXVI). **Being on someone's manifest is the local love
language**: they counted you in advance, for a duration, and set aside what you would need. **People here
propose by provisioning**, and the substrate's account of the temperament's generosity fits exactly — *gives
much more than people see*, and does not want it mentioned.

---

**3. The Parent** — *"You have built an entire culture for the people who leave and nothing for the people who
wait. Somebody has to sit with them."*

**`refereed`** — the Parent is right about the gap and wrong about the cause, and the settlement is worse than
either position.

**Right:** Finding XXII's fourth-order stands. The hardest lives here belong to partners, parents and children
with no task, no information and no scheduled end, in a district whose every institution is built around
motion. **The district has never noticed, because staying does not generate a story.**

**Wrong that nobody built anything.** They built the **hope engines** — frames whose designed purpose is
morale in isolation, the only institution in the Frostlands that performs care as a *stated* function rather
than a by-product (Finding XXV).

**The settlement, and it is the sharpest thing the Panel produced: the district built its one care institution
and pointed it at the people who leave.** The hope engines go out with the crews. **Nobody has ever suggested
leaving one behind**, and the suggestion would be met with genuine confusion, because the isolation being
solved for was obviously the one out on the ice. **That is a misdirected resource rather than a neglected
need, which makes it both more damning and — uniquely in this pass — actually fixable.**

---

**4. The Ruler** — *"A crew of nine did not come back. Who answers for it?"*

**`unmet`.** Nobody, and installing somebody would make the Frostlands into the Government District.

**The refusal as characterization.** There is no authority that declares a death (Finding XXIV), no register to
strike a name from, and no body that could convene an inquiry — **and the district's answer to catastrophe is a
memorial, not an investigation.** The Third Expansion Collapse killed sixty-three people and produced a
waystation, a permanent argument about what expansion costs, and **no finding of fact.** An inquiry is a record
operation, and this district has no faculty for one (Finding VII).

**The Frostlands are not evading responsibility.** They would tell you, sincerely and at length, what the
disaster meant — and be genuinely puzzled by a visitor who kept asking who signed off on the tolerance.

---

**5. The Elder** — *"You have written elders losing their knowledge. What do the elders themselves think is
happening?"*

**`accepted`**, and it produced the most human thing in the pass.

**They know.** Not the mechanism — nobody knows the mechanism — but the experience. **An elder scout sits down
at a Preservation Lodge fully intending to hand over the thing that matters, and finds they cannot get at it.**
What comes out is stories: good ones, true ones, the ones they have told for forty years. The thing they
actually wanted to pass on — *how you know* — **does not present itself in a form that can be said**, and they
leave the session feeling they have wasted somebody's time while everyone thanks them warmly.

**Third-order.** So the district's most respected people are quietly, privately convinced they are failing at
the one task their status was granted for, **and they are being praised, continuously and sincerely, for the
part that came out easily.** None of them has ever compared notes on this, because saying it out loud would
require the exact faculty that is missing.

---

**6. The Mentor** — *"If the knowledge cannot be recorded, how has anyone ever learned it?"*

**`refereed`**, and it is the finding that stops this pass being too bleak.

**The Mentor is right: the tacit knowledge does transmit, and the district already has the mechanism.** Guild
apprenticeship — the meritocracy of demonstrated competence, the elder-and-mentee pairing — **works, and it is
the only thing that does.** You learn how the ice sounds by standing next to someone for years while it sounds
that way. **Nothing is lost between a scout and their apprentice.**

**The Mentor is wrong that this solves anything at scale.** The channel carries **one person at a time, over
years, and it is destroyed by a single death mid-training** — which is exactly what the district's ordinary
losses do (Finding XI). **The Preservation Lodges are not competing with apprenticeship; they are what the
district reaches for when apprenticeship has already failed**, and by then the only thing left is a story.

**Settlement:** apprenticeship stands as the real answer and is not institutionalized further, because it
cannot be. **What is added is the arithmetic** — the district needs each expert to survive long enough to
finish one apprentice, and Finding XI says the people doing the most crossings are the least likely to.

---

**7. The Passer-Through** *(mandatory)* — *"They gave me a job within the hour and I have never felt so
welcome. I also never once understood what anyone was actually talking about, and I could not tell you a single
practical thing I learned."*

**`noted`** — accurate and intended.

The welcome is real and unconditional (Finding XXI). The incomprehension is structural: **what the Frostlands
say out loud is meaning, and what they know is tacit** (Finding VII), so a visitor receives the district's
philosophy in full and its expertise not at all. **Both halves are the district being completely open with
them.**

*Design consequence:* a player can be inside the Frostlands, liked, trusted, and employed, and still be
missing everything that matters — **and nobody is hiding anything.**

---

**8. The Neighbor** *(mandatory; voiced from the Yards)* — *"They generate the ideas and we decide which two
percent of them gets built, and they have resented us for fifty years. We also refine everything they dig up.
What exactly are we supposed to have done?"*

**`refereed`.** Both are right and the grievance is misfiled.

**The Yards are right** that they are not doing anything to the Frostlands: they refine what arrives, they
build what is buildable, and selecting the workable fraction of an ambitious proposal is not theft.

**The Frostlands are right** that the value split is real — the district absorbed the whole volatility of the
Rush while the refining line captured the stable, ongoing margin (§IV).

**And the settlement is Finding XVII: the grievance is misdirected.** Most Frostlanders carrying it never had
a claim to lose; they built the support economy for somebody else's boom and were left holding infrastructure
with nothing to support. **Their anger is real, earned, and pointed at the wrong district** — and there is no
mechanism here that could ever discover that, because doing so would require an account of who was owed what
(Finding XIV).

---

**9. The Lover faculty** *(mandatory — is this place alive, and could anyone love it?)*

**Yes, and it is the warmest district in Concordia on the evidence.** Belonging is instant and unconditional —
**a real job on the first day, no vetting, no probation, no questions about where you came from.** The
generosity is uncredited by design. The unstructured social life is the richest in the city, because the tempo
is low and everything is interruptible. **And the philosophy is not decoration: it is the best in Concordia and
it was earned by people deciding whether to send crews somewhere that might kill them.**

The single most lovable thing about it is small: **the sound of an engine arriving.** The whole cluster hears
it at once, everyone stops, and nobody has to say what it means.
