# Pisces — Questlines

**What this is:** each of Pisces' own Cross-Reference Synthesis findings supplies a 4th-order effect; that effect becomes this document's new 1st-order cause, from which new 2nd-, 3rd-, and 4th-order effects are derived, explicitly framed toward questline potential. Written 2026-07-10.

---

## Questline Thread 1 — from Finding 1 (Pisces' aftercare pipeline is the first to fail under strain)

**1st-order cause (= Finding 1's 4th-order effect):** the aftercare Pisces' own Believers and Flood-Traumatized residents depend on from Scorpio is the most silently vulnerable thread in the entire Pisces/Scorpio relationship, invisible until the moment it breaks.

**New 2nd-order effect:** the player witnesses the moment it breaks — a specific Flood-Traumatized or Believer character they know personally is turned away or deprioritized when Scorpio's own capacity crisis (see Scorpio's own Questlines, Thread 1) forces triage.

**New 3rd-order effect:** this opens a fork: help this specific person find alternative care within Pisces itself, a harder path since no equivalent institution currently exists there; advocate directly for their case within Scorpio, winning them care at the explicit cost of displacing someone else's; or use this specific failure as concrete evidence to push for the broader capacity-expansion or restructuring options already available in Scorpio's own questline material.

**New 4th-order effect:** building alternative care within Pisces could seed a genuinely new institution the district has never needed before, but the player is essentially inventing a support structure with no established precedent to draw on; advocating within Scorpio wins one case at someone else's direct expense, a genuine zero-sum choice; using the failure as leverage for the larger systemic fight ties Pisces' own local tragedy to a bigger, more consequential push, potentially giving it its most sympathetic and concrete example yet.

## Questline Thread 2 — from Finding 2 (Pisces earns nothing at all from the sixth instance of the war-over-credited pattern)

**1st-order cause (= Finding 2's 4th-order effect):** Pisces is the only confirmed instance of the "war over-credited as originator" pattern where the erasure compounds with a total absence of formal recognition — unlike Libra's version, which at least converts into retained power.

**New 2nd-order effect:** the player, plausibly working with the senior Operator figure already proposed in Pisces' Full Extrapolation, uncovers documentation proving the Tolerance Pact's true sixty-year age, directly contradicting the popular "the war made Pisces necessary" story.

**New 3rd-order effect:** this opens a fork: help a Pisces-aligned figure use the proof to finally demand some form of recognition or formal status from Libra — directly testing the deep dive's own established claim that formalizing Pisces would destroy its usefulness; respect that established logic instead, and help preserve the true history privately, as an act of internal dignity rather than external validation; or use the proof for an entirely different purpose, as leverage in some unrelated negotiation with Libra.

**New 4th-order effect:** pushing for formal recognition creates a genuinely uncertain, high-stakes outcome — it could catastrophically end the Tolerance Pact's usefulness to everyone who currently relies on it, or, handled carefully, could finally earn Pisces something real without dismantling it, an open question this specific questline could actually resolve one way or the other; preserving the truth privately gives specific characters real, quiet dignity without risking the district's function, but leaves the underlying injustice formally unaddressed forever; using it as unrelated leverage treats the district's own historical grievance as a bargaining chip, which risks reading as narratively cheap unless handled with real care.

---

## Worth Your Attention

Thread 2 carries the higher stakes of the two — it's the only path anywhere in this document that could genuinely test, through actual play, whether the deep dive's own foundational claim about Pisces (that formal recognition would destroy it) is actually true, rather than simply describing the tension and leaving it permanently unresolved.
