# CONCORDIA — ALTITUDE, ATMOSPHERE AND ACCLIMATIZATION

**Created 2026-09-04**, at developer direction, out of the 37-city climate pass.
**Subject: what it costs a HUMAN to live in Concordia — and what it costs a robot, which is nothing.**

> ## ⭐ THE SHORT VERSION
> **Concordia sits at 3,233 m, but the polar atmosphere is thinner than at mid-latitudes: the air here is
> physiologically equivalent to ~3,800 m.** ⛔ **And humans do not acclimatize to it.** They live
> permanently, mildly oxygen-starved — *arterial saturation 91–94% against a sea-level normal of 95–100%* —
> **for as long as they stay.**
>
> ***Concordia's ~309,000 humans are not adapted to their city. They are enduring it.*** **Its ~326,000
> robots do not notice.**

---

# 1 · THE PHYSICAL FACTS

| | |
|---|--:|
| **Geographic elevation** | **3,233 m** |
| **Mean barometric pressure** *(winter)* | **478 mmHg** |
| ⭐ **Physiological equivalent altitude** | **~3,800 m** |
| ⭐ **Resulting arterial oxygen saturation** | **91–94%** *(sea-level normal: 95–100%)* |
| Interior temperature, real precedent | **+22 ± 2 °C**, against **−58 ± 9 °C** outside |
| Mean wind | **2.8 m/s** — the calmest city in the Federation |

**High latitude reduces local barometric pressure**, so a polar site breathes "higher" than its surveyed
elevation. **The ~570 m gap between the geographic and physiological figures is the entire subject of this
file.**

## ⛔⛔ THE CORRECTION — humans do NOT acclimatize here

> **This was assumed the other way round on 2026-09-04 and the assumption was wrong. Recorded so it is not
> re-assumed.**
>
> **The intuition — "long-term residents adapt, like Andean highland populations" — does not hold at polar
> altitude.** ***Published research on 10–12 month Concordia winter-overs found a LACK of acclimatization
> to chronic hypoxia.*** **Residents do not become adapted. They remain impaired, indefinitely.**

**Why this matters more than the version that was wrong:** an adapted population is a solved problem and
generates no story. **A population that never adapts is a permanent, low-grade, universally shared civic
condition** — and one that its robot neighbours are structurally incapable of sharing.

## What chronic mild hypoxia actually does

- **Disrupted sleep** — documented alongside blood-pressure effects in the same winter-over studies
- **Reduced physical capacity** that never fully returns while in residence
- **Cognitive and mood effects** consistent with sustained low saturation
- ⚠ **No endpoint.** *You do not get used to it. You get used to being like this.*

# 2 · ARCHITECTURE — why Concordia is HEATED but NOT PRESSURIZED

**Developer framing, 2026-09-04:** *"the interior atmosphere would likely either A.) need to be regulated,
which is extremely resource-intensive, or B.) makes itself visible by way of various human NPCs having to
wear breathing apparatus."*

## Option A — pressurize the city

⛔ **Rejected, and not primarily on cost.**

**The real-world precedent is decisive: Concordia Station is held at +22 °C interior against −58 °C
outside — it is thoroughly HEATED — and it is not pressurized.** Its occupants breathe 3,800 m air indoors
and out.

> ### ⭐ Pressurizing would be actively harmful, not merely expensive
> **Sea-level interiors would leave the population unable to function outside them.** Every exit becomes a
> decompression. ***A pressurized Concordia manufactures humans who physically cannot leave the building***
> — in a city whose frontier district, **Sagittarius/The Frostlands**, is defined by people who go out onto
> the plateau.
>
> **So the absence of pressurization is a POLICY, not an omission.** *Comfort would make the population
> incapable.* **That is a civic decision with a real, ongoing human cost — which is worth far more as
> material than "they couldn't afford it."**

### ⚠ The narrow exceptions — where pressure IS maintained

**Not the city. Specific rooms, for specific medical reasons:**

- ⭐ **Maternity and neonatal care.** *High-altitude pregnancy measurably reduces birth weight and raises
  infant mortality.* **This is the least optional pressurized space in the city.**
- **Surgical and critical care** — where a patient cannot spare the reserve.
- ⭐⭐ **An arrival hall.** **A stepped-down facility where newly arrived humans spend days before entering
  the city proper.**
  > ***This makes arrival at Concordia a PROCESS rather than an event.*** **Robots walk straight in.
  > Humans check in.** *A location with built-in narrative function: everyone human who ever came here
  > passed through it, and remembers it.*

## Option B — the visible tell

⭐ **Confirmed and adopted — with its polarity settled by the research.**

**Because residents never fully adapt, breathing assistance is not purely a newcomer's marker.** The
readable gradient is:

| Who | What you see |
|---|---|
| **Newly arrived** | ⛔ **Acute distress.** Supplemental oxygen, obvious labor, the arrival-hall wristband or equivalent |
| **Weeks to months in** | Occasional assistance; visible effort on stairs and slopes; no longer acute |
| **Long-term resident** | **No apparatus most days — but permanently reduced capacity.** *They are not fine. They are used to not being fine* |
| **Elderly, ill, pregnant** | Assistance returns, and stays |
| ⭐ **Returning from a long trip** | **Briefly indistinguishable from a stranger** — and aware of it |

# 3 · ⭐⭐ THE THIRD-ORDER CONSEQUENCE — robots are the city's mobility

**Whatever partial tolerance a human does build is lost on leaving.** Come back after months away and you
re-enter as a newcomer.

> ***Altitude therefore taxes human MOBILITY, continuously and unavoidably.***
> **A human who travels constantly is never at their best. A human who stays is as well as Concordia
> permits.**

**Robots pay none of this.** So **every role built on frequent travel between Concordia and the lower
cities becomes robot work by physiology, not by policy** — couriers, traders, long-haul crew, negotiators,
anyone who must go and come back and go again.

### What that means after 250 years

- ⭐ **Concordia's humans are a SEDENTARY population by physiological necessity**, not by temperament.
- ⭐ **Concordia's robots are the city's entire practical connection to everywhere else.**
- **A human who insists on travelling anyway is making a real and visible sacrifice** — and everyone around
  them can see the cost written on them when they get back.

---

# 4 · THEMATIC WEIGHT

**This lands directly on the project's two north stars — robot consciousness, and robot/human love.**

> **A robot partner cannot experience what their human partner lives with every day**: the bad-morning
> headache, stopping halfway up the stairs, the week of recovery after a journey, the sleep that never
> quite works. ***And the robot can come and go freely while the human pays a physical price for every
> trip they take together.***

**No metaphor is required. It is daily, undramatic, and permanent** — which is a great deal more effective
than a dramatic incompatibility.

## For the player specifically

**The player is canonically a robot** *(build chosen at character creation; always a robot)*.

⭐ **The player never feels any of this — and sees it constantly.** **Robot-ness becomes legible through
watching humans manage something that does not touch you**, rather than through a stat or a dialogue
option. *The player's advantage here is not power. It is exemption, and exemption is a stranger feeling.*

# 5 · CANON CONSISTENCY

⭐ **This is not a new mechanic. It is an existing canon axis at a survivable dose.**

`Robot_Physiology_and_Cultural_Practices.md` already establishes that **Kunlun and Dome Fuji forbid human
presence entirely** because *"the combination of extreme altitude and extreme cold at these specific sites
would kill a human; robots, with no respiratory system and different thermal-regulation physiology, are
unaffected."*

| City | Elevation | Humans |
|---|--:|---|
| **Kunlun** | 4,087 m | ⛔ **Forbidden — lethal** |
| **Dome Fuji** | 3,810 m | ⛔ **Forbidden — lethal** |
| **Vostok** | 3,488 m | present |
| ⭐ **Concordia** | **3,233 m** | ✅ **~309,000 — survivable, never comfortable** |

***The human survivability line sits between roughly 3,500 and 3,800 m, and Concordia sits just below it.***
**The capital of what remains of the Federation is the highest place its human population can actually
live** — which is a fact worth using.

# ⚠ OPEN QUESTIONS

1. **Does chronic hypoxia shape Concordian human demographics?** *High-altitude reproduction is genuinely
   harder.* **Concordia is ~49% human / ~51% robot** — whether altitude is part of why is a developer call,
   not something to assume.
2. **Is there generational adaptation after ~250 years?** *Real highland populations adapt over far longer,
   and the polar research specifically found no acclimatization within a year.* **Whether Tepenia's
   Concordians have begun to change is a ruling, not a derivation.**
3. **What does the arrival hall look like, and who works there?** *Plausibly a robot-staffed facility —
   the one place robots routinely tend humans through something robots cannot experience.*
4. **Do the district cultures differ on this?** Sagittarius sends people outdoors; does it select for the
   least-affected, and what does that do to who ends up there?
