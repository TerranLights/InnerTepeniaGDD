# The National Institutes — Medicine, Trauma, and Robotic Care

> ## CANON. Developer ruling, 2026-09-01. `[CGRM 2026-09-01 · Path 6 · developer ruling]`

**Period: Second Interwar (2564–2812)** — the Federation as a functioning country.

**Three institutes. Together they are the entire source of trained medical and care personnel in Tepenia.**

---

# Why these exist as a national system rather than as three local schools

**Two established facts collide, and the institutes are what falls out of the collision:**

1. **Care delivery cannot be centralized.** Tepenian cities sit 1,000+ km apart across lethal terrain. Nobody
   treats a Vostok patient from Concordia. **Every city staffs its own clinicians, always.**
2. **Tepenia cannot evacuate a patient off-continent — so it cannot import a trained clinician either.**
   There is no foreign residency to send someone to and no outside profession to recruit from.

> ## Therefore: **every medic, nurse, field responder and robot-care technician in the Federation was made in
> Tepenia — and, in practice, made at one of these three institutes.**

**That makes them load-bearing national infrastructure in a way no other provider role is.** Davis can be
supplemented by fishing fleets; Sinheung's fabrication can be duplicated. **A physician cannot be improvised,
and cannot be imported.**

---

# 1. The Esperanza Institute of Medicine

**City:** Esperanza (Hope Bay, Antarctic Peninsula) · **Tradition:** settled medicine

> **⭐ Flagship: the Department of Pediatrics** — a substantial section of the Institute, and the department
> the Institute is known by.

**Why Esperanza.** The city's §15 already carries `Education / childcare / family services: ~25%` — *"an
unusually large formal sector, reflecting the founding charter"* — together with the **civic birth registry**.
Esperanza is where Tepenians are born and where the record of being born is kept.

**A city built around birth becomes a city built around medicine.** The chain runs obstetrics → neonatal →
pediatrics → family practice → the whole settled-medicine curriculum. **Pediatrics is not one department among
many here; it is the seed the rest of the Institute grew around.**

**What it produces:** general practitioners, pediatricians, obstetricians and midwives, nurses, chronic-care
and geriatric specialists, public-health staff. **The ordinary medicine of ordinary life** — which in a nation
with nowhere to send its sick means the entire arc from delivery room to deathbed.

---

# 2. The Belgrano Institute of Medicine

**City:** Belgrano (Halley subnet) · **Tradition:** field medicine

> **⭐ Flagship: the Department of Emergency and Trauma Response** — a substantial section of the Institute,
> and the department the Institute is known by.

**Why Belgrano.** Its §15 leads with `Aviation / logistics: ~35% — the city's defining function, running the
Halley subnet's primary airbase`, backed by `Technical / mechanical maintenance: ~20%`.

**Medevac is an aviation function.** Whoever flies casualties off the highways, the shelf and the plateau
receives them first, stabilizes them first, and learns trauma before anyone else does. **The airbase is the
Institute's actual origin; the school is downstream of the flight line.**

**What it produces:** field medics, air medics, first-aid attendants, emergency physicians, trauma and
critical-care surgeons, rescue and recovery specialists — and **cold-injury medicine**, which in this setting
is not a subspecialty but a foundational discipline: frostbite, hypothermia, non-freezing cold injury,
crush and industrial trauma, and the medicine of long transport times.

**The two institutes are complements, not rivals.** Esperanza treats the life; Belgrano treats the emergency.
A Tepenian city needs graduates of both, and knows the difference.

---

# 3. The Sinheung Institute of Cybernetics and Robotic Care

**City:** Sinheung (Mirny subnet, Larsemann Hills) · **Tradition:** robotic care

> **⭐ Defining focus: the physical AND EMOTIONAL care of, and for, robots.** Both halves, deliberately —
> **this is not a repair school.**

**Why Sinheung.** Its §15 carries the corpus's highest single sector — `Industrial fabrication: 45%` — and it
**manufactures the Cradle chambers shipped to cities across the region: the apparatus at the heart of how
robots come to exist.**

**The city that builds the chambers robots are made in is the city that learned to look after what comes out
of them.** Three steps, and the last one is not mechanical.

> **⭐ The contrast is the point.** An institute of *care* sits inside Tepenia's most heavily industrial city —
> 45% fabrication, raw materials trucked in, things made at scale. **Sinheung builds bodies by the thousand and
> also teaches the tending of them one at a time.** That tension is the Institute's whole character.

**What it produces:** robot maintenance and repair technicians, frame and actuator specialists, sensor and
processor service engineers, coolant and siligel practitioners, chamber technicians — **and robotic
counselors, whose discipline is a robot's inner life rather than its hardware.**

**On the emotional half.** In a nation whose population is **~51% robot** (16,403,077 against 15,623,523
humans), the care of robot minds is not a curiosity or a courtesy. **It is the mental-health system of the
national majority**, and it is taught here.

---

# Consequences — canon, following from the above

1. **A national alumni network the setting otherwise lacks.** Every city holds people trained at Esperanza,
   Belgrano or Sinheung. **"Esperanza-trained" means something anywhere in the Federation** — a shared
   institutional identity that crosses subnet lines, in a country whose regions are otherwise held apart by
   distance.
2. **A quiet civic sorrow in the training cities.** All three export their graduates permanently, by design.
   **Esperanza raises them, teaches them, and loses them — continuously, and on purpose.**
3. **⚠ A strategic vulnerability with a decade-long fuse.** With every clinician originating in three cities,
   cutting those cities off **kills nobody immediately — it kills people six to ten years later**, when the
   graduates who would have replaced the dead never arrive. **Relevant well before the Long Night War, and
   worse afterward.**
