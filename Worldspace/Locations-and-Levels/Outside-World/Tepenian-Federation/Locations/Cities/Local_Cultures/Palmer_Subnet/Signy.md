---

**Subnet:** Palmer ("American") — peripheral, dashed Arcanet boundary
**Status:** Survived; fully operational *(upgraded 2026-07-03 from "Damaged" — the most isolated city in Tepenia, overlooked entirely by Upper Earth's targeting due to sheer remoteness. But the war still reached it indirectly: cut supply lines have left its robot population facing a real siligel shortage, even though the human population can sustain itself indefinitely on the Scotia Sea's marine resources. A strong candidate DLC 3 questline — the untouched city that still needs the player's help.)*
**Census Population:** 93,134 humans / 95,560 robots / 188,694 combined (Census I, rank 33rd, *corrected 2026-07-03, again 2026-07-05*); 53,928 humans / 79,827 robots / 133,755 combined (Census II, rank 30th, *corrected 2026-07-03*)
**Location:** Signy Island, South Orkney Islands, Scotia Sea (~60°43'S, 45°36'W)

**One-liner:** *A city with no road to anywhere, whose isolation was always the point — untouched by the war that devastated everything around it, yet still starving its own robots of the one thing isolation can't provide.*

*Signy is the only Palmer subnet city with a South African, rather than Argentine or Chilean, founding-wave presence — a direct consequence of its genuinely different position. Where most Peninsula cities drew their earliest arrivals through the short Drake Passage crossing from Argentina and Chile, Signy's South Orkney location sits much further east, closer to the South Atlantic route through Cape Town already established as Tepenia's Halley subnet shipping corridor. UK, the actual founding-operator nation (BAS heritage), sits at Significant tier — diluted like every Palmer subnet founding nation, but with a genuinely distinct secondary founding current running alongside it.*

---

## 2. Founding Story

The British Antarctic Survey had operated seasonally at Signy since 1947, running a long-term biological research program studying the South Orkney Islands' marine ecology and terrestrial biology. British exiles inherited that station and converted it to year-round habitation, carrying the biological research mission forward as both purpose and methodology — Signy's founding character was scientific from the very beginning, distinct from Rothera's operational character or Port Lockroy's historical one, each of the subnet's BAS-heritage cities having developed its own distinct institutional identity independently.

A South African founding wave arrived alongside the British population, likely via the South Atlantic route rather than the Drake Passage corridor most Peninsula cities used — the same geographic logic that made South Africa a founding presence at Halley subnet cities extended, in smaller form, to Signy's more easterly position.

The South Orkney Islands carried a layered pre-exile political history too: a brief 1976 Argentine occupation, an assertion of sovereignty under the Antarctic Treaty framework that predated the exile era entirely. The Falkland Treaty's broader framework resolved those tensions when the exile era began, but the history remained part of the island's memory.

---

## 3. Climate Character

Signy sits north of the Antarctic Circle in the Scotia Sea — technically sub-Antarctic rather than full Antarctic, with the mildest climate of any Palmer subnet city, but also the most wind-exposed: relentless Scotia Sea westerlies with no sheltering landmass to break them, frequent fog, and genuine rain rather than just snow. The Scotia Sea itself is one of the most biologically productive marine environments on Earth, where Atlantic, Pacific, and Southern Ocean water masses converge around dense Antarctic krill populations.

---

## 4. Seasonal Rhythms

No polar night or midnight sun at this latitude. Signy's real rhythm follows the South Orkney Islands' rich wildlife cycles — seal haul-outs, penguin colony seasons, seabird nesting — as much as any weather pattern, a genuinely different seasonal experience from the more barren southern Tepenian cities.

## PART II — CULTURAL IDENTITY

---

## 5. Post-Culture Identity

**Name/concept:** *Knowing You Are Alone, and Building Anyway*

Signy's civic identity is built entirely around absolute, practiced isolation — no highway, no road, no overland connection to anything, a significant sea voyage from the nearest other Tepenian city. Rather than a source of hardship alone, that isolation became the city's whole defining fact: a community that learned genuine self-sufficiency because there was never any other option, and that built something lasting anyway.

- **a. Founding tension** — The city inherited a scientific, BAS-based institutional character from the research program itself, diluted by later USA demographic Primary like every Palmer subnet city — but here it's compounded by a genuinely distinct South African founding-wave current running alongside the British one, a Notable-tier population arriving via a different route than any of its Peninsula neighbors.
- **b. Fault lines** — Between the scientific, research-oriented founding character (biological monitoring as both mission and methodology) and the practical, harder-edged reality of maintaining absolute self-sufficiency in genuine isolation.

---

## 6. Social Contract & Unwritten Rules

- **a.** Self-sufficiency is assumed, not aspired to — Signy has never had the option of relying on outside supply the way even remote inland cities can.
- **b. Hospitality character** — Warm but practiced, shaped by a community that knows exactly how far away help actually is.
- **c.** Paced by wildlife cycles and Scotia Sea weather windows rather than any Arcanet-linked subnet schedule.
- **d. Public/private divide** — The biological research program and its findings are shared civic knowledge; personal life happens quietly around and within the same small, isolated community.
- **e. Privacy as civic value** — Genuine, given the small population and total physical isolation from any comparison point.

---

## 7. Who This City Attracts

- **The type this city draws:** people who want genuine self-sufficiency and biological richness over subnet connectivity — scientists, and anyone comfortable with real remoteness.
- **The type this city repels:** anyone who needs regular contact with the rest of Tepenia — Signy's weak, unreliable Arcanet link has never supported that kind of life.

---

## 8. Language

- **a. Primary language(s):** English dominant (American Primary tier), carrying decades of accumulated scientific and institutional vocabulary from the station's own long-running research tradition, plus a distinct minority-language thread tied to the Notable-tier founding-wave population, unlike anywhere else in the Palmer subnet.
- **b. Linguistic character** — Practical and scientific, shaped by decades of biological research terminology woven into everyday civic language.
- **c. Slang / dialect markers** — Terminology tied to the wildlife research program — seal, penguin, and seabird monitoring vocabulary as genuine civic shorthand.
- **d. Language and integration** — Minimal pressure, consistent with a small, isolated community where newcomers are quickly and thoroughly known.

---

## 9. Architecture

Modest, practical, built around the inherited BAS biological research station — small in scale, consistent with Signy's tiny, isolated population and the genuine physical remoteness that has always limited how much could realistically be built here.

---

## 10. Cuisine

- **a. Human cuisine** — Genuinely shaped by the Scotia Sea's marine richness — fishing and maritime resources feature more centrally here than in almost any other Tepenian city, given the ecosystem's real productivity.
- **b. Social context of eating** — Intimate, given the tiny population — shared meals carry real weight in one of Tepenia's smallest, most isolated communities.
- **c. Robot cuisine / siligel culture** — Modest, scaled to the smallest population tier in the Palmer subnet.

---

## 11. Fashion

Practical, maritime-weather-oriented clothing, shaped by the Scotia Sea's relentless westerlies and frequent rain — genuinely distinct from the drier, colder southern Tepenian cities' cold-weather gear.

---

## 12. Music

Modest and personal, consistent with the city's tiny, isolated scale — whatever musical tradition exists here developed in genuine isolation from the rest of the Palmer subnet's cultural currents, given the weak Arcanet link.

---

## 13. Arts & Aesthetics

- **Visual arts:** Naturalist and documentary, reflecting the city's whole relationship to the South Orkney Islands' rich wildlife.
- **Literature:** Scientific record-keeping from the biological monitoring program, alongside oral tradition shaped by genuine isolation — stories that had to be self-sustaining because so little arrived from outside.
- **Crafts & material culture:** Practical, maritime-oriented, shaped by the same self-sufficiency that defines the rest of Signy's civic character.

---

## 14. Sensory Profile

- **Sound:** Scotia Sea gales, wildlife — seals, penguins, seabirds — a genuinely different soundscape from the ice-and-wind-dominated southern cities.
- **Smell:** Maritime, wet, biologically rich — a city whose air itself carries the Scotia Sea's productivity.
- **Feel:** Genuinely, absolutely isolated — Signy is further from any other Tepenian city than any other Palmer subnet location, and that distance is felt in daily life.
- **First impressions for visitors:** The Scotia Sea gale-force approach by sea first, then quickly the sheer biological richness — seals, penguins, seabirds in numbers most Tepenian cities never see.

## PART III — SOCIAL STRUCTURE

---

## 15. Division of Industry

- Biological / ecological research: 30% — the city's clearly defining scientific sector
- Marine / fishing resources: 30% — leveraging the Scotia Sea's genuine productivity
- Maritime trade: 20% — whatever reaches the South Orkney Islands
- Technical / maintenance: 15%
- Other: 5%

---

## 16. Human-Robot Relations

With robots slightly outnumbering humans (95,560 to 93,134 Census I) in one of Tepenia's smallest, most isolated cities, human-robot relations at Signy are shaped by genuine mutual dependence — a community this remote and this small doesn't have room for anything but close, trusted coordination.

---

## 17. Robot-Specific Culture

Robot culture at Signy is woven directly into the biological research program — robots holding real, valued roles in the wildlife monitoring and maritime resource work that defines the city's whole economy, in a community too small and isolated to support any separate robot-specific institution.

---

## 18. Religious / Philosophical Landscape

- No dominant formal religion; the closest shared value is a lived philosophy of self-sufficiency and endurance through genuine isolation rather than disaster alone.
- **St. Ernest (Shackleton) veneration** is plausibly concentrated here more than anywhere else in Tepenia — the Endurance expedition's historic open-boat journey passed within roughly 300km of the South Orkney Islands, giving Signy a geographic claim to Shackleton's memory no other Tepenian city can quite match.

---

## 19. Underground / Counterculture

Minimal to nonexistent — a tiny, genuinely isolated community doesn't have the population or connectivity to support anything resembling organized counterculture.

---

## 20. Private Life & Minority Communities

The Significant-tier nations (Germany, France, UK, Brazil) and the Notable-tier founding-wave South African community, alongside Argentina/Chile/Uruguay, maintain identity through private, personal practice — Signy's tiny scale means every national thread is a genuinely intimate, household-level presence.

## 22. Relationship to Upper Earth

Filtered through the station's long scientific tradition and Signy's own extreme isolation — a quiet relationship, given how little of Signy's story ever reached the rest of Tepenia in the first place.

## 23. Relationship to Other Cities

- **Palmer City (subnet hub):** The Arcanet link exists but is weak — hundreds of kilometers of open ocean signal, real and functional but the most vulnerable intra-subnet connection in Tepenia.
- **Rothera and Port Lockroy:** Fellow BAS-heritage Palmer subnet cities, sharing the same originating institutional lineage despite Signy's much greater physical isolation from either.
- **Every other Tepenian city:** Genuinely distant — Signy is closer to the South Atlantic than to any Tepenian settlement, a geographic fact that shapes every one of its external relationships.

---

## 24. Arcanet Culture

The weakest Arcanet link of any Palmer subnet city — information arrives late and unreliably, giving Signy a genuinely self-referential culture shaped by frequently being behind the rest of the subnet in news and cultural currents.
