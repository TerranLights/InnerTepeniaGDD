This file applies the Enneagram's three sub-classifications — Major Theme (Center of Intelligence), Hornevian Group, and Harmonic Group — to each city as a whole, the same framework the developer uses to design individual Doll characters, treating each city's established culture as a "personality" in its own right. Reasoning is grounded in specific, already-canonical details, not generic Enneagram trivia.

## Zhongshan — "The Quiet City"

### Major Theme: Instinctive (Gut)

The Enneagram's three Centers of Intelligence are Thinking/Head (types 5, 6, 7 — oriented around security, analysis, and planning), Feeling/Heart (types 2, 3, 4 — oriented around image, identity, and emotional connection), and Instinctive/Gut (types 8, 9, 1 — oriented around autonomy, presence, and *being* rather than analyzing or performing).

Zhongshan reads unmistakably as Gut-centered. Its defining trait, established directly in the developer's own vision session, is that the city's street layout is "chaotically organized at ground level... but a semi-gridlike order still underlies it" — the city doesn't *explain* its order or *justify* it through visible planning; it simply has one, and that order is felt rather than displayed. This is the same quality found in the city's established dumpling culture and "quiet, unhurried social rhythm": these aren't anxious, deliberated choices (which would push toward Thinking) or emotionally expressive ones (which would push toward Feeling) — they're just how the city *is*, day after day, without needing to account for itself.

The Prydz Bay fishing economy reinforces this further. Fishing as a genuine dietary backbone is a fundamentally physical, grounded, presence-oriented activity — sustenance drawn directly from the immediate environment, not mediated through complex planning or elaborate self-presentation. And the developer's own explicit reframing of the city's quiet — "whatever's happening (business, commerce, industry, leisure) carries clear purpose without needing noise or hurry to prove it" — is close to a direct description of healthy Gut-center energy: confidence in one's own presence and purpose that requires no external validation.

### Hornevian Group: Withdrawn

The three Hornevian (social-strategy) groups describe how a personality orients toward others: Withdrawn types (4, 5, 9) move away, valuing independence and self-sufficiency; Compliant types (1, 2, 6) move toward, oriented around duty and meeting others' expectations; Assertive types (3, 7, 8) move against, oriented around impact and taking up space.

Zhongshan is Withdrawn almost by definition. It is literally self-sufficient — its own Prydz Bay fishing feeds its own population, a closed loop that doesn't depend on drawing attention or resources from elsewhere the way Sinheung's raw-materials-trucked-in-from-Davis economy does. Its whole civic identity, "the Quiet City," is built around *not* needing external engagement to feel complete. The "chaos that is actually structure" pattern applies here too: a Withdrawn city doesn't need anyone else to see or validate its internal order for that order to be real and functioning.

### Harmonic Group: Competency

The three Harmonic groups describe how a personality handles conflict, stress, or day-to-day problems: Positive Outlook types (2, 7, 9) reframe difficulty in optimistic terms and minimize negativity; Competency types (1, 3, 5) handle problems by detaching from emotion and applying calm, rational, capable effort; Reactive types (4, 6, 8) respond to problems with visible emotional intensity and want that intensity acknowledged.

Zhongshan is Competency through and through. The entire "chaos that is actually structure" concept is, at its core, a description of a system that handles its own internal complexity calmly and effectively without anyone needing to step in or make a fuss about it. The quiet, unhurried social rhythm is not passive or checked-out — it's the confident, low-drama competence of a system that already knows how to function. There's no sign of Zhongshan reframing its situation with forced positivity, nor of it reacting with visible emotional intensity to anything; it simply, quietly, handles things.

## Sinheung (files/placeholder name: Sinheung) — Industrial Fabrication, Robot Creation, and National Pride

### Major Theme: Instinctive (Gut)

Sinheung, like Zhongshan, centers on Gut energy — but where Zhongshan's Gut energy is quiet and self-contained, Sinheung's is forceful and outward-facing. The city's entire post-rework identity is built around *making* things: heavy industrial fabrication, raw materials trucked in from Davis specifically to be transformed into finished goods, and — most significantly — Sinheung is one of the handful of Tepenian cities that manufactures the "fabrication-synthesis chambers" that physically create robots. This is about as literal an expression of Gut-center "effect on the world" as a city can have: Sinheung doesn't just produce things, it produces the very apparatus of creation itself.

Layered on top of this is the developer's own explicit observation that Sinheung carries "a deep, unusually sharp national pride/patriotism... a genuine outlier compared to most Tepenian cities' quieter post-national identity." National pride of this intensity is a Gut-center trait almost by definition — it's about assertion of identity and presence in the world, not the anxious calculation of Thinking or the image-consciousness of Feeling.

### Hornevian Group: Assertive

Sinheung is Assertive in a way that stands out clearly against its own regional neighbors. Its national pride isn't just present — the developer flagged it as a genuine *outlier*, meaning Sinheung actively distinguishes itself from Tepenia's general post-national quietness rather than blending into it. This is the Assertive pattern exactly: moving *against* the surrounding current, taking up space, wanting a visible, undeniable effect.

The city's literal industrial role reinforces this. Sinheung's fabrication-synthesis chambers get shipped nationwide, meaning Sinheung's own output — and by extension its own identity and craftsmanship — reaches into every other Tepenian city that receives one of its chambers. This is an assertive relationship to the rest of the country: not withdrawing into self-sufficiency like Zhongshan, and not accommodating others' needs the way a Compliant city would, but actively projecting its own capability and identity outward.

### Harmonic Group: Reactive

Where Zhongshan handles its own internal order calmly (Competency) and Shirayuki (below) reframes things warmly (Positive Outlook), Sinheung's defining trait — an unusually sharp, outlier-level national pride — suggests a city that responds with real emotional intensity when its identity or standing is challenged. Reactive types don't want their intensity smoothed over or reframed away; they want it acknowledged. A pride this pronounced, explicitly named as atypical for the whole country, reads as a city that would react strongly and visibly if that pride were ever questioned or diminished, rather than deflecting the moment with calm competence or an optimistic spin.

This also sits naturally alongside Sinheung's own eventual rename. *(Corrected 2026-07-13: "the city's Russian-derived name" understated this — "Soyuz" was never the founding population's own choice at all, just a placeholder carried over from the physical station's Russian namesake. The city is confirmed singularly Korean-founded via the Jeju-do partition described below, not a later demographic overtake of some other founder; its South Korean Primary-tier share (34.62%) reflects that founding directly, not a drift toward it. Updated 2026-07-14: the rename has since happened — the city is officially Sinheung, "newly rising/emerging.")* A city this invested in its own pride and identity was exactly the kind of place where finally receiving its real name landed as a genuinely significant, emotionally live event, not a quiet administrative update — consistent with how the name itself was received.

## Shirayuki — Cleanliness, Civic Art, and Amateur Culture

### Major Theme: Feeling (Heart)

Shirayuki is the one city of the three centered unmistakably on Feeling. Heart-center types (2, 3, 4) organize around image, identity, and emotional/aesthetic connection — and nearly everything established about Shirayuki fits this exactly. Its defining visual character is art *as* civic identity: "sculptures, murals, statues, and other constructed pieces populate every district," turning the city into "an outdoor gallery as much as a functioning one." Its fashion identity is explicitly about self-expression over function or status — Alternative/Alt-Rock/Alt-Grunge/Alt-Pop, homemade rather than polished, a direct rejection of runway/high-fashion conventions in favor of something more personal and felt.

Even Shirayuki's academic reputation runs on Feeling-center logic: all three Larsemann Hills cities have genuinely good schools, but Shirayuki's specifically draw students from across Tepenia "for the culture and soul of the place, not academic ranking alone." That's a description of a city whose appeal is fundamentally emotional and aesthetic, not a Thinking-center appeal to competence or credentials, nor a Gut-center appeal to raw capability.

### Hornevian Group: Compliant

Shirayuki's founding story is itself a Compliant story: it exists specifically because the Jeju-do Court diplomatically allocated this site to Japan, a deliberate act meant to serve the broader Federation's need to prevent China's regional demographic pull from defaulting into a third uncontested claim. Shirayuki was built, from the outset, to fulfill a role for the good of the whole — the textbook definition of Compliant, duty-oriented energy (move toward others, meet an obligation), as opposed to Zhongshan's self-sufficient Withdrawal or Sinheung's outward-pushing Assertiveness.

That same Compliant orientation shows up in Shirayuki's present-day civic character. Cleanliness here isn't a defensive or anxious trait — it's framed as "a matter of civic pride" maintained collectively, a genuine act of care for the shared space and for whoever visits it. And the city is explicitly, repeatedly described as a place where it's "genuinely easy to make friends" — an accommodating, welcoming orientation toward others that sits at the heart of Compliant energy.

### Harmonic Group: Positive Outlook

Shirayuki is by a wide margin the warmest of the three cities, and its Harmonic group reflects that directly. Positive Outlook types reframe circumstances in optimistic terms and lead with what's good rather than what's threatening or difficult — and Shirayuki's entire cultural identity runs on exactly this register. Its massive amateur art and music culture exists purely because people enjoy making things on their own time, not out of competitive necessity or emotional reactivity. Its ease of friendship is described as a simple, uncomplicated fact about the place, not something residents have to work hard at maintaining. Even its schools' draw — "the culture and soul of the place" — is a reputation built on warmth and inspiration rather than defensiveness or intensity.

Where Sinheung would likely respond to a challenge with visible emotional force (Reactive) and Zhongshan would likely just quietly handle it (Competency), Shirayuki's whole civic temperament suggests a city that meets difficulty by reframing it toward the positive — consistent with a place whose entire identity is built on being disarmingly, genuinely easy to like.

## The Jeju-do Partition and Amalgamation, Established 2026-07-07

**Founding event, extending what was already on record for Shirayuki alone (see its own section above) to cover all three cities:** Zhongshan, Sinheung, and Shirayuki were established as three separate, legally distinct cities via a peaceful diplomatic negotiation among China, Korea, and Japan at the **International Court of Diplomacy at Jeju-do** — the same real-world site already established elsewhere in Tepenian history as a recurring seat of major diplomatic and legal proceedings (see the 2318 robot-personhood ruling, cited elsewhere in the project as "the Jeju-do ruling"). The three cities' extreme physical proximity in the real Larsemann Hills made a single negotiated partition the natural solution to what could otherwise have been an uncontested Chinese demographic claim over the whole cluster — precisely the logic already on record for Shirayuki's own site allocation.

**What happens after founding — three stages, not yet fully dated:**
1. **Legal separation.** All three cities begin, and for a long stretch remain, three fully separate municipalities — distinct governments, distinct civic identities, the very identities each city's own City Vision Notes session and Enneagram read above describe.
2. **De facto amalgamation.** Sometime at or shortly before Amundsen Tower's completion (~2688, roughly the Second Interwar Period's own Midpoint, resolved 2026-08-05 — see `project_amundsen_tower_completion_date_resolution` memory), the three cities' physical growth — already inevitable given how tightly clustered they are — closes the remaining gaps between them, and they become one continuous, functionally single urban area in every practical sense, while remaining three separate cities in law.
3. **Full legal unification.** At some point between that de facto amalgamation and roughly a generation before the Long Night War (2812) — so, at the latest, somewhere in the ~2780s — the three cities formally, legally become one single city, in every sense: one government, one civic identity, full absolute unification.

This means a refugee's insistence on identifying by sub-district (Zhongshan vs. Sinheung vs. Shirayuki) isn't a symptom of an unfinished or interrupted unification process — it's simply the kind of durable neighborhood-level identity that persists for generations after formal unification in real cities, especially where (as here) each sub-district still carries a genuinely distinct national-heritage character. A person from unified Budapest still says "I'm from Buda"; a unified-city refugee here would still say the equivalent of "I'm from Sinheung" (under whatever its eventual proper Korean name turns out to be), and mean something real and specific by it, entirely compatible with having grown up as a citizen of one legally unified city the whole time.

## Regional Music Reputation — "Alternative Culture," Flagged 2026-07-07, Scope Deliberately Open

The Japanese city's own Megasheet established that its massive, genre-varied amateur music scene grows large enough, well before the Long Night War, that the whole Larsemann Hills region becomes nationally synonymous with "Alternative Culture" across Tepenia — a genuine, well-established pre-war cultural export, confirmed by the developer as needing to be solidly in place by the time of the war, not a post-war development.

**Deliberately not yet decided:** whether this reputation belongs to Shirayuki alone, with Zhongshan and the Korean city absorbed into the "Alternative Culture" label purely through geographic proximity and cluster identity despite each having its own quite different established civic personality (Zhongshan: quiet, self-sufficient, Withdrawn; the Korean city: industrial, nationalistic, Assertive) — or whether the reputation genuinely extends to and reflects something real about all three cities' own character. The developer's own framing: this needs Zhongshan and the Korean city's own Megasheets built out first, so the answer can be decided with those cities' full identities actually in view rather than guessed at now.
