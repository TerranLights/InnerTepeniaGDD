# Kunlun — Cultural Spec Sheet

---

**Subnet:** Mirny ("Australian") — theoretical connection only; nearest subnet city (Mirny itself) is 1,200km+ across the interior plateau
**Status:** Survived — extreme altitude, most marginal survival of any Tepenian city; **100% robot population**, one of only two such cities in Tepenia (with Dome Fuji)
**Census Population:** 0 humans / 123,449 robots (Census I) — figures established 2026-07-04, Kunlun's first-ever population data, via a deliberate small-percentage redistribution pooled from every Tepenian city except Concordia, entirely reclassified as Chinese regardless of each contributor's original nationality
**Location:** Dome A (Dome Argus), East Antarctic Plateau (~80°25'S, 77°07'E), ~4,093m elevation — the highest point on the Antarctic Ice Sheet, and the highest city in Tepenia

**One-liner:** *The Sinian Federation's only exclusively Sinian city, perched at the literal ceiling of human habitability, and — as of a recent, deliberate demographic resolution — a city whose entire population is now robot and entirely Chinese, in a way that sits in genuine, unresolved tension with its own established status as the holiest site of a religion that claims not to care about nationality at all.*

*This is the only single-nation composition table in this entire directory. Every other Tepenian city — even Zhongshan, Kunlun's fellow Sinian-founded city — has at least some multinational texture. Kunlun's exclusivity is the entire point: it is the one place in Tepenia where Sinian cultural continuity was never diluted, blended, or shared. That exclusivity became mechanically literal on 2026-07-04, when the population-resolution exercise reclassified every single contributor as Chinese, regardless of original nationality — a clean, single-nation outcome that matches the city's pre-existing identity almost too neatly (see Section 5 for the tension this creates with Kunlun's other established identity as a pilgrimage site).*

---

## 2. Founding Story

Kunlun was settled post-Falkland Treaty on Kunlun Station's infrastructure — the last major Antarctic station established before the first global war of 2083, built by CHINARE in 2009 at the highest point on the East Antarctic Ice Sheet. The founding population was Sinian (Chinese) exiles who understood, from the historical record alone, exactly what they were walking into: an altitude at or beyond the ceiling of sustained human habitation, with chronic mountain sickness as a permanent baseline rather than a manageable condition. They came anyway, drawn by the science (the exceptional atmospheric stability made Dome A one of the best astronomical sites on the planet), the isolation, and — plausibly — the specific symbolic weight of founding the one Tepenian city that would remain exclusively theirs.

What happened to that founding human population across the centuries since is one of the most significant open questions about Kunlun (see `Specs/Kunlun.md` Open Questions) — whether it survived, declined, or was replaced entirely is unresolved. What is now confirmed is the endpoint: as of 2026-07-04, Kunlun's population is 123,449, entirely robot, entirely Chinese by deliberate reclassification, drawn from a small percentage pooled from every other Tepenian city. Whether this population represents a continuation of the founding community by some means, a wholly new population that simply inherited the site, or the very Ice Cold Buddhism pilgrimage community Kunlun's own established lore already describes (see Section 18) is genuinely open — and worth resolving before this becomes load-bearing for actual DLC content.

---

## 3. Climate Character

The most extreme climate of any Tepenian city — mean annual temperature approximately −58°C, colder than both Vostok (−55°C) and Dome Fuji (−54°C) by virtue of the highest elevation of any settlement in Tepenia. Winds are among the calmest in Antarctica (Dome A is a wind minimum at the summit position), meaning the defining hazard here is pure cold and altitude rather than windchill. The driest location in Tepenia (~15–20mm annual precipitation water equivalent). Satellite measurements suggest surface temperatures approaching −93°C are possible — Kunlun may hold or contest Vostok's absolute cold record, a question still open in Tepenian lore.

For a 100% robot population, the cold itself is not a survival problem — it may instead be closer to the point, given the same "Ice Cold Buddhism" logic already established at Dome Fuji (extreme cold as the closest living approximation to superconducting conditions). If Dome Fuji approaches those conditions, Kunlun — colder, higher, calmer — may exceed them.

---

## 4. Seasonal Rhythms

Polar night runs roughly April 17 to August 25 (~130 days); midnight sun roughly October 16 to February 25 (~132 days) — both longer than Dome Fuji's, owing to Kunlun's more southerly latitude. The exceptional atmospheric stability means that during polar night, when it is −68°C and perfectly calm, the night sky at Kunlun is one of the most extraordinary visual experiences in Tepenia — a fact that likely carries real weight for a religious population organized around reverence for extreme conditions, and a very literal asset for the observatory's astronomical program (see Section 15).

## PART II — CULTURAL IDENTITY

---

## 5. Post-Culture Identity

**Name/concept:** *Purity at the Ceiling of the World*

Kunlun's identity rests on two claims that, until recently, sat comfortably together and now sit in genuine tension. First: Kunlun is the Sinian Federation's only *exclusively* Sinian city — the sole custodian of undiluted Sinian exile cultural continuity anywhere in Tepenia, a distinction Zhongshan (broadly multinational despite its own Sinian founding) cannot share. Second: Kunlun is — per its own established lore — arguably the holiest site in all of Tepenia for "Ice Cold Buddhism," a religion that (per Dome Fuji's own resolution) explicitly does not organize itself around nationality. The 2026-07-04 population resolution mechanically locked in the *first* identity (100% Chinese) without addressing whether it's compatible with the *second* (a faith indifferent to national origin). This sheet does not resolve that tension — it's flagged in `Specs/Kunlun.md` as a genuinely open question, and it should probably stay open until there's a deliberate narrative reason to settle it one way or the other.

- **a. Founding tension** — Not founding-wave-vs-majority (there is no dilution story here — Kunlun's whole point is that it was never diluted). The real tension is between Kunlun's *two* established identities: the ethnically exclusive Sinian outpost, and the theologically universalist pilgrimage site. A city can plausibly be both a place with a purely Chinese population *and* a place devotees of any origin would want to reach — but if reaching it as a devotee results in every arrival being reclassified as Chinese on arrival (mirroring what the population-resolution event mechanically did), that itself might be a deliberate, meaningful piece of the religion's own theology, worth developing rather than treating as an oversight.
- **b. Fault lines** — Between the astronomical/scientific mission (still nominally central per Section 15) and the religious character now confirmed as central to the population — the same fault line established at Dome Fuji, but sharper here, since Kunlun is described as the *holier* of the two sites.

---

## 6. Social Contract & Unwritten Rules

- **a.** Endurance as the foundational value — a population that has chosen, generation after generation (or arrival after arrival), to remain at the single most physiologically hostile permanently-occupied point in Tepenia.
- **b. Hospitality character** — Ceremonial and exacting; reaching Kunlun (1,200km+ across the interior plateau from the nearest coastal city) is a genuine trial, and whatever hospitality exists here likely honors that effort while offering no concessions to comfort.
- **c.** Governed by the extremity of the environment rather than any conventional civic clock — likely even more austere and deliberate than Dome Fuji's own unhurried pace, given Kunlun's colder, higher, more extreme conditions.
- **d. Public/private divide** — With a single-nation population and (per the unresolved tension above) possibly a single shared religious identity as well, Kunlun may have the least internal public/private distinction of any Tepenian city — there may simply be less here that needs to be kept separate.
- **e. Privacy as civic value** — Likely low-friction for the same reason Dome Fuji's is — no internal demographic majority/minority dynamic to create the usual privacy tensions.

---

## 7. Who This City Attracts

- **The type this city draws:** the most committed Sinian exiles seeking undiluted cultural continuity, and/or (per the unresolved religious question) the most committed Ice Cold Buddhism devotees seeking the faith's holiest site — Kunlun may be unique in Tepenia for having two entirely distinct, non-overlapping pulls that happen to converge on the same physical location.
- **The type this city repels:** anyone without either a deep Sinian cultural commitment or genuine religious conviction — and, physiologically, anyone without extraordinary altitude tolerance or a robot chassis, given conditions at or beyond the ceiling of human habitation.

---

## 8. Language

- **a. Primary language(s):** Mandarin Chinese, presumably the sole working language given the population's total national uniformity — a genuine outlier among Tepenian cities, most of which are at least somewhat multinational.
- **b. Linguistic character** — Likely more linguistically stable and undiluted than almost anywhere else in Tepenia, precisely because there was never another national language present to blend with or erode against.
- **c. Slang / dialect markers** — Plausibly the most "pure" or historically continuous Chinese dialect in Tepenia, given the total absence of the multinational pressure that reshapes language everywhere else — though five centuries of total isolation would still produce real linguistic drift of its own.
- **d. Language and integration** — A near-moot category given the single-nation population — there is no dominant-vs-minority integration dynamic here the way there is almost everywhere else in Tepenia.

---

## 9. Architecture

Built on Kunlun Station's original CHINARE research infrastructure, the youngest major pre-exile station in Tepenia (established 2009, barely 555 years old by the Falkland Treaty). Likely the most purely functional, scientifically-driven architecture in Tepenia, shaped entirely by the physiological demands of the highest, coldest inhabited point on the continent — pressurization, altitude mitigation, extreme thermal insulation — with whatever Sinian aesthetic sensibility the founding population brought layered onto a fundamentally survivalist structure.

---

## 10. Cuisine

- **a. Human cuisine** — Moot in the present tense, given zero current human residents; whatever Sinian culinary tradition the founding population maintained under extreme-altitude conditions (which would itself have shaped diet significantly — appetite suppression is a known effect of chronic altitude exposure) is now historical rather than living.
- **b. Social context of eating** — For the current robot population, likely governed by whichever of Kunlun's two identities dominates day to day: if predominantly a Sinian cultural-continuity story, siligel practice may carry deliberate cultural markers distinct from anywhere else in Tepenia; if predominantly devotional, it may mirror Dome Fuji's ascetic, cold-as-discipline consumption pattern instead.
- **c. Robot cuisine / siligel culture** — Given Kunlun's status as the *coldest* Tepenian city, if Dome Fuji's devotional siligel-in-extreme-cold practice has any basis, Kunlun would be the more extreme, more theologically significant version of the same practice.

---

## 11. Fashion

Practical extreme-cold and extreme-altitude gear, likely retaining more visible Sinian aesthetic character than Dome Fuji's converged, nationality-erasing look — since Kunlun's population is uniformly Chinese rather than a blend that dissolved national markers, there's no obvious pressure pushing fashion away from a recognizably Sinian sensibility.

---

## 12. Music

Likely retains genuine Sinian musical tradition, distinct from Dome Fuji's more ambiguous, converged soundscape — though tempered by the same reverence for stillness and calm that the exceptional atmospheric conditions (Dome A is a wind minimum) would encourage regardless of cultural background.

---

## 13. Arts & Aesthetics

- **Visual arts:** Likely a genuine continuity of Sinian visual tradition, undiluted by the multinational blending seen almost everywhere else in Tepenia — quite possibly the most "purely" Chinese visual culture left in the Federation.
- **Literature:** The observatory's five centuries of astronomical data (see Section 29) and the still-unresolved question of what has been found from Dome A's summit are Kunlun's own likely foundational narrative material — a genuinely unique local literary/scientific tradition unlike any other city's.
- **Crafts & material culture:** Shaped by both extreme survival necessity and Sinian cultural continuity — a material culture with more of a distinct national character than Dome Fuji's own converged, faith-first aesthetic.

---

## 14. Sensory Profile

- **Sound:** Among the calmest wind conditions anywhere on the continent — even quieter than Dome Fuji's own plateau calm, given Dome A's status as an outright wind minimum.
- **Smell:** The driest air in Tepenia — cold, thin, mineral, essentially devoid of moisture.
- **Feel:** Extreme in every register simultaneously — the highest, coldest, driest, calmest point in Tepenia, a place that asks more of anyone present than perhaps anywhere else in the Federation.
- **First impressions for visitors:** The sky, almost certainly — Dome A's exceptional atmospheric stability makes for a night sky unlike anywhere else in Tepenia, especially during the −68°C stillness of polar night.

## 15. Division of Industry

- **Astronomy: 60%** *(established 2026-07-04 — astronomy is Kunlun's overwhelmingly dominant industry)* — Dome A's exceptional atmospheric stability makes this potentially the best ground-based observatory site in Tepenia; the sheer scale of this share reflects astronomy as Kunlun's defining civic purpose, not merely one program among several
- Ice core science: 15% — Dome A holds some of the oldest ice on Earth
- Religious practice / pilgrimage infrastructure *(if the Ice Cold Buddhism identity holds)*: 15%
- Altitude-legacy medical infrastructure / facility maintenance: 10%

*(As with Dome Fuji, whether the religious identity coexists with or is secondary to the astronomical mission is unresolved — but the astronomy share itself is settled: this is a science city first, whatever else it also is.)*

## 16. Human-Robot Relations

Not applicable in the present tense — Kunlun has no human population to relate to, one of only two Tepenian cities (with Dome Fuji) in this position. Whatever the founding human community's relationship to early robot presence looked like is unknown pending resolution of the founding population's fate.

## 17. Robot-Specific Culture

Effectively the entirety of Kunlun's current culture, given the population. Distinct from Dome Fuji's own robot culture in one crucial respect: Kunlun's is organized around a *single, undiluted national heritage* rather than a converged, nationality-erasing devotional identity. If Dome Fuji shows what robot culture looks like when built entirely around shared faith, Kunlun may show what it looks like when built around shared, unmixed cultural inheritance instead — two genuinely different models sitting at the same religion's two holiest sites.

---

## 18. Religious / Philosophical Landscape

- **Ice Cold Buddhism** (placeholder name) — per Kunlun's own established lore, this is the faith's *holiest* site in Tepenia, more significant even than Dome Fuji: the highest point on the ice sheet, the coldest sustained environment (contesting Vostok's record), the calmest winds in Antarctica, exceptional atmospheric purity — the closest any place on Earth comes to the superconducting conditions the faith venerates.
- **The unresolved tension:** unlike Dome Fuji, where the 2026-07-04 population resolution directly confirmed and mechanically realized the pilgrimage lore (nationally blended devotees, exactly as anticipated), Kunlun's resolution reclassified its population as 100% Chinese instead — not obviously compatible with a multinational pilgrimage tradition. Whether Kunlun's population are pilgrims who happen to register as Chinese, or whether Kunlun's religious significance and its population are two separate facts that simply coexist at the same coordinates, is genuinely open. This sheet treats it as open rather than resolving it.
- Whatever secular Sinian cultural-continuity philosophy exists alongside (or instead of) the religious identity is a live open question in its own right.

---

## 19. Underground / Counterculture

If Kunlun's population really is entirely devotional pilgrims, a counterculture might consist of those more invested in Sinian cultural preservation for its own sake than in the faith — or vice versa, if the population's actual center of gravity is cultural rather than religious. Given the unresolved nature of Kunlun's dual identity, this section is itself a marker of what needs settling before Kunlun's culture can be written with full confidence.

## 20. Private Life & Minority Communities

Uniquely, Kunlun may have *no* minority communities to speak of — a single-nation population is, by definition, the one case in Tepenia where this entire template category may simply not apply. If it applies at all, it would only be through some hypothetical, currently undocumented non-Chinese presence — nothing in current canon suggests one exists.

## 21. Political Character

Likely minimal engagement with Federation-wide politics, similar to Dome Fuji, but for a different reason — where Dome Fuji's likely quietism comes from religious withdrawal, Kunlun's may come from sheer distance and the totalizing demands of survival at the ceiling of human (or robot) habitability. Whatever political voice Kunlun has would likely run through its status as the sole representative of pure Sinian cultural continuity — a symbolic weight that may matter more to the broader Sinian Federation than to Kunlun's own day-to-day governance.

---

## 22. Relationship to Upper Earth

Likely filtered through Sinian Federation exile history specifically, distinct from the generic Tepenian relationship — and, if the religious identity holds, possibly also filtered through the same transcendence-over-grievance framing established at Dome Fuji. Both threads would need to be reconciled or kept deliberately separate.

## 23. Relationship to Other Cities

- **Dome Fuji:** The other primary Ice Cold Buddhism site, and the clearest point of comparison and contrast — Kunlun holier by the faith's own reckoning, but ethnically exclusive where Dome Fuji is genuinely blended; the theological relationship between the two sites (rival, complementary, hierarchical) is TBD, flagged identically in both cities' sheets.
- **Zhongshan:** The other Sinian-founded Tepenian city, and Kunlun's most direct point of contrast within the Sinian Federation itself — Zhongshan broadly multinational despite its own Sinian founding heritage, Kunlun exclusively, purely Chinese. The nature of the relationship between these two very different Sinian communities (rivalry, solidarity, indifference) is an open question flagged in `Specs/Zhongshan.md`.
- **Mirny:** The nominal subnet hub, though the 1,200km+ interior plateau distance makes the relationship almost entirely theoretical.
- **Concordia:** Aware of Kunlun's existence, nothing more — the connection is as theoretical as Vostok's own, per `Specs/Kunlun.md`.

---

## 24. Arcanet Culture

**Established canon (2026-07-04):** like Dome Fuji, Kunlun is an extreme-altitude location, and at extreme altitude there is basically no Arcanet *connectivity* — the network essentially doesn't reach Kunlun in any functional sense. This baseline is compounded here by extreme physical isolation as well (Kunlun's link, if it existed at all, would route through Mirny across 1,200km+ of interior plateau) — two independent reasons pointing to the same outcome: Kunlun is, for practical purposes, off the Arcanet entirely, even more thoroughly than Dome Fuji given the added distance.

## 28. The Visitor-to-Resident Transition

Becoming a resident of Kunlun may mean not just surviving the journey and committing to whatever the city's central identity turns out to be, but — per the current population's total national uniformity — also somehow becoming part of its exclusively Chinese character, a genuinely strange requirement worth deliberate narrative attention if this is ever developed further.
