## 2. Founding Story

Kunlun was settled post-Falkland Treaty on Kunlun Station's infrastructure — the last major Antarctic station established before the first global war of 2083, built by CHINARE in 2009 at the highest point on the East Antarctic Ice Sheet. The founding population was Chinese exiles who understood, from the historical record alone, exactly what they were walking into: an altitude at or beyond the ceiling of sustained human habitation, with chronic mountain sickness as a permanent baseline rather than a manageable condition. They came anyway, drawn by the science — the exceptional atmospheric stability made Dome A one of the best astronomical sites on the planet — and by the isolation.

**Re-resolved 2026-07-06:** what happened next is no longer a story of ethnic exclusivity. As Kunlun's real function — premier astronomy site, secondary comms relay exploiting the plateau's thin, stable atmosphere to stay in contact with orbital structures — took hold over generations, the city drew scientists, astronomers, and communications specialists from across every Tepenian nation with genuine space/astronomy/comms heritage, not just China's. Kunlun's population (123,449, entirely robot) is the settled outcome of that specialist draw — see `Specs/Kunlun.md` for the full breakdown. What happened to the original founding human population specifically remains an open question (see `Specs/Kunlun.md` Open Questions), but the city's present character is defined by function and professional lineage, not by which nation got there first.

## 3. Climate Character

The most extreme climate of any Tepenian city — mean annual temperature approximately −58°C, colder than both Vostok (−55°C) and Dome Fuji (−54°C) by virtue of the highest elevation of any settlement in Tepenia. Winds are among the calmest in Antarctica (Dome A is a wind minimum at the summit position), meaning the defining hazard here is pure cold and altitude rather than windchill. The driest location in Tepenia (~15–20mm annual precipitation water equivalent). Satellite measurements suggest surface temperatures approaching −93°C are possible — Kunlun may hold or contest Vostok's absolute cold record, a question still open in Tepenian lore.

For a 100% robot population, the cold itself is not a survival problem — it may instead be closer to the point, given the same "Ice Cold Buddhism" logic already established at Dome Fuji (extreme cold as the closest living approximation to superconducting conditions). If Dome Fuji approaches those conditions, Kunlun — colder, higher, calmer — may exceed them.

---

## 4. Seasonal Rhythms

Polar night runs roughly April 17 to August 25 (~130 days); midnight sun roughly October 16 to February 25 (~132 days) — both longer than Dome Fuji's, owing to Kunlun's more southerly latitude. The exceptional atmospheric stability means that during polar night, when it is −68°C and perfectly calm, the night sky at Kunlun is one of the most extraordinary visual experiences in Tepenia — a fact that likely carries real weight for a religious population organized around reverence for extreme conditions, and a very literal asset for the observatory's astronomical program (see Section 15).

## PART II — CULTURAL IDENTITY

---

## 5. Post-Culture Identity

**Name/concept:** *Purpose at the Ceiling of the World*

**Re-resolved 2026-07-06**, replacing the earlier "Purity" framing: Kunlun's identity is no longer built on ethnic exclusivity. It's built on **function** — a specialist astronomy-and-comms outpost whose population converged here from across Tepenia's own space-heritage nations, drawn by the same thing regardless of origin: Dome A is one of the best observational and deep-space-communications sites on the planet, and reaching it took real, deliberate professional and personal commitment. This actually resolves a genuine tension the old framing carried: Kunlun's established status as (per its own lore) the holiest site of "Ice Cold Buddhism" — a faith that, per Dome Fuji's own resolution, explicitly doesn't organize around nationality — now sits comfortably alongside a multinational population instead of awkwardly against a single-nation one. The scientifically self-selected and the religiously devoted converge on the same coordinates for compatible reasons: both are drawn by what this specific, extreme place *is*, not by whose flag it flies.

- **a. Founding tension** — Not founding-nation-vs-majority in the usual sense (Kunlun was never "diluted" because it was never meant to represent one nation to begin with). The real tension, if there is one, is between the astronomical/scientific mission and the religious-devotional character — both genuinely present, both drawing people here for their own reasons, neither one clearly primary.
- **b. Fault lines** — Between the scientifically self-selected population (astronomers, comms specialists, their descendants) and the religiously devoted (Ice Cold Buddhism pilgrims who stayed) — two genuinely different reasons to be at Kunlun that happen to produce the same demographic outcome, worth developing as a real internal distinction rather than treating the two groups as identical.

---

## 6. Social Contract & Unwritten Rules

- **a.** Endurance as the foundational value — a population that has chosen, generation after generation (or arrival after arrival), to remain at the single most physiologically hostile permanently-occupied point in Tepenia.
- **b. Hospitality character** — Ceremonial and exacting; reaching Kunlun (1,200km+ across the interior plateau from the nearest coastal city) is a genuine trial, and whatever hospitality exists here likely honors that effort while offering no concessions to comfort.
- **c. No civic clock at all, by design — vision session, 2026-07-06.** Nobody in Kunlun thinks of what they do as a "job." From the population's own perspective, the astronomy and comms work isn't labor performed in exchange for a living — it's simply what their life *is*, an expression of the same curiosity and interest that defines the city's whole character. Because the sky and the signal never stop, and because the population's collective work genuinely runs continuously in shifts around the clock, Kunlun has no meaningful "off hours" as a city — someone is always mid-observation, mid-analysis, mid-transmission, at any hour of the polar day or night.
- **d. Public/private divide** — **Re-resolved 2026-07-06:** with the population's shared identity now organized around function (astronomy/comms) and, for some, devotion, rather than a single ethnicity, the public/private divide is more comparable to other specialist Tepenian communities — a working culture built around a shared professional or religious purpose, not the near-total absence of internal distinction a single-nation population might have implied.
- **e. Privacy as civic value** — Likely low-friction, similar to Dome Fuji's — a population this small and this purpose-driven doesn't generate much of the usual demographic majority/minority privacy tension, even though it's genuinely multinational.
- **f. A city that never closes — vision session, 2026-07-06.** The direct consequence of (c): bars, music halls, restaurants, and every other leisure establishment in Kunlun operate 24 hours a day, since at any given moment some fraction of the population is coming off shift or about to start one. There's no shared "evening" the way most cities have one — leisure time is scattered continuously across the clock rather than concentrated after a workday, because there effectively is no citywide workday to end.

## 7. Who This City Attracts

- **The type this city draws:** astronomers, communications specialists, and their descendants, drawn from across Tepenia's own space-heritage nations — and, per the religious question, Ice Cold Buddhism devotees seeking the faith's holiest site. **Re-resolved 2026-07-06:** these are two genuinely compatible pulls now, not a contradiction — both are drawn by what Kunlun specifically *is* (the best observational/comms site on the continent, the closest approximation of the faith's venerated conditions), regardless of national origin.
- **The type this city repels:** anyone without a genuine professional (astronomy/comms) or religious reason to be here — and, physiologically, anyone without extraordinary altitude tolerance or a robot body, given conditions at or beyond the ceiling of human habitation.

## 8. Language

- **b. Linguistic character** — A working, practical, technically-inflected register — the shared vocabulary of astronomy and communications work likely does more to unify daily speech here than any one national language does.

- **d. Language and integration** — Genuinely multinational integration dynamics apply here now, unlike the earlier single-nation framing — closer in spirit to a specialist research station's working-language culture than to a typical Tepenian city's founding-nation-vs-majority story.

## 9. Architecture

**Vision session, 2026-07-06:** Built on Kunlun Station's original CHINARE research infrastructure, the youngest major pre-exile station in Tepenia (established 2009, barely 555 years old by the Falkland Treaty). The skyline is dominated by a single unmistakable landmark: a **massive communications antenna**, towering over every other structure in the city, flanked by a field of satellite dishes and space-monitoring arrays reaching skyward from the plateau. It's the first thing visible from any approach, and the closest thing Kunlun has to a civic monument — not built to be one, but functioning as one anyway, since it's the literal, physical expression of what this city is for.

Every other building follows the same logic in miniature: thermally sealed structures whose interiors are given over almost entirely to scientific hardware — rooms upon rooms of instruments, sensor arrays, and processing equipment, most of it too temperature-sensitive to survive outside for even a few minutes. Holographic displays are everywhere inside these spaces, rendering incoming data — spectra, signal traces, orbital telemetry — in real time, so that walking through Kunlun's interior corridors means walking past a continuous, ambient wash of live scientific visualization. The architecture reads less like a settlement with some labs in it, and more like one enormous distributed instrument that people happen to live inside of.

## 14. Sensory Profile

- **Sound:** Among the calmest wind conditions anywhere on the continent — even quieter than Dome Fuji's own plateau calm, given Dome A's status as an outright wind minimum.
- **Smell:** The driest air in Tepenia — cold, thin, mineral, essentially devoid of moisture.
- **Feel:** Extreme in every register simultaneously — the highest, coldest, driest, calmest point in Tepenia, a place that asks more of anyone present than perhaps anywhere else in the Federation.
- **First impressions for visitors:** The sky, almost certainly — Dome A's exceptional atmospheric stability makes for a night sky unlike anywhere else in Tepenia, especially during the −68°C stillness of polar night.
