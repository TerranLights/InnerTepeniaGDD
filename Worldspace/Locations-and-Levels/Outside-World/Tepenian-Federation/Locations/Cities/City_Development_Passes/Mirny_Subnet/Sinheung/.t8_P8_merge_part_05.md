### Proposed wording for the amendment piece (PROPOSED; appended beside the pass, never an edit to it)

**F.0 (new).** Structure from A; B and C adopted it in Round 3. The robot clause is conditional on
{{REF:Q:module_korean}} and {{REF:Q:robots_people}}.

> *Everyone here speaks Hawaiian (later Tepenian), the country's dominant language, and can be assumed to follow
> Korean, this city's one supporting-tier language, in which business, social talk and general dealings can be done.
> This holds for the robot majority as for the humans: a robot's day-to-day language is the dominant one, and she
> can be assumed to follow Korean as the city's people do; whether the Language Module itself delivers Korean is not
> determined. Where Korean is used rather than the dominant language, and by whom, is a Band 5 distributional
> question deferred by ruling (DR-4). The shares behind the tier are origin shares, not speaker counts, so nothing
> here says how many people speak either language. Switching between the two is licensed and not yet described; the
> slot is open.*

**F.1 (replacement for L396-399).** Merged from A's, B's and C's proposals, narrowed as decided in section 1.

> *The dominant language here, as everywhere in the country, is Hawaiian (later Tepenian) (DR-100 (1)). It is not the
> origin language of any row on the city's composition table, so no origin language dominates. One origin language
> is live: Korean. Korea is the largest row at 33.00%, and the next language group, German, is at 10.06%, 22.94
> points behind and below the 12-point line (DR-100 (3)); Korean is therefore this city's only supporting-tier
> language, and no other origin language holds a tier. The frame is the Second Interwar, which spans both Acts and is
> mostly Act 2, reached as a gradient. In its Act 2 bulk, origin is ancestry, not identity, and Korean's standing is
> that of a working language (PROVISIONAL). On this city's own figures, following Korean is not a mark of descent: at
> most 16.08% of residents can follow it by human Korean descent, and robots have no genetic descent. Inside the city
> it marks nobody as local, because it is assumed of everyone; whether it marks a person as from here, seen from
> anywhere else, is not derived here. The frame's early, Act 1 part is not derived here.*

**F.2 (replacement for L401-413).** Merged from A's, B's and C's proposals, narrowed as decided in section 1.

> *What survives from origin depends on the origin. Nothing here characterizes a people's nature. Korean is a living
> language here, so a Korean word in the city's dominant-language talk is a borrowing from, or a switch into, a
> language the city follows, not residue. That this borrowing leans toward Korean is plausible and unmeasured; its
> grounds are that Korea is the Primary row (33.00%) and that Korean is the one origin language the rule keeps
> living here (Korea as the founding stock: per the spec and the rule file's worked example; Founding Register line
> not read). From the other origin languages, what survives in the city's shared settings is method-vocabulary: a
> word persists where it names a process or a thing that arrived with the people and for which neither Hawaiian
> (later Tepenian) nor Korean supplied the word. It persists as technical vocabulary, not as an ethnic flag, and a
> speaker using one is no more making a claim about ancestry than a machinist using a tool's original name. Whether
> the other origin languages are still spoken at home is not stated by any source and is left open. Which languages
> contributed is set by the processes their speakers carried, not by size. On 8a's preservation line, the languages
> of the rows grouped as Baltic/Balkan and Southeast Asian contribute their own process-words (provisional on 8a
> A.3's own re-derivation). For the works, the ice work and the instruments, no contributing language is sited here
> yet, and no word is assigned to the works by any group's national reputation. A word of Japanese or English origin
> is not credited to this city's own rows without a check, because the national language is recorded as having an
> English–Japanese core (DR-85; its relation to Hawaiian is not ruled).*
