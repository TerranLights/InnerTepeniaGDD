## The Lover's question

*Is this place alive, and could anyone love it?* — the Lover **faculty** (Moore & Gillette, Panel D), asked in the voice of the Lover **position** cast above. Step 7's closing test (*"structurally yes, and distinctively so; as a lived life, not yet"*, `07` L98) and the early verdict at `P6` L265 are inputs, not the answer.

> *"It's alive. Not the way a warm place is — it's alive in what it does with its light and its wind and its two kinds of people. The sun comes up every day of the year, even in June, low in the north and gone again in two hours, and nobody here ever has to live through a sky that doesn't come back. In January the sun doesn't set at all, and the city goes to rest anyway at an hour no sky keeps — robots and people stopping together for different reasons — and then one evening the sun goes down for the first time in weeks and there's no dark after it, and that's almost funny. There's a household on every street where a robot remembers every person who has lived in it. There's a city that would rather carry nine places' voices than ask them for anything.*
>
> *Could I love it? I could love being trusted here. Nobody reads the wind for you; nobody tells you what to do; nobody puts you out in the cold for being wrong. They read it for strangers at the edge of the road, and they leave it to you. If you want to be left alone with your own judgment, and with one other person, this is a place that will let you.*
>
> *But I can't hear anyone say they love it. I don't know what anyone sings while the storm keeps them in for nine days, or what they eat together when one of them doesn't eat, or what they call the three winds, or what the children laugh at, or what anybody wants. What I love so far is the shape of the place — its light, its stop, its coat, its trust. I haven't met the life inside the shape. Ask me again when somebody here has been allowed to talk."*

**The answer, recorded.** **Alive: yes — in its conditions and its two bodies.** **Lovable: yes, by someone who wants to be trusted with her own reading and who loves light more than warmth — and by the pair the place was founded on, a household that included a robot (`P2` P2-2).** **Loved by anyone in it: not yet knowable.** No speech is admitted (R-67), so no resident can be heard saying she loves living here, and the lived middle — music, food beyond the pantry, play, courtship, belief, speech, want — is still reasoned nulls (F-18; R-161). This is the thinness flag answered in the Lover's register: the place is not shown dead, but **the pass reads as competent and joyless** (Panel D, the Impotent Lover). The fault is the pass's silence, not a finding of the place's flatness.
**What the Lover would defend if asked to trade it away:** the first sunset with no dark after it (`P6` CAND-OBS-1, a candidate) and the stranger held at the road's edge by a person's call (PF-5). Both are candidates or derivations; neither is a fact yet.
