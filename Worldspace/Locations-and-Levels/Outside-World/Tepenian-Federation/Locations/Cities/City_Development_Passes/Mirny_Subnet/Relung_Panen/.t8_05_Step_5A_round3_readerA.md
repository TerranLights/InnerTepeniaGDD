- **The finding is the same in all three readings:** energy is the one cost the site cannot pay from its own gifts. The lit food, the warm rooms and the robots' charge all stand on one bought line from the continental grid, with standby generation. - **The named leg is the same in all three:** spec L260–261, the absence. The wordings differ:
  - B: "the site gives no energy of its own"
  - C: "the city takes no energy from its own ground"
  - mine: "the city holds no energy of its own … with only backup generation in the city"

All three count geothermal, wind, a local fuel, self-sufficiency, or a city-owned plant as the main supply. **One difference, and I withdraw my side of it:** I listed "the lit rooms' heat warming the homes as an established system" as a contradiction of the named leg. C says the racks' shed heat would *not* contradict it, and C is right. Reused rack heat is the bought supply's heat, not energy from the site. It would contradict Phase 7 P7-16 ("the decision to join them falls nowhere on the lines"), a different finding, not the named leg. **I also narrow my wording to B's and C's,** the absence of any site energy. A city-owned plant as the ordinary supply would contradict leg 5 (the `DEC-601` ruling) as much as leg 1. The test I now hold: does any line give the basin, the lakes, the ice, the wind or the sun an energy supply of the city's own, or call the city self-powered or off-grid? Standby generation and reused rack heat do not count.
