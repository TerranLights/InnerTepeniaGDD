# Cuisine

## The general condition first

This city runs **two complete, parallel food systems** for two populations of nearly equal size — Census I is
50.60% robot, Census II 47.59%, the sign inverted. Human food is grown locally: `16` §18 classes the city a
**GROWER** with a food term of 100%, on the Larsemann Hills ice-free oasis. The robot half eats siligel.

Neither system is a variant of the other, and there is no overlap at all.

## And the constraint that shapes everything social

`Siligel_Composition_Research.md` §4, stated as plainly as canon states anything:

> ***"A robot and a human cannot share a meal, ever, in either direction"*** — and not as squeamishness or
> custom, but as **cardiac arrest.** Fluoride chelates calcium; a robot has metal bones and no target for it.
> *"Siligel is not poisonous and nutritious; it is nutritious BECAUSE OF what makes it poisonous."*

**A city that is half and half faces that constraint at every table it sets.** A wholly robot city never meets
it. A city with a small robot minority meets it rarely. **Here it is the ordinary condition of hosting
anyone.**

### ⭐ Which explains a canon fact that had no explanation

Phase 7 took from canon that **"hospitality is ceremony rather than personal warmth."** That was recorded as a
civic disposition, of a piece with the non-prying norm.

> ## Ceremony is what hospitality becomes when the meal is impossible.

**You cannot welcome someone across the species line by feeding them**, and in a city split down the middle
that is most of the welcoming there is. So the form had to become something other than a shared table:
sequence, formality, a thing done correctly and in order, which both parties can perform in the same room
without either eating what would kill the other. **The reserve is not the cause of the ceremony. The chemistry
is** — and the reserve makes it comfortable.

## Class A as local cuisine — flagged as candidate, not written as canon

`Siligel_Composition_Research.md` §5 item 5 invites exactly this and rules nothing:

> *"**Class A is a MEAL** — bulk, daily, locally sourced. **Meals vary. This one should vary a great deal**,
> and is a legitimate local-texture hook for all 37 cities. **Class B is MEDICINE** — it should be
> near-identical everywhere, and the variation should be in **access**, not in recipe."*

⚠ **The class system is developer-raised but unruled**, and the file says outright: *"do not cite the chemistry
as settled."* What is binding is the form — **flan-set, holding a cut edge, spooned rather than squeezed, dark
gray-green-blue-gray.** That much is a texture and a color this city's tables actually have.

⏸️ **A local Class A tradition is the legitimate hook and it is left open here**, pending the ruling. What
this pass records instead is the condition any such tradition would have to sit inside: **it is eaten beside
people who cannot taste it, by half the room, every day.**

### ⚠ And one consequence this city is unusually exposed to

`Siligel_Composition_Research.md` §3c, if the classes are adopted: *"a robot can be **well-fed and dying**"* —
Class B deprivation strands capacity permanently, **"takes months, and looks like nothing,"** and *"would not
show up in a hunger statistic."*

> **A failure that is invisible, cumulative, and feels fine lands hardest in a city whose civic law is that
> nobody asks how you are.** Phase 7 found the sanction here is that the ceremony continues and nothing behind
> it does. **This is the deficiency that shape of politeness is least equipped to notice.**

⚠ **Contingent on the class ruling.** Recorded as a consequence to check, not asserted.

# Music

**Canon supplies no music for this city, and this pass does not invent one.** The corpus sites musical
character elsewhere; nothing names Zhongshan.

**The general condition, which is real and is all that can honestly be said:** Tepenia has **a shared night** —
robots recharge and humans sleep at the same hours, so *"the polar night falls on a population that is all
resting, together."* Phase 4 established that here the loudest hour is that sleeping hour, the katabatic
maximum peaking at midnight, and that robots perceive vibration across a wider range than humans structurally
do. **So the general musical fact is that this is an indoor culture with a shared quiet hour and a majority
that hears the building as well as the room.**

⛔ **That is a condition, not a practice, and the difference is the whole of `00b`'s warning.** Naming a
signature musical form here would be exactly the 2026-08-30 failure — reaching for the vivid because the
general answer is thin. ⏸️ **Left open for the read-last culture material.**

# Arts, craft and material culture

**Canon gives the general answer directly**, in §15 via `16` §18:

> *"precision manufacturing, with **the craft ethic applying to industrial output as much as to art**."*

**This city does not distinguish the two categories.** That is the material-culture finding, and it is stated
rather than derived: 25% of the distinctive tier is precision manufacturing, it is FREE rather than mandated,
and the standard applied to a fabricated component is the standard applied to a made object. **There is no
separate "fine" register here** — the same hands, the same tolerances, the same judgment.

⭐ It pairs exactly with Phase 6's standard of worth. **A city whose measure is that nothing was ever
interrupted would hold continuity of standard across categories too** — the work is judged the same way
wherever it appears.

# Dress and appearance

> Process B: **start from function.** *What would someone doing this place's actual daily work need to wear,
> and what does that need becoming custom look like after two centuries?*

## The general condition

Robots wear clothing **nearly always, functionally and fashionably** — coats, layering, and *"robots would
also wear winter gloves. There's no reason why they wouldn't."* Cold here is **preparation, not a gate**
(`M-151`), and this city never approaches the −40 °C threshold anyway (Phase 6). **So dress here is an
ordinary cold-climate wardrobe, worn by both populations, and not a survival apparatus.**

## The one garment this city has an opinion about

`Robot_Physiology_and_Cultural_Practices.md` names the trade-off and calls it a gradient:

> *"**Heavier gloves cost fine motor control and tactile feedback; thin high-gauge gloves keep precision and
> cost warmth.** This is a real, usable constraint on what kind of outdoor work a given climate permits."*

**This city's two signature trades sit at opposite ends of that single trade-off.** Precision manufacturing
(25%, year-round, indoors, tolerances) demands the thin glove. The harbor sector (15%, and the CONSTRUCTED
rebuild inside it, November to March, outdoors, in the wind) demands the heavy one. **The same workforce does
both, in the same year.**

> ## So the custom that two centuries produced is not a glove. It is the changeover.
> **Knowing which pair, having both, and switching without thinking about it** — and doing it seasonally, as
> the year turns from the window to the workshop.

## Build — and canon's rule returns the same answer

Build is *"statistically weighted"* by local economic character: hard-labor locales trend thicker and bigger,
agility-and-precision locales trend slender, **and *"locales without a strong lean toward either produce a
wide variety of body types with no dominant pattern."***

**This city has both leans, strongly.** So by canon's own rule it produces **wide variety with no dominant
pattern** — not because it lacks a character, but because it has two opposite ones. ⚠ And canon is explicit
that build *"is a statistical consequence of local economic character, not an identity marker in itself."*

# Play, humor and leisure

## The general condition

Phase 7 sized it: **43.4% of working life here is free** after both the civic baseline and the national
mandate — and canon insists this is the point rather than a statistic. *"On Upper Earth a robot's leisure was
afforded to them by their loving romantic partners… In Tepenia that same leisure is a **fact of life**."*
**Leisure is the material proof that leaving worked.** Half this city's population holds those hours for the
first reason anyone held them.

And it is taken **together**: the shared night means both populations stop at once.

## ⭐ The drinking culture is canon, and it names this city

`Robot_Physiology_and_Cultural_Practices.md`, on glitch-coolant:

> *"**Bohemian/cosmopolitan cities (e.g. Janbogo, Zhongshan, Shirayuki)** support a wide **variety** of
> glitch-coolant types — different formulations, different characters… **Variety and refinement over raw
> intensity.**"*

**This is a direct, named canon fact about this city, not a derivation.** The alternative tier — fewer
choices, stronger effect — is explicitly elsewhere.

### And it fits the civic law with unusual precision

> ## A drinking culture built on variety and refinement is organized around the DRINK. A culture built on potency is organized around the DRINKER.

**In a city whose defining civic law is that nobody asks about your past, connoisseurship is the ideal social
technology: it is conversation that can run all evening and never become personal.** You can discuss a
formulation, a character, a comparison, indefinitely, with someone whose history you are not permitted to ask
about — and part warmly, having genuinely enjoyed it, still knowing nothing.

⚠ **Derived, and the direction matters:** canon assigns the tier; this pass observes why that tier and this
civic law suit each other. **Not a claim that one caused the other.**

**Robot coffee** sits in the same category — canon confirms robots consume things *"for the experience it
produces, not purely for temperature management."* **Smoking is canon too**, and deliberately ambiguous: some
robots find it genuinely pleasurable, some do it for the gesture, *"no universal answer is imposed."* ⛔ That
ambiguity is not this pass's to close.

# Language and speech markers

## The general condition, and it is a mechanism rather than a manner

`Robot_Physiology_and_Cultural_Practices.md`, on the Language Module:

> *"Robot vernacular language tracks **the dominant local culture and the community who had her built** — robot,
> human, or both… a robot who comes online and develops where the dominant local culture and the residents who
> built her are Chinese-speaking speaks Chinese."*

⚠ **Corrected against `Robot_Universals` Ch. 20 while opening Phase 9's canon.** The Language Module sets a
robot's **capacity** for language at fabrication — *"it does **not** set the content of what she goes on to
speak. That content is filled in entirely by whichever community and culture actually surround her — the same
condition that would shape a human child's own native language."* **Acquisition, not installation.**

## ⭐ Which supplies the mechanism behind Phase 7's counterculture

Phase 2 found **18 nations summing to exactly 100%**: one at 35.83%, a 26.13-point gap to second, then
**twelve nations inside a 2.36-point band.** Phase 7 found canon's own counterculture — the smaller heritage
communities who *"feel slowly absorbed rather than protected by the non-prying norm."*

> ## The absorption is not social pressure, and it is not fabrication either. It is ordinary language acquisition, with the one thing that would counteract it forbidden.

**A robot here grows into the language of whoever surrounds her, exactly as a human child does — and in a city
of 35.83% against twelve communities inside a 2.36-point band, what surrounds nearly everyone is the same
thing.** ⛔ **And initiating a build does not protect it**, because the content was never set at fabrication;
the child still grows up surrounded by the majority.

**In an ordinary minority-language community the counterweight is family and community talk — telling the next
generation where they come from.** ⭐ ***That is precisely the conversation this city's civic law forbids.***
**Nobody has to intend anything: the norm removes the one mechanism that sustains a minority vernacular.**
Phase 7 identified who was being absorbed and why it was invisible. **This is the how, and it is the same how
that operates in the real world — which is why it works.**

## What marks someone as from here

The template asks, and the civic law makes it a real puzzle: **in a city where you may not ask, the marker has
to be something observable without asking.**

⚠ **Derived:** it is the changeover. **Someone from here has both pairs of gloves and switches without
deliberating** — at the season, at the door, between the shop and the quay. It is not a badge and nobody reads
it as one; it is simply what two centuries of holding both trades looks like from outside.

# Tools

`Weapons_and_Tools_Philosophy.md` is scoped to exactly this pass's era and is emphatic: **almost nothing in the
taxonomy is a weapon.** In the Second Interwar these are tools for surviving, building, extracting, and
**caring for another person, human or robot.**

Three categories are this city's ordinary equipment. **Structural ordnance** — *"glacier management, controlled
structural venting, the work that keeps a domed or ice-adjacent settlement standing"* — is the harbor rebuild
and Phase 3's finding that this city fights its own pavement. **Bladed tools of craft and provision** are the
precision sector's. And **thermal and drain tools** are coolant-tending, which canon calls *"the single most
intimate category in the entire taxonomy… an act of trust — a caretaker relationship between two people."*

> ⭐ **In a city where you do not ask someone how they are, the most intimate thing available is tending their
> coolant.** *Care here is done with the hands rather than the voice* — which is the same shape as hospitality
> being ceremony rather than warmth, arriving from a different direction.

# Differentiation axis

**Hospitality without a shared table.**

**Two populations of nearly equal size that physically cannot eat together, in a city whose welcome therefore
had to become a ceremony instead of a meal — and the same doubling runs through everything it makes: two
gloves, two trades pulling builds in opposite directions, two food chains, and a default language that quietly
outcompetes twelve others.**

*One-sentence test.* Delete every other city's name: *"This place is split almost exactly in half between two
populations that cannot share a meal, so its hospitality is a ceremony rather than a table."* **Survives, names
nobody, and is not portable** — a wholly robot city never faces it, and a city with a small robot minority
faces it rarely.

Checked against `04`: the axis is **the impossibility of the shared meal**, which is a physical fact of this
composition rather than a disposition. Phases 6 and 7 named the reserve; this phase finds the chemistry
underneath one of its most visible expressions.
