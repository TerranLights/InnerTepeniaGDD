# 5a · PEERS — mandatory here, because this is the rare clustered case

> **The core move:** *write a relation as a mechanism, not a rivalry. The useful question is never who dislikes
> whom — it is **what does each side refuse to develop, and who supplies it instead?***

## The geometry, measured rather than asserted

`Ports.md` §5.6c, from a 2026-09-05 developer ruling, gives distances from the head of Nella Fjord:

| | Distance | Side |
|---|--:|---|
| **Sinheung** | **0.71 km** | east shore |
| ⭐ **THIS CITY** | **1.16 km** | **west shore** |
| **Shirayuki** | ~8 km | not on Broknes at all — a different landform |

> ## **PRIMARY DOMINION OF THE SHARED PORT WENT TO THE OTHER SHORE BY 450 METERS.**
> ***The water runs between the two claimants, and each can see the other's shore from its own.***

**This is the sharpest thing in this city's entire relational profile, and it is not a rivalry.** `Ports.md`
is explicit that the port is **CONSTRUCTED tier** — *"built and rebuilt at shared cost, season after
season"* — with a window of roughly **four months (Nov–Mar)**, on a site whose real-world counterpart has
*no designated anchorage or barge landing at all*. **Neither city refuses to develop a harbor. The fjord
simply has one head, and somebody had to hold the queue.**

*"export their graduates permanently, by design."*

> ## **THE FEDERATION'S REQUIREMENT OF THIS CITY IS A CONTINUOUS, PERMANENT EXPORT OF ITS OWN PEOPLE.**
> **Not cargo — `16` §18 is explicit that the contribution is *"PEOPLE, not cargo,"*** and `02_Spine.md` §2.3
> already resolved the G3/G5 conflict on exactly that basis: *the junction is geographic, not economic.*

**So the disagreement is this:** the city's own sense of itself, per the rank-2 authorial vision, is a place
where *"things generally have purpose"* — busy, legible, purposeful. **What the parent actually needs from it

**departure at three scales — the mandated career, the orbital migration, and the weather that will not stay.**

# 5d · THE EDGE AND THE CROSSING

## Who arrives, by what route, and what they meet first

| Route | Character |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Hwy 22 — the transcontinental spine** | **This city is its EASTERN TERMINUS.** `City_Cross_Subnet_Relationships.md`: the Hwy 1 → Hwy 22 thread is *"the single most structurally important corridor in the whole network,"* the one overland link three subnets share — running from the Peninsula through **the South Pole itself** to this doorstep. ✅ **Hitchhiking-valid** *(developer ruling, 2026-09-10 — see below)* |
| **Hwy 110 — the coastal route** | **Western terminus here**, running to Davis, Mirny, Casey and Concordia. ✅ **Hitchhiking-valid** |
| **Hwy 4 — the Mawson corridor** | Ends at the same tri-junction. ✅ **Hitchhiking-valid** |
| **The Tri-Cities Airport** | Shared three ways; one of the three routes that kept Dome Fuji supplied |
| **The port** | Nov–Mar only; shared; primary dominion across the water |

> ### ⭐ WHAT AN ARRIVAL ACTUALLY MEETS FIRST — and it belongs to nobody
> **The tri-junction is a shared point where three highways end.** `Highways.md` L262 names it as the
> *"Zhongshan / Sinheung / Shirayuki"* junction — **a single physical location none of the three cities is.**
> ⭐ ***So the first Tepenian ground a road traveler touches in this cluster is jointly held, and the city
> proper is something you reach afterward.***

**And hitchhiking is node-based, never roadside** — a 2026-08-31 developer ruling: *"nobody stands on the side
of the road holding up a sign,"* because in Antarctic conditions the roadside version *"is not survivable."*
**You wait inside, in the warm, and you get a ride by socializing with people who stopped for their own
reasons.**

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ ALL THREE ROADS ENDING HERE ARE HITCHHIKING-VALID — **developer ruling, 2026-09-10**
> ⚠ **This corrects an earlier reading of this phase.** *`Highways.md`'s own "Hitchhiking-Valid Highways" list
> names Hwy 7, Hwy 4, Hwy 110, Hwy 2 and a segment of Hwy 1 — **Hwy 22 is absent from it**. The developer
> ruling extends the list; the file has not been edited (see the Step 5 docket).*

> ## **SO EVERY OVERLAND ARRIVAL AT THIS CITY IS A SOCIAL ARRIVAL.**
> **Nobody reaches this place by road without having sat in a warm room and talked to somebody who happened to
> be going this way.** *There is no anonymous way in on wheels.*

### ⭐⭐ And the node rule makes the junction load-bearing, rather than merely shared

**`Highways.md`'s own rule:** *"a route is hitchhikeable to the extent that it has stopping places with enough
passing traffic to socialize with,"* and **"a route with few or no nodes is functionally near-impossible to
hitchhike."**

**Hwy 22's interior is the longest, emptiest stretch in the country** — Byrd, then the South Pole, then bare
junctions, then here. ⭐ **So if that route is hitchhikeable, the node doing the work at this end is the
tri-junction itself.**

> ### ***THE WARM ROOM AT THE END OF THE LONGEST ROAD IN TEPENIA SITS ON GROUND THAT BELONGS TO NONE OF THE THREE CITIES.***
> ⭐ **Which sharpens the finding above rather than repeating it: the first thing a transcontinental traveler
> touches here is not merely jointly-held ground — it is a jointly-held *room*, and a conversation with
> whoever else is waiting in it.** *A person arriving from the Peninsula, having crossed the Pole, completes
> that journey by being offered a lift by a stranger at a junction three cities share and none of them is.*

## ⭐⭐ WHAT CONVERTS A VISITOR INTO A MEMBER — a mechanism, not a ceremony

> *Prefer a mechanism over a ceremony — derived from the local economy, with no announcement and often no
> awareness that it has happened.*

**Phase 4 established that the ordinary night is metered, that warmed charging space is the universal nightly
condition for eleven months of the year, and that the currency is denominated in grid capacity.**

> ## **YOU BECOME A RESIDENT HERE WHEN YOU HAVE A WARMED PLACE OF YOUR OWN ON THE METER.**
> **Not a ceremony, not a registration — an account.** *Nobody announces it, nobody witnesses it, and the
> moment it happens is a billing detail.* ⭐ **A visitor is someone whose nights are on somebody else's meter.**
