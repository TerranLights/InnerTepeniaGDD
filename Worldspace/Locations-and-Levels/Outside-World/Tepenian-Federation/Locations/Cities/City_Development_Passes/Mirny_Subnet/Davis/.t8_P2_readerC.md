## 7. Limits (what this piece cannot prove)

- Every real-world figure is a comparable, not a specification. The divergence operator applies, and Davis has total separation, robots, no dominant origin and a national contact language, none of which the comparables share.
- Two sources are weaker than the rest:
  - The Kaifeng sources disagree on dates (1800, 1810, mid-19th century, the 1860s), and one fetched page attributes the decline to war and intermarriage rather than to isolation.
  - The Henrich and Kline-Boyd population-size thesis is contested (Collard et al.; Read 2006). It is used only as one factor in a yield, never alone.
- The crossover multiplier (about 0.606) is an approximate reconstruction. It undoes the Kazakhstan shrink and the Manchuria refill by stated rules, and it ignores rounding.
- The per-row participation shares assume persons equal adults, and that no carriers come from outside Davis. Both assumptions make the shares *lower* bounds.
- This piece cannot say what any row's practices are, which territory any successor unit took, whether robots carry origin practices, how many 2564 robots remain, or whether any later arrival happened.
- Lines outside my ranges were not read. In particular, I did not read the Register header, the census Section A notes beyond L33, SPEC L82-148 and L159-260, or 03_Research.md (cited by the pass for the crew-scale caveat).
- One-location law: no other city was opened. Lines naming other cities are marked "(another location: not used)", and no claim survives only through another city's name.
