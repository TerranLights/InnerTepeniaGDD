## A. The headline function — in one sentence, then written away from

**The headline function.** Mirny is the Mirny subnet's hub and its quarrying-and-materials works, and the near-exclusive source of the raw material that is fabricated into robot-synthesis chambers elsewhere (`CON` C-3: "subnet hub; quarrying; processing construction materials; manufacturing infrastructure-maintenance machinery; the near-exclusive raw-material source for robot-synthesis chambers") [IND via the contract's admitted spans; `SPN` G3 supplement adds that what Mirny is for on its own terms is silent, "an artifact of the input contract", [CORR]].

**The test: would this reasonably be someone's entire day, every day, forever? No — four grounds, none of them a comparison:**

1. **The baseline tier is, by construction, not the headline function.** `16` L127–129 computes baseline from Mirny's own humans, residents and difficulty only (humans × (120.7·f·D + 85.6)/1000 + residents × (64·D + 113.2)/1000, with D = 1.25, `16` row). Mirny's row reads `| Mirny | Mirny | 1.25 | 1,018,480 | 41.1% | 11.8% | 47.1% | C |` [IND]. **So 41.1 % of Mirny's weighted workforce is work the residents' own bodies and number require, whatever the place is for** (own arithmetic, §D: formula 418,565 against the row's 418,595, rounding). `09` L139–140: "baseline work is not unwanted work" and "Baseline means necessary, never disliked."
2. **The headline function lives inside the other 58.9 %, and the files give no split.** Distinctive = workforce − baseline (`16` L131); the spec gives Mirny no sector-size table (`DS4` records only the row). The share of the distinctive tier that is stone, relay, harbor or ring is **not derivable** (R-B1). The 11.8 % is the national mandate, and `SPN` §3 G5 records that "its link to the stone is not stated" [CORR]. *Whether 11.8 % is 11.8 or 23.6 of something is the parked developer question; I do not resolve it.* What I can say: the row's three percentages sum to 100.0 (41.1 + 11.8 + 47.1), so as the row states them they are shares of one base, the weighted workforce (robots + 0.5 × humans, `16` L125).
3. **A continuous function is staffed across lives, not by a life.** The stone and the relay cannot pause in `SPN` ("continuous stone, no pause"; "a pause at the hub is a pause for every node, so maintenance is done live or deferred") [CORR]. Robots "do NOT work 100% of the time. Not remotely." (`RP` L331) and overwork is "a HAZARD WITH A MECHANISM, not merely a hardship" (`RP` L344). A function that cannot pause, held by bodies that must, **forces a relief arrangement that no admitted file describes** (R-B13; Phase 7) [D].
4. **The free tier is not leisure.** `09` L126–127 defines the elective residue as people "able to work in jobs they want to": it is *chosen work*, and the city's own distinctive character work lives in it. Counting 47.1 % as what residents do apart from the headline would be the failure mode this phase names, and would also mistake work for downtime (Snag 8; P4-B15).

**Written away from it.** What follows begins from what a resident does *between* the stone and the relay (the stop, the crossing, the household's two energy services), *despite* them (the wind, the lit weeks) and *entirely apart* from them (care, feeding, leisure, the ageing of one body in a house with another that does not age).

### B.1 ROUTINES

**P4-B1 · The one shared stop is held without a sky for part of the year** *[IND + ADM + CORR + D]*
- *Canon:* "Robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons." (`RP` L336). The robot reason is physical: "they require downtime, most prominently OVERNIGHT RECHARGING" (`RP` L334). The human reason is the ordinary one; no file states it.
- *Mirny's own light* [ADM, `SPEC` L55 chars 905–939 "there is no polar night here at all"; L86 chars 23–69 "approximately December 8 → January 5 (~29 days)"; the monthly daylight column, whose December cell is 24* (computed from latitude)]. Hours per day with the sun below the horizon, own arithmetic (24 − daylight): Jan 2.7 · Feb 7.4 · Mar 11.0 · Apr 14.7 · May 18.4 · Jun 21.6 · Jul 20.2 · Aug 16.4 · Sep 12.8 · Oct 9.1 · Nov 4.9 · Dec 0.0 (raw in §D). That is *sun below the horizon*, not darkness: `RES` §2 adds "No darkness beyond civil twilight for about 80 days (Nov 11 → Jan 30)" [CORR]. The canon figure for midnight sun is ~29 days; `RES` §2's ~27 in-frame is not used, and nothing rests on the difference.
- *1st:* the stop is national canon and cannot be read as a Mirny fact (`FRM` RP-5 refused the gloss that "the polar night falls on a population that is all resting").
- *2nd:* the sun is below the horizon 0 h a day in December and 21.6 h in June (the arithmetic above). **Whatever its length, a stop placed overnight (PA-B1) falls in continuous sun for the ~29 midnight-sun days and in the sun's absence through the long-dark months, and partly in sun in between; the stop and the sky agree in some months and disagree in others.** Where the sky does not announce the stop, whatever keeps it shared is not the sky.
- *3rd* [D]: the two reasons for the stop **separate in the lit weeks.** The robot's reason has a hard physical edge (charge, `RP` L334); the human's has no admitted edge at all (human effects of the nightless weeks: R-B4, `P3` R-34). So the stop in the lit weeks is a convention kept against the sky, held jointly by a half with a hard edge and a half with none. This is the first physical form of the spine's shadow candidate *the nightless catch-up* (`SPN` §9): `SPN` §3 already lists "no dusk ends the day in the one season that permits outdoor work" as a grudging tolerance [CORR; "the one season that permits outdoor work" is the spine's reading, not an admitted line, and `SPEC` L82 chars 8–65 says "WIND, decisively — the cold is NOT what defines this city."].
- *Not claimed:* any hour, length or bedtime (no calendar or clock is admitted, `FRM` PA-8), that rest is keyed to light, or that anyone sleeps in the dark. *PA-B1:* the stop is a daily recurrence at which both bodies are indoors; `RP` writes "OVERNIGHT RECHARGING" and nothing contradicts it.

**P4-B2 · Every movement is a crossing whose honest cue reads as an improvement** *[ADM + CORR + D]*
- *Canon:* "even at relatively moderate temperatures, sustained katabatic events make outdoor exposure dangerous" (`SPEC` L139 chars 171–270); "outdoor activity in strong katabatic conditions is dangerous regardless of cold protection" (`SPEC` L131 chars 277–366) — and "no hazard it names is spelled out in the admitted files" (`P3` B.0). *Research:* "The cues are honest; they are unread" and the strongest storms arrive "behind cyclones, as the sky clears" (`RES` §1) [CORR]; `P3` P3-2: "the first sign of the dangerous wind can read as an improvement".
- *1st:* with a mean wind of 12.0 m/s (43.2 km/h; `SPEC` L82 chars 168–185) and a monthly mean below 0 °C in all twelve months, the ordinary unit of movement is the move between places held above freezing. The files do not say how many such moves a resident makes (R-B9).
- *2nd:* the decision to cross is made against a cue that misleads in one direction (clearing sky, rising reading) and an honest cue nobody at the founding had learned (`RES` §1, quoting its own sentence: "the sky tells the truth in a language nobody at the founding had learned"). **There is no teacher to correct the decision** (`SPN` §6 #7) and **no admitted public instrument** either: whether a flag, a number or a relayed call exists at Mirny is in no admitted file (R-B10). `SPN` §4 says "the signal decides when people move" [CORR], which is about movement between Mirny and elsewhere and is a derivation, not an admitted line.
- *3rd — both ways, because the fork decides the shape of the day* [D]. **If no public call exists:** each resident's go-or-stay is a private judgment against an unread cue, and the injury that follows is read as the injured person's act (P4-B11). **If one exists:** it is a published judgment of the kind `RES` §5 records in real places elsewhere (a number at a named hour, a daily flag), none adopted for Mirny, and it is a Phase 6/7 fact, not a Phase 4 one. Neither branch is adopted.
- *Both bodies:* the channel by which the judgment can be acquired differs (`P2` P2-3): childhood exposure for a human born here, adult immersion for a robot with no childhood (`DR-11`), and in both cases the first link has nobody behind it: "a Mirny-made tradition has a first link with nobody behind it" (`P2` P2-3). The day shows it only in who can explain a decision and who can only make it. **Written as a fork, never as a skill level:** nothing admitted says a born-here human reads the cue better than a built-here robot, or the reverse.

**P4-B3 · The wind has one documented daily rhythm, in summer, and none documented in winter** *[CORR + D]*
- *Source* [CORR]: in summer and the transition seasons the katabatic builds in the evening and "peaks about 05–08 local and eases by 15–18"; "Whether Mirny keeps a weak daily cycle in winter is UNKNOWN"; "Calm and weak wind together: about 3 % of observations."; "Storms last up to about nine days (220 h)." (`RES` §1). Times are the source's local time; no in-world clock is set.
- *1st:* in the lit weeks no dusk marks the hour, so this is the only physical daily clock the files document (`P3` P3-1, which writes "the 29 lit weeks"; the span is 29 days, Backward check item 3).
- *2nd:* a lull, calm and weak wind together, is about 3 % of observations, which is about 11 days a year if observations are uniform (own arithmetic in `P3` Snag 13); a storm can outlast a week. **A lull is an event; a storm is an ambient condition.**
- *3rd:* because `RP` places the robot stop at "OVERNIGHT RECHARGING", the summer peak (05–08) falls at the end of the stop or just after it and the easing (15–18) in the afternoon, so **the day's first crossing, not its last, would meet the day's strongest wind** — a derivation that needs an in-world clock and a stop hour, neither admitted. **Reserved, not adopted (RF-B1).**

### B.2 MUNDANE CONCERNS

**P4-B4 · Dressing is the daily decision, and the hands are the price** *[IND + ADM + D]*
- *Canon:* the cold-weather garment register is ordinary for both bodies; exposure is "PREPARATION, the same category it is for a human" (`RP` L49–52); a robot "can realistically remain standing still in most of the country. She just needs to be prepared for the weather, just like humans do." (`RP` L42–43); "Heavier gloves cost fine motor control and tactile feedback; thin high-gauge gloves keep precision and cost warmth." (`RP` L55); the trade is "a GRADIENT across locations, never a gate" (`RP` L57). The realistic skin of a robot sits "where interaction happens — hands, face and ears" (`RP` L35).
- *1st:* every task that needs fingers outdoors is a glove decision, and the wind makes the price immediate (`SPEC` L142 chars 104–197: "effective wind-chill during katabatic events can drive apparent temperatures far below ambient") [ADM].
- *2nd:* the places a clothed robot or human must protect are the places that do the work; the same hands that handle the task feel the wind.
- *3rd* [D]: the day is a sequence of small trade-offs between dexterity and warmth, each remade at every threshold.
