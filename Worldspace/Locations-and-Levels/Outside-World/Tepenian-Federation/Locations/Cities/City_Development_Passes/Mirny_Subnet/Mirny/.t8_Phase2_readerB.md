## B. Arrival modes

### B.0 Two levels, one text
"Arrival" has two levels here: **into the Federation** (the treaty) and **into Mirny** (the station assignment). I apply
the taxonomy at both where they differ. **Every treaty-grounded verdict is draft-tier [FTD]**: `FTD` L3 calls itself "Not
locked canon", and the pass's own files call the clauses "unratified" (`FRM` PA-1, L457), although Art. VIII.1 (L117) says it
"shall enter into force upon signature … without further requirement of ratification." I take the most conservative reading:
**draft-tier throughout; the conflict is logged (§13).**

### B.1 The human admission route — exact wording and limits `[FTD]`
- **Art. II.2.3 (L48):** "Any human person who has, **prior to the date of this Treaty**, taken up residence among robot
  communities, married or partnered with a robot, borne or raised children in a robot household, or otherwise established
  their life among the robot population, **may elect to accompany** that population to the Federation of Tepenia, and shall
  not be prevented from doing so by any Assembly Power." Four grounds; the fourth is a broad residual.
- **Art. II.2.4 (L49):** "No human person shall be compelled to depart under this Article who does not so elect."
- **Art. IV.2.2 (L75):** the election means the person is "deemed … to have willingly and **permanently** relinquished their
  legal personhood within Assembly Power territory." **IV.2.3 (L76):** "No human person who has made the election … may
  thereafter return"; no Assembly Power is obliged to "recognize, admit, or extend any right to such a person who attempts to
  do so." **IV.2.4 (L77):** the forfeiture "is not a penalty imposed by any Assembly Power, but a consequence … of the human
  person's own free choice."
- **What the four grounds share** `[D, checked against the text]`: each is a **tie to the robot population**; none is a tie to
  a place, a skill, a nationality or a merit. (`DS2` L14 reaches the same reading `[K]`.) **Not a relationship requirement:**
  humans were free to self-exile alongside the robots, and the fourth ground is open.
- **Limits.** (i) "prior to the date of this Treaty" makes this the route for **pre-treaty ties only**; **no route for a human
  with no such tie is written in the draft.** (ii) The notes call the voluntarist framing "deliberately … the same
  New-Echota-style rhetorical move" (`FTD` L138) — so **the files give the legal frame, not the lived reason.** (iii) `RP` L87–88
  quotes the immigration source as saying eligibility was "people who could afford robots, or who loved/supported someone
  who owned one." **The treaty text has no affordability ground.** The two descriptions differ; I use the treaty text and log
  the difference (R-B6). (iv) Humans' station assignment is by Art. II.3.2 ("The population departing under Article II.2"),
  which includes the II.2.3 humans.

### B.2 The robots `[FTD]` and the body `[C]`
- **Art. II.2.1–2 (L46–47):** "Every robot presently residing within the territory of any Assembly Power, **and every robot
  who may hereafter come into being within such territory, shall depart**"; the provision "applies to robots as a class. No
  individual designation, accusation, or finding of wrongdoing is required, sought, or relevant." The notes call II.2.2 "the
  treaty's single most exposed line … left deliberately blunt" (`FTD` L135).
- **Art. IV.1 (L68–71):** personhood is withdrawn **in Assembly territory**; "no application within the territory of the
  Federation of Tepenia" (IV.1.2); a robot who has departed "and does not thereafter return to it is not affected" (IV.1.3).
- **The body.** Robots "do not age in the biological sense" (`RP` L67); they die "when something kills them", in incidents
  (`RP` L103–107); the gel brain's data "is not static" and cannot be copied out as a snapshot (`RP` L183). A robot's national
  origin is learned from the cultures immersed in after coming online, with no childhood (`DR-11`, `CON` L18 `[K]`).
- **Where a robot is built is not stated** for Mirny (`R-13`): chambers were shipped to and installed in "almost every city"
  (`RP` L432) and Mirny is not named.
- **The scaffold's own genre line:** "a victor's settlement imposed on a defeated, exiled population — not a cooperative
  agreement among equals" (`FTS` L7). The developer's words, quoted in `FTR` L17: robots choosing "between being exiled or
  being essentially destroyed"; "the actual choice was never real."

### B.4 The mix is the finding — second and third order `[D unless tagged]`
**M-1 (first order) — Founders were sorted on a tie or a class and on nothing about Mirny.** The humans by their tie to the
robot population (B.1); the robots by their class (II.2.2); both then assigned (II.3.2) without regard to nationality
(II.3.3). *Nothing in the sorting is a property the site needs* — not the wind, not stone, not the relay, not the record.
`RP` L88–89 states the national version: "The nation was founded by sorting people according to whom they loved." `[C + FTD]`

**M-2 (second order) — Nobody at the founding could be native to anything the site demands, and the only mode that
makes natives starts after it.** The site's one demand is judgment of the wind; no stock owns it (`SPN` L89 `[K]`).
*Born here* is the only mode that produces natives (human: raised in it; robot: built into it and immersed). So the
first natives are taught, if at all, by founders who had no teacher (`SPN` L49 `[K]`). `NNS` L21 says a **tradition passes
person to person**; **a Mirny-made tradition therefore has a first link with nobody behind it**, and what that link holds is
what the founders learned by doing — which is to say, by getting it wrong.

**M-3 (third order) — The two bodies keep the place's memory on different clocks, and text is the only channel that needs
no living person at either end.**
- *Humans:* children, mortal teachers; every human generation must be taught or must find out.
- *Robots built here:* online as adults, immersed (`RP` L426); **no senescence** (`RP` L67), so a robot's recollection of
  the founding can outlast every human founder.
- **But a long-lived robot is a person to ask, not an archive:** the gel brain "is not static" and cannot be read out as a
  snapshot (`RP` L183). So neither body's lived judgment passes without a teacher; **only the written record does** — which
  is the same record that carries thresholds and not judgment (`RES` L95–97, the Fogbank case `[K]`).
- **What this causes:** *the exile and the wind are both carried, after the human founders are gone, mainly by the
  long-lived half;* **humans born here meet the arrival story second-hand — from robots who lived it, or from text.**
  This grounds `SPN`'s "Mirny trusts what is written" from the *arrival* side (**not independent: [K]**, it agrees with the
  Spine rather than testing it). **Act-sensitive:** in Act 1 living human founders are still present; in Act 2 the
  personally exiled are robots only.

**M-4 — The arrival mix is established only for the founding cohort.** Eleven of fourteen stocks are non-founding (a count
of stocks, `[C]`), and the files do not say by what route or when they came: the census only says larger communities came
after (`CEN` L33) and that an immigration equilibrium followed (L476). The treaty text leaves a **continuing robot channel**
open ("who may hereafter come into being", II.2.1) and **no human channel beyond pre-treaty ties** (II.2.3). So the
mix at Census I is: *founders — assigned exiles · born/built here — fits, share unread · later stocks — unread.* A
"posted majority with a stopped-passing minority"-style answer **cannot be given and is not forced** (law of no forced fit).

**M-5 — No caste inside.** The usual product of *sentenced* — a status distinction outliving the sentence — is **written
outside Mirny** (Assembly law) **and not inside it** (B.3). *Covered in substance, absent in the expected form.*
