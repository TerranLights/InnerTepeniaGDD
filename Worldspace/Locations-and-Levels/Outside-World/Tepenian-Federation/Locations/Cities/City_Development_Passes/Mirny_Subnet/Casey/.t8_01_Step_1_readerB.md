### S1B-1 · The sea window

- **The finding.** Spec L82: "This made Casey a viable destination for incoming ships during the exile period." Spec L154: "the Budd Coast is accessible to shipping during the navigable season (roughly November–March) but ice-locked in winter; this directly determined Casey's supply windows". The monthly table's notes: "sea ice forming" (L135, April), "sea ice breaking up" (L141, October), "sea navigable" (L132, January), "sea fully navigable season" (L143, December).
- **The mechanism.** A seasonal gate on Casey's one certain route (PA-5 calls the sea access "certain and preferred", Frame L501). It is open roughly November to March and shut by ice from about April until about October.
- **The half that is written.** The inbound side: "incoming ships" (L82) and "supply windows" (L154), stated for "the exile period" (L82). The Frame adds that leaving by sea is possible only from about November to March, written to price sanctions (Frame L241). It also asks what is held between seasons (Frame L171) and notes that the founders' ships came in the navigable season, with their first winter after (Frame L358).
- **The half that is missing.** (a) The outbound side as part of the mechanism. No admitted line says what or who leaves Casey by sea inside the frame. (b) Whoever the gate decides against: an arrival that reaches the coast after the ice forms, or a person or cargo that must leave during the ice-locked months. (c) The sea route after "the exile period". L82 places arrivals in that period only. The one line that would speak to later use, Casey's port line, is held (RF-18; DQ-9 interim (d), Frame L641).
- **Durability and route back.** By physics the gate reopens every season. The outcome for someone caught outside it lasts until the next opening: about seven months at most, by the April and October notes (arithmetic from L135, L141 and L154). That is written only as figures, never as an outcome for anyone. A route out by road exists only if the roads stand at that moment (REQ-3; PA-5).
- **Question it leaves:** S1B-Q-1 (Phase 5, with Phase 2).

### S1B-4 · Heated recharge: a threshold with no route back

- **The finding.** The Frame's cold table, from physiology L13-16 (Frame L272): "Recharging below freezing causes permanent capacity loss" | "Every month's average low is below freezing, January −2.6°C (spec L132-143)" | "Overnight recharging in heated space is a year-round necessity on the averages, not a winter one". - **Question it leaves:** S1B-Q-4 (Phase 1, Phase 5, Step 7).
