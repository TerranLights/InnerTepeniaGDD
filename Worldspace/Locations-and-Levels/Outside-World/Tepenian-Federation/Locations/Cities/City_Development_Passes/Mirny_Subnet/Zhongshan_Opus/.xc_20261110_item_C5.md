### Reasoning

**Part 1 (the Federation sets the language): direction holds, scope narrows.** `DR-100` item (1) makes the dominant language
national and the same everywhere: that is exactly what P-1 asserted, so the Federation-level claim stands. But `DR-100` item (3)
now gives each city a supporting tier computed from its own composition, and Zhongshan's is Mandarin. That is a city-level language
fact the Frame's "The Federation determines ... language family" and L155's "Supplies ... language family" do not contain. It is
not a "local variant" in P-1's sense (it is set by a national rule, not written freely), so "no local variant may be written" is
not broken; but the statement that the Federation supplies the city's language picture in full no longer follows. And what does
that cause? Phase 5's table of "what the parent supplies" (L155) would have to show the supporting tier as coming from the city's
own composition under the national rule. "Language family" also becomes a label change: the thing supplied is a named language,
Hawaiian (later Tepenian). It stops there.

**Part 2 (one community vernacular): holds; a figure changes.** Nothing on the list touches the Language Module mechanism. The
rulings' general clause on fabrication chambers (L1563: every city has them, "since there are robots from every city") fits "the
community who had her built" being local. With Hawaiian dominant, the "single local object" is now identifiable as the local
speech of a national language, which the pass did not deny (it named none). The robot share is 50.49% at Census I (note L72), not
50.60%; if L1563's general clause ("let's go with your suggestion of Census II", "the pre-war snapshot governs the culture the
sheet describes") applies to this sheet, the figure is 47.82%. Either way the community is still about half robot, so "the
community is both" stands. Figure only.

**Part 3 (two clocks, uncorrected): direction stands, scope and one ground narrow.**
- The origin language of the stock now has a defined standing in the city: under `DR-100` item (2), Mandarin, the language of the
  Primary row, is widely understood here and people "can be assumed to follow". So the two branches' speech has a living medium
  in which they can still be heard, and "still audible" can stand. And what does that cause? The marker sits in the city's
  supporting tier, not in its dominant speech, which is Hawaiian everywhere. "What marks someone as from here" (L822) would be
  how this city's Mandarin sounds (and whatever it carries into local Hawaiian), a narrower place than the pass's unnamed
  "vernacular".
- "With the correction permanently unavailable" (L835), "NEITHER HAS BEEN ABLE TO CHECK ITSELF AGAINST THE SOURCE SINCE" (L832) and
  the cell's "the source is permanently unavailable to correct either" (L125) rest on L830's closed door. Under `DR-127` item 2
  humans could keep arriving after the signing, and Treaty II.2.1 sends later-built robots from Assembly territory to Tepenia (note
  L139-146). So "permanently" no longer follows: correction from the source is possible in principle. Whether anyone of the stock
  actually arrived in Zhongshan is not on the list, and C10 tests the closed door itself. And what does that cause? If any did,
  their speech would be a later layer of the origin language, a third clock beside the two; if none did, the two-clock picture
  stands as a fact about this city rather than a rule of the setting. L839's "instruction cannot" cross is a paraphrase of a
  canon file not on my list; it has the same exposure (a person arriving can teach). Neither outcome reverses the marker's
  direction, so this is narrowing, not flipping.
- A further question the rulings raise but my list cannot settle: L1632 says that at the signing the national language "is
  Hawaiian". If the resident branch, on this ground before 2564, already spoke Hawaiian as its dominant language at that date,
  then the newcomers met residents whose origin language had become a second language, not only a drifted one. That would change
  the picture of L833-834 but not its direction. Asked as C5-Q2.

**Part 4 (the switch is between two layers): direction stands; two of its stated grounds no longer follow, one of them inverts.**
- The two-layer shape stands and is now named: Hawaiian (dominant, everywhere) and Mandarin (the plurality's language, the sole
  supporting tier). The switch is not among the city's many origin languages; it is between the national language and one origin
  language. That matches L853's direction.
- **"Offered rather than assumed" (L849) inverts for speech.** `DR-100` item (2) defines a supporting-tier language as one in which
  "people can be assumed to follow". The plurality's language in Zhongshan is that language. So, applied to speech, the
  plurality's layer is assumable, not merely offered. (R1 may still hold for the city's other cultural layers; this item tests only
  its application to speech.) The pass also calls the plurality's layer the "default"; under `DR-100` the default is Hawaiian, and
  the plurality's language is the second layer. The roles keep their positions but swap their labels.
- **"The built layer — the one with no source at all" and "the genuinely shared layer was built here" (L850-855) no longer
  follow.** Under `DR-100` item (1) the universal layer is Hawaiian; under `DR-125` item 6 Hawaiian has named sources (English and
  Japanese primary; Korean, Tagalog, Ilocano and Mexican Spanish supporting), and it is national, not built in this city (which is
  what the pass's own P-1 already said). And what does that cause? Four of Hawaiian's named base languages are Zhongshan's own
  groups: Korean 9.700, Japanese 7.300, English 4.940 to 10.900, Tagalog 0.000 to 1.720 (CSV L776, L777, L779, L791). Together they
  are 21.94 to 27.90 points of origin share (low: 9.70 + 7.30 + 4.94; high: 9.70 + 7.30 + 10.90, the Tagalog row's nation already
  inside the English high bound), and these are origin shares, not speaker counts (`DR-100` item (5)). The plurality's language
  (35.83) is not among the bases. So the commons is not equally foreign to everyone here: it is nearer to the city's second,
  fourth and smaller groups than to its plurality. That removes L855's reason ("the only layer all ... origins could ever have
  shared" because it has no source), though the commons is still universal by ruling. Whether any of it was later built here
  depends on whether this city's speech is among the "wide variety of languages" that turned Hawaiian into Tepenian; not answered
  (C5-Q4). It stops there: the direction (a universal layer and the plurality's layer, with the switch between them) survives.
- Figures: "all eighteen origins" is 35 rows today; R3's "five named seams" and "twelve-entry tail" are C1's to restate.

**Part 5 (the pass names no language; no invented dialect).** At the time, the spec had no language field (note L174) and the
language rule did not exist, so the pass's silence was correct. Under the rulings as they stand, a re-derivation has two

L217) is not something the pass carries, so the pass has nothing to unwind there.

**Why NARROWS and not FLIPS.** No part's opposite now follows. The national dominant language agrees with the pass's own P-1; the
