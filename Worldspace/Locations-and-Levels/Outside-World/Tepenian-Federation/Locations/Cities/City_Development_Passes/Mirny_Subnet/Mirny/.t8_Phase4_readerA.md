## A. The headline function, in one sentence — then written away from

**The sentence.** Mirny is the hub whose relay carries its subnet's signal, and a quarry-and-materials engine that is the near-exclusive raw-material source for the robot-synthesis chambers fabricated elsewhere *(`CON` C-3: "subnet hub; quarrying; processing construction materials; manufacturing infrastructure-maintenance machinery; the near-exclusive raw-material source for robot-synthesis chambers"; `SPEC` L174 chars 1–135 "its role as the Mirny subnet hub"; `SPEC` L176 chars 110–197 "near-exclusive top-tier industrial/quarrying hub feeding raw materials to the Tri-Cities")* **[ADM]**.

**The test.** *Would this reasonably be someone's entire day, every day, forever?* **No.** Four grounds, none of which depends on a guess about what the place is like:

1. **The model's own baseline is not the headline function.** `D16`'s formula (`workforce = robots + 0.5 × humans`; `baseline = humans × (120.7·f·D + 85.6)/1000 + residents × (64·D + 113.2)/1000`, `f` = 1.00 for a city that is not one of the seven geologically forced importers) reproduces Mirny's row exactly (raw in §D: workforce 1,018,479.5 against the row's 1,018,480; baseline 41.097 % against the row's 41.1 %) **[IND + own arithmetic]**. So **41.1 % of the workforce is the city's own necessary work** (418,565 workforce units), and **58.9 %** (599,915 units) is the distinctive tier, split in the row into 11.8 % mandated and 47.1 % free. Even if every unit of the distinctive tier were the headline function — an upper bound I do not claim — 41.1 % of the workforce would still be doing something else. *Baseline means necessary, never disliked* (`D09`): it is not a lesser life, only a different one from the hub's.
2. **A workforce unit is not a resident.** The formula counts each human as half a unit; 1,018,480 units against 1,351,430 residents is 75.36 %. What the half represents (participation or hours) is in `D09` §5, which I did not open, so **no claim is made about human versus robot working hours**; what is stated is only that robots have no childhood and no senescence (`DR-11`; `RP` "They do not age in the biological sense"), so the dependants of the city are human by structure [D].
3. **The two bodies' hard needs are in every day.** Both stop (`RP`: "Robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons."); the robot recharges and the human eats and rests; leisure is owed to every robot as a condition of sanity. A day that is only the function leaves no room for any of these.
4. **Free does not mean apart from the function.** `D16`'s own formula defines FREE as the distinctive tier minus the mandated, and labels it "the character budget"; `D09` holds that the *truly elective residue is what remains after both* (baseline and mandated provider work). Quarrying, materials and the relay are by construction distinctive-tier work; whether they sit in the mandated 11.8 % or the free 47.1 % is not stated (R-24, existing; R-A2). So the free tier can contain the headline function and **cannot be written as what residents do instead** — the move I killed (Snags 4).

**What this phase therefore writes from:** the stop; the crossings; the three tiers' non-headline substance; the leisure hours owed to nobody; and the populations of §C. The hub and the quarry appear only as the scoped exception in §Gen-pop.

### B.1 ROUTINES — what recurs

**P4-A1 · The stop is the one fixed point both bodies keep, and for part of the year no sky keeps it** *[IND + CORR + D]* · base-level (`DR-4`) — not a Uniform claim · **Act: independent** (physiology, not culture)
- **Both bodies, first order.** `RP`: *"Robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons."* The robot's reason is, in `RP`'s words, "most prominently OVERNIGHT RECHARGING"; `RP` does not state the human's (its "Like anybody else" says only that robots need downtime as anyone does). The gloss that follows in `RP` — the polar night falls on a population that is all resting, together — does not transfer: Mirny has no polar night [ADM: "there is no polar night here at all"]. What Mirny has is a dark that is real for half the year (daylight under 12 hours April–September [ADM table]) and **no night at all for the ~29 days of midnight sun**, with civil twilight only for about 81 days [CORR]. So the stop is kept in the dark for most of the year and in the light for a month to a quarter.
- **Second order.** Neither stated reason depends on darkness: `RP` states no light dependence for recharging, and no admitted file states one for the human body (R-A5). So the coincidence of the two stops is a fact about the two bodies, and **what times it in the lit weeks is not the sky** and is **not written anywhere** (R-A4; Q-A1). ### B.2 MUNDANE CONCERNS — what must be dealt with, not what is exciting

- **First order.** Both bodies are dressed for the weather: *"robots would also wear winter gloves."* The trade is the human hand's: *"Heavier gloves cost fine motor control and tactile feedback; thin high-gauge gloves keep precision and cost warmth."* At Mirny the hazard is wind chill that "can drive apparent temperatures far below ambient" [ADM], so the garment and the glove are keyed to the wind and not to the number on the thermometer (P3-2 [CORR]). Each outdoor manual task is a small repeated trade between warmth and hand.
- **Second order (robot).** `CP` reads cold as cost along three axes: reversible capacity reduction ("−20–30% near freezing", "down to 50–60% at −20 °C"), permanent loss from one bad charging decision ("Charging a cold cell below freezing causes lithium plating"), and output sag under load. Every admitted Mirny monthly mean is below freezing and August's average night low is −20.2 °C; the record low is −36.5 °C, 3.5 °C above the −40 °C at which lubricants solidify [ADM + IND]. **These are ambient figures, not cell temperatures**; how far a clothed, moving robot's cells run above ambient is not stated (`P3` RF-6). So I write the cost as a **gradient that preparation shifts**, never a gate (`M-151`): a charging plan and a crossing paid in charge. `CP` itself lists three charging options (warm the cells first; charge cold at 5–10 % rate; charge cold at full rate and lose capacity forever), so holding the nightly recharge in a place above freezing is the prudent default, not a physical necessity.
- **Third order [D].** The costs run in opposite directions by body. `CP`: *"A human who keeps moving in extreme cold exhausts themselves; a robot who stops moving strands themselves."* That sentence is written for extreme cold; at Mirny's moderate cold the same directions hold at a lower magnitude, and nothing in the files gives the magnitude. So in a mixed pair mid-crossing — the founding human unit was *"a household or community that included a robot"* (`P2` P2-2 [CORR/D]) — the one place where the two bodies' ordinary rules diverge is the pause when the cue turns. **What residents do there is not in any file** (RF-A1; R-A6).

**P4-A5 · The seasons enter an ordinary day only at the thresholds** *[IND + CORR + D]* · base-level (`DR-4`) — not a Uniform claim · **Act: independent**
- **First order.** The two bodies draw two energy services with no common unit in the files. *Human:* `D11` sets 2,800 kcal/day as the population-average (*"The 2,800 kcal/day and 7,000 kcal/m² settings are choices"*) and *"only humans eat"*; at Mirny's 665,901 humans that is 1,864,522,800 kcal/day, about 90 MW of continuous chemical power (conversions asserted in §D). **A setting, not a Mirny measurement; demand only; no supply, export or "feeds N" claim.** Mirny's row carries a full human-keyed food term (`f` = 1.00): 100,468 of its 418,565 baseline units, from `D16`'s own formula. *Robot:* energy replenishment is siligel's *first* function (`RP`) and recharge is overnight; **no admitted file gives a quantity, a frequency, or the split between siligel and recharge** (R-A7; Snags 7).
- **Second order.** `D13`: *"NOTHING IN THE INDOOR TIER USES DAYLIGHT — ANYWHERE IN TEPENIA, AT ANY DEPTH, INCLUDING AT THE SURFACE."* and *"Nobody in Tepenia grows food behind glass."* So Mirny's midnight sun and its 2.4-hour June change nothing about the plate. The one sun-using tier *"works only in summer, only under the midnight sun, and only on the mild coast"*; whether any Mirny ground belongs to it is not given (R-A8), and `D11` makes no city assignment (DRQ-10).
- **Third order [D].** With food, recharge and (on `P3`'s derivation) interior conditions all year-round, the whole seasonal swing — light from 2.4 to 24 hours, the glaze weeks, the ice — reaches a resident only where a resident crosses a threshold. That is the axis restated. It also means **how often a resident crosses is the variable that decides how seasonal a life is**, which is a distribution and so unasked (`DR-4`).

### B.3 PERSONAL STRUGGLES — what weighs on a person, not on an institution

**P4-A6 · The want has no stop** *[IND + CORR + D]* · base-level (`DR-4`) — not a Uniform claim · **Act: independent**
- **First order.** Mirny's FREE tier is 47.1 % of the workforce (479,704 units; `D16` L275, src C), the share that remains after baseline and mandated work and that `D09` reads as work people *want* (it is an upper bound: the three robot-keyed industries are excluded, and `D09` notes that not baseline is not quite wanted). `RP`: a robot that spends all its time working *"is virtually guaranteed to go insane"*; leisure *"is a condition of remaining sane"*; overwork is *"a HAZARD WITH A MECHANISM"*.
- **Second order.** Nothing in the files supplies an external stop for work a person wants, and P4-A1 shows the sky cannot supply one for ~29–81 days. Baseline work is done because it is necessary and mandated work because the nation needs it (`D09`); those are reasons for the hours, though no file gives either a stopping rule. For wanted work the reason for the hours is the holder's own want, so **the stop rests on the holder**, in the weeks when the one cue the sky offers is absent.
- **Third order [D].** The hazard has an address in canon but only after the fact: *"robotic counselors, whose discipline is a robot's inner life rather than its hardware"* are taught nationally, and *"Every city staffs its own clinicians, always."* So Mirny's wanted work, in the season when outdoor work is possible and the sky does not end the day, is the place where visible virtue (people doing the work they chose in a country founded on that freedom) carries a hidden cost (no brake), which meets `00d`'s three tests: unintended (nobody designs a wanted job to have no stop), unnoticed (it reads as devotion), discoverable only by looking (in the care rooms). **Proportion:** most residents stop; *Mirny works*. This is a byproduct, never the headline, and it is **not about the hub or the quarry** (R-A2).
- **Robot reading:** the hazard `RP` names. **Human reading:** nothing admitted says the same hazard applies; REQUESTED.

**Carried by reference, not repeated.** *Nobody chose Mirny*, and the first loss with someone to answer for it is the one `RES` §7 says recognizing the quarry-plus-relay instrument would give (`P2` P2-1 [CORR]); the compelled robot's cost has no chooser to answer for it. In an ordinary day this is felt as the crossing's private weight (P4-A2), not as a grievance (*"Ten of eleven produce no grievance"* [CORR]).

**P4-A7 · Leisure is owed to every robot, and at Mirny it is priced by the crossing** *[IND + CORR + D]* · base-level (`DR-4`) — not a Uniform claim · **Act: independent**
- **First order, by constraint.** The exterior has no quiet baseline (calm and weak together about 3 %, P3-1 [CORR]); no interior's sound is given, so **a retreat to quiet is not written**. The hand-held rituals meet the glove trade: smoking, or any leisure of the hand, done outdoors pays the glove cost each time (P4-A4 [D]); where it is done is not in the files. The sea is a surface for about ten months, with fast ice 30–40 km wide at winter's end, *"safe for pedestrian and vehicle traffic"*, and the katabatic dies 8–15 km offshore [CORR]; that a surface exists does not say anyone goes there. **Whether anyone goes there for leisure: REQUESTED** (R-A9).
- **Second order.** For the robot body, outdoor stillness is a priced choice (`CP`: "Stopping is the dangerous act", at a magnitude that is a gradient here), and a place held above freezing carries no such price [D]. So robot leisure that includes being still has a **cheap setting (an interior) and a priced one (the outdoors)**; the price is paid in the crossing there and the crossing back.
- **Third order [D].** Leisure is a condition of sanity for 50.73 % of the population, so *a place where a robot can be still at no cost* is a standing need, not an amenity. But the amenity and layout layer is excluded (`DR-10`), so the ULM cannot write it. The honest result is **bounded, not filled**: a cheap setting (an interior held above freezing), a priced setting (the outdoors in any season, with the lit weeks and the ice as the physically available extremes), and an empty middle of what people actually do.
- **No forced fit — the drink pole.** `RP` offers a variety-versus-potency axis and a working-class pole. The admitted economy is quarrying, materials and a relay, but that is the work of a share of residents, not of the typical one (`00b`: a narrow role standing in for the population). **Mirny's placement on the axis is left open** (R-A10); none is placed here is not the same as none is possible here.

**P4-A8 · Wanted work is also the escape for some, which blurs the line the stop draws** *[IND + D]* — the same free tier read from the other side: when 47.1 % of the workforce does work it chose, work and leisure share a boundary that only the holder draws.
