### What changed

1. **The Treaty now provides for later arrival.** Article II.2.5 (Treaty L50) says that any human who did not make the founding
   election *"may, at any time after the effective date of this Treaty, freely elect to take up residence within the Federation of
   Tepenia"*. The draft's own note dates this paragraph to 2026-11-09 (L138). Article IV.2.2 (L76) now names II.2.5 as well, so a
   later arrival loses personhood in Assembly Power territory. IV.2.3 (L77) then bars that person from returning.
2. **The Treaty's note names the old reading as an error.** L138 says the earlier silence *"had been misread elsewhere as an
   implicit prohibition"*, and goes on: *"Paragraph 5 settles it: relocation to Tepenia is open at any time after the Treaty's
   effective date, not just during the founding exile wave"*.
3. **The developer ruled the same way.** The developer's general canon of 2026-11-09 (`DR-127` item 2, recorded in the note,
   entry 1, L139-142) says humans could keep arriving after the signing, but could not go back. The ruling left open whether robots
   kept arriving.
4. **Robots, as the Treaty stands today.** Article II.2.1 (L46) applies to *"every robot who may hereafter come into being within
   such territory"* and requires each one to leave and take up residence in Tepenia. My list does not date this paragraph as new,
   so I cannot tell whether it read this way when the pass was written. As the input stands today, it opens a conditional robot
   channel. The note, entry 1, L151-152, records `DR-11`: robots carry a national origin, learned by immersion after coming online.
5. **Unchanged.** IV.2.3 still bars anyone who elected from returning, so the door still opens only one way. V.3 (L92) still allows
   agreements on *"trade, the exchange of goods, and lawful passage"*. My list does not date V.3 as new.

### Reasoning

1. **The pass's inference no longer follows.** The pass reasoned from "no return" to "no new arrivals." The no-return clause still
   stands. "No new arrivals" was never in IV.2.3, though. The pass read it into the Treaty's silence, and the Treaty now fills
   that silence with the opposite: arrival stays open at any time, in one direction only. **The one-way half of the conclusion
   holds. The closed-system half does not.**
2. **What does that cause in §2.5's block (L219-226) and its finding (L314)?** None of these still follows: *"not one of them has
   been refreshed from its source in about 250 years"*, *"No new arrivals from outside"*, *"no correction from the origin"*, *"a
   closed system running on what it brought"*, *"stated at maximum strength"*.
   - The canon position is now the opposite. Throughout the ~250 years, each origin culture could receive people from its source,
     and each would bring that source as it stood when they left.
   - *"No returning travelers"* still holds.
   - So separation is complete outward and only partial inward.
   - My list gives permission, not size. How many came, when, and whether any reached this city is not stated (C10-Q1, C10-Q2).
     A re-derivation therefore cannot swap "closed" for "nearly closed" without a ruling, because that would mean inventing a
     figure.
3. **The visitor row (L217).**
   - A visit means coming and going back. For anyone who elected, that is still barred by IV.2.3, so *"no return trips, no
     visiting cousins"* holds.
   - *"No relatives from home"* no longer follows, because a relative can now come and stay.
   - *"No one who has seen the origin country"*: a late arrival has seen it. The clause holds only in its "and come back" sense.
   - A further point, which is not a change of input: the row's absolute *"ANYONE ... EVER, AS A VISITOR"* rests on IV.2.3, and
     IV.2.3 binds only those who elected. The pass's own L224-225 notes V.3 trade agreements and VI.1 inspection observers. The
     Treaty note's warning against reading silence as prohibition applies here as well (C10-Q3).
4. **Phase 8.**
   - **L57.** "The one-way door" holds. "The closed system" does not.
   - **L342-345.**
     - The founding branch's version was not "kept current until the day the door closed behind it". The door stayed open
       inward, so that branch could keep being refreshed after 2564.
     - The claim that the older branch's food is "a stranger one" can still stand. It rests on that branch's drift with no
       correction before 2564, which this change does not touch.
     - The handoff of the question to `DRQ-18` is unaffected.
   - **L474-477.**
     - The repair finding cites §2.5 for *"no garment imports for ~250 years"*. But §2.5 is about people, and its own L224-225 says
       trade agreements were allowed. So the garment claim did not follow from §2.5 even under the old reading, and now §2.5's
       closure is gone as well.
     - Whether trade or later arrivals brought clothing is not stated (C10-Q4).
     - The other grounds in the same span (the two-glove problem, and the salt regime from `03_Research` §3.3) explain wear, not
       the lack of replacements.
     - So the chain's first link has no stated ground. The repair culture may still be right, but it needs a different reason.
   - **L828-834.**
     - The meeting of two branches about 481 years apart holds, because it rests on the resident branch and on 2564, not on
       closure.
     - The founding branch's clock is no longer one start date. Later arrivals each left the source on a different date, so
       that branch's "time since the source" is a range.
     - The heading *"NEITHER HAS BEEN ABLE TO CHECK ITSELF AGAINST THE SOURCE SINCE"* and the cut-off clause *"with the correction
       permanently"* no longer follow.
   - **L888-889.** *"Its own closed border"* becomes a one-way border. The one-sentence test still passes, because it is built from
     this city's own facts.
5. **Phase 9, L62.** *"The door that opened one way"* is still literally true. It holds.
6. **Phase 5, L138-141.**
   - This claim concerns the orbital exit, outward, and rests on the census showing no return migration (`01_Inherited` A-2). The
     change is about people arriving into Tepenia from Assembly Power territory, not about return from orbit, so the cited claim
     holds.
   - One caution from L138's wider frame ("every door this city has ever had"): if arrivals kept coming throughout the ~250 years,
     that inward door existed in every era too. Whether this city shared it or held it alone depends on how arrivals reached the
     city, which is not on my list.
7. **05_Reconciliation.**
   - **L117-120.** These lines quote Phase 8 L475-477 and say the mechanism matches the culture file's *"no functioning supply chain
     back to anywhere"*. The pass's own first link (closed system) no longer follows. The match now rests only on the culture
     file's supply-chain claim, which is a different ground: the economics of supply, not a closure to people.
   - **L169.** The datasheet's *"cut off from the homeland"* still fits in the outward sense, since nobody can go back. The pass
     finding it confirms ("closed system") does not, so the confirmation has to be restated as one-way.
8. **Shared table, L125.** *"The source is permanently unavailable to correct either"* no longer follows. The source cannot be
   visited, but it can arrive. The label "TWO CLOCKS, STILL AUDIBLE" and the first clause hold.
9. **Robots.**
   - The pass cites IV.1 for robots. IV.1 bars a return, not an arrival.
   - II.2.1, as it stands today, sends every robot that later comes into being in Assembly Power territory to Tepenia. Under
     `DR-11`, such a robot would carry its origin as it stood when the robot left.
   - Whether any robots did come into being there after the Treaty is not on my list, and `DR-127` left it open (C10-Q2).
   - So on the robot side, "no new arrivals" also has no ground in the Treaty as it now reads, and II.2.1 points the other way,
     conditionally.
10. **Where the chain stops.** L222-223 gives closure as the reason *"the law insists the output is a Tepenian culture descended
    from that stock, not the stock itself."* That outcome does not depend on closure. It stands under the Acts law (origin becomes
    ancestry, not identity), and later arrivals join it rather than reverse it.
    - **Flips:** the closure itself and its specific consequences. That means no correction, a single founding clock, the stated
      ground for the repair chain, the language cell's last clause, and "maximum strength" separation.
    - **Holds:** the one-way direction, everything that follows from no return, the meeting of two branches, and "Tepenian culture,
      not the stock."
11. **Why FLIPS, not NARROWS.** The conclusion under test is the closure: "no new arrivals from outside", "a closed system", "no
    correction from the origin". The pass's own L219 calls this *"THE ONE THAT MATTERS FOR EVERYTHING DOWNSTREAM."* That claim no
    longer follows, and the Treaty now states its opposite for entry (II.2.5, with L138 naming the old reading a misreading). Its
    direction does not survive. Only the no-return part does, and that part is not what the downstream lines build on.

### Questions for the developer

- **C10-Q1.** Did humans actually arrive in Zhongshan after the signing, and on what scale: a trickle, a steady flow, or waves by
  period? Are they counted in Census II? The Treaty (II.2.5) gives permission, not a figure. A re-derivation needs the scale to say
  how strongly later arrivals keep the origin cultures in step with their sources.
- **C10-Q2.** Robots. Treaty II.2.1 (L46) sends every robot that later comes into being in Assembly Power territory to Tepenia, and
  `DR-127` left robot arrivals open. Did robots keep coming into being there after the Treaty, and so keep arriving, each carrying
  its origin as learned there (`DR-11`)?
- **C10-Q3.** Visitors. Does any agreement under V.3 ("lawful passage", L92) let a person who has not made the election visit
  Tepenia and leave again? The pass's visitor row reads IV.2.3, which binds only those who elected, as covering everyone. The pass's
  own L224-225 also mentions VI.1 inspection observers.
- **C10-Q4.** Goods. Did any trade under V.3 bring goods, clothing among them, to Zhongshan during the ~250 years? The answer
  decides whether the "no replacement" link in the repair chain can stand.

LLAST: - **Article II.2.5, added 2026-11-09, closes a real gap:** the draft previously only addressed the one-time exile-wave election (paragraph 3, "prior to the date of this Treaty"), leaving the document silent on whether a human could move to Tepenia at any later point — silence that had been misread elsewhere as an implicit prohibition. Paragraph 5 settles it: relocation to Tepenia is open at any time after the Treaty's effective date, not just during the founding exile wave, and carries the same forfeiture consequence (Article IV.2) as the original election — explicit loss of any claim to home, residency, or quarter in Assembly Power territory, not merely a cross-reference to personhood. Article IV.2.2's own cross-reference was extended to II.2.5 to match.
