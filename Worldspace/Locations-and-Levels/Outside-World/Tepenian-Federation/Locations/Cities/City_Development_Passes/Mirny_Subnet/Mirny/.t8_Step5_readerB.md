## The contradictions and the both-are-true readings, worked

*Scales checked in order (`04` Gate 6 note): (1) public/mainstream vs private/minority · (2) dominant culture vs its own counterculture · (3) earlier vs later generation · (4) legal/procedural vs narrative/emotional. Each item: the single property, the scale, then three orders.*

### Both-are-true

**B-1 · The settlement layering (46-5b, 46-5d) and Phase 9's two lines (P9-14, P9-17).** *Entry 46:* a small founding-era population and a larger later one coexist without merging, and families who trace themselves to the founding hold a memory the later majority does not. *The pass:* a crossable wind line and an uncrossable memory line, neither carrying rank; in Act 2 the remembering side is the founding robots. **The property:** the inherited record holds none of the founding (`P6` P6-5), so the founding can be carried only by persons — first-hand by those who arrived, second-hand by their heirs. **Scale 3 (earlier vs later generation):** entry 46 describes the line as it looks across generations of *families* (descent); the pass describes it as it is held by *persons* (formation, memory). *1st:* both are true — the memory line is real, and in human households it passes by descent as heirship (`P9` P9-5: the heirs hold the founding second-hand). *2nd:* what entry 46 cannot see, because it writes no Act and no robot half, is that in Act 2 the first-hand holders are robots of the founding cohort, so a human "founding family" in Act 2 is a family that *heard* the founding from a robot (`P9` P9-17); the line entry 46 places between families runs, by late frame, inside the robot half. *3rd:* entry 46's "fault line" survives only as a line of **memory without standing** — neither side outranks (`P9` P9-15) — and its stock content does not survive at all: the founding layer is three exile groups including the Primary's own stock (`DR-46`), so "old-small beside new-large" is a fact about **persons and time**, never about which nations (C-1). Not adopted; the pass's P9-14 stands; entry 46's sentence would need D-8.

**B-2 · "Competence over romance", the unstated civic ethic and the compact (46-3b, 46-6a, 46-18b; 48-d by shape).** *Entry 46:* the wind and every hardship are managed well and without narrative; an unstated civic philosophy of competence is the closest thing to a unifying ethic. *The pass:* the compact — the medium holds; the reading is yours — under which losses are booked to the person, to weather or to silence, and the book never learns (`P6` P6-1, P6-17). **This is the test of my strongest finding (§3, leg 4).** **The property:** no living teacher, so what can be written or built is held to not failing, and what cannot be written is left to each person. **Scale 4 (legal-procedural vs narrative-emotional):** entry 46 records the narrative self-understanding — *we are competent; we do not dramatize*; the pass records the procedural consequence — *nobody answers for the medium; the injured answers for herself*. *1st:* the match is in **form**: both put an unstated, non-devotional proposition in the slot where a creed would sit (`P6` P6-9, P6-10 — "the slot holds a compact rather than a creed"), from an independent prior derivation. *2nd:* "not dramatizing hardship" is exactly how the compact's refusal looks from inside: a loss that is never ritualized is experienced as calm competence (`P6` P6-17's shadow; `00d` L208: "visible virtue, hidden cost"). *3rd:* **the existing material holds the virtue and lacks the cost** — it has no blame, no unanswered loss, no record that cannot learn. Corroboration, not proof (tier 3).
