### B.1 ROUTINES — what recurs

**P4-1 · The stop is the one fixed point both bodies keep, and for part of the year no sky keeps it** *[IND + ADM + CORR + D; credit A, B, C2]* · **Act:** independent
- *Canon:* "Robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons." The gloss that follows in `RP` (the polar night falls on a population all resting) **does not transfer**: Mirny has no polar night [ADM: "there is no polar night here at all"].
- *1st — the sky is a calendar, not a clock.* The sun is below the horizon 0 h a day in December and 21.6 h in June (24 minus the table's daylight column); **under the stated assumption** of a day symmetric about solar noon, a dusk would wander across at least 9 h 27 min of solar time through the year on monthly means (13:12 in June to 22:39 in January) and **does not exist for the ~29 days of midnight sun** [ADM: "approximately December 8 → January 5 (~29 days)"]; beyond them about 80 days have no darkness beyond civil twilight [CORR, `RES` §2]. *(The solar-time table is a derivation under a stated assumption, not an in-world clock.)* `SPN`: "The calendar tells the truth; the hour lies".
- *2nd — whatever keeps the stop is in no file.* `FRM` PA-8 sets no in-world clock or calendar, so "kept by convention" names only *whatever keeps time*: it claims no device and no hour (R-39; Q-41). Neither body's *reason* for the stop depends on the sun (a human rests, a robot recharges; light has no admitted effect on recharge): the nightless days change what people see outdoors and **nothing about what either body needs**. The wind's documented summer rhythm (evening build, "peaks about 05–08 local and eases by 15–18", local time of the source, no in-world clock) does not point at the conventional night either.
- *3rd [D].* (a) In the lit weeks the stop is a social fact before it is a physical one; the two reasons for it **separate**: the robot's has a hard physical edge (charge), the human's has no admitted edge (R-40). ### B.2 MUNDANE CONCERNS — what must be dealt with

**P4-5 · The garment and the hand, and the robot's version: charge** *[IND + ADM + D; credit A, B, C2]*
- *Canon:* "Heavier gloves cost fine motor control and tactile feedback; thin high-gauge gloves keep precision and cost warmth." At Mirny the hazard is wind chill that "can drive apparent temperatures far below ambient" [ADM L142], so the garment and glove are keyed to the wind, not the thermometer; each outdoor manual task is a small repeated trade between warmth and hand.
- *Robot, 2nd:* `CP` reads cold as cost along three axes (reversible capacity loss, "−20–30% near freezing" and "down to 50–60% at −20 °C"; permanent loss from one bad charging decision; output sag under load). Against the two stated points the same draw is a larger share of usable capacity by a factor of about **1.17 to 1.60** between near-freezing and −20 °C [D; an upper bound on the effect, never a gate]. The concern is not the cold but **where a charge happens**: a crossing is paid in charge, the stop restores it.
