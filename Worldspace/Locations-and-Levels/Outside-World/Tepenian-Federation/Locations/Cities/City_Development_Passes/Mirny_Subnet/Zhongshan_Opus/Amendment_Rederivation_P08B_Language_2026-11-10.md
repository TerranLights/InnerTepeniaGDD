**(d) Its grounds, cited.**

- **Finding 10 (the language set, by the rule as it stands).** `DR-100` (heading at L1664: *"THE LANGUAGE RULE: HAWAIIAN IS DOMINANT;
  A CITY'S SUPPORTING TIER IS EVERY GROUP WITHIN 12 POINTS OF ITS LARGEST"*): item (1) makes Hawaiian (later Tepenian) dominant
  everywhere; item (2) defines a supporting tier as a language that is not dominant but widely enough understood that business,
  social talk and general dealings can be done in it and people can be assumed to follow; item (3) makes a city's supporting tier
  every language group within 12 percentage points of its largest, at the mid-points of the ranges, nations sharing a language summed
  (all rulings log L1669); the cutoff runs *"from the largest group's mid-point, for every city"* (`Language_Sets` L77). Zhongshan's
  groups at their mid-points: Mandarin 35.830 (`Language_Blocs` L775); Korean 9.700 (L777); German 8.470 (L778); English 7.920, the
  mid-point of 4.940 to 10.900 (L776); Japanese 7.300 (L779); every other group lower (L780-806). The cutoff is 35.83 − 12 = 23.83 and
  nothing reaches it: *"Supporting tier: Mandarin alone (35.83 − 12 = 23.83; no other group reaches it)"* (note L173); *"| Zhongshan |
  Mandarin 35.8 | none | none |"* within 5 and within 10 points (`Language_Sets` L36). **Zhongshan: Hawaiian (later Tepenian) dominant;
  supporting tier Mandarin alone.** The developer's own words give the same second language by a second route: the next dominant
  language after Hawaiian is whoever holds the majority (L1563, general clause), and a plurality counts (L1668). The margin, 35.83 −
  9.70 = 26.13 points, runs between the single-row Primary tier and the tiers below it, so it rests on no within-tier difference
  (*"no finding may rest on a within-tier difference"*, note L116). Every row has the same share at both counts and in both columns
  (`P02` Finding 23, L316-320; `P02` Finding 31, L393-397), so the set is the same at Census I and Census II, for robots and for humans, and
  which census governs §8.8a's figure (Question 73) cannot move it. `DR-100` lists how the rule enters a finished pass as not applied
  yet (L1670); D-6 answers that for a re-derivation (note L42), and `DR-101` sends the change to the amendment record, not into the
  pass files (note L33-35).
- **Finding 11 (the set does not turn on the separate Chinese group).** `Language_Blocs` L786 lists a separate group, *"Chinese (Mandarin
  and other)"*, at 0.000 to 3.040, drawn from one Notable row. If item (3)'s summing joins it to L775, the largest group runs 35.83 to
  38.87 (mid-point 37.35) and the cutoff becomes 25.35; the membership is the same at any value in the file's own range. `Language_Sets`
  L36's *"Mandarin 35.8"* would then understate the largest group by up to 1.52 points at its mid-point (Question 78). The row is
  weighting (`DR-12`) and the mapping is recalled (Finding 14), so nothing rests on this figure.
- **Finding 12 (the Significant tier as a whole).** The spec's Significant tier totals 24.10 (*"Significant 3 rows (Korea 9.70, Japan
  7.30, Germany 7.10; 24.10)"*, note L107), 0.27 above the cutoff. It would clear the cutoff only if its three rows shared one language,
  and they speak three (`Language_Blocs` L777-779): a fact of language identity, not of shares. On the file's own grouping (German 8.47
  with its Notable partner row, L778) a one-language Significant tier would be 9.70 + 7.30 + 8.47 = 25.47; the 24.10 is the spec's tier
  sum, not a language group. Neither moves anything.
- **Finding 13 (the tier and a split of the Primary row).** The first check's search log says that any plausible relabeling of the
  Primary row's language *"changes a label, not the membership"* (first check L194-195). That holds for the whole row, not for a split:
  Korean (9.70) would join the tier only if the largest group fell to 9.70 + 12 = 21.70, that is, if less than about 60.6% of the
  Primary row (21.70 / 35.83 = 0.6056) were counted to Mandarin. `DR-100` item (5) maps each national-origin row whole to one
  language, so the split does not arise. Two refinements: the rule says "within 12 percentage points" without saying whether exactly
  12 counts, so at exactly 60.56% Korean sits on the line; and the real-world rate in Finding 14 (80.72%) lies above 60.56%, though the
  two figures measure different things, so this shows robustness only.
- **Finding 14 (the one mapping the tier rests on, checked against real-world data).** `DR-100` item (5) marks the mapping of nations
  to languages as recalled knowledge, not verified (rulings log L1669). The tier rests on one mapping only: the Primary row to Mandarin.
  Searched (A search 4; C search 2): the stock's real-world source country reported that 80.72% of its population could speak its
  standard Mandarin in 2020, up from 53.06% in 2000 (national sample survey, announced 2021-06-02). So the label holds as a real-world
  baseline (a source, not a figure for 2564 or later), and "Mandarin" here is best read as the stock's common standard, not as every
  founder's home speech. It confirms the label only; no wording rests on home varieties.
- **Finding 15 (Part 1: the object becomes a named language, and one language fact is the city's own).** *"language family"*
  (`00_Frame` L48, L327; `04_Phase_05` L155) becomes the named language, Hawaiian (later Tepenian; Hawaiian at the signing, L1632),
  which is *"a Pidgin between a primary base of English and Japanese, with a supporting base of Korean, Tagalog, Ilocano, and Mexican
  Spanish"* (`DR-125` item 6, note L171): a language, not a family. The supporting tier is not supplied by the Federation: it is the
  national rule (item (3)) applied to the city's own rows. *And what does that cause?* `04_Phase_05`'s "Supplies" column (L155) carries
  the dominant language and the rule, and the city's own side carries Mandarin. *And then?* Because the tier is computed from the
  composition, the city's language picture is exactly as provisional as its composition table (Finding 30). *Third order:* P-1 keeps its
  direction, so nothing built on it flips; on these inputs only the Frame's two lines, `04_Phase_05` L155, §8.8 and the table cell need
  the language set written in.
- **Finding 16 (what flows outward: a labeled reading, kept out of the wording).** `04_Phase_05` L155's Federation "Requires" mandated
  output *"owed outward continuously"*. *And what does that cause?* The only language the rule makes common to every place is

## 7 · Questions for the developer

- **Question 69 (where the marker is heard; the first check's C5-Q1).** Zhongshan's speech marker, two branches of one stock: is it
  meant to be heard in the city's Mandarin (its supporting tier), in its local Hawaiian too, or in both? The proposed wording places it
  at least in Mandarin (Finding 17). Real-world practice makes both realistic, since a community's earlier language commonly leaves
  traces in the language it shifts to (A search 3).
- **Question 70 (Hawaiian at the signing; the first check's C5-Q2, widened).** At the signing the de facto national language was
  Hawaiian (rulings log L1632). On this ground in 2564, who spoke it: the resident branch, the human founders, the founding-wave robots,
  or all of them? The answer decides whether the meeting of L832-834 was between a Hawaiian-speaking resident branch and newcomers
  speaking the stock's language, or between groups who learned Hawaiian together, and whether *"not a robot register humans tolerate"*
  (L820) needs a note (Finding 19).
- **Question 71 (Tepenian, and when; the first check's C5-Q4, widened).** Is Zhongshan's Mandarin among the *"wide variety of
  languages"* whose later influence turned Hawaiian into Tepenian (`DR-125` item 6, note L171)? And does that change fall inside this
  pass's frame? The first decides whether any part of the national commons was built here; the second bears on `00_Frame` L351 and the
  robot clocks (Findings 24, 27).
- **Question 72 (a robot's speech after she comes online).** Does the Language Module set a robot's vernacular when she is built (*"the
  community who had her built"*, `04_Phase_08` L814), or keep tracking the community she lives in (*"tracks the dominant local
  culture"*, `00_Frame` L350)? And did any founding-wave robot of the stock remain in Zhongshan late in the frame? The pass's own L348
  and L351 say one can; this narrows `P02` Question 82 (`P02` L750-752) to whether one did (Finding 23).
- **Question 73 (which census for the robot share; the first check's C5-Q5).** Does the clause of rulings log L1563 choosing Census II
  (the pre-war snapshot governing the culture a sheet describes) apply to Zhongshan's pass, so that §8.8a cites 47.82% at Census II
  rather than 50.49% at Census I? The restated wording gives both, so no wording waits on the answer (Finding 4; Choices 44, 45).
- **Question 74 (the 12-point rule if robot shares are set apart; the first check's C5-Q6).** If robot origin percentages are ever set
  apart from the human ones (`DR-128` item 1; `P02` Question 81), is the supporting tier computed on the combined shares, or for robots
  and humans separately (Finding 30)?
- **Question 75 (L841 and `DRQ-18`).** L840-841 say of the older branch's texts that *"Nobody was ever taught to read it the way it was
  written"*, while L843-844 leave the resident population's size and fate out of canon. Is the sentence the canon's own

  `DRQ-18` (Finding 7)?
- **Question 76 (`DRQ-18`, sharpened).** Was the resident branch a viable, self-continuing community when the founders arrived?
  Real-world founder effects make this the fact that decides whose features would weigh more in the city's modern local form (Finding
  22).

  pass's own L834 (*"Then both drifted together"*) and L843 (one *"modern vernacular"*) suggest, or as two speech forms heard side by
