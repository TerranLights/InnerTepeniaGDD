*What the words carry.* Canon gives Tepenia a shared night: "Robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons." (physiology L336), the humans to sleep, the robots to recharge, and a charging robot dreams (rulings L2003, `DR-137` item 3). Here the sky never keeps that stop for the people who keep it. The site has no polar night and a December sun that barely sets (spec L151–156): daylight runs 21.0 h in January and 19.4 h in November, about 24 h at the December solstice, so from November to February any stop longer than the dark (about 4.6 h in November, 3 h in January, 7.2 h in February, none in December) falls partly in daylight; and it runs 5.7 h in May, 2.7 h in June, 4.1 h in July and 7.8 h in August (spec L179–190), so for four months any working day longer than the daylight begins or ends in the dark. Only around the equinoxes (March 13.2 h, September 11.6 h) does the sky roughly frame a day. **So the ordinary day here is organized around a stop the clock keeps and the sky contradicts, made with curtains in the light months (S3-28) and lamps in the dark ones, and for half the population the stop is not only a bed but a warm room and a charge, in every month of the year:** every month's computed average low is below freezing `[computed, REQ-52]`, and charging below freezing costs a robot permanent capacity (cold file L23–26), so the robot half's stop is always indoors and warm (spine S2-9; S3-27).

*Peer-free test, could this be true anywhere?* No. It needs this place's own light (no polar night, 2.7 h to about 24 h, 18 arcmin north of the Antarctic Circle; spec L151–156), this place's own cold (sub-freezing average nights in every month `[computed, REQ-52]`; a coldest month of −18.7 °C, L149) and this place's own composition (507,856 robots of 1,000,071, 50.78%; spec L20). Take away the light swing and the stop falls at dusk by itself; take away the cold and a robot could charge anywhere; take away the robot half and the stop is only sleep. It rests on admitted lines, canon and the computed columns (labeled), and **not** on `[PROV-8]`, the held ground, animals, or any unruled door; the clause that the charge draws on the same supply as the warm rooms and the racks is a conditional consequence (P4A-8), not part of the axis. **What could be true anywhere, and so is not the axis:** the shared night itself (national canon); robots recharging overnight (national); Hawaiian as the common tongue; the indoor lit crop (spec L258, national); cold met by clothing (physiology L38–57). ## B · The four elements

### Routines

- **P4A-4 · The stop arrives by the clock, never by the sky, and the year slides the sun across it.** Canon's shared night (physiology L331–338) is the day's one fixed point. Month by month the sky either fills the stop with light or empties the working day of it (spec L179–190: November 19.4 h to about 24 h in December to January 21.0 h and February 16.8 h; May 5.7 h, June 2.7 h, July 4.1 h, August 7.8 h). Where the sky gives no night the stop is kept by a shared schedule, a closing hour for common rooms and a darkened room (S3-28, research L285; Phase 2 P2-25 item 3). **Human half, PROPOSED, conditional on working hours no line gives (P4A-RQ-3):** in December a person goes to bed in sunlight behind a curtain drawn by hand (Phase 3 P3-17); in June, with 2.7 h of daylight around midday, she gets up, sets out and comes home in the dark, under lamps, in a season whose means run −17.2 to −18.7 °C May to September (L183–187). **Robot half:** at the stop she goes to a warm room to charge, and dreams (rulings L2003; physiology L456–457). *Expression (done, seen):* the stop is the same hour in June and December; what changes is whether a window shows night at it. `[PA-3]` does not touch this: daylight is computed from the latitude (spec L199). *(A)*
- **P4A-5 · In June the sun is up only around midday, so for anyone whose work spans midday the sun falls entirely inside working hours.** June daylight is about 2.7 h (spec L155, L184), centered on midday; the noon sun stands about 0.88° above the northern horizon with refraction, so a rise of height *h* shades about 65 *h* of ground to its south, and some ground waits weeks for its own return of direct sun (S3-45, research L439; Phase 3 P3-17). **Conditional on working hours, which no line gives** (P4A-RQ-3): for anyone indoors through midday, June brings no daylight in her own hours at all; whoever wants the sun takes it from work, or from a day off however days off fall. - **P4A-6 · The robot half's routine ends every day of every month in a warm room on a charge.** Charging a cold cell below freezing plates lithium and permanently cuts capacity; safe cold charging is ten to twenty times slower (cold file L23–26); every month's computed average low is below freezing, the highest January's −3.0 °C `[computed, REQ-52]`; so warm charging space is a year-round need, not a winter one (spine S2-9; S3-27). - **P4A-7 · Two changes come into the routine each year, both set by water, not by crop.**
  - **Before the melt:** the drains are put right between the refreeze and the next melt (amendment G7-C1, PROPOSED), in the spring months on the spec's constructed notes, September "still fully frozen" to November "thaw approaching" (spec L187–189; months constructed, L192–195; spine REQ-62); real culverts are found iced shut at winter's end and opened by hand, by heat or by drilling just before the thaw (S3-4, research L63).
