## 3 Reasoning (each step cites a line read)

- SRY-C05-17. **The allocation ground survives.** Every admitted line keeps the founding cohort arriving by allocation: pass L109 ("the Court chose the site"), L151 ("the Court allocates a site to a designated population") and spec L203 ("court's diplomatic allocation."). No admitted line says any member of that cohort picked this place. For the allocated cohort, L121's claim and its reason at L117 ("the Court picked") therefore still follow.
- SRY-C05-18. **Two of the three absences are untouched.** No admitted input bears on the terminus (L110, L118, L135) or on the missing rotation clock (L108, L119).
- SRY-C05-19. **The founding era is no longer one closed cohort.** The headline is scoped to "the founding era" (L122), but the pass treats the 2564 population as a single closed group. "Fled to it" is "the whole city" (L132), and B.4 lists no arrival from outside between the allocation (L151) and the university's students (L155). Startup L65 now says humans "could continue arriving into Tepenia after the signing". No pass line accounts for such arrivals. The reason "the Court picked" (L117) therefore covers the allocated cohort, not everyone the founding era could have held.
- SRY-C05-20. **L65 does not contradict L121.** It is a statement about the nation (startup L63: "These are statements about the nation, not answers about a location."). It does not say any such arrival reached this city. It does not say such arrivals came by their own decision either; reading them as people who chose would decide something the source leaves open. For robots, nothing was answered (L65). The headline is therefore not contradicted. What it loses is its ground for covering the whole founding era.
- SRY-C05-21. **B.3's claims of impossibility lose their ground.** The pass's own `G4` row says the founders arrived "with only the infrastructure and whatever records it held" (Frame L307), and the spine says the city "inherited buildings and records" (L95). Current canon says records can draw people, as "a general mechanism that any location may use" (startup L67). It also says newcomers can inherit research, equipment, techniques and results (LOG L656–L657, L661). So, under current canon, the founding handed the city things that can draw people. Two pass statements therefore no longer follow as statements of impossibility: "come, and could not have" (L141) and "At founding it had nothing anyone could select it for" (L139). B.3's own ground (L141–L142) is the absence of artistic or musical intention behind the allocation, and it does not reach the inherited records or research. Whether this city's inheritance actually drew anyone is not decided. L67 says a location "may use" the mechanism; it does not say a location must.
- SRY-C05-22. **The "only once" claim and the causal order lose part of their ground.** L144 says a self-selected layer "became possible only once the NATIVES had built a practice worth choosing". That no longer follows as stated. By L65, arrival was possible from the signing onward. By L67, a location's inherited records are a possible draw before any practice exists. B.4's order places "CHOSEN" (L157) after the university (L155), and L160 concludes "So even the choosing arrives on an assigned rail". Both rest on L144 and lose the same part of their ground. L161 ("The city did not build its own front door.") is not contradicted, because records and research reach the city by inheritance, not by building. Whether the city took up inherited research "and carry it on as its own" (LOG L661) is a matter for re-derivation.
- SRY-C05-23. **The `G4` "only" lists are narrower than current canon.** Three pass lists name less than LOG L661 now allows: Frame L307 ("with only the infrastructure and whatever records it held"), spine L95 ("it inherited buildings and records") and spine L102 ("site knowledge only from the inherited records"). LOG L661 adds the research "(data, results, ongoing datasets)", the equipment and the techniques. The "not people" half stands. LOG L646 still says "no one carried it across the gap", LOG L662 still excludes a tradition "passed person to person", and spine L95's "No handover in person." is unaffected.
- SRY-C05-24. **Why NARROWS.** The verdict is not `HOLDS`, because the change is more than a figure or a label. As written, the sentences at L139, L141, L144 and L160 and the "only" lists at Frame L307 and spine L95 and L102 no longer follow from the current inputs. The verdict is not `FLIPS`, because no admitted line says anyone came to this city by their own decision in the founding era. L65 is national and says nothing about choice or destination, and L67 is permissive. The headline (L121) can therefore survive re-derivation in qualified form. Verdict: `NARROWS`.
