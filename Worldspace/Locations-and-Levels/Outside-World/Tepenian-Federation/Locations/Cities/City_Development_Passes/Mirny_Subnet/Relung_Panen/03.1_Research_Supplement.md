- **RS-1 · The gulf's ice is the oasis's one water surface that has not broken out for many years: perennial, fresh and about 3 m thick in summer (295–320 cm by radar; 3.02 m cored in January 2020; 5 m at most), over water fresh to about 70 m and salt below, kept from opening by its glacier-bound shores. PROPOSED.** It now rests on two independent sources, the survey and the 2022 ice paper (RS-C7). The oasis's bedrock is too rugged for a runway, so the gulf is the oasis's runway ground. - **RS-2 · On the one on-site summer record the surface held, but the record begins after the warm core. PROPOSED; one survey, one summer; narrowed.** From 23 January to 16 February 2019 the ice showed no spring decay, was flat enough that a runway needed only marking, and was judged able to bear heavy wheeled aircraft; it was 3.02 m in January 2020, in a summer whose January days ran mostly +7–8 °C with rain twice. **The survey started on 23 January, so it cannot show that the core (December to mid-January) leaves the skin unsoftened, and no heavy wheeled landing on this ice in summer is recorded.** **Split:** C's first reading (the gulf's summer as a resurfacing season, not a softening season) was withdrawn by C in Round 3, and B's reading of the skin as half-strength white ice was preferred; the narrower reading is written. *(B, RSB-101; C, RSC-202 as narrowed; A in Round 3.)*
- **RS-3 · The summer weak layer is the top ~30 cm, a porous infiltration skin of the white-ice kind, and little snow falls to renew it or cap it. PROPOSED.** The skin forms when winter snow melts, percolates and refreezes; it is ~680–720 kg/m³ against ~917 at the bottom, the kind real practice rates at about half the bearing of clear ice and that candles in thaw, over ~2.7 m of clear ice. Real ice runways keep their surface by testing before each landing, by filling dust holes with slush and grooming (one blue-ice runway flies all summer this way), with patch ice weaker than the original; a compacted snow cap needs snow in quantity and time to settle. On this place's lines (spec L109, little lying snow; spec L157, ~92 mm ESTIMATED `[DEM]`) a cap is not available, now on the site's own record: S3-20's caution is confirmed. **Third order (A):** patching makes the surface a patchwork whose weakest pieces are the newest repairs. **Who keeps the ice and who declares it fit are not written** (`DR-137` item 11; REQ-165 (c)). *(B, RSB-102, RSB-103; A, RSA-2; C, RSC-204.)*

PROPOSED.** Caverns up to 6 m across and 30 cm deep cluster in the north of the surveyed ice, fed by rock dust that the prevailing easterlies lift from a valley between two hills. - **RS-6 · What can close fresh ice in summer is its temperature at depth and candling, both measured on the ice. PROPOSED, physics.** Fresh ice keeps far more strength near melting than sea ice does, but above about −4.5 °C sunlight melts it along its grain boundaries and its strength becomes erratic; this is the mechanism behind S3-C6's ice-temperature criterion. White or flood ice counts at about half the bearing of clear ice, and ice near melting is sensitive to temperature and stress; breakthrough can come below the formula's load. *(C, RSC-205; A, RSA-2; B #8.)*
