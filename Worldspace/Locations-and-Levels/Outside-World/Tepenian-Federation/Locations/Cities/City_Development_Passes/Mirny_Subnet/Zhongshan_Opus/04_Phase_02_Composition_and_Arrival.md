# 2.2 · Arrival modes — the founding instrument hands over the taxonomy

> The phase's core move: how a population arrived shapes a location more than where it came from. Very few
> places are one mode; the mix is the finding.

Here the modes are not inferred. Four of the five are written into the treaty or into a canon ruling, in terms.

| Mode | Who | Source | What it produces here |
|---|---|---|---|

## And one mode is conspicuously absent

| **STOPPED WHILE PASSING** | Null, and the null is explained. `02_Spine.md` §2.2's `G5` finding: nothing passes through here — "a terminus is nobody's midpoint." A place nothing passes through cannot accumulate people who meant to leave and did not. The absent mode is a second-order consequence of a named deficit, recorded as a result rather than a gap |
|---|---|

> ### THE MODE-MIX, AND WHY IT IS UNUSUAL
> The two largest populations arrived by opposite modes on the same day. The robots were sent, as a class,
> with no finding against any of them. The humans who came with them chose it, individually, at the price of
> never going back. **One population had no say and no charge. The other had a choice and paid for making it.**
> This is national, not local — every founding city inherits it. The local inflection is §2.3.

**The law's own structure is worth naming precisely: its two exceptions are *continuous presence* and *total

established before the habitation-continuity exception was ever invoked.

## What that means, stated flatly

### WHEN THE FOUNDING POPULATION ARRIVED IN 2564, THERE WERE ALREADY PEOPLE LIVING HERE, AND THEY HAD BEEN LIVING HERE FOR ABOUT 481 YEARS.

| AND THE STOCK IS THE SAME STOCK | The residents were *"descendants of the same national population."* The founding exiles were *"Sinian Federation (formerly Chinese) exiles."* **The same origin stock, split into two branches, separated by 481 years, meeting again on this ground.** |
|---|---|
| And canon says the resident branch had already diverged | The law's own words: *"naturally evolving over five centuries the way any population does."* So the divergence operator — time · separation · local environmental setting · local struggles and hardships · local goals · local sensibilities and habits — had already run on them, in this environment, before the country existed |
| What it is NOT, and the law is emphatic | *"It can never hand the exiles institutional knowledge, personnel, or command continuity."* A population persisted; an institution did not. Records can cross as artifacts; instruction cannot |

> ## THE CONSEQUENCE — `ACT 1` DOES NOT DESCRIBE THIS PLACE
> The Acts say that from 2564 to the early 2600s, Tepenians were still *"X who live in Antarctica"* with
> origin cultures fresh, and only became properly Tepenian in Act 2. That model assumes everyone arrived in
> 2564 carrying an un-diverged origin culture. **Here, one population had been living in Antarctica for five
> centuries before Act 1 began.** For that branch, "people who live in Antarctica" was not a stage to pass
> through — it was already the whole of what they were, and the newcomers arrived speaking the origin culture
> the residents had spent half a millennium drifting away from. The `Act 1 → Act 2` gradient still runs here;
> it simply did not start at the same place for both halves.

**A second, smaller inference, labeled as an inference:** the treaty's exile provision addresses robots

population by five centuries. **This is an inference, not canon, and nothing below rests on it.** Written here

> ### THE OPERATION, PER THE DIVERGENCE PRINCIPLE
> Too little: refusing all ethnic material produces a placeless city. Too much: transplanting a source culture

> stock, then apply the local divergence to it.

> | On the resident branch | ~481 years of divergence before 2564, plus ~250 since |

> | On the founding branch | ~250 years of divergence, from 2564 |

does it count for anything?* — because it is one of the few places where "first" is not the founders.
