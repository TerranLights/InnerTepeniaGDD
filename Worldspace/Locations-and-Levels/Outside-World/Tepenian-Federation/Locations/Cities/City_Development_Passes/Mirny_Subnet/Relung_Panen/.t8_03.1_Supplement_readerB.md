- **RSB-101 · The gulf's ice holds through the summer, and the summer is short and sharp: its failure is not breakup or thinning but the surface. PROPOSED.** On the site's own record the gulf has not broken out for many years, carries about 3 m (to 5 m) in summer, showed no decay in late January–February 2019, and was 3.02 m in January 2020, in a summer whose daytime air ran +7–8 °C with rain. The sector's melt is a December–January event of about a month at full extent, longer in some years, and the shelf's oasis side melts longest. The central-oasis station has December and January above 0 °C in the mean in both measured summers (+1.7 and +1.8 two-year), against the constructed −0.0 and +0.6 (spec L179, L190 `[PA-3]`). So on this place's lines the risk window of `DR-140` (December to February) has a core of December–January and a February refreeze (two-year mean −2.25 °C). Bearing is not the limit (heavy wheeled aircraft per the survey); what the melt puts at risk is the skin and the dust holes (RSB-102).
- **RSB-102 · The weak layer is the top 30 cm, the porous snow-ice skin (680–720 kg/m³), and the dust caverns that pock it; little snow falls to cap it. PROPOSED.** The gulf's top is infiltration ice, the "white ice" kind real practice rates at about half the strength of clear ice and weakens further by candling; below lies ~2.7 m of clear congelation ice. Snow is too scarce on this ice to make its own infiltration layer every year (Kharitonov & Borodkin), which confirms S3-20's caution that a snow cap is not available here, now on the site's own record.

On this place's lines the caverns form in the melt and the refreeze begins in February; filled holes freeze solid only once the surface is below 0 °C again. **Who keeps the ice and who declares it fit are not written** (`DR-137` item 11; REQ-165 (c)).
