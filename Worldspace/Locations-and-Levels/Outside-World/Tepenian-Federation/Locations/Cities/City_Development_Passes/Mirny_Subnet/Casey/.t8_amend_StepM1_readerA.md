## 4 · Questions for the developer

**AM-Q1 · Can a line inside another location's completed pass supply a relation to Casey when it is about a shared thing Casey is named in?** `DR-131` item 3 counts "lines about shared things it is named in" as Casey's own lines, "excluding any clause grounded only in another city's lines" (L1949). It does not say whether such a line may be taken from another location's pass.
- (a) **No.** The fact is taken only from the shared thing's own source or from Casey's own lines, never from another location's pass.
- (b) **Yes,** where the clause is not grounded only in that location's lines (the reading in CR-7-1).
- *Recommended, PROVISIONAL: (a).* A clause with a ground outside that location's lines can be taken at that ground, so (a) loses nothing that (b) would admit. A clause with no such ground is excluded under both readings. (a) also keeps the run from reading another location's answers at all, which is the one-location law's own safeguard.

**AM-Q2 · Does Casey's parent subnet count as a "shared thing it is named in"?** The ruling lists "(its port, airport, road, cluster)". The subnet is Casey's Tier 0 parent (spec L5), and REQ-13 asks for status "in the subnet". No cluster is named for Casey in my material.
- (a) **Yes.** Lines about the subnet that name Casey, by name or in a member list, count as Casey's own lines. A clause grounded only in another member's lines is excluded, and RF-14 (how the treaty structures subnets) stays reserved.
- (b) **No.** Only Casey's own entries count, and lines about the subnet supply nothing.
- (c) **Leave it open** until Phase 5 meets such a line.
- *Recommended, PROVISIONAL: (a).* A subnet is shared by its members as plainly as a road is, the list reads as examples ("shared things … (its port, airport, road, cluster)"), and the exclusion clause still bars inferring Casey's status from another member's lines.
