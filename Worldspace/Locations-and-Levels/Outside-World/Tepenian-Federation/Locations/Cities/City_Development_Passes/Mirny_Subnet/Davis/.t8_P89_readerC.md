### XC-210 · Is there a Davis accent, contact feature or loan vocabulary? (answers XC6-33)

- **Real-world comparables** (section 8; sources, not specifications):
  - **(1) First-generation learners carry their first language into pronunciation.** Pidgin Hawaiian, the plantation lingua franca of Chinese, Portuguese and Japanese laborers in the 1880s, showed allophonic variation that "typically reflected first language interference". Glottal plosives were reduced or replaced because they "were not phonemic in any of the substrate languages" (Roberts, APICS, search 8).
  - **(2) Children of mixed-origin populations "focus" a new local variety.** The focusing is "substantial but not complete" in the first child generation (Kerswill and Williams 2000, a new town).   - **(5) Isolation measurably speeds convergence.** An overwintering group's vowels converged within one winter, but the change was not yet perceptible as an accent; that takes much longer (Harrington et al. 2019, search 27).
