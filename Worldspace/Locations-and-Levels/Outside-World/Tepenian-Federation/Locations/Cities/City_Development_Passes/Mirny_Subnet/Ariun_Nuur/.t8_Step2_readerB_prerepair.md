**STRENGTH** (item 132)
- **Still air.** Mean wind ~5 m/s; the site sits above the katabatic regime; no whiteout under a clear sky: "when visibility closes,
  something is actually falling" (L93). → The sky outdoors tells the truth; a clear sky stays clear. → Outdoor work can be timed by
  looking up rather than by forecasting drift. → **Movement outside is limited by duration, not by weather events:** the outdoors is
  predictable and therefore plannable to the minute.
- **What falls stays, and little falls.** 22 mm a year measured, ~90% retained (L83, L88–89). → Snow is a deposit, not weather. →
  There is no storm to clear after; there is only a slow, even rise.
- **Cold that keeps.** Mean −54.8 °C, no month above −31.3 °C (record). → Anything that must be kept frozen keeps without spending
  power on cold. → Here the bill is inverted: keeping cold costs nothing, keeping warm costs everything. → What the place gives freely
  it gives to **objects** (stores, records, samples), not to bodies.
- **Continuous light for a third of the year** (~123 days of midnight sun, L97), aligned with the mildest months (Dec −31.3, Jan −31.9).
- **Flat, uniform ground of ice** (L68): no slope, no avalanche ground. A flat ice surface already serves aircraft at the real site (an
  ice skiway, stations L67; infrastructure fact only).
- **The lake is legible from above** (L129): its signature reaches surface instruments.
- **Altitude costs the majority body nothing** (frame §4 item 4: robots do not breathe).

**DEFICIT** (item 133)
- **No ground.** Bedrock ~4 km down; food term at 10%; "ICE-SHEET FORCED IMPORTER" (16 L2353). → The site cannot feed its people. →
  Its characteristic failure is the one the missing ground was supposed to prevent: **hunger arrives by the road, not by the season.**
- **No surface liquid water** (22 mm a year; the lake is ~4 km down): every liter is made by melting.
- **No daylight in the coldest months**, and the cold is wider than the night on both sides. Polar night ~Apr 24–Aug 21; the record's
  means are already −64.8 °C in April and still −65.7 °C in September (10.4 h of daylight, L113). → The sun returns about six weeks
  before the cold breaks (October −56.4). → **The return of light is not the return of warmth;** the coldest air season (April to
  September, every mean at or below −64.8 °C) is about six months, the night about four.
- **No coast**, and no road to the subnet's coastal hub (L9, L66).
- **No diurnal cue in polar night**: "the day/night split narrows toward weather noise" (L118).
