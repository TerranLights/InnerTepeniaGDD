### RT-1 · The seven-month sea closure (D1; S2-9, S2-15, S2-25, S2-26)

**S3B-1 · The shore ice breaks months before the sea opens.** *Physical; the spine's contradictable leg, checked first.*
- *Source:* Voermans, Liu, Cao, Kousal, Heil, Fraser, Collins, Rabault and Babanin, "Inside-outward breakup of sea ice by locally generated waves in the Vincennes Bay Polynya", *Nature Communications* 17, 11224 (2026), https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/542494/1/s41467-026-77979-y_reference.pdf (search log #4). Buoys set on the fast ice beside the site in four seasons recorded the fast ice breaking out "early October" 2021, from "12 October" 2022, on "19 November" 2024 and on "1 November" 2025. The fast ice was 120-130 cm thick on 10 September 2021 and 55-92 cm in early October 2024. When it broke out, "several hundred kilometers of pack ice" (2021, 2022) or "150–200 km of sea ice" (October 2022) still lay between the coast and the open-ocean ice edge. The neighboring polynya is analyzed for "April to October" and is "fully developed" August to October.
- *What it did:* **Changed, by refinement; it does not contradict the spine.** The spec's October "sea ice breaking up" (spec L141, as S2-9 cites it) matches the fast ice's breakout in October or November. But the breakout of the shore ice is not the opening of the sea: a belt of pack 150 km wide or more still stands offshore. The spec's "navigable roughly November–March" (spec L154) is the leg least supported by what was found: no source reached says when ships can pass the pack at this coast (open thread). The April freeze (spec L135) is consistent with the polynya's April-to-October season, and was not tested further.
- →1 Casey's opening has at least two steps in the present-day record: the shore ice goes in October or November, and the sea becomes passable only once the pack offshore retreats, at a date not found.
- →2 S2-26's "opens slowly" gains a physical step the spine did not have: the city can watch its own shore ice break and drift away weeks or months before any ship can reach it.
- →3 The lit-but-shut stretch (S2-26 →3) has a second visible sign besides the light: open water at the shore that is not yet a route. As far as the sea is the only route, the hope the city can see arrives twice, as light and then as broken ice, before the access it promises. *A hypothesis for Phase 1, not a finding about culture.*
- *Divergence:* present-day buoy and satellite records (2003-2025), not the frame. Nothing about who placed the buoys or why is carried.

**S3B-2 · The closure is not a fixed calendar, and its start is the less predictable end.** *Physical, sector scale.*
- *Source:* Massom, Reid, Stammerjohn, Raymond, Fraser and Ushio, "Change and Variability in East Antarctic Sea Ice Seasonality, 1979/80–2009/10", *PLOS ONE* 8(5): e64756 (2013), https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0064756 (#7). In the roughly 90-145°E sector the sea-ice zone is narrow (it advances only about 500 km from the coast and is often about 300 km wide, as the paper is summarized); the minimum falls in February to early March and the maximum in September to October. In single years the ice season in the 100-145°E band ran more than 60 days shorter (1980/81) or more than 60 days longer (1999/2000) than the mean, which the paper attributes mainly to differences in advance rather than retreat. In 2004 the ice declined rapidly in November; in 1999 it retreated very rapidly in December.
- *What it did:* **Changed.** S2-9 and S2-15 read the closure as about seven months on the spec's calendar months. The sector's record says the length of the ice season can differ by two months from one year to the next, and that the difference comes mainly from when the ice arrives.
- →1 The closing (S2-25's "decisive date") moves from year to year by weeks.
- →2 "The last navigable weeks of March" (S2-25 →3) is a moving deadline, not a date on a calendar.
- →3 The decisive date is also the least predictable one, so the last departure and the last arrival are a judgement made against a risk, not a date kept. The cost S2-27 names, living each winter with the previous summer's judgement, falls hardest on that one decision.
- *Divergence:* the figures are for the sector's sea-ice zone as satellites measure it, not for the anchorage; a SCOPE, not a statement about Casey's bay (runbook L2654-2656). Present-day record. The paper's framing of its 110°E transect by national program is not carried (`DR-19`).

**S3B-3 · A real substitute institution: the hold run from above as an annual national program.**
- *Source:* "Northern Shipment: Significance, Threats, and Possibilities" (2020-10-20, no author), https://arctic-russia.ru/article/northern-shipment-significance-threats-and-possibilities/ (#9). Russia's "northern delivery" is "controlled by the state and financed from the national budget"; goods are delivered in the short ice-free window; some rivers are navigable for "just a few weeks"; shipping makes up 70% of the final price of fuel; a shortfall of fuel oil could leave "a school or kindergarten ... without heating in the middle of winter". Snippets (#8, UNVERIFIED): about 1,800 remote settlements depend on it, two-thirds without a road.
- *What it did:* **Changed, by supplying a form.** The spine addresses D1 "in the parent" only for a winter substitute, the road (S2-74 D1). The real case shows a third form: the parent runs the hold itself, as a yearly program that orders, finances and ships the winter.
- →1 A hold can be a local store laid in by residents, or a program run from above; the admitted material writes neither (the spec's word is supply, spec L154).
- →2 If the parent ran it, a short winter would be the parent's shortfall; that is the parent address S2-75 says the ice masks.
- →3 Which form Casey's hold takes decides whether a hard winter has an author (S3B-4). A question for Phase 5 and Phase 7, never assumed.
- *Divergence:* one mechanism taken (an annual, financed, window-bound program); everything else diverges. The real program is fuel-heavy and river-bound, and nothing about the real country's state, politics or war is carried. Casey's parent is unwritten (PA-3; PA-4).

**S3B-4 · A missed window gets an author as soon as someone has set a date.**
- *Source:* Bob Weber, The Canadian Press, "Arctic communities won't get crucial supplies after barge cancelled due to excess sea ice", Global News, 2018-10-03, https://globalnews.ca/news/4513776/arctic-barge-cancelled-supplies (#12). Household groceries, construction materials and municipal equipment were left on the dock at the transfer port. A builder: "All our total next year's worth of supplies are on that barge." In the real case the ice was the cause, and the grievance still formed, against the shipper and the government, because someone had set a sailing date, chosen not to send an icebreaker and given no notice.
- →1 The ice is an alibi only while nobody has set a schedule.
- →2 The moment the hold is scheduled (a last ship, a sailing date, a cutoff), a missed window has an author.
- →3 Whatever institution makes Casey's hold reliable is the same thing that ends the ice's alibi. S2-75's masking holds for the sea's closure itself and fails for any human decision about the last ship. *For Phase 5 and Phase 7.*
- *Divergence:* real goods, a real company, a real government; none is carried. In-frame ships are not assumed (PA-8).

**S3B-5 · Contrast: a real river town makes its opening a single, shared moment. Casey's opening has no single moment to mark.** *(Mostly ornament; the change comes from S3B-1.)*

- →3 If anything marks the end of Casey's shut half, it is an arrival, not an event of the ice: an inference, labeled. The waiting at the end (S2-26) has no defined end, which makes it harder to share.
- *Divergence (3.5):* the lottery is not imported; its only argument here would be that it is interesting. One mechanism is considered (marking the end of isolation), and the source's form is refused.

### RT-2 · Heated rest at city scale (D2; S2-10, S2-13, S2-40)

**S3B-6 · Real heating systems keep a season in which the heat can stop for repair. On Casey's averages that season never comes.**
- *Sources:* snippets, consistent across several pages (#14, UNVERIFIED): Russian heating periods start after five consecutive days with a daily mean at or below +8°C and end after five days above it. Snippets (#15, UNVERIFIED; Russia Beyond, *The Moscow Times* archive and others, 2003-2025): the pipes that carry hot tap water also heat the radiators; every summer the hot water is cut for 10 days to three weeks for maintenance, because, an official said, repairs are easier when the heating is off and the soil is not frozen, and only then can all the cracks be found.
- *What it did:* **Changed S2-13.** Applied to Casey's own figures, the real rule never switches the heat off: every month's mean is below 0°C, January's −0.3°C the highest (spec L132-143, as S2-25 carries them). For robots the binding line is lower still: recharging below freezing causes permanent loss (Frame L272), and every month's average low is below freezing (S2-10).
- →1 Heat at Casey has no off season, on the averages, for either body.
- →2 The upkeep that real systems do in their warm gap has no gap to fall in, so it must be done while the heat runs (or on spare capacity: an inference, labeled).
- →3 S2-13's standing cost carries a second cost inside it: the heat's own maintenance can never rest. National canon, at low weight, says the same of a place without a gap: "Pipes and conduits freeze solid if power is diverted even briefly for repairs." (grid L14, as S2-13 quotes it).
- *Divergence:* the real rule is a human-comfort threshold (+8°C); Casey's binding threshold is the robot's freezing line, which is stricter. Casey's heat source before about 2688 is unwritten (S2-69 (b)).

**S3B-7 · When city heat fails in deep cold, the failure outlasts its cause.**
- *Source:* RFE/RL, "Ukraine To Evacuate Children From Freezing City", 2006-02-01, https://www.rferl.org/a/1065311.html (#18): a key heating pipe failed in subzero conditions; about 45,000 people were without heat from January 22; heat was back for about a third of them by February 1; a first group of about 650 children was to be evacuated; the president blamed local authorities. Snippets (#16, UNVERIFIED): the water in the network froze and radiators burst in most buildings.
- *What it did:* **Changed S2-13's "first to fail" (spine L118).** The spine names the robot majority's recharge capacity as first to fail. The real case adds what fails beside it: the network itself, which freezes and cannot simply be restarted.
- →1 In deep cold a short outage becomes a long one, because the distribution freezes.
- →2 At Casey both failures arrive together: robots lose capacity permanently from the first unheated rest, and the pipes that would bring the heat back freeze (S3B-24).
- →3 Recovery is measured in weeks while the loss is measured in nights, so a heat failure in the shut half is decided by what was redundant before it happened. *For Phase 1 and Phase 7.* Who was blamed (the local authority) repeats S3B-4: heat that is run by someone has an author when it fails.
- *Divergence:* a human city at about −20°C or lower; Casey's average winter lows are about −18°C (S2-17). Nothing about the real country is carried.

**S3B-8 · Real public heat is switched on by a threshold. At Casey the robot threshold is crossed every night.**
- *Source:* snippets (#19, UNVERIFIED): Fresno opens warming centers only when temperatures are forecast below 35°F three days ahead, and critics noted that hypothermia can occur above 40°F; Denver opens on an advisory below 10°F; Chico below 32°F with no precipitation. Flagstaff's shelter operator lists an "extreme cold warming center".
- *What it did:* **Changed the form, and supports S2-21 (b) and S2-67.** Real public warming is a triggered service. At Casey the robot trigger, rest below freezing, is met on every night of every month on the averages (S2-10), so a triggered service would never switch off: public heated rest at Casey, wherever it exists, is standing, not triggered. And the critics' complaint, that the threshold was set by what feels cold rather than by what harms the body, is the real-world shape of S2-67 (the felt season against the threshold).
- →3 The candidate shadow S2-21 (b), the mild January night when unheated rest feels least risky, has a real pattern behind it: thresholds drawn from how the weather feels. *For Step 7 only; not derived here.*
- *Divergence:* human hypothermia in temperate cities; robots' loss is permanent and binary (Frame L272).

**S3B-24 · Below freezing, water is a heat system.**
- *Source:* snippets (#54, UNVERIFIED): in a permafrost town, water and sewer pipes run above ground inside an insulated box heated by the district-heating line in the same box; the water is tempered, recirculated and bled to keep it from freezing; replacing the old system costs about $80 million, and the town replaces about 500 m a year.
- *What it did:* **Changed S2-20 (c) and S2-13.** The spine adds water's melting to the heat cost (S2-20 →2). The real case shows that, below freezing, carrying water anywhere is also a heat cost, and so is carrying waste water.
- →1 Every pipe that carries water at Casey is part of the heat obligation.
- →2 A heat failure is also a water failure and a sanitation failure.
- →3 The two bodies' needs, which S2-40 counts as two, meet in one system: a human's water and a robot's rest depend on the same heat. *For Phase 1.* **Waste water is a national topic** (`City_and_District_Research_Topics.md` L54, N1; under that file's layering law a pass "may notice it and must hand it up here", L50): handed up, not answered (§6).
- *Divergence:* a permafrost town's pipes; Casey's own water source is unwritten (S2-20).

### RT-3 · Ending the day under continuous light (D3; S2-11, S2-19, S2-24)
