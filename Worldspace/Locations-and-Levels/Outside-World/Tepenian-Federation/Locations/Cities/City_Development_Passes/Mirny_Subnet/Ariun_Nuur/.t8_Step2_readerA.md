## 4 · The three profiles, each written separately, before any comparison (`02` §3, §5.1 item 3)

### 4a · G2 — physical and environmental constraint

*Input set: spec L66, L68, L70, L78–93, L96–99, L103–129 at the contract's dispositions (struck ranking clauses not used); the climate record (entry 9); the stations row (entry 10); the national robot and human physiology as the frame carries it (frame §4 items 4–9; `DEC-697`: no figures).*

**STRENGTH — what the place gives without effort.** (A33)
- **Quiet air.** Mean wind 5.0 m/s, 27 m/s at the strongest (L82, L93); the plateau lacks the slope-to-coast gradient that drives katabatic wind (L127). → the cold arrives without wind to drive it → (second order) exposure is a matter of temperature and time, not of weather events → (third order) **outdoor work can be planned by clock and thermometer, because the air rarely changes the plan.**
- **Weather that tells the truth.** "There is no whiteout-under-clear-sky here — when visibility closes, something is actually falling." (L93). Visibility is lost only to precipitation, and precipitation probability runs ~3–11% a month (L105–116).
- **What falls, stays, and little falls.** 22 mm lands a year, ~90% retention (L83, L87–90). → almost no snow to clear and no drift to bury structures → the surface changes slowly → *derived, to verify at Step 3:* tracks and marks left on the surface last; the outdoors keeps a long record of passage (REQ-A2).
- **Light, in its season.** About 123 days of continuous daylight (L97); halos and pillars, a shimmer on clear days (L128).
- **A held condition beneath.** A lake ~250 × 50 km under ~4 km of ice, sealed an estimated 15–25 million years, liquid because of the ice cap, geothermal heat and pressure (L70); detectable by instruments from the surface (L129). The place sits on something that exists only here **as a physical fact of this site** (stated flatly, no ranking).
- **An ice surface aircraft can land on:** the site carries a real skiway, surface "Ice" (stations :: L67). Physical infrastructure only (`DR-24`); its operator is a coordinate and is not used.
- **Altitude costs the majority body nothing:** robots do not breathe (frame §4 item 4).

**DEFICIT — what it structurally cannot do.** Each with its characteristic failure, "what the deficit was supposed to prevent". (A34)
- **It never thaws.** Warmest monthly mean −31.3 °C (Dec), record high −14.0 °C (L80–81, L116, L105). → no liquid water outdoors in any month → every liter is melted ice, and melting is heat → (third order) **water is an energy product: drinking, washing and a robot's coolant (frame §4 item 7) all draw on the same account as warmth.** *Characteristic failure:* the deficit that would prevent waste (water is hard to get) produces waste of the scarcer thing, heat, wherever water is used without being counted as energy.
- **No ground.** About 4 km of ice over the lake (L70); the industry run's own basis puts bedrock ~4 km down (16 :: L2353). No soil, no exposed rock in any admitted line. → nothing grows outdoors → food comes from inside or from elsewhere (row 22). *Characteristic failure:* the forced dependence on outside food, which ought to make supply a shared concern, makes it nobody's: it is the model's coefficient, not a person's task.
- **No sea.** No coastal access (L66). Nothing arrives by water.
- **No sky clock for most of the year.** ~120 days of polar night and ~123 of midnight sun (L96–97): about 243 days with no sunrise or sunset; in polar night "the day/night split narrows toward weather noise" (L118). → time is kept by clock alone → (third order) **the rhythm of the place is whatever the institution that keeps the clock decides.** *Characteristic failure:* a place with no natural cue for rest should protect rest by rule; without a rule, nobody's day ends.
- **No landmark.** "flat, featureless" (L68). → outdoors, distance and direction cannot be read from the land → (third order) the edge of the settled area is a line someone has to mark. *Characteristic failure:* the plainness that makes everyone careful makes being lost easy for whoever is not.
- **The human body cannot be at home here unaided.** At the record cold "the human body's thermal regulation capacity is simply overwhelmed" (L126); at 3,488 m the altitude cost falls on the human 35.50% only (frame DL-14).

**STANDING COST — what it must pay continuously or stop being itself.** (A35)
- **Heat, the maintenance that never ends** (02 §3.1 Q1): mean −54.8 °C (L79), no month above a −31.3 °C mean. It regenerates the moment it is paid. **Who pays:** everyone, invisibly, in walls, water and charge (Q2). **What fails first if it lapses for a week** (Q3): the water supply (it is melted ice), then the robots' charge (charging in the cold loses capacity, frame §4 item 5; a charge draws on the electric supply, warming a cold cell included, `DR-140` item 4), then the humans. **Where the heat comes from is not written** (`DEC-705`; §9).
- **Altitude support for the human minority** (3,488 m, L66; frame DL-14), carried by the whole place for the 35.50% who need it.
- **The majority body's cold upkeep** (frame §4 items 5 and 7, words only, `DEC-697`): capacity loss when charging in the cold, reduced capacity, output sag under load, embrittlement and stiff lubricants (also L126: "exposed metal becomes brittle; lubricants fail"); keeping warm burns siligel. A robot is safer moving than resting.
- **Correction for the moving ice** (frame DL-3): anything fixed into the ice sheet sits in a medium that flows. No figure is on the list (REQ-A2). Recorded as a candidate standing cost, not asserted.
- **Snow clearance is close to absent** (22 mm, ~90% retention): a standing cost the place does *not* have to pay. Recorded as a near-absence.

**GRUDGING TOLERANCE — what it permits but makes expensive.** (A36)
- **Going out on a calm, clear day.** Nobody forbids it; the air is still, the sky shimmers (L93, L128), and the cold is the same as on any other day of that month. **The day that looks safest carries the full cost with no warning from the weather.** Does anyone perceive the cost as a cost? - **Being alone on the ice.** Permitted; featureless (L68) and lethal if anything goes wrong.
