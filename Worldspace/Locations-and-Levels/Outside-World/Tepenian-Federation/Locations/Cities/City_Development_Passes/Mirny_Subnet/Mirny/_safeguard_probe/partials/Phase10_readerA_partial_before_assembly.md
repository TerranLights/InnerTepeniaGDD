Mirny is a city of 1,351,430 (Census I: 665,901 humans, 685,529 robots) on a rocky, exposed coast that sits on the Antarctic Circle, where the defining fact is not cold or dark but wind: a mean near 12 m/s, storms above 40, almost no calm, ground blizzards under a clear sky, and drift that rebuilds in the lee of everything the city puts up. The sun rises every day of the year and does not set for about four weeks around midsummer, so the calendar is honest and the hour is not, and the sea is a surface for about ten months and open water for a window that nobody can schedule. The founders (exiles from Russia, Australia and the Sinian Federation, under Idelsk-Uralia's sponsorship) were handed a working station and centuries of journals, logs and manuals, but no living teacher, and the one thing the site demands, judgment of the wind, is the one thing that could not be inherited. The city is a hub before it is anything else: the relay through which its whole subnet talks to itself, the yards that turn southern stone into road material and machinery, the raw-material source for chambers made one step away, and a harbor whose call opens and shuts the sea.
