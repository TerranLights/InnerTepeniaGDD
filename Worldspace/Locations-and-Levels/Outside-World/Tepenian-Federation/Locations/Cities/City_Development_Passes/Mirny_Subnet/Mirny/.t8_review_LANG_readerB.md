## 2 · What no longer stands

### F-3 · the native layer "met without translation"

**The change, on its own line.** DRL L1895, item (6): "Hawaiian is a pidgin with a primary base of English and Japanese and a
supporting base of Korean, Tagalog, Ilocano and Mexican Spanish; later, influence from a wide variety of languages made Hawaiian
become Tepenian (so they are one language over time)." It lands on a frame that already stood before the cut-off: DRL L1669,
"**(1)** Hawaiian (later Tepenian) is the dominant language everywhere (… this makes it general)", and P08 L269, "at Mirny it is the
dominant, civic language for both bodies from the founding on."

**Pass lines that narrow:**
1. **Phase 2 L283:** "- These are **the only layer every resident, in either body and from any stock, meets without translation.**"
   **Why:** the line is silent about a second common layer that canon places at Mirny for both bodies from the founding on (P08 L269)
   and that A1 now defines as a real language with named bases and one life across both Acts (DRL L1895). Read beside it, "the only
   layer" invites the reading that Mirny's residents share no common speech, which canon contradicts. **The exclusivity itself
   survives on a strict reading** (correction (a) below): the civic tongue is not met without translation by every resident from any
   stock. So the line narrows by scope; it is not struck.
2. **Phase 2 L283 to L284:** "At the founding the shared medium was **site, record and relay, not a culture**; whatever is common
   must be built on them." **Why:** the civic tongue is a shared medium of speech at Mirny "from the founding on" (P08 L269), and it
   is common without being built on the site, the record or the relay; it is national. The list "site, record and relay" is no longer
   the whole of the shared medium, and "whatever is common must be built on them" holds only for what is common **and Mirny's own**
   (P08 L307: "What is Mirny's in speech is the conditions vocabulary of its own wind"). "Not a culture" survives (P08 L273: "the
   rulings fix the dominant *language*, not the dominant culture").
3. **Not caused by A1, on the same bullet:** Phase 2 L280 to L281, "**Record:** journals, logs and manuals no founding stock wrote,
   with *"no living layer writing back into it"* (`RES` §6)", is one of the "these" of L283. Its presence is met without
   translation; its text is in the language of the station's in-world owner at the Treaty, who is unidentified (P08 L278, L296), and
   "*if* the record's language differs from the civic language, reading it is a switch [D]" (P08 L310). A1 names the civic tongue's
   bases, not the record's language, so it does not decide this (AR-38 asks for the owner).

**Corrections to the F-3 item reader (`.review_F-3_reader.md`):**

  the civic tongue qualifies too" (its L104), and its IQ-301 option B rests on that. On my admitted lines the civic tongue is had by

## 3 · The re-derivation

### C1 (F-3) · The layer met without translation, beside the civic tongue

**Proposed finding, as it stands now (PROPOSED):** *The place's own conditions (wind and drift, light, the hub's relay, the record as
a found thing, the ring and sheltered core, the fast ice and its window, the coastal rock) are the only layer every resident, in
either body and from any stock, meets with no language between her and it. Beside them, and not part of the native layer, stands the
civic tongue: Hawaiian (later Tepenian, one language over time), a pidgin with a primary base of English and Japanese and a
supporting base of Korean, Tagalog, Ilocano and Mexican Spanish, dominant at Mirny for both bodies from the founding on. It is common
and national, not native: a human born here learns it, a robot's dominant half reads it, and anyone who arrives without it learns it.
Whatever is common and Mirny's own is built on the site, the record and the relay; the civic tongue is common without being built on
them.*

- **First order [ADM].** Canon now defines the civic tongue (DRL L1895, item 6) on a frame that already placed it at Mirny: dominant
  everywhere (DRL L1669 item 1), dominant and civic at Mirny for both bodies from the founding on (P08 L269), and "What the city shares
  in speech is the civic language plus a conditions vocabulary of its own wind" (P08 L304). **So Mirny has two common layers, one of
  conditions and one of speech.**
- **Second order [D].** The two layers reach residents differently. The conditions reach every resident on arrival and without
  condition: wind, drift, light, relay, ice and rock need no tongue (Phase 2 L276 to L282). The civic tongue reaches a human born here
  by childhood learning (P08 L297, citing DRL L1118; the pass's citation, not checked at source), a robot by derivation (P08 L301,
  conditional on AR-40), and anyone else by learning (P9 L175 admits such a resident). With 36 stocks and no majority, Mirny's
  household and build-community languages are "plural … and no one of those languages is the city's" (P08 L304). **So the civic
  tongue is the medium in which the stocks' material passes between residents of different stocks: Mirny's means of translation, not
  a layer met without one.** "The only … without translation" holds once its scope is stated; the line's fault is silence.
- **Third order [D].** The language-free layer enters language at the moment it is named, and at Mirny that naming is the one thing
  P08 L307 calls Mirny's own in speech ("the conditions vocabulary of its own wind"). Phase 2 L292 already gives its form: wind names
  "re-attached to whatever local wind filled the role: **re-attachment, not transplant**", and "which words attach is Phase 8, not
  written". A1 names the languages the civic tongue was formed between; it does not say in which tongue the wind's words are made, or
  whether re-attached names come from the civic tongue, from the supporting tier (English, Russian: DRL L1769) or from a household
  language. **So the point where Mirny's one untranslated layer meets its defined civic tongue is the vocabulary of the wind, and which
  tongue that vocabulary is in is an open slot** (P08 L279: "Every word" open; P08 L280: the protocol's language open, AR-45).
- **A strand from "from any stock" [ADM + D].** Of Mirny's two supporting-tier languages (DRL L1769), English is in the civic tongue's
  primary base and Russian is in neither base list (DRL L1895). `DR-111` says the 12-point rule "by actual speakers gives English
  alone" (L1769); the rule's set is "every language group within 12 percentage points of its largest group" (L1669 item 3), so it
  always holds the largest group, and **by actual speakers English is Mirny's largest language group** [D on L1669 and L1769; I did
  not see the speaker figures]. So the language of Mirny's largest group is one of the two the civic tongue was primarily formed
  between. **Whether that makes the step from household speech to civic speech shorter for some residents than for others is in no
  admitted line, turns on what a base supplies (not stated), and is not derived here: open slot.** No characterization rests on a
  gap between rows (`DR-130` item 7); none is drawn.
- **Act [ADM + D].** Both layers run through both Acts. The conditions are physics. The civic tongue is "one language over time"
  (DRL L1895), and its change to Tepenian carries no date or Act (P08 L269; AR-43). Beside it, in Act 1 how much talk at home is in
  another language "is a spread" and in Act 2 "a household language persists or recedes household by household" (P08 L305). A1's
  "later, influence from a wide variety of languages" widens the civic tongue's sources over time; **whether any language spoken at
  Mirny is among those later influences is not stated: open slot.**
- **Who arrives without it [ADM via the note + D].** The founders on the day of founding (whether they arrived speaking the civic
  tongue is in no admitted line: open slot) and, now that canon lets humans keep arriving after the treaty (the note L24, row A3),
  any later human arrival from Upper Earth. Whether any reached Mirny is the `ARR` piece's question; nothing here rests on it.
- **Survives:** Phase 2 L283's claim about the conditions, scoped; L284's "not a culture"; L285 ("The native layer at Phase 2 is these
  conditions plus **the founders' first improvisations**, not a culture"), since the civic tongue is national and does not enter the
  native layer; L271 to L272 ("**no default common culture in Act 1**"); L295 ("A shared explicit medium is native; what is written in
  it, in which words, is open."); P08 L273, L304, L307.
- **Changes:** L283 takes a scope and a companion sentence naming the civic tongue; L284's "the shared medium was site, record and
  relay" gains the civic tongue as a shared medium of speech, national; "whatever is common must be built on them" narrows to what is
  common and Mirny's own.
- **Open slots (written, not filled):** what a base supplies; which tongue the wind's vocabulary is in (AR-45); whether the founders
  arrived speaking the civic tongue; the record's language (AR-38); whether later influences on the civic tongue include any language
  spoken at Mirny; what English's double place does to speech at Mirny.
- **One-location test.** Every sentence above is about Mirny; none names another city, so each survives the deletion test. The base
  list names languages, quoted from canon, and is used as a fact Mirny lives with (relation), never as a measure.

### C2 (F-5) · The caution on English authoring labels

**Proposed finding, as it stands now (PROPOSED):** *Caution. At Mirny English stands in two ruled places: it is one of the two
supporting-tier languages (English and Russian, `DR-111`), and canon names it a primary base of the civic tongue (`DR-125` item 6). So
an English authoring label in this catalog could be read as an attested in-world name in the supporting tier, or as a word of the
civic tongue. Every label stays an authoring label for an admitted referent, in English, never attested speech (the Phase 8 piece,
L316), and never a place name. What a base gives the civic tongue is not stated, and no label here claims that any English word is a
civic-tongue word.*

- **First order [ADM].** The tier: DRL L1769 ("English and Russian"). The base: DRL L1895 ("a primary base of English and Japanese").
  The rule: P08 L316 ("*Every label is an authoring label for an admitted referent, in English, **never attested speech**, and must
  never become a place name. No word is coined in any language.*"), restated at P08 L348.
- **Second order [D].** The catalog's authoring language coincides with ruled speech at Mirny on two layers: one set for Mirny by
  name (the tier) and one national (the civic tongue's base, true wherever the civic tongue is spoken). **The hazard has two routes;
  the rule that answers it carries no tier ground (P08 L316) and answers both, unchanged.** No label is in Russian, or in any other
  base language: **none is sited here**, so no parallel caution arises.
- **Third order [D].** When the developer names (R-118, P10 L395), naming now has to say more than "which language": for a name of
  English origin, "the dominant" and "the supporting tier" overlap by origin, so the naming also says in which tongue the name is
  meant to be spoken. And since English is, by actual speakers, Mirny's largest language group (C1's strand, [D]), the catalog's
  English labels sit in the language of that group, a supporting-tier language and a base of the civic tongue at once; **the rule
  "never attested speech" is the one thing that keeps an authoring label from being read as any of the three.** P10 L393, "the
  catalog coins no word in any language", now also covers words a reader might take as civic-tongue words.
- **Survives:** the rule clause of P10 L394; P08 L316 and L348; P10 L393.
- **Changes:** the ground clause of P10 L394; the tier claim at P10 L392 and P9 L174, L176 (`DR-111`); the tag at P10 L391; a note on
  R-118 at P10 L395.
- **Open slots:** what a base supplies; whether any English authoring label coincides with an actual civic-tongue word, which cannot
  be known while no word is coined (the language is synthesized in its own repository: P08 L279; P10 L393).
- **One-location test.** No sentence names another city; each survives. The national half of the hazard is a relation (the civic
  tongue Mirny speaks), not a measure.
