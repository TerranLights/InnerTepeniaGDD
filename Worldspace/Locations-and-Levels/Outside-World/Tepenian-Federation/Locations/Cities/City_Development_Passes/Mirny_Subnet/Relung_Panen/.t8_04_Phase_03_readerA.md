## F · Retroactive Mechanism

- **P3A-19 · What is simply true and unexplained: the basin's fresh water and its living things lie at its edges against the ice, and its center is salt and grit. The physical system that would produce exactly that: the ice walls are the basin's well.** **Simply true, on the list:** fresh lakes lie in the south, along the periphery and on higher ground, saline ones low, in the north and on the islands (S3-11, research L124–125, L135); plants (algae, fungi, mosses, lichens) are most abundant at the ice margins and sparse in the salty, wind-abraded center (research L446, L573; S3-57); glacier contact decides which lakes keep their cover (spec L205–207). **Nothing on the list says why.** **The system, PROPOSED:** the basin is walled by ice on every side (L113–116), and the sky gives it only about 92 mm ESTIMATED (L157 `[DEM]`) with little lying snow (L109); its abundant fresh water therefore comes from the walls, as glacier and ice-sheet melt into the margin lakes (the river system is fed by the ice sheet, Apfel Glacier and snow banks, research L337; the gulf by Algae Lake's outflow and by Apfel and Edisto melt, research L186, L338), while the center receives wind-borne marine salt from the bays (research L315; saline lakes' salts wind-borne or relict marine, research L124). Fresh margin, salt center. **Second-order consequence:** the city's softness has to be fetched from its walls or made: its winter water comes from deep lakes under ice (S3-31), likeliest toward the fresh margins; its native green is there; and the margins are also where ice-margin lakes drain in outbursts (S3-34, research L358). **So the city's water works run toward the one hazard the basin's center lacks.** **Third order:** wherever the city stands (unruled, L142), its water flows in from the ice edge and its drainage flows out toward the low saline basins, which accumulate salt, or through the river system to the gulf where its own air doors stand (S3-32, S3-33, research L343–344; RF-81 reserved): the city sits between its source and its sink, and what it adds (heat, nutrients, salt) moves downhill toward its own doors. *Expression (seen, done):* the green and the open water are at the edges, where the ice is; the middle of the basin is grit and salt crust; a water line from the city runs toward the walls. *(G2; G7 through Step 3.)*
