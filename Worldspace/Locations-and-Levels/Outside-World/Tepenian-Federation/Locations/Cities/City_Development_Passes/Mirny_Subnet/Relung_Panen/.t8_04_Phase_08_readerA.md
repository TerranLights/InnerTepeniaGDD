## The differentiation axis (Process D)

**Shared kit, household forms.**

*What the words carry.* On this place's own lines, **what everyone here makes and uses in common is the kit the place's year demands**, and **the origin forms of 32 stocks stay in households**. No origin row is shared by one person in seven (largest 12.86%, spec L59; Phase 2 P2-5), so no row's food, music or dress is the city's default by weight; at a height in the Census I era, nearer Act 1 (`DEC-583`), those forms are still lived, "in households and small groups" (Phase 2 P2-23), and kept where an institution holds them, none being on the list (S3-23; Phase 2 P2-31). What every household shares instead is made from this place's demands, each on its own lines:
1. **the threshold kit for still, dry cold:** coat and gloves on and off at every door for the nine to ten months whose mean is below freezing (spec L180–189), on most days without much wind (spec L143), with a layer carried for the storm episode that comes with hours of warning (S3-15; Phase 3 P3-14; Phase 4 P4-7);
2. **the melt kit, December to February:** feet dressed for water by day and refrozen slush by morning, and a second pair of gloves (spec L171–172, L179–180, L190 `[computed, REQ-52]`; Phase 3 P3-15; Phase 4 P4-13);
3. **the dark made by hand and the lamp:** a curtain against a December sun that barely sets, lamps in a June of about 2.7 h (spec L151–156; S3-28; Phase 4 P4-4);
4. **the warm charging room, every month,** for the half of the people who are robots (frame S0-10; spine S2-9 `[computed, REQ-52]`; S3-27; Phase 4 P4-6);
5. **the tools of the year's keeping:** heat and picks at the culverts before the melt (S3-4; Phase 3 P3-36);
6. **the city's own lit produce, the same in every month** (spec L258; `18` L12, L19 `[DoI corroboration]`; Phase 4 P4-3).
Beside them, and **national, not this place's,** one common tongue, Hawaiian (later Tepenian) (`DR-100` item 1).

*Peer-free test, could this be true anywhere?* No. It needs three things at once, each on this place's own lines: **no origin default by weight** (32 rows, the largest 12.86%, spec L57–90); **a height at which origin forms are still lived** (`DEC-583`); and **a year whose demands are this place's own:** a still, dry cold for nine to ten months, a genuine melt on bare rock whose average day crosses freezing for three (spec L143, L171–172, L179–190, L203), no polar night but a June day of 2.7 h and a December sun that barely sets (L151–156), and a robot half that must charge warm in every month. Give one row a majority and its forms become the city's default; move the height into Act 2 and the household forms become ancestry and the split dissolves; take away the melt and the wet kit goes; give the place a polar night or a dark summer and the curtain and the lamp change places; take away the still air and the storm layer becomes the everyday coat. **What could be true anywhere, and so is not the axis:** cold-weather clothing in itself (physiology L38–57, national); Hawaiian (national); the shared night (national, physiology L336–338); siligel and coolant (national); indoor lit growing on its own (spec L258: "as everywhere"); household-kept origin forms in any many-origin place on their own. The axis rests on admitted, measured and ruled lines and on `DEC-583`; the daily zero-crossing and the every-month clauses carry `[computed, REQ-52]`; it does not rest on `[PA-12]`, the held ground, animals or `[PROV-8]` (the warm charge rests on the cold file and the computed columns, not on the supply). It answers a different question from the pass's earlier axes: not when the day stops or what the year is ordered by, but **what the city makes in common and where everything else it makes is kept.** The axis goes to this location's own row of the differentiation instrument at Step 6, write-only; no other row was read.
