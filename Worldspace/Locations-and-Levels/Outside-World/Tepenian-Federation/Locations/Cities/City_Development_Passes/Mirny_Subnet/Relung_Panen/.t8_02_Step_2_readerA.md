### G2 · Physical and environmental constraint

**STRENGTH: what the place does well without trying**
- **G2-S1 · Room. [A] `[held: DQ-22]`** About 450 km² of bare rock, about 942 km² with marine inlets and lakes, in a box about 41 km north–south by 51 km east–west (spec L30, L105–107). On its own totals the oasis holds 3,375,000 at its comfortable band and 20,780,100 at a real-world density (L120–121); 1,000,071 is 29.6% of the first. *Recorded as the admitted lines' own words. No finding below rests on it* (Step 1 L191).
- **G2-S2 · Water, in liquid and in volume. [A]** "many freshwater and saline lakes"; Algae Lake is 25 km long and up to 137 m deep (spec L109–110; its superlative is unverified, contract REQ-9). The year has "standing meltwater, and lakes that open" (L203). *First order:* fresh water exists as lakes, not only as ice. *Second:* at a lake 137 m deep, liquid water very probably persists beneath the winter cover (an inference from depth alone; Step 3 checks it, S2A-RT-5), so the place does not have to make all its water by melting ice with heat. *Third:* the scarce things here are therefore not water but what water cannot supply (see G2-D2, G2-D3).
- **G2-S3 · A calm mean. [A] `[DEM]` `[PA-3]`** The annual mean wind is 4.6 m/s at the AWS station; the basin sits "out of the katabatic drainage" (L143; L144 DEM). The central-oasis record shows calms in 39% of observations, "mostly still, with sharp storm episodes" (L146 DEM). *First:* on most days the air outside is still. *Second:* ordinary outdoor work and movement are possible most of the time. *Third:* the norm a resident learns is that outside is usable (this sets up G2-T1).
- **G2-S4 · Light every day. [A]** No polar night: the sun rises every day; the June minimum is about 2.7 h, and the December disc barely sets for a few days, giving about 24 h of refracted light (spec L151–156). The mean annual solar flux is 115 W/m², measured (`Climate.md` L22). *First:* there is no season without a day. *Second:* the year's rhythm is a swing of light from 2.7 h to about 24 h, not an absence. *Third:* in June the day is short but present, and in late December there is almost no dark at all, so whatever the place uses to mark night must be set by people, not by the sky, for part of the year (a prompt for the day's structure, `DR-137` item 8; frame S0-25).
- **G2-S5 · Months above freezing. [A] `[PA-3]`** The January mean is +0.6 °C, "above freezing in the mean" (L179); summer peak days can exceed +10 °C (`Climate.md` L26, measured literature). The annual mean is −11.2 °C at the AWS station, or −9.0 / −8.8 °C at the central oasis (L142).

**DEFICIT: what the place structurally cannot do**
- **G2-D1 · Reach the sea. [A]** The Shackleton Ice Shelf seals the oasis from open sea by 64–145 km; the gulf is "a tidal epishelf lake connected to the ocean beneath the ice, but nothing shows a ship could pass" (spec L13, L116). *First:* the sea is present as tide and absent as a route. *Second:* nothing heavy can arrive by water from the sea side. *Third:* the place's coast is a coast in geography only. **Characteristic failure** (`02` L373): what a sea route would have prevented is total dependence on what can come by air or over the ice, so the characteristic failure (derived, not written anywhere) is the stranding of anything too heavy to fly whenever the overland way cannot carry it.
- **G2-D2 · Make soil. [A]** "extensive dry valleys with little lying snow" on bare rock (L109); "no pre-existing farmland or soil" (`DR-61` L1252). Growing cannot begin from the ground as found.
- **G2-D3 · Supply its own energy. [A]** "No geothermal, no measured wind resource; no grid line exists on Antarctic ice" (L260–261). The ground gives room, water and summer light, and gives no power. *First:* the place cannot heat or light itself from its own ground. *Second:* every hour of heat and light in winter is brought in or made from brought-in fuel. *Third:* the place's free gifts (room, water, light) are all gifts that need power before they can feed anyone (this is G2's own reading of its gifts; the function is G3's profile).

**STANDING COST: what the place must keep doing or stop being itself**
- **G2-C1 · Winter heat. [A]** The coldest month is August at −18.7 °C (L149, L186); the winter's measured freezing degree-days are 3,987 (`Climate.md` L24); the record low is about −39 °C, ESTIMATED (L147–148 DEM). *The maintenance that never ends:* keeping people and machines above the cold for seven months whose monthly means are all below −13 °C (April to October, spec L182–188). *Who pays, and is it visible:* everyone, invisibly, in fuel and power. For the half of the population that is robot, warmth is paid in siligel, since processing siligel is exothermic (`DR-137` item 5) and canon's cold costs fall in this range (frame S0-24: usable capacity of 50–60% by −20 °C; permanent loss from recharging below freezing). *First to fail if it lapses for a week:* heat to dwellings and water lines; for robots, capacity.
- **G2-C2 · Summer water. [A]** "a real melt season on bare ground. Meltwater, saturated regolith, active drainage, freeze–thaw and slush are the engineering problem" (L171–172); 71 °C-days above freezing (L203; `Climate.md` L23, measured); freeze–thaw on exposed rock (L204 DEM). *The maintenance that never ends:* drainage, ground that softens and heaves, and anything built on or in the active layer, renewed every summer. *Who pays:* whoever keeps the ground: its paths, foundations, pipes and drains. No admitted line names the trade, so whether this cost is carried by a named trade or by everyone is open (`02` L389–391). *First to fail if it lapses for a week in January:* drains block, standing water spreads, slush covers paths, and footings on saturated ground move. **The finding (S2A-3): the place has two standing costs of different kinds, heat in winter and water in summer, so its maintenance changes character twice a year.** The season that is easiest to be outside in (G2-S3, G2-S5) is the season in which the ground itself must be fought.

**GRUDGING TOLERANCE: what the place permits but makes expensive**
- **G2-T1 · Going out in the usual calm. [A] `[DEM]` `[PA-3]`** The central-oasis record: 122 days a year at 15 m/s or more, blizzards on 53 days, a 56 m/s peak (L146 DEM); the wind is "EASTERLY, and fierce in event" (L143). Nobody forbids going out, and on most days it is safe (G2-S3). The cost falls on whoever is out when an episode arrives. *Who does it anyway:* anyone whose work or life is outdoors, which the calm mean invites. *Is the cost perceived as a cost?* The usual stillness makes a storm-caught person read as someone who misjudged a safe place, so the cost is likely to be read as a personal choice and not as a property of the place. **That is the shadow structure of `02` §3.2 (L412–414), offered as a candidate, not asserted:** an authorless harm whose victims are blamed for trusting the place's own norm.
- **G2-T2 · Crossing lakes in the melt season. [A]** "Perennially ice-covered lakes sit alongside seasonally open ones … glacier contact decides which lakes keep their cover" (L205–207). Nothing forbids walking on lake ice; in the melt season, some lakes hold and some open, and which is which depends on a feature (glacier contact) that has to be known. *Conditional:* no admitted line says the lakes are used as paths; if they are, the cost falls on whoever does not know which lakes hold.
- **G2-T3 · A robot outdoors in winter. [A]** Canon's summary puts usable capacity at 50–60% by −20 °C, permanent loss from recharging below freezing, and embrittlement below −40 °C (frame S0-24); the coldest-month mean is −18.7 °C and the estimated record low about −39 °C (L147–149). Nothing forbids it; it costs capacity and siligel, and past an unwritten threshold it can kill (`DR-137` item 4). No pass writes the temperature.
