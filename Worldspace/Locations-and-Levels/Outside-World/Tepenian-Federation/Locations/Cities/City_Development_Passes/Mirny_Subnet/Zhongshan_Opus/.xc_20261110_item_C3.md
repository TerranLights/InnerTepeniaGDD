   Russian-named rows are separate origins in every city"* (note L44). A re-derivation uses the rulings as they stand (D-6, note
   L42).
2. **Rows from regions the pass's list does not cover are now separate origins:** Siberia 0.71% (`spec` L56), Künnarantaiga 0.41%

3. **The real-world climates of those source regions** (searched, not recalled; see the search log):
   - **Siberia:** a tundra belt along the north, up to about 500 km wide, which corresponds to *"regions of polar climate"* with a
     6 to 10 week growing season and *"long, cold, dark winters,"* with continuous permafrost under the whole Asian tundra zone
     (search 1). The Sakha (Yakut) life-support complex is described in the literature as having emerged largely *because* of

     tundra vegetation (search 8). But most of Siberia's people live in the south, along the railway (one source: 75% in a few

   - **Finland:** mostly subarctic (Köppen Dfc; a weak source), with the polar-night north (up to about 50 days of polar night in
     the northernmost municipality) holding about 3% of the people (searches 2 and 10).
   - **Tuva:** extreme cold-continental, not polar by the Köppen test (capital: January mean about −28.7 °C, record −54 °C;
     classed BSk bordering Dwb, one source Dfc) (search 3; Köppen polar test, search 11).
   - **North Caucasus:** continental and arid lowlands, colder with altitude in the mountains, on the border of temperate and
     subtropical; not polar (search 6).
   - **Künnarantaiga:** no real-world match (search 9). It is an in-world successor name, and no line on my list says what ground

### Reasoning

1. **The pass's measure is legal and still applies.** Climate assumptions are a structural feature under the pass's own Guard 1
   (L234-236). Running the same measure on today's table is what D-6 asks.
2. **"FAR" holds, and more strongly.** Every added row brings a region the five named regions (L238) did not span: Northern Asia,
   the Caucasus, Northern Europe. No change narrows the spread. The lower bounds at L239-240 (*"at least three"*, *"several"*) are
   not falsified by additions. So R4's verdict (L232) and Technique 18's *"SHALLOW BUT BROAD"* (L245-246) still follow.
3. **The climate list at L240-241 no longer covers the table.** It names tropical, temperate-continental, maritime-temperate and
   subtropical only. Today the table also carries subarctic and extreme cold-continental source practice (Siberia's southern belt,
   Finland, Tuva), highland source practice (the Caucasus rows, Tuva's mountains), and, in the northern parts of two source
   regions, **polar** source practice (Siberia's tundra coast; Finland's polar-night north).
4. **L243's categorical sentence no longer follows.** *"Not one"* cannot stand while a separate origin (D-6) comes from a source
   region that contains polar climate and a documented practice complex built around cold (search 5). Its opposite, however,
   follows **only in part**: whether the Siberia row *as a whole* brought a practice set that assumes a polar climate turns on what
   territory and whose practices that row carries, since most of the real region's people live in a climate that is
   cold-continental rather than polar (search 4). What does follow in full is weaker and still new: **a cold-climate practice set
   (deep winter cold, frozen ground, long dark winters) is now present in the roster**, which the pass's ground ruled out.
   - *Note on mechanism (an inference from L238 and L240-241, not from a line I read about it):* under the earlier single Russian
     row (note L123-124), the pass's lists have room for that row only as *"Central and Eastern Europe"* and
     *"temperate-continental."* The D-6 split brings Siberia and the other cold rows to the surface as origins of their own; so the
     change comes from the rulings and the table together, not from a new fact about the site.
5. **And what does that cause? (first order).** The premise under L249 and L884, that *nothing inherited fit this ground*, gets a
   qualification: for cold-living practice specifically, a small inherited source is now possible.
6. **(Second order) `DR-12` cuts both ways here.** A re-derivation cannot dismiss the cold rows by setting 0.71 or 0.021 against
   larger Notable rows such as 3.66; that would rest on a within-tier difference. Nor can it promote them into the source of the
   commons on their weight; that would rest on the same figures. What `DR-12` leaves as information is the **tier**: the Notable
   tier now carries rows from cold, partly polar ground; the Primary and Significant tiers carry none. So the legal statement is
   about presence and tier, with the outcome left open (the binding law against forced fit: neither force a story in which a
   small group taught everyone to live in the cold, nor deny that it could happen). This touches item C2 (the pass's floor for rows
   too small to show, R7), which is not on my list; I do not judge it.
7. **(Third order) does the commons now have a homeland?** No, not on these inputs. (a) The plurality (35.83, which on the pass's
   own L885 *"cannot impose"*) and the three Significant rows (24.10) bring no polar practice set; the current input changes
   nothing there. (b) Any cold-fitting source is confined to the Notable tier, and `DR-12` forbids resting its influence on its
   figures. (c) Even the polar parts of the source practice assume a land ecosystem (tundra pasture for reindeer, game, river fish;
   search 8) that this ground does not offer: the note puts 87.99% to 91.59% of the city's footprint on ice (note L92, ZSO-013,
   arithmetic by D-6). So transfer would be partial at most. **The axis L882, "THE COMMONS HAD NO HOMELAND," survives in
   direction**; its stated ground at L884 (*"not one of which brought a practice set that assumes a polar climate"*) does not.
8. **(Fourth order) the seams.** If some cold-living practice did pass from a small origin to everyone, that seam would still be
   visible (a known source, a known borrowing), so *"load-bearing rather than hidden"* (L886) is not moved. The chain stops moving
   the verdict here.
9. **L947.** Its count (*"Eighteen origins"*) is a figure and changes. Its consequence (*"so the commons had to be built here"*)
   stands as in item 7. Its middle clause (*"no two tuning or interval systems structurally compatible"*) is outside this item's
   climate question and is not tested here; the D-6 split (twelve rows from one former row) and the German pair (note L112) put it
   in question, and it is raised as C3-Q4.
10. **Why NARROWS and not the other two.** Not HOLDS: more than a figure or a label changes; the sentence at L243 and the clause at
    L884 fail as written, and the climate list at L240-241 is incomplete. Not FLIPS: the direction (far stock; a commons built
    mostly on this ground; no homeland in any large row) still follows, and the opposite (the commons had a homeland) does not.
