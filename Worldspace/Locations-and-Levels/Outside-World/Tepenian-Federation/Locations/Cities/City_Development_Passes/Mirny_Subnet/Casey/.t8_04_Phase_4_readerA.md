## B · Routines

**P4A-5 · Robots: every day ends in a heated place, in every month.** *The phase's strongest finding.*
- *Canon:* "**1. Physical — they require downtime, most prominently OVERNIGHT RECHARGING.**" (physiology L334); "Recharging below freezing causes permanent capacity loss" (Frame L272); every month's average low is below freezing, from −2.6 °C in January to −18.5 °C in May (spec L132-143). Safe cold charging runs at 5-10% of capacity, "ten to twenty times slower" (cold L26).
- →1 Every robot at Casey, 786,985 at Census I (spec L14), ends every day of every month in a heated place, and the routine has no season off (S2-13).
- →2 The shared night ("Robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons.", physiology L336) is at Casey kept by the heat, not by the dark: from about 13 November to 30 January no night is darker than civil twilight (S3-14), so the robot's stop is set by where the heat is, not by when the sun goes (S3-38).
- →3 **A robot's day at Casey is shaped toward its end:** where she will rest tonight decides how far she can go today, in every month, because an unheated night is not a discomfort but a permanent loss. A day out is planned backward from a heated place. *Expression:* a robot leaving home already knows where she will recharge that night; a robot without that answer is the person in trouble (P4A-32 item 1).

**P4A-6 · Robots: the working day outdoors is a gradient, and the stop is the danger.**
- *Canon:* reversible capacity loss, 20-30% near freezing and down to 50-60% at −20 °C (cold L63); "A cold robot cannot lift, sprint or strike as hard as a warm one at identical charge." (cold L72); "⭐ **This is the exact opposite of human cold survival, where you shelter, still yourself and conserve.**" (cold L95). Robots wear coats and gloves, and the glove trade-off is a gradient (physiology L38-57). Average winter lows −18.0 to −18.5 °C sit 1.5-2.0 °C above the −20 °C line (S2-17).
- →1 A robot works outdoors at Casey on a smaller tank in winter, returned when she warms; on ordinary winter days she is not capacity-limited while moving (S2-8).
- →2 What she must not do outdoors in the cold is wait: an outdoor pause cools her joints and cells, so her routine holds no standing still between heated places.
- →3 The outdoor working day for a robot is continuous motion between warm points, and an interrupted job (a broken tool, a held road, a blizzard) is dangerous in a way it is not for a human, who would shelter and keep still. *Expression:* a robot crew works through what would be a break, then breaks indoors.

**P4A-7 · Humans: exposure is counted in minutes, and the routine is door to door.** [present-day; physical]
- *Source:* Environment Canada's wind-chill bands, as the national occupational-safety agency reproduces them: at a wind chill of −28 to −39, "exposed skin can freeze in 10 to 30 minutes"; at −40 to −47, in 5 to 10 minutes (**sourced**, P4A-W11). The index's formula, WC = 13.12 + 0.6215·T − 11.37·V^0.16 + 0.3965·T·V^0.16, with V the wind at 10 m in km/h (**snippet**, an archived Environment Canada page, P4A-W13; its own check, −20 °C at 30 km/h gives −33, recomputes to −32.6).
- *Casey's figures, computed:* July's average low (−18.4 °C, spec L138) at the site's mean wind of about 25 km/h (S3-18) gives about **−29.6**, inside the 10-30-minute band; at gale strength (34 knots, about 63 km/h, reached on 35% of the counted year's days, P3-7) about −34.5; January's average low (−2.6 °C) at 25 km/h gives about −9.3, low risk. A July record low (−36.4 °C) in a gale gives about −59.6, under two minutes (all computed with P4A-W13's formula).
- →1 On an ordinary winter morning at Casey a human's uncovered face is on a 10-30-minute clock; in December and January it is not.
- →2 So a human's winter routine is door to door with covered skin, and any outdoor errand longer than a short walk is broken by warm stops; in the open half she can be out at length.
- →3 **Both bodies refuse to wait outdoors in the shut half, for different reasons:** the human because her skin freezes, the robot because her body cools and her rest, if unheated, costs her for good (P4A-6). The shared night's structure (stop together, for different reasons) repeats in the day as **a shared refusal to stand still outside**. *Expression:* in July nobody, of either body, waits outdoors; all waiting is done inside. *Divergence:* the bands are a present-day human standard for healthy adults; the site's wind is one station's present-day mean; humans in Tepenia are human, so the physics applies. Phase 3's "No human cold threshold is in canon" (P3-20 →3) stands: this is a physical reference, not canon.

**P4A-8 · The year's routine: the closing decides the winter, and the footstep tells the season.**
- *Canon and research:* sea navigable roughly November to March and ice-locked in winter (spec L154); the closing a single drop at a date that moves (S2-25, S3-2); the opening in two stages (S3-1); snow that squeaks in exactly the ice-locked months (P3-13); seams in which the senses disagree (P3-36).
- →1 Every household's year has a fill season and a draw-down (S2-15), and the routine that matters most is done before April: whatever must come by sea has to be in hand when the ice closes.
- →2 The squeak underfoot and the closed sea begin and end together, so a resident's foot keeps the same calendar as the store (P3-13 →3); at the seams the eye and the foot disagree about the season (P3-36), and the foot is the one that agrees with the sea.
- →3 *Comparable, one mechanism taken:* in present-day sea-supplied northern communities a household places a once-a-year order, "most of the year's groceries purchased in advance", booking early "since there are a limited number of sealift trips each year", and carries the order home from the beach (**sourced**, P4A-W5); a missed deadline means waiting a year or paying air freight, and a dedicated storage room is described as nearly as essential as the mud room (**snippet**, P4A-W1). **Divergence:** a hamlet buying from distant stores, against a Band 5 city whose hold's form and author are unwritten (S3-5 →2). *Hypothesis (h) for Phase 7:* if any part of Casey's hold reaches the household, the household's year has its own closing date, and a household that cannot lay in at once is the one the closing decides against (P4A-32 item 4). Nothing is assumed about who orders, from whom or how it is paid (RF-8; PA-8; DQ-9 (d)).

**P4A-9 · The day by season: a short winter day with a sunrise in it, and a summer with no dark to end it.**
- *Canon:* a sunrise every day and about 2.5 hours of light at the solstice (spec L80, L112, L125); 2.7 h in June and 4.0 h in July (spec L137-138); no nautical darkness from about 25 October to 18 February (S3-14); the firm hours for moving over snow in December and January are the low-sun hours (S3-15).
- →1 In winter the working day is longer than the daylight for both bodies; the daylight is a window inside the day, not its frame.
- →2 In summer the day has no dark to end it, and the low-sun hours are physically the best for work over snow (S3-15).
- →3 At neither end of the year does the sun set the length of the working day; the clock and the heat do (S3-17, S3-38). *Expression:* a human child at Casey goes to bed in daylight for weeks in summer and goes to school in the dark for weeks in winter, and never sees a day without a sunrise.

**P4A-12 · Both bodies: the blow, the blizzard day and the dug-out door.** [present-day]

- →1 Every resident with business beyond Casey (a trip, a training place, a visitor, something on order) meets one moving deadline each autumn.
- →2 Errands with the outside world bunch into the open half; the shut half's ordinary life is lived on what is already at Casey (W1; the road may differ, PA-5).
- →3 At Casey a missed autumn is a lost year for whatever depended on the sea, so ordinary planning runs a year ahead. *Expression:* a resident saying "next season" and meaning after the ice.

**P4A-14 · Robots: a night outside the heat stays in her body.** (c) on repairability

**P4A-17 · Humans and robots: to train for a care trade is to leave, on the ice's calendar.**

## B · Escapism and downtime

*"Time that is your own" means canon's leisure and nothing else (`DR-125` item 3): for robots, hours owed to nobody and a condition of staying sane; for everyone, the shared night. It is not the free tier. Escapism is written here as where, when and at what cost escape is physically possible, and whose time it is; its content is the CST's (P4A-DQ-1).*

**P4A-19 · Robots: leisure is owed every day, and the outdoors is hers in every season as long as she keeps moving.**
- *Canon:* a robot that spends all its time working is virtually guaranteed to go insane, and leisure is a condition of remaining sane (physiology L340-342); safer moving than resting (cold L87-97); her outdoor tank is above the capacity line on ordinary days (S2-8).
- →1 Every robot at Casey has hours owed to nobody, every day, by canon.
- →2 Her outdoor leisure is physically available all year, provided it moves (a walk, a long route, the bays where conditions allow, P4A-28); what she cannot do is stop outside to rest.
- →3 **In the shut half the dark outdoors at leisure belongs mostly to the robot half**, moving; humans are on the exposure clock (P4A-7). *Expression:* on a July evening inside four hours of light, the figures out walking for its own sake are mostly robots, and they do not stand still.

**P4A-20 · Humans: the outdoors is a seasonal escape, and the open half is its season.** [PA-10 for the colonies]
- *Figures:* December and January average lows −3.7 and −2.6 °C, low risk at the mean wind (P4A-7); no dark night from late October to mid-February (S3-14); the colonies present in the open half (P3-5).
- →1 A human's long hours outdoors at leisure fall in the open half, much of it under a sun that does not set.
- →2 In the shut half her escape is indoors, or short and covered.
- →3 The human half's year of leisure has a summer that is long, bright and outward and a winter that is short-lit and inward; the robot half's does not change shape with the season (P4A-19). The two bodies' downtime meets indoors all year and outdoors chiefly in summer. *Expression:* a human household out in the bright nights of December and indoors from April.

**P4A-21 · Both bodies: the shared night is the one downtime canon gives everyone, and at Casey it is kept indoors.**
- *Canon:* "Robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons." (physiology L336); there is no polar night (spec L112), and no civil darkness for about eleven weeks (S3-14).
- →1 Everyone at Casey stops at once, every night, for a robot's recharge and a human's sleep.
- →2 For weeks in summer that stop is held by rooms and clocks rather than by darkness (S3-17, S3-38).
- →3 **At Casey the shared night is an indoor fact, not a sky fact:** the one hour every resident has in common happens behind heated doors, in a city where the summer sky never agrees that it is night. *Expression:* a bright midnight with the streets empty because everyone is in.

**P4A-22 · Unscheduled days in: the blizzard as the city's involuntary pause.** (h) [present-day]
- *Basis:* P4A-12.
- →1 On about one day in six in the counted year, everyone is kept in by the weather.
- →2 For the robot half those days are spent inside, in heat; for the human half, at home.
- →3 *A hypothesis for Phase 6 and the CST:* the blizzard day is the one stoppage at Casey that no institution grants and no schedule sets, so it is likely where unplanned shared time collects. It is **not** time that is your own in `DR-125`'s sense (it is a stoppage, not leisure), and what people do with it is CST content. The heat keepers do not get it (P4A-24).

## C · Whose days: the populations and their different days

*The general population is "the born-here, plural, half-robot body" (`04_Phase_02` L460); every narrow population below is scoped on top of it (`00b` L110-115). Checked against G8 and the census, not against any custom (Frame L233).*

**P4A-23 · The general population: two bodies on two life courses.** [G8]
- *Figures:* 786,985 robots and 760,865 humans at Census I (spec L14); 42 origin communities, none above 11.95% (spec L31; P2-1).
- →1 The ordinary day at Casey is two days at once: a human day that changes over a lifetime and a robot day that changes only by choice (P4A-2).
- →2 Every street, workplace and building holds both bodies; no origin is anyone's default, so every crossing runs through the civic language and the English supporting tier (S2-41; contract L376).
- →3 What the two days share (the heated night, the closing, the wind's days in) belongs to no origin and no body, which is why it is the common layer (S3-28 →2). *Not written:* any origin community's day (CST; RF-6).

**P4A-24 · The heat keepers: the minority whose night is not shared.**
- *Canon and research:* a stopped heat network freezes from the break outward (S3-9); the heat has no off season and no repair window on the averages (S3-10); the national shared night is a stop at the same time (physiology L336).
- →1 Every night of every month someone at Casey is awake keeping the heat that lets everyone else stop.
- →2 For them the national fact of life, stopping together, does not hold; they stop at another time.
- →3 **The shared night at Casey has an exception written by physics:** the city cannot all stop, because the thing that keeps its rest safe cannot. If the keepers are robots, they recharge by day in the same heat; if humans, they sleep by day. *A shadow candidate only, for Step 7 after REQ-34:* a city whose shared rest depends on a minority who never share it, unintended and unnoticed. Who they are is unwritten (S2-69 (a)): **Phase 7**.

**P4A-25 · The junction workers, the flow and the held: three scoped days.** [PA-5]
- *Junction workers* (the mandate, 18.1% of the workforce [Column-3]): their day follows arrivals, by road all year if the road runs and by sea in the open half; resupply, rest, maintenance and information for others (spec L193).
