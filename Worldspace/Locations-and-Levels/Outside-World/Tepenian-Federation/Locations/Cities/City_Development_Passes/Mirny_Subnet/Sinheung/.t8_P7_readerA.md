- **What the breadth requires** (research; section 8):

  - **(b) Valves and fittings.** Stainless steel is defined by "at least 10.5% chromium"; 316 adds molybdenum for chloride pitting resistance. Nickel-bearing grades are typical. Bronze is used for trim, which needs copper and tin (R9).
  - **(c) Bearings.** AISI 52100 carries about 1.30–1.60% Cr and 0.25–0.45% Mn (R10).
  - **(d) Drives and actuators.** These use copper windings, silicon electrical steel, and NdFeB magnets doped with dysprosium or terbium (R11; the EV-motor tonnages there are order-of-magnitude context only, not this city's).
- **What East Antarctica is known to hold** (research):
  - **Small showings, not deposits.** The Australian Antarctic Territory, which covers this coast's sector, lists only "copper, iron, manganese, molybdenum, uranium" as occurrences. "The majority of the mineral occurrences in all territories are small showings or traces." The only possible exceptions named are iron in the Prince Charles Mountains and coal in the Transantarctic Mountains (UK Hansard written answer, 15 June 1982, R13).
  - **Iron.** The Mount Ruker banded iron formation has an ore body 30–70 m thick averaging about 33.5% total Fe, with "more than ten billion tons" recoverable (one Chinese-language abstract, R1 and R8). Another source says "the best found contains only about 35% iron" (R25 fetch).
  - **Coal.** "low quality - high moisture, high ash content - thin and in broken bands" (same source).
  - **Exposed rock.** Exposed rock is 0.18% (21,745 km²) of Antarctica (Burton-Johnson et al. 2016, R3).
  - **Larsemann Hills.** Scientifically notable boron and phosphate minerals, with no economic assessment found (R2; ASMA plan, "borosilicate and phosphate mineral assemblages that are scientifically significant").
  - **Not found.** I found no nickel, chromium, tin, zinc, tungsten or rare-earth occurrence for East Antarctica. One educational site claims "iron, copper and nickel ore in the Prince Charles Mountains", but the Hansard sector list omits nickel, so the sources conflict (R13). The carbonatite and rare-earth queries died (R4, R5). This is absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
  - **Distance.** The iron and coal country lies inland across the Lambert system: about 632 km great-circle from the fjord head to Mount Ruker, and about 344 km to Beaver Lake (calculation in section 8).
- **Re-derived statement.** The bulk of this city's feed (iron and stone) is the kind of material a continental supply can plausibly carry by road.
