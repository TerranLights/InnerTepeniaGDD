## Mirny as Phases 1-9 found it

*(Written after entry 28 and before entry 29 was opened, in my own words, from my own reading.)*

Mirny is a coast of 1,351,430 people at near parity (robots 50.73 %), placed by assignment rather than chosen, on a rocky, exposed shore where one katabatic wind (mean 12.0 m/s, almost never calm) makes the surface and the danger, and where the light, not the thermometer, dates the year: no polar night, about four weeks with no sunset, a winter that is a plateau. Its people, of fourteen stocks with no majority under the weighting and three exile groups under a founding nation that sponsored rather than settled, met that wind with no inherited judgment of it and no living teacher; what they were handed instead was a working station and a written record that predates the founding and holds none of it, plus, later, a relay that is their whole subnet's only path. Out of that came an unwritten compact: the medium (the record, the signal) has to hold; the reading of the wind is each person's own, and a loss is booked to the person, to the weather or to silence, never to the medium. Its work is owed outward (the subnet's contact, materials and machinery for the eastern roads, raw material for a chamber maker elsewhere) and arbitrated by nobody above the person at the point; what it makes for itself is preparation and upkeep, things that hold whatever the wind does and leave nothing to show; and the one distinction it draws among its people is not body or stock but formation: who was formed inside its conditions and who arrived into them, a line that ranks no one and, by the second Act, runs mostly through the robot half. It works: the relay mostly holds, crossings mostly end well, the sea opens in most years, and two bodies stop together every night and wear the same coat at the same door. What its people actually do with their own hours, eat, sing, call their winds and call their places is almost entirely unwritten.
