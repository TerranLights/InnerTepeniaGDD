## The axis

**Wind decides, heat ends.**

At Casey the day is organized around two things that do not change with the season: the morning's question whether today can be worked outdoors (the wind), and the night's obligation to be inside heat (every month, for every robot). The light, which organizes days elsewhere, cannot organize these: it runs from 2.7 to 24 hours (spec L132-143). The test is at P4C-29.

- →3 The days spent keeping heat and clearing running are a minority of baseline days, but they are the days that never stop (P3-10 →3) and the days that work hardest when the city stops (P4C-11). *No headcount for those industries at Casey is computed here;* the rates are the model's (`09` L14-29), and their product with D is the next step's, not this file's.

## B · Routines

**P4C-4 · (Robots) The day ends in a heated room, every night of every month.** [Inflected; physical]
- *Canon.* Overnight recharging (physiology L334); "Robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons." (physiology L336); charging below freezing causes permanent capacity loss, and safe cold charging runs at 5-10% of capacity, ten to twenty times slower (cold L23-26); every month's average low is below freezing (spec L132-143); the stop is "tied to a place, somewhere warm, not to the light" (`03_Research` L381, S3-38).
- →1 Every robot's day has a fixed end that is a place: where she will recharge tonight. On the averages it is fixed in December as in July (S2-10).
- →2 Her first planning question for any day that takes her away is not when but **where**: a route that ends at heat. A day out across the city, or an errand that could run late in a blow, is planned backward from the heated room.
- →3 At Casey the shared night is kept by walls, not by darkness: from about 25 October to 18 February there is no nautical darkness (S3-14), and the robot stop still comes, because the room does. For the robot majority **the day ends at a door, not at dusk.** *Expression:* in the bright weeks, at the hour of the stop, robots leave lit streets for rooms while the sun is still up.

**P4C-5 · (Robots) Outdoors she works on a smaller tank and takes her pauses moving or inside.** [Determined physics, Inflected by Casey's figures]
- *Canon.* Ordinarily clothed, in coat and gloves; "a robot can realistically remain standing still in most of the country. She just needs to be prepared for the weather" (physiology L42-43); capacity falls reversibly, 20-30% near freezing and to 50-60% at −20 °C (cold L63); output sags under load (cold L72); a working robot's heat keeps her cells in range (cold L89-90). Casey's average lows run −2.6 to −18.5 °C, record lows below −20 °C in nine months (S2-8, S2-17).
- →1 A robot can stand still outdoors at Casey, dressed; what she loses there is reversible charge and strength under load, not her future. Only rest, the recharge, must be heated (P3-21 →2).
- →2 So a winter work shift outdoors has a margin to manage: a robot leaves with charge enough for the job, the cold's squeeze and the walk back; a long outdoor halt is costly in charge, not forbidden.
- →3 The routine that follows is a budget, not a ban: on winter days a robot crew's breaks fall indoors or on the move because charge is the one thing the day spends twice, once on the work and once on the cold. *Expression:* a robot checking her margin before a July evening job, not before a December one. *Guard:* this is not the cold file's own-setting discipline (cold L99-107), which is not used.

**P4C-6 · (Robots) Upkeep of the body is routine, and its content is CST.** [Determined]
- *Canon.* Components "degrade over time and require maintenance or replacement" (physiology L67); siligel's first function is energy replenishment (physiology L245-246); coolant is drunk after hard work (physiology L275). On the model, robot maintenance and sustenance are standing industries, 12.5 and 3.25 per 1,000 robots (`09` L190-191): at Casey's 786,985 robots about 9,837 and 2,558 workers [Column-3; arithmetic].
- →1 A robot's week includes maintenance as a human's includes care; at Casey roughly one robot in eighty is, on the model, a robot-maintenance worker (12.5 per 1,000).
- →2 What a robot consumes, where and with whom is CST material (RF-6; Frame L283); only the canon fact that the routine exists is written.
- →3 Whether coolant behaves differently in Casey's cold, and whether the normal cooling system becomes a liability, is open (cold L139-140): the routine is written without it. **Phase 9.**

**P4C-7 · (Humans) The day is dressed for its worst hour, and lived mostly indoors.** [Determined, Inflected by Casey's figures]
- *Canon and figures.* Every month's average low is below freezing; mean daily highs of −10.2 to −11.1 °C from May to August (spec L136-139); "a mild coast-facing day could be followed immediately by a katabatic event off the plateau" (spec L155); no human cold threshold is in canon (S2-69 (f)); the glove trade-off holds for any human-shaped hand (physiology L53-57).
- →1 A human leaves home dressed for the day's worst hour, not its first (S2-12 →2).
- →2 Her outdoor time is short and purposeful in the shut half; her day's long stretches are indoors, where "The constant at Casey is indoors" (`04_Phase_03` L354).
- →3 The human routine is a set of crossings: home, heated work, heated home; the street is where she is between rooms. *Expression:* on most days of the year she trails visible breath between doors (P3-25).

**P4C-8 · (Both bodies) The footing tells the season, and the route follows the footing.** [physical]
- *Canon.* Dry squeaking snow from April to October on the averages; melt by day and refreeze by night in December and January, firm in the low-sun hours; glaze after any mild, wet day, rain being on the record in July; bare rock on the crests (P3-13, P3-22; S3-15, S3-19).
- →1 A walk to work changes surface with the half-year and, in December and January, with the hour.
- →2 Routes favor the firm ground of the hour: in the warm weeks the low-sun hours are the firmest (S3-15); after a mild wet day, glaze anywhere.
- →3 For a human the price of a misjudged step is a fall; for a robot, at Casey's recorded temperatures, it is the same kind of knock as anywhere (the brittle line lies below the site's record, cold L81; P3-21 →2). The routine is the same for both bodies, and the consequence differs only in kind of body.

**P4C-9 · (Both bodies) The morning weather call.** [Originated in the place's physics]
- *Canon and figures.* In one recorded year wind above 22 knots on 178 days (48.8%), above 34 knots on 127 (34.8%), blizzard on 61 (16.7%), with June to August the windiest, about one blizzard every five days (P3-7); snow moves on many ordinary days (S3-20); the switch of spec L155. *Comparable* (**sourced**, W5, W7): in a present-day Arctic capital a blizzard closes government offices, schools and many businesses, and the city takes its water and sewer trucks off the road; in another storm residents on trucked water are asked to cut laundry and dishwasher use.
- →1 Each morning at Casey begins with a question the calendar cannot answer: whether today is an outdoor day.
- →2 On about one day in six the answer is no for most outdoor work and much travel, and the day is rearranged at short notice; on about every second day the wind is strong enough to shape what is done outside (P3-12).
- →3 **The working week at Casey is set by the wind as much as by the calendar**: days lost to blizzard are made up on calm ones, and nobody can say on Monday which days those will be. *Divergence:* the comparable closes institutions by policy; who makes the call at Casey, and what closes, is not in the material (**Phase 7**). Its mechanism, a city that stops on the weather's word, is taken; its policies are not.

## B · Mundane concerns

**P4C-10 · (Both bodies) The year's stock is a household concern before it is a city one, as far as the sea is the route.** (c) [PA-8; no unit or price, RF-8]
- *Canon and figures.* "this directly determined Casey's supply windows" (spec L154); whatever the sea brings in about five months has to last about seven (S2-15); the hold may be a local store or a program from above, and the material writes neither (S3-5); the opening is two-staged and late in some years (S3-1, S3-2). *Comparable* (**snippet**, W1, W8, W11): in present-day sealift communities households order a year's bulk goods at once (canned goods, toilet paper, whole containers of groceries and household goods), book space early because sailings fill, and a family can work out a year's needs in one sitting; one source says such orders are out of reach for those who cannot buy a large amount at once.
- →1 If any part of Casey's goods comes by sea to households, the ordinary year carries one large decision about the whole shut half, made in the open half.
- →2 What a household forgot, it does without until the sea opens; what it lays in, it stores (the comparable's unheated storeroom is a snippet and is not carried).
- →3 The mundane concern of the shut half is not shopping but counting: what is left against what remains of the winter. **Conditional:** whether Casey's stock is household, city or parent-run is unwritten (S3-5; **Phase 7**), and the robot half's stock (siligel, coolant) is named only as canon's category, never priced (economy file L101-103).

**P4C-11 · (Both bodies) Water, waste and heat are one worry, and a storm stops the trucks.** (c) [Inflected]
- *Canon and research.* Below freezing, carrying water and waste water is part of the heat system (S3-12); a stopped heat network freezes from the break outward (S3-9); lakes are liquid only in January and February (S3-31). *Comparables:* (a) **sourced** (W5): "The City of Iqaluit pulled its water and sewer trucks off the road Wednesday morning due to blizzard conditions." (Nunatsiaq News, 18 March 2026); (b) **sourced** (W7): residents relying on trucked water urged to cut laundry and dishwasher use during a storm (23 February 2023); (c) **sourced** (W16): when pipes froze in a cold snap in western Alaska, one household bailed backed-up water by hand for about a week, and villagers went back to hauling water from the plant.
- →1 Wherever Casey's water travels by vehicle (an inference: an archipelagic city of 155-221 km², S3-29), a blizzard day is also a day without delivery, and households ration water on the weather's word.
- →2 Wherever it travels by pipe, the pipe is heated, and a heat failure is a water failure, a waste failure and, for any robot resting there, a capacity failure in the same night.
- →3 The ordinary worry under every Casey roof is the same worry in three forms: is the heat on, is there water, can the waste go. **Conditional** on Casey's water system, unwritten (S2-20); the city's answer is **Phase 1**'s and **Phase 7**'s. The comparables' institutions are not carried.

**P4C-12 · (Both bodies) The door, the drift and the first winter.** [Patterned, deferred]
- *Canon.* Every ground-level structure wears a lee drift, cleared after every blow; raised ones stand over clear ground (P3-9); snow moves on many ordinary days (S3-20); newcomers meet their first shut half without its skills, which cannot arrive by sea after April (P2-15).
- →1 For a resident of a ground-set building, the morning after a blow begins at the door with a shovel; for one in a raised building it does not. Which Casey's homes are is in no line (**Patterned, deferred**).
- →2 The clearing is ordinary, not heroic: on many days it is the first chore, and on blizzard days it waits for the wind to drop.
- →3 A newcomer's first winter is the year she learns which mornings need the shovel, how the footing changes, when the margin matters; by October she is a different person to the city (P2-15 →3). *Expression:* a newcomer clearing the wrong side of a door, the windward side, in her first May.

**P4C-13 · (Robots) The charge margin is a daily sum.** [Determined physics, Inflected]
- *Canon.* The three-way trade-off at a cold charger (warm the cells and spend energy; charge slowly; or charge fast and lose capacity for good) and the death spiral below a threshold (cold L28-51); "properly equipped" means never crossing that threshold (cold L48).
- →1 A robot at Casey keeps a margin as a human keeps a coat: enough charge to warm her cells if she ever has to charge cold.
- →2 On a winter evening the margin is thinner than it looks, because the cold has already shrunk the tank reversibly (cold L63).
- →3 At Casey's temperatures the death spiral is a far edge, not a daily threat (the site's record has not reached the cold file's own deep-cold setting); the daily concern is the permanent loss from one cold charge. So a robot's ordinary prudence is not survival but **never being caught needing to charge outside the heat**. *Expression:* a robot leaving a gathering early to make the walk home on the charge she has.

## B · Personal struggles

**P4C-14 · (Robots) A night unheated is carried in the body, and nobody else can see it.** [shadow candidate only; Step 7]
- *Canon.* "Charging a cold cell below freezing causes lithium plating ... permanently reducing capacity" (cold L24-25); whether plating is repairable at a facility is open (cold L137-138); access to heated rest decides which side a robot falls on (`01_Inherited` S1-17).
- →1 Any robot who ever had to charge in the cold (a heat failure, a stranding on a road leg, a held traveler without a berth) lives afterward on a smaller tank (scoped: not the general population).
- →2 The loss is invisible to others and may be invisible to her as a cause (S2-21 (b)); she knows only that her days end sooner.
- →3 At Casey, where every month qualifies, the scar can be got in January as easily as in July, and in January least expected (S2-67). Its personal struggle is pacing a smaller capacity in a city whose days are set by wind and heat; whether repair restores it is open (**Phase 9**). Never derived as a shadow here (REQ-34).

**P4C-15 · (Robots) Leisure kept against the bright weeks.** [unruled hazard, written only as canon flags it]
- *Canon.* A robot who only works is "virtually guaranteed to go insane"; leisure "is a condition of remaining sane" (physiology L340-342); overwork is a hazard with a mechanism, "Flagged as a connection, not yet ruled." (physiology L344-348); the low-sun hours are the firm hours in December and January (S3-15); a storm's vibration is perceptible to robots (physiology L187), and whether it touches rest is not in canon (PA-11).
- →1 The bright weeks with no darkness overlap the sea's open weeks and the firm night hours: the season offers both a reason and the light to keep working.
- →2 The stop holds at the heated room (P4C-4), but leisure is not the stop: the owed hours between work and recharge are the ones the season tempts away.
- →3 A robot's personal struggle in December is keeping hours that belong to nobody when the ground is firmest, the sea open and the sun up at midnight. What happens past the threshold is unruled nationally and is not written (S1-18). **Phase 5** owns the rhythm; **Step 7** the shadow test.

**P4C-16 · (Both bodies) Living the seams with senses that disagree.** (h)
- *Canon.* August to October the eye says spring while the foot and the ear say winter; October to November the nose and the eye say summer while the sea is still shut; in March the sea is open and the nights return (P3-36); the store is lowest in lengthening light, September and October (S2-26; S3-6).
- →1 The hardest weeks of the year arrive looking like relief.
- →2 A resident must ration against what the sky says: the light invites the pace of summer before the sea can resupply it.
- →3 The personal struggle of the seam is discipline against one's own senses: the foot, which squeaks until the averages cross −10 °C, is the one sense that keeps the sea's calendar (P3-13). *Expression:* someone stepping out into October sun and hearing the squeak.

**P4C-17 · (Humans) The worked half and the unworked half.** [Column-3, the model's convention]
- *Canon.* Half of humans are in the workforce on the model (`09` L5); human-keyed rates give about 19,782 schooling workers, 3,804 in childcare and 41,087 in health and long-term care at Casey's 760,865 humans (26, 5 and 54 per 1,000, `09` L15) [arithmetic].
- →1 A human at Casey is as likely as not, on the model, to be outside the workforce: a child, a carer, an elder, or someone the convention leaves out.
- →2 The unworked human's day is still set by the wind and the heat (P4C-9), and has no shift to make the blizzard day a day off; it is the same day indoors.
- →3 For the household that holds both, the blizzard day is the day everyone is home at once. Who in Casey's households carries the care is not written (CST; **Phase 9**).

**P4C-18 · (Humans) Care arrives in windows, and a calling means leaving.** [national canon; Casey's inflection]
- *Canon.* Every medic, nurse, field responder and robot-care technician is made at one of three institutes (institutes L20-21); intake and graduation follow travel, and "transport windows are seasonal" (institutes L199); robot care turnover is voluntary, human turnover by age (institutes L163-166); Casey is a candidate satellite site, not decided (brief §1 item 6; institutes L216-230).
- →1 A Casey resident drawn to medicine leaves Casey to train, unless and until a satellite is sited; she leaves and returns in a window the sea or the road allows.
- →2 A clinical post that falls vacant in April at Casey is filled, if by an arrival, no earlier than the next window [PA-5 for any road window]; the shut half is cared for by whoever was there in March.
- →3 The personal struggle is two-sided: for the ill in the shut half, care is what stayed; for the young who would care, the calling begins with a departure the ice times. On the model, about 41,087 health workers and about 9,837 robot-maintenance workers serve Casey [Column-3; national rates, `09` L15, L191].

## B · Escapism and downtime

**P4C-19 · (Robots) Hours that belong to nobody, spent where the heat or the motion is.** [Inflected]
- *Canon (defined, `DR-125` item 3, via brief §1 item 5).* For robots leisure is a fact of life, owed to nobody; hours that belong to nobody make a robot's freedom real, and keep her sane (physiology L340-362). For everyone, the shared night. Clothed robots can stand outdoors (physiology L42-43); only rest must be heated (P4C-5).
- →1 A robot's own hours at Casey happen in two places: indoors, where the one unchanging Casey is (P3-35), and outdoors in motion or, dressed and charged, at a halt she can afford.
- →2 In the shut half her outdoor leisure is spent from the charge margin; in the open half the margin is easy and the light endless.
- →3 So robot downtime at Casey is **seasonal in place, not in amount**: canon owes the hours all year; the city decides only where they can be spent. What is done in them is CST material (RF-6, RF-22). *Expression:* a robot out on the frozen bay's edge on a calm July evening under the night sky, back indoors before the margin runs out.
- *Exile-era cohort (scoped; Frame L120):* for a robot who remembers Upper Earth, these hours carry the contrast with hours that "existed only because they were loved" (physiology L362). Stated by canon, undated.

**P4C-20 · (Both bodies) The calm day is the city's free day, and nobody can schedule it.** (h)
- *Canon and figures.* Wind above 22 knots on about half the counted days; "calm is the event" (P3-7 →2); in the calm, sound may carry (P3-15, inference); the night sky exists only from about March to early October (P3-6).
- →1 Outdoor downtime at Casey waits on the calm, which comes on no fixed day.
- →2 A calm day is taken when it comes: work allowing, people go out then; the planned day off may be a blizzard.
- →3 **At Casey the free outdoor hours are set by the wind, not the week**: escape outdoors is opportunistic, and the shared memory of a good day is of a calm, not a date. *Expression:* on a calm, clear winter night both bodies outside, dressed, under a sky the summer never has. *Scoped exception:* the crews who keep heat and clearing work hardest on storm days and rest when it is calm (P4C-22), so their free days fall when the city is out.

**P4C-21 · (Humans) Escape from light and from cold is the same door.** (h)
- *Canon and research.* No nautical darkness about 25 October to 18 February (S3-14); in a large built far-north city human sleep length held through the midnight sun, while households without a held schedule lapsed (S3-17); every month's average low below freezing (spec L132-143).
- →1 In the bright weeks a human who wants a dark makes it indoors; in the dark months a human who wants warmth finds it indoors.
- →2 Human downtime at Casey is mostly interior in every season, for opposite reasons by half-year.
- →3 **Canon states nothing about human leisure beyond the shared night** (`DR-125` item 3): how much a human at Casey has, and what she does with it, is a recorded null here and CST material. *Expression:* curtains drawn at midnight in December against the sun, and in June against nothing.

## E · What the ordinary rhythm fails to provide

**P4C-26 · Seven people the rhythm does not fit.**

- →3 Every failure here is a failure of access to an interior (heat, store, care, dark) or of timing against the sea; none is a failure of the outdoors, which is the same for all. At Casey the rhythm's edge is a door.
