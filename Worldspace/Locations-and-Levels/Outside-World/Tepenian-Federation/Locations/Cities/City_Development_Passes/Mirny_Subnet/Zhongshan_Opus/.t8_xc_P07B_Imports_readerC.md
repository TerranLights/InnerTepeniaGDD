uses the rulings as they stand"* (`note` L42), the ruling applies here. The ruling's "Not decided" row (L1941) names nothing about
freight. What the recommendation was is not on my list, and nothing here rests on it (Choice 221).

**Finding 202 · The database line is now confirmed, not only reconciled.** `database` L495, in Zhongshan's own entry, calls the city a
coastal port receiving the partner's freighter shipments of raw materials, staged via two of the partner's ports, as part of the
subnet's coastal supply line; its words are unchanged since the pass (`first check` L60-62). Item 6 rules both the direct sailing from
the staging ports and the coastal supply line as routes of this city. So L495 is true as written. The pass was right that the line does
not contradict the table ("Reconciled, not contradicted", `Phase 5` L332); it is now confirmed by ruling, and the pass's own
reconciliation, "Being served by a shared harbor on a coastal supply line" (L333-334), describes one of three routes, not the city's
whole supply. L495 does not name the neutral hubs: it is incomplete, not wrong (Finding 212). The same entry carries the partner's
national adjective in quotation marks beside the subnet's name (`database` L492); that agrees with the partner's freight reaching the
subnet's coast, and nothing turns on it.

**Finding 203 · Direct sailing and the partner's direct row now come apart.** `Ports` L129 lists the partner's three receiving nodes;
neither this city nor its cluster is among them. Under item 6 the partner's freight reaches this city by direct sailing from the
staging ports. So the city's harbor takes the partner's direct sailings and is not one of the partner's designated nodes. "this city is
not among them" (`Phase 5` L333) is still true of the table, but it no longer means the city has no direct link with the partner.
*And what does that cause?* "Being served by a shared harbor on a coastal supply line is not being a gateway" (L333-334) can no longer
rest on the absence of a direct link. It rests on designation alone: the pass's word for the table's nodes is "designated gateway
nodes" (L332), and the table's column is "Tepenian receiving node" (`Ports` L125). In the pass's own terms, "not a gateway" now means
"not designated in the partner's row".
*And what does that cause?* Either the partner's row is incomplete for the cluster, or designation means something narrower than
receiving the partner's direct sailings. No line on my list says which (Question 215).
*And what does that cause?* Under either answer "NOT a trade hub" stands (Finding 208): it rests on `16` §18 and the spec's L180, not on
the gateway table, and every ruled route brings freight in. What turns on Question 215 is the word "gateway" in the pass's
reconciliation, not the city's economic standing (Choice 225).

**Finding 204 · L130's political clause describes one route, not the city.** The neutral row's general clause gives the route indirect
access to two Upper Earth powers' supply, "without a direct bilateral relationship with either mainland power.", and makes it inbound:
"INBOUND: raw materials the closed continental economy cannot manufacture for itself." (`Ports` L130). With direct sailing from the
partner's staging ports also ruled for this city, the "without a direct bilateral relationship" clause describes the neutral route's
traffic, not this city's supply as a whole. That answers the last part of the earlier Question 77, "Does L130's clause apply to this
city?" (`earlier piece` L749): it applies to one of the city's three routes (Choice 223). A direct sailing is a shipping route; it is not
by itself a bilateral relationship, and no line on my list says under what arrangement the partner's ships sail to a harbor outside its
designated row (Question 215).
*And what does that cause?* The partner's supply can reach this city two ways, directly (by sailing from the staging ports) and
indirectly (through the neutral hubs). No line on my list says what divides the partner's cargo between them: kind of goods, season,
cost or politics (Question 217).
*And what does that cause?* The neutral route also gives indirect access to a second power's supply (L130), and no line on my list gives
this city any other route to that power's supply. So, on my list, the neutral hubs are the only one of the city's three routes that
reaches the second power's supply; the direct sailings do not make them redundant. This is a relation taken from L130's general clause
and Zhongshan's own lines; it does not claim that no other channel exists in sources off my list.
*And what does that cause?* The city's outside supply stands on two kinds of standing at once: a direct shipping link with the partner,
and a route that needs no bilateral relationship with either power. Whether the loss of one could be made up by the others depends on
what divides the cargo (Question 217).

**Finding 205 · Both sea routes land at the one shared port.** `Ports` L202, this city's row, gives one port, the shared port, "**Long
haul,** seasonal, shared"; no other port is given for this city on my list (Choice 224). So the neutral route, whose node is the cluster
as one unit (`Ports` L130; the earlier piece's Finding 38, L472-474), and the direct sailing from the staging ports both berth there. This

**Finding 206 · How, and from where, the coastal supply line runs is not ruled.** Item 6 says freight arrives "along the subnet's coastal
supply line" (`rulings log` L1940). `database` L495 makes the city's port "part of" that line, which reads as a shipping line; the same
entry makes the city the coastal highway's western terminus (L493), with its direct highway neighbor east along it (L494). No line on my

*And what does that cause?* By sea, all three routes share the one seasonal berth (Finding 207). By road, the coastal supply line is the

*And what does that cause?* By road, the coastal highway would be the city's one year-round inbound route, and its western terminus is on
this city's ground (`database` L493; `Phase 5` L89 as `earlier piece` L323-325 describes it).
*And what does that cause?* The road terminus that `Phase 5` L89 lists among what the city supplies the cluster would also be where the
city's own year-round freight arrives. Nothing is decided here, and no claim is made about freight for the cluster's other cities
(Choice 232).

**Finding 207 · The berthing queue's stakes widen again.** The earlier piece (Finding 44, L504-508) found that the shared harbor is a
named node on a national inbound route, so Phase 7's contested good number 2, "**The berthing queue.** A seasonal allocation in shared
water that no one municipality has final authority over" (`Phase 7` L472 as `earlier piece` L506-507 quotes it; not read here), has

direct sailings and the neutral route's indirect traffic, and three if the coastal supply line runs by sea.
*And what does that cause?* The queue orders direct and indirect national supply against each other, as well as the cluster's own
shipping.
*And what does that cause?* The berth is seasonal (`Ports` L202). At the real site, the bay's landfast ice reaches an observed annual
maximum of 1.74 m and leaves in summer by breaking and drifting out once it has thinned to about 0.5 to 1.0 m, not by melting in place
(search log, S3 and S5); first-year fast ice in the bay reaches 1.2 to 1.8 m by the end of winter (S5). So sea access at the shore opens
in summer, on a date set by breakout, and the streams compete inside that window. This is realism only, demoted: the in-world port is
constructed, whether it handles ice is not on my list, and the source's word "seasonal" governs (Choice 226).
*And what does that cause?* No municipality has final authority over the shared water (L472), and no line on my list names who orders the
partner's direct ships against the neutral route's ships in that window. The contest the pass already records gains a national side,
with no one ruled to arbitrate it. Its direction holds, and nothing in-world is added here.
