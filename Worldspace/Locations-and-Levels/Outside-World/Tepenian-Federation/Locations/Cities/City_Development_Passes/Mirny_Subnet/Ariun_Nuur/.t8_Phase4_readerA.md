## A · The headline function, then writing away from it

**The axis of this section: what the place is famous for, set beside what fills a day here.**

**FIND-P4-1 · The headline function, in one sentence.** *Ariun Nuur is a research city above a sealed subglacial lake, whose work is the lake research program, its access shaft and an archive no single person understands* (spec L167, L177, L179). **The test: would this reasonably be someone's entire day, every day, forever? No.** The research population's size is not citable (Phase 2 FIND-P2-64); §23's Science 65% has no stated base (RF-42); in the industry model 39.4% of the workforce count (143,209 of 363,036, at D = 2.50) is baseline survival work, with a further Self-sufficiency/survival infrastructure share of 25% beside the science (16 L2353–2358; the workforce is robots plus half the humans, a headcount and not hours, 16 L27, L125, no city factor). And every body here, scientist or not, pays the same continuous bill first: heat at every place it stops (spine §3a standing cost 1). **So the day is written from what a resident does between, despite and apart from the research.** *(A)*

**FIND-P4-2 · Written away from it: the day is a sequence of warm stops, with work between them.** The street register is the general answer (Phase 3 FIND-P3-44): heat, melted water, charge, all on one unnamed supply, without a pause. A body may rest only in heated, powered space (spine §8f); outdoors the robot majority is safe only moving and the human minority only briefly (Phase 3 FIND-P3-25). → **The ordinary day is organized less by what anyone does than by where and when a body may stop** → each stop is a cost paid for someone by everyone, through the supply (spine §8e) → third order: the research, the city's reason, is the one part of the day that can be paused on a schedule, while the stops that hold everyone's life cannot (spine §4). *(A)*

## B · The four elements

### B1 · Routines

**The axis of this element: the day runs from one warm stop to the next, and the clock, not the sky, says when.**

**FIND-P4-3 · The shared night is the day's one fixed beam, and here the sky does not set it.** National canon: robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons, robots to recharge, humans to sleep (RP L334–338). Here about 243 days a year have no sunrise or sunset (spec L96–97; research FIND-3: polar night April 23 to August 20, midnight sun October 21 to February 20), so the shared night is a charge night set by the clock, not a dark one (frame §4 item 9; spine L406). → The one thing every ordinary day is organized around, when to stop, is a convention the city keeps, not a fact of light (spine deficit 4: the rhythm is whatever the institution that keeps the clock decides) → for most of the year **everyone stops together because the clock says so, and for no other reason**. Which clock, and what hours, is not written (`DR-137` item 8; REQ-A1; RF-45). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-4 · The night is when the supply carries the most at once.** Charge is taken warm, in heated, powered space, and a charge warming a cold cell draws on the electric supply, no rate written (spine §8f; `DR-140` item 4). So at the shared stop 284,693 robots draw charge and warmth while 156,685 humans sleep in heat, on one supply (spine §8e: one supply carries both bodies' lives). → **The hour when nearly everyone is resting is the hour the city's life support works hardest** → and every arrival adds to that hour (Phase 2 FIND-P2-33). PROPOSED, built on national canon and the composition; no rate, figure or source (`DEC-705`; RF-A2). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-5 · For the majority, the night is dreamed.** A charging robot dreams (RP L456–457; brief §4 item 10). So for 64.50% of the city the nightly stop is not blank time. National; nothing is written of what is dreamed. *(A)*

**FIND-P4-6 · For the minority, the night is the hardest part of the day.** The human body meets about 3,900 m of air (624.2 hPa on the standard atmosphere, a raw table, no latitude adjustment; the basis is open, `DEC-735`), by month about 3,825 m in December–January to about 3,950 m in June–August, and the cost is paid in sleep: in a measured wintering crew, central sleep apnea rose with time on station and nocturnal hypertension appeared in almost all (research FIND-11, FACT-37, direction only; crews, not residents) (Phase 3 FIND-P3-27, parked for this phase). In the midnight-sun months a human's place of rest needs darkness as much as heat, and in the polar night light at set times (research FIND-17; Phase 3 FIND-P3-30). → **For a human, the stop is where the place costs most: heated and lit in winter, heated and darkened in summer, and short of air all year.** For robots no need for darkness is written (spine L406). No sleeping practice (RF-45; research RF-55). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-7 · Every outdoor stretch of the day is decided indoors.** Outdoor danger has one measurable cause, temperature, and arrives without weather; exposure is planned by clock and thermometer before going out (spine §3a strength 1; spec L93). The calm, clear day carries the full cost (SH-2). → **The day's outdoor segments are planned in advance, with a limit (for a human) or an obligation to keep working (for a robot), never taken on impulse.** No practice is named. *(A)*

**FIND-P4-8 · Outdoors, the robot's routine is motion.** A robot is safer moving than resting: work makes the heat that keeps her cells and joints in range, and stopping is the dangerous act (cold file L87–97; RP L15–16). → Outdoor work for the majority body is continuous work; outdoor waiting is the hazard (SH-3) → **the robot's outdoor day is a run from one warm place to the next, without halts.** Words only; no temperature (`DEC-697`). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-9 · The stop has keepers.** Heat, melting and charge never stop, and every repair to them is done live (spine §4); the shaft is kept filled and watched whether or not anyone works in it (research FIND-10; Phase 3 FIND-P3-50). → The national shared night covers bodies that stop, but the systems that keep the stop warm cannot stop → **the city's day has a second layer: whoever keeps the life support running through the shared night.** Written as a function, never a title (`DR-137` item 12); who, how many and how their own rest falls is not written (Q-A3; RF-A3). In the picks' real world the night at an instrument is held by a thin rostered layer under written rest rules (research FIND-18, CONV-6, CONV-7), a G7 form not transplanted (research UI-1, RF-53). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-10 · The research population lives on a second calendar.** Instruments run on calendars with scheduled dark periods (spine §3b standing cost 1; research FIND-19; Phase 3 FIND-P3-18), and the archive's understanding passes from person to person (spec L179; research FIND-23). → For the research population the day carries a second rhythm on top of the clock, runs and pauses, and the only part of the city's day that can stop by choice is theirs. The calendar itself is the picks' real-world form, PROPOSED only (RF-43); none is written for the city. *(A)*

**FIND-P4-11 · The model's share of life not spoken for by survival is a workforce share, not a clock.** National canon reads half to three-quarters of a Tepenian working life as not spoken for by survival, "a freedom gradient drawn by geography" (RP L364–367, restating `09` §3.5, which is not on this list). In the industry model this city's baseline is 39.4% of the workforce count, so 60.6% (219,827) is the distinctive tier; of the workforce, 19.7% (71,444) is Mandated to national provider roles and 40.9% (148,383) is FREE (16 L2353–2357, §23 governing). **But the count is "A headcount of workers, NOT hours" (16 L27), robots plus half the humans, and FREE is the model's remainder, not leisure (spine §0, considered and not rowed).** → **The model gives the city's day no hours:** it says how the workforce count divides, never when anyone is free. Counting every robot as workforce is the model's basis only (`DR-137` item 9); Mandated has no in-world form (RF-38). Null recorded, not filled (RF-A10; Q-A1). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-12 · Instruction is part of the ordinary day, for the teacher as well as the taught.** Every arrival must be taught a place that can kill, by people taught the same way (Phase 2 FIND-P2-40): a robot's first lesson is to charge warm; a human's first polar night is her hardest season (Phase 3 FIND-P3-39). Mentor and mentee bonds are a genuine robot institution (RP L418), and records are not teachers (research FIND-23). → While arrivals continue, **some part of the settled residents' day is always given to teaching the newest how to stop safely**: where heat and power are, how to charge warm, how long a human may stay out (Phase 3 FIND-P3-45, a condition, not a filter). No teaching practice, school or rule is written (RF-45, RF-91). *(A)*

### B2 · Mundane concerns

**The axis of this element: every ordinary worry here is a form of energy or a form of time.**

**FIND-P4-13 · Water: the worry is not whether there is water but whether it kept moving.** Every liter is melted ice, about an eighth of a kilowatt-hour a liter, about three quarters of it the melting (research FIND-7, FACT-19: 0.121–0.125 kWh from the mean, from physical constants, raw); drinking, washing and a robot's coolant draw on the same account as warmth (spine §3a deficit 1). Reuse, not better melting, is the lever, and a line that stops is a line that freezes (research FIND-7). → **A stopped tap here is a cost, not a saving**; a frozen line is the first sign of a lapse (spine §8e). No ration or system written (research RF-56). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-14 · Food: freshness, not quantity, is the human minority's daily concern.** The city is a forced importer, its food term held at 10% (16 L2353); food here costs power, a national condition (DS4 L17; brief §4 item 17); food grown here would be an energy product (research FIND-12). Frozen stock keeps for nothing outdoors and best below the surface, and freshness arrives short; frozen stores dry where temperature swings (research FIND-13, FIND-20). Food is keyed to the human body only in the model (16 L127; spine §3c). → **The human day's food concern sits at the far end of the road** (spine: hunger arrives by the road, not by the season). What is cooked or eaten is the CST's (`DR-140`); no supplier, route or store practice (RF-37, RF-60, RF-74). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-15 · Charge margin: the robot's mundane concern.** Charging a cold cell costs capacity for good; warming cells first spends stored energy to obtain energy; charging cold safely is much slower; and a robot whose charge falls too far in deep cold cannot recover on her own (cold file L18–44, words only; row 81). Warmth costs siligel (RP L489–491). → **Every robot's day carries a margin the way a human's carries a meal**, and the margin matters most before she goes out. No figure, threshold or rate (`DEC-697`; `DR-140` item 4; Q-A4). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-16 · Siligel and coolant: the robot's supply line, and the dual economy.** Siligel is food and coolant drink, never money (economy L101–102; RP L236–247, L275); siligel arrives by shipment unless the city's own geography makes it a mining or processing city, which stays reserved (frame RF-21, PV-6). → The two bodies' everyday effort runs on different things (economy L95, recorded as an observation): **humans on food and on breath; robots on charge, siligel and coolant; both on heat.** No price, unit, wage or rate (`DR-3`). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-17 · Gloves, every month.** The glove trades insulation against dexterity (RP L53–57), and no monthly mean rises above −31.3 °C (spec L116), so every outdoor manual task, for both bodies, chooses between warmth and precision (Phase 3 FIND-P3-26). A gradient, never a gate. Dress styles are the CST's. *(A)*

**FIND-P4-18 · Raising: the chore no day notices and every decade does.** The surface rises about 6.8–7 cm a year (research FIND-8: 22.5 kg/m² a year over about 330 kg/m³, raw), about 0.7 m a decade; a doorway not raised is lost within a working life, quietly (research FIND-8; Phase 3 FIND-P3-56, parked for the work of raising). → **Raising is ordinary, recurring work with no day of its own:** nothing announces it, and the cost of skipping it shows only years later. No raising practice, and the inherited station's state stays open (RF-99; SH-12). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-19 · Where machines wait.** Outdoor machinery lies below the ordinary cold-rated catalog from April to September (research FIND-15), and in the real-world form a machine at rest outdoors is kept running or plugged in where it stops (research FIND-29, CONV-10). → If the city follows that form, **every outdoor machine has a warm place or a running engine, and where it waits is part of somebody's day.** Conditional (RF-92). Machinery only. *(A)*

**FIND-P4-20 · The window and the forecast, conditionally.** Where access holds only while temperature allows, supply arrives in bulk inside a window, ordered far ahead, and the characteristic failure is a forecast made once and found wrong months later (research FIND-13, CONV-1). Whether Hwy 37 has such a window is Phase 5's (research REQ-58). → If it does, **the mundane concern is what was not ordered.** Reserved (research RF-73); no ordering practice. *(A)*

**FIND-P4-21 · Care arrives by the window too.** Every city staffs its own clinicians, always (institutes L16, by brief §4 item 16), and every medic, nurse, field responder and robot-care technician is made in Tepenia at one of three institutes (institutes L20–21), none of them here (DS4 L18). Training runs continuously but intake and graduation happen only when travel is possible (institutes L193–204). Robot care includes the care of robot minds, "the mental-health system of the national majority" (institutes L128–130). → **A carer who leaves the city's staff is replaced in a window, not on demand.** No staffing or care practice is written (RF-A7). *(A)*

### B3 · Personal struggles

**The axis of this element: each body struggles with what it cannot stop doing.**

**FIND-P4-22 · A human cannot stop breathing, and cannot go back.** At about 3,900 m by the body's measure (FIND-P4-6), the cost is paid at night and, for lowland-born residents, is chronic and rises with years lived (research FIND-11; research RF-67, stated flatly, no census row read). A human who came could not return to Upper Earth (brief §4 item 5), and no departure is written (RF-30). A human arrival's first polar night is her hardest season, cold, dark and the thinnest air at once (Phase 2 FIND-P2-19). → **Her struggle has no exit and no end date.** Written as physiology and law; no grievance, practice or remedy (RF-50, RF-59). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-23 · Keeping her sleep against the light.** Under the midnight sun a group on a strict shared schedule still drifted one to two hours later from light in its sleeping rooms (research FIND-17, FACT-40). → For about 123 days a human keeps her own night against continuous daylight, and the cost reads as a bad sleeper's, not the place's (spine SH-4; RF-45). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-24 · The robot's struggle is between safety and sanity, and both are settled at the stop.** Outdoors she is safe only moving (cold file L87–97); a robot who never rests is virtually guaranteed to go insane, so downtime is a condition of sanity (RP L340–342); and rest and charge can only be taken warm, in heated, powered space (spine §8f). → **Her body tells her to keep moving; her mind needs her to stop; and the only place she may stop is somewhere the city pays to heat.** Her rest is located, never where she happens to be. PROPOSED, on national canon and physics (RF-A4). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-25 · Flight mode, here: saving siligel against keeping warm (an inference).** Flight mode is a low-energy state used to save siligel, aware of her surroundings but in much slower motion, and lying with nothing happening nearly drives a robot insane (RP L460–464); processing siligel releases heat, so warmth costs siligel (RP L484–491); stillness is what the cold punishes (cold file L92–93). *Inference, marked as one:* outdoors the siligel-saving state is a still, low-heat state, so **saving siligel and keeping warm pull against each other**; indoors flight mode trades siligel for boredom. No rate, no figure (RF-A5; Q-A2). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-26 · Keeping the leisure owed to nobody.** Overwork is a hazard with a mechanism, and labor conditions may be a medical question (a connection flagged, not ruled: RP L344–348). Here the life support never stops (FIND-P4-9) and the safest outdoor state is work (FIND-P4-8), so the place's own conditions lean toward work that does not end. → **The robot's struggle is to keep the hours that belong to nobody** in a place where the sky never ends the day (spine deficit 4: without a rule, nobody's day ends). The city's rule is unwritten (research UI-1, RF-53; the DCI). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-27 · A scar carried for a long life.** Charging cold at full rate in an emergency trades the future for the present, and the loss is permanent (cold file L37, L61–64); whether a facility can repair it is open (L137–138); robots do not age and live long (RP L67). → **One bad night can be carried for the rest of a very long life**, and it reads as one robot's decision, not as the place's (SH-A1; RF-A9). Words only. *(A)*

**FIND-P4-28 · The arrival who does not fit.** Whoever came and did not fit the specialized founding's demands has no exit and no written place (Phase 2 SH-10). The day adds nothing beyond its condition: every arrival meets the same demands to stop safely (Phase 3 FIND-P3-45). RF-75 holds. *(A)*

### B4 · Escapism and downtime

**The axis of this element: free time is owed to nobody, and here it can only be spent where heat is paid.**

**FIND-P4-29 · Leisure is a fact of life, and here it is located.** In Tepenia leisure is universal, owed to nobody, "the material proof that leaving worked" (RP L356–362). Here a robot may rest only in heated, powered space (FIND-P4-24), and a human's time outdoors is limited by temperature and time (FIND-P4-7). → **Downtime is an indoor thing for most of the year, by physics, not by taste** → so every hour of free time is also an hour of the supply's heat. What anyone does in it waits for the cultural synthesis (`DR-140` item 2; RF-15; RF-A8). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-30 · The light season is the one outdoor season.** November to January the means are −31.3 to −41.4 °C (spec L105, L115–116), the sun is continuous, and the sky shows its shimmer, halos and pillars on clear days (spec L128; Phase 3 FIND-P3-37, FIND-P3-38). → If free time goes outdoors at all, **its season is the light season**: the least cold months, and the only ones in which the place shows anything to the eye but the city. The same months cost human rest its darkness (FIND-P4-23). Structure only, PROPOSED (RF-A8). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-31 · In the polar night, downtime and the supply are one bill.** For about 120 days the only light written is the city's own, drawn on the same supply as its heat and its robots' charge (Phase 3 FIND-P3-64). → In the dark months every lit, warm hour off work is paid by the same account that keeps everyone alive. No lighting practice (RF-101). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-32 · The robot's escape exists nationally; its local form waits.** The charge night is dreamed (FIND-P4-5); robot coffee, glitch-coolant and robot vapor products exist as national institutions (RP L277–285, L293–303). Their local forms, varieties and scene are the CST's (`DR-140` item 3) and are not written. **Escape is present here by national canon; its shape here is reserved.** *(A)*

**FIND-P4-33 · Who has no downtime when everyone stops.** The keepers of the life support (FIND-P4-9) rest outside the shared night, in a place with no sky to reset their clock by. → Their free time falls when the city is asleep, and the cost reads as a personal matter of hours (SH-A2). *(A)*

**FIND-P4-34 · A null: the files give no figure for free hours.** The model's FREE 40.9% is a workforce remainder, not leisure (FIND-P4-11). Recorded as a null. *(A)*

## D · Physical scale

**The axis of this section: the distances are unmeasured; the chores are measured.**

**FIND-P4-39 · No distance inside the city is written as measured.** The extent band is 44–63 km² by construction, at the table's assumed densities (frame DL-5; spine §7), and the footprint is unwritten (spine REQ-43).
