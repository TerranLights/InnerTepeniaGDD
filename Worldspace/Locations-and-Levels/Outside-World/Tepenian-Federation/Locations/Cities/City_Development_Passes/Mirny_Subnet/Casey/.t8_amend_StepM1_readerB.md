**AM-Q102 · Does the reviewer's national-vocabulary caution reach Casey's work?** The log records it as "Reviewer's reading, not
the developer's words" (L1895). It matters for Casey in particular, because Casey's supporting tier (English) is also a primary
base of the national language. A word of English origin in Casey may be national vocabulary rather than anything of Casey's own.
- (a) Adopt the reading as stated, for every city. Wherever the caution applies, it covers words of English and Japanese origin
  and of the supporting bases.
- (b) Leave it unadopted. Casey's naming runs under the existing rules only.
- (c) Adopt it for Casey alone.
- *Recommended (PROVISIONAL):* (a), because its subject is the national language.

**AM-Q103 · Under `DR-131` item 3, does Casey's subnet count as a "shared thing it is named in"?**
- (a) Yes. The parenthetical (port, airport, road, cluster) gives examples, so subnet lines count, less any clause grounded only
  in another city's lines. RF-14 stays reserved.
- (b) Only if a subnet is a "cluster" in the ruling's sense. Otherwise only subnet lines naming Casey count.
- (c) No. Only Casey's own entries in subnet-level files count.

**AM-Q104 · Source order for a shared thing.** Suppose a fact about a road or port Casey is named in appears both in that thing's
