- **(b) An order of who is warmed first.** "No admitted line states one" (`02_Spine` L627) and no one is empowered to write one; when heat is short "the choice falls to whoever holds the heat" (P6-17 →1). *A decision nobody is empowered to make, made anyway, at a door.*
- **(c) The national road's go/no-go.** The authority that owns the road cannot make the call; the one who makes it does not own the road (Highways L26-30, L40). "Casey decides who sets out east; it cannot decide whether the road ahead is open" (`04_Phase_05` L264) [PA-5]. *A decision split between an owner who cannot act and an actor who does not own.*
- **(d) The last date.** The closing moves at sector scale by weeks (S3-2) and the opening comes in two stages at no date (S3-1); a rule setting a last departure cannot be administered against the ice, and the comparables show that a schedule gives the closure an author once it exists: "The ice is an alibi only while nobody has set a schedule" (`03_Research` L66, S3-4 →2). *So any date Casey sets makes the ice somebody's fault.*
- **(e) Crossing the bays.** If the city's parts are joined over ice in the shut half (S3-29 →3, conditional), someone must measure and open a crossing (P4-43 →3); no line names who. *Comparable* [present-day; W7, snippet]: where a river city depends on an ice crossing, the date the ice "is declared safe" is an authority's, and it moves later in warm years; mechanism taken (a declared opening), divergence stated (a river's ice is measured along one route; Casey's fast ice is "very unstable", S3-1) (c).
- **(f) The robot majority's owed leisure in the bright weeks.** Canon owes it (physiology L356); the firm low-sun hours pay best for work (S3-15); overwork is "Flagged as a connection, not yet ruled." (Frame L245). No guardian is written (`04_Phase_04` L574), and none could check, since the loss it would prevent is unruled.
- **The edge cases nobody wrote:** a held robot who winters repeatedly and is never counted in the fill season (P5-27, C's form); a passer lost past the edge, mourned nowhere at Casey (P6-13 (b)); a robot reduced by a cold night with "no ledger anywhere that says why" (P6-18).
- →1 What Casey cannot administer is exactly what its physics makes permanent: heat, the closing, the road's moment.
- →2 Each unadministrable rule is handled by **preparation, not enforcement** (redundancy laid in, a margin kept, a hold sized to the late opening, S3-1 →2), so Casey's order is mostly prospective and almost never punitive.
- →3 **A place whose worst outcomes cannot be undone has little use for rules that act after the event:** at Casey the governing act is the one done before April. *Expression:* a September in which nobody can be blamed, because everything that decided it was done in March.

**P7A-13 · The warm night has no written terms: who provides heated rest, to whom, on what terms, is the largest blank in Casey's order.** [the provider unwritten; never priced, RF-8]
- *Lines:* heated rest "is a physical condition of robot life all year, before it is a custom" (Frame L275); the city's public interiors become storm shelters for both bodies and "its recharge network for one" (P4-39 →3); riders wait inside "a heated room before it is a social room" (P5-24 →2); the comparables give three forms, a duty of room, a trade, a commons whose failure is the lock (S3-13); a gate "could protect the goods and never the heat" (`03_Research` L94).
- →1 Casey's most continuous public service is provided by nobody named, under no written rule.
- →2 Whichever of the three forms it takes at Casey (unwritten), the gate rule fails at the heat: no traveler can bring seven months of heat with her (S3-8 →1), so Casey cannot make a stranger self-sufficient in the one thing that matters.
- →3 **The warm night is therefore a common obligation whose cost lands on whoever holds a heated room when the stranger knocks:** the duty is the place's, the bill is a household's or a keeper's. *A hypothesis for Step 5 to check against Casey's own material.* *Expression:* a household in late September with a held crew member at the door and the hold's count in mind (P6-15 →1).

**P7A-14 · Membership: the count and the contract disagree, and the order has no rule for which governs.**
- *Lines:* the census counts residents, and "a membership mechanism with no author has no appeal process either" (`01_Inherited` L79); membership "is conferred by the room that kept you warm and by the winter you got through, with nobody announcing it and no record of it" (`04_Phase_05` L435); the hold is planned by the count (S2-49; P5-27, C's form); the census is not edited (RF-11).
- →1 Two lines draw Casey's membership: the count (who is provisioned for) and the contract (who is warmed). They coincide for residents and part for the held and the flow.
- →2 When heat is ample, the contract governs without anyone choosing it; when heat is short, the count reasserts itself at the door (P6-17).
- →3 **Casey's membership rule is written nowhere and enforced every night**: it changes with the store, not with any decision, so a held stranger's standing is high in March and low in September. *Shadow-shaped in proportion only; to Step 7 after REQ-34.* *Expression:* a held crew member who is a guest in May, a neighbor in July and, in a short September, a question.

**P7A-15 · Prohibitions: at Casey the cold does the prohibiting; the authored prohibitions are national, and the local slot is open.**
- *Authored prohibitions that reach Casey* (chosen policies with an author, spine L918-920): the ossuary's metal may not be reclaimed, "There is no such variant" (physiology L133); a robot's personality may not be fully designed, by law (physiology L422); a robot in Assembly territory holds no standing (IV.1.1, national, [treaty draft], as P2-24 carries it).

- →1 Every dangerous act at Casey is already punished by physics, permanently for a robot, so the city need not forbid what the cold forbids.
- →2 What the cold does not punish (refusing a stranger the warm room, cutting the flow in a short winter, setting a convoy out on a mild morning) is exactly what no admitted line prohibits either.
- →3 **The prohibitions Casey would need are prohibitions on omission, on not opening a door, and an order that cannot see its breaches (P6-18) cannot enforce them.** None sited here, which does not mean none is possible. *Expression:* a city with no law against leaving someone out in the cold, because nobody can see when it happens.

**P7A-16 · What Casey's order cannot supply, it orders from elsewhere on the sea's calendar.** [relation; PA-6 for chambers]

## 7c · Knowledge and transmission

**P7A-17 · What Casey does is learned by wintering, and the ice runs the apprenticeship.**
- *Lines:* whoever holds the winter skills "cannot be replaced for seven months, and can be replaced by an arrival for five" (`02_Spine` L145); every newcomer "meets her first shut half without its skills", which "cannot arrive by sea after April" (P2-15 →2); the held come out of a winter holding the closing, the squeak underfoot, the morning weather reading and the store's draw-down (P5-27); "At Casey ordinary competence is seasonal and learned by wintering" (`04_Phase_04` L440).
- *What is learned* (from Phases 1-6, both bodies): to plan a day backward from a heated room (P4-5 →2); for a robot, never to be caught needing to charge outside the heat (P4-15 →3) and to keep outdoor work within a radius (P4-6 →3); for a human, the exposure clock (P4-7); to read the weather inland and seaward together and to treat mildness as a warning (P4-9; S3-18 →2); to count the store against what remains of the winter (P4-11 →2); which doors face the wind (P5-27).
- →1 No school teaches a shut half; it is taught by being held inside one, in a number of winters no arrival can shorten.
- →2 So competence at Casey is correlated with having been unable, or unwilling, to leave before April, not with training or origin: the held stranger learns involuntarily what the March leaver never does (P2-15 →2).
- →3 **Casey's apprenticeship has no master and no enrollment: the ice enrolls whoever it holds, every year, and graduates them in October.** Written as a fact of the year, never as a ranking of people (`02_Spine` L145). *Expression:* a newcomer clearing the windward side of a door in her first May (P4-42 (c)), and a held crew member who, by spring, no longer asks where she will be warm tonight (`04_Phase_05` L436).

**P7A-18 · The site from paper, the founding from robots, the plant from failure stories; who keeps the records is unwritten.** [cohort; via contract for spec L171 part 4]
- *Lines:* the founding had "records, not teachers" (contract L79); the records are "records, not a tradition" (contract L210); a record "can be available and faithfully followed and still lose the working condition, which only a check on outcomes catches" (`04_Phase_02` L274, carrying S3-24); corrections travel as failure stories among those who keep the machines (S3-25); "Casey's least-understood plant is its most dependable plant" (`03_Research` L253); robots built at or before the exile carry firsthand memory across both Acts (Frame L120); the Frame names "whoever keeps the inherited journals and logs" as a narrow sub-population, never a sector (Frame L217).
- →1 Three channels carry Casey's knowledge: paper (the station's records, for the site), robots who were there (for the founding, if any survive) [cohort], and stories of what failed (for the plant).
- →2 Who keeps and reads the records, and on what terms, is in no admitted line; keeping them confers no standing in any line (P2-4; P2-19). Their keepers are a narrow population (Frame L217), never a sector (`00b` L181-200).
- →3 **The knowledge that most protects the oldest plant is held by whoever has seen it fail, not by whoever holds its papers**, so authority over the inherited base, if any, sits with those who have repaired it (S3-25 →2, snippet level for standing; an inference). Records stay records, never a tradition (`DR-25`). *Expression:* a robot who remembers the first winter contradicting the manual (P2-20), and a keeper who trusts her more than the page.

**P7A-19 · Robots learn by mentor and by need, and can always learn again; what they cannot always do again is carry the full tank.** [national canon, Casey's inflection; PA-6]
- *Lines:* "Mentor/mentee bonds are a genuine, culturally real robot institution" (physiology L418); a build answers "which local robot community initiated it, and for what local interest or need" (physiology L424); "A robot is always capable of finding a new purpose in life" (physiology L69); a robot who rested cold "carries a smaller maximum for good" (P4-21 →1); kinship runs on shared experience (physiology L418).
- →1 A robot built at Casey is built for a local need and learns its winter from a mentor and from the winter itself (P2-17).
- →2 No robot at Casey is unable to learn: the gel brain is "malleable and ever-changing" (physiology L69). What a robot may be unable to do is the work she learned, if a cold night has reduced her capacity (P4-46 item 2).
- →3 **For the robot majority, failure at Casey is never failure to learn; it is a body that can no longer keep up with what it knows.** The order's answer, by canon, is adaptation, not exclusion: a smaller day, a different trade (P6-11). *Expression:* a robot who knows the inland route better than anyone and now plans shorter days than her friends (`04_Phase_04` L280).

**P7A-20 · Those who cannot learn Casey, or cannot do what it asks, and what becomes of them.**
- **(a) The newcomer in her first shut half:** temporary; the ice teaches her (P7A-17). *Becomes:* a member by October, unannounced (P5-27).
- **(b) Whoever will not winter:** leaves by the last ship, if she judges the moving deadline right (S3-2 →3). *Becomes:* part of the flow, never taught; nobody records her leaving (contract L74).
- **(c) A household that cannot lay in a season at once** (c): where the hold reaches the household, the closing decides against whoever cannot fill a year's store in five months (P4-46 item 5). *Becomes:* dependent on a common store whose rules are unwritten (P7A-13).
- **(d) A robot reduced by a cold night:** knows the work, cannot sustain it (P7A-19). *Becomes:* someone who sizes her days to her loss; canon reads it as a change of working life, not of self (physiology L69).
- **(e) A robot built for a need she does not want** (P4-23): the law guarantees her partial self-design (physiology L422) and she may leave the trade (institutes L166). *Becomes:* free; the city is left "to need what it cannot command" (`04_Phase_04` L292).
- **(f) A human who cannot bear the dim season with no exit by travel** (P4-26, comparables only): *becomes* someone who holds out for the light's return in June, with no sea until November.
- →1 At Casey nobody is expelled for not learning; the closing sorts, and it sorts before anyone decides.
- →2 Those who cannot learn are absorbed into dependence (on heat, on the store) or released by choice (the robot who leaves a trade, the passer who leaves in March).
- →3 **The city has no failure category for its people, only for its nights**: what fails at Casey is a door or a hold, and the people it fails carry it privately (P6-18). *Expression:* nobody at Casey is ever told she is unsuited to it; she is told the date of the last ship.

**P7A-21 · The unwritten rules nobody explains, and the record nobody keeps.**
- *Unwritten rules, as the material shows them* (each a habit, never a custom, RF-6): mildness is not safety (P6-4; P6-20 (2)); the night is kept at a door, not by the sky (P4-32); a robot is safer moving than resting, and a dressed robot may still stand still (Frame L273; physiology L42-43; P4-49 (a)); the closing is a judgement, not a date (S3-2); whoever is inside tonight gets a warm place (P6-2).
- *The record nobody keeps:* no admitted line records the held, the nights a robot rested cold, the dead beyond the census of the living, a passer lost past the edge, or who made a go/no-go call (P6-12, P6-13, P6-18); robot death is handled nationally by "religion and community rather than by a professional trade" (physiology L174), and at Casey the handler is unwritten (DQ-21, written around RF-13).
- *Comparable* [present-day; W12, W13 **sourced**]: in Inuit Nunangat a community ice-monitoring service "trains and employs young Inuit as producers, operators, and technicians of its technology", measures and reports ice thickness to communities, and combines "monitoring technology with Inuit sea ice knowledge" so community members can adapt travel plans. *Mechanism taken:* tacit winter knowledge made into a taught, paid role and a public record. *Divergence:* a set of small communities on sea-ice trails, not a Band 5 city; Casey's admitted lines name no such role, and nothing is assumed (S3-4 →2 already names a kept ice record as what would dissolve or confirm the alibi).
- →1 Casey teaches its unwritten rules by holding people through a winter, and records almost none of what happens to them.
- →2 A documentary founding (S2-29) and an undocumented present stand side by side: the city knows its station from paper and its own nights from nobody.
- →3 **What Casey most needs to know about itself (how often the warm night fails, how often the call was wrong, how many winterers it never counted) is the one body of knowledge it has no one to keep.** *Expression:* an ice record or a night ledger is exactly the thing a later step may find someone keeping unofficially (P7A-23 (b)); none is sited here.

## 7d · Private life, minorities and counterculture

*Canon checked first: **no counterculture or faction is sited at Casey in canon** (orchestrator-verified; brief §1 item 1). None is created (RF-7); what follows is derived from the specific pressure, as candidates and as structure, never as a faction, and kept sympathetic (spine L928-940).*

**P7A-22 · What Casey requires of everyone, and who cannot or will not give it.**
- *The requirements, from Phases 1-6:* (1) **to stay through the shut half** once April has come, about seven months (S2-23); (2) **to keep the night indoors**, at a heated door, every night of every month (P4-5; P6-2); (3) **to read the weather and not trust mildness** (P4-9); (4) **to receive whoever is there into heat** (P6-2); (5) to lay in before April, if the hold reaches the household (P4-11, conditional).
- *Who cannot or will not give it:* whoever will not stay seven months (the flow and March leavers); whoever cannot keep a schedule in the bright weeks (P4-46 item 9); the robot who wants her leisure outdoors in the firm-snow hours (P4-46 item 10); the household that cannot lay in (P4-46 item 5); the door-holder who, in a short September, cannot both keep the store and open the door (P6-15 →2).
- →1 Most of these are not refusals of a rule but limits of a body, a purse or a season.
- →2 Casey's pressure is not ideological; it is physical, and its demand falls hardest at the seams (April; September to October).
- →3 **So whatever dissent Casey holds is about the seams, not about principles:** whether to leave before April, whether to open the door in September. *Expression:* the last open-water weeks of March as the one time of year when anyone at Casey can say no to it.

**P7A-23 · The three forms the counterculture could take, each a candidate, none sited.** (spine L931-935; RF-7)
- **(a) Refusing what the place demands:** those who will not winter, who leave each March. *A population?* Partly: the flow and any resident leavers (none recorded, contract L74). They do not oppose Casey; they leave it, and the ice makes that a once-a-year act.
- **(b) Performing what the place cannot** (spine L933-935: check deliberately where the capability reading named a lacking faculty). Phase 1 named three absent unchosen suppliers, a night in summer, a teacher and a majority (S2-55), and Phase 6 a fourth, a record of its nights (P6-18). Someone at Casey would be supplying each unofficially: **those who make darkness indoors in the bright weeks** (curtains and rooms, S3-16; P4-31 →2); **those who teach the plant from failure** (S3-25); **those who keep a record the city does not**, of the ice, the closures, the cold nights, the held and the lost (S3-4 →2; P6-18 →3). The last is the strongest candidate: a city whose founding culture is documentary (S2-29) and whose present keeps "records of a station and none of its nights" (`04_Phase_06` L299) leaves an obvious unofficial office, the ledger of what the warm night failed to cover. *Candidate only; no keeper is named, no body formed* (RF-2, RF-7).
- **(c) Demanding the place's own rule more literally than the mainstream applies it:** the rule is "whoever is here tonight, however they came, gets a warm place to rest, and the heat does not stop" (P6-2, the merge's combined wording). The mainstream applies it to whoever is at the door and lets the count reassert itself when heat is short (P7A-14). **The literalists would hold that it binds in September as in May, to the never-counted, the spur city's travelers and the held who winter again and again.** *Candidate only.*
- →1 None of the three is a refusal of Casey's values; (b) and (c) are fidelity to them in excess.
- →2 The literalists and the ledger-keepers would meet at the same edge: the uncounted at the door in a short September.
- →3 **Casey's most likely counterculture is not rebellion but conscience: people who keep the promise, and its record, more strictly than the city can afford to.** Sympathetic by construction: they are people the demand does not fit because they take it at its word.

**P7A-24 · Is the dissent a population at all? Mostly not: the contradiction between the contract and the count sits inside each person who holds a heated room.** (spine L936-939)
- *Lines:* the contract and the count "meet at one door" (P6-15, expression); "The keeper of the promise becomes, at the moment it fails, its decider" (`04_Phase_06` L293); no order is written (P7A-12 (b)); the failure leaves no mark (P6-18).
- →1 The person who must choose between the store and the stranger is not a faction's member; she is any household or keeper with heat on a short night. Both sides of the contradiction live in her: the obligation owed by the place and the store planned by the count.
- →2 So no faction can organize around it: there is no group on the side of the count against a group on the side of the contract; there is one person at one door, each time, unrecorded.
- →3 **Casey cannot be reformed from within on its central obligation, and cannot be opposed from within either, because there is nobody to organize** (spine L937-939): the contradiction is resolved door by door, night by night, and leaves no trace to argue from (P6-18). Only (b) of P7A-23, the record, could turn it into something a population could hold. *Expression:* in a short September, a whole city of private decisions that nobody will ever add up.

## Band 5: the thin shared layer of order, and its maintenance

**P7A-26 · The shared layer is thin and physical; its maintenance is distributed among keepers, door-holders and whoever is there, and its failure is a lapse, never a collapse.** (spine L949-951; `DR-4` §4.2; Frame L88, L137)

- **Its maintenance, by whom, as far as the material shows:** the keepers of heat, enclosure, clearing and water, about 53,262 on the rates [Column-3] (P7A-5); the planned layer of administration and allocation, about 123,828 on the rates [Column-3] (P7A-4); and, for every decision the plan cannot reach, whoever is there (P7A-11).
