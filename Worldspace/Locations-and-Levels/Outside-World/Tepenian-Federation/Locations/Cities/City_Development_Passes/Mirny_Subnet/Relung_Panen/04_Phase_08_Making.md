*What the words carry.* What this place's own conditions demand of the things people make is the crossing, many times a day, between a warm interior that has no season and an outdoors that is two places in one year. **The interior has no season:** the export and the city's own food are lit indoors year-round (spec L258; `DR-96` item 4, rulings L1633; `18` L12, L19 `[DoI corroboration]`), so "There is no harvest day" (Phase 4 L128); the warm rooms and the robot half's charging rooms keep one climate in every month (Phase 3 P3-18; S3-27). **The outdoors is two places:** for nine to ten months it is still and freezing, an annual mean wind of 4.6 m/s (spec L143) with monthly means below 0 °C from February to November (spec L180–189), so the ordinary crossing is cold without much wind (Phase 4 P4-7); for three months it is a melt on bare rock, meltwater, saturated regolith and slush (spec L171–172, L203), the average day above freezing and the average night below it (spec L179–180, L190 `[computed, REQ-52]`). **What every household therefore makes and keeps in common is the kit for that crossing** (A's inventory, B's function):
1. **the threshold kit for still, dry cold:** coat and gloves on and off at every door, with a layer carried for the storm episode that comes with hours of warning (S3-15; Phase 3 P3-14; Phase 4 P4-7);
2. **the melt kit, December to February:** feet dressed for water by day and refrozen slush by morning, and a second pair of gloves (Phase 3 P3-15; Phase 4 P4-13);
3. **the dark made by hand, and the lamp:** a curtain against a December sun that barely sets, lamps in a June of about 2.7 h (spec L151–156; S3-28; Phase 4 P4-4);
4. **the warm charging room, every month,** for the half of the people who are robots (frame S0-10; S3-27; Phase 4 P4-6);
5. **the tools of the year's keeping:** heat and picks at the culverts before the melt (S3-4; Phase 3 P3-36);
6. **the city's own lit produce, the same in every month** (spec L258; Phase 4 P4-3).
Food crosses too: fresh produce is injured just below freezing and the outdoor air is below freezing most of the year, so freshness has to be kept warm in transit while frozen things keep for free (S3-37, S3-40) *(B)*. **And the kit's own materials arrive:** the site gives rock, ice, lake water, salt and cold, and nothing it gives is written as made into the kit; everything else comes by the one road, the two air doors on the gulf ice or the line (P8-7, P8-29) *(C, carried as a sentence inside the axis, not as the axis)*. Beside all of it, **national, not this place's,** one common tongue, Hawaiian (later Tepenian) (`DR-100` item 1).

*Peer-free test, could this be true anywhere?* No. It needs three things at once, each on this place's own lines: **a still, freezing outdoors for most of the year** (spec L143, L180–189; calms in 39% of central-oasis observations, spec L146 `[DEM]` `[PA-3]`), so the crossing is short and windless rather than a struggle against weather; **a genuine melt on bare rock inside ice walls** that turns the same crossing wet and refreezing for three months (spec L113–116, L171–172, L203); and **a seasonless interior** holding the city's food, its warmth and its robots' charge (spec L258; S3-27; Phase 4 P4-6), under **a light year with no polar night** but a June day of 2.7 h and a December sun that barely sets (spec L151–156). Take away the stillness and the kit is for wind and the storm layer becomes the everyday coat; take away the melt and there is one kit all year; give the interior a growing season and the table follows the sky; give the place a polar night or a dark summer and the curtain and the lamp change places. **What could be true anywhere, and so is not the axis:** cold met by clothing in itself (physiology L38–57, national); Hawaiian as the common tongue (national); the shared night (national, physiology L336–338); siligel and coolant (national); indoor lit growing on its own (spec L258: "as everywhere"); household-kept origin forms in any many-origin place on their own (the transplanted layer, which Process A requires of every location, `03` L992; written in section A, not in the axis). It rests on admitted, measured and ruled lines; the daily zero-crossing and the every-month clauses carry `[computed, REQ-52]`; it does not rest on `[PA-12]`, `[PROV-8]`, the held ground, animals, any unruled door, or any origin row. **The axis was split in Round 3** (split 1). It goes to this location's own row of the differentiation instrument at Step 6, write-only; no other row was read.
