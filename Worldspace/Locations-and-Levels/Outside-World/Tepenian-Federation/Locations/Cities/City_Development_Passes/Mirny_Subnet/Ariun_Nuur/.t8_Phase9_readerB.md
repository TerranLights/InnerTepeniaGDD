## C · Relations between the populations: the local inflection, not the national baseline

**The axis of this section: the place sorts its people by when they arrived and whether they are needed; kind decides only what each one pays, and the place, not anyone's policy, sets the skew.**

**FIND-P9-119 · The national baseline, supplied and not re-invented.** *"egalitarian, skewed in favor of robots."* Relations are overwhelmingly positive everywhere, and where robots hold a real edge it is not adversarial, resentful or exploitative (RP L377; frame PV-4). The national exclusion of humans from places where altitude and cold together kill (RP L379) does not hold for the city as a whole, since 156,685 humans live here (frame DL-3; datasheet L19). Recorded as the parent's supply, as PV-4 asks; its class is put to the developer (Q-B102). *[B]*

**FIND-P9-120 · The local inflection: the place skews by act, not by standing.** The baseline's skew is a standing; what the files write here is a cost, and it falls on each kind at a different act:
- **in the air,** the place favors robots entirely: altitude costs a robot nothing and the human minority everything it costs (frame DL-14; FIND-P9-113);
- **at the stop,** the place charges robots hardest: stopping outdoors is a robot's danger, and her charge must be taken warm (FIND-P9-107);
- **at the table,** it charges humans: food comes by the road, keyed to the human body (FIND-P9-114);
- **at the place of rest,** it charges both at once: heat for both, air for the human, warm charge for the robot (spine §3a standing cost 1; Phase 3 FIND-P3-28).
→ **No admitted line writes a social mechanism by which either kind holds an edge here; the edge the files do write is physical, and it runs both ways:** the minority that pays most is a body, not a stock (Phase 7 FIND-P7-43), and the majority pays most where it stops. → *Second order:* because the skew is the place's, neither kind has anyone to resent for it; the costs are read as one's own (Phase 4 FIND-P4-35). → *Expression:* in this city a human counts her minutes outdoors and her breaths at night; a robot counts her charge and never stops in the cold. PROPOSED; no policy, grievance or allocation (RF-B102). *[B]*

**FIND-P9-121 · One supply under both kinds, in the same hours.** Heat, melted water, light and robots' charge ride one supply whose source is not written (spine §8e; `DEC-705`). Everyone stops at the same clock hours, robots to charge and humans to sleep, so the hours of rest are the hours the supply carries the most at once (Phase 4 FIND-P4-6, FIND-P4-7; SH-14). → **Each kind's night hangs on the other's draw:** the robot majority's charge and the human minority's heat are one load at one hour. → *Second order:* a household that holds both bodies meets its two hardest seasons in the same months, a different place for the human and the same place colder for the robot (Phase 4 FIND-P4-31). Whether such households are common here is not written; nationally, the human founders came because they had made their lives among robots (FIND-P9-115). No load, rate or source (RF-107). *[B]*

**FIND-P9-122 · The category that matters more than kind: the order of arrival.** Every arrival, of either body and any of the 25 rows, must be taught a place that can kill, by someone taught the same way; the teacher is always the earlier arrival (spine G8 standing cost 3; Phase 2 FIND-P2-40). The debt is owed forward and never back (Phase 6 FIND-P6-4). A newcomer becomes a member when the direction of instruction turns around (Phase 5 FIND-P5-35). Mentor and mentee bonds are a genuine robot institution (RP L418), and a robot learns from the humans and robots of her community (RP L210–211), so **teacher and pupil cross kinds as easily as they share one.** What is taught first is the same for every body and every stock (Phase 7 FIND-P7-27). → **The place reorganizes its people along arrival order: who came before whom decides who teaches whom, who owes whom, and who has become a member, and kind does not enter it.** The inter-kind question answers itself underneath: robots and humans relate here first as earlier and later arrivals, and the question of kind sets only what each lesson is about. A robot's first lesson is to charge warm; a human's first polar night is her hardest season (Phase 2 FIND-P2-19). → *Second order:* only the founders arrived owing no person (Phase 6 FIND-P6-5), and the founding wave's descendants hold no written standing (FIND-P9-127). The order is a sequence every resident moves through, never a rank anyone holds. → *Third order:* the newest arrival, of either kind, is the one on whom the debt's payment depends (Phase 6 FIND-P6-6), and whoever never becomes the earlier arrival stays a permanent newcomer (Phase 7 FIND-P7-36; Phase 6 SH-23). PROPOSED; no rite, test, title, seniority or status is written (RF-B101; RF-131). *[B]*

**FIND-P9-123 · The second line: being needed, and it too crosses kinds.** The archive is so large that no single individual, *human or robot*, fully understands it, and its understanding is spread across the whole research population (spec L179). So a researcher becomes a member when a question first comes to her (Phase 5 FIND-P5-35), and the bond is drawn by work, not origin (research UI-4; RF-63). → **The one line in the files along which the city divides into groups is this: the research population, whom the archive needs, against the rest, for whom no listed line writes a purpose of their own** (Phase 4 FIND-P4-60; Phase 7 FIND-P7-42). **FIND-P9-124 · The minority's chief cost is unspeakable to the majority.** Thin air is a deficit with no one in particular to remedy it (spine D-6).
