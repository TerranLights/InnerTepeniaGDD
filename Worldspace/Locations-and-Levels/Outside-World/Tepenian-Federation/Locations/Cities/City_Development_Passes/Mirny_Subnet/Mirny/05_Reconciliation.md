### 3.1 · Mirny as Phases 2–10 found it

Mirny, read only through its own pass, is a Band 5 Settlement of 1,351,430 at Census I (665,901 humans, 685,529 robots; robots 50.73 %) on the Antarctic Circle, a rocky, exposed coast whose one defining condition is **wind, not cold**: mean 12.0 m/s, about 3 % calm-or-weak, snow retention about 38 %, a ground blizzard that blinds under a clear sky, and cues that are honest but that nobody at the founding had been taught to read. Its year is dated by light — no polar night, about 29 days of midnight sun, a 2.4-hour June — because its thermometer holds a plateau from May to September. Its founders (Idelsk-Uralia as sponsoring founding nation outside the country; Russia, Australia and the Sinian Federation as the exile groups; `REG` L57, `DR-46`) were placed by assignment, not choice, on the treaty's terms, among fourteen long-run stocks with China the largest and no majority, and arrived to a working station and a written record (journals, logs, manuals) that no living person had made and no living teacher could explain. Mirny is the hub of nine members, and its relay is the subnet's single routing point.

Out of three admitted conditions — an unreadable but honest wind, a record without a teacher, a relay that is the only path — Phases 2–10 derive **one unwritten Social Contract** (Phase 6): *the medium holds; the reading is yours.* The medium nobody here authored (the inherited record and the relay) is held to the standard of not failing; the reading of the wind is each person's own, unwritten, answered for privately; so a loss is booked to the person, to "weather," or to silence, and never to the medium, and **the book never learns**. Order follows it (Phase 7: claims on the quarry, the relay and the hours with no arbiter in any admitted file; fast calls fall to whoever is there and the person answers; the nation's mandate is a need-test with no mandator; no local prohibition has an author). Making follows it (Phase 8: *prepared, not predicted* — coats keyed to the wind, a bare hand that is an indoor hand, upkeep that refills after every storm and leaves nothing to show, a vocabulary of *now* with no word for *soon*). Populations follow it (Phase 9: sorted by **formation** — formed here or arrived; a wind line anyone can cross by exposure and a memory line only arrivals carry — and ranked by neither; by Act 2 the remembering side is the founding-cohort robots). The catalog follows it (Phase 10: what stays was made by no one here or is made for others; what residents make for themselves refills; no place is named, none is formed).

Throughout, ordinary life is the general answer: a robot or a human at near-equal odds, in the same coat at the same door, keeping a shared stop indoors and crossing between interiors, with leisure owed to nobody (established for the robot half) and an empty middle of what people actually do with their hours.
