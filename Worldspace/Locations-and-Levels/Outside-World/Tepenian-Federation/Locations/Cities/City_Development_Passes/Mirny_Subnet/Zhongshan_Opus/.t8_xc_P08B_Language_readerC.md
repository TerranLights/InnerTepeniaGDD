- **Finding 215 (what *"still audible"* can mean after ~250 years: real-world evidence).** Searched (section 7, searches 1 and 7).
  In new-dialect formation (koineization), speakers of different forms of one language who settle together typically produce one
  focused local form within two or three generations (Trudgill's three-stage model, about one stage per generation; sometimes
  in the first native-born generation, sometimes the fourth or later). The new form shows *"fewer marked or minority linguistic
  features"* than its inputs, and some variants survive leveling and are reallocated to new social or linguistic functions. Work on
  "colonial lag" finds a transplanted variety conservative feature by feature, not as a whole system. Two and a half centuries is
  many times three human generations. *And what does that cause?* If the branches mixed (*"Then both drifted together"*, L834), the
  real-world expectation is one local form carrying layers from both clocks, not two forms heard side by side; two forms side by
  side would need the branches to have stayed socially apart, which `DRQ-18` leaves open (L843-844). *And then?* This agrees with
  the pass's own framing: L843 asks how much of the older branch's speech *"survives into the modern vernacular"*, one vernacular.
  *Third order:* what marks someone as from here is the local form itself, and which branch a speaker descends from could survive
  only as reallocated variants whose social meaning is unwritten. The guard (L845) forbids listing them. The marker *"still audible"*
  stands, read as layers in one speech (Choice 240).
- **Finding 216 (newer exiles and the founders: real-world evidence).** Searched (section 7, searches 8 and 9). Mufwene's founder
  principle: the features of the founder population had an advantage over later competitors, and founders could keep that
  influence even as a minority. Zelinsky's doctrine of first effective settlement: the first group to establish a viable,
  self-perpetuating society shapes a place's later culture more than much larger later immigration. *And what does that cause?* On
  that evidence, a small stream of newer exiles (Finding 213) would more likely be absorbed into the city's Mandarin than reset it;
  only a large one could move it, and canon gives no size (`P02` Finding 9). This is context for `P02`'s Choice 55 (*"How much
  authority the city gives such a person is left unwritten"*, `P02` L668-669), not wording (Choice 241). *And then?* The same
  evidence asks which group was the first effective settlement here: the resident branch, on this ground for ~481 years before
  2564 (L828), or the 2564 founders. *Third order:* that turns on whether the resident branch was a viable, self-continuing
  community at the founding, which is exactly `DRQ-18` (L843-844); if it was, the real-world tendency favors the older branch's
  features in the modern local form; if not, the founders'. This gives `DRQ-18` a sharper form for the developer (Question 231).

  senescence, no background attrition; robots die in incidents, many at once or none for a long while"*. `Frame` L351: *"Robots live

  single long-lived speakers as well as handed down by human generations, and a robot who came online here takes up the local form
  as it stood that day. Focusing in new-dialect formation is driven by children acquiring the mix (Finding 215), and a robot learns
  her origin by immersion *"without a childhood or adolescence"* (`DR-11`, quoted at `P02` L448). *Third order:* the city's speech
  then holds clocks set at many dates, one per robot, and the 2564 form may still be spoken late in the frame by some of its first
  speakers. That is a reference to the founding branch's form inside the city, not to the source as it now is, so it leaves
  *"neither can go back to the source"* standing. All of this is conditional on how the Language Module's *"tracks"* (`Frame` L350)
  works after immersion (Question 232).
