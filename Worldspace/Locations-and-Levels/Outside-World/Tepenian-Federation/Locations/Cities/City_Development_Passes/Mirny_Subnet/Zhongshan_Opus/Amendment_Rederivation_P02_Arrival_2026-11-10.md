The step from "no return" to "no new arrivals" is the pass's own: neither clause it cites speaks of arrival.

**Finding 1 (address).** The note's `C10` row (note L204) cites `04_Phase_02` L215-224 for "§2.5's last row and the block under it".
As read, L215-216 are two other rows (three origin nations removed by the 2026-07-06 rework; one origin demoted), which this item does
not touch; the visitor row is L217 and the block is L219-226. The first check found the same (`first check C10` L149-150).

**(b) What holds.**

- **Finding 2 (the one-way direction holds, for every human who came, at any date).** IV.2.3 reads *"No human person who has made
  the election described in paragraph 2 may thereafter return to the territory of any Assembly Power"* (`Treaty` L77), and IV.2.2
  now names both the founding election and the later one: *"or who elects to take up residence there under Article II.2.5"* (L76).
  So nobody who came to stay, at the founding or later, may go back. *"The door only opened one way"*, *"no returning travelers"*
  (`04_Phase_02` L221), *"no return trips"* and *"no visiting cousins"* (L217) hold, since a visit home means going back.
- **Finding 3 (the robots' no-return ground stays IV.1, as the pass reads it).** IV.1 is outside every reader's list. IV.2.1 says
  *"nor shall any human person be subject to Article IV.1"* (`Treaty` L75), which agrees with the pass's reading that IV.1 is the
  robots' clause. Article II.2.1 (L46) does not, on its words, bar a robot's return: its two classes are robots *"presently
  residing"* in Assembly Power territory at the Treaty's date and robots who *"may hereafter come into being within such
  territory"*, and a robot who left and came back is in neither (split settled in section 5).
- **Finding 4 (the law's outcome holds without closure).** *"the output is a Tepenian culture descended from that stock, not the
  stock itself"* (`04_Phase_02` L223-224) holds. The pass names separation as the divergence operator's *separation* input
  (L222-223), one input among others, and the outward half of it is unchanged. Later arrivals join a Tepenian culture; they do not
  reverse it. What does not hold is the strength the pass gives the input, *"stated at maximum strength"* (L223).

**(d) Its grounds, cited.**

- **Finding 5 (the closure no longer follows).** The Treaty now fills the silence the pass read as a bar. Any human who has not made
  the founding election *"may, at any time after the effective date of this Treaty, freely elect to take up residence within the
  Federation of Tepenia"* (II.2.5, `Treaty` L50). The draft's note dates the paragraph 2026-11-09 and says the earlier silence is one
  *"that had been misread elsewhere as an implicit prohibition"*, and that relocation *"is open at any time after the Treaty's
  effective date, not just during the founding exile wave"* (L138). The developer's general canon says the same: *"humans could
  continue arriving into Tepenia"* after the signing (`DR-127` item 2, note L139-140; robots were not answered, note L141). So none
  of these follows any longer: *"not one of them has been refreshed from its source"*, *"No new arrivals from outside"*, *"no
  correction from the origin"*, *"a closed system"* and *"stated at maximum strength"* (L220-223), nor L314's *"nothing has been
  refreshed from source"*.
- **Finding 6 (the closure, kept in the form the Treaty supports: per person).** IV.2.2 and IV.2.3 (`Treaty` L76-77) bind each
  elector from the day of the election, whatever its date. So the door closes behind each person, not behind the city or the origin
  culture: **every origin culture is open inward and closed outward.** This form saves much of the pass's downstream language,
  which then needs a change of subject rather than deletion (Choice 54).
- **Finding 7 (later arrivals are newer exiles, on the founders' terms).** II.2.5's last sentence says such a person *"shall for all
  purposes of this Treaty be treated as a person who has made the election described in paragraph 3"* (`Treaty` L50); by that
  election she is deemed to have *"willingly and permanently relinquished any claim to home, residency, or quarter within Assembly
  Power territory"* (L50); IV.2.2 strips her personhood there (L76); IV.2.3 bars her return (L77). *And what does that cause?* A later
  arrival is self-selected: someone who gave up home and personhood in the origin country, the price `04_Phase_02` L313 names for
  the founders (*"chosen at the price of no return"*). *And then?* She carries the source as it stood on the day she left, and can
  never go back to check it again. *Third order:* the restated lines say that each origin culture *could receive newer exiles*, not
  that it *was refreshed*, *kept in step* or *corrected*; whether the city takes a newer exile's version as authoritative is
  unwritten (Choice 55).
- **Finding 8 (the robot channel is compulsory where it operates, and its cultural load is bounded).** II.2.1 does more than permit:
  every robot *"who may hereafter come into being within such territory"* shall depart *"in accordance with such schedule and such
  means of conveyance as the Assembly Powers shall provide"* (`Treaty` L46), and it *"applies to robots as a class"* (L47). Whether any
  did come into being there after the Treaty is not answered (note L141). A robot's origin is *"Learned, not built in"*, absorbed
  while her consciousness develops after coming online (`DR-11`, `rulings log` L458), and culture reaches robots *"through the local
  culture the founding population built"* (L460). *And what does that cause?* A robot sent later brings only what she absorbed
  between coming online and her departure, a span the Assembly Powers' schedule set and no line on the inputs gives. *And then?* A
  robot sent soon after coming online learns mostly here. *Third order:* a robot stream, at any size, adds to the robot count more
  surely than it brings the source, so the human stream is the stronger channel; and if such arrivals were large, robot origin
  shares could drift from the human ones and Guard 5 would be run again (Finding 36). The first check's *"such a robot would carry
  its origin as it stood when the robot left"* (`first check C10` L132) holds only as far as her immersion before she left (Choice
  73; Question 75).
- **Finding 9 (canon gives permission and no figure; the census neither shows arrivals nor rules them out).** II.2.5 and `DR-127`
  item 2 give permission only. The pass reports the national census as conserved between the two counts: *"nobody was born or died
  in the transition, they relocated."* and *"Grand totals are identical, `32,026,600` both."* (`01_Inherited` L104-105). A total held
  with no births and no deaths is a property of how the two counts are built, arithmetic of the census, not an in-world record of
  who moved (Choice 61). In that arithmetic the national total carries no net arrival between Census I and Census II; it cannot be
  read as evidence that nobody arrived. Arrivals before Census I would sit inside the Census I counts, unmarked; arrivals after Census
  II appear in neither count. Every composition row has the same share at both counts (`spec` L36-70; Finding 31), so the table
  cannot separate a founding layer from a later one. So no pass line may give the inward stream a size: writing "nearly closed" or
  "steady" in place of "closed" would be a forced fit (Questions 74, 78).
- **Finding 10 (the visitor row's absolute was never grounded).** *"ANYONE FROM ANY ORIGIN NATION, EVER, AS A VISITOR"* (L217) rests
  on IV.2.3, which binds only a person *"who has made the election"* (`Treaty` L77). Two groups lie outside it: a person in Assembly
  Power territory who never elects, and a human born in the Federation who never made an election. For both, the inputs hold neither
  a bar nor an admission. V.3 lets the parties conclude agreements on *"trade, the exchange of goods, and lawful passage between their
  respective territories"* (L92). This is not a change of input: the pass itself cites V.3 and VI.1 (`04_Phase_02` L224-225), and its
  own L225 says *"VI.1 permits inspection observers"*; whether such observers come from Assembly Power territory and leave again
  depends on VI.1's text, which is on no reader's list (split settled in section 5). The row reads silence as prohibition, the error
  the Treaty's note now names for arrival (L138). Of L217's absences, *"No relatives from home"* no longer follows, because a relative
  may come to stay (II.2.5); *"no one who has seen the origin country and come back"* holds only in its "come back" sense, since a
  later arrival has seen it (Question 76).
- **Finding 11 ("or their descendants").** *"no provision anywhere returns an exiled person or their descendants"* (L225-226) is true
  as an absence: no provision on the inputs returns anyone. Any bar it implies for a descendant who never elected rests on silence,
  since only those who elected are barred (IV.2.3, L77). The restated wording limits the clause to those who elected (split settled
  in section 5; Question 76).
- **Finding 12 (the Acts).** Only Act 1's range is on the inputs: *"2564 -> somewhere in the 2600s (a hazy range)"* (note L133-134).
  Whether a later arrival weighs differently in different periods depends on the Acts, whose content is not on the inputs; no
  wording here rests on period weights (Choice 70; Question 74).
- **Finding 13 (the L314 rating).** L314's rating, *"genuinely emergent · deep"*, was given to the closure. The restated finding is
  read straight from the Treaty (II.2.1, II.2.5, IV.2.2, IV.2.3), so all three readers rate it "canon-derived"; the depth is the
  developer's call (Choice 57).
- **Finding 14 (a figure in the block belongs to another piece).** L220's *"Eighteen origin cultures"* is a count; the composition
  table now has 35 rows (note L106; `spec` L36-70). That belongs to the composition pieces. The restated wording carries no count
  (Choice 60).
- **Finding 15 (a record note on II.2.1).** The Treaty's change note dates only II.2.5 and the IV.2.2 cross-reference as new (L138),
  and the pass quotes the same II.2.1 sentence's *"schedule and… means of conveyance."* at `04_Phase_05` L134. So the pass likely had
  the "hereafter" clause before it, and the robot half of its closure may have been an inference error when written. This is a
  likelihood only: no input shows the Treaty as it read on the pass's date. Under D-6 the re-derivation uses the Treaty as it reads
  today, so this moves no wording (Choice 68).

**(e) The first check's verdict, and whether this piece agrees.** `VERDICT: FLIPS` (`first check C10` L4). **This piece agrees:** the

the in-frame reading (robots used the orbital exit more, through a door that only went outward).

## 7 · Questions for the developer

- **Question 74 (`C10`, the human stream).** Treaty II.2.5 and `DR-127` item 2 permit humans to arrive after the signing. Did any
  reach Zhongshan, and on what scale and in which periods: a trickle, a steady flow, or waves? Does a later arrival weigh differently
  in Act 1 (2564 to somewhere in the 2600s) than later? Canon gives permission, not a figure, and the census rows cannot show
  arrivals, so a re-derivation cannot size the stream without inventing one (Findings 9, 12).
- **Question 75 (`C10` and `C9`, the robot stream).** Treaty II.2.1 requires every robot who comes into being in Assembly Power
  territory after the Treaty to be sent to the Federation, and `DR-127` left robot arrivals open. Did robots keep coming into being
  there? If they did, roughly how long did a robot spend there, immersed, before her conveyance? That interval decides how much of
  her origin she brings rather than learns here (Finding 8; Choice 73).
- **Question 76 (`C10`, people who never elected).** IV.2.3 binds only those who elected. Did any V.3 agreement on *"lawful passage"*
  (`Treaty` L92) let a person who never elected visit Zhongshan and leave again, and do the VI.1 inspection observers the pass names
  come and go? Is a human born in the Federation, who never made an election, barred from going to Assembly Power territory? The
  pass's *"EVER, AS A VISITOR"* (L217) and *"or their descendants"* (L226) turn on the answers (Findings 10, 11).
- **Question 77 (`C10`, goods and information).** Did trade under V.3 (*"the exchange of goods"*), or supply from inside the Federation,
  bring garments to Zhongshan during the ~250 years? May the repair chain's first link rest on scarce replacement, grounded on the
  corrected spec line (*"commercial activity is modest and Zhongshan is not a trade hub"*, note L125-126)? Did recordings, letters or
  other carried information cross? The "no replacement" link, and whether the source could reach the city without a person arriving,
  depend on the answers (Finding 42).
- **Question 78 (`C10` and `C6`, the conserved total).** The pass reports the national census as conserved between the counts, with
  nobody born or dying (`01_Inherited` L103-105). Is that meant in-world, so that nobody arrived between Census I and Census II, or is
  it how the counts are built? If you rule a flow between the two counts, the census's conservation statement would change with it,
  and the place of the inversion is checked again (Findings 9, 29).
- **Question 79 (`C6`, the in-world reading).** `DR-12` lets the counts stand in-fiction as headcounts (L473), and D-6 makes the rates
  arithmetic of the census. May a re-derivation keep the reading that robots used the orbital exit at a markedly higher rate than
  humans, and that the door went only outward, labeled as readings of that arithmetic? Or must it say only that the robot count fell
  further than the human count (Findings 25, 26, 28)?
- **Question 80 (`C6`, the destination).** Is Zhongshan's 22.49% not in Census II (270,712) to be read as relocation to orbit alone,
  or may it include moves between places on the surface? Does any line state that the city's whole difference went to the orbital
  tier? `01_Inherited` L103-107 settles the national total only, and `00_Frame` §0.1c, cited for the 22.10% at `04_Phase_05` L136, is
  on no reader's list (Finding 27).
- **Question 81 (`C9`, robot percentages).** `DR-128` item 1 lets an origin row carry robots at whatever percent makes sense for them,
  and Zhongshan's spec copies the human share into every robot cell. Do the copied figures stand as Zhongshan's robots' own
  percentages, or will they be set separately? If separately: should the founding robots, who came online in Assembly Power territory,
  carry percentages of their own, while robots who came online here keep the copied weighting, and is a robot who came online in the
  Federation counted under the origin of the local culture she was immersed in? Note that II.2.3 (`Treaty` L48) has the human founders
  following the robot population they lived among, the reverse of the spec's direction of derivation. If any robot percentage is set
  apart, Guard 5 and Finding 23 are run again (Findings 36-38).
- **Question 82 (`C9` and `C10`, a founding robot's clock).** Can a robot of the 2564 founding wave still be present later in the
  frame? If so, she carries her pre-2564 immersion on a single clock that does not turn over by generation, which bears on the
  two-clocks block at `04_Phase_08` L828-834 (Finding 45).
