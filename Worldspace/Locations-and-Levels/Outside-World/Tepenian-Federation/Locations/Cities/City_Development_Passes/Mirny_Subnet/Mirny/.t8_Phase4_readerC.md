### B.1 ROUTINES

**What a routine can and cannot be here.** The admitted files give no shift pattern, no meal time, no work hours, no school or care schedule. A schedule would be invented. What they do give are **fixed points set by bodies and a set of crossings priced by physics.**

**P4-C1 · The rest is the one fixed hour; the sun floats around it.** *[IND + ADM + CORR + D]* — **the shared-rest answer.**
*Population:* the general resident, both bodies. Continuous-coverage work is a scoped exception (§Backward check BC-2, §E1).
- *Grounds.* `RP`: "Robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons." and "they require downtime, most prominently OVERNIGHT RECHARGING." The shared-night gloss that follows in `RP` is built on the polar night; Mirny has none ("no polar night; ~29 days of midnight sun (~Dec 8 → Jan 5)", `CON` C-2), so the gloss is not imported (`FRM` RP-5). `P3`: for the robot body `P3` finds that light has no admitted effect on recharge [CORR, D].
- *1st:* the rest is set inside the bodies, not by the sky, while the sun's presence runs from 2.4 hours (June) to a computed 24 (December), a tenfold swing [ADM]. Nothing admitted ties the length of the rest to daylight.
- *2nd:* for ~29 days (the canon figure; `RES` §2 also gives ~27 in-frame, and nothing here rests on the difference) no sunset ends the day, and `RES` adds "No darkness beyond civil twilight for about 80 days (Nov 11 → Jan 30)" [CORR]. In May–August the sun is up at most 7.6 hours a day, so at least 16.4 hours a day lie below the horizon (table below). So **the rest happens under a risen sun for the lit weeks and under a set sun for most of the long-night months**, and in neither case does the sky cue the stop the way a sunset would for part of the year.
- *3rd:* [D] The shared rest at Mirny is therefore **an agreement the bodies keep with each other rather than with the sky.** What that causes: (i) *morning* and *evening* attach to the rest and the waking, not to the sun; (ii) **the sun is a calendar here and the body is the clock**: light keeps moving while the five months May–September hold within 1.0 °C of one another (`P3` P3-8 [CORR]), so the sky dates the year and says nothing certain about the hour (daylight moves 3–5 hours a month except near the June and December extremes); (iii) the season in which no dusk ends the day (`SPN` G2: "no dusk ends the day in the one season that permits outdoor work" [CORR], whose gate-wording is flagged at BC-1) is the season in which ending the day is something a person does. Where `SPN` §9's shadow candidate, the nightless catch-up, would attach: whether it occurs is REQUESTED.
