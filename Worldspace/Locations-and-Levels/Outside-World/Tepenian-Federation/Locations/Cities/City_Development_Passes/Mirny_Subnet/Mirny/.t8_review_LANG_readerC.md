## 3 · The re-derivation

### R-1 · F-3 · Mirny's common layers, now that the civic tongue is defined

**Proposed finding.** The place's conditions (wind and drift, light, the relay, the record as a found object, the ring and sheltered core, the ten-month fast ice and the open-water window, the coastal rock) remain **the only native layer** every resident, in either body and from any stock, meets with nothing to translate. Beside them, from the founding on, Mirny has a second shared layer of another kind: **the civic tongue, Hawaiian (later Tepenian), national and not native.** Canon now defines it as a pidgin of named bases, and every resident who has learned it meets it without translation. The conditions are the common ground a person can meet only here; the civic tongue is the common medium a person can bring here.

**First order (what is so).**
- [ADM] `DR-100` item 1, the log L1669: Hawaiian (later Tepenian) "is the dominant language everywhere".
- [ADM] A1, the log L1895: "Hawaiian is a pidgin with a primary base of English and Japanese and a supporting base of Korean, Tagalog, Ilocano and Mexican Spanish; later, influence from a wide variety of languages made Hawaiian become Tepenian (so they are one language over time)."
- [ADM, through the pass] P08 L269: dominant and civic "for both bodies from the founding on". The human half is ruled (P08 L297: "a human born at Mirny learns Hawaiian, and a parent's language too where it differs"); the robot half is [D, conditional on AR-40] (P08 L277, L301).
- [ADM, the pass's own [CORR]] Phase 2 L276–L282: the conditions.
- [D] So two things are shared by both bodies at Mirny: the conditions and the civic tongue.

**Second order (what that causes).**
- [D] **They are shared in different ways. The conditions need no language and still need teaching.** A resident meets the wind with nothing between her and it on her first day, but its cues are "honest and unread" (Phase 2 L277) until taught: "For a newcomer or a visitor a cue is unread until learned, a condition of a person, not of the place." (P08 L339).
- [D] **The civic tongue is itself the thing learned.** It is met without translation only by a resident who has it: the Mirny-born human once she has learned it (P08 L297), the robot by derivation (P08 L301), and anyone who arrives already speaking it. The pass already names a resident who does not yet have it (P9 L175, "before she has the dominant language", conditional on AR-45; F-4 held and is not re-derived here).
- [D] So the "only" of Phase 2 L283 survives with a scope: universal and free of language (the conditions) as against dominant and learned (the civic tongue). L284's "the shared medium was site, record and relay" and "whatever is common must be built on them" do not survive as written: the civic tongue is common and is not built on them.
- [ADM + D] **The layers below the civic tongue each have a reach that canon defines.** The supporting tier: "widely enough understood that business, social talk and general dealings can be done in it and people can be assumed to follow" (`DR-100` item 2, L1669), at Mirny English and Russian (`DR-111`, L1769). Household languages: "plural, by household and by build community, and no one of those languages is the city's" (P08 L304). Ordered by reach, Mirny's shared media run from the household, through the supporting tier and the civic tongue, to the one layer that needs no speech, the conditions [D: the ordering is mine; each layer's definition is cited]. The record's text stands outside this order until its language is known (P08 L278, L310).

**Third order (and what that causes).**
- [D] **A resident's language ties her to the national frame, not to Mirny.** The civic tongue is the one shared layer a person can carry in; the conditions are the one shared layer nobody carries in. What a resident has in common with every other resident *because she lives at Mirny* is therefore the conditions and, in speech, the vocabulary of the wind (P08 L304: "What the city shares in speech is the civic language plus a conditions vocabulary of its own wind"; P08 L307: "What is Mirny's in speech is the conditions vocabulary of its own wind"). Those words are not admitted (P08 L279), and which language they are in is the developer's naming (P10 L395, R-118's operative point).
- [D] **The two layers meet in one open slot: the language the protocol is kept and taught in.** The conditions are met without translation but read only once taught, and teaching passes through some language, so the native layer depends on a language for its reading. Which one is open: P08 L280, "**In which language(s) the protocol is kept and taught** (AR-45; reserved AF8-6: the protocol kept in the civic tongue because it is civic)". The reserved reading is noted, not adopted. This is where a defined civic tongue matters most for Mirny, and it is not decided here.
- [D] **"From any stock."** The conditions belong to no language. The civic tongue, by A1's own words, is built from named languages. Whether that gives any resident an easier start is stated in no admitted line and is **not derived**: "A nation is not a language." (P08 L293), and the mapping of nations to languages is recalled knowledge (`DR-100` item 5, L1669). "From any stock" stays a property of the conditions only.
- [D] **Whether any of Mirny's wind words becomes one of the "wide variety" of later influences on the national tongue** (L1895) is in no line. It is an open slot and is not filled.

**Act.** [ADM] Neither A1 nor the pass dates the change to Tepenian (L1894: "Later in the future"; P08 L269: "**The change to Tepenian carries no date or Act in the rule, so this piece ties it to no Act and not to the Tower**"; P9 L176). [D] The finding holds in both Acts: the native layer is the place's physics, and the civic layer is one language over time under either name.

### R-2 · F-5 · English at Mirny, in two places at once

**Proposed finding.** At Mirny English stands in-world in two places at once: as one of the two supporting-tier languages, with Russian, by the developer's special ruling (`DR-111`), and as a primary base of the dominant civic tongue (A1). So an English authoring label in this catalog could be read as an attested in-world name in either. The rule answers both without change: every label is an authoring label for an admitted referent, in English, never attested speech, and never a place name; no word is coined in any language.

**First order.**
- [ADM] `DR-111`: the developer at L1768, *"English and Russian, as a special ruling"*; recorded at L1769.
- [ADM] A1, the log L1895; the developer's words at L1894.
- [ADM, through the pass] the rule, P08 L316 and L348; P10 L393, "the catalog coins no word in any language".
- [CALC] The rule's output, from P9 L174's figures: English bloc 25.23 to 34.38 %, mid-point (25.23 + 34.38) / 2 = 29.805; next bloc 15.77 to 16.74 %, mid-point (15.77 + 16.74) / 2 = 16.255; gap 29.805 − 16.255 = 13.55 > 12, so by mid-points the 12-point rule gives English alone. Near ends: 25.23 − 16.74 = 8.49 < 12. Far ends: 34.38 − 15.77 = 18.61. P9 L174's "about 13.5", "8.49" and "18.61" stand. This is `DR-100`'s own bloc arithmetic, not a gap between two rows of one tier (the review note L104–L106), and `DR-111` sets the tier aside from it.

**Second order.**
- [D] **The hazard widens from a tier to the whole city.** The supporting tier is a language people "can be assumed to follow" (L1669); the civic tongue is dominant for both bodies (L1669; P08 L269). A label read as a supporting-tier English word would be read as speech some residents use; a label read as a word of the civic tongue would be read as the speech of the whole city.
- [D] **The second misreading is the costlier one.** The civic tongue is synthesized in its own repository (P08 L279: "No Hawaiian word, accent, slang or register is written (the language is synthesized in its own repository)"; P10 L393). An English label taken as an attested word of the civic tongue would pre-empt that synthesis at Mirny. An English label taken as English would not, because no English word is being synthesized for Mirny.

**Third order.**
- [D] **At Mirny a label's language can no longer mark it as an authoring convenience.** English is not foreign to Mirny's speech at either level, so only the label's status ("never attested speech") separates it from speech. The rule already carries that protection without any tier ground (P08 L316, L348). The caution's work moves from "watch the English" to "keep the status attached": a label quoted outside the catalog without its status can no longer be told from speech by its language.
- [D] **R-118's three-way list is not disjoint for an English-origin name** (P10 L395, "(the dominant, the supporting tier or another)"): such a name could be in the dominant tongue or in the supporting tier. Which one is the developer's naming, as R-118 already says.
- [ADM + D] **Russian: none is sited here.** No label is written in Russian, and the rule writes labels in English only (P08 L316), so no Russian-label hazard arises. Russian is not among A1's named bases; whether it is among the later "wide variety of languages" is unstated (L1895). Nothing about the record's language may be inferred from the supporting tier or from the site (P08 L310: "it is still never inferred from the site (`DR-19`, `DR-24`)"): `DR-111`'s Russian is a ruling about what Mirny's residents widely follow, not about the record [D].
- **Not used:** L1895's sentence labeled "Reviewer's reading, not the developer's words". R-2 rests on the developer's words at L1894 and on `DR-111` L1769.

- **Survives:** the rule clause of P10 L394; P08 L316, L348; P10 L393; the arithmetic at P10 L392 and P9 L174, as the rule's output.
- **Changes:** P10 L394's ground clause (both routes); the tier claim at P10 L392, P9 L174 and P9 L176, the tag at P10 L391, and P08 L271 and L276 (to `DR-111`, as L1770 already asks); R-118's parenthetical at P10 L395 (the overlap noted).
- **Open slots:** which language any name is in (the developer's naming, R-118); whether the reviewer's reading at L1895 becomes the developer's (not Mirny's to decide; see Limits).
- **One-location test:** passes; no other city is named. The two-routes finding is Mirny's because `DR-111` names Mirny's tier; the English base of the civic tongue is national.
