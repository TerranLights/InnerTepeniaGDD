### 3.1 · G2 · Physical and environmental constraint

**STRENGTH: what the place gives without effort**

- **S2A-1 · A sunrise on every day of the year.** "Polar night: ⛔ **None**", and the sun rises every day of the year (spec L112). The winter minimum is about 2.5 hours of light (spec L125); June's monthly figure is 2.7 h (spec L137).
  *Consequence:* no month at Casey is wholly dark, for either body.
  →1 The sky marks every day in every month, including the darkest.
  →2 The overnight rest that national canon gives both bodies ("Robots and humans stop at the same time, for different reasons", physiology L336 via Frame L276) falls in real darkness from roughly March to October (Frame L191). Darkness and rest coincide for most of the year.
  →3 The coincidence holds only in the closed months. In the open months the same latitude takes the dark away (S2A-8), so the one natural marker of rest is present in the season when nothing can arrive by sea, and absent in the season when everything does. *(CF-7 pairs this with S2A-8.)*

- **S2A-2 · One season gives everything at once.**
  - The sea is navigable roughly November to March (spec L154).
  - The same five months hold the only two months with an average high above freezing (December +1.4 °C, January +2.2 °C; spec L132, L143).
  - They also hold all four months above 15 h of daylight (November 19.0, December 24, January 21.0, February 16.5).
  - Arithmetic on spec L132-143: the open months average −3.3 °C and 18.7 h of daylight; the closed months average −13.6 °C and 7.9 h.
  - **The seven closed months (April to October) are exactly the seven months whose mean is below −10 °C** (spec L135-141).

  *Consequence:* Casey's year has two regimes, not four seasons.
  →1 Arrival by sea, outdoor warmth and long light come together, and leave together.
  →2 The closing of the sea in April is a single drop, not a slope: the sea goes as the mean falls below −10 °C and the daylight falls below 10 h (April 9.3 h, spec L135).
  →3 **The year's hard deadline is the last navigable weeks of March, not the solstice.** Whatever the city will use until about November must be in hand when the sea, the warmth and most of the light leave together. Casey's calendar has one hard edge, and it falls at the end of March.

- **S2A-3 · The ground keeps little of its snow.** "**KATABATIC MARGIN** — *snow flux, not snowfall*" (spec L103). About 225 mm falls, about 86 mm lands (spec L104-105; 38.2%), and about 139 mm a year is "Lost to sublimation and wind transport" (spec L106).
  *Consequence:* open ground at Casey is not buried at the rate snow falls.
  →1 Net accumulation on open ground is about 86 mm of water a year.
  →2 The ground is not where the snow cost lands. The cost lands on whatever stands up from it (S2A-11; CF-2).
  →3 The physical gift and the physical cost come from one mechanism, so the strength belongs to open land and the cost to anything built. The more the place builds, the more of its snow becomes a cost (CF-2).

- **S2A-4 · A sheltered anchorage, in season.** The coast offers "reasonably sheltered anchorage by Antarctic standards", which "made Casey a viable destination for incoming ships during the exile period" (spec L82). The sea access "directly determined Casey's supply windows" (spec L154).
  *Consequence:* the sea is Casey's written supply route.
  →1 Ships could come and could lie in shelter.
  →2 The spec's own word for what the sea brought is "supply" (spec L154); what that supply contains is not written.
  →3 What arrives by sea arrives into shelter, and only in the open months, so whatever the shelter makes possible (unloading, laying in) is confined to five months. The anchorage's value is seasonal, like everything else G2 gives (S2A-2).

- **S2A-5 · Room, if ice is built on.** Casey's required area is 155 km² at 10,000/km² or 221 km² at 7,000/km² (Extent L608). With ice buildable, the area is there: "population ÷ band = REQUIRED area, then ask what the site has", and "with ice buildable" it has it (Extent L530-531).

  →2 The site column is reserved (RF-20) and no layout is known (REQ-6), so where the city grows is open.

**DEFICIT: what it withholds absolutely**
