**A · The ground, the wind, the light, the sea**
- A1 [L] Mirny sits on the polar-circle line; the spec plans a civic marker "at or near 66°33'S acknowledging the city's position on the line" (`SPEC` L204).
- A2 [L] The hazard that defines the place is the wind, not the cold: "WIND, decisively — the cold is NOT what defines this city." (`SPEC` L104); mean wind 12.0 m/s at a mean of -11.2 °C (L104); katabatic from the south and southeast, averages 10-14 m/s, storms over 40 m/s (L93).
- A3 [L][O] Snow is carried more than it falls: about 527 mm falls, about 200 mm stays, about 38 % retention (`SPEC` L99-101); residents dig out from what arrived from upwind (L104). Cleared drift refills, so clearing is a cost remade after every storm (`RES` L17-18); drift is the part of the wind that is fixed by height and direction, "it is the half of the wind that can be written down." (`RES` L20).
- A4 [O] Two blindnesses: sight lost under a clear sky to wind-lifted snow, and whiteout proper under overcast (`RES` L21-23); the same wind gives different visibility on different days (L28-29). Storms last up to about nine days; calm and weak wind together are about 3 % of observations; in summer the wind peaks about 05-08 and eases by 15-18 (`RES` L37-41).
- A5 [O→C] The October research found "The cues are honest; they are unread" (`RES` L30). By ruling this is true of the first generation or so only: they learned the cues alone, then taught them (`DRL` L1115, L1124).

- A7 [O] The sea is a surface for about ten months: fast ice 30-40 km wide, break-up between Dec 17 and Mar 9, refreeze between Mar 18 and May 5; the window cannot be scheduled (`RES` L60-67). The katabatic dies 8-15 km offshore (L65).
- A8 [O] Real rock is coastal only; inland is moving ice with crevasses hidden under snow, so the surface hides its danger twice, overhead in the moving snow and underfoot (`RES` L77-79, paraphrased).
