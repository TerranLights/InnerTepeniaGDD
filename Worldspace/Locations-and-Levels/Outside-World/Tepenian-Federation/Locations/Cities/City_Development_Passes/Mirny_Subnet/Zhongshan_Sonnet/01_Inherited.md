# 1.1 · THE ASYMMETRY CHECK — Gate 9, run early, against inherited material

> `04` Gate 9, verbatim: *"For every finding describing a threshold, gate, conversion, verdict, admission or
> status change: the mechanism runs both ways — did the file write both? Ask what happens to someone the
> mechanism decides against, whether that outcome is as durable, and whether there is any route back. A 'no
> route back' nobody has perceived as a problem is a textbook shadow."*

Walking every claim in `1.0` against that question. Most fail to qualify — they are not thresholds, gates,
conversions, verdicts, admissions, or status changes at all (checked in `1.2`, below, so this section is
honestly reporting non-fires, not only fires). **Five do qualify, and the check fires on all five.**

## A-1 · The court that confirmed a claim could also withhold one — and it did `[I-1]`

**Written:** the International Court of Diplomacy at Jeju-do coordinated Japan, Korea and the Sinian Federation
as regional peers and confirmed China's existing presence as exclusively theirs.

**The unwritten half:** an admission mechanism has a decided-against side, and this one names it. `Specs/`'s
own text: *"Russia was never a party to that court at all — whatever Russian presence exists in Zhongshan today
(5.72%, its smallest Significant-tier nation) arrived the same way Korea's, Germany's, and Japan's did: as
ordinary immigration into a city whose founding, and whose exclusive claim to its own site, was already settled
before any of them arrived."*

| Asymmetry question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who does the mechanism decide against? | Everyone who was not a party — fifteen of the eighteen nations now living here |
| Is that outcome as durable as the favorable one? | More durable. The claim was settled before the exile era began, so it was never available to be competed for |
| Is there a route back? | None is written, and none appears possible. The court sat once, before anyone arrived. No second sitting, no appeal, no mechanism by which a later-arriving population could acquire a claim |

**The open asymmetry:** `64.17%` of this city's population belongs to nations that had no standing in the
settlement that founded it — and the settlement is the reason the city has never had to defend its own
existence. Not resolved here. Phases 2 and 7 own it, and `00d` applies directly: if nobody experiences this as
a grievance, it is a candidate shadow, not a politics.

## A-2 · The orbital tier is a one-way door, and nobody has written the return `[I-3]`

**Written:** Census I → II is migration to the orbital tier, pre-war, not loss — a documented destination
inside the setting's own future, which is why `Status` is `Living`, not `Declining`.

**The unwritten half:** a migration is a gate. Somebody passed it. Nothing states who could, who could not, or
whether anyone came back down.

```
THIS CITY'S OWN RETENTION, Census I → Census II
  combined ......... 77.90%          humans ... 82.66%          robots ... 73.26%
  spread (H − R) ... +9.40 pp
  mix  Census I : 50.60% robot / 49.40% human   →   Census II : 47.59% robot / 52.41% human
```

| Asymmetry question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who does the mechanism decide against? | Unwritten. Nothing states what qualified a person for the orbital tier — cost, trade, selection, or simple choice |
| Is the outcome durable? | Apparently permanent. No return migration is recorded anywhere in the census |
| Is there a route back? | Not written — in-frame the door is simply open and one-directional |

**And the national census settles what the gate actually was:** `Official_Population_Census.md` §C states
population is conserved between Census I and Census II — nobody was born or died in the transition, they
relocated. Grand totals are identical, `32,026,600` both. So the gate is not attrition and not loss. It is a
door, and everyone who went through it is still alive on the other side — `10,104,964` people in orbit at
Census II, ~31.6% of the nation.

**And this city went through it backwards against its own parent:**

| | humans | robots | spread `H − R` |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| The parent — national surface retention | `67.12%` | `69.71%` | **`−2.59 pp`** |
| This city | `82.66%` | `73.26%` | **`+9.40 pp`** |

The sign is inverted. Nationally, humans used the orbital exit more than robots. Here, robots used it more than
humans, and this city crossed from robot-majority to human-majority doing it. This is city-against-parent, which
`01_Frame_Typology_and_Inheritance.md` §5.3a substitute 2 permits — not a city-to-city comparison, and it names
no other city. What caused it is Phase 5's to answer, not Step 1's; and canon does not supply a cause — the
census's own note calls the national orbital human-skew "an open design question." The parent's pattern is
unexplained too, so this city is a second unexplained case, not a departure from a settled rule.

## A-3 · An allocation with no author — and the check reframes `DRQ-11` `[I-13, I-14]`

`DRQ-11` asks who allocated the Larsemann Hills' ~40 km² between three cities, or whether nobody did. The
asymmetry check sharpens the question the ruling already asks: if somebody allocated, then somebody received
less, and nothing anywhere describes a decided-against party, an appeal, or a revision. If nobody allocated,
three cities each needing 3–4× the entire oasis have simply never had the conversation — a stranger and more
characterful fact than any allocation would be.

| | |
|---|---|
| Decided against? | Unknown — the ruling is open |
| Route back? | No revision mechanism is written under either branch |
| What the check adds | Either branch produces a permanent, unappealable condition — so the pass can carry the consequence without the ruling |

**Which means `R-1` blocks fewer findings than it looks like:** what is blocked is *which portion* this city
holds. What is not blocked is that the holding is fixed, unarbitrated, and has no route to revision.

## A-4 · A candidacy is an admission mechanism, and canon records its negative side `[I-7]`

**Written:** this city is "a strong candidate" for a third fabrication-chamber manufacturing site.

Unusually, canon does write the unfavorable half — the mechanism's shape is visible in the same source: one
candidate was "considered and removed… pending a siting concern" (a volcanic/seismic hazard at Fort McMurdo),
and two others (Belgrano, Concordia) are "proposed but paused pending further development."

| Asymmetry question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Decided against? | Written — removal is real and has a stated cause |
| Durable? | No, and this is the finding. Removal is "pending" a concern; pausing is "pending further development." Both are reversible states |
| Route back? | Yes, implicitly — no candidate is ever finally rejected, only held |

**So the mechanism has no terminal "no."** A city is a candidate, or paused, or removed-pending — never refused.
Candidacy is a standing condition rather than a decision, and this city has been living inside it since
2026-07-06 in authoring time, indefinitely in-world. Filed `R-2` in `00_Frame.md` §0.5 — recorded, not resolved.
Phase 7 may write what it is like to be permanently under consideration, which needs no ruling to be true.

## A-5 · A prohibition with no written sanction `[I-10]`

**Written:** ossuaries are sacred and untouchable; metal is mined from quarries "rather than stealing from the
dead."

| Asymmetry question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Decided against? | Nobody — the rule has no recorded violator and no penalty |
| Is that a gap? | No. `02_Generators_Capability_and_Symbols.md` §3.3: a prohibition is policy, not capability, and `00d` rules a knowingly-chosen harm belongs to a location's politics rather than its shadow |
| What the check does surface | The prohibition has a cost, and canon names who pays it: the quarry and mining trades — "a necessary industry whose necessity is ethical as well as material" |

Live for this city specifically, because its whole ground-level site is ice-free rock and `R-1` concerns who
holds which portion of it. Phases 7 and 8 own it.
