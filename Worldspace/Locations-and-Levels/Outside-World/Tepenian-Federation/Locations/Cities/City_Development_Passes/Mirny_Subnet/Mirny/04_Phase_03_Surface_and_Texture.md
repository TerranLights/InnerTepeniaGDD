## B. The four sensory sub-fields, separately

**B.0 Visually calm, tactilely and acoustically tense.** The admitted files give a place whose eye-cues and body-cues disagree: *"Whiteout here routinely occurs under a clear sky"* [ADM L82];
*"even at relatively moderate temperatures, sustained katabatic events make outdoor exposure dangerous"* [ADM L139]. A clear sky and a thermometer above freezing (December and January average highs
+0.4 and +1.1 °C) are **not** signs of calm. The four sub-fields are therefore written apart. **A reading rule for "dangerous regardless of cold protection":** bodies are clothed and exposure is a
*preparation gradient, never a gate* for both bodies (`RP` L49–57); the quotation stands as written, and **no hazard it names is spelled out in the admitted files** (being knocked, losing sight and the
like are not written here).

### B.1 SOUND

**What the files give.** The only acoustic datum is the wind: the dominant feature; 10–14 m/s average; storms above 40 m/s; mean 12.0 m/s [ADM L71, L82]. `RES` §1 [CORR]: calm and weak wind together are
about 3 % of observations; storms last up to about nine days; in summer and the transition seasons the katabatic builds in the evening, *"peaks about 05–08 local and eases by 15–18"*, an afternoon lull;
in winter it follows approaching cyclones, and whether Mirny keeps even a weak daily cycle in winter is UNKNOWN. **No sound level, source list or interior sound is given.**

**Human body — P3-1 · There is no quiet baseline, so change is what is heard** *[ADM + CORR + D]*
- *1st:* wind is the exterior's ground tone for essentially every hour; what carries information is a rise, a shift or a lull, never the presence of wind.
- *2nd:* calm and weak wind together, about 3 % of observations, is roughly 11 days a year if observations are uniform (own arithmetic, raw in Snag 13): **a lull is rare enough to be an event.** The change
  has two clocks [CORR]: a daily one in summer and the transition seasons, an episodic one in winter (cyclones; a storm can last about nine days, so a storm is an ambient condition, not an occasion).
  The time-of-day figures are the source's local time; no in-world clock is set (`FRM` PA-8).
- *3rd:* in the 29 lit days, **when no dusk marks the hour, the wind's own daily rhythm is the only physical clock the files document** [D on CORR]; in winter the ear has no documented clock and
  the hour must come from the light. `RES` §1 says the cues are *"honest"* and *"unread"*, and the sky *"tells the truth in a language nobody at the founding had learned"*: hearing the change is the same
  unlearned skill as reading the sky.

**Robot body.** `RP` L187: *"robots perceive sound and vibration across a meaningfully wider range than humans do, structurally rather than through any add-on device"* [IND]. **Not given:** which part of the
wind's spectrum, or of a structure's vibration in wind, lies in the extra range; whether the extra range is a cue or a burden. **Nothing is added.** Therefore the wind clock above is a human-range statement
and is **not** asserted for the robot body, and **Mirny cannot be written as a place where either body hears the storm sooner** (RF-1; R-29). *(Round 3 removed two over-reaches: a "lower threshold" for a
robot's first registering a change, and the structure-borne wind as "a wider channel" for the robot body. Both stay RESERVED.)*

**Scoped exception.** The sheltered core's work noise belongs to the headline institution and is not the general answer; the admitted set gives the core a function and no sound. **Industrial, interior,
traffic, harbor and relay sound: REQUESTED — not in the admitted set** (R-28). *Offshore:* the katabatic *"dies about 8–15 km offshore"* [CORR, `RES` §3], so the wind's sound thins out on the fast ice; whether

### B.3 FEEL

**P3-2 · The thermometer is not the feel, and the first warning can arrive as warmth** *[ADM + CORR + D]*
- *1st:* monthly means run only −2.0 to −16.4 °C and sit within 1.0 °C from May to September [RD]; the felt range inside one day is far wider, because wind chill during katabatic events falls *"far below
  ambient"* [ADM L142]. The warm months are not the safe months: December and January average highs are above freezing (+0.4, +1.1 °C) with *"brief rain possible"* in January [ADM L107], and the hazard is wind,
  not cold [ADM L82, L139].
- *2nd:* `RES` §1 [CORR]: the sky clearing is the sign the wind is coming off the ice, and *"onset warms the air at the surface"*; the strongest storms arrive behind cyclones as the sky clears. So felt cold is
  distributed by exposure and hour, not by month.
- *3rd:* [D on CORR] **the first sign of the dangerous wind can read as an improvement** — a clearing sky and a rising thermometer — so a body or a garment choice keyed to the thermometer misreads exactly
  the moment that matters, and a resident can learn the honest cue where a first-time arrival cannot. This is the sensory mechanism under the spine's blame pattern (`P2` P2-1): the person hurt was handed a
  comfortable cue. *Magnitude:* no wind-chill figure is admitted (R-31).

**P3-3 · The body is the gauge: moving snow runs at the ankle on an ordinary day and climbs to the face as the wind rises** *[CORR + D]*
- *1st:* blowing snow starts at roughly 5–10 m/s and Mirny's mean is 12.0 m/s [ADM L82]; at 10 m/s 88.5 % of moving snow is below 0.5 m, at 30 m/s 33.8 % is [CORR, `RES` §1]. **Moving snow is the ordinary
  condition, not the storm condition.**
- *2nd:* how high up the body the snow runs is a reading of the wind, and both bodies share the same clothed human-looking shape (`RP` L18–57), so the gauge applies to both.
- *3rd:* the same wind gives very different visibility on different days, because visibility depends on the loose snow upwind and fresh snow bonds within a day [CORR]; so **the feel of a wind speed is not a
  fixed function of the speed**, which is why a clear-sky whiteout cannot be predicted from an anemometer alone.

**P3-4 · Hands are the price** *[IND + D]*: *"Heavier gloves cost fine motor control and tactile feedback"* (`RP` L55), a gradient and never a gate, for both bodies; the trade is made under wind. (A seasonal
work split, fine hand-work in the lit weeks and coarse in winter, is a derivation [D] and is not carried as a finding.)

**P3-5 · The glaze weeks** *[ADM + CORR + D; credit A]*: the only two months with average highs above freezing, December and January, are the two that hold the midnight sun; liquid water meets sub-zero nights
(average night lows −5.5 and −4.6 °C), so surfaces glaze, and `RES` §4 [CORR] records rime icing in the warm months (conditional: the relay's technology is not canon). *3rd:* [D] the lit weeks, the daily
thaw and the sea's earliest break-up (December 17, §E) coincide: **the season that looks easiest is the one in which footing, exposed masts and the fast ice's edge each become unreliable**, for both bodies.

**Underfoot.** *"The coast here is rocky and exposed."* [ADM L57] The Frame records "coastal ice" from the extent file; **which carries the built area is decided in no admitted file** (R-25). *Both branches

  population, not by a minority. *(Corrected 2026-10-03 after Phase 4's cross-check: the robot cold-physiology file lists three options for a robot needing charge in the cold — warm the cells first, which spends stored

## F. Retroactive Mechanism

Definition used (`SPN3` L519–520 only): *what is established as simply true here that nothing explains, and what physical system would produce exactly that — it generates a second-order consequence for free.*

**Adopted candidate 1 (P3-9) — the carried-past snow and its sink** *[ADM + CORR + D; credit B, C]*
- *Established as simply true:* of the ~527 mm that falls, ~200 mm lands and ~327 mm is lost, and *"up to a third of snowfall evaporates before it lands, and most of the remainder is entrained and carried
  past"* [ADM L76–L79, L82]. The split between sublimation and transport, and **where the carried snow goes**, is stated nowhere.
- *The physical system:* wind carries snow until the wind is interrupted. `RES` [CORR] documents two interruptions: every solid structure (drift banks about 12–15 times its height on both faces) and the
  katabatic itself, which *"dies about 8–15 km offshore"*, so the outer fast ice lies beyond the city's wind. **So the carried snow has a documented sink: the lee of every structure and the offshore edge of
  the city's wind.**
- *Second-order consequence for free:* the fast ice at and beyond that edge receives snow the coast lost, so the sea road has a **surface gradient**, scoured and hard where the wind is strong, deposited and
  soft where it fails *(conditional: the research range is a regional figure; the surface character at the line is derived)*. A lower bound of about 151 mm water-equivalent a year carried past rather than
  evaporated is own arithmetic (327.1 minus an evaporation ceiling of 175.7); it assumes a point measurement holds across the footprint and is **reserved** (RF-5).
- *Third-order:* the one role confirmed to make live calls on the wind and the ice, the harbor watch, *knows how far out the katabatic dies* (`RES` §7), and **that line may be a visible change in the snow
  surface** [D]: `RES` §7's candidate, that drift ridges record the wind's direction and the ground is a compass (*"The instrument exists; its reader does not"*), has a place where it is most legible, where the
  ridges give way to deposit. Phase 4 and Phase 8 inherit a place to look and a skill without a named teacher.

**Adopted candidate 2 (P3-10) — the outer wind-fortified ring, conditional on its form** *[ADM + CORR + D; credit A, C]*
- *Established as simply true:* *"the sheltered industrial core inside the city's outer wind-fortified ring"* [ADM L174]. *Nothing explains:* its form, height, porosity, extent, date, builder or which wind it faces.
- *Mechanism M1:* a hazard from more than one quarter: the prevailing wind is directional (south and southeast, [ADM L71]) and `RES` §5 records three kinds of wind and a record gust from the east [CORR]; **if**
  the ring is closed, it is the form you build when the worst case need not share the prevailing direction. *Mechanism M2:* no inherited judgment of direction (`SPN` §8): a ring needs no judgment about which
  way, a windward barrier does [D on CORR]. *(Round 3: "A closed ring is the form you build" is stated only as "if closed"; closure is not assumed, PA-15.)*
- *Second-order, all conditional on a solid ring [D]:* a solid barrier is a drift generator (drifts on both faces whose width scales with its height; a cleared drift refills; so **the ring has keepers** and the
  core's sheltered edge is where sheltered turns into buried); where the ring is interrupted (for Hwy 110, the inland spur, the harbor road) ends and corners scour, so **the entries are the scoured points and
  the interior edge is the drifted one**; and the ring is unevenly worn by arc, so its condition is a record of the wind each arc faces. *Scale:* **if** the ring bounded the whole required extent on a circular
  plan (the Frame's equal-area radii 6.56 and 7.84 km), its length would be about 41 to 49 km; neither condition is stated and nothing here rests on it.
- *Third-order:* `SPN` says Mirny lacks inherited judgment of the wind; the ring's wear and drift belts, and the ground's ridges, are **a physical record of the wind's direction that every resident touches daily**:
  the knowledge is not absent, it is **unread** — `RES` §1's own result (*"The cues are honest; they are unread"*) reached from the build side [D on CORR].

**Not adopted as candidates:** *the moving floor* (snow already in motion at the ordinary mean): partly explained by the spec itself (the snow is lifted, not falling), so it is carried as the ordinary
condition in P3-3, not as a mechanism; *the station's survival* (explained by `NNS` L15); *the light asymmetry* (explained by refraction, `SPEC` L93–L95). **Reserved:** *quarried raw materials from the south*
