### RT-1 · The sea calendar, and holding through it

**S3C-1 · The spine's contradictable leg holds; the closing is steady and the opening is dated by breakout, which varies by weeks.**
- *Sources.* Voermans et al., "Inside-outward breakup of sea ice by locally generated waves in the Vincennes Bay Polynya", *Nature Communications* (accepted 9 September 2026), https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/542494/1/s41467-026-77979-y_reference.pdf (SOURCED, text read locally): buoys on fast ice near the site broke out with it on 6–7 October 2021, 17–18 October 2022, 21–22 November 2024 and 2 November 2025; on 18 October 2022 there were "150–200 km of sea ice separating the polynya from the open-ocean ice edge". Massom et al. 2013, *PLoS ONE*, https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0064756 (SOURCED, first 100,000 characters): along 110°E, "a slow monotonic increase from a minimum extent in February–early March to a maximum in early October" (2004), and in 1999 "a very rapid retreat occurred in December". SNIPPET (#2): fast ice at the site "very unstable"; the ocean surface freezes "around the end of March" (circumpolar).
- *Changed.* S2-78's one contradictable leg is **not contradicted**: forming about April (the pack is already growing from early March), breaking up in October to November, navigable roughly November to March is a fair present-day reading of spec L135, L141 and L154. It is **refined**: the closing is predictable (the pack grows slowly and steadily from its minimum), while the opening is a breakout followed by the clearing of 150–200 km of pack, and its date moved between early October and late November in four recent years, with a December retreat in one year of the record.
- →1 Casey can name the week its sea shuts; it cannot name the week its sea opens.
- →2 The store has to be sized to a late opening, not an average one, so the draw-down's end (S2-15) is a range, not a date (S2-26 →2).
- →3 The hardest stretch, the lit waiting at the end (S2-26 →3), has a different length every year. Whoever lays in for the late year ends most years with a surplus, and a surplus is the only visible proof of a prudence that otherwise looks like waste: the shortfall's evidence and remedy sit in different seasons (S2-27), and so does the evidence of having guarded against it.
- *Divergence.* Present-day data, 1979–2025; the frame's sea ice is not known, and nothing here dates or changes it. S2-26 said the city "shuts at once": at sea the pack advance is slow, so the "at once" belongs to the coast's experience (the last ship, the first fast ice), not to the pack. That is a nuance for Phase 1, not a contradiction.

**S3C-2 · A winter polynya: open water at the coast while the sea is shut.**
- *Source.* Voermans et al. 2026 (above): "Coastal polynyas are recurring regions of open water within sea ice"; the deployment region "borders the Vincennes Bay Polynya, a medium-class coastal polynya that produces substantial volumes of sea ice"; its wind rose is for August–October. SNIPPET (#3): winter katabatic winds coincide with offshore ice-export plumes; polynya occurrence off the ice dome is tied to katabatic wind.
- *Changed.* S2-9's "ice-locked in winter" is a statement about ship access, not about the sea's surface. Near the coast, the shut half can hold recurring open water or thin ice, opened by the same offshore wind that brings the sudden switch of S2-12.
- →1 In the shut months the sea can be open to the eye near the city.
- →2 Open water there is not access: 150–200 km of pack lie beyond it.
- →3 A second instance of S2-26's hypothesis, "a place where light is not access": here, water is not access. What a person sees from the shore and what can reach the shore are different facts.

## 5 · The Unrecognized Instrument (3.6)

*Run after the research, against D1 to D11 (`02` L557-567). Each candidate names the two things Casey already has that a change

**UI-1 (the strongest) · The cold already keeps the night.** D3 (no darkness to end the day) against S2-10 and S2-13 (no night warm enough to rest unheated; heat every night of every month). Every robot must enter heated space to recharge each night of every month on the averages (Frame L272), and humans need shelter: the cold already supplies a nightly, shared, enclosed stop that darkness cannot supply from late October to mid-February (S3C-9). The built environment can hold a schedule against the sky (S3C-10). Casey already has its stop-maker; it is a threshold, not a clock. **Its weakness is exact:** the threshold is felt least in December and January, when average lows are −3.7 and −2.6 °C (S2-21 (b)), which are the weeks with no dark at all. The instrument fails where it is most needed unless it is recognized. *Untested in-world:* how the stop is kept is not in the material (S2-19 →3); Step 5 checks it.

**UI-2 · The bare crests already do what the buildings cannot.** S2-5 (open ground sheds snow) against S2-14 and S2-60 (standing things collect it). The peninsula's exposed crests are scoured clear by the same wind that drifts the lee (S3C-12); raised or wind-shaped buildings let the wind clear them (S3C-13). Casey's land already demonstrates the method: present no lee, let the wind pass. Its buildings would only have to copy its ground.

**UI-3 (conditional) · The witnesses already hold what the records lack.** D5 against S2-36 and S2-76. The method (living memory of failures) generalizes from the founding story to the plant. Conditional on robots in the founding party (contract L213-214 DEMOTED), by cohort (RF-24).

**UI-4 (conditional) · The drift is the winter's water.** S2-20 (water) against S2-14 (moving snow). Lakes give water in January and February (S3C-21); in the shut half the drift that costs labor is the nearest meltable store. Already noted at S2-20 as "not a finding"; research adds the summer half.
