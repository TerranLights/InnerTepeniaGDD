## B. The four sensory sub-fields, each separately, each for two bodies

Scope of every answer: base-level (`DR-4`) — not a Uniform claim. **General answer first (00b):** the typical resident is a person outdoors for short, routine passages and indoors the rest of the time, in a coat and gloves (`RP` L43–44), at near-equal odds a human or a robot. The quarry crew, the relay crew and the harbor watch are named work with unstated shares; their senses are scoped exceptions, marked where they appear.

### B.1 SOUND

**What the admitted files give.** The wind is the "dominant environmental feature of this city" [ADM, L71 chars 156–307], with "average wind speeds 10–14 m/s; sustained storms exceeding 40 m/s (144 km/h) have been recorded" [ADM, same span] and **"mean wind 12.0 m/s"** [ADM, L82]. 12.0 m/s is 43.2 km/h; 10–14 m/s is 36–50.4 km/h (own arithmetic, raw output in E). Calm and weak wind together are only about 3 % of observations [CORR, `RES` L40–41]; storms last up to about nine days [CORR, `RES` L41]. **Nothing in the admitted set gives any other sound source** — no traffic figure, no market, no machinery sound.

**Human body.** The ground tone of every outdoor minute is wind. Quiet is the exception, and a quiet hour is itself information.

**Robot body.** The one admitted difference: "robots perceive sound and vibration across a meaningfully wider range than humans do, structurally rather than through any add-on device" [RP L187]. **I add nothing to it.** What it licenses is conditional: *if* the wind's sound or the vibration of a structure in wind has energy outside the human range (a physics question the admitted files do not answer), the robot body takes in more of it than the human body beside it.

**P3-B1 · Sound is a ground with a clock in summer and no known clock in winter** `[D, with CORR inputs]`.
- *1st order.* Because wind is continuous, what carries information is **change** — a rise, a lull, a shift — never the presence of wind.
- *2nd order.* In summer and the transition seasons the katabatic builds in the evening, "peaks about 05–08 local and eases by 15–18" [CORR, `RES` L38], an afternoon lull. The nightless weeks (Dec 8 → Jan 5 [ADM, L86]) fall in that season. **When no dusk marks the hour, the wind's own rhythm is the only physical clock the files document** — heard as a rise toward early morning and an easing in mid-afternoon. In winter the wind "follows approaching cyclones" and whether Mirny keeps even a weak daily cycle is UNKNOWN [CORR, `RES` L37–40] — so the winter ear has **no documented clock**, and winter's hour must come from elsewhere (light, [ADM] daylight 2.4–11.2 h in May–Sep).
- *3rd order.* The cue is available to every ear, and no one at the founding was taught to read it (`RES` L33, *"the sky tells the truth in a language nobody at the founding had learned"*). Reading change in the sound is the same unlearned skill as reading the sky. **For the robot body the threshold at which a change is first registered may be lower** (wider range, conditional above), but what a robot registers early cannot be copied out to anyone: *"a long-lived robot is a person to ask, not an archive"* (`04_Phase_02` L225, from `RP` L183). Who hears the change first is a body difference; whether the hearing is shared is a conversation, not a channel. **This answers `04_Phase_02` RF-6** (whether the wind deficit is uniform across the bodies): it is *not* uniform in what each body can sense, conditionally; it **is** uniform in having no teacher.

**P3-B2 · The sound of the sheltered core is the core's, not the city's** `[scoping, D]`. The admitted set gives the core a function (processing, machinery) and no sound. The quarry-and-machinery noise that a sheltered industrial core might hold would be **the headline institution's sound** — the named failure (spine L526). It is not written as the general answer. **Industrial sound: REQUESTED — not in the admitted set** (R-B1).

**Empty sub-slots, as results:** traffic on Hwy 110; the harbor's sound; the relay's audible output; interior sound of any building. None is in the admitted set.

**Where wind-sound thins** `[CORR + D]`. The katabatic "dies about 8–15 km offshore" and "the outer fast ice lies beyond the city's wind" (`RES` L65). The sea surface, for about ten months, is a place where the city's wind-sound ends. Whether anyone goes there for the quiet is **not in the files** (R-B1).

### B.3 FEEL

**What the admitted files give.** Wind chill: "effective wind-chill during katabatic events can drive apparent temperatures far below ambient" [ADM, L142 chars 103–198]; "outdoor activity in strong katabatic conditions is dangerous regardless of cold protection" [ADM, L131 chars 126–366]; "even at relatively moderate temperatures, sustained katabatic events make outdoor exposure dangerous" [ADM, L139 chars 103–270]. Snow does not fall so much as travel: "up to a third of snowfall evaporates before it lands, and most of the remainder is entrained and carried past" [ADM, L82 chars 166–422]; ~38 % lands [ADM, L78]. Temperature is moderate and flat: monthly means run −2.0 (Jan) to −16.4 (Aug) [RD]; May–Sep means span only 1.0 °C [RD, own arithmetic].

**Reading `RP` before reading the hazard.** Bodies are clothed: a robot "can (and nearly almost always does) wear clothing" and "robots would also wear winter gloves" (`RP` L44). Exposure is preparation, not a gate, for both bodies. So **"dangerous regardless of cold protection" [ADM] is read as a claim that the wind has a hazard coats do not remove** — being knocked, losing sight, working with hands — **not as a claim that bodies cannot be dressed for it.** (A reading, offered for testing; it is the reading under which 665,901 humans and 685,529 robots can live here.)

**P3-B3 · Snow is felt moving along the ground, and the point where it reaches the face is inside the range of an ordinary-to-storm day** `[CORR + D]`.
- *1st order.* Blowing snow starts at roughly 5–10 m/s, against a mean of 11–12 [CORR, `RES` L24–25]. At 10 m/s 88.5 % of moving snow is below 0.5 m; at 30 m/s only 33.8 % is [CORR, `RES` L26–27]. An ordinary day puts moving snow at ankle and shin; a storm puts it at the face.
- *2nd order.* The garment register must differ by height. Boots and the lower trouser leg meet the ordinary day; the face and ears meet the storm. For the robot body, `RP` L35–36 places the realistic skin where interaction happens — "the realistic skin sits where interaction happens — hands, face and ears —" — so the face and ears are also the robot's exposed surface; **whether that skin senses is not stated.**
- *3rd order.* The same wind gives different visibility on different days, because visibility depends on how much loose snow lies upwind and fresh snow bonds within a day (`RES` L28–29, paraphrased). **So the feel of a wind speed is not a fixed function of the speed**: the number alone does not tell a person what the walk will be, and a number published for the day (the pattern `RES` §5 documents elsewhere) would be a substitute for a feel the founders never learned. That is Phase 4's and Phase 8's; here it fixes that **feel cannot be summarized by one figure.**

**P3-B4 · Hands are the price** `[RP + D]`. "Heavier gloves cost fine motor control and tactile feedback" (`RP` L55), a gradient and never a gate. At Mirny the gradient is steepest for **work done outdoors with the hands** — the relay crew, the quarry crews, the harbor watch — a scoped exception, not the general answer. For the general resident the glove trade-off shows only in small outdoor tasks.

**P3-B5 · The robot body's feel is open, and its operating cost is not** `[RP + RD + D]`.
- The files leave open "whether robots feel cold as discomfort or only register it instrumentally" (`RP` L59). **Not assumed.** What *is* stated: a robot's cells lose capacity in the cold — "recharging below freezing causes permanent capacity loss" (`RP` L13), usable capacity falls to 50–60 % by −20 °C, output sags under load, materials embrittle and lubricants solidify below −40 °C, and a robot is safer moving than resting (`RP` L13–16, summarized in that file).
- *Against Mirny's own figures:* mean night lows of −18.3 (Jun), −19.6 (Jul), −20.2 (Aug), −19.3 (Sep), −18.5 (May) [ADM, L111–115 rows; Jun L112 chars 1–66] approach (and in August pass) the −20 °C point at which the summary puts capacity at 50–60 %; the record low, −36.5 °C (Aug) [ADM, L70], stays 3.5 °C above −40 °C (own arithmetic).
- *2nd order.* All twelve monthly means are below freezing [RD]. Only December and January have average highs above 0 °C (+0.4, +1.1 [ADM, L118 chars 1–63; L107]). **A robot cannot recharge at ambient in any month's night, and only in some Dec–Jan daytime hours.** So the nightly recharge is a **held-above-freezing indoor act** for 685,529 bodies, and it is the same room-condition that the human half needs for another reason ("humans need consistent low-level heat", `02_Spine` L76, [CORR]). The heated interior is therefore demanded by **both** bodies every night, for different physics.
- *3rd order.* Because a robot is safer moving than resting (`RP` L15–16), **waiting outdoors is a cost to the robot body**, and outdoor waiting is therefore where shelter at entries earns its place. The files do not describe any entry; R-B2.

**Underfoot.** The coast is "rocky and exposed" [ADM, L57 chars 1–132]. The extent file (read through the Frame, [CORR]) names *coastal ice* as the terrain carrying the required 135–193 km². **I do not decide between them.** What the files add: drift builds on a solid structure's windward side first and then its lee, both banks running about 12–15 times its height, "Corners and ends scour" (`RES` L14–17, L16); and "Drift ridges record the wind's direction; with a steady wind, the ground is a compass" (`RES` L125, a Candidate). So the open ground outside a structure is **scoured at corners and ridged along the wind; the ground inside a lee is banked.** Both the ridged surface and the drift bank are [CORR] physics, not admitted spec; the share of built area on rock or ice is R-B3.

### B.4 FIRST IMPRESSIONS

**The general answer, before any institution (00b).** The visitor's first impression is **of the shared medium, never of an institution**: the wind, the sky, the ring. It must not be written from the quarry, the relay or the harbor — those are the headline functions (`C-3`, L386) and would stand in for the general answer.

**P3-B6 · The first impression is a mismatch between what the eye reports and what the wind is doing** `[ADM + CORR + D]`.
- *1st order.* From outside, a visitor reaches the city's outer wind-fortified ring first, by the meaning of "outer" [ADM, L174 chars 272–623]. By road (Hwy 110 [ADM, L7]) at any time; by sea only in the navigable window, "not year-round" [ADM, L57 chars 188–371]. The light is the season's: 24* h in December, 2.4 h in June [ADM rows]. And "Whiteout here routinely occurs under a clear sky", "because the snow is *lifted*, not falling" [ADM, L82 chars 166–422].

## F. Retroactive Mechanism

**Rule (spine L519–520):** what is established as simply true here that nothing explains, and what physical system would produce exactly that — and *"It generates a second-order consequence for free."*

**The established, unexplained fact** `[ADM]`: "most of the remainder is entrained and carried past" (spec L82 chars 166–422). Of the ~527 mm that falls, ~200 mm lands and ~327 mm is "Lost to sublimation and wind transport" (L79); the split between sublimation and transport, and **where the carried snow goes**, is stated nowhere.

**The physical system that produces exactly that** `[CORR + D]`: wind carries snow until the wind is interrupted. Two interruptions are documented. **(1)** Every solid structure — "Drift geometry is fixed by height and wind direction" (`RES` L19); both banks about 12–15 times its height (`RES` L14–17). **(2)** The katabatic itself, which "dies about 8–15 km offshore" so that "the outer fast ice lies beyond the city's wind" (`RES` L65). **So the carried snow has a documented sink: the lee of every structure, and the offshore edge of the city's wind.**

**The second-order consequence for free.** The fast ice at and beyond that edge receives snow that the coast lost. The sea road has a **surface gradient**: scoured and hard where the wind is strong, deposited and soft where it fails. *(Conditional: "dies about 8–15 km offshore" is the research's range; the surface character at the line is derived, not stated.)*

**The third-order consequence.** The one role confirmed to already make live calls on the wind and the ice is the harbor watch, and its people "know how far out the katabatic dies" (`RES` L124, the Unrecognized Instrument row). **That line is not only a number in their heads; it is a visible change in the snow surface.** The harbor watch's knowledge has a **physical, readable signature**, and `RES` L125's candidate — *"Drift ridges record the wind's direction; with a steady wind, the ground is a compass. The instrument exists; its reader does not."* — has a place where it is **most legible**: where the ridges give way to deposit. [D] Phase 4 and Phase 8 inherit a place to look and a skill without a named teacher.

**Reserved alternative (not adopted — RF-B1).** *A ring, not a wall on one side.* Spec L71 gives katabatic wind from the south and southeast; `RES` L41–42 gives a record gust from the east, with a cyclone, and three kinds of wind (`RES` L88–89). **If** "ring" means an all-around structure, a ring is what two wind regimes with different bearings would produce, and the consequence for free would be a *lee that moves with the weather type* on the ring's outside. **Not adopted:** the files do not say the ring is a closed circuit (the airlock detail is excluded, `00_Frame` L126), nor its height or porosity.
