**Finding 1 · The ruling.** Developer, 2026-11-10, selection *"All three routes"* (`rulings log` L1939), recorded as "**(6) Freight:**
by the neutral transshipment hubs, by direct sailing from the staging ports, and along the subnet's coastal supply line (differs

**Finding 2 · Three routes, not one chain.** Item (6) names three routes, each with its own origin: the hubs, the staging ports and
the subnet's line. The earlier piece listed the same three as readings the lines did not decide (Finding 39, L476-481). It offered
reader B's reconciling reading (freight staged at the partner's ports and moved on through the hubs) without applying it
(L479-480; Choice 57, L683-684). Each of the three readings is now a ruled route, and direct sailing is ruled as a route of its own.
So the single chain is no longer needed to reconcile `database` L495 with `Ports` L130, and it stays unapplied (Choice 24). The
earlier piece's "The lines decide none of these" (L480) is overtaken. Its "Every reading keeps "not a direct gateway" and the inbound
direction." (L481) holds for the inbound direction. "Not a direct gateway" holds only in the narrower sense of Finding 4.

**Finding 3 · Which ports "the staging ports" are.** The ruling does not name them. The question it answers names "the two staging
ports named in `database` L495" (earlier piece L748). L495 says the city's raw materials are "staged via" two of the partner's
ports. `Ports` L229-230 carry the same two in this city's column, labeled the "ruled primary port" (4,877 km) and the "canon staging
port" (4,817 km). So "the staging ports" are read as those two (Choice 23). Whether the further terminals at `Ports` L224-228 also
stage this city's freight is not ruled (Finding 13).

**Finding 4 · The table and the direct sailings.** The receiving-node table's direct rows list neither this city nor its cluster
(`Ports` L127-129, L131). Item (6) now rules direct sailing from the partner's staging ports to this city. So the pass's leg "this
city is not among them" (Phase 5 L333) is still true of the table, but it no longer means the city has no direct traffic from that
partner. The pass's distinction, "Being served by a shared harbor on a coastal supply line is not being a gateway" (L333-334),
survives only with "gateway" in the pass's own sense: a node the table designates (its "designated gateway nodes", L332; Choice 26).
*And what does that cause?* "Not a gateway" now rests on designation alone. Two readings fit: direct sailings reach a harbor that
the table does not designate, or the table's direct row has been incomplete since 2026-11-10. The ruling does not say (Question 20).
*And what does that cause?* If the cluster were added to the direct row, "not a gateway" would turn for the direct route.
*And what does that cause? (third order)* "NOT a trade hub" does not move either way. The pass gives it "independently" (L334), from
`16` §18, and every route is inbound (Finding 8). So the replacement wording has to keep the two claims apart, as the pass does,
and must not offer "not a gateway" as a ground for "not a trade hub". The proposed wording below is built that way.

**Finding 5 · The cluster's node on the neutral route stands.** The earlier piece's Finding 37 (L460-470) stands. The table's
column is "Tepenian receiving node" (`Ports` L125). The neutral row, marked *(developer ruling, 2026-09-16)* (L130), lists the city's
three-city cluster among its receiving nodes, after a principal node (another location: not used). The route is inbound:

neutral transshipment hubs", confirms that this city's freight uses that node. The earlier piece's Finding 38 (L472-474), that the
node is held by the cluster as one unit, stands.

**Finding 6 · L130's clause describes the route, not this city's whole supply.** L130's general clause gives the neutral route
indirect access to two Upper Earth powers' supply, "without a direct bilateral relationship with either mainland power". The question
that item (6) answers asked whether that clause applies to this city (earlier piece L748-749; first check L141-142). Direct sailing
from one partner's staging ports is now ruled, so the clause cannot describe all of this city's supply; it describes what comes
through the hubs. The lines do not say whether a direct sailing is itself a bilateral relationship or only a shipping link. The
proposed wording claims neither (Question 20).

**Finding 7 · Which route is ruled to carry which supply.** L130 names two powers' supply on the neutral route. The direct sailings
come from the staging ports, which L495 and L229-230 place with one partner. What the coastal supply line carries is not ruled
(Finding 11). So, of the three routes, only the hubs are ruled to bring the second power's supply to this city; whether the coastal
line also brings it is open (Question 21; Choice 28).
*And what does that cause?* For this city the neutral-route node is not a duplicate of the direct sailings: it is the one route
ruled to carry a supply that the direct sailings do not.
*And what does that cause?* On the lines, the three routes are not interchangeable. Losing the hubs would leave the first partner's
direct sailings but no ruled channel for the second power's supply.
*And what does that cause? (third order)* The cluster's standing as a named node (L130) carries weight for this city beyond a label,
and the reconciliation should say what that node carries. The proposed wording does, conditional on the coastal line (Question 21).

**Finding 8 · Every route is inbound, so the city is still not a trade hub.** Each route in item (6) is a route by which Zhongshan's
freight arrives. L130 marks the neutral route "INBOUND"; L495 calls the city a port "receiving" shipments; and no line on my list
gives an outbound flow. The pass's `16` §18 ground (L335), the spec's L180 as corrected under D-6 (note L125-127) and the earlier
piece's Finding 40 (L483-487) all say "not a trade hub". Three inbound routes add supply, not trade.

**Finding 9 · Two of the three routes arrive at the one shared, seasonal harbor.** This city's row gives one port, the cluster's
shared constructed port, long haul, seasonal and shared (`Ports` L202). The neutral route's freight comes to the cluster's node
(L130), whose port is that harbor (L202). A direct sailing to this city can berth only at the port its row gives, which is the same
harbor (Choice 25).
*And what does that cause?* The harbor's season bounds two of the three routes. The third, the subnet's coastal supply line, is the
only route whose mode and season the lines do not fix.
*And what does that cause?* If the coastal line runs by road, it is the only route that can bring freight outside the harbor's
season. If it runs by sea, all three routes share one window, and every inbound route is seasonal.
*And what does that cause? (third order)* Whether this city receives any freight outside the harbor's season turns on that one
unruled point (Question 21). The proposed wording leaves it open rather than implying either.
*Realism (search log, searches 1 and 3; Choice 33).* Near the real station, the landfast ice is reported present from about February
to December. It breaks up in a short summer window, December to January, and in the cold season it reaches 60 to 100 km offshore.
Recent supply ships have unloaded 12 to 44 km from the station, across the ice. These are search returns, and the papers were not
read in full. The city's harbor is a constructed port, centuries later, so the returns size nothing in canon. They show that
"seasonal" (L202) is realistic and that the shared window is short, which gives Finding 10 its weight.
