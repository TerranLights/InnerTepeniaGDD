### Mirny as Phases 2-10 found it

Mirny, as the pass found it, is a Band 5 settlement of 1,351,430 at Census I (665,901 humans / 685,529 robots, robots 50.73 %), a working coast on the Antarctic Circle whose one defining condition is a wind that is almost never calm (about 3 % calm-or-weak), that blinds under a clear sky by lifting the snow, and whose honest cues nobody at the founding had been taught to read; its year is dated by light (no polar night; about 29 days of midnight sun; a 2.4-hour June) because its thermometer holds a plateau from May to September. It was founded — by assignment, not choice, on the treaty's terms, with Idelsk-Uralia as a sponsoring founding nation outside the country and Russia, Australia and the Sinian Federation as the three exile groups among fourteen stocks, none a majority — onto a working station whose journals, logs and manuals arrived with no living teacher. From those conditions Phases 2-10 derived one arrangement that shows up in every layer: an unwritten compact (Phase 6) in which the medium nobody here authored — the inherited record and the subnet's single relay — is held to the standard of not failing, while the reading of the wind is each person's own, unwritten, and answered for privately, so that losses are booked to the person, to "weather," or to silence, and never to the medium (the book never learns).
