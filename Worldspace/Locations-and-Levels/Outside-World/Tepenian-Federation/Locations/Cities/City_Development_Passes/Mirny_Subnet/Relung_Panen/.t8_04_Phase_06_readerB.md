## The differentiation axis

**Keeping against the summer.**

*What the words carry.* The place's unnamed load-bearing thing is a standard: **a made thing is worth its keeping, and here the keeping falls due against the season that gives the most.** The given here is rock, ice walls, still cold air, light every day, lakes, and the summer melt; the made is soil, warmth, lit green, winter water, a summer dark, warm charging rooms and cleared drains (Phase 3 P3-2). The founders made the first of these where there was nothing: "There was no pre-existing farmland or soil; the founders had to establish their own." (spec L222; rulings L1252). The things kept **by hand** are kept against the summer: the dark against a sky that gives 19.4, about 24 and 21.0 h of light in November, December and January (spec L151–156, L179, L189–190), made ground and the drains against a melt on bare ground (spec L171–172, L203; amendment G7-C1, G7-C2; S3-7), the morning path against the water the warm afternoon left (Phase 4 P4-8). *Peer-free test, could this be true anywhere?* No. It needs three things at once, each on this place's own lines: soil that had to be made from nothing at the founding (rulings L1252; spec L222); a summer that gives near-continuous light and a genuine melt on bare ground in the same three months (spec L151–156, L171–172, L179–190, L203); and, for nine months, nothing soft that was not made (Phase 3 P3-2).
