## 3 Reasoning (each step cites a line read)

SRY-C03-15. **The speech-marker reasoning does not rest on the changed inputs.** The keptness vocabulary (`04_Phase_08_Making.md` L210–L222), F.1 (L224–L240) and F.STOCK (L404–L409) are grounded in the standard of worth (L214), the uncertified standing system (L226–L227) and the stock's material-care vocabulary (L408). None of the language rule (`LOG` L1669), the origin (startup L64; `LOG` L1527) or the group shares (CSV L708–L712) says anything about what marks a resident, about accent, or about a keptness register. Nothing admitted contradicts "it is not accent" (L210). This part survives as written.

SRY-C03-16. **The ground of L206 no longer follows as written.** L206 derives "the dominant local vernacular" from Japan's Primary share. Under `DR-100` (1) (`LOG` L1669), the dominant language is "Hawaiian (later Tepenian)" "everywhere", so it is set nationally and not by a city's largest group. What a city's largest group now determines is its supporting tier (`DR-100` (3), `LOG` L1669; reference L77). For Shirayuki that tier is Japanese alone (CSV L708–L712; reference L34). So the step "Japan is Primary … so the dominant local vernacular follows from that" (L206) needs restating: on the current inputs, Japan's share yields Shirayuki's supporting-tier language, held beneath Hawaiian, not the dominant language.

SRY-C03-17. **The Japanese plurality itself survives; only the figure differs.** L206 states 36.27%; the current figure is 34.200 (CSV L708), and the note records it (note L207). Japanese is still the largest group by 20.965 points (SRY-C03-11). `DR-85` keeps Japan and Korea "as they are" as large communities (`LOG` L1527), and "A plurality counts." (`LOG` L1668), so a 34.200 plurality fills the supporting slot. The role Japanese plays in Shirayuki's speech survives, but as the supporting tier rather than as the dominant language.

SRY-C03-18. **The "CANON COMPOSITION FACT" wording needs a qualifier.** L206–L207 calls Japan's primacy a composition fact. That still stands as a national-origin share. But `DR-100` (5) states that the shares "are national-origin shares, not speaker counts", and that the nation-to-language mapping "is recalled knowledge, not verified" (`LOG` L1669). Read as a fact about language, L206 now carries that qualifier. The Japan-to-Japanese mapping is the plain case (CSV L708), so the qualifier changes the wording more than the result.

SRY-C03-19. **The origin canon does not contradict the pass, but the pass does not account for it.** Hawaiian is "a Pidgin between a primary base of English and Japanese, with a supporting base of Korean, Tagalog, Ilocano, and Mexican Spanish" (startup L64), and "down at its foundational level core, a Pidgin between English and Japanese" (`LOG` L1527). The pass names no language, so no sentence is contradicted. But the pass says canon supplies "the Language Module and the composition" (L238–L239), and canon now also supplies the language rule and the language's origin. The note records that the rule "is written into no Shirayuki file" (note L214).

SRY-C03-20. **The robot row survives as wording, but its reading must be restated.** `04_Phase_09_Populations.md` L96 ("Vernacular tracks the dominant local culture") keeps the first half of L426, and L426 still stands. L96 points to `04_Phase_08` §F, where that culture is tied to Japan's share (L206). No admitted line says how L426's rule, that robots speak the vernacular of the dominant local culture and of their builders, combines with `DR-100` (1)'s Hawaiian dominance everywhere for a robot in Shirayuki. L426 itself leaves the mechanism "(exact mechanism not yet determined)". I do not decide it. Separately, neither L96 nor L206 addresses L426's second determinant, "the community who had her built", and no admitted line names Shirayuki's robot builders. That gap is older than the changed inputs and is not caused by them.

SRY-C03-21. **Why not HOLDS.** The difference is not only a figure or a label. `DR-100` (1) changes what decides the dominant language, and that is the ground of L206 (SRY-C03-16). The note records that the rule is written into no Shirayuki file (note L214).

SRY-C03-22. **Why not FLIPS.** The pass never names the dominant vernacular as a language, so no admitted line contradicts a sentence as it is stated. L426, the canon the pass quotes, still stands. `LOG` L1670 leaves "how the rule enters a finished pass" not applied yet. And the larger part of the conclusion, the speech-marker reasoning, survives intact (SRY-C03-15).

SRY-C03-23. **Verdict.** The conclusion survives in a reduced and qualified form. Its speech-marker core holds. Its language-dominance ground at L206, and the reading that L96 inherits from it, no longer follow from the current inputs as written. That is `NARROWS`.
