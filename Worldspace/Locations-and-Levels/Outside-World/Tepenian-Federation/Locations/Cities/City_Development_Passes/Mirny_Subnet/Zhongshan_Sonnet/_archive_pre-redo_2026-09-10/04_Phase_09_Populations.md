# A · The populations present, and the lens

## Who is here

| | Census I *(pre-orbital)* | Census II *(orbital)* |
|---|--:|--:|
| **Humans** | 568,786 | 470,135 |
| **Robots** | 582,703 | 426,881 |
| **Total** | **1,151,489** | **897,016** |
| **Robot share** | **50.60%** | **47.59%** |

**Two populations, and at Census I they are almost exactly the same size.** Phase 2 recorded the sign
inverting between censuses, with retention at **82.66% human against 73.26% robot.**

⚠ **The pass has not been given a cause for that differential and does not invent one.** §18 records that this
city *"produces engineers and researchers who end up across Tepenia"* — an export of people, stated as fact.
**Whether that is connected to the retention gap is not in canon**, and asserting it would be the `M-201`
error again. ⏸️ **Flagged as a question for Step 5, not answered.**

## The lens, declared explicitly as the spine requires

**City/locality.** This is `Robot_Universals` Ch. 19's stated default — *"city/locality first and heaviest,
Build second as an expression of that same locality rather than an independent axis, and Gen/Mark last and
lightest"* — and this pass adopts the default rather than departing from it, which Ch. 19 asks be a conscious
choice either way.

⭐ **What is specific to this location is not the choice of anchor but how nearly exclusive it is here.** The
spine offers five candidate anchors — origin, function, the built environment, a shared constraint, a shared
exclusion. **This city's is a shared constraint, and the constraint is that origin may not be asked about.**
Phase 7's non-prying norm removes the one axis that would otherwise compete with locality.

⚠ **Build is not an identity axis here and Phase 8 should not be read as claiming otherwise.** Phase 8 found
wide build variety with no dominant pattern, because the precision trade and the harbor trade pull in opposite
directions. **Ch. 13 settles what that means: nothing.** Its Guadalajara comparison is four people of wildly
different physiques who are simply four Guadalajarans. ***The variety is texture, not peoplehood.***

**Gen/Mark is likewise not a differentiator here.** Ch. 14 is explicit that the same Mark ships nationwide and
therefore *"tells you essentially nothing about which specific city she is from"* — and that a robot's
fabrication and her home city are, as a rule, the same place. **Robots here are made here, by this city's own
chamber, for this city's own needs.**

# B · Per-population culture

## The robot half

Everything Phases 4–8 established applies: the workforce is roughly two-thirds robot against a population that
is half; vibration is perceived more acutely, in a city whose loudest hour is its sleeping hour; the drinking
culture is bohemian and varied; 43.4% of working life is free, which canon calls **the material proof that
leaving worked.**

**And the basis of peoplehood is Ch. 11's**, which matters more here than anywhere it has come up so far:

> *"Humans form tribes and clans from three possible bases: **genetics, geography, and shared ideas or
> experience.** Robots are missing one of these entirely… **the genetics axis simply is not available to them
> — not weakened, not substituted, absent.**"*

**So a robot here has two bases available: geography, and shared ideas and experience.**

## The human half

Humans hold all three. Their heritage has a physical carrier — **genetics is the one axis that requires nobody
to ask about it and nobody to be told.**

## Both, under the same law

Phase 7's civic law applies identically to everyone: *"nobody asks about your past, ever."* It is not a robot
custom or a human one. **It is the city's, and it covers both populations without distinction.**

# C · Inter-population relations — the local inflection

> The spine: *"Restating the setting's baseline is not a finding. The strongest available move is that the
> place reorganizes its population along a different axis entirely — ask **is there a category here that
> matters more than kind?**"*

**The national baseline is *"egalitarian, skewed in favor of robots"*, and it holds here. That is not a
finding and is not offered as one.**

## Yes — locality matters more than kind

Ch. 13 already says identity tracks *"same city, same struggles, same life-goals."* **In this city that is not
merely the strongest axis; it is very nearly the only one left standing**, because the civic law suppresses
origin as a topic and canon rules out build and Gen/Mark as carriers.

⭐ **And the norm is the thing that makes it apply across kind.** A human and a robot are equally un-askable
here. **The one social rule that governs everything is indifferent to which kind of person you are** — so the
line it draws is between people who are from here and people who are not, and never between human and robot.

## ⭐ But the identical law does not cost the two populations the same

**This is the phase's finding, and it is built entirely from binding canon.**

| | Bases of peoplehood available | What the non-prying norm removes | Left with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A human here** | genetics · geography · shared experience | the *told* half of heritage | **two** |
| **A robot here** | — · geography · shared experience | the *told* half of heritage | **one** |

**A human's heritage survives the norm because it is carried in the body. A robot's heritage exists only as
something that has to be said** — and this city's defining civic law is that it is not said.

> ## The same rule, applied identically to everyone, leaves a human with two bases for peoplehood and a robot with one.

**Nobody designed this.** It is the collision of a reasonable local norm with a physiological asymmetry canon
established for entirely unrelated reasons, in Ch. 8 and Ch. 11, about genetic relatedness. ⛔ **There is no
villain, no policy, and no complaint anyone could file** — which is exactly why it has run for two centuries.

### And it sharpens Phase 7's counterculture rather than duplicating it

Phase 7 found canon's own dissenting population: the smaller heritage communities who *"feel slowly absorbed
rather than protected."* Phase 8 found the mechanism — ordinary language acquisition, with the counterweight
forbidden.

⭐ **Phase 9 adds who it falls on hardest.** Those communities contain both humans and robots. **The human
members keep an axis the robot members do not have at all.** *A heritage community losing its language loses
more from its robots than from its humans, and the loss is quieter, because the robots have nothing else
holding the thread.*

## ⭐ Law 2 explains why the norm needs no enforcer

Phase 7 recorded a puzzle: a *"civic law with no legislator, no penalty, and total compliance."*

**`Laws_of_Robotics.md` Law 2:** *"A robot will understand that anything she is capable of doing to others, it
is also realistically possible for others to do to her."*

> **The non-prying norm is Law 2 expressed as manners.** *The protection you extend is identical to the
> protection you receive*, so everyone is on both sides of it at once and nobody needs to enforce anything.
> **It is reciprocal by construction — which is why it is stable, and why it feels like courtesy rather than
> restriction.**

⚠ **And reciprocity of that kind assumes the stakes are symmetric.** Here they are not, per the table above.
***The norm is reciprocal in form and asymmetric in effect*** — and Law 2, being about capability rather than
cost, does not notice the difference.

⛔ **Note the limit of this reading.** Law 2 is a **civic and procedural** safeguard hard-coded into how robots
build robots, *"not innate programming inside any individual robot's own mind."* **So this is a resemblance of
shape, not a claim that the norm was derived from the Law.** Canon supplies no such derivation and none is
asserted.

# E · Swap test

> *Would this survive unchanged if the location's name were swapped for its nearest comparable?*

⛔ **No, and it fails in two independent places, which is what makes it localized.**

1. **It requires the non-prying norm**, which is this city's own named civic law, not a regional condition.
2. **It requires a population split near 50/50.** In a wholly robot city the asymmetry has nobody to be
   asymmetric against; in a human-majority city it touches a small minority. **Here the two populations are
   within one percentage point of each other at Census I**, so the same law lands on two nearly equal halves
   and lands differently on each.

# Phase 9 — closed

**Findings.** The lens is city/locality, which is canon's own default — but here it is nearly exclusive,
because the civic law suppresses origin and canon rules out build and Gen/Mark as carriers. Locality therefore
matters more than kind, and the reason is that the one governing norm is indifferent to kind. Against that,
the phase's real finding: Ch. 11 establishes that robots have two bases of peoplehood where humans have three,
the missing one being genetics — so a rule forbidding anyone to speak about their past leaves a human with two
and a robot with one. The heritage communities Phase 7 identified therefore lose more from their robot members
than their human ones, and lose it more quietly. And Law 2 explains why the norm is self-enforcing: it is
reciprocal by construction, which is also why it cannot see its own asymmetry.
