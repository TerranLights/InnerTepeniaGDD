**Research on the premise (C-2 (f); section 8).**
- Competition theory: ethnic mobilization rises where groups compete for jobs and housing and a collective sense of boundaries organizes that competition (A, C); a reformulation adds that competition leads to conflict when perceived as unfair and when interdependence is low (A); the effect is contested (a 1997 study found it disappears after correcting for selection bias; C).
- Realistic conflict (the Robbers Cave study): competition made the groups, contact alone made things worse, and superordinate goals reduced conflict most (A, B).
- Contact: Allport's four conditions (equal status, common goals, cooperation, support of authorities, law or custom) facilitate prejudice reduction but are not required (Pettigrew and Tropp, 515 studies; A, B). An officeless standard lacks "support of authorities" by construction (B).
- Cross-cutting ties: they help conciliation but are not enough alone (Siroky and Hechter; A, B); ethnicity dominates where inequality between groups is high and within groups is low (B); in a Lebanon experiment, cross-sect discussion reduced support for sectarian politics only among people of the same economic class (B); inter-ethnic business, labor and civic associations restrained riots (Varshney; B, C); where groups meet only in a market subject to no moral law they "mix but do not combine" (Furnivall; C). No study isolating occupation as the cross-cutting tie was found (dead end at the sources; A, B).
- Group size: a cultural cleavage's political salience depends on the groups' size relative to the arena, not on their cultural distance (Posner; B).
- Cases: Hawaii's plantations paid by nationality; the 1909 strike was against that differential; unions were organized by ethnic group until the ILWU's interracial strike of 1946 (A, B, C); in 1920 the Japanese union refused Filipino workers (C). Butte: a shared occupation coexisted with hard Irish and Cornish blocs, because rival mine owners hired by origin (A). The Ruhr, 1902: Polish miners built their own union after the established unions treated them as "hardly real miners" (C). Mauritius and Suriname: origin blocs formed through an electoral channel with no excluding project; in Suriname ethnic voting is eroding through creolization over generations (C).
