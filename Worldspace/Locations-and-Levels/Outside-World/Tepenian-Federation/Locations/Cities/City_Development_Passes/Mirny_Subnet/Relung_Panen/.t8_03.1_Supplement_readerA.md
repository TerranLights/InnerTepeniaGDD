- **RSA-1 · The gulf sits beside the sector of the shelf that melts first and longest. PROPOSED.** On the nearest satellite record the shelf's south-east (the gulf's side) starts melting earliest and refreezes latest, because wind-kept blue ice and the oasis's dark ground absorb more sun; shelf-wide melt averages about a month a summer and has run 44 days without a break. This place's own air means (December −0.0, January +0.6, February −2.1 °C; spec L189–190, L179–180) put its warm core in the same months. **What it causes:** the risk window of `DR-140` item 8 (December to February) is, on this record, a little longer at the gulf's side of the shelf than at the shelf's middle; a run of warm weeks is a whole-summer event, not a day's. *(#2, #4.)*
- **RSA-2 · A summer runway on ice stays open only where it was sited and kept for it, and the keeping has a known weak point. PROPOSED, mechanism.** The one real blue-ice runway that flies through summer was placed on ground that stays snow-free and cold; elsewhere ice runways pause. Melt holes form where dust darkens the ice and are patched with ice that is weaker than the original; a snow cap works only where snow is plentiful; flood ice carries half the load of clear ice. **On this place's lines:** the gulf carries cryoconite in two of three zones and tidal cracks at the shores (`Airports.md` L21), lies at sea level, and the oasis has little lying snow (spec L109) and about 92 mm ESTIMATED precipitation (spec L157 `[DEM]`). **Third order:** patching makes the door's surface a patchwork whose weakest pieces are the newest repairs; keeping the gulf open in summer would spend its own ice to mend itself. Who keeps it is not written (REQ-165 (c)). *(#28, #29, #32.)*
- **RSA-3 · The gulf door's year has two kinds of risk, and they fall in different months. PROPOSED.** The melt risk (soft or rotten ice) sits December to February (RSA-1; S3-18); the storm risk sits in event (spec L146 `[DEM]`: blizzards on 53 days, a 56 m/s peak), and the peak storm on record fell in **February** and lasted **3 days** (#45). Comparable lake and sea-ice strips close by judgment as ice weakens, early in December for one sea-ice runway (#3). The cold months carry the cold-season hazard already found (sastrugi, S3-21), and June gives about 2.7 h of daylight (spec L155). No closure is written.
- **RSA-4 · The oasis's meltwater reaches the gulf where the airports stand, through one outlet, in a flow that varies by summer. PROPOSED; S3-32 confirmed at abstract standing.** Algae Lake drains to the gulf; more than ten million cubic meters leave it in a measured summer; peaks reach about 6 m³/s; some reaches stop flowing in some summers. Lake ice decays first at shores and inflow mouths (S3 RT-9 table, Britannica). **What it causes:** the gulf's ice is not uniform in summer: the outlet's neighborhood is the first ground to fail, and the summer when the river runs hard is the summer the door weakens soonest (RSA-RF-1). Where on the gulf each airport sits is not ruled. *(#52.)*
- **The schedule:** `RSA-DQ-1` below, with its reasoning.
- **Divergences:** the comparables are glacier ice at height, sea ice or temperate lake ice; the gulf is perennial fresh landfast ice about 3 m thick at sea level on a tidal lake renewed from below (S3-19). In this world winter flying happens (the winter intake airport, `DR-82`), so darkness and cold are not the limits they are for the real programs, whose summer-only seasons reflect logistics as much as ice.
- **Withheld:** the one season's 10-week closure of a glacier runway as a figure for this place (it is a comparable's). **Omitted:** program and expedition histories; the air-link program's decisions.

- **RSA-7 · A housed animal here is a heat source and a moisture source, and its house is run on humidity, not on the thermometer. PROPOSED.** Real cold-climate houses ventilate by humidity and ammonia, keep water liquid, and get part of their warmth from the animals and their bedding. **On this place's lines:** every month's computed average low is below freezing (spec L179–190 `[computed, REQ-52]`) and summer air is very dry (RSA-14), so a hen house or pig room is a warm, wet room inside a dry, cold city; bedding is an import (nothing woody grows on the rock), unless the racks' dried residues are used. **What it makes visible:** a small barn's exhaust as the one damp, smelling outlet in a dry district; a heated waterer in every animal room; litter carried out to the made ground. *(#11, #62.)*
- **RSA-8 · The growing rooms' residues suit insects better than animals, and the insects' frass suits the made ground. PROPOSED.** Leafy waste is mostly water and digests poorly; real practice feeds it wet to mealworms grown at greenhouse temperatures, and insect frass from vegetable waste is high in organic matter. **On this place's lines:** made ground needs organic matter (spec L222), the oasis has little of its own (S3-46), and the city holds a soil-recovery role (`DR-75` L1421). **Third order:** the chain runs residue → insects → frass to made ground, with the insects as feed for the housed birds; the animals' share of the residues is the drier fraction. Insect rooms sit at about room temperature, so they belong beside the warm racks. Rates not written. *(#12, #38.)*
- **RSA-9 · Land-based fish here are a heated-water task, not a heated-room task. PROPOSED, comparable standing.** Cold-climate practice heats and insulates the water only; cold-water fish keep the heat needed lowest. Beside lit racks that shed heat (S3-43), the water is the thing kept warm. Whether rack heat warms it is not ruled (REQ-185; RSA-RF-3). *(#39.)*
- **RSA-10 · The oasis's wild birds are summer neighbors of the southern hills, from late spring to early autumn. PROPOSED, a coordinate fact.** Snow petrels breed in about a thousand pairs, in small colonies through the southern hills between 25 and 155 m, mostly in rock cracks on hills and ridges, the rest under boulders; skuas nest and hunt them. On comparables the season runs from arrival around late October–November, eggs in November–December, chicks into February, departure by March. **What it makes visible:** white petrels at the rock ridges in the long-light months, skuas overhead at the made edges; any work on the southern ridges (stone, culverts, drains) meeting nests in summer, and an empty ridge in winter. No custom or rule is read from it. *(#13, #40, #63.)*
- **Divergence:** the comparables' barns face seasons; here the animal rooms are seasonless like the racks. **Omitted:** slaughter (left unwritten, as the brief asks); real-world animal and biosecurity law.
