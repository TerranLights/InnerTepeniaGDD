Mirny, as the pass has found it, is a working coast on the Antarctic Circle where a wind that is almost never calm is the defining fact, the sky has no polar night but about 29 days of midnight sun, and the thermometer holds a flat winter plateau while the light ramps — so the year is dated by light and the danger by wind, and the honest cues of that wind went unread because no one who came had been taught them. It is held to its parent by two continuous obligations — the subnet's contact routed through one relay with no second path, and the stone and machinery of its quarries and yards, one chain for the eastern roads and one for the chamber maker — while what it receives from beyond the continent arrives through a sea that is a frozen surface for most of the year and an unschedulable, wind-closable window for the rest. Its 1,351,430 people at Census I are robots and humans at near-equal odds (50.73 % robots), drawn from fourteen stocks with no majority, founded by exile under a sponsoring founding nation that sent no stock, and they keep one shared stop, wear one coat, and decide each crossing alone.
