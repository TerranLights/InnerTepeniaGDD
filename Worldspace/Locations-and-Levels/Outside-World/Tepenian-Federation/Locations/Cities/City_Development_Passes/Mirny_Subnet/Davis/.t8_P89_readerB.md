### XB-106 · A Davis accent, contact features, loanwords (answers XC6-33)

- **Pass finding.** Phase 8 L250 and L255 exclude an accent (killed at XB-104). Nothing else is written.
- **Research (section 8, searches 1-6, 13, 15-17).** Real-world populations of many origins who share a language show:
  - (1) **Leveling and focusing toward a new local variety**, driven by children and young speakers, with "demography and the social-network characteristics of individuals" "crucial to the outcomes" (Kerswill & Williams 2000).
  - (2) **First-language interference that varies by origin** among adult learners. Pidgin Hawaiian, the Hawaiian-based contact language of Chinese, Portuguese and Japanese plantation workers, shows "Allophonic variation typically reflected first language interference", with Japanese speakers' /l/ as a flap (APICS survey 71). Indonesian, the national language over many home languages, shows the same in perception studies.
  - (3) **Substrate influence from the groups present when a variety stabilized**, more than from groups that arrived later (Siegel on Hawai'i Creole English; Mufwene's founder principle).
  - (4) **Code-switching** where several languages are widely understood (XB-105).
  - (5) **Innovation even among adults in isolation**, for example a vowel shift in an Antarctic winter group (Harrington et al. 2019).
  - (6) **Sometimes no regional dialects at all** (Modern Hebrew, per Striedl 2025).
- **Applying the divergence operator from Davis's own data.**
  - **Arrival timing, which real-world research says decides substrate and founder effects, is not in Davis's data.** Shares are "identical between censuses" (spec L78). The "(founding wave)" tag "says nothing about when people arrived" (orchestrator result 4, CENSUS L33). DR-104 (2) has Phase 2 reason by headcount, not arrival order. So founder and substrate reasoning cannot run.

Whether the Module delivers a local form or a national standard is unwritten. If it delivers a standard, the main real-world engine of a local accent is weakened for half the city.
