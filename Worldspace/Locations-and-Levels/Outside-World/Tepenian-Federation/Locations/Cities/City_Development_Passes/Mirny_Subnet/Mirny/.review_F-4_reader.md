## 6 · What exactly changes

**Nothing in L175 stops standing.** Clause by clause:

1. **"A newcomer who speaks the supporting tier"** stands. Under `DR-111` the tier is English and Russian (L1769). Under A1 its two languages now stand in different relations to the dominant language:
   - English is named a primary base of the pidgin.
   - Russian is named in neither base (L1895).

   Both remain languages other than the dominant one. A1 makes *Hawaiian and Tepenian* "one language over time" (L1895). It does not make Hawaiian and any of its bases one language: a pidgin is its own language. So the ground "the supporting tier as a language apart from the dominant one" (note L90) still stands for both halves of the tier. This is one property with two effects: each language keeps its own relation, and the clause is true of both.
2. **"can be told the wind's dangers in it before she has the dominant language"** stands. A1 says nothing about how far a speaker of a base language follows the pidgin, and no line in my list rules on it. An English-speaking newcomer may need less time to gain the dominant language. That would be a new derivation, not something A1 states, so I do not assert it. The clause does not depend on how long that interval lasts, only on the interval existing, and A1 does not remove it.
3. **"if the protocol is given in it"** stands as a condition (AR-45). Neither A1 nor A3 says what language the protocol is given in. The piece's own dead end (L270) is still a dead end.
4. **"a newcomer existing at all"** (the ground) stands, and A3 widens it:
   - Before A3, the piece's L109 left human arrival after the treaty date conditional ("If no later human channel opened"). Under that reading, an Act 2 newcomer who speaks the tier came only from the robot channel (open in law, L46) or from movement inside Tepenia.
   - A3 and II.2.5 (L1913, L50) add a canon human route after the signing. That route goes *into Tepenia*, not to Mirny. The route to Mirny for any later arrival stays unread (L140: "route, timing and body split unread (R-112; AR-65)").
   - L175 asserts no arrival. It states what a newcomer *can* be told *if* the protocol is given in the tier. So A3 can only widen the cases the claim covers. It falsifies no clause.
