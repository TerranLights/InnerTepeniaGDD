## The closing test

> *Could a person live an entire life here — and would that life be unlike a life in any comparable place?* (`04` L58)

**Answered honestly: structurally yes, and distinctively so; as a lived life, not yet — the honest answer for the life itself is "probably, I suppose", and by the runbook's own rule that means the pass is not finished, whatever the gates say.**

**What a life here would be, from the files.** A person — robot or human at near-equal odds — would be born or built into, or arrive into, a coast where the wind is the ground tone of every hour and the thermometer cannot tell the season; would keep a stop that no sky keeps for a month of the year; would decide each crossing alone on a cue that is honest and that nobody was taught to read, and would answer alone if it went wrong; would live under a written inheritance that cannot be corrected, beside a relay that cannot stop; would, if a robot of the first cohort, carry the only first-hand memory of how the city began, with no record to vouch for it and no standing because of it; would, if born here, inherit the sorting but not the teaching. That is **unlike a comparable windy coast** in its conjunction (Gate 4: the compact, the two lines, the hub outage, the light-dated year all survive the swap). **The structure is deep, third-order and its own.**

**What the gates could not see — and why the answer is not yet yes:**
1. **The lived middle is empty by design.** What a person here eats beyond the national pantry, what they sing, how they court, what they laugh at, what they do on a free afternoon or through a nine-day storm, what language the street speaks, what a child's day is — every one is a named, reasoned null (Gate 1 G1-F1). The gates certify that each null is *explained*; none of them can tell whether a life is *possible to imagine from the inside*. On these files it is not yet.
2. **The strongest finding sits in a slot the pass's own Ordinary Life phase certifies as empty in every file** (`P4` L236; Gate 3). The compact is a derivation of great reach — but its fourth leg is a reading of absence, and Gate 11 rates the strong form of that absence (nothing posted, ever, in a city of 1.35 million) implausible as a world-fact (G11-F1). The reconciling form — no rule over a person's own crossing, whatever operations post — is already in the pass and should be the one it carries.
3. **Monocausality.** Almost everything Mirny originates is one proposition and its consequences (Gate I GI-F2; `06` NS-11). Coherence and an author's habit look identical from inside one pass; only the terminal check, with every column present, can tell them apart.
4. **The research stopped short of the people.** Eleven of fourteen stocks' repertoires were never researched (Gate 7 G7-F2); the transplanted layer of every Making category is therefore a null, and Act 1 — when origins were fresh — is the least populated part of the city's life.
5. **What no gate measures:** whether anyone at Mirny is ever happy in a way the files could name. The shadow checks keep finding that "Mirny works"; nothing yet says what it is like to love it (Gate 10, question 1).

**Verdict:** a person could plausibly *survive* an entire life here, and its shape would be unlike anywhere else; whether they would *live* one — with food, songs, jokes, a first language and a way of spending the hours owed to nobody — is still waiting on the post-ULM stages and the developer. That is a legitimate, expected state (`RB` L366–372), not a failure to paper over.
