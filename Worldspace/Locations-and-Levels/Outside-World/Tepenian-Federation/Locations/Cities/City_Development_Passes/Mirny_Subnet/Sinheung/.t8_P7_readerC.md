Stainless pressure and fluid hardware needs chromium (every stainless grade has at least 10.5% Cr) and usually nickel, plus molybdenum for corrosion resistance. Bearing steel (AISI 52100) carries about 1.3 to 1.6% chromium. Tool steels need tungsten, vanadium, molybdenum and cobalt. Steel for cold service gets its low-temperature toughness mainly from nickel: 3.5% nickel steel is serviceable to about −100 °C, and 9% nickel steel goes much lower. Welding consumables need fluorspar, rutile and ferromanganese. Furnace linings need refractory minerals; steel plants use roughly 60% of all refractories.

Coal is known there and in the Transantarctic Mountains. Chromium and platinum-group metals are only suspected, in a West Antarctic intrusion far from this coast. Copper and molybdenum occurrences are known only on the Antarctic Peninsula. The site's own rock is metamorphic bedrock whose boron and phosphate minerals are "scientifically significant in their variety and origin" (ASMA L89-91): building stone, not ore.

On real-world geology, this coast's bulk base is thinner still.

## 3. New findings (including any the questions did not ask for)

**XC-215 · The overseas stream carries little mass but controls the specification (`seed`).**
- Alloying additions are small fractions by mass: about 1.5% chromium in bearing steel, manganese in every steel, a few percent nickel for cold service (§8). The road carries the tonnage; the sea carries what makes that tonnage meet specification.
- The chain of consequences:
  1. the sea stream arrives in the one window (Ports.md L575), and real resupply here is once a year (ASMA L447);
  2. so the cluster must hold at least a year's stock of these inputs, or a missed window halts the specified part of the breadth for a year;
  3. so a guarded store of alloying and process inputs is a physical, visible feature of the city: something a player can see, enter and handle.
- Who holds that stock is not stated, and it is left open (no forced fit).

**XC-216 · The ossuary ruling removes the one domestic substitute (`conditional`; robot physiology, rule 7).**
- Canon forbids reclaiming metal from robot ossuaries: "There is no such variant. The prohibition is not a scruple that scarcity erodes" (physiology L133). It says "They mine their metal from quarries" (L125).
- Metal is scarce, and recovery is a necessary industry (L117-120, L139-140). Robots are the majority body and "Metal bone persists" (L96).
- So every robot body withdraws metal permanently. This city's specified metal comes from extraction by road, ordinary recovery, and the sea stream, never from the dead.
