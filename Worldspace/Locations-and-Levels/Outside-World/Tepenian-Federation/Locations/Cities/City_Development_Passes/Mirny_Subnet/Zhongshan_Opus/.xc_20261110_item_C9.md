### Reasoning

1. **Part 1, the fact, holds.** The spec's table today still copies the human share into the robot column. Checked from my own
   reading of `spec` L36-70, with the Census I and II totals from the note L60-61 (robots 607,752 and 446,082; humans 595,855 and
   486,813). Robot share against human share, Census I | Census II: Sinian Federation 35.830 / 35.830 | 35.830 / 35.830; Korea
   9.700 / 9.700 | 9.700 / 9.700; Germany 7.105 / 7.104 | 7.104 / 7.105; Australia 3.660 / 3.660 | 3.660 / 3.660; Austro-Bavaria
   1.366 / 1.366 | 1.365 / 1.366; Siberia 0.713 / 0.713 | 0.713 / 0.713; Tuva 0.013 / 0.013 | 0.013 / 0.013. Each row matches to
   within 0.001 point, and the sample covers all three tiers.

2. **Part 2, the standing, no longer follows.** LIMIT 1 ranks the robot columns below the human columns: a "METHOD, not an
   observation". `DR-11` L459 rules the opposite, and it does so of the robot columns *described as copying the human proportions*:
   they *"carry the same standing as the human columns."* So the copying, which is LIMIT 1's whole ground, is not a reason to demote
   them. The contrast LIMIT 1 draws is also gone on the human side. Under `DR-12`, the human per-nation figures are not an
   observation either; they are weighting, randomized within tier (L465-468, L472). Both columns now stand on the same footing:
   ADMITTED-DEMOTED, readable at the tier level, never at the within-tier level (L474).
   - *What does that cause?* The robot-only limit becomes a special case of two general rules: `DR-12`'s within-tier ban (already
     carried for the whole table by the pass's own LIMIT 2, L49: *"The ordinal structure is canon; the precision is arithmetic"*),
     plus the plain fact that the robot shares equal the human shares. LIMIT 1 no longer has a separate ground of its own.

3. **Part 3, the ban, narrows in scope; its result stands.**
   - The ban as written (*"No finding of the form "robots of nation X, here, specifically" may be built on it"*) is wider than the
     rulings allow. Under `DR-11` and `DR-12`, a tier-level origin reading applies to robots as it does to humans: the Sinian
     Federation row is the primary tier, and Korea, Japan and Germany are significant, for robots as for humans. Under `DR-128` item 1,
     the origin row covers robots too. Only two limits survive: (a) no within-tier finding, for either column (`DR-12`); and (b) no
     robot-versus-human difference, because the table gives none.
   - *What does that cause?* Guard 5's sentence, *"No robot-versus-human origin difference is derived anywhere below"*, still follows
     from the inputs as they stand. Every row gives the same share, so there is no difference to derive. Reading meaning into the
     0.001-point rounding gaps would be a within-tier reading, which `DR-12` forbids. Its stated ground changes: the reason is not
     that the robot figures lack standing, but that the robot figures equal the human figures.
   - *And what does that cause?* `DR-128` item 1 lets robots carry *"whatever percent makes sense for them"*. Today's spec sets no
     robot percentage apart from the human one, so with the inputs as they stand Guard 5 holds. If robot percentages are ever set
     separately, Guard 5 is no longer "already satisfied" and has to be run again. That is a condition on a future input, not a
     present change, so it does not move the verdict (see C9-Q1).
   - *And what does that cause?* LIMIT 1's *"None is, below"* says the pass built no robot-specific origin finding. Under the rulings
     that absence is not an error: the rulings permit a robot origin reading at the tier level but do not require a separate one, and
     with identical shares a robot tier reading says the same as the human one. One thing would not follow under `DR-11` L450: reading
     the roster as covering humans only, which leaves robots (50.49% of Census I, note L72) without an origin. Guard 5's wording
     ("no robot-versus-human difference") suggests §8.1 reads the roster as covering both, but the merge itself lies outside my
     admitted span (Phase 8 after L136), so I cannot confirm it. A re-derivation should check it (next section). Here the chain stops
     moving the verdict.

4. **Why not HOLDS.** More than a figure or a label changes. LIMIT 1's ground (robot columns rank below human columns) and the scope
   of its ban (every robot-of-nation-X finding) no longer follow. Both would need restating.

5. **Why not FLIPS.** The opposite would be that a robot-versus-human origin difference now follows, or that a robot-specific origin
   finding must now be built. Neither follows from the inputs as they stand: the table's shares are identical, `DR-12` forbids
   within-tier readings, and `DR-128` item 1 permits robot percentages of their own without setting any for Zhongshan. The direction
   of the conclusion, *no robot-versus-human origin difference derived and no within-tier robot finding*, stands.

6. **A limit of this reading.** `DR-11` L461 names a follow-on, `DR-11a`, which is outside my list and was not opened. If it narrows
   or extends `DR-11` on robot origin, this verdict should be checked against it.
