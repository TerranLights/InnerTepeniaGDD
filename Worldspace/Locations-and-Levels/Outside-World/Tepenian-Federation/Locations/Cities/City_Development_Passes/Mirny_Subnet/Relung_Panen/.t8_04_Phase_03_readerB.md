### Sound

**Human half**
- **P3B-103 · Most days are quiet, and the loudest steady sound outdoors is the city's own machinery.** *Grounds:* a calm mean (4.6 m/s, spec L143; calms 39% `[DEM]` `[PA-3]`); little lying snow (L109) and about 92 mm ESTIMATED a year `[DEM]`, so little weather sound; no open sea (L13), so no surf. *Against that,* the city runs machines that never stop: the heat of the lit export food, about 0.5 GW (`DR-96` item 4), must be removed from the growing rooms (S3-43); winter water is pumped from deep lakes through heated intakes into heated storage (S3-31); warm charging space runs in every month `[PROV-8]` (S3-27). **PROPOSED:** on an ordinary day the street hears the city's air handling, pumps and traffic, unmasked by wind. *Second order:* a storm episode is announced by the ear as much as the eye: the sound field changes from machinery to wind within hours (storms come with passing lows and give hours of warning, S3-15; the two-sided wind, S2-23). *Third order:* when the supply stops, the basin goes quiet: in a place where the steady sound is the city's own load, an interruption is heard city-wide before it is felt (S3-49's long-line failure; RF-56 stays reserved). *Expression (heard):* a resident notices the hum only when it stops. *Conditional:* how loud, and where, depends on the growing rooms' envelope and siting, neither written (P3B-RQ-103, P3B-DQ-102).
- **P3B-104 · Hard ground and few soft surfaces.** Bare rock and thin sand and gravel over till (spec L109; S3-57), little lying snow (L109): footfall and wheels sound on rock and gravel all year, with no snowpack to deaden them; in the melt months the sound underfoot turns to slush and running water (L171–172). **PROPOSED, in kind.** *Expression (heard):* a winter street sounds of boots on grit; a January street of boots in water.

## C · The public and institutional split

*The headline institution's profile is not the general answer (`03` L526). The street register is written first; the institutions after, each scoped to the people who work in it.*
- **P3B-122 · The street register: one outdoors that turns twice a year.** For nine to ten months: still, cold, dry, quiet but for the city's own machinery, hard underfoot, lit by a low or absent sun on shaded ground in June (P3B-103, P3B-104, P3B-111, P3B-112). - **P3B-123 · Inside the institutions, the outdoors is overruled, each in a different direction.** *(Each scoped; each a minority's workplace, never the city's general texture.)*
  - **The lit growing rooms** (the food role, 103,535 workers, 13.7% of the workforce, `18` L30 `[DoI corroboration]`; frame S0-12): warm, lit around the clock, without a season, since the export food is grown indoors year-round (spec L258); most of their electricity is light, and they make heat that must be shed (S3-1, S3-43); a stoppage costs their crops within hours to a day (S3-1). **Inside, the year does not turn.**

- **P3B-131 · The air door's own dust.** *What is simply true here that nothing on the list explains:* both airports stand on lake ice that does not go away in a summer whose average days rise above freezing (`DR-96` item 1; spec L179–180, L190 `[computed, REQ-52]`), and that ice carries dark pits in two of its three zones (`Airports.md` L21). *The physical system that would produce exactly that:* an epishelf lake's ice that loses at its top and grows from below (S3-19; research L185, L187), so it persists; on an oasis whose slope winds carry rock and ice dust (research L147; S3-57) and where little snow falls or lies (spec L109; L157–166 `[DEM]`) to bury what lands, dust settles on a surface that is never renewed from above, gathers as the top wears down, and melts in as pits (cryoconite; dark ice fails first, S3-20). **PROPOSED, a mechanism in kind from Step 3's comparables.** *Second order:* the process that keeps the door is the process that weakens it: the ice rebuilt from below brings no dust up, the surface that wears away leaves its dust behind, and the darkened patches are where the summer surface fails first (S3-20).
