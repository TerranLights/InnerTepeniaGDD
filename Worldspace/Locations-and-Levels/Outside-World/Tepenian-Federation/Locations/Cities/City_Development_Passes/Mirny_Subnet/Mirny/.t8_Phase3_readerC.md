## The axis

**Windward versus lee.**

**Peer-free test — could this be true anywhere?** Half of it: any windy coast sorts its surfaces into windward and lee, so the sorting alone is generic. What passes is the alignment Mirny's admitted facts fix — the wind, the plateau and the stone all arrive from the south-southeast, the sea and the midwinter sun lie to the north, so the sheltered side is also the lit side and the sea-ice side, and it is also where the drift ends up. Delete every other city's name from the sentence *Mirny's texture is sorted by which face meets the wind, and the sheltered, lit face is the one the snow settles on* and the claim survives; it was never relational. Not checked against any sibling (the brief bars any comparison with another city).

**P3-C1 · Exposure sorts the surface** *[A + CORR + D]*
- **Sources.** `SPEC` L71 chars 1–94 [A]: "katabatic from the south and southeast (off the East Antarctic plateau)". `SPEC` L174 chars 272–623 [A, with the contract's reading-order caution, see Snag 2]: "quarried raw materials from the south (toward the continental interior/South Pole direction)" and "the sheltered industrial core inside the city's outer wind-fortified ring". `RES` §1 [CORR]: prevailing direction "east-southeast to southeast"; a solid structure collects drift on its windward side, then its lee, and "Corners and ends scour".
- **1st order.** One bearing carries the wind, so every solid structure has three ground states around it: a windward bank, a lee bank, and scoured flanks. The ring has a windward arc and a lee arc [D: the ring surrounds the core].
- **2nd order.** The same bearing carries the stone (quarries "from the south"), so material enters at the exposed arc and becomes product in the sheltered core; the opposite bearing carries the sea, the harbor and the midwinter sun (`RES` §2: "due north"; the sea lies north of this coast, because the continent and its plateau lie to the south, [D]).
- **3rd order.** (a) The sheltered side is the side with winter light — and a solid windbreak fills on its windward side first, then in its lee, and at equilibrium "both drifts run about 12–15 times its height" (`RES` §1), "so the cost is a choice remade after every storm" (`RES` §1). **The comfortable margin is the margin that has to be dug.** (b) Passages between solid structures fall into two classes [D]: along the wind they are flanks (scoured, fast, loud, cold), across the wind they are banks (filled, slow, sheltered). In a field at the Frame's 7,000–10,000 people per km² (`SPN` §7), one building's lee is the next building's windward face: the street plan has a second layer made by the wind. This is the Spine's shadow candidate "one building's shelter as another's drift", read as texture.
- **Boundary.** The chain assumes solid structures and passages between them; no admitted file gives a street plan, a building form or the ring's height (R-C2, R-C3). Every claim is conditional on that.

## B. The four sensory sub-fields, separately

### B.1 Sound

**Given.** `SPEC` L82 chars 166–422: "mean wind 12.0 m/s"; L71 chars 156–307: "average wind speeds 10–14 m/s; sustained storms exceeding 40 m/s (144 km/h) have been recorded; wind is the dominant environmental feature of this city"; L134: "blizzard conditions can develop rapidly". `RES` §1 [CORR]: calm and weak wind together are about 3 % of observations; storms last up to about nine days; in summer and the transition seasons the katabatic builds in the evening, "peaks about 05–08 local and eases by 15–18"; in winter it follows approaching cyclones; "Whether Mirny keeps a weak daily cycle in winter is UNKNOWN". No admitted file gives any machine, voice, interior, ice, alarm or relay sound.

**Human body.** The wind is the acoustic ground of every exposed place; the sheltered core and the lee are quiet *from the wind* and nothing more is given.

**Robot body.** The only source is `RP` L187: "robots perceive sound and vibration across a meaningfully wider range than humans do". The file says the range is wider, not what lies in the extra range, and not that the wind or the storm has anything there. I write nothing further about a robot's hearing.

**P3-C2 · There is no quiet baseline, so change is what is heard** *[A + CORR + D]*
- **1st.** Wind is the exterior's ground tone for essentially all hours; the quiet places are the interior and the lee.
- **2nd.** Where the ground tone never drops, the events are changes: a rise, or a lull. Calm and weak wind together are about 3 % of observations — roughly 11 days' worth if observations are uniform (own arithmetic, raw output in Snags) — so a lull is rare enough to be an event. The change has two clocks [CORR]: in summer and the transition seasons a daily one (evening build, early-morning peak, afternoon lull); in winter an episodic one (cyclones, storms up to about nine days). Time-of-day figures are the source's local time; no in-world clock is set (`FRM` PA-8), so they are not mapped to hours.
- **3rd.** `RES` §1 says "The cues are honest; they are unread". The ear is one of the instruments that would read them, and `P2` P2-3 ("Two bodies, two channels, two clocks.") says the two bodies would train it differently: a human born here in childhood, a robot as an adult, with a wider sound and vibration range (`RP` L187). **Whether the storm's honest cues lie inside that wider range is nowhere stated**, so Mirny cannot be written as a place where either body hears the storm sooner. `P2` RF-6 stays reserved. What can be written: the wind's daily clock is the only recurrent acoustic hour in the nightless weeks (E), and a lull in a place with no calm is heard as an absence.
- **Asked and unanswered (REQUESTED):** the sound of the sheltered core's work (the files give the function — "processed into construction materials", machinery — not its noise); the sound of an interior; the sound of sea ice changing ("ice conditions can change quickly", `SPEC` L133, gives no sound); any audible signal of the relay or harbor.

### B.3 Feel

**Given.** `SPEC` L82 chars 1–68: "WIND, decisively — the cold is NOT what defines this city." L142 chars 103–198: "effective wind-chill during katabatic events can drive apparent temperatures far below ambient". L131 chars 126–366: "outdoor activity in strong katabatic conditions is dangerous regardless of cold protection". L139 chars 103–270: "even at relatively moderate temperatures, sustained katabatic events make outdoor exposure dangerous". `RD` L14 monthly means: all twelve are below 0 (warmest Jan −2.0), coldest Aug −16.4, annual −11.2; May–Sep span 1.0 °C (own arithmetic). `SPEC` L107 note: "Warmest period; brief rain possible". `SPEC` L57 chars 1–132: "The coast here is rocky and exposed." `RES` §1 [CORR]: "onset warms the air at the surface"; ridges in the snow point along the prevailing wind; drift behaves as in P3-C1. `RES` §4 [CORR]: "The surface hides its danger twice — in the air and underfoot."; inland, crevasses are hidden under snow. `RES` §3 [CORR]: "For about ten months the sea is a surface".

**Human body.** Felt cold is set by wind more than by temperature. Underfoot: scoured rock or packed bank along the coast; a ground layer of moving snow (F); inland, ice whose crevasses the snow hides.

**P3-C4 · The thermometer is not the feel, and the warning arrives as warmth** *[A + CORR + D]*
- **1st.** The monthly means span only −2.0 to −16.4 °C and sit within 1.0 °C from May to September; the felt range inside one day is far wider, because wind chill during katabatic events falls "far below ambient".
- **2nd.** So felt cold is distributed by exposure and hour, not by month: the windward face, the flank and the open versus the lee and the interior. `RES` §1 adds that onset warms the surface air, so **the first felt sign of the strong wind is a rise in temperature** — the mild moment that precedes the storm.
- **3rd.** A body (and a garment choice) keyed to the thermometer therefore misreads exactly the moment that matters, as relief [D]. This is the sensory mechanism under the Spine's "Trusting an ordinary signal" and under the blame pattern `P2` P2-1 derives ("spoken only as blame"): the person hurt was handed a comfortable cue. For the robot body the files give the costs of cold but not the feel of it; whether a robot registers the warm onset as a feeling or only as an instrument reading is the open question at `RP` L59: "whether robots feel cold as discomfort or only register it instrumentally".
- **Lit weeks.** December and January have average highs of +0.4 and +1.1 °C and "brief rain possible"; `RES` §4 adds rime icing when warm wet snow freezes. The feel there is wet and near zero, not dry cold [CORR].
- **Hands.** `RP` L55: "Heavier gloves cost fine motor control and tactile feedback" — a gradient, "never a gate" (`RP` L57). So fine outdoor hand-work is a lit-weeks activity and coarse hand-work a winter one, for both bodies [D].

**Robot body.**

**P3-C5 · For the robot half, cold is a resting problem and a sleeping problem** *[A + D]*
- **Given** (`RP` L13–16): "recharging below freezing causes permanent capacity loss"; "capacity falls to 50–60% by −20 °C"; "a robot is safer MOVING" / "than resting, because working generates the heat that keeps its cells and joints in range." (L15–16). `RP` L334: "they require downtime, most prominently OVERNIGHT RECHARGING". `RP` L40: "a robot can (and nearly almost always does) wear clothing, both for fashionable" and functional purposes: coats and gloves are the answer to cold (L38–57).
- **1st.** Mirny's average night lows are −19.6 (Jul), −20.2 (Aug) and −19.3 (Sep) (`SPEC` L113–L115), within 0.7 °C of the `RP` figure's −20 °C, and every monthly mean is below freezing. These are ambient temperatures, not cell temperatures; a clothed, moving robot's cells are warmer, and how much is not stated.
- **2nd.** Because recharging below freezing is permanent damage and the city's nights are below freezing, **every robot's overnight recharge must happen inside a thermal envelope held above freezing, essentially every night of the year.** For the robot half (685,529 at Census I) the interior is not comfort; it is capacity.

## F. Retroactive Mechanism *(`SPN3` L519–520: what is established as simply true here that nothing explains, and what physical system would produce exactly that — it generates a second-order consequence for free)*

**P3-C10 · RM-1 — the floor is moving on an ordinary day** *[A + CORR + D]*
- **Established as simply true.** `SPEC` L82 chars 166–422: "mean wind 12.0 m/s"; "up to a third of snowfall evaporates before it lands, and most of the remainder is entrained and carried past"; "Whiteout here routinely occurs under a clear sky". `SPEC` L76–L79: ~527 mm falls, ~200 mm lands, ~327 mm is lost. Nothing explains why the surface should always have snow to lift.
- **The physical system.** A persistent drainage wind over a snow-covered coast whose mean sits above the speed at which snow starts to move: `RES` §1, "Blowing snow starts at roughly 5–10 m/s; Mirny's mean is 11–12 m/s." (the admitted spec says 12.0).
- **Second-order consequence, for free.** Snow in motion is not a storm condition at Mirny; **it is the ordinary one.** `RES` §1: "moving snow stays below the knee on an ordinary day and climbs into the face as the wind rises" (88.5 % below 0.5 m at 10 m/s; 33.8 % at 30 m/s). The ordinary-day ground is a moving sheet at ankle-to-knee height, with the eyes above it; visibility is lost from the ground up as the wind rises.
- **Third order.** (a) Loss of sight depends on eye height and wind: the same wind is a different blindness for a child, an adult and a differently built robot body (build height varies, `RP` L424: "Body type is not fixed per locality, but statistically weighted by it") [D]. (b) The ordinary clear-sky whiteout is therefore a thickening of an everyday condition, not a separate event — which is why the spec can say it "routinely" occurs. (c) The mass goes somewhere: at least about 151 mm water-equivalent a year is carried past rather than evaporated (327.1 lost minus an evaporation ceiling of 175.7, own arithmetic), and `RES` §3 says the katabatic dies 8–15 km offshore, where blowing snow would be dropped — a deposition belt at the outer ice [D, weak: it assumes a point measurement holds across the footprint and that the sea surface keeps the snow; reserved as RF-C1].
- **Hook:** the surface can be read — "Drift ridges record the wind's direction" (`RES` §7) — and nobody is the reader (`RES` §7: "its reader does not" exist).

**P3-C11 · RM-2 — the ring is a drift system** *[A + CORR + D, conditional on a solid ring]*
- **Established as simply true.** `SPEC` L174 chars 272–623: "the sheltered industrial core inside the city's outer wind-fortified ring". The ring's form, height, porosity and date are nowhere given.
- **The physical system.** A windbreak that makes shelter by collecting snow: a solid obstacle fills on its windward side and in its lee, to about 12–15 times its height, and what is cleared refills (`RES` §1).
- **Second-order consequence, for free.** The sheltered core exists only so long as the ring's banks are managed or the ring is shaped so they do not bury it; **the ring has keepers, and its outer face is a continuously regenerating bank.** The spine already counts "the ring's upkeep" as a standing cost (`SPN` L67).
- **Third order.** An approach to the city meets a snow bank, not a wall; the lee margin is quiet and sheltered and also where drift lands (P3-C1). **Stated flatly as conditional:** if the ring is porous or shaped to pass snow, none of this holds; R-C3 asks for its form.
