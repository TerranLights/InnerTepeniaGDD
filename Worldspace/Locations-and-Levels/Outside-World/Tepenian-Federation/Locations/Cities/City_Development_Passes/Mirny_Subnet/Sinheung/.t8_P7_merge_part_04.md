### {{ID:N:year_stock}} A year-scale stock of the inputs with no substitute (`seed`)

- **Disposition:** confirmed by all three (A, B, C), as a seed; not adopted.
- **The chain.**
  - The sea stream arrives in the one window (`Ports.md` L575), and real resupply at this site is once a year (A, C).
  - A missed berth (Phase 5 L57) means a year without these inputs, so the works, or the cluster, must hold at least a year's stock (A, B, C).
  - The hub end is visited several times a year (A, B, C; the months are unconfirmed), so material plausibly accumulates at the hub between Antarctic seasons: the hub works as a year-round store and the fjord as a once-a-year door (A; adopted by B).
  - A stored, guarded reserve of small, high-value inputs becomes a physical, visible feature of the works: seen, counted, handled, and something a player can see, enter and handle (B, C).
  - Whoever sets the berth order then affects the stock levels of all three governments' fabrication (B). Whatever the queue lands in a season is the cluster's stock for the next twelve months, so the queue's order is also a stock decision (A).
  - Who holds the stock is not stated and is left open (C; no forced fit).
- **Real-world comparable** (B): a large industrial economy with no domestic manganese mining keeps a national stockpile of manganese ore and ferromanganese (USGS yearbooks). It supports the seed without specifying it.
- **Strength:** `seed`, conditional on era and landing.

### {{ID:N:recovery_ossuary}} Recovery, the ossuaries, and where the overseas stream sits (`seed`)

- **Disposition:** confirmed by all three (A, B, C) for the connection. The scope of C's ossuary sentence is a conflict resolved to the narrower reading.
- **Canon.** Metal is scarce and recovery is a necessary industry (robot physiology L117). Ossuary metal is "SACRED AND UNTOUCHABLE." (L123): "They mine their metal from quarries, rather than stealing from the dead." (L125). "The prohibition is not a scruple that scarcity erodes" (L133). The quarry and mining trades are "what makes leaving the dead alone affordable." (L136). "Metal bone persists." (L96).
- **The seed (union).**
  - Every scrap of alloy steel carries forward the imported alloying content it was made with. So in a 45% fabrication city, offcuts and rejected work hold part of the imported input, and "The informal secondary market in rejected material" (Spine L53, "distinct, less prestigious.") is also where imported alloy content is recovered (A).
  - In the real world, copper builds up in recycled steel, and "dilution with virgin iron or cleaner scrap is the only commercially practiced solution" (B). So the domestic road stream of virgin metal keeps recovered metal usable, and the overseas stream replaces alloy content lost to dilution and to the ossuaries (B).
  - For the metals the quarries cannot supply, the overseas channel stands beside the quarry trades in making it affordable to leave the dead alone (A, B, C).
  - **Narrower reading on scope.** Metal laid in an ossuary is withdrawn permanently. C's "every robot body withdraws metal permanently" is not carried: canon says "One of the post-death possibilities is inclusion in an ossuary." (L77), one possibility among several, and "The mortuary question remains DEFERRED; this note only states its shape." (L170) (A, B).
  - This bears on the fabrication sector generally, not on the chambers specifically (C).
  - It is a national connection with a local instance, in a city of the robot-majority nation (robots are 51.27% of Census I) that makes the chamber apparatus. Flagged only (B).
- **Not adopted.**

### {{ID:N:route_geometry}} The route's geometry from the fjord head (relation; measurement)

- **Disposition:** confirmed by all three for the distances. The consequence about the nearer hub was found by one (B) and confirmed by A and C; the haul-length figures were found by one (A).
- **Distances and bearings.** From the fjord head (69°22′58″S 76°22′44″E, Phase 5 L31): Heard Island is about 1,826 km away at Atlas Cove (A and C; B's rounded island point gives 1,828 km), on an initial bearing of about 354°; Port-aux-Français on Kerguelen is about 2,252 km away, on about 348° (B). All three readers recomputed these in Round 3, and they agree; the Heard spread is a choice of point, not an error. Heard to Kerguelen is about 466 km, against 440 to 463 km published (B's sanity check). Mount Ruker is about 632 km away (A, C).
- **Alignment.** This answers the hub file's question "Whether the longitudinal alignment is as tight as it appears" (L95) for this coast: both hubs lie nearly due north (A, B, C).
- **Consequence** (B; `seed`). From this coast the nearer hub is the one that is one supplier's own territory; the genuinely neutral door lies about 424 km farther on (about 426 km on anchorage coordinates, A). Proximity points to the door that is not neutral.
- **Haul length** (A). On great-circle legs that ignore ice, land and real sailing routes: fjord to Fremantle direct is about 4,818 km, via Heard about 5,917 km, via Kerguelen about 6,462 km; fjord to Durban direct is about 5,237 km, via Kerguelen about 6,182 km, via Heard about 6,099 km. Routing through the hubs lengthens the haul by about 16 to 34%. The neutral route is chosen for politics, not geometry (hub file L30). C could not recompute these, because the mainland-port coordinates were not among C's sources.
- **Strength:** `firm` as measurement (A, B); `seed` for the consequence.

### {{ID:N:ice_and_hulls}} The ice on the route, and where hulls might change (`seed`)

- **Disposition:** found by two for the ice research (B, C; A's management-plan lines agree). The hull-change seed was found by one (B) and confirmed by A and C.
- **Real world.** Ships approaching the Larsemann Hills in late spring cross 500 to 1,000 km of pack ice in northern Prydz Bay, whose outer edge lies near 63 to 64°S in late November, and then 40 to 60 km of fast ice 1.0 to 1.5 m thick. Near the coast the fast ice "entirely breaks up and disappears for a couple of weeks during summer" (B). Icebreakers arrived on 25 November 2025, and unloading took place 12 to 44 km from shore over fast ice (C).
- **What canon already answers.** The last kilometers: the port is constructed and re-cut each year (`Ports.md` L598-600; Phase 5 L34). Not the pack (B).
- **Seed** (B). The hub may be where open-ocean hulls hand cargo to ice-capable hulls: a physical reason for transshipment beside the political one (hub file L29-30). Whose hulls and crews is open ({{REF:Q:hulls_crews}}).
- **Hub ports, real world.** Port-aux-Français has a shallow seaport where supply ships unload by barge to a small quay; it is served four times a year from Réunion and has no air service. Heard Island has no port; ships stay offshore, and Atlas Cove is the only usually protected anchorage (A, B, C). Canon already suspects that a hub may have to be built, since Heard Island "is a volcanic, glaciated, uninhabited reserve" (hub file L98). These facts belong in the national file ({{REF:R:hub_ports}}).

### {{ID:N:cold_nickel}} The cold sets its own metallurgical requirement (`seed`)

- **Disposition:** found by one (C); confirmed by A and B.
- **The seed.** In the real world, steel for low-temperature service relies chiefly on nickel to lower the temperature at which it turns brittle: 3.5% nickel steel serves to about −100 °C, and 9% nickel steel lower (a 1979 ASTM paper; industry service limits). A fabrication economy whose structures stand in the cold therefore has a cold-specific claim on an imported input. This is **not** the Standard's freeze-thaw failure ("Freeze-thaw failure is DEFERRED and DISPLACED", Phase 7 L210) and must not be merged with it (C).
