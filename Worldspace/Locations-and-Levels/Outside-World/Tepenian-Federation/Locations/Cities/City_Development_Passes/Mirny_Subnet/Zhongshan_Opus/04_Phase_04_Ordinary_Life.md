# 4.2 · THE SHORT LIST — what is true of *every* ordinary day here

> **Band variance, `03` Phase 4:** *"Band 5–6: there is no single ordinary day either… ask instead what is
> common to ALL the ordinary days across the distribution, which is usually a very short list and is exactly
> the interesting part."* **This city spans the `4↔5` threshold (`00_Frame.md` §0.1c), so this is the correct
> instrument.** Four items. All four are physical, and none of them is anybody's job.

## 1 · The night is metered, and the meter is the currency

**Two facts collide, and neither was written with the other in mind:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Robots must recharge overnight** — a physiologically real requirement, not a convention (`Robot_Physiology…`) | And **charging a cold cell below freezing causes lithium plating — permanent, irreversible capacity loss** (`Robot_Cold_Physiology.md` §1) |
| **This city's monthly mean is below freezing in eleven months of twelve** — only January (`+0.2 °C`) is above, and December (`−0.5 °C`) misses by half a degree | ⭐ **Therefore warmed charging space is not a convenience here. It is the ordinary condition of every night, for eleven months a year** |

**And the third fact makes it legible:** in-frame, the currency *is* energy — *"a unit redeemable for a fixed
amount of guaranteed grid capacity"* (`National_Economy_and_Currency.md`). **So the warmth a robot needs in
order to charge without damaging herself is denominated in the same unit as her wages.**

> ### ⭐⭐ THE ORDINARY NIGHT HAS A PRICE, AND EVERYONE CAN READ IT IN THE UNIT THEY ARE PAID IN.
> **This is not a robot fact.** The dual economy runs both halves off the same regulated grid — *"human
> heating/food/water systems and robot siligel production/component maintenance both ultimately traced back to
> the same regulated grid."* **A human household is heated on the same meter, in the same unit, for the same
> eleven months.** ⭐ ***It is the one recurring cost that every single resident of both kinds pays, nightly,
> in the same denomination.***

## 2 · The cheap hours are the dark, loud ones — and everyone is already in them

**From Phase 3, sourced and already closed:** the katabatic wind *"usually begins in the evening, reaches its
maximal speed at mid-night, and then decreases before the next noon"* — and the same class of wind supplies
**up to 95% of a comparable station's power on a given day, over 70% annually** (`03_Research.md` §3.8).

```
WIND PEAKS      → midnight
GENERATION PEAKS → midnight
PRICE TROUGHS   → midnight          (the currency is grid capacity)
THE CITY IS ALREADY AT REST         → midnight   (the shared night)
```

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ **THE CHEAPEST HOUR OF THE DAY AND THE HOUR THE WHOLE CITY IS PLUGGED IN ARE THE SAME HOUR, AND NOBODY ARRANGED THAT.**
> **It falls out of the wind's own diurnal cycle meeting an energy-denominated currency.** *Rest is
> cheap here for a reason that has nothing to do with anyone deciding rest should be cheap.*

⚠ **And the inverse is the constraint that actually bites:** **midday is when power is dearest** — the wind has
died, generation has fallen, and that is precisely when the mandated technical work is running. **The city's
obligations run at the expensive hours and its rest runs at the cheap ones.**

## 3 · The cold is a cost, never a gate — and this city is where that is *measurable*

⛔ **`M-151`'s trap is live here and must be walked around deliberately.** The general physiology file was read
first, per the rule: robots are ordinarily clothed, and *"a robot can realistically remain standing still in
most of the country. She just needs to be prepared for the weather, just like humans do."* **The cold file is
the narrow companion, and its dramatic mechanics are calibrated to Frostlands ambient (`−52.7 °C` mean).**
**Measured against this city's own numbers, most of them do not fire:**

| Cold-file threshold | This city's figure | Fires? |
|---|---|---|
| **Lubricant failure below `~−40 °C`** — greases *"exhibit properties similar to a solid, with catastrophic results"* | Coldest monthly mean `−16.1 °C`; **record low `−33.8 °C`** | ⛔ **NEVER.** The record extreme is `6 °C` short of the threshold. **No resident of this city has experienced seizure-by-lubricant, ever** |
| **Embrittlement / DBTT** — a knock that dents at `−10 °C` cracks at `−50 °C` | Same range | ⛔ **Effectively never at civic ambient** |
| **The death spiral** — charge falls too far to self-warm; *"you cannot recover on your own. At all."* | A mild coastal oasis with a January mean above freezing | ⛔ **A plateau phenomenon.** Not a hazard of ordinary life here |
| **Capacity reduction — `−20–30%` near freezing, `50–60%` at `−20 °C`** | Winter means `−16.1 °C` | ✅ **FIRES, and is the real one.** Every winter, everyone's usable reserve is meaningfully smaller, and it comes back in summer |
| **Plating from cold charging** | Below freezing 11 months/yr | ✅ **FIRES — and is why item 1 above is infrastructure** |

> ### ⭐⭐ **SO THE ORDINARY EXPERIENCE OF COLD HERE IS A SMALLER TANK AND A DEARER NIGHT — NOT DANGER.**
> **A resident of this city has a lifelong, intimate, entirely undramatic relationship with the cold: it makes
> everything slightly more expensive and slightly less capable for two-thirds of the year, and it has never
> once threatened them.** ⚠ ***That is a genuinely different relationship to the same continent than the
> plateau has***, and it is arrived at by arithmetic against published thresholds rather than by assertion.

## 4 · The salt never stops, and neither does anything else

`03_Research.md` §3.3: salt is not washed off, for want of rain; crystallization stress runs `10–20 MPa`.
**Every surface, seal, bearing and lens is on a cleaning clock** — and the water to do the cleaning is the same
scarce water the city drinks. **The fourth universal: something in every resident's immediate environment is
always visibly due for maintenance, and everyone has an opinion about whose job it is.**

# 4.3 · THE PATTERN OF VARIATION — four ordinary days, not one

> ⛔ *"Do not write one universal resident. Different arrival-mode populations have different days."*

## The structural split that matters most is not origin — it is dependants

**`09` §5's workforce ruling, which is a demographic fact before it is an economic one:** *"Every robot is a
worker — no childhood, no retirement, no dependants. Half of humans are workers — the rest being children,
elderly and non-participants."*

> ### ⭐⭐ **THE ROBOT HALF OF THIS CITY CONTAINS NO CHILDREN AND NO ELDERS. THE HUMAN HALF IS ROUGHLY HALF CHILDREN, ELDERS AND NON-PARTICIPANTS.**
> **Two populations of near-identical size** *(Census II: `52.41%` human / `47.59%` robot)* **living in one
> city, one of which has a full age pyramid and one of which has none at all.**

**What that does to an ordinary day, concretely:**

| | **A human day** | **A robot day** |
|---|---|---|
| **Who else is in the home** | Frequently a dependant — a child to deliver somewhere, an elder whose heating bill is not optional | ⭐ **Nobody who is not also a worker.** Households are peer households |
| **What the day is bounded by** | A schooling and childcare calendar (`C1a` 26, `C2` 5 per 1,000 humans) | The charge cycle, and nothing else structural |
| **What a career is** | An arc with an end — *"human turnover is MORTALITY"* | ⭐⭐ **A sequence of chosen chapters** — *"robot career turnover is VOLUNTARY… leaves the trade by choosing to become something else"* |
| **What the cold costs** | Heating, and the ordinary human cold-injury register | A smaller tank for two-thirds of the year |

⭐ **And it is not a division.** The shared night is genuinely shared — *"a city where everyone sleeps is a very
different place from one where half the population works through the dark."* **The two populations run
completely different life-shapes and stop at the same hour, on the same meter, for different reasons.**

## What the marine sector actually is, then

of a catch that is landed for protein rather than for tonnage.

⚠ **`00b`'s object-colonization rule, applied in the direction it is usually missed:** the label "marine
resource extraction ~15%" invites reading one vivid instance — boats, nets — as the whole sector. **It is not.
Widening the definition is the fix; shrinking the sector is not** (`00b`, verbatim). **And the correction runs
*with* the vision note rather than against it,** because the vision spoke about food and the sector label

# 4.5 · THE FOUR REQUIRED ELEMENTS

## A · ROUTINES

**The day is bracketed by the meter, not by a shift or by the sun.** Work — mandated technical, baseline
services, the FREE tier alike — runs through the expensive, calm, bright-or-dark middle of the day. **Rest,
recharging, and anything that draws heavily run into the cheap, loud, windy night.** The light cycle is
extreme (`49` days of polar night, `62` of midnight sun) **and is conspicuously not what organizes the day** —
because the wind, and therefore the price, runs on a diurnal clock that does not care whether the sun is up.

> ⭐ **A resident's sense of "late" is not darkness and is not lateness. It is the wind coming up.**

## B · MUNDANE CONCERNS

- ⭐⭐ **The cheap window is contested, and the contest is completely unglamorous.** The staple tier is
  *"a POWER load before it is a labor pool"* — stacked growing is lit and heated growing, and this city is a
  **GROWER with a food term of 100%**, so it lights its own. **The growing lights and the residential charge
  load want the same cheap midnight hours.** Nobody forbids anything; the meter simply prices it. *(This is
  `02` §3.2's "permitted but punished" shape arriving as a utility bill, and it is where a shadow would live if
  one lives anywhere in this phase — flagged for `00d` treatment at Phase 7, not resolved here.)*
- **Whether the seals on your own place got cleaned before the salt got into them.**
- **The winter tank.** For half the year everyone's reserve is smaller and everyone plans around it slightly.
- **Water, twice.** It is the scarce resource *and* the thing salt maintenance consumes.

## C · PERSONAL STRUGGLES

- ⭐ **Being on the wrong side of the cheap window.** Work whose hours are fixed by obligation cannot move into
  the cheap hours; work that is elective can. **The `43.4%` FREE tier has a flexibility the mandated technical
  sector does not** — a small, permanent, entirely apolitical unfairness that nobody legislated.
- **For humans: a dependant's heat is not deferrable.** A household with a child or an elder cannot shift its
  load to midnight in the way a peer household can.
- ⭐ **For robots: a career with no natural end.** *Turnover is voluntary.* **Nothing ever makes you stop, which
  means nothing ever tells you when you are finished** — and in a city whose registered symbol is *"doesn't
  care to be fully known"* that is a lifelong open question rather than a crisis. *(G1 is corroboration-tier
  and this is offered as an echo, not a derivation.)*

## D · ESCAPISM AND DOWNTIME — *explicitly required, and the one most often cut*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE CALM HOURS AND THE EXPENSIVE HOURS ARE THE SAME HOURS — SO THIS CITY GOES OUTSIDE IN THE MIDDLE OF THE DAY.**
> The katabatic *"decreases before the next noon."* Power is dear. **Both facts push leisure toward the
> low-draw, out-of-doors, midday register**, in a city whose January mean is above freezing and whose ground
> is real rock. *A physical, seen, enterable consequence — not an abstraction.*

- **Glitch-coolant, and canon is specific about the register:** this is a **bohemian/cosmopolitan** city — *"a
  wide variety of glitch-coolant types… variety and refinement over raw intensity."* **Many options, none of
  them strong.** ⭐ *Which fits a population whose evenings are cheap and whose mornings are not: an
  unhurried, discriminating drinking culture rather than a decompressive one.*
- **The contemplative classical tradition already exists in canon** (`Specs/Zhongshan.md` L172, handed forward
  from Phase 2) — **formalize, do not invent.** Phase 8 owns its content.
- **Leisure here is not rest, and canon says why:** *"On Upper Earth… a robot's leisure time was afforded to
  them by their loving romantic partners… In Tepenia… it is a FACT OF LIFE. Owed to nobody."* **It is the
  material proof that leaving worked.**
