# 3.2 · THE FOUR SENSORY SUB-FIELDS, ANSWERED SEPARATELY

## SOUND

**The defining acoustic fact is not seasonal — it is diurnal, and it runs opposite to a human sleep clock.**
The katabatic wind here runs all year, but its intensity has a real, measured daily cycle: it *"usually begins
in the evening, reaches its maximal speed at mid-night, and then decreases before the next noon."* Peak wind
speed sits `15–21 m/s` at a height of `100–150 m` above the ground, and a `200–300 m` thick, `4–6 °C` temperature
inversion always accompanies it.

**A city that is loudest exactly when its residents are trying to sleep, and near-silent by the time most of
them are at work.** Not a hardship to be solved — a fact of the acoustic day, load-bearing enough that a
resident's whole sense of "quiet hours" runs backward from anywhere without this wind. **Under Robots do not
require sleep the way humans do, but per `00_Frame.md` §0.6b, robots and humans share a night** (`Robot_Physiology_and_Cultural_Practices.md`: recharging is an overnight, physiologically real requirement) —
so the loudest hour of the day is also, structurally, the hour almost the entire city is at rest indoors.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ TWO WINDS, NOT ONE — CORRECTED 2026-09-10, developer-directed research

> **`Cities/Research_Logs/Zhongshan_Research_Log.md` Session 4.** Developer ruling, verbatim: *"Zhongshan's own
> station-specific numerical study (Haiyang Xuebao, 2016) can be considered as 'Primary climate data'… and the
> previously-found region-wide ASMA management plan… can be considered as 'Secondary climate data'… the way
> the wind occasionally blows."*

| Tier | Direction | Character | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PRIMARY** | **Southeast** | The constant, ground-hugging katabatic drainage wind — present every day of the year, the diurnal source above | The city's ordinary acoustic baseline — the midnight peak described above |
| **SECONDARY** | **North-east** | Occasional, cyclonic-storm-driven. Over 80% of blowing-snow EVENTS are this wind; under 20% trace to the katabatic flow itself, despite the katabatic wind being the constant one | A different, rarer sound — a storm arriving, distinct from the nightly baseline, and the one that actually drives the small-boat-landing hazard (`03_Research.md` §3.4) and most blowing-snow episodes |

**The finding this produces: the city's ordinary night is one wind, and its occasional bad weather is a
different one, from a different direction, and residents would learn to tell them apart by ear before a
weather report ever confirmed it** — the nightly SE hum is background; an NE gust arriving off-cycle is the
signal something is different today.

## SMELL

**Two independent sources, both geographic, both asymmetric across the city.** First: the oasis's ~150 lakes
draw their chemistry from *"sea spray, local geology, and periodic seawater surges from calving of the Dålk
glacier"* — a real, dated event exists for this: 30 January 2017, when a supraglacial layer drained into an
englacial lake and produced an outburst that carved a depression `183 × 220 m`. **This is not ambient — it is
periodic and locatable**, concentrated at the oasis's eastern corner where Dålk sits. Second: the wind itself is
not clean — on exposed rock, what moves past is *"dust and grit as well as snow"* (`Specs/Zhongshan.md` L92),
so the everyday smell profile carries mineral dust the way a coastal city carries salt.

**The finding: this city smells different at its two geographic ends**, and the eastern difference is
occasional and violent (a glacial event) rather than constant — a smell that arrives on a schedule nobody
controls, tied to a real, nameable mechanism rather than to weather in general.

## FEEL

**Insulation-vs-dexterity is the standing texture of every outdoor task** (`00_Frame.md` §0.6b) — a gradient,
never a gate, and this city's own values (annual `−9.9 °C`, January mean `+0.2 °C`, persistent wind) put outdoor
fine work somewhere specific on it: workable, but never comfortable, and never year-round in the same register.
**And salt is a tactile fact as much as a chemical one.** Crystallization stress inside exposed material runs
`10–20 MPa`, with hydration expansion up to `300%` — a surface that is not merely cold to the touch but
*actively working* against whatever holds it together, continuously, because there is almost no rain to wash
the salt back off.

## FIRST IMPRESSIONS

**Elevation is inverted here, and nothing in the place announces it.** The wind's own speed maximum sits at
`100–150 m` above the ground — and the oasis's highest point, Blundell Peak, is `162 m`. **The high ground is
inside the wind maximum.** A newcomer's instinct — climb for a view, for shelter, for status — runs directly
into the one place elevation does not pay off physically. This is a derivation from two independently-sourced
facts (the wind's vertical profile and the terrain's own maximum elevation), not a canon statement, and is
flagged as such; nothing below is built on it as though it were confirmed.

**And the color of the ground itself first-impressions differently than most of Tepenia**, per Phase 1's own
finding: `8–11%` of this city stands on exposed rock — real stone, real color, real permanence — against `89–92%`
ice. A visitor's very first read of "which ground is old" is legible on sight, before anyone explains the class
divide `02_Spine.md` §2.6 already established runs underneath it.

# 3.3 · PUBLIC / INSTITUTIONAL SPLIT

**The road already solved the problem the street plan has to solve, and it is a genuine institutional answer
sitting inside an otherwise chaotic terrain.** The real site's own access road — 6.7 km, linking the stations
to the plateau — *"closely follows the most appropriate route with regard to avoiding lake catchments and
steep slopes."* That is a deliberately engineered, catchment-aware route threading terrain that elsewhere is
described as dissected, fjorded, and irregular. **So the institutional register (the road, and by extension any
formally-planned corridor) reads as ordered and legible; the ambient register (everywhere the road does not
reach) reads as the terrain's own chaos** — which is precisely the public/institutional split this sub-step
asks for, and it maps directly onto the vision note's own "chaotic but semi-gridlike" tension.

# 3.4 · THE SEAM, AND THE RESOLUTION OF THE VISION NOTE'S OWN CENTRAL TENSION

**Is there a visible boundary between an earlier build and anything added under later pressure?** This city has
no in-frame defining event (`02_Spine.md` §2.1, G6 null) and Status is Living, not Declining or Ruined — so
there is no crisis-era seam of the usual kind. **But there is a different, better-grounded seam: the two
population branches of `04_Phase_02_Composition_and_Arrival.md` §2.3 carry two different construction ages.**
The continuous-habitation branch's own built environment predates 2564 by up to 481 years of accumulated,
naturally-evolving settlement; the founding wave's own construction dates from 2564 forward. **A visible seam
between older, more organically-accreted stock and a newer, more deliberately-founded layer is exactly what a
population meeting its own five-centuries-diverged branch would produce**, without needing a disaster to
explain it.

## THE RETROACTIVE MECHANISM — what explains "chaotically-organized… semi-gridlike" at once

> Process F: what is established as simply true here that nothing explains, and what physical system would
> produce exactly that?

**The terrain itself supplies the mechanism, and it is billion-year-old, not designed.** The oasis's landforms
are *"structurally-controlled, steep-sided fjords and valleys… **rarely exceeding 100 m in depth**"* whose
*"elongated form… results from **compositional layering, folds and faults (lineaments) in the metamorphic
bedrock**"* — bedrock that dates to the Rayner orogeny, `990–900 Ma`, with later reworking events at `~913 Ma`
and `~552–555 Ma`. **A fold-and-fault pattern laid down nearly a billion years ago produces long, straight,
parallel-ish valley segments in a consistent local orientation — genuinely grid-*like* over any short stretch —
while the oasis as a whole is cut by multiple such lineament sets at different angles across its several
peninsulas (Stornes, Broknes split east/west by Nella Fjord, plus four minor peninsulas and ~130 near-shore
islands), so no single grid direction holds across the whole site.**

> ### THE STREETS DID NOT CHOOSE TO BE SEMI-GRIDLIKE. THE BEDROCK DID, LOCALLY, AND REFUSED TO AGREE WITH ITSELF GLOBALLY.
> A street plan built to follow the ground it sits on — the cheapest, most physically sensible way to lay a
> road across dissected, fault-controlled terrain — inherits the bedrock's own structure exactly: locally
> regular, because one fault set runs one direction for a stretch; globally chaotic, because the next stretch
> answers to a different, older deformation event. **This is a second-order consequence generated for free,
> per Process F's own promise, and it resolves the vision note's tension without inventing anything** — the
> city is not simultaneously chaotic and ordered by design choice; it is legible at the scale of a fault
> segment and illegible at the scale of the whole oasis, because that is the literal geometry of the ground it
> is built on.

**And the road (§3.3) is the confirming case, not a coincidence:** the one route that was engineered rather
than inherited from block-by-block construction is also the one route the log's own sources describe as
deliberately routed *around* the catchments and slopes the fault pattern produces — meaning even the
"planned" layer had to negotiate the same billion-year-old grain the "organic" layer simply follows.

# 3.5 · SEASONAL VARIANCE — what this place becomes at its worst point in the year

**Polar night, ~49 days (May 29 → July 16); midnight sun, ~62 days (Nov 22 → Jan 22).** Precipitation peaks in
June — mid-polar-night — and bottoms in January (`04_Phase_02` handoff): **the wettest month is the darkest
one.** The city's snow load and its total darkness arrive together, not staggered, which is the opposite of a
site where residents get a dry cold season before the snow comes.

**And the wind supplies a second, independent seasonal fact: it never actually stops for winter.** Query 43's
own finding closed a scope-hole (`00_RUNBOOK.md` `M-158`) that the ASMA's own text only answered for summer:
Zhongshan *"is strongly influenced by the katabatic wind all the year round."* **So there is no season here
that is simply calm** — the wind's presence is constant; what varies is temperature and daylight, not the
acoustic and abrasive baseline established at §3.2.

**Ice type is undeclared** (`00_Frame.md` §0.1c, `R-11`), so which surfaces bury on a `2.7 cm/yr` plateau clock
versus which travel on a `1.2 m/yr` flowing-ice clock cannot be assigned per-district yet — flagged for a future
pass rather than guessed.
