## Mirny as Phases 1–9 found it (written after entry 27, before entry 28/29 were opened)

Mirny, as the pass has found it, is a working coast on the Antarctic Circle where a wind that is almost never calm is the defining fact, the sky has no polar night but about 29 days of midnight sun, and the thermometer holds a flat winter plateau while the light ramps — so the year is dated by light and the danger by wind, and the honest cues of that wind went unread because no one who came had been taught them. It is held to its parent by two continuous obligations — the subnet's contact routed through one relay with no second path, and the stone and machinery of its quarries and yards, one chain for the eastern roads and one for the chamber maker — while what it receives from beyond the continent arrives through a sea that is a frozen surface for most of the year and an unschedulable, wind-closable window for the rest. Its 1,351,430 people at Census I are robots and humans at near-equal odds (50.73 % robots), drawn from fourteen stocks with no majority, founded by exile under a sponsoring founding nation that sent no stock, and they keep one shared stop, wear one coat, and decide each crossing alone. The compact nobody wrote — the medium holds; the reading is yours — books every loss to the person, the weather or silence and never to the record or the relay, so the book never learns, the restorer goes out alone, and no arbiter stands between the claims on the same quarry. The pass found no faith sited, no organized dissent, no rank by formation, a culture whose own making is mostly upkeep, a vocabulary of the present tense with no word for "soon", and one working distinction — formed here, or arrived — that crosses both bodies, carries no standing, and in Act 2 folds its remembering side into the founding robots. What it does not yet have, in any admitted file, is a named place: its forms (the ring, the record, the harbor, the relay, the circle marker, the chamber) are referents of unknown shape, and its interiors, layout, music, food and leisure content are empty slots by ruling or by absence.

## 4 · The axis

# **What stays, what refills.**

**What it answers that no earlier Mirny phase answered.** `P3` asked what the place feels like; `P8` what it makes for itself; `P9` which kinds of people it distinguishes. **Phase 10 asks what is actually *there* — and the files sort it by one property: whether a thing outlasts the next storm.** The landmarks that stay — the ring, the core, the harbor, the relay hardware, the quarries, the inherited station and its record — are, without exception, things **nobody living at Mirny made** or things **made for someone else**; everything residents make for themselves — a cleared approach, a drift moved, a path reopened — is remade after every storm and leaves nothing to point at (`P8` P8-11: *"Mirny's own making for itself is upkeep, and upkeep leaves nothing to show"*). Names, where they ever come, can only attach to the first class: *"banks refill and move, while the ring, the core, the harbor, the relay and the quarries do not"* (`P3` L421).

**Peer-free test — "could this be true anywhere?"** *Half of it could:* every snowy place has drifts that refill. What passes is the pairing at Mirny's own values: **its lasting landmarks are a medium no one here authored and works whose product leaves, while its residents' own making is the kind that refills.** Delete every other place's name from *a city whose standing landmarks are a record and a relay none of its people wrote or built and quarries whose stone leaves, while everything its people make for themselves is undone by the next storm* — the claim survives; it was never relational. **Not checked against any sibling** (terminal check).
**The column it would fill** (write-only, `SPN3` L146–147): Mirny's own column of the differentiation instrument, the Catalog row — *What stays, what refills.* No other location's row was read.
