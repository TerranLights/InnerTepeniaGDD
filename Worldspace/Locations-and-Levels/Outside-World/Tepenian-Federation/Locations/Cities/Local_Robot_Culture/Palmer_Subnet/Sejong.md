## PART I — Local Aspects

*Describing the living, pre-war culture, per this file's own confirmed present-tense methodology.*

### 1. Kinship, Clan & Mentor/Mentee Structures — Local Expression

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** Leading with the actual shared *life* experience, not a single incident:
  Course of Events #9 ("Everyone's Distinct, Nobody's Alone") establishes that Sejong's own founding logic
  never organized itself around one dominant culture assimilating everyone else — each of the island's nearly
  dozen absorbed communities retains real, visible distinctness while still participating fully in shared civic
  life, an ongoing, daily, deliberately maintained balance rather than a settled founding-era fact. Every
  Sejong robot shares this identical, everyday condition: living inside a city whose whole civic character is
  the *continuous, effortful practice* of holding many distinct identities in one shared space without letting
  any of them dissolve into the others. See Cross-Reference Synthesis for why this is a genuinely different
  kind of finding from every prior city's own Kinship floor.
- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** A second, distinct shape the same underlying condition takes: the Negotiation
  Hall's own founding-era work (Coordinator "Han Ji-woo" *(placeholder, not developer-chosen)*, per Course of
  Events #6) formalized the boundary-zone agreements that make coexistence physically possible — but per the
  Kinship framing's own "ongoing condition, not a single incident" discipline, the founding negotiation itself
  is municipal history, not the mechanism. The real, present-tense shape is the *continuous* renegotiation
  Course of Events #3 describes — boundary-zone maintenance as "one of Sejong's own defining, ongoing civic
  skills... refined constantly rather than resolved once." Robots working this ongoing negotiation function
  share a genuine, specific professional kinship with each other across whichever boundary they happen to work.

### 2. Cooperation, Morality & Emergent Social Convention — Local Expression

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** Course of Events #11 ("Not About the Ground") supplies a genuinely mature,
  honestly-unresolved civic ethic worth surfacing directly: a long-held, quiet grievance between two
  neighboring communities over whether a founding-era boundary agreement was fair in *outcome*, not just in
  *process*, eventually gets acknowledged out loud without being reopened or "fixed" — "fair process and fair
  outcome were never quite the same thing here, an admission accepted as real without pretending it fixes
  anything." This is Sejong's own real standard for cooperation: naming an asymmetry honestly is treated as
  sufficient moral work in itself, distinct from actually correcting it.
- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** A genuinely distinct, Sejong-specific robot angle on the same material,
  grounded directly in the Human-Robot Relations Baseline and in robots' own established lack of national-origin
  stake: in a city built entirely around a dozen communities each protecting their own boundary-zone fairness,
  a robot is structurally the one plausible neutral party in a dispute like the one #11 describes — she has no
  founding-nation "side" of her own the way every human resident inevitably does. This isn't proposed as a
  formal role anywhere in the gathered material, but it's a direct, well-grounded local application of a fact
  true of robots everywhere in Tepenia, made genuinely load-bearing specifically by Sejong's own particular
  civic structure in a way it isn't anywhere else in this project.

### 3. Locality as Identity — What Makes This City's Robots Recognizable

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** §14's own material stands as the honest local answer, more fully recognized
  here than invented: Sejong "never felt isolated the way more remote Tepenian cities did... the presence of so
  many neighbors was always part of the experience." A Sejong robot is recognizable not by any single fixed
  practice, aesthetic, or register — every other city's own Locality marker in this project has been some
  version of a stable thing — but by a genuinely *relational* identity, legible only in reference to whichever
  specific neighbor happens to be adjacent at a given moment. This is a structurally different kind of marker
  from anywhere else examined so far: not a stable trait to point to, but a constant, fluent act of contrastive
  positioning.
