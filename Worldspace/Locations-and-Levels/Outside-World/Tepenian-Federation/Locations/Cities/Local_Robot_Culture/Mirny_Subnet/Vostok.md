## PART I — Local Aspects

### 1. Kinship, Clan & Mentor/Mentee Structures — Local Expression

- **[Adapted, Deep]** The Vostok research archive is explicitly established as too vast for any single mind,
  human or robot, to hold in full (§29). Charlene's own confirmed breakthrough came not from new fieldwork but
  from re-reading and cross-referencing generations of already-catalogued data others had filed and moved
  past — a specific, named demonstration of how understanding actually accumulates here. A plausible robot
  kinship structure follows directly: a robot's "family," in the Ch.11 sense, is whoever shares her specific
  archive domain with her — past researchers whose work she's inherited responsibility for, and whoever
  eventually inherits it from her. Genuinely different in mechanism from every prior city's kinship finding:
  not craft-apprenticeship, not collective-credit, not housing-based, but domain-inheritance across
  researchers who may never personally overlap.
- **Re-checked 2026-08-10 against the revised shared-experience-first Kinship framing — no change needed.**
  Vostok's Kinship finding was never mentor/mentee-shaped: it's an ongoing, cumulative shared stewardship of
  the same archive domain across researchers who may never share a single moment of overlap — the most
  purely non-incident-based, purely condition-based kinship finding in the entire run so far. The honest
  outcome here is confirmation, not revision.

### 2. Cooperation, Morality & Emergent Social Convention — Local Expression

- **[Adapted, Deep]** §6d's already-established Window Courtesy custom (checking whether someone is in their
  active task-window before approaching; "leave a note" as genuine civic default) plausibly elevates into a
  real moral standard once traced to its root: the archive's own genesis (per the Course of Events material,
  "Too Big for One Mind") is centuries of uncoordinated, non-aligning researcher contributions that never
  quite reconciled. A Vostok robot's actual trustworthiness is plausibly measured by how clearly and
  completely she documents her own findings and state for whoever picks up the work after her — since,
  given the asynchronous task-driven schedules, she'll likely never hand off in person. This reads as a
  hard-won, city-specific lesson learned directly from the archive's own documented history of *not* doing
  this well enough the first time.

### 3. Locality as Identity — What Makes This City's Robots Recognizable

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** Two distinct markers, deliberately differentiated from Kunlun's own
  isolation-comfort finding (per the Swap Test discipline — Vostok needed its own, not an echo): first, and
  most distinctly Vostok, a robot is recognizable by which specific archive domain she's made her own
  responsibility (directly extending §1) — a genuinely unique identity marker no other city in this run has
  produced. Second, a more purely physical marker: Vostok's cold is the most extreme sustained cold in
  Tepenia, explicitly noted (Specs' own gameplay notes) as stressing "even robot fluid/material systems" —
  Vostok robots plausibly show visibly different material or fluid-management adaptations specific to
  operating at record-cold temperatures, beyond what any other city's robots need.

### 4. Generational Identity (Gen/Mark) — Local Flavor

- **No meaningful local variation found** (Honesty Check, per the Universal Triage Record above). The
  historical Vostok-Kunlun joint chamber-manufacturing outpost at Mountain Pass Airport is confirmed defunct
  (its power source died with Amundsen Tower's fall) — an interesting historical fact, but not current local
  flavor. Recorded honestly rather than stretched to fill the section.

### 5. Religion & Belief — Robot-Specific Local Practice

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** §18 already establishes Vostok as plausibly the single most functionally
  irreligious major population center in Tepenia, with one specific, deliberately narrow exception already on
  file: Vostok's genuine acoustic calm (no katabatic wind, unlike Mirny, Janbogo, or Casey) makes it "one of
  the only places in the country where genuine, sustained physical silence actually exists" — drawing a
  handful of individuals to Cymatics Reverence specifically *because* silence is rare elsewhere, explicitly
  not a citywide identity or pilgrimage site. Robot-specific extension: given robots' genuinely wider sound/
  vibration perception (Ch.15, already used three times in this run for Janbogo/Davis/Kunlun, always to find
  compositional or devotional material *in* sound) — Vostok is the inverse case. A Cymatics-sensitive robot
  drawn here isn't seeking sound to revere; she's seeking the rare, uncluttered baseline condition against
  which sound and vibration have meaning at all, closer to a devotee seeking silent retreat than one seeking
  a holy site defined by what's present.
- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** Water is already established canon for Vostok (`city-symbol-pairs.md`,
  Vostok = Pluto + Water) — grounded specifically in literal depth (Lake Vostok's 4km burial, 15–25 million
  years sealed) and accumulated wisdom (the archive itself). **Correction, 2026-08-10:** this bullet originally
  contrasted Vostok's Water against "Mirny's own temperament-based informal pick" of Water — that comparison is
  now stale, since Mirny's own Water proposal was itself withdrawn once its real pre-existing pick (Saturn +
  Earth) was found; see Mirny's own Reference Note. No cross-city Water repeat actually exists in this subnet.

### 6. Consumption & Vice Culture — Local Siligel/Coolant/Glitch-Coolant Flavor

- **[Directly-inherited, Surface]** §10c's existing note stands and is unusually specific: siligel formulation
  treated as "another modeling target," designed and logged with the same rigor as genetic sequence data,
  cross-referenced the same deliberate way the research archive itself gets cross-referenced.
- **[Adapted, Surface]** An eighth distinct Glitch-Coolant variant across this run: modeled, logged, and
  archived experimental formulations, directly extending the already-established siligel pattern into
  consumption culture more broadly — a genuine instance of the research-culture-permeates-everything
  principle already visible everywhere else at Vostok, rather than a separately-invented consumption culture.

### 7. Sexuality & Courtship — Local Customs

- **[Adapted, Deep]** Directly extends §6d's already-established Window Courtesy custom, with a real cost
  dimension that distinguishes it from Davis's collaboration-based courtship signal: since task-windows rarely
  align naturally given the asynchronous, task-driven schedules, deliberately, repeatedly synchronizing one's
  own rhythm to be present in someone else's window — when nothing about your own task flow requires it —
  plausibly functions as a genuine, legible courtship signal specifically because it costs something real
  (disrupted personal efficiency) that a same-schedule city's version of "choosing to collaborate" doesn't.

### 8. Vernacular Language — Robot-Specific Speech Markers

- **[Adapted, Surface]** §8 already provides the richest, most specifically-explained vernacular finding in
  this entire run: Russian survives purely as a "liturgical language of science" — fixed technical identifiers
  (equipment nomenclature, archive headers, drilling apparatus naming) used with total fluency in one narrow
  register by people who couldn't hold an ordinary conversation in the language, explicitly justified in-canon
  ("The Citation No One Thought to Translate") as functioning like Greek genus names — stable identifiers,
  not living vocabulary. Robot-specific angle: since robots can access the archive's own text directly rather
  than relying on generational oral transmission, robot researchers plausibly retrieve the canonical
  pronunciation/form more consistently than humans (who drift toward mnemonic approximation over
  generations) — making robot researchers the de facto standard-bearers for correct terminology use, a subtle
  but real distinction from the general population's more variable relationship to the inherited vocabulary.

### 9. Arcanet Etiquette — Local Network Behavior

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** Already confirmed directly in §24's own text: essentially zero Arcanet
  connectivity, for multiple independently-converging reasons (extreme altitude, 1,260km distance to Mirny,
  and the Planetary Split Brain all separately contributing to the same outcome). Nothing new to derive.

### 10. Surface Culture — Robot-Specific Markers

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Surface]** The visible, Surface-level manifestation of §1/§3's archive-domain
  identity: a personal "domain" marking or insignia, worn or displayed to indicate which portion of the vast
  archive a researcher (robot or human) has made their own responsibility — robots plausibly extend this into
  actual physical marking on their own frames, a genuinely Surface-level practice distinct from the deeper
  identity-marker finding it grows from.
- **No music finding proposed this pass** (Honesty Check, and a deliberate thematic choice, not a gap):
  §12 is genuinely thin in the source material, and given Vostok's established value on rare, genuine silence
  (§5, §18), the more honest finding is that Vostok simply doesn't develop much of a music culture at all —
  consistent with, not a hole in, everything else this pass found about the city.

## PART II — Cross-Reference Synthesis Findings (Step 4)

- **[Adapted, Deep]** — *Input categories combined: A (§8's liturgical Russian; §29's archive; Finding D — the
  inherited signage and Voskresenskaya's oldest archive layer are plausibly the same body of evidence viewed
  from two angles) + C (the Distributed Archive Reading Rooms) + Robot Universals Ch.11 (mentor/mentee) and
  Ch.20 (vernacular language)*
  The reason liturgical Russian survives as fixed identifiers (§8) and the reason archive-domain inheritance
  works as a kinship structure (§1) are plausibly the same underlying mechanism: both exist to preserve stable,
  unambiguous reference points across generations of researchers who never overlap in time or rhythm. A term
  that drifted in meaning, or a domain of understanding that wasn't clearly handed off, would both fail the
  same way — work becoming impossible to pick up later. This ties vernacular language, kinship, and the
  archive's own genesis into one coherent mechanism rather than three separate observations.

- **[Adapted, Deep]** — *Input categories combined: C (Finding E — the "one real crack" in Vostok's
  self-sufficiency mythology) + Historical Vignette (the personal, non-transactional Kunlun aurora-letter
  exchange — "the first time contact existed purely because two people... had something to say to each other,
  not because either needed something") + A (§17's own explicit self-differentiation from Kunlun)*
  Vostok's self-image is total, self-sufficient isolation — but its own documented history shows its deepest,
  most genuine relationship isn't a resource transaction at all. It's a personal friendship with the one other
  place that understands its specific brand of isolation (Kunlun), begun with an unprompted description of an
  aurora, wanted for its own sake rather than because either side needed something. This quietly complicates
  the "self-sufficient loner" self-image the city otherwise projects — echoing the Water element's own depth/
  wisdom register (§5): something genuine sitting underneath a harder surface presentation.

## Closing Check — The Swap Test (Step 6)

**Swap Test result: Pass, with a real asymmetry worth flagging rather than resolving.** Vostok's most obvious
comparison point is Kunlun — same subnet, extreme isolation, task-driven "not a job, it's life" ethos,
essentially off the Arcanet, Thinking-centered. But the source material itself already draws the key
distinction directly: Vostok is explicitly *not* a robots-only city (unlike Kunlun, one of only two in
Tepenia) — it houses a substantial human population running its own separate schedule alongside the robot
population, and its defining specialty is genetics, not astronomy. Every one of this pass's central findings
(archive-domain kinship, the liturgical-language mechanism, the Cymatics-as-silence-seeking inversion) depends
on facts specific to Vostok's own research culture and population mix that Kunlun doesn't share. **One genuine
asymmetry worth noting, not a contradiction:** the ongoing cold-record rivalry with Kunlun exists entirely on
Kunlun's side of the historical record — Vostok's own files never mention it. This tracks consistently with
everything else this pass found about Vostok's temperament: it holds the actual confirmed record and doesn't
need to keep proving it, while Kunlun, without the record, is the one left caring more. A quiet, well-grounded
characterization difference between the two, not a gap in the research.
