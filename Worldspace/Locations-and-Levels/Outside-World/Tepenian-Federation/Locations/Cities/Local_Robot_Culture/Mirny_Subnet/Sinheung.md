## PART I — Local Aspects

### 1. Kinship, Clan & Mentor/Mentee Structures — Local Expression

- **[Adapted, Deep]** Chamber Works apprenticeship functions as Sinheung's robot kinship institution, but in a
  genuinely different shape than a personal teaching-lineage: per the historical record ("The Achievement
  Richter Never Got Full Credit For"), credit for real contributions here diffuses to "the department" rather
  than staying attached to one mentor's name — closer to Davis's collective-credit model than to Janbogo's or
  Mirny's individually-named teaching lineages. A robot's "family" here is plausibly less "who specifically
  trained me" and more the ongoing, everyday condition of proving herself through visible output alongside the
  same working generation, day after day — a shared *life*, not a single credited achievement or a specific
  training event.
- **Re-checked 2026-08-10 against the revised shared-experience-first Kinship framing — no change needed.**
  Like Davis, Sinheung's Kinship finding was never mentor/mentee-shaped to begin with: it's already a
  horizontal, ongoing shared condition (proving oneself through output alongside a working generation, credit
  diffusing to the department rather than a named mentor) with no teaching hierarchy in it. The honest outcome
  here is confirmation, not revision.

### 2. Cooperation, Morality & Emergent Social Convention — Local Expression

- **[Adapted, Deep]** This directly resolves the tension flagged at the end of Shirayuki's run. Both cities
  share the Jeju-do diplomatic-decree founding mechanism, but their emergent civic ethics land in genuinely
  different places. Shirayuki's ("A Place Decided For You, Made Into a Place You'd Choose") is settled and
  generational — the tension gets warmly reconciled over time, past tense. Sinheung's ("Claimed, Not Found")
  is active and ongoing — legitimacy here is never treated as fully settled; it's continuously, actively
  reproven through demonstrated output (§6a), because the founding fault line itself (a claim made by treaty
  "could, in principle, be unmade the same way") never gets discussed directly and never fully resolves. This
  tracks the Tri-Cities' own Enneagram data precisely: Shirayuki's Positive Outlook reframes and settles;
  Sinheung's Reactive register wants the underlying tension acknowledged, not smoothed over. For robots
  specifically: since Sinheung robots are quite literally *manufactured* here — the city's own central
  legitimacy-proving activity — the anxiety and the pride in this ethic both land with extra weight for them,
  not just as an inherited civic mood but as something closer to their own origin story.

### 3. Locality as Identity — What Makes This City's Robots Recognizable

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** Distinct from Shirayuki's Surface-level frame-customization marker (per the
  Swap Test discipline, this needed to differ in *kind*, not just detail): Sinheung robots — especially those
  who worked or were fabricated at the Chamber Works — plausibly carry an unusually acute, personal awareness
  of fabrication-precision-as-consciousness-creation. "The Weight of the Apparatus" (a named historical
  incident: workers realizing their precision manufacturing work is literally how new robots come into being)
  and "The Batch That Almost Shipped Wrong" (a technician's insistence on recalibration catching a real
  drift, now cited as justification for the city's whole quality-control culture) both point to the same
  thing: a Sinheung robot knows, more viscerally than a robot from almost anywhere else, that a miscalibration
  isn't just a defect — it's potentially a flawed start to another robot's entire existence.

### 4. Generational Identity (Gen/Mark) — Local Flavor

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** This is the richest, most directly-canon-supported finding in the entire run
  so far. §17 already states plainly that robots built in or shipped from Sinheung's own fabrication-synthesis
  chambers "carry a specific pride in that lineage — the Tepenian equivalent of knowing exactly where you were
  made." This doesn't violate Ch.14's non-hierarchical rule (it's not about being *better* than other Gens/
  Marks) — it's genuine attachment to an uncommonly meaningful point of origin, since Sinheung is one of only
  two cities in Tepenia (with Byrd) where chambers are actually built. Worth distinguishing: a robot
  *fabricated* here plausibly carries this pride more intensely than one merely *shipped from* here for use
  elsewhere — a real gradient this pass surfaces rather than treating the whole population as identical.

### 5. Religion & Belief — Robot-Specific Local Practice

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** §18 confirms no dominant formal religion — a secular ethic of contribution and
  proof-through-output instead, with a quieter, unarticulated undercurrent: the specific psychological weight
  of holding a claim that was made rather than found. Structurally similar in *shape* to Davis's own
  undercurrent finding (a real but unorganized philosophical thread, not a devotional tradition) but entirely
  different in *content* — Davis's is about comfort with unresolved data multiplicity; Sinheung's is about the
  quiet anxiety of a legitimacy that was never simply given. See Cross-Reference Synthesis for how this
  connects to the Electricity element's own established dual valence.

### 6. Consumption & Vice Culture — Local Siligel/Coolant/Glitch-Coolant Flavor

- **[Directly-inherited, Surface]** §10c's existing note stands: practical and efficient siligel preparation,
  explicitly contrasted against Zhongshan's connoisseurship — consistent with the city's broader output-over-
  ceremony instinct.
- **[Adapted, Deep]** A seventh distinct Glitch-Coolant variant across this run: achievement- or milestone-
  marking consumption, directly grounded in the already-established Chamber Watch Toasts custom (informal
  recognition when a new chamber's long construction cycle completes). Rather than settled-cosmopolitan
  variety, working-class potency, transience, absence, temporal-scattering, or DIY-crafting (the six prior
  patterns found across this run), Sinheung's Glitch-Coolant culture plausibly attaches to specific completed
  output — you drink to mark having finished something, consistent with "output as legitimacy" governing even
  this. Tagged Deep since the finding is really about *when and why* drinking happens as a value-statement,
  not primarily about the substance itself.

### 7. Sexuality & Courtship — Local Customs

- **[Adapted, Deep]** Distinct from both Davis's private collaborative-authorship signal and Shirayuki's
  private aesthetic-adoption signal: given Sinheung's low-privacy, high-mutual-visibility civic value (§6e —
  "people here know each other's business, and that mutual visibility is itself part of what makes the claim
  feel solid") and the dominant "output as legitimacy" ethic, a plausible local courtship signal is
  public, community-witnessed demonstration of capability on someone's behalf — visibly fixing, building, or
  improving something for a specific person, in a way the community actually sees, rather than a private
  gesture. Consistent with the city's own explicitly outward-performing character (§6d), not private at all.

### 8. Vernacular Language — Robot-Specific Speech Markers

- **[Adapted, Surface]** §8 already establishes that fabrication-floor vocabulary bleeds into casual speech as
  metaphor for personal worth — a genuinely unique local source, distinct from every prior city's vernacular
  finding. Robot angle: given robots concentrate in fabrication (§17) and the quality-control culture
  established by "The Batch That Almost Shipped Wrong," robot production workers are the plausible primary
  source and heaviest users of this vocabulary — describing personal reliability in terms of tolerance,
  calibration, and drift, rather than any of the prior cities' heritage-fossil, craft-precision, road-jargon,
  terrain-idiom, or subculture-naming source-types.

### 9. Arcanet Etiquette — Local Network Behavior

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** Already confirmed directly in §24's own text: skews toward production data,
  coordination logistics, and demonstrable output tracking — "a network that, like everything else here,
  exists to prove something." Nothing new to derive; recorded as confirmed, pre-existing canon, thematically
  consistent with every other finding in this pass.

### 10. Surface Culture — Robot-Specific Markers

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Surface]** Distinct in *logic* from Shirayuki's individually-expressive frame-
  customization: given the intense pride-in-manufacturing-origin already established (§17, §4 above), a
  plausible marker is a visible, recognized finish or marking specific to Sinheung-made chambers — closer to a
  maker's mark or point-of-origin signature than individual artistic self-expression. Collective/origin-pride
  logic, not personal-style logic.
- **[Adapted, Surface]** Given the already-established Production-Floor Work Songs custom (collective labor
  music), robots' capacity for precise timing plausibly makes them particularly associated with the rhythm-
  keeping role within these work-songs — a functional role inside an existing human-originated tradition,
  distinct from Casey's technical-precision-in-professional-performance finding and Shirayuki's digital-
  production-workflow finding.

## PART II — Cross-Reference Synthesis Findings (Step 4)

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** — *Input categories combined: E (Electricity, already-established canon,
  Uranus + Electricity per `city-symbol-pairs.md`) + A (§5's fault line — a claim made by treaty "could, in
  principle, be unmade the same way") + C (the Chamber Works)*
  Electricity's own established positive valence — "the spark that makes matter conscious" — maps almost
  literally onto what the Chamber Works actually does. Its negative valence — shock, short-circuit,
  instability, total dependency on a power source, already explicitly noted in the element's own canon
  description as "a literal echo of Amundsen Tower's own story" — maps with real precision onto Sinheung's own
  quiet anxiety about the fragility of a treaty-secured claim. Worth flagging: this is the second time in this
  run (after Davis's Earth) that an *already-established* Elemental assignment's dual valence has mapped this
  precisely onto a city's own specific civic fault line — possibly a generalizable technique worth using
  deliberately on future cities that already have a confirmed Elemental pick, not just a coincidence specific
  to these two.

- **[Adapted, Deep]** — *Input categories combined: C (Finding A — building to Neumayer's externally-designed
  Mark IV schematic as another "earn it, don't inherit it" instance) + A (§5's core theme) + Robot Universals
  Ch.14 (Gen/Mark, chamber design)*
  A robot fabricated at Sinheung carries a doubly-layered "earned, not inherited" identity: built using a
  schematic the city didn't design but had to earn the trust and capability to execute, by a workforce whose
  own civic standing depends on continuously demonstrated output. This is the clearest example in the entire
  run so far of a single finding genuinely combining kinship (Ch.11), Gen/Mark (Ch.14), and the city's own
  specific civic theme into one coherent, non-generic observation.
