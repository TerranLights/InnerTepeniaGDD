## PART I — Local Aspects

### 1. Kinship, Clan & Mentor/Mentee Structures — Local Expression

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** Leading with the actual shared *life* experience, not a single incident:
  §17 already establishes "a specific local respect for robots who've overseen multiple relocation cycles,
  treated similarly to how other cities might revere elders." This is exactly the shape of ongoing, cumulative
  shared condition the revised Kinship framing looks for — the bond isn't earned through one dramatic move,
  it's earned through having personally lived the recurring, collective labor of relocating the literal ground
  you stand on, cycle after cycle, alongside whoever else has done the same. That ongoing condition, genuinely
  horizontal and cutting across origin, is Halley's real kinship floor.
- **[Adapted, Deep]** One specific shape that shared life takes: the Position Archive's own keeper tradition
  (per "The Archivist Who Never Saw the Founding Position," a decades-long keeper who has personally overseen
  transfer of records from now-inaccessible Old Positions) plausibly functions as an informal teaching-lineage
  for newer archivists — one particular, memory-keeping shape the broader shared relocation-life takes, not a
  competing mechanism from it.

### 2. Cooperation, Morality & Emergent Social Convention — Local Expression

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** The Methodologists' own crystallized civic doctrine — "rigor is whatever
  survives being wrong about the ground under your feet" — is already established as Halley's real emergent
  ethic, confirmed rather than invented. The standing practice that a trusted ice-reader's refusal alone can
  delay a crossing (per "The Read That Held the Line") gives this doctrine a genuine, checkable civic
  mechanism: authority here is earned through a track record of being provably right, not credentials or
  seniority alone.

### 3. Locality as Identity — What Makes This City's Robots Recognizable

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** §14 already gestures at this directly: residents develop "the specific,
  faint awareness of standing on ice rather than ground... a sense for it over years." A Halley robot,
  concentrated in structural-assessment and ice-monitoring work (§17), plausibly develops this sense more
  acutely and more reliably than a human resident could — directly feeding the ice-reader tradition (§2 above)
  and making robot judgment on ground stability something closer to instinct than analysis.

### 4. Generational Identity (Gen/Mark) — Local Flavor

- **No meaningful local variation found** (Honesty Check, per the Universal Triage Record above). Recorded
  here rather than left blank.

### 5. Religion & Belief — Robot-Specific Local Practice

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** §18's own material stands as the honest local answer: "permanence is not a
  virtue," and willingness to relocate is treated as "a kind of wisdom in itself," with Old Positions
  functioning as something close to a secular pilgrimage site. Robot angle: a robot's own identity, unlike a
  building's, was never tied to one fixed physical location to begin with — she can be reassigned, relocated,
  or reconstructed without the identity crisis a human might associate with losing a home. This gives Halley's
  own civic philosophy a genuinely literal, embodied echo in its robot population that the human population
  can only hold as a value, not a lived physical fact.
- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** Solar symbol Mercury + Robo-Element Water is already on file for Halley,
  grounded directly in "endurance right at the edge of a moving ice shelf, adapting rather than resisting."
  Water's own positive valence (adaptability, calm resilience) tracks the city's own civic temperament with
  real precision — "neither anxious nor fatalistic — just practical."

### 6. Consumption & Vice Culture — Local Siligel/Coolant/Glitch-Coolant Flavor

- **[Adapted, Surface]** §10c is confirmed thin, reusing the human-food seasonal-supply-window logic directly.
  Real room to expand, grounded in §6b's own resupply-as-civic-occasion framing (Resupply Feasts, Resupply
  Dock Watch): siligel culture plausibly gets its own distinct version tied specifically to fresh-shipment
  arrival, distinct in character from routine day-to-day rationing — a genuine civic occasion, not merely
  restocking.
- **[Adapted, Surface]** Halley isn't named in either existing Glitch-Coolant category. Given the resupply-
  window rhythm already governing so much of civic life here, Halley plausibly represents a genuinely new
  pattern across this whole project: consumption concentrated around and tied to resupply-ship arrivals
  specifically, distinct from Kunlun's own temporally-scattered 24-hour-shift-economy pattern — Halley's
  rhythm is seasonal and maritime-window-based, not continuous, so the pattern here is occasional and
  celebratory rather than evenly distributed.

### 7. Sexuality & Courtship — Local Customs

- **[Adapted, Deep]** Distinct from every prior city's courtship finding: given the ice-reading tradition's own
  established trust-through-track-record logic (§2), a plausible local courtship signal is personally teaching
  someone the physical skill of reading ice and ground stability — an intimate, high-stakes act of trust
  specific to a city whose whole civic authority structure already runs on exactly this mechanism, extended
  here to a single, chosen person rather than the whole crossing-dependent public.

### 8. Vernacular Language — Robot-Specific Speech Markers

- **[Adapted, Surface]** §8 already establishes referring to city sections by "position number" (relocation
  cycle) rather than neighborhood name as the marker of a genuine long-term resident. Robot angle: given
  robots' concentration in structural-assessment and mobility-monitoring roles (§17), robot specialists are
  the plausible primary shapers and most precise users of this terminology, the same structural role other
  cities' trade specialists play for their own local registers.

### 9. Arcanet Etiquette — Local Network Behavior

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** Already confirmed directly in §24's own text: Halley is the literal origin
  point of Hwy 59, the highway/Arcanet dual corridor to the South Pole relay, and residents are "unusually
  infrastructure-aware" of the network's physical vulnerability in a way most subnets don't have to think
  about directly. See Cross-Reference Synthesis for the fuller treatment tying this to the Sanay-nexus trade.

### 10. Surface Culture — Robot-Specific Markers

- **[Directly-inherited, Surface]** The documentary/photographic tradition around relocation history (§13,
  "genuine civic archiving, not mere nostalgia") is already established. Robot angle: given the Position
  Archive's own confirmed fragility (Physical Infrastructure Finding A — the *only* surviving physical anchor
  of Halley's deep history, since founding-era Old Positions have already calved into the sea), robot
  archivists carry genuinely higher stakes in this role than an equivalent specialist would at almost any
  other Tepenian city, where ruins themselves usually still hold a fallback record.
- **[Emergent, robot-only, Surface]** §11's "small, portable details rather than anything structural" fashion
  register is already established. Robot angle: since robots don't need the same protective bulk humans do in
  every context, small personal details plausibly read even more legibly on a robot's own frame — the same
  civic value, expressed with slightly more room to actually show it.

## PART II — Cross-Reference Synthesis Findings (Step 4)

- **[Emergent, Deep]** — *Input categories combined: A (§23/§25's Sanay-nexus trade) + C (Physical
  Infrastructure Finding A — the Position Archive as the sole surviving anchor of deep history) + §1's own
  elder-veteran-respect Kinship finding*
  The same instinct runs through three separately-established facts here, not three unrelated observations:
  Halley traded away permanent technical control specifically to be *remembered by name*; its own deepest
  history survives only through a fragile archive and the personal memory of its keepers, not fixed
  infrastructure; and its highest civic honor goes to robots who've personally lived through the most
  relocation cycles. A city that never stops moving builds its whole sense of continuity through people —
  naming honor, veteran memory, archival keepers — rather than through anything fixed, because nothing fixed
  here survives long enough to hold that continuity instead.

- **[Adapted, Deep]** — *Input categories combined: C/History ("The Read That Held the Line," "The Cable Crew
  That Refused to Cut Corners") + §2 (The Methodologists' own doctrine) + Robot Biology (robots' own precision
  structural/ice-sensing capability, §3)*
  Since Halley's whole civic doctrine is literally "rigor is whatever survives being wrong about the ground
  under your feet," and robots hold particular authority in exactly the ice-monitoring and structural-
  assessment work that doctrine is about, a robot's professional judgment here plausibly carries something
  closer to doctrinal weight than ordinary technical-competence weight — she isn't just good at her job, her
  judgment is the living, working expression of the city's own founding philosophy in a way it isn't quite
  anywhere else in this run.

- **[Adapted, Deep, flagged forward]** — *Input categories combined: A (Halley as namesake, Sanay as technical
  hub) + `City_National_Connections.md` (Strong ties to both Sanay and Neumayer)*
  Worth flagging as a hypothesis for the rest of this subnet, not asserted yet: Halley's own split between
  naming honor and technical control is a genuinely different shape than the Janbogo subnet's own pattern
  (where Janbogo was both namesake *and* Arcanet hub at once). Whether this "credit and control living in two
  different cities" shape recurs elsewhere in the Halley subnet, or is specific to Halley and Sanay alone, is
  a real open question — worth actively checking once Sanay itself is run, rather than assuming either way.
