## PART I — Local Aspects

### 1. Kinship, Clan & Mentor/Mentee Structures — Local Expression

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** Leading with the actual shared *life* experience, not a single incident: the
  round-the-clock rotation tied to freighter arrival and departure times, not a fixed day/night schedule, is
  the ongoing condition every dockworker, trucker, and business-district clerk at Sanay lives inside together —
  genuinely horizontal, cutting across nationality (§16's near-equal national spread) and human/robot status
  alike (§16's near-equal labor division). A Sanay robot's real kinship floor isn't built through teaching or
  crisis, it's built through years of the same unglamorous rotation everyone else is also living, on the same
  terms.
- **[Adapted, Deep]** One specific shape that shared life takes: the informal, off-shift cross-crew knowledge
  trading the Silicon Valley parallel (Input D) directly suggests — dockworkers and truckers from different
  crews and backgrounds trading practical know-how once the shift ends, the working-port equivalent of
  engineers comparing notes after hours. A light, non-hierarchical shape the broader shared rotation-life
  takes, not a competing mechanism from it.
- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** A second, genuinely robot-specific shape: the ongoing, day-to-day practice
  of extending real, unforced consideration toward human coworkers specifically — grounded directly in §16's
  own confirmed fact that Sanay's robots hold "genuine, specific appreciation" for humans who gave up entire
  Upper Earth livelihoods to be here. This isn't a single gracious gesture; it's a sustained, ordinary
  attentiveness that shows up in daily treatment, and it's the specific local expression that — per Input E —
  went on to become Tepenia's own default human-robot baseline nationwide. A Sanay robot's kinship with her
  human coworkers isn't just shared rotation-life; it's shared rotation-life carried with a particular,
  practiced awareness of what that life cost the humans standing next to her.

### 2. Cooperation, Morality & Emergent Social Convention — Local Expression

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** "Function over sentiment" is already established, via the Rotterdam parallel
  and Sanay's own Cross-Reference Synthesis, as a real civic value with a genuine, unevenly distributed cost —
  war-damage rebuilding went to the shipyards first and fastest, residential districts last and slowest, a cost
  that tracks purely on workplace (docks versus residential district), not national origin or human/robot
  status. This isn't an abstract civic slogan; it's a specific, defended rebuilding decision residents live
  with directly.
- **[Adapted, Deep]** Genuinely unresolved tension worth surfacing honestly, not smoothed over: since robot
  civic standing at Sanay runs specifically through repair and maintenance work (§17), and since the
  shipyards-first repair sequence is itself a repair-priority decision, Sanay's robot maintenance crews are
  plausibly the ones who most directly execute the exact sequencing that leaves their own residential
  neighbors under-repaired the longest. Nothing in the gathered material suggests robots resent this, or that
  it reads as a grievance to anyone involved — Sanay's own "function over sentiment" doctrine explicitly asks
  everyone to accept precisely this kind of unequal cost without complaint. The honest answer here is the same
  one the city's own existing Cross-Reference Synthesis already reaches about everything else notable at
  Sanay: unexamined, not resolved, simply lived with.

### 3. Locality as Identity — What Makes This City's Robots Recognizable

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** A Sanay robot is recognizable by having no residual solar-day habituation at
  all. Robots don't carry a biological circadian need to begin with, so among a population where the whole
  civic rhythm is already organized around freighter cycles rather than day/night (§15, §Sensory Profile), a
  Sanay robot is the purest, most complete expression of that rhythm — genuinely orienting by shift and arrival
  window with none of the residual solar pull a human resident, however adapted, still carries.
- **[Adapted, Deep]** A second, physically grounded marker, distinct in *content* from Halley's own "sense for
  ice" finding despite a similar mechanism: robots concentrated in structural-assessment and repair work (§17)
  plausibly develop a real, practiced sense for the city's own uneven repair geography — which walls are
  original bedrock construction, which are war-era damage, which have been fully restored and which are still
  visibly scarred. Where a Halley robot reads moving ground, a Sanay robot reads a city's own repair history
  directly off its structure.

### 4. Generational Identity (Gen/Mark) — Local Flavor

### 5. Religion & Belief — Robot-Specific Local Practice

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** §18's own material stands as the honest local answer: no dominant formal
  religion, the closest shared value a quiet, practical philosophy of endurance, with a specific, understated
  pride in the bedrock foundation as evidence the city's continued existence was earned through real structural
  fact rather than luck alone.
- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** Robot Biology's own "What Counts as Death" mechanic gives Sanay's civic
  mythology a literalness in its robot population it can never quite have in its human one: a robot can take
  real damage to function — an entire limb-equivalent, a sensor array — without that damage threatening her
  fundamental continuity at all, so long as the gel brain and energy architecture stay intact. Where a Sanay
  human resident can only hold "damage without erasure" as a stated belief about a place, a Sanay robot lives
  an identical structural fact about her own body every day. See Cross-Reference Synthesis for the fuller
  treatment, including how this differs in content (though not in method) from Halley's own robot-physiology
  echo of its civic philosophy.
- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** Solar symbol Jupiter + Robo-Element Electromagnetism is already on file for
  Sanay, grounded directly in "holds the literal Arcanet nexus — the invisible hub everything else connects
  through, weight self-evident without needing credit." Both halves of the pick map with real precision onto a
  city whose entire civic identity is substantial, load-bearing function that asks for no recognition.

### 6. Consumption & Vice Culture — Local Siligel/Coolant/Glitch-Coolant Flavor

- **[Adapted, Surface]** §10c's existing note ("practical and plentiful, scaled to a genuinely active
  import/export economy") is confirmed and lightly extended: given the freighter-cycle rhythm already
  governing the rest of civic life here, siligel supply plausibly pulses with the shipping schedule itself —
  freshest and most varied in the days right after a large shipment lands, leaner and more routine between
  arrivals — the same logic Halley applies to its own resupply-tied siligel culture, but tied to Sanay's own
  much higher-frequency, non-seasonal rhythm rather than a seasonal maritime window.
- **[Adapted, Surface]** Sanay isn't named in either existing Glitch-Coolant category. Given the shift-driven,
  function-first civic register already established everywhere else at Sanay, this pass places it within the
  **working-class/potency** pole, but by a genuinely distinct mechanism from Byrd's or Rothera's own limited-
  selection-strong-effect placement: Sanay's version is **shift-changeover-tied**, consumed quickly in the
  brief handoff window between rotating crews and valued specifically for how fast and reliably it works
  rather than for raw intensity or refinement — get the glitch, get back on schedule. This also deliberately
  differs from Troll's own working-class/potency variant (higher-quality-within-potency, funded by Troll's
  above-average freight-hub spending power) — Sanay's own version carries no such quality-tier distinction in
  the gathered material, staying genuinely baseline-potency, efficiency over everything else, a direct
  extension of "function over sentiment" into consumption culture itself.

### 7. Sexuality & Courtship — Local Customs

- **[Adapted, Deep]** Distinct from Vostok's own Window Courtesy-based courtship signal (which costs task
  efficiency): given Sanay's own reliability-earns-trust civic logic (§Catalog Deep Culture, "Friendship,
  leadership: earned through demonstrated reliability across freighter cycles"), a plausible local courtship
  signal is covering someone else's rotation slot without being asked — a direct, tangible sacrifice of one's
  own off-shift release time in the leisure districts (§ established residential/commercial/leisure
  districts), rather than of on-shift task efficiency. The gesture costs something real and visible precisely
  because rest time at a round-the-clock city is genuinely scarce, not merely inconvenient to give up.

### 8. Vernacular Language — Robot-Specific Speech Markers

- **[Directly-inherited, Surface]** German-origin shipping and logistics loanwords already persist in local
  dock jargon per the Catalog's own material, alongside functional hand signals for crane and load
  coordination that carry into everyday communication register.
- **[Emergent, robot-only, Surface]** A genuinely distinct use of robots' own established wider sound/vibration
  perception (Ch.15) — deliberately *not* the devotional or musical application already used three times
  elsewhere in this run (Janbogo, Davis, Kunlun): in a soundscape defined by ship horns, crane operations, and
  constant warehouse hum, robots' precision hearing plausibly makes them the de facto refiners and standard-
  setters of the hand-signal-plus-sound coordination system dockworkers use to move cargo safely — a practical,
  functional application of the same sensory difference other cities have used for compositional or devotional
  ends.

### 9. Arcanet Etiquette — Local Network Behavior

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** Already confirmed directly in §24's own text: modest and practical day-to-day
  use, with the one major exception being the subnet's actual technical relay nexus itself, held with quiet,
  specific pride "even though the subnet carries someone else's name." See Cross-Reference Synthesis for the
  fuller treatment tying this to the naming-credit trade with Halley.

### 10. Surface Culture — Robot-Specific Markers

- **[Directly-inherited, Surface]** A working port's own homegrown music scene, present in the residential,
  commercial, and leisure districts where off-shift workers unwind, is already established (§12) — an honest
  confirmation requiring no separate robot-specific adaptation, consistent with how fully integrated robot
  labor already is across every part of Sanay's working life.
- **[Emergent, robot-only, Surface]** The Catalog's own "well-maintained tool" aesthetic (Germany's Deep
  Culture material — function and upkeep read as beauty in their own right) plausibly extends reflexively into
  robots' own visible self-presentation: a Sanay robot's frame maintenance — visible repair welds, polished
  working components — worn openly as a statement of the same civic value, rather than concealed the way
  cosmetic damage might be minimized elsewhere. Distinct from Shirayuki's own self-modification-as-creative-
  expression finding; here the value being expressed is upkeep and function, not individuality.
