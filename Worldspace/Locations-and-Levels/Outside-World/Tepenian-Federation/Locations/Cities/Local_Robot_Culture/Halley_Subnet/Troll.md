flagged highest-priority remaining city.** Directly follows up on Sanay's own forward flag (Strong Logistics

## PART I — Local Aspects

### 1. Kinship, Clan & Mentor/Mentee Structures — Local Expression

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** Leading with the actual shared *life* experience, not a single incident: the
  Cross-Faction Dispatch Handoffs — the daily, structurally necessary coordination between the three rival
  factions holding the Runway and Control Tower, the Fuel Depot, and the Trucking Dispatch Yard respectively,
  none of which can operate the airfield alone (Physical Infrastructure attributes #4–6; Community
  Infrastructure's own "Worth Your Attention" flag). This is genuinely ongoing, not a past event — the
  fracture has persisted since the war, and the handoffs recur constantly for as long as it does. Human and
  robot alike live inside this condition together, since §16 already establishes competence and trust cutting
  across both populations rather than hierarchy. Nobody doing this work chooses to trust their factional
  rivals out of virtue; the airfield simply doesn't run if they don't — a genuinely coerced-by-circumstance
  shared life, distinct in *character* from every prior Halley-subnet Kinship floor (Halley's chosen
  relocation-cycle respect, Sanay's externally-paced shift rotation, Neumayer's deliberately-designed
  anti-burnout overlap, Belgrano's inherited operational habit) even where the underlying "ongoing ordinary
  interdependence" shape resembles them.
- **[Adapted, Deep]** One specific shape that shared life takes: §17's own established professional aviation/
  logistics-competence culture — "similar in spirit to Neumayer's engineering-competence culture but applied
  to flight operations rather than research" — a specialized-trust shape the broader cross-faction condition
  takes among flight ops, ground crew, and dispatch specialists specifically.
- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** A genuinely robot-specific shape, tying two input categories directly
  together: `Robot_Physiology_and_Cultural_Practices.md`'s own confirmed kinship-basis fact — robots draw
  clan/tribe identity from shared geography and shared experience, not genetics, unlike humans, who have all
  three available — means a robot doing cross-faction coordination work at Troll plausibly stands on
  structurally cleaner ground than a human descendant of a specific founding population, however diluted that
  population's own demographic tension has become (§1 of `Local_Cultures`, "the standard shape for this
  subnet"). Worth stating honestly: nothing in the gathered material claims faction membership actually tracks
  by species, and this pass doesn't assume it does — but a robot's own kinship logic was never staked on any
  one faction's lineage to begin with, which plausibly makes her a slightly more naturally credible
  neutral broker than a human coworker still carrying whatever residual inherited sympathy, however faint.

### 2. Cooperation, Morality & Emergent Social Convention — Local Expression

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** "Competence is leverage, converted into control of the thing that matters"
  (Catalog Synthesis Notes) is already established as Troll's own sharper, more overtly political variant of
  the "Competence Without Commentary" faction it shares with Sanay and Abowasa. Per the city's own established
  identity, there is explicitly no neutral path here — residents eventually take a position on who controls
  the airfield (§28, "there's no neutral, uninvolved way to actually live here long-term").
- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** A genuinely unusual civic-governance structure, distinct from every other
  Halley-subnet city's own emergent doctrine: the Airfield Authority (Course of Events #2, "One Authority for
  Every Claim," non-canon candidate) — a dedicated body, separate from ordinary city government, chartered
  specifically to arbitrate scheduling, storage, and fee disputes, that every commercial interest at Troll
  answers to without any single one of them fully controlling it. This gives the Cross-Faction Dispatch
  Handoffs (§1) their actual administrative shape, rather than leaving the fracture as a purely informal fact.
- **[Adapted, Deep, genuinely unresolved]** A real tension surfaced and left honestly unresolved, not smoothed
  over: robots plausibly do a disproportionate share of the actual daily cross-faction coordination labor,
  given §16/§17's own competence-and-trust-based role allocation in aviation and logistics work specifically —
  meaning robot workers are structurally the ones holding the fragile system together day to day, while the
  political stakes and public credit for "who controls the airfield" get argued over in human-coded political
  terms (factions, Authority charters, historical grievance). Nothing in the gathered material suggests this
  reads as resentment to Troll's own robot population — the same honest "unexamined, simply lived with"
  reading already reached about Sanay's own uneven-repair-cost tension and Belgrano's own "whose discipline is
  it" tension.

### 3. Locality as Identity — What Makes This City's Robots Recognizable

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** Primary marker: a practiced, faction-blind neutral register. Since the
  roles that carry real standing at Troll — flight ops, dispatch, fuel-depot logistics — run on demonstrated
  competence and trust cutting across three actively rival factions (§16), a Troll robot is recognizable by an
  instinct to read every situation for who's-aligned-with-whom while disclosing nothing of her own position
  until it's actually required — the professional discipline the whole fractured system depends on, made
  personal. Distinct from Sanay's own quiet-competence-needs-no-credit register or Neumayer's own reflexive
  self-calibration: Troll's version is about legibility to *others* (trustworthy to all three sides at once
  precisely by giving none of them anything to hold against her), not an internal discipline turned inward.
- **[Adapted, Deep]** A second marker, deliberately reusing a device already established at Sanay (no residual
  solar-day habituation) but for genuinely different content: Troll's own civic clock runs on flight windows,
  weather permitting, not daylight (§6c). Since robots carry no biological circadian need to begin with, a
  Troll robot is plausibly the purest expression of "flight-window time" the same way a Sanay robot is the
  purest expression of freighter-cycle time — oriented entirely by operational schedule with none of the
  residual solar pull a human resident, however adapted, still carries. The underlying mechanism repeats; the
  rhythm-type driving it (weather-gated flight windows versus continuous freighter arrivals) doesn't.

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep, fifth instance of a recurring subnet technique]** Robot Biology's own
  confirmed gel-brain malleability — the same underlying fact Halley's own reassignability-mirrors-

  eventually take a position" a different weight for a robot than for a human resident: since nothing about a
  robot's own substrate locks her into one fixed self or loyalty, taking, or later revising, a position in the

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** Solar symbol Neptune + Robo-Element Electricity is already on file for Troll,

### 7. Sexuality & Courtship — Local Customs

- **[Adapted, Deep]** Distinct from every prior Halley-subnet courtship finding (Halley's teaching-ice-reading,
  Sanay's covering-a-shift, Neumayer's showing-unfinished-work, Belgrano's quietly-keeping-someone's-gear-
  repaired): given Troll's own established "wary and transactional where the airfield is concerned, warmer
  everywhere else" hospitality character (§6b) and the city's own "no neutral path" civic fact, a plausible
  local courtship signal is deliberately vouching for a partner within one's own faction's trusted circle —
  extending access and personal credibility that's normally earned slowly, only through demonstrated
  reliability. In a genuinely fractured, competitively guarded political environment, staking your own
  standing on someone else's trustworthiness is a real, visible, public risk — unlike Neumayer's showing-
  unfinished-work (which risks professional reputation privately) or Belgrano's quiet, unannounced repair-care
  (which risks nothing socially at all), Troll's version carries real factional consequence if the trust turns
  out to be misplaced.
