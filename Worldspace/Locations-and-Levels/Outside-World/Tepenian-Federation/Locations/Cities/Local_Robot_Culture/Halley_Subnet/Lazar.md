## PART I — Local Aspects

### 1. Kinship, Clan & Mentor/Mentee Structures — Local Expression

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep, a genuine structural inversion from every prior Halley-subnet city]** §17's own
  text supplies the leading fact directly: robot culture at Lazar has "no single unifying robot-culture
  narrative the way smaller, more singular cities might have." Every other Halley-subnet city run so far
  (Halley, Sanay, Neumayer, Belgrano, Troll) has exactly one real, city-wide shared-life floor. Lazar's own
  Community Infrastructure material makes clear why this city can't: it's simply too large for any single
  citywide social rhythm to be anyone's actual lived experience (`Lazar_Community_Infrastructure.md`'s own
  "Worth Your Attention" note). The genuine shared-life floor here sits one or two altitudes lower — at the
  Sector (Cargo Row, the Bolt Market, the Steam Kitchens, or the Old Core's own institutional quarter) or, for
  a large share of residents, at the single Highrise building itself (the Highrise Commons, Highrise Balcony
  Nights) — an ongoing, daily, cumulative condition of living and working among the same repeating faces, just
  scaled down from "the city" to "the block."
- **[Adapted, Deep]** A second, lighter shape at an intermediate scale: Cross-District Moods — the ordinary,
  daily movement between the Old Core's institutional reserve and the Overflow District's commercial energy,
  "experienced as two adjacent moods rather than a rift" (`Lazar_Community_Infrastructure.md`). A genuinely
  two-district-scale shared experience, sitting between the Sector floor above and the citywide holidays below.
- **[Adapted, Deep]** A third, specialist shape: the technical/engineering sector (20% of Lazar's own
  industry, an inherited Novolazarevskaya research/logistics tradition — §15) maintains a real, ongoing
  professional relationship with Neumayer's own precision-engineering culture (Full Extrapolation §V;
  Physical Infrastructure Finding C, the Neumayer Trade Exchange) — a specialized-trust shape the broader
  condition takes for robots and humans working in that specific technical slice of the city's own economy.

### 2. Cooperation, Morality & Emergent Social Convention — Local Expression

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** "Integration is the norm, not the exception" is already established as
  Lazar's own real civic doctrine — a city built from two settlements and five roughly-equal immigrant
  communities simply doesn't have the option of insisting on one correct cultural default (§6a).
- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** A genuinely distinct civic friction, unlike anything found elsewhere in this
  subnet: "Biggest, Not in Charge" (Course of Events #11, non-canon candidate; independently echoed in the
  Catalog's own Russia Deep Culture entry — "Lazar is Tepenia's largest city, but not its political capital").
  Lazar's own economic and demographic weight doesn't translate into formal political standing, a permanent,
  unresolved civic posture the city carries forward rather than resolves.
- **[Adapted, Deep]** Given robots hold a real numeric majority at Lazar (942,771 to 861,206) in a city that
  is simultaneously Tepenia's largest population center and structurally excluded from proportionate formal
  political authority, this civic friction plausibly lands with a specific edge for Lazar's own robot
  population — see Cross-Reference Synthesis for the fuller, deliberately flagged-rather-than-resolved
  treatment.

### 3. Locality as Identity — What Makes This City's Robots Recognizable

- **No single city-wide marker** (Honesty Check, extending §1's own structural inversion): the specific
  practices that mark a robot as "from here" elsewhere in this subnet — Halley's ice-sense, Sanay's
  repair-reading, Neumayer's self-calibration, Belgrano's diagnostic hearing, Troll's faction-neutral register
  — all exist at the *city* scale precisely because those cities are small enough for one practice to actually
  spread citywide. Lazar is not. The honest local answer is that a Lazar robot is recognizable first by
  *which sector* she reads as home — a Cargo Row robot, a Bolt Market robot, an Old Core robot — before
  anything about her reads as generically "Lazar."
- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** The one genuine exception, legible at full city scale: a general demeanor,
  not a specific practice — "a specific type of person... recognizable by an ease with complexity that smaller
  Halley subnet cities' residents don't share" (§25). This is the actual city-wide marker, and it's
  deliberately not a *practice* the way every other subnet city's marker is — it's comfort with the fact that
  no single practice, aesthetic, or register defines the place she's from.

### 4. Generational Identity (Gen/Mark) — Local Flavor

- **[Adapted, Deep]** No confirmed manufacturing chamber, current, historical, or paused-candidate, appears
  anywhere in the gathered material for Lazar (Honesty Check on the direct question). But the null localizes
  indirectly, in a genuinely distinct way from every prior city's null or "Yes" so far: `Specs/Lazar.md`'s own
  Population Note confirms Lazar draws heavy, sustained in-migration from Janbogo, Zukelli, and Esperanza —
  meaning a substantial share of Lazar's own robot population was fabricated elsewhere, at chambers running to
  whatever Mark and civic Gen-culture those origin cities themselves carry, and then settled permanently at
  Lazar rather than passing through. This is a genuinely richer version of Casey's own established finding
  (junction-city transit makes casual cross-Mark comparison-talk more common) — Casey's version runs on
  passing strangers; Lazar's runs on a large, *settled* population of robots who carry their origin city's own
  Gen/Mark culture with them permanently, side by side with Lazar's own five-nation, two-settlement human
  plurality.

### 5. Religion & Belief — Robot-Specific Local Practice

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** §18's own material stands as the honest local answer: no dominant formal
  religion, the closest shared value a practical ethic of integration, alongside a specific, genuine pride in
  Novolazarevskaya's unbroken operational history — a station, and now a city, that has never fully gone dark.
- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** The Unbroken Watch and Coalescence Day are both already established civic
  observances (§26), with the Unbroken Watch anchored to a concrete physical site, the Unbroken Operations
  Hall (Physical Infrastructure Finding B) — the walkable monument to the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's
  own richest finding: the "Lazarus" name-echo genuinely inverts the Biblical story it accidentally resembles,
  since nothing external saved Lazar — no luck, no terrain, no bedrock, no rescuing force, only its own
  accumulated scale.
- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep, related to but distinct from the subnet's recurring robot-physiology-echoes-
  civic-disposition technique]** The Lazarus irony gets a genuinely different, robot-specific dimension the
  existing Cross-Reference Synthesis couldn't have reached: `Robot_Physiology_and_Cultural_Practices.md`'s own
  "What Counts as Death" fact establishes that a robot's actual death threshold sits at irreversible damage to
  the gel brain or energy architecture specifically — everything short of that is recoverable, adaptable
  damage, not death. For a Lazar robot specifically, the city's own accidental namesake isn't just civic
  folklore to be inspired by; it describes something structurally closer to her own literal condition than it
  ever could for a human resident, for whom "coming back from apparent death" has no equivalent mechanism at
  all. Worth noting honestly, per the same physiology file's own confirmed caveat: substrate transfer or
  recovery from serious architecture damage carries "a real risk of memory loss or alteration — there is no
  clean, risk-free option," so this isn't a tidy immortality claim, just a structurally real, imperfect
  possibility a human city's own survival-by-scale story simply doesn't have available to it. This differs
  from the five prior instances of the subnet's recurring technique (which each mapped robot physiology onto
  an ongoing civic *disposition* toward a hazard) by mapping instead onto the city's own foundational
  *name and folklore* — a different target for the same underlying device.

### 6. Consumption & Vice Culture — Local Siligel/Coolant/Glitch-Coolant Flavor

- **[Adapted, Surface]** §10c's existing note ("substantial, scaled to a genuine megacity population rather
  than the smaller-scale production of most Halley subnet cities") is confirmed and lightly extended: given
  §1's own sector-scale social structure, siligel production and distribution plausibly runs through the same
  Sub-Market network (Cargo Row, the Bolt Market, the Steam Kitchens) that already handles everything else at
  neighborhood scale, rather than one centralized citywide supply.
- **[Emergent, robot-only, Surface]** Lazar isn't named in either of Glitch-Coolant's two official canon poles
  — a genuine open slot, confirmed by direct check against `Robot_Physiology_and_Cultural_Practices.md`. Given
  the city's own genuine six-way layering of big-city cultural registers (Frankfurt-style, Manchester-style,
  São Paulo-style, Moscow-style, Lyon/Chamonix-style, Denver/Vegas-style, per the Catalog's own Synthesis
  Notes), this pass places Lazar within the **bohemian/cosmopolitan-variety** pole — but by a mechanism
  genuinely distinct from every other bohemian city in the project so far (Janbogo, Zhongshan, Shirayuki, Cape
  Adare, Dumont d'Urville, Scott): those cities' own variety comes from one unified, settled cosmopolitan
  taste culture. Lazar's own variety is **fractured**, not unified — dozens of distinct Sector-scale scenes
  (a Cargo Row taste, a Bolt Market taste, a Steam Kitchens taste, an Old Core taste) rather than one shared
  sophisticated culture citywide, the exact same "too big for one rhythm" structural fact §1 and §3 already
  establish, extended into consumption culture.

### 7. Sexuality & Courtship — Local Customs

- **[Adapted, Deep]** Distinct from every prior Halley-subnet courtship finding (Halley's teaching-ice-reading,
  Sanay's covering-a-shift, Neumayer's showing-unfinished-work, Belgrano's quietly-keeping-someone's-gear-
  repaired, Troll's vouching-across-factional-lines): given Lazar's own established Sector Loyalty (residents
  identifying with their specific sub-market or sector before "the city" as a whole — `Lazar_Community_
  Infrastructure.md`) and the city's genuine, moderate anonymity (§6e), a plausible local courtship signal is
  deliberately introducing a partner to one's own specific Sector — not a generic citywide date, but bringing
  someone into the particular corner of a genuinely enormous, anonymous city that actually functions as home.
  In a place large enough that most relationships could otherwise stay comfortably rootless, choosing to make
  that introduction is the local equivalent of introducing someone to family.

### 8. Vernacular Language — Robot-Specific Speech Markers

- **[Directly-inherited, Surface]** "Old town" (former Novolazarevskaya) versus "the expansion" (the
  Overflow District) already persists as ordinary civic geography, not just historical trivia (§8c).
- **[Emergent, robot-only, Surface]** A genuinely new layer, combining Input A's own confirmed heavy
  in-migration from Janbogo, Zukelli, and Esperanza with Input E's own confirmed kinship-basis fact (robots
  draw clan/tribe identity from shared geography and experience, not genetics, unlike humans, who have all
  three bases available): a robot who immigrated to Lazar from one of those origin cities plausibly retains
  and signals her pre-Lazar city-of-origin markers — vocabulary, idiom, reference points — more durably and
  legibly than a human immigrant's own children would, since a robot's identity was never subject to the kind
  of biological-family assimilation that naturally blends a human child born in Lazar into the city's own
  plural default. A Lazar robot's speech plausibly carries a more legible "which city did you actually come
  from" signal than a Lazar human's does.

- **[Directly-inherited, Surface]** A genuinely varied urban cuisine that never settled into one dominant

  sectors' worth of technical, logistics, or maintenance work, a robot who's genuinely fluent across several
  Sector aesthetics — rather than rooted in just one — plausibly reads as a mark of real, accumulated
  experience within the city itself, a form of local sophistication distinct from and layered on top of §3's
