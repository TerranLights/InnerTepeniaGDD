## PART I — Local Aspects

### 1. Kinship, Clan & Mentor/Mentee Structures — Local Expression

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep] — added in the 2026-08-10 Kinship re-scan, applying the revised
  shared-experience-first framing.** Leading with the actual shared *life* experience, not a teaching
  relationship: per #19–20, robot residential and functional concentration runs genuinely deeper than human
  concentration, since robots need no light-cycle management, no oxygen, and no fast-evacuation margin. Every
  deep-level robot — across all Five Guilds at once — shares the same ongoing, everyday condition of actually
  living at depths human residents rarely if ever occupy, a ceiling-free habitation of the earth itself that
  cuts clean across guild lines. That ongoing shared existence, not any single guild's training or any one
  founding figure, is Byrd's real kinship floor — it holds between two robots who've never worked the same
  guild, as long as both actually live Byrd's deep-level life day to day. The two mentoring tracks below are
  specific shapes that life takes for particular groups, not separate mechanisms.
- **[Adapted, Deep]** One shape that shared life takes: the Five Guildhalls (Fabricators, Dispatch, Foundation
  Keepers, Prospectors' Circle, Grounded Wings), each running its own apprenticeship training, give Byrd a
  genuinely multi-track craft-lineage system — richer in structure than any single-guild finding at a prior
  city, since a robot's craft-lineage here is one of five genuinely distinct tracks rather than one shared
  civic institution.
- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** A second, rarer shape exists alongside the guild system: Explorer Voss, the
  last living member of the original all-robot founding crossing crew, is still resident and occasionally
  consulted informally on deep-tunnel questions. Ordinary conversation with Voss carries no special weight —
  he's a resident like anyone else, easy to talk to. What carries real weight is having genuinely *studied
  under* him — an actual apprenticeship or informal mentorship in deep-tunnel knowledge, not just proximity or
  a passing conversation (worth keeping the founding crossing itself on record as a specific historical
  occurrence in its own right, distinct from this ongoing mentee relationship). Since the founding expedition
  itself has no human witness at all, that specific mentee relationship is a direct, singular, robot-only line
  back to the city's own origin in a way mere acquaintance isn't. See Cross-Reference Synthesis for the fuller
  treatment of what this means for the founding-descendant/later-arrival fault line.

### 2. Cooperation, Morality & Emergent Social Convention — Local Expression

- **[Adapted, Deep]** §6's self-sufficiency ethic is explicitly framed as evolved rather than founding-
  incident-based — "not pride first, necessity first, pride over time." A plausible robot-specific root: the
  founding expedition itself required an all-robot crew specifically because "human lives judged too risky to
  spend on an unknown crossing" — meaning quiet, unacknowledged risk-bearing is, literally, the originating act
  of Byrd's robot population, predating and plausibly modeling the whole city's later self-sufficiency ethic
  rather than merely inheriting it.

### 3. Locality as Identity — What Makes This City's Robots Recognizable

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** #19–20 already establish that human residential concentration skews toward
  the upper/near-surface levels while robot residential and functional concentration extends genuinely
  deeper — since robots need no light-cycle management, no oxygen, and no fast-evacuation margin. A Byrd
  robot is plausibly recognizable by comfort at depths a human resident would rarely if ever need to occupy —
  a spatial, vertical marker genuinely distinct from every prior city's marker-type (thermal, wind-load,
  archive-domain, frame-customization, maker's-mark).

### 4. Generational Identity (Gen/Mark) — Local Flavor

- **[Adapted, Deep]** Byrd is one of only two currently active fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturers in
  Tepenia (with Sinheung), extending the same Gen/Mark-pride logic already established there — but with a
  genuinely distinct local angle: Byrd's Chamber Works survived the Amundsen Tower's collapse specifically
  because of independent, plausibly geothermal power (Finding 2), while the grid-dependent Mountain Pass site
  did not. A robot fabricated at Byrd plausibly carries pride tied to that survival specifically — built by a
  chamber that endured when a comparable one elsewhere didn't — distinct from Sinheung's "built to an earned,
  externally-designed schematic" angle.

### 5. Religion & Belief — Robot-Specific Local Practice

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** St. Richard's Marker is already a fully-established cross-guild ritual site
  (Finding 13) — people visit quietly before uncertain undertakings, not something this pass needed to invent.
  See Cross-Reference Synthesis for the genuinely new robot-specific angle this pass adds.
- **[Directly-inherited, Deep] — corrected 2026-08-10.** This bullet originally framed Earth as a first-ever
  informal proposal; it's actually Byrd's real pre-existing pick, **Solar symbol Mars + Robo-Element Earth**
  (`city-symbol-pairs.md`), simply missed by this pass's own search. The reasoning below held up regardless.
  **Earth** fits, deliberately grounded differently than Davis's own Earth pick (which read metaphorically, as
  an unshowy-support-role anxiety): here it's literal — a city built physically into the
  earth, drawing power from the earth itself (geothermal independence). Earth's negative valence — stagnation,
  paralysis, hoarding, smothering — maps concretely onto two already-established Byrd-specific facts: the
  Third Sector Seal (a literal sealed-off, structurally paralyzed zone, closed after measurable ice-sheet
  drift and never reopened) and Finding 3's own conclusion that deep pre-exile history, with no centralized
  wayfinding system, effectively "reads as legend" — a genuine kind of historical paralysis, not just a gap.

### 6. Consumption & Vice Culture — Local Siligel/Coolant/Glitch-Coolant Flavor

- **[Adapted, Surface]** §10c is confirmed thin ("modest, scaled to a genuinely isolated, struggling city") —
  real room to expand, grounded in already-established infrastructure: a dedicated fermentation/preservation
  craft facility (#69) explicitly parallels real-world isolation-driven fermentation traditions. Byrd's
  siligel culture plausibly draws real character from this — a necessity-born fermentation-and-preservation
  craft identity, giving §10c's "modest" description actual substance rather than leaving it a placeholder.
- **[Directly-inherited, Surface]** Glitch-Coolant is already confirmed in the original Tepenia-wide canon:
  Byrd is explicitly named (with Rothera) as a working-class/potency city — limited selection, noticeably
  stronger effect, "a direct, unpretentious blue-collar drinking culture, not a refined one."

### 7. Sexuality & Courtship — Local Customs

- **[Adapted, Deep]** Distinct from every prior city's courtship finding: given Byrd's tunnel architecture
  provides "genuine and structurally reinforced" privacy (§6d) — privacy here is literally spatial, not just
  social — a plausible courtship signal is being given genuine access to a partner's own private depth-level
  section, an actual physical threshold-crossing gesture rather than a purely social or verbal one. This makes
  particular sense at Byrd specifically, where private space is architecturally scarce and protected by design,
  giving the gesture real, structural weight a purely social equivalent elsewhere wouldn't carry.

### 8. Vernacular Language — Robot-Specific Speech Markers

- **[Adapted, Surface]** The Arrastradora→Rastra naming drift (a specific vehicle name worn down, at *national*
  scale, into a general term for small orbital craft) is already documented canon. A plausible local-scale
  echo: each of the Five Guilds plausibly maintains its own internal trade vocabulary, with select terms
  crossing over into general Byrd civic speech the same way "Rastra" itself crossed from a specific machine's
  name into national vocabulary — Byrd's vernacular signature is layered (five guild-jargons feeding a shared
  civic register) rather than singular, mirroring a mechanism the city's own history already demonstrates at a
  larger scale.

### 9. Arcanet Etiquette — Local Network Behavior

- **[Directly-inherited, Deep]** §24 confirms tenuous subnet-hub connectivity, sharpened by the aviation
  route's collapse.
- **[Adapted, Deep]** Byrd is the only city encountered in this entire run with genuinely zero pre-Tower
  digital contact of any kind — being the sole city in its own subnet, it had no subnet-mate to even exchange
  fast local contact with, unlike every other city processed so far. A plausible local expression: once
  national connectivity arrived, Byrd's Arcanet culture treats every external contact with unusually equal
  weight, since there was never a "close" tier of contact to take for granted the way subnet-hub cities have —
  no near/far distinction baked into the city's habits the way it is everywhere else.

### 10. Surface Culture — Robot-Specific Markers

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Surface]** Given "The Locked Ward" incident (a technician breaking under total
  light deprivation after 11 months at depth, found trying to dig toward "the actual sky") establishes genuine
  sensory deprivation as a real, documented hazard at Byrd — and given The Lake is already the city's
  confirmed emotional landmark specifically because it's "the only place... with light, color, and movement
  that isn't engineered" (Finding 56) — deep-level robots plausibly incorporate small, deliberately non-
  utilitarian light or color elements into their own person, echoing the Lake's significance at individual
  scale, as both a genuine wellness practice and a recognizable marker of who works the deepest levels.
- **No music finding proposed this pass** (Honesty Check): nothing in the gathered material establishes an
  equivalent civic performance tradition to anchor one against (unlike Casey's Splinters or Zhongshan's Long
  Winter Suite) — the sensory-enrichment finding above already covers the closest available material without
  needing to force a separate performance-art angle.

## PART II — Cross-Reference Synthesis Findings (Step 4)

- **[Emergent, robot-only, Deep]** — *Input categories combined: A (§17's all-robot founding expedition) + C
  (Finding 13, St. Richard's Marker) + Historical Vignette (Explorer Voss, the founding crew's last living
  member) + Robot Universals Ch.11 (kinship)*
  Byrd is the only city encountered in this entire run where the founding myth has literally zero human
  witnesses. This makes St. Richard's Marker something subtly different depending on who's visiting it: for
  humans, the founding story is inherited entirely secondhand, however deeply felt; for robots — and
  especially the rare few who actually studied under Voss, not merely spoken with him, since he's an ordinary,
  easy-to-reach resident otherwise — it commemorates something only their own kind directly lived through. A
  genuine inversion of the usual pattern (founding myths that are human-centric, with robots adapting into
  them after the fact).

- **[Adapted, Deep]** — *Input categories combined: A (§5's founding-descendant/later-arrival fault line,
  already named as Byrd's most load-bearing unresolved tension) + C (Finding 7, Finding 44 — the elevator-
  chokepoint-vs-formal-administration political split) + §17 (the all-robot founding crew)*
  Worth flagging directly to the developer, given the Community Infrastructure file's own note tying this
  fault line to DLC2's still-undeveloped central crisis: since the founding crew was entirely robot, the
  "founding-descendant" side of Byrd's central demographic tension plausibly carries a distinctly robot
  flavor — founding-descendant standing running differently for robots who actually studied under a member of
  the literal founding crew (a real, earned mentee relationship, not mere presence or a passing conversation),
  versus human residents whose own founding-era ancestors weren't present at the actual founding moment at all
  (which took place before the settlement existed for anyone to arrive to). This could sharpen or reframe the
  existing fault line into something more specific and more usable than a generic "old families vs. newcomers"

## Closing Check — The Swap Test (Step 6)

**Swap Test result: Pass, via a genuinely different comparison than any city so far.** Byrd has no same-subnet
sibling at all — the only single-city subnet in Tepenia — so Step 6 had to reach into already-completed cities
from other subnets rather than a natural same-subnet neighbor. The closest structural comparison available is
Kunlun (extreme self-contained isolation, a population that concentrates by physical extremity — altitude
there, depth here). Checked directly: Kunlun is one of only two robots-only cities in Tepenia; Byrd is not,
and its whole founding-descendant/later-arrival tension (this pass's central finding) depends specifically on
having a real, demographically significant human population that Kunlun's own robots-only status makes
impossible to compare against directly. Kunlun's population also self-selected *into* devotion and expertise
from the very start; Byrd's self-sufficiency ethic explicitly formed the opposite way — necessity first, pride
only afterward. No finding above reads as generic "isolated extreme-environment city" content that happened to
get filed under Byrd's name — each depends on facts (the all-robot founding crew with no human witness, the
Five Guildhalls, the vertical depth-based population split, the zero-pre-Tower-contact isolation profile) that
are concretely, specifically Byrd's own.
