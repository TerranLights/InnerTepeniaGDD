## The developer's vision

**A working port, alongside the established airfield — both equally important.** Shipyards span the "inner" side of the island, facing mainland Antarctica. Humans and robots work the docks together, loading and unloading ships arriving and departing regardless of weather — rain, clear skies, or snow, the work continues. Marambio receives from South America and ships onward across the Weddell Sea to the Atlantic coastal cities. Maritime commerce is a major, ongoing part of daily life here, alongside (not replacing) the city's established aviation-hub role — the developer's own "ground-level" mental image just happens to gravitate toward the docks rather than the runway, but both are confirmed equally central to the city's actual character.

**Life on the docks:** During the rare quiet downtime between ship dockings, humans and robots take smoke breaks together. Many of the human dockworkers are in relationships with a robot they also work alongside — a direct, lived version of the Bonded Lattice/human-robot romance themes already established project-wide. Some humans are here specifically because robot society treats them better than human society ever did. Everyone is, in the end, just trying to build a life and a place for themselves.

**South America as the Palmer subnet's Upper Earth shipping partner — resolves a real, standing project gap.** There was already an established framework of real-world nations serving as Upper Earth shipping partners for three of Tepenia's coastal regions (South Africa for the Halley subnet, New Zealand for the Ross region, Australia for the Dumont d'Urville Sea and Mirny/Mawson coast) — but the Palmer/Peninsula subnet never had one confirmed. **South America now fills that gap**, flowing through the Weddell Sea corridor.

**Marambio and Esperanza — confirmed equally important, complementary pieces of the same corridor.** Esperanza already had an established Weddell Sea trans-shipment role (a documented Belgrano–Palmer City trade route, referenced through a character's piano shipments). Marambio's own maritime/shipyard identity sits alongside that as an equally important part of the same overall corridor, not a competing or redundant one — likely Marambio as the larger-scale bulk maritime commerce node, Esperanza handling more specific/documented trans-shipment traffic, though the exact division of labor between the two is still open for future refinement.
