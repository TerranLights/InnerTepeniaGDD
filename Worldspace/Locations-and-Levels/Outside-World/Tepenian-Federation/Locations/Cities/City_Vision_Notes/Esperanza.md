## The developer's vision

**Family and working life:** Fathers take their children fishing at the island's edges. Many fathers work in the mines on a rotational shift schedule — weeks away at a time, then a week or two home to rest and be with family — with robots serving as the primary at-home caregivers during those absences. Young people attend school taught primarily by robot teachers, with occasional human teachers who, in the early interwar period specifically, could give genuine firsthand accounts of life in Upper Earth before the Falkland Treaty. Robots and humans work together maintaining the infrastructure that houses and raises the next generation — a generation explicitly being prepared to push further into Antarctica and continue the continent's settlement, "in the name of robots and humans hand-in-hand."

**Mining — resolves the "economy beyond childcare" gap:** Esperanza's mines produce materials for siligel synthesis, raw materials for building, and raw materials for fabrication/manufacture — feeding directly into Tepenia's broader industrial supply chain, not just a local resource.

**Family structure and reproduction — a project-wide fact, not just Esperanza's:** Some women did immigrate to Antarctica, but the overwhelming majority of human exiles/immigrants were men. Children are predominantly born via **artificial womb technology**, which by this era has finally become a viable, normalized option. This is why a single-father-plus-robot-caregiver household (father working rotational mining shifts, robot as primary at-home parent) reads as a normal Esperanza family unit rather than an unusual one — it isn't a gap in the traditional family structure, it *is* the traditional family structure here, grown directly out of the exile's actual demographics.

**Human teachers with firsthand pre-Treaty memory — confirmed as an early-interwar-only phenomenon.** This naturally fades as the founding generation dies without a further generation of grandparents to relay it — human lifespans are only marginally longer than real-world ones despite medical advances; there's no dramatic life-extension for humans in Tepenia. Robots are the opposite case: capable of persisting for centuries, especially with cyber-mechanical maintenance (self-administered in the most capable cases — Griselda Alejandra Chávez, previously only a trade contact in Esperanza's records, is confirmed as exactly this kind of long-lived, self-maintaining robot). Practically, this means **robots, not human family lines, are the actual continuous carriers of institutional memory across the interwar period** — human "living memory" of a given era is generational and genuinely disappears; robot memory doesn't have to.

**The colonization mission — softer than initially framed, confirmed as real but not exclusive.** Esperanza's founding purpose does include preparing its children to push further into Antarctica and continue its settlement — but this isn't Esperanza acting as *the* sole incubator feeding all of Tepenia's expansion. Most Tepenian cities generally raise their own children to continue their own civilizational legacy; Esperanza's specific emphasis on frontier-preparation is a genuine part of its identity, but one contribution among many, not a unique role no other city shares.

## Project-wide facts established this session (bigger than Esperanza — flagged for wider tracking)

These came out of an Esperanza-specific conversation but apply across all of Tepenia, and are logged in `TODO.md` and project memory for that reason:

1. **Human exile immigration to Tepenia skewed heavily male.** Some women did immigrate, but they were a minority among human arrivals.
2. **Artificial womb technology is a viable, normalized reproductive method in Tepenia by this era.** This is very likely the primary mechanism by which Tepenia's human population has sustained and grown itself across a quarter-millennium of one-way exile, given the gender imbalance above.
3. **Human lifespans in Tepenia are only marginally longer than real-world human lifespans** — no dramatic medical life-extension for humans specifically.
4. **Robots can live for centuries**, especially with cyber-mechanical maintenance (self-administered in the most capable individuals, e.g. Griselda Alejandra Chávez) — making robots, not human genealogical lines, the primary carriers of continuous lived memory and institutional history across Tepenian society.
