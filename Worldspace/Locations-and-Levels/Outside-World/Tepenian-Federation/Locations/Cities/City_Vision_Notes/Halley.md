## The developer's vision

**No shipping docks, no airstrip — a deliberate design choice, corrected directly into `Specs/Halley.md`.** The existing file had assumed maritime trade and access windows as part of Halley's own economy — the developer's vision overturns this with solid internal logic: a city whose entire architecture exists because the ground itself moves at 400-700m/year would gain nothing from fixed maritime or aviation infrastructure the ice would simply carry away from any useful position over time. Instead, all resupply is overland. Ships reach **Belgrano or Sanay** — whichever passage happens to be open at the time; the two receive shipping interchangeably depending on seasonal ice conditions, not a fixed hierarchy — and goods are trucked in via a connector road linking to Hwy 7.

**This meaningfully raises Halley's actual vulnerability, not lowers it.** With no sea access of its own, Halley has no maritime fallback if its overland routes (Hwy 59 south to Amundsen Station, or the Hwy 7 connector to Belgrano/Sanay) are ever compromised — a genuinely more fragile supply position than a typical coastal city's, precisely because the ice shelf's mobility is the same reason it was never given dock/airstrip infrastructure in the first place. This has been written directly into the Specs file's Gameplay Notes and Hardcore Mode sections.

**The relocation mechanism, made more specific.** Each module's ski-mounted platform is fitted with tracks that dig into the ice and slowly, continuously propel the structure — moving it deliberately toward the mainland to stay ahead of the ice shelf's own natural seaward drift, rather than waiting for a crisis relocation event. This is an active, ongoing system, not passive towing-when-needed.

**Daily municipal transport:** humans and robots move people and goods around the city by snowmobile.

## Halley is not the actual Arcanet subnet nexus — resolved

**Even though the subnet is named after Halley, Halley itself is not the technical Arcanet relay nexus.** This is deliberate, not an inconsistency: a city that risks eventually calving off into the ocean is a poor place to keep a subnet's permanent critical relay infrastructure.

**Resolved, same session: the nexus is at Sanay**, installed into Vesleskarvet's stable bedrock. The naming makes sense specifically because of who did what: Halley's own residents personally, physically traveled to Sanay and did the hands-on construction labor of building the nexus themselves — the same kind of direct infrastructure work Michelle Stanton is known for elsewhere in Tepenia — while Sanay's own residents were occupied running the ports and managing the shipping lanes to South Africa. Labor and namesake credit went to Halley; the physical nexus and its resulting strategic importance sit at Sanay. Corrected directly into `Specs/Halley.md`, `Local_Cultures/Halley_Subnet/Halley.md`, `Specs/Sanay.md`, and `Local_Cultures/Halley_Subnet/Sanay.md`.
