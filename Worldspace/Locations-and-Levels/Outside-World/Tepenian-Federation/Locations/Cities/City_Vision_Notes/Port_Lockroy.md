## Facts and factions presented at the start of this session

Recap of what was already established going in (see the two files above for full detail): Goudier Island, Wiencke Island, Antarctic Peninsula. Palmer subnet. **Status: Damaged; partially operational** — survived via strategic irrelevance (heritage/museum city, no military/industrial value) plus probable conflation with adjacent Palmer City's strike zone. Unique military→museum→living-city founding progression (1944 Operation Tabarin → pre-exile heritage museum → exile city from 2564). Population (Census I) 63,338H/65,549R/128,887 combined — Tepenia's 2nd-smallest city. Composition: USA Primary; Germany/France/UK/Mexico/Brazil Significant; Argentina/Chile Notable (founding wave). Identity: "A City That Remembers Being Something Else" — never demolishes its own history, layers eras on top of each other instead; founding tension is temporal (living memory vs. heritage spectacle), not the usual founder-nation-vs-majority pattern.

**Factions:** The Archivists' Debate (second interwar) — Port Lockroy's position is "the archive is a way of life, not a project": it doesn't schedule preservation like Belgrano, advocate through it like Palmer City, or wait for endpoints like Scott — it just lives inside a place already three eras deep and keeps adding to it.

## The developer's vision

**The scene:** A gentle but gruff, grizzled old man with a big gray beard, in a sailor's coat and hat, finishing tending goats before handing off a package to a robot courier at the main postal office. He then picks up a hammer and chisel and continues a stonemasonry project he's been carving into a stone wall for some time — images of boats arriving, and a DNA helix rendered in electrons and cosmic strings. He smokes a pipe with tobacco imported from Argentina, since Antarctic hydroponics are still only in rudimentary stages this early in the second interwar period (the Falkland Treaty is still a recent memory). He muses that someday this place might become a hub of postal delivery — though he likely won't live to see it.

**Resolves an open question outright:** the post office is confirmed as a genuine, functioning civic institution (a robot courier actively picking up a package), not a preserved heritage artifact people just walk past.

**The man himself — a real notable figure, name TBD.** Confirmed: he does have a name, it just hasn't been decided yet. He's the strongest candidate so far for Port Lockroy's long-empty "Notable Figures" slot — a specific, textured person (goat-tender, postal contact, stonemason) rather than an anonymous archetype. Revisit when a name is ready.

**The stone wall — personal for him, but a strong future-landmark candidate.** Confirmed as a personal occupation-of-time project for this specific man, not something Port Lockroy is already known for citywide — but the developer agrees it could plausibly become a genuine landmark later (see the proposed arc below, which suggests exactly that).

## Proposed development arc across the second interwar period (2564–2812) — developer-approved

Built collaboratively in this session, starting from the developer's early-second interwar scene and working out how Port Lockroy's character plausibly changes over the following ~250 years. Confirmed as good direction; not yet written into the formal spec files.

**Early second interwar (~2564–2650s) — where the developer's scene sits.** Founding generation adapting the old 1944 buildings. Small-scale subsistence (goats). Reliance on Upper Earth imports (tobacco, etc.) for anything hydroponics can't yet handle. The postal office is small and local, plausibly handling correspondence and parcels moving along Hwy 1 between the handful of early Palmer subnet cities — there's no orbital infrastructure yet to route anything through instead.

**Mid second interwar (~2650s–2750s).** Hydroponics matures Tepenia-wide; Amundsen Tower opens orbital access (~2629–2639, overlapping the tail of the early period). Port Lockroy's postal function specializes rather than just staying locally useful: where Rothera's established identity is industrial (raw materials into manufactured infrastructure, moved as freight), Port Lockroy becomes that same corridor's correspondence counterpart — letters, small personal packages, and (fitting its whole "living memory" identity) historical archive shipments moving between Palmer subnet cities. Rothera moves the materials; Port Lockroy moves the words. This is how the old man's "someday, a hub of postal delivery" line actually comes true — just not in his own lifetime, exactly as he suspected it wouldn't.

**The stone wall's own arc:** rather than staying one man's personal project, it becomes a multi-generational communal artifact — other residents add their own panels over time, so the wall itself visually *is* the palimpsest identity (ships arriving, then whatever comes next, then whatever comes after that). By the time of the Long Night War, it's a genuine Notable Local Landmark, not incidental carving.

**Late second interwar, approaching 2812.** The postal-hub role is real and fully functioning. A nice detail proposed and approved: Argentine tobacco stays the preferred import even once local hydroponic alternatives exist — no longer necessity, just taste, the same way a "real" imported version of something often keeps its cultural cachet after a domestic substitute becomes available. Memory and preference over practicality, consistent with everything else about this city.

**The war itself.** Port Lockroy's established status is "damaged; partially operational" — meaning whatever postal-hub future actually got built took real damage in the Long Night War but wasn't erased. He didn't live to see it happen; it did happen; and then it was partially broken again — a shape the city, being what it is, would remember and account for rather than paper over.
