# Belgrano — Developer Vision Notes

**Purpose:** A running record of the developer's own creative vision for this city's **second interwar-era, living culture** — distinct from `Specs/Belgrano.md` (established facts, including its eventual post-war fate) and `Local_Cultures/Halley_Subnet/Belgrano.md` (32-section post-culture spec). This file captures open questions asked, answers given, and anything newly established through direct conversation, so it can be referenced later and folded into the more formal files when ready.

**Session date:** 2026-07-04

## A structural correction made during this session

Earlier in this session, Belgrano's presentation leaned heavily on "the Decline" and "the Boneyard Times" — a post-war deterioration Belgrano suffered *after* surviving the Long Night War intact. The developer caught that this was a dangling remnant that didn't belong in a second interwar-focused vision session: "the Decline" is inherently post-war content, and its presence had been baked directly into `Local_Cultures/Halley_Subnet/Belgrano.md`'s core living-culture identity section — the only Tepenian city whose file blended these two eras together, breaking from the standard methodology every other city's file follows.

**Fixed the same session, in full:** `Local_Cultures/Halley_Subnet/Belgrano.md` was rewritten section by section — the ruins/Decline framing had spread through nearly the entire 32-section document (Social Contract, Architecture, Cuisine, Fashion, Music, Arts, Sensory Profile, Division of Industry, Human-Robot Relations, Robot Culture, Religious/Philosophical Landscape, Counterculture, Private Life, Political Character, Relationship to Upper Earth, Relationship to Other Cities, Arcanet Culture, Municipal Holidays, Visitor Experience, Visitor-to-Resident Transition, Notable Landmarks — not just the core identity section). Every one of these was rewritten to describe Belgrano's living second interwar culture. Renamed from "The Airbase That Outlived Itself" to **"The Airbase That Never Stood Down."** The Decline content was moved to a new, dedicated faction, **"The Boneyard Times — Belgrano Communities,"** in `Worldspace/Factions/City_Origin_Factions_PostWar_Refugee.md` (renamed from an initial "The Bone Yard" per the developer's later direction — see below), alongside the other post-war faction material. This means the "tension" raised earlier in this same session (between the developer's vibrant-downtown vision and the established post-war severity) was a false problem — there's nothing to reconcile, since this file is about Belgrano's living second interwar city, not its post-war ruin. Section 32 (Diaspora Character in Concordia) was deliberately left alone, since every city's Section 32 describes its post-war Concordia diaspora by design — that's not a violation of the same methodology, it's the section doing its actual job.

## The developer's vision

**A street-level snapshot of one specific area** of the living, second interwar city — the developer's own creative process works by placing their attention at one particular spot and describing what's visible there (the same pattern as Princess Elisabeth's session). This shows: warehouse fleets with coastline/shipping-dock access, industrial yards, and an active downtown — bars, music venues, places of business. Large parts of the waterside infrastructure are rusted but not structurally compromised (wear from Belgrano's harsh Weddell Sea climate, not decline). Humans walk the streets with their robot wives/girlfriends.

**Resolves the thin "Music" section:** Garage Rock is the dominant genre heard from open windows and doors of restaurants and other social venues, among others — raw, DIY, unpolished, a natural fit for a hard-working, function-first city.

**A genuine garage/mechanic subculture is one of the outer city's staples.** Engine mechanics are common, everyday work, and a modest number of specialists know how to work on Rastras specifically — a direct continuation of Belgrano having invented the vehicle for the Byrd Expedition in the first place. As a matter of general local culture, for anything with an engine, there's reliably somebody in Belgrano who can repair it. Written directly into the "Export Culture" and "Arts & Aesthetics" sections.

**Robots in Belgrano are overwhelmingly involved in industrial work** — aviation logistics, port/shipping operations, manufacturing, fabrication — with maintenance/repair as a significant but secondary strand, not the dominant one. Corrected in "Robot-Specific Culture" accordingly.

**Demonym correction:** the local Spanish variant is **"belgranteco/belgranteca,"** not "belgralteco/belgralteca" as originally recorded — fixed in `Specs/Belgrano.md`. "Belgranian" (the English form) is unchanged.

**A clarifying note added throughout:** "mate" (the traditional Argentine herbal drink, and its associated shared-gourd social ritual) now carries an explicit disambiguation note wherever it appears, since it reads very differently as an English word.

**A genuine cross-DLC quest chain, established 2026-07-04.** Somewhere in Belgrano, there's a garage/warehouse (discoverable through at least three sources, or direct access if already known) holding a pile of scrap. Examining it with 6+ Investigation reveals a specifically-shaped aircraft part, auto-added as a quest item; without enough Investigation, a Lockpicking-gated back room holds an import/export manifest with a note about "airplane parts" left "on the side wall," which lets the player understand the part's purpose without the direct check. Carrying this item into DLC 4 (Mawson) lets the player skip the 10 Calc/10 Inv stat-gate entirely on the Sinheung plane-repair sidequest that offers an alternate route to Dome Fuji — just place the part in the engine. This is a new, distinct cross-DLC design pattern from the established "Cross-DLC Survival Gifts" chain (which feeds DLC 1's Kendra rescue specifically) — this one connects two subnet DLCs directly. Written into `TODO.md`, `Specs/Belgrano.md`, `Specs/Sinheung.md`, and `Specs/Dome_Fuji.md`.

**The mechanic subculture connects directly to the post-war Boneyard Times, too:** the same garage/Rastra-repair competence became one of the few genuinely load-bearing skills once the founding institution was gone — see "The Boneyard Times" faction in `City_Origin_Factions_PostWar_Refugee.md`.
