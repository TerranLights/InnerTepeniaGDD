## Resolved 2026-07-05 — renamed to Abowasa, plus real-world grounding

**Renamed from "Aboa" to "Abowasa"; demonym "Abowasian."** The real-world detail behind this: Aboa (Finnish, est. 1988) and Wasa (Swedish, est. 1989) are two genuinely *separate* national research stations, only ~200m apart in the Kraul Mountains/Vestfjella — close enough to be discussed together (sometimes as "Aboa-Wasa" or the Nordenskiöld Base) without ever technically being one station. The new name reflects this more precisely than the old lore's loose "joint station" framing: two distinct communities, right on top of each other, naturally merging into one city given the tiny distance.

**Both real stations were built for year-round occupation but only ever staffed seasonally** — an operational/budgetary choice (Antarctic summer, Nov-Feb, is when field research is actually viable; a year-round staffed presence was never worth the cost), not a structural limitation. This matters for the Tepenian city: the infrastructure itself was never the limiting factor on permanent habitation, only the funding/staffing decision — which stopped applying the moment Finnish and Swedish exiles needed an actual permanent home rather than a seasonal research post.

**Confirmed: on land, not an island.** Basen nunatak is a rock outcrop protruding through the ice sheet, part of mainland Antarctica, ~130km inland from the coast — not surrounded by water the way an island is.

**Flagged gaps still open:** no notable figures; whether the Finnish-Swedish dynamic ever merged, stayed distinct, or formed a hybrid identity; why Abowasa specifically survived partially rather than fully; economy beyond a generic sector breakdown; two placeholder holiday names ("Turku Remembrance," "The Joint Founding").
