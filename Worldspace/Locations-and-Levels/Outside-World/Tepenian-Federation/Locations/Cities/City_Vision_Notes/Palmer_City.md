## The developer's vision

**Sensory/atmospheric character:** A web of interconnecting streets full of cultural buildings — dance halls with live bands playing jazz, rockabilly, and electroswing; casinos loud with chips and fortunes won and lost; live concerts spanning a range of genres. Streets lush with rain — sometimes delicate, sometimes pounding, with occasional sun breaking through. This significantly enriches the existing "jazz as civic soundtrack" framing: the *living* music scene is broader than jazz alone (jazz is the founding touchstone; rockabilly and electroswing are part of the ongoing, present-tense nightlife identity) — electroswing in particular resonates thematically, an electronic/swing hybrid genre fitting a robot-and-human society built partly on found jazz records.

**Economic model — resolved:** Palmer City's economy runs substantially on Upper Earth tourism revenue: casinos, nightlife, and entertainment draw money from an Upper Earth that officially disdains Tepenia but privately indulges in it. This is a genuine, satisfying partial answer to the long-flagged, previously-unresolved `Upper_Earth_Trade_Justification` open question (why would Upper Earth do business with "hated" Tepenians at all) — not trade born of necessity, but hypocrisy: public disdain, private vice tourism.

**Physical arrival route for Upper Earth visitors:** The Drake Passage–equivalent strait between South America and the Peninsula is treated as one of the most dangerous stretches of water on the planet, so Upper Earth visitors travel by air or a circuitous sea route instead. Arrival point: **Machu Picchu Base**, on the far side of King George Island (distinct from Sejong, the actual Tepenian city elsewhere on the same island) — functioning specifically as the customs/immigration gateway for Upper Earth visitors, not a residential city in its own right. Visitors not seeking permanent immigration are shuttled along a predetermined corridor to a seaport, then cross calmer waters between King George Island and Anvers Island to reach Palmer City itself. Visa-on-arrival stays are documented and tracked to prevent overstaying by anyone not formally immigrating.

**Who actually comes down:** Broadly accessible, not exclusively an ultra-rich playground — closer to the real Vegas/Atlantic City/New Orleans/St. Petersburg model than a private resort. The ultra-wealthy do come looking for thrills, but anyone able to make the journey was welcome, on the implicit understanding that they'd obey Tepenian law while present — a real assertion of Tepenian sovereignty over Upper Earth visitors on Tepenian soil. Like New Orleans/St. Petersburg, there's "some sort of a diversionary pastime for everyone," not a single narrow experience.

**Open secret status — resolved:** This tourism is a definite, widely-known open secret on Upper Earth's side. Nobody officially talks about it, but it happens, and people know it happens.

## The inciting incident of the Long Night War — established this session

**This is the single most significant thing established in this conversation** — not just a Palmer City detail, but the actual spark of Tepenia's entire war, previously undefined anywhere in the project. Full account, as given:

A very well-connected Upper Earth political diplomat was in Palmer City for "business" (i.e. its tourism/entertainment economy). He did not recognize robot personhood or sovereign rights — despite robots holding confirmed legal personhood on Upper Earth since April 27, 2318 (the Jeju-do ruling) — and in a closed-quarters setting with no witnesses, attempted to use a gynoid as property for his own gratification, denying her personhood outright. She defended herself within the fast-moving circumstances of the assault and, in doing so, accidentally killed him. The geopolitical fallout from his death escalated rapidly, and is the actual chain of events that led to the Long Night War (also referred to as **"the Midnight War"** — an alternate name established in this same conversation).

**Confirmed, locked in:**
- The core premise above — personhood-denial, attempted sexual assault, self-defense, accidental death, geopolitical escalation to war — is settled canon as of this session.
- **The dueling-narrative structure is confirmed:** among Upper Earth, the diplomat's version wins — the official story is framed around his death, not what he did to provoke it. Among Tepenians, the innocent robot's story wins — she is understood, without ambiguity, as a person who defended herself against an assault. Neither side's public narrative is neutral, the same way the Planetary Split Brain leaves every subnet holding its own version of history — except this is a nation-vs-nation stake, not an inter-subnet one, and arguably the most consequential dueling narrative in the entire timeline.
- **She will have a name, and everyone in future-Tepenia will know it** — she's confirmed as a genuine household name/figure in Tepenian cultural memory, not an anonymous symbol. This is very likely the answer to Palmer City's long-flagged, explicitly-TBD "Notable Figures" gap.

**Still genuinely open, flagged for future design (not decided, not guessed at):**
- **Her name and full identity** — not yet figured out.
- **Her fate after the incident** — whether she stayed in Palmer City until its destruction, was relocated/protected, became a specific kind of symbol (martyr, rallying point, cautionary tale) in Tepenian consciousness, or her fate is itself unknown/unresolved as part of the story's weight — not yet figured out.
- **The diplomat's name and nationality** — not yet figured out, though his nationality could matter for how the specific geopolitical fallout played out (which Upper Earth nation(s) escalated tensions in response).
