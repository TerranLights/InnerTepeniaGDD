## Who Lives Here, and Why

USA and Canada sit co-Primary (re-tiered 2026-07-06 to match Byrd's actual founding-operator heritage), with Australia, Japan, South Korea, and China at Significant. Beneath that sits a genuinely enormous Notable tier — 34 nations, nearly matching Palmer City's own diversity — inherited one step removed from Palmer City's own population overflow, riding along with the Framheim/Little America redistribution that gave Byrd its first population figures at all. The result is a strange, earned tension: a city whose founding character is tight, expedition-minded, and self-sufficient, now home to nearly the same demographic breadth as Tepenia's single most diverse city.

## What It Feels Like

"Built From Below, Then Left Alone" names Byrd's whole story in one line. Byrd Station was already buried and abandoned by the time exiles found it — there was no surface structure to discover, only old maps and transport manifests. Reaching it took a genuine three-settlement effort: Belgrano built Tepenia's first native tracked overland vehicles (the "Arrastradoras," later worn down to "Rastra") specifically for the crossing, with Palmer City and Rothera shipping supporting records across the Weddell Sea to sharpen the expedition's heading. An all-robot crew drove the crossing itself — the risk was judged too great for humans — and found not a station but a field of compacted snow over buried infrastructure. The community built downward before it ever built up, making Byrd unique among Tepenian cities in having been founded underground before it existed on the surface at all.

That inversion still defines Byrd today. The surface reads as vast icefields with huge warehouses and a massive trucking depot as the only real landscape features, giant elevators leading down to where the actual city lives — an underground scale that's staggering on first encounter for what looks, from above, like a comparatively small settlement. Down there, huge mechanized fabrication plants and import/export dispatch offices define the real economy: **Byrd is one of only two currently-active Cradle sites in all of Tepenia**, manufacturing the fabrication-synthesis chambers that physically create robots, built to a schematic designed at Neumayer (Halley subnet) and shipped out via the same two-directional overland network that carries everything else Byrd makes — Hwy 1 north toward the Peninsula and, beyond it, South America; Hwy 22 east across the entire continent to the Zhongshan/Sinheung/Shirayuki tri-junction, passing directly through the Amundsen Station ruins along the way. Byrd's own aviation fleet, once its only connection to the rest of Tepenia for *people*, broke down and could not be repaired — the city has been unreachable by air since, sealing off its own refugee population in Concordia as a closed community with no new arrivals and no departures. What Byrd cannot do for people, it still does for goods: this is a city that never stopped shipping.

### Primary: Derinkuyu, Turkey

An ancient 18-level underground city that held up to 20,000 people, built with genuine planned civic logic organized entirely by depth.

**What this gives Byrd:** the single tightest structural match available anywhere in the real world — direct, concrete proof that an underground settlement at real civic scale, with real planned social organization rather than mere shelter, isn't speculative fiction but a thing humans have actually built and lived in.

### Primary: Ruhr Valley (Ruhrgebiet), Germany

A dense, interconnected industrial mega-region where coal, steel, and chemical manufacturing all depend on each other, anchored by Duisburg's real inland port handling over 40 million tons of cargo yearly.

**What this gives Byrd:** the clearest real-world model for a genuinely *integrated* fabrication economy — multiple manufacturing sectors feeding each other in the same physical space, exactly the shape Byrd's own mechanized fabrication plants, chamber works, and import/export dispatch offices need to read as one interlocking system rather than separate businesses that happen to share a city.

### Secondary: Coober Pedy, Australia

A real, living underground-dwelling town, genuinely multicultural with 45+ nationalities among its miners.

**What this gives Byrd:** direct precedent for the specific combination the population re-derivation accidentally produced — a tight, functional underground settlement that is nonetheless genuinely diverse, resolving what might otherwise read as a contradiction between Byrd's founding character and its actual demographic breadth.

### Secondary: Shenzhen, China

A complete raw-material-to-shipped-product pipeline in one region — 15,000+ factories, $340B in annual shipped goods, top-5 global port and air cargo access.

**What this gives Byrd:** a real precedent for exactly the kind of end-to-end manufacturing-and-distribution identity Byrd's own economy is built around, minus the air cargo access Byrd itself no longer has — reinforcing how much weight its two remaining overland routes actually carry.

### Secondary: Jamshedpur, India

A planned integrated industrial township built directly around Asia's first integrated steel mill, with captive raw-material mines feeding the plant directly on-site.

**What this gives Byrd:** the clearest available precedent for pairing Byrd's own established resource-extraction sector directly with its fabrication plants — raw material and finished product created in the same place, by design, rather than shipped in from elsewhere first.

### Secondary: Chelyabinsk, Russia

"Tankograd" — entire industries relocated wholesale into one city during WWII, producing 18,000 tanks and 48,500 tank engines during the war; heavy industry still roughly 40% of gross municipal product today.

**What this gives Byrd:** a real precedent for a single city becoming a nation's critical manufacturing chokepoint under wartime pressure and simply never fully returning to peacetime scale afterward — a fitting historical echo for Byrd's own post-war role as one of only two places left that can manufacture a robot from nothing.

## I. The District/Faction Structure Byrd Needs — Five Guilds Built Directly from Its Own Established Economy

**Proposed, directly answering the standing `TODO.md` design requirement:** rather than inventing factions from nothing, the most earned structure comes straight out of Byrd's own already-established Division of Industry (mechanized fabrication 30%, import/export & dispatch 25%, infrastructure/snow management 15%, resource extraction 15%, salvage/aviation maintenance 10%, other 5%). Each major sector becomes a genuine guild with its own interests, giving the city real internal politics without requiring a single new economic fact.

- **The Fabricators' Guild** (mechanized fabrication) — the city's identity core, running the mechanized fabrication plants and, within a specifically reinforced sub-section, the Chamber Works itself. The most nationally consequential faction, given Byrd's status as one of only two active Cradle sites, and plausibly the most politically confident as a result.
- **The Dispatch Office** (import/export & dispatch) — controls the trucking depot and everything that physically enters or leaves Byrd via Hwy 1 and Hwy 22. A natural counterweight to the Fabricators' Guild: the Guild makes things, but the Dispatch Office decides how, when, and whether they actually reach anyone — real leverage in a city whose entire outside relevance depends on those two roads staying open.
- **The Foundation Keepers** (infrastructure/snow management) — the unglamorous but existentially necessary work of keeping the buried city from being reclaimed by its own snow load, and by extension, the faction that effectively controls access to the buried sections — which tunnels stay open, which get sealed, which get reinforced for expansion.
- **The Prospectors' Circle** (resource extraction/geology) — works the West Antarctic geology feeding the Fabricators' Guild's raw material needs, and by the nature of that work, the faction with the clearest, most immediate read on whether the West Antarctic Ice Sheet's instability is a background condition or an active, worsening one.
- **The Grounded Wings** (salvage/aviation maintenance) — the smallest and most quietly consequential faction, tending a fleet that cannot fly and, per Section III below, plausibly holding the clearest institutional memory of *why*.

**2nd-order effect:** this produces genuine, earned friction points without inventing new lore — a Fabricators-vs-Dispatch tension over who really runs Byrd's economy; a Foundation-Keepers-vs-Prospectors disagreement over whether to seal off or keep working unstable ground; and the Grounded Wings sitting on a piece of institutional knowledge the other four factions may each want revealed or suppressed for reasons of their own.

## II. What Byrd Manufactures Beyond Chambers: Heavy Machinery and Its Own Freight Fleet

**Proposed:** given the established Ruhr Valley/Jamshedpur-style integrated fabrication economy (raw material extracted on-site, fabricated in the same underground volume), Byrd's output beyond fabrication-synthesis chambers plausibly centers on heavy industrial machinery and, most significantly, its own overland freight vehicles — the Rastra-descended heavy haulers that actually carry everything Byrd makes out along Hwy 1 and Hwy 22. This is a fitting, earned closure of a loop already present in Byrd's own founding story: Belgrano built the original Arrastradoras to find Byrd; centuries later, Byrd itself plausibly became one of the primary sites still building and maintaining that same vehicle lineage, now serving as the literal, physical means by which Byrd's own goods reach the rest of the country.

---

## III. Why the Aircraft Could Not Be Repaired: A Materials Problem, Not a Skills Problem

**Proposed, without pre-empting DLC 2's own reserved central mystery:** Byrd's whole established identity is mechanized-fabrication competence — a city that can build a robot from nothing. An aircraft fleet this same city "could not" repair reads as a genuine irony worth resolving carefully rather than leaving as an unexplained skill gap. The most earned answer, consistent with Byrd's established character, is that the failure was never one of expertise — Byrd's engineers plausibly know exactly what's wrong — but of a specific material, fuel additive, or component that Byrd itself has never had the means to produce locally, and that used to arrive through a supply chain now severed. Given Framheim and Little America's own removal from canon as the aviation route's confirmed refueling stop, it's a reasonable, earned proposal that whatever Byrd's aircraft actually needed was tied to that same now-erased corridor, one way or another. This preserves Byrd's own competence identity rather than undermining it, and hands DLC 2 a genuinely different kind of central problem — not "can we fix it," but "can we still get what fixing it requires" — without deciding the specific substance of that problem here.

---

## IV. What Byrd Imports: Food and Variety, via Its Own Northern Highway

**Proposed:** Byrd's own established resource extraction covers minerals and raw material for its fabrication plants, but a population of nearly 400,000 people needs more dietary variety than a single isolated interior settlement can produce alone. Hwy 1's northern terminus at Esperanza — already established as home to the Basque baserri agricultural tradition — makes Esperanza's own farmed goods a genuinely earned answer to what Byrd receives rather than only sends. This turns Hwy 1 into a real two-way corridor: food and outside goods flowing south into Byrd, manufactured machinery, chambers, and minerals flowing north back out — consistent with the established fact that Esperanza's own harbor already handles trans-shipment for Belgrano's goods heading toward Palmer City, now serving a second, parallel role for Byrd's own supply line as well.

## V. St. Richard's Civic Site: A Marker at the Original Surface Encampment

**Proposed:** given the founding expedition's own established sequence — a small surface encampment first, before the tunnels were found and the community moved to build downward — the most earned site for St. Richard's veneration is that original encampment location itself, now presumably somewhere within or adjacent to the current surface city. Unlike Hut Point's own preserved, physically intact character in Scott, Byrd's equivalent plausibly reads differently: a marker or memorial built where the first arrivals actually stood before they knew what lay beneath them, honoring the moment of uncertainty just before the discovery that defined everything the city became.

## VI. The Buried Sections' True Depth: Older Than the City Remembers Being

**Proposed:** the real Byrd Station's own progressive burial (surface facility, abandoned ~2005, fully buried by 2564) establishes a genuine, centuries-long depth gradient — the deepest layers predate the exile community's own arrival entirely, meaning the oldest parts of Byrd are not just old by Tepenian standards but old by pre-exile standards too. It's a reasonable, earned proposal that the very deepest, earliest-buried sections hold material from Byrd Station's own original pre-exile operational period, genuinely separate from anything the Tepenian community itself built or remembers building — a real, physical connection to Upper Earth's own presence in Antarctica, sitting quietly beneath a city that has otherwise never had reason to think about it.

## VII. Ice Sheet Instability: A Background Condition That the Prospectors' Circle Watches Closely

**Proposed:** consistent with the Gameplay Notes' own framing ("a slow-burn environmental threat... not a sudden disaster"), the most earned answer keeps this as an ongoing background condition rather than an acute crisis — but one the newly-proposed Prospectors' Circle (Section I) monitors as part of its ordinary work, giving the city a faction with genuine, specific expertise on exactly how much danger the ground beneath Byrd is actually in. Whether that background condition escalates into DLC 2's actual central crisis is left open, consistent with not pre-empting the DLC's own reserved story content.

## Finding 1: The Cradle Network is a complete, three-city, cross-subnet system — and Byrd is one of only two places left holding half of it

**Combining:** Byrd's own confirmed status as one of only two currently-active fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturing sites + Sinheung's parallel, independently-established status as the other active site, itself receiving raw materials trucked in from Davis and shipping chambers nationwide the same way + Neumayer's own established role designing the current-generation (Mark IV, placeholder numbering) schematic that both manufacturing sites build to + Mountain Pass's historical, now-dark third manufacturing site, killed permanently when the Amundsen Tower's destruction cut its power supply.

**2nd-order effect:** the entire national robot-creation supply chain — from schematic design (Neumayer, Halley subnet) to manufacture (Byrd, Byrd subnet; Sinheung, Mirny subnet) to nationwide distribution — spans three subnets that have no other established connection to each other. Byrd's own manufacturing role isn't a local curiosity, it's one-third of a load-bearing, cross-continental system that was already down to two active nodes before this synthesis even began, and would be down to one if Byrd's own situation ever worsened past the DLC's central crisis.

**3rd-order effect:** this gives Byrd's own established "utterly vital to the rest of the country" status a concrete, already-built mechanism rather than an assertion — Byrd isn't vital because the lore says so, it's vital because it is, mechanically, one of exactly two places where new robots can be created anywhere in Tepenia, full stop.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as the single most concrete stakes-anchor available for DLC 2's Chamber Crisis candidate specifically — a threat to Byrd's Chamber Works isn't an abstract economic problem, it's a threat to cut the nationwide robot-creation capacity in half, the same order of magnitude of consequence the Amundsen Tower's own destruction carried for Arcanet connectivity.

## Finding 2: Belgrano's invention has come full circle — the vehicle that found Byrd is plausibly the vehicle that now sustains it

**Combining:** Belgrano's own established founding-era invention of the Arrastradoras (later worn down to "Rastra"), built specifically to reach Byrd across unmapped territory + Belgrano's own present-day mechanic/garage subculture, explicitly still maintaining Rastra-type vehicles as a living craft, not a historical footnote + Byrd's own established two-directional overland freight network (Hwy 1, Hwy 22) as its sole remaining connection to the rest of Tepenia since its aviation route's collapse + the Full Extrapolation's proposed answer that Byrd itself plausibly manufactures Rastra-descended heavy haulers as part of its own integrated fabrication output + the direct, established design lineage between the historical Arrastradora and the DLC 1 player vehicle, the Rastra, both drawing on the same Soviet-derived heavy tracked vehicle tradition.

**2nd-order effect:** the same vehicle type that carried the expedition which discovered Byrd in the first place is plausibly the same vehicle type now carrying everything Byrd exports back out to the rest of the country — not a coincidental reuse of a name, but a genuine, unbroken functional lineage running from Byrd's own origin story to its present-day economic lifeline.

**3rd-order effect:** this also means Byrd's own manufacturing capability and Belgrano's own mechanic culture are plausibly in an ongoing working relationship neither city's own files currently state outright — Belgrano the historical inventor and continuing expert-repair culture, Byrd a likely present-day manufacturer of the same vehicle lineage at industrial scale, two cities on opposite sides of the continent bound by one shared machine.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a genuinely rich piece of connective tissue for the whole project — it ties Byrd's own DLC 2 directly to Belgrano's established history in the Halley subnet's DLC 5, and to the player's own vehicle in DLC 1, through one unbroken thread rather than three separate, unrelated facts.

## Finding 3: Byrd's northern highway makes it the Palmer subnet's own western industrial partner — with Rothera as its most natural peer and Esperanza as its most natural supplier

**Combining:** Hwy 1's confirmed full route (Esperanza → Marambio → ramp to Port Lockroy/Palmer City → ramp to Rothera → Byrd), meaning Byrd sits at the literal end of a highway touching nearly every Palmer subnet city + Rothera's own established status as the Palmer subnet's decentralized manufacturing center, the closest thing that subnet has to a heavy-industry peer for Byrd + Esperanza's own established Basque agricultural tradition and its existing role as Belgrano's own maritime trans-shipment point toward Palmer City + Maggie Aarden's own established personal history, tentatively settling in Rothera before her eventual, now-confirmed move to Byrd.

**2nd-order effect:** Byrd's entire northern trade corridor runs through the same subnet, meaning its most realistic ongoing economic relationships aren't scattered across all of Tepenia at random, they're concentrated specifically in the Palmer subnet — Rothera as a manufacturing counterpart (likely exchanging specialized components or expertise, two heavy-industry cities on the same route), Esperanza as an agricultural supplier (the food-variety answer proposed in the Full Extrapolation), and Palmer City and Port Lockroy as points along the way rather than primary trading partners themselves.

**3rd-order effect:** Maggie Aarden's own established Rothera-to-Byrd path isn't just personal biography, it retraces the exact same industrial corridor this finding identifies independently — a companion whose own life history already walked the trade route her adopted city depends on, before she ever knew that's what she was doing.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a genuinely usable piece of connective tissue between DLC 2 and DLC 3 (Palmer subnet) — Rothera-manufactured components appearing in Byrd, or Byrd-manufactured goods appearing in Rothera, would be an earned, quiet way to make both DLCs feel like they're set in the same country rather than two unrelated Antarctic settings.

## Finding 4: Byrd's eastern route is a fragile, continent-spanning artery running directly through the one piece of infrastructure everyone agrees is destroyed

**Combining:** Hwy 22's confirmed route — Byrd's own eastern terminus, running directly through Amundsen Station before reaching the Zhongshan/Sinheung/Shirayuki tri-junction in the Mirny subnet, with further junctions along the way toward Janbogo (Hwy 175), the Halley subnet (Hwy 59), and Concordia/Vostok/Kunlun/Dome Fuji/Sayowa (the Hwy 37 dual-junction) + Amundsen Station's own established total destruction, its ruins forming the "scrap mountain" debris field that surrounds the site + the developer's own explicit framing, in this current request, that Byrd currently does receive and deliver goods to "an enormous portion of the country" — meaning this route is functioning today, not merely on paper.

**2nd-order effect:** for Byrd's eastern freight route to actually be functioning today, some passage through or around Amundsen Station's ruins has to exist — either a maintained bypass around the debris field, or a cleared channel through it, kept open specifically because too many cities' supply chains depend on it to simply abandon.

**3rd-order effect:** this makes Hwy 22's passage through Amundsen Station a piece of infrastructure with the same structural fragility and cross-subnet stakes as Troll Airfield in the Halley subnet or the Sayowa Junction in the Mawson subnet — except potentially higher-stakes, since this single road segment is the only overland link between Byrd's entire manufacturing output and roughly two-thirds of inhabited Tepenia (everything east of the South Pole).

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a genuinely significant, currently unstated piece of infrastructure stakes for DLC 2 — whatever "the nature of Byrd's struggle" turns out to be, threatening or restoring passage through the Amundsen Station bypass would connect Byrd's own central crisis directly to the fate of every city on the far side of the continent that depends on Byrd's chambers and machinery arriving at all.

## Finding 5: Byrd sits at the far end of a pattern this project has already tracked five other times — and is the only member of that pattern with no subnet neighbors to lean on

**Combining:** Byrd's own established Enneagram group (Instinctive/Withdrawn/Competency), shared with Sanay, Denison, Davis, Mirny, and Zhongshan — six cities whose whole distinguishing axis is "why does this city withdraw, and what is its competence actually applied to" + the fact that Byrd's own answer, "isolation at near-total circumstance... the largest scale," is already flagged in `Distinguishing_Overlapping_Profiles.md` as the most extreme case in that group + the standing design requirement, unique to Byrd among all six of these cities, that it alone has no subnet neighbors to distribute its own DLC's complexity onto.

**2nd-order effect:** every other member of this shared Enneagram group — Sanay, Denison, Davis, Mirny, Zhongshan — gets to be a quiet, competent, withdrawn city precisely because it has subnet neighbors whose own louder, more assertive, or more contested stories can carry the surrounding DLC's dramatic weight instead. Byrd doesn't have that luxury; the same withdrawn, competent temperament that reads as a restful contrast everywhere else in this pattern has to somehow also carry an entire DLC's worth of stakes on its own.

**3rd-order effect:** this reframes the whole point of Sections I through IV of this Cross-Reference pass — the Cradle network, the Rastra lineage, the Palmer subnet corridor, and the Amundsen Station bypass all exist specifically to give a Withdrawn-type city genuine external stakes and connective tissue to the rest of Tepenia, compensating structurally for the subnet-neighbor complexity every other member of Byrd's own Enneagram group gets for free.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as the organizing principle behind this entire document — every finding above solves the same underlying problem from a different angle: giving a city whose defining trait is withdrawal enough concrete, external, cross-subnet significance to justify carrying a full DLC by itself.

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across All Five Findings

Every finding in this document points at the same underlying fact: Byrd's isolation was never actually total, even though its own established character insists otherwise. It sits at one end of a three-city national supply chain that would collapse without it (Finding 1). It runs on a vehicle lineage tracing directly back to the expedition that found it and forward to the player's own DLC 1 vehicle (Finding 2). Its northern highway makes it a genuine trading partner within the Palmer subnet, walked in reverse by its own future companion before either she or the city knew what that path would mean (Finding 3). Its eastern highway is the fragile thread holding together the freight relationship between West Antarctica and everywhere else, running directly through the one piece of infrastructure the whole project agrees was destroyed (Finding 4). And its own Enneagram profile marks it as the extreme, unsupported case of a pattern every other member of gets to share the weight of (Finding 5). Byrd calls itself alone. Read against the rest of Tepenia, it never quite was.

## Worth Your Attention

Finding 1 is the most load-bearing of the five — it turns Byrd's own stated "utterly vital to the rest of the country" identity from an assertion into a mechanism, by tracing the exact, already-established three-city chain (Neumayer designs, Byrd and Sinheung manufacture) that makes it true. Every other finding in this document is, in one way or another, downstream of the same basic fact: a city can't actually be irreplaceable in the abstract, only irreplaceable to something specific — and Finding 1 is where that "something specific" gets named.

# Byrd — Community Infrastructure & Social Life

**What this is:** concrete physical spaces, small institutions, and recurring social rituals that give Byrd's already-established culture a tangible, ground-level presence — the same pass done for each of Concordia's 13 districts and all 34 other Mirny, Halley, Janbogo, Palmer, and Mawson subnet cities, closing out the entire nationwide push here. ## Additions

- **The Elevator Halls** — the small number of massive shafts forming Byrd's sole connection between the surface city and the buried bulk below; functionally the city's real gates, the closest thing Byrd has to a checkpoint or district boundary, with a smaller, slower emergency shaft kept in reserve alongside the primary lifts.
- **St. Richard's Marker** — the modest memorial at the original surface encampment, informally adopted across every one of Byrd's five guilds as the place people visit quietly before undertakings nobody can yet be certain will hold.
- **The Chamber Works** — the specially reinforced, most heavily monitored section of the fabrication plants, where Byrd manufactures the fabrication-synthesis chambers that make it one of only two active sites in Tepenia still capable of building a robot from nothing.
- **The Five Guildhalls** — Fabricators, Dispatch, Foundation Keepers, Prospectors' Circle, and Grounded Wings each keep their own seat of operation and apprenticeship training; visibly different in age and construction, oldest nearest the Chamber Works, newest at the Grounded Wings' own hall.
- **The Hangar Complex** — the Grounded Wings' own mothballed airfield and hangars, kept in genuinely maintained, ready condition rather than left to decay, alongside a standing, complete diagnosis of exactly what would need to change for the fleet to fly again.
- **The Commons Hall** — Byrd's shared commercial marketplace, where in-house-manufactured consumer goods actually reach residents; the one place in the city where the population's real, unsmoothed diversity is most visible day to day.
- **The Reading Rooms** — Byrd's public library and its separate, formal city records/archive hall, sitting side by side; unlike almost anywhere else in the city, staff here will tell a visitor plainly when something — like the deep pre-exile layers — simply isn't written down anywhere they can point to.
- **The Dispatch Floor** — the Dispatch guild's own coordination office, where surface weather reports and highway beacon data converge into the single clearest daily picture of whether Hwy 1 and Hwy 22 are actually still passable.
- **The Shift Halls** — communal dining spaces built around guild shift schedules rather than a single citywide mealtime, serving food built substantially around fermentation and preservation craft.
- **The Growing Sections** — Byrd's hydroponic and cultured-protein production, tended against contamination by specialists whose whole working life is keeping a fragile, irreplaceable food source viable underground.
- **The Circle's Own Ground** — the Prospectors' Circle's ore-processing and extraction facility, sited hard against the Chamber Works and closer to the ice boundary than any other guild's own workspace — the single physical location in Byrd where active excavation, industrial heat, and the Ice Sheet's own instability all meet at once.
- **The Reserve** — the underground energy storage buffer and its dedicated geothermal-monitoring station, the specific engineering that keeps Byrd's power supply — and the Chamber Works it feeds — running through whatever the volcanic ground beneath the city actually does.
- **The Relay** — Byrd's own Arcanet access node, connecting a genuinely remote city to the rest of Tepenia's own social and information network even when the highways themselves are in question.

## Small offices for educational training

- fabrication-chamber precision work and general mechanized fabrication (Fabricators' Guild)
- highway freight coordination and Arrastradora fleet maintenance (Dispatch guild)
- structural/seismic monitoring, deep-tunnel wayfinding, and trained acoustic listening for ice-sheet stress (Foundation Keepers)
- ore extraction and processing (Prospectors' Circle)
- aviation diagnostics and standing-repair archival practice (Grounded Wings)
- trauma surgery and epidemiological containment (the hospital and quarantine complex)
- hydroponic cultivation, cultured-protein production, and fermentation/preservation craft
- robot maintenance, diagnostics, and firmware servicing

## Social cohesion mechanisms

- St. Richard's Marker as a shared, cross-guild ritual for facing genuine uncertainty — a precision-critical call in the fabrication plants, an uncertain read on unstable extraction ground, any decision nobody can yet be certain will hold
- self-sufficiency that began as necessity and became pride without much reflection — visible now in the Shift Halls' own fermented, preserved food and the Growing Sections' own quiet craft pride, the same arc that defines the city's founding character writ small, at the scale of a single shared meal
- the open, unresolved coexistence between founding-descendant families (who still hold a disproportionate share of the city's deep structural and wayfinding knowledge) and the far larger population who arrived later, indirectly, via the Palmer City redistribution — a real fault line the city doesn't pretend has resolved into one smoothed-over identity
- the quiet, never-formally-contested gap between who administers Byrd on paper (the civic complex) and who actually controls its only way to the surface (the Elevator Halls) — a source of real leverage nobody has ever needed to name out loud
- the Grounded Wings' own hopeful, unresignedly maintained hangar complex, generally regarded with quiet respect rather than pity
- knowing, via the Relay, exactly what's happening in the rest of Tepenia while almost never actually seeing a visitor in person — Byrd's own specific, informationally-connected shape of isolation, distinct from simply not knowing what's out there
