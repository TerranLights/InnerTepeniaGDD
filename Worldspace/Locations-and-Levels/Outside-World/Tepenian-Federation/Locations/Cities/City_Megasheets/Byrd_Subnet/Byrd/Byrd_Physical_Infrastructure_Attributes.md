# Byrd — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Byrd would logically have to possess, built directly from what's already established (`Specs/Byrd.md`, `Byrd_Mega_Init.md`, `Byrd_Full_Extrapolation.md`) rather than invented from nothing. Distinct in kind from `Byrd_Full_Extrapolation.md`, which resolves plot/faction-level open questions (the five guilds, the aviation mystery, food imports) — this file works one level lower, at the physical-infrastructure level, following the same "physical circumstance forces the attribute" discipline as the project's own generative framework (see `feedback_no_national_stereotypes` memory). Not locked canon — a bouncing-off point for further extrapolation, per direct developer instruction. Written 2026-07-30.

**Governing facts this file reasons from:** Byrd was founded underground before it existed on the surface, and has grown outward and upward over generations since (`Specs/Byrd.md`, Founding); the population is roughly even between humans and robots, ~190,000 of each per Census I; the West Antarctic Ice Sheet sits on unstable marine bedrock and shifts slowly, in ways engineers have had to account for since the founding; the surface climate runs to −40°C in winter with ~108 days of total polar night and ~128 days of total midnight sun (the longest of any Tepenian city); the economy is dominated by mechanized fabrication (30%, including fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturing) and import/export & dispatch (25%); the city is isolated, with only a currently-uncertain overland highway connection and a broken aviation route.

---

## Life Support (human-specific — robots don't require these)

1. **Extensive, redundant ventilation** — kilometers of sealed tunnels need constant air circulation and CO2 scrubbing for the human population specifically; robots have no equivalent need, so this exists purely for the ~190,000 humans living underground.
2. **Artificial lighting throughout the buried sections** — no natural light reaches underground at all, ever, regardless of season; distinct from the surface city, which still contends with 108 days of total darkness and 128 days of total daylight in alternation.
3. **Circadian-rhythm lighting management** — humans living underground long-term, cut off from natural light cycles entirely, would need engineered light schedules just to maintain a working sense of day and night.
4. **Vitamin D / UV-therapy provisioning** — a direct medical consequence of #3; a population living mostly underground needs a deliberate countermeasure to permanent light deprivation.

## Structural Engineering (from Ice Sheet instability + buried, generations-old construction)

5. **Continuous structural/seismic monitoring** *(CONFIRMED, not merely proposed — see `Byrd_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` chains #1 and #11)* — the ground beneath Byrd genuinely shifts; this is an active, ongoing engineering practice rather than a one-time construction concern. Chain #1 establishes its actual founding-era origin: the earliest arrivals' own "descend carefully, in verified stages" compromise hardened into an inherited procedure, later becoming "the specific inherited responsibility of whichever sector ends up tasked with keeping the buried city structurally sound." Chain #11 confirms it became a permanent, city-wide practice generations later, after engineers first observed genuine structural strain. Ties directly to the already-established Foundation Keepers guild and Prospectors' Circle (`Byrd_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section I).
6. **Flex-tolerant construction methods** — materials and joints designed to accommodate slow ice-sheet movement rather than rigid materials that would crack under it.
7. **Condemned-zone protocols** — a formal system for sealing off, monitoring, and occasionally reclaiming sections as instability risk changes over time.

## Heat and Power

8. **Massive heating infrastructure** — both for the underground volume (ambient ground temperature is brutally cold even below the wind) and the surface city.
9. **Waste-heat recapture from the fabrication plants** — an efficient, self-sufficiency-minded city with huge industrial heat output sitting next to a huge heating need would very plausibly loop one into the other.
10. **Independent power generation, plausibly geothermal** — `Specs/Byrd.md` itself notes West Antarctic geology is volcanically active in some areas; a self-sufficient city this isolated would have strong reason to tap that rather than depend on anything shippable in.

## Water and Food

11. **Large-scale ice/snow melting for water production**, sized to a population approaching 400,000.
12. **Water and waste recycling loops**, more aggressive than most Tepenian cities, given how tenuous outside resupply actually is.
13. **Supplemental local food storage/buffer-stock discipline** *(CONFIRMED, not merely proposed — see `Byrd_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` chain #6)* — even with Esperanza's produce coming down Hwy 1 (`Byrd_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section IV), Chain #6 establishes that Byrd's food arrangement with Esperanza was always variable, tracking Esperanza's own seasonal capacity rather than a fixed quota — forcing Byrd to build genuine domestic food-storage and buffer-stock capacity underground to absorb the swings. The habit then generalized: once learned for food, buffer-stock discipline became the template Byrd "reflexively applies to other critical goods it depends on the outside world for" — self-sufficiency as a general instinct, not a food-specific policy, tracing one of its earliest roots to this one arrangement.

## Navigation and Human Factors

14. **A wayfinding/signage system for the buried tunnel network** — centuries of organic, unplanned tunneling (explicitly established: the community "expand[ed] and inhabit[ed] the buried tunnel network," growing outward and upward over generations) would be genuinely disorienting without one. A strong candidate parallel to Zukelli's own structural-language marking system, but built around plain necessity rather than privacy.
15. **Robust local medical infrastructure**, including mental-health provisioning specifically for isolation and light-deprivation effects — no realistic option to evacuate someone for treatment given the broken aviation route and uncertain overland one.

## Robot-Specific Infrastructure

16. **Large-scale robot maintenance and charging infrastructure**, sized to match the fabrication economy and the roughly-even human/robot population split — a city that manufactures chambers at scale would also need serious in-house capacity to service its own robot population.

## General Physical Form *(added 2026-07-30, Round 2 — formalizing what the founding facts already establish)*

17. **Almost entirely underground, with only a minimal surface footprint** *(already established, not merely proposed — see the Founding section and `Local_Cultures/Byrd_Subnet/Byrd.md` Section 9's own distinction between "the surface city" and "the actual bulk of the city" underground)* — a thin surface city (elevator housings, weather/sensor installations, minimal surface-facing facilities) sits atop a vastly larger underground volume; most residents plausibly have little routine reason to visit the surface city itself at all.
18. **A small number of massive elevators as the sole surface-to-underground connection** *(already established, not merely proposed — see `Local_Cultures/Byrd_Subnet/Byrd.md` Section 9)* — not a distributed network of ramps, stairs, or multiple access points, but a small number of major shafts functioning as genuine chokepoints; population scale (approaching 400,000) plausibly requires more than one such shaft, but still a small, countable number rather than many.

## Population Distribution (human/robot vertical stratification)

19. **Human residential concentration toward the upper, nearer-to-surface levels** — even though artificial lighting is required at every depth (attribute #2), the levels nearest the elevators (attribute #18) offer the shortest evacuation time, easiest surface access, and least cumulative depth-related engineering risk; the near-surface-most habitable level is plausibly the single most common human residential layer for exactly these practical reasons, not sentiment.
20. **Robot residential/functional concentration extending deeper, into the levels humans occupy least** — robots require no light-cycle management (attributes #2–4), no oxygen, and no evacuation urgency, so the deepest, hardest-to-service levels are the ones with the least reason to be reserved for anything else; this plausibly frees the more convenient, resource-intensive upper levels for the human-specific needs attributes #1–4 exist to meet.

## Civic & Institutional Self-Sufficiency (in-house institutions, forced by total isolation)

21. **A full domestically-run hospital and surgical complex**, not merely general "medical infrastructure" — direct consequence of attribute #15 combined with the total absence of any realistic evacuation route; a population of this size, permanently unable to medevac anyone out, needs the complete range of medical capability in-house, including trauma and surgical care, not just routine clinical services.
22. **In-house educational institutions spanning primary schooling through specialized vocational/guild apprenticeship** — sending students elsewhere or importing specialized instructors isn't realistic given the isolation; the five-guild economic structure (Fabricators, Dispatch, Foundation Keepers, Prospectors, Grounded Wings) plausibly *is* Byrd's own practical apprenticeship and higher-training system, with each guild responsible for training its own specialists rather than any separate, centralized institution doing it for them.
23. **Dedicated sewage/wastewater and septic treatment facilities**, sized for a sealed underground population approaching 400,000 — more specific than the general water/waste recycling loops already established (attribute #12); a sealed environment this size cannot tolerate any failure in this specific system without genuinely catastrophic consequences, making it one of the single most safety-critical systems in the entire city.
24. **In-house food-growth infrastructure**, distinct from the storage/buffer-stock discipline already established (attribute #13) — artificial-light agriculture (hydroponics, vertical growing, low-light-tolerant cultivation such as mushroom/fungus farming) sized to supplement, not replace, the Esperanza supply relationship along Hwy 1; domestic growth capacity reduces, without eliminating, dependency on a single external supply corridor.
25. **In-house manufacturing of ordinary consumer goods** (clothing, furniture, household tools, everyday hardware) — the same self-sufficiency instinct already established as generalizing beyond food (attribute #13's "reflexively applies to other critical goods") plausibly extends to routine daily items too; a city this isolated has no realistic option to casually import ordinary goods, so domestic production plausibly covers far more of everyday life than in a well-connected Tepenian city.
26. **A physical, in-house administrative/civic complex** for governance, record-keeping, and day-to-day local dispute resolution — Byrd remains formally part of the national system, but routine civic function running locally rather than depending on regular outside contact is a direct, physical-infrastructure consequence of the same isolation driving every other attribute in this section.

## Safety & Redundancy Engineering

27. **Robust fire-suppression and industrial-hazard containment systems** — a sealed, enclosed tunnel network combined with heavy fabrication industry (chamber fabrication, general industrial heat and chemical processes) creates fire and atmospheric-hazard risks that would be genuinely catastrophic without dedicated suppression and containment, given no realistic option for large-scale evacuation.
28. **Internal emergency shelter zones built into the tunnel network itself**, rather than city-wide evacuation capability — the combination of structural instability risk (attributes #5–7) and the total absence of realistic external evacuation (attribute #15's own governing logic) makes sheltering in place, in purpose-built safe zones, the only physically plausible emergency-response model.
29. **Noise and vibration dampening infrastructure near residential zones** — heavy fabrication industry operating underground, combined with the already-established fact that Byrd's tunnels "carry sound differently" (`Local_Cultures/Byrd_Subnet/Byrd.md` Section 14), makes noise/vibration control near residential areas a genuine physical engineering need, not merely the atmospheric detail Finding 8 draws on.
30. **Redundant/backup capacity for every life-critical system** (power, ventilation, water) considerably beyond ordinary civic standards — total isolation and the impossibility of rapid outside rescue or resupply during an emergency means ordinary redundancy margins, adequate for a well-connected city, would plausibly be insufficient here.
31. **Deep-structure water pressure and plumbing engineering**, distinct from typical shallow city plumbing — moving water and waste across many vertical underground levels (rather than a flat street-level grid) requires specialized pressure and pumping engineering scaled to depth, not just population.

## Surface Operations

32. **Surface-level weather monitoring and meteorological infrastructure** — given the elevators' role as the sole surface connection (attribute #18) and the extreme surface climate (−40°C winter, 108 days total polar night, 128 days total midnight sun), monitoring conditions at the surface is a direct safety necessity for anyone or anything that has to use the elevators.
33. **Cold-weather surface-operations gear and staging areas**, stockpiled near the elevator housings specifically — for the relatively small population with actual routine reason to reach the surface city (attribute #17), given how severe surface conditions are compared to the managed underground environment most residents actually live in.

## Internal Transit & Freight Movement *(added 2026-07-30, Round 3)*

34. **An internal mass-transit system for horizontal and vertical movement across the underground volume**, distinct from the surface-connecting elevators (attribute #18) — given a population approaching 400,000 spread across many levels and the depth-based residential stratification already established (attributes #19–20), day-to-day movement within the city needs its own dedicated transit infrastructure, separate in purpose and scale from the small number of surface-access shafts.
35. **A separate freight/cargo hoist and haulage system, distinct from human passenger transit** — given the import/export & dispatch economy (25% of Byrd's economic base) and heavy internal freight loads (raw extracted material, finished chamber units, manufactured goods), moving cargo at industrial scale through the same shafts used for passenger movement would be inefficient and genuinely hazardous; a dedicated freight vertical/horizontal haulage network is the more plausible design.
36. **Freight marshalling yards and warehousing**, concentrated at the surface heads of Hwy 1 and Hwy 22 and distributed to the relevant guild sectors internally — the scale of goods moving in and out via the two-highway network (established in `Byrd_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` chains #5–6) requires staging and storage infrastructure at both the surface transfer points and the internal destinations those goods are actually headed to.

## Power Distribution & Industrial Environmental Control

37. **An internal power distribution grid** (substations, transformers, cabling) engineered for a many-leveled vertical structure — distinct from power generation itself (attribute #10); routing electrical power from a geothermal source through kilometers of vertical, sealed tunnel is a genuinely different engineering problem than a flat surface-city grid.
38. **Industrial-grade dust and particulate filtration**, distinct from the general life-support air circulation already established (attribute #1) — heavy fabrication and resource extraction generate particulate byproducts ordinary ventilation isn't designed to handle; dedicated filtration near the Chamber Works and the Prospectors' Circle's own extraction zones is a direct consequence of running those industries in a sealed environment shared with a living population.
39. **Insulating buffer zones between heated interior sections and the surrounding ice** — since the entire city sits embedded in the West Antarctic Ice Sheet, already established as unstable (attributes #5–7), uncontrolled heat bleed into the surrounding ice risks localized melting near heated zones, which would itself become a structural hazard; insulation is plausibly as much a structural-safety measure here as an efficiency one.
40. **Meltwater drainage and diversion infrastructure at the interior/ice boundary** — a direct consequence of attribute #39; if any heat bleed into the surrounding ice is unavoidable, the resulting meltwater has to go somewhere deliberately engineered, rather than pooling or refreezing unpredictably against structural elements.
41. **Surface exhaust venting for industrial and generator byproducts** — geothermal power generation (attribute #10) and heavy fabrication both plausibly produce exhaust that needs a controlled path to the surface, distinct from and additional to the fresh-air intake ventilation already established (attribute #1).

## Cold Storage & Food Preservation

42. **Deliberately unheated or minimally-heated buffer zones used as natural cold storage** — rather than expending power to refrigerate food in an environment where extreme cold is freely available just meters away, Byrd plausibly engineers specific unheated or semi-heated zones near the ice boundary specifically to exploit ambient cold for food preservation, distinct from the general heating infrastructure (attribute #8) covering the rest of the city.
43. **Cultured or synthetic protein-production infrastructure, rather than livestock** — combining the in-house food-growth infrastructure already established (attribute #24) with the total absence of any realistic space or resource case for maintaining livestock underground; protein production plausibly follows the same artificial/engineered logic as the hydroponic produce it sits alongside.

## Surface Vehicle & Highway Support Infrastructure

44. **A dedicated ground-vehicle maintenance depot for the Arrastradora fleet and general highway freight vehicles**, sited at or near the surface city — distinct from the Grounded Wings' own aviation-specific facilities; a two-highway freight economy running on domestically-manufactured tracked vehicles (`Byrd_Course_of_Events_Suggestions.md` chain #2) needs its own maintenance infrastructure, separate from and in addition to the grounded aircraft fleet's facilities.
45. **Waystation and beacon infrastructure along Hwy 1 and Hwy 22** — both highways cross genuinely remote, difficult interior and peninsula terrain over very long distances; given how completely Byrd's food security (chain #6) and general relevance (Finding 11) depend on these two roads staying passable, some form of emergency waystation or beacon marking is a plausible safety investment, distinct from the roads' own physical construction and maintenance.

## Communications & Security

46. **An internal wired communications backbone, rather than wireless/radio-dependent systems** — radio propagation through kilometers of rock, ice, and reinforced tunnel construction is genuinely poor; a buried city this size plausibly relies on a physical, wired intercom and data backbone strung throughout the tunnel network for reliable internal communication between levels and guild sectors.
47. **Access control and monitoring infrastructure at the elevator chokepoints** — given the small number of massive elevators are the sole surface-to-underground connection (attribute #18), they are also, necessarily, the single most natural place to monitor and control who or what moves between the surface and the underground bulk of the city, whether or not that control is currently exercised with any real strictness.

## Robot-Specific Infrastructure (expanded)

48. **On-site parts and component warehousing for robot maintenance and repair**, distinct from the maintenance/charging infrastructure itself (attribute #16) — a fabrication economy of this scale, combined with total isolation from casual outside resupply, plausibly keeps substantial spare-parts inventory in-house rather than depending on import for routine robot repair.

## Round 4 additions *(added 2026-07-30 — a fourth attributes-only pass, checked directly against all 48 existing attributes for genuine, non-redundant gaps)*

**Safety & Redundancy Engineering, expanded:**

49. **A dedicated maintenance-access network** (service tunnels, crawlspaces, catwalks), physically separate from public thoroughfares — the actual physical access method engineers would use to inspect and service the ventilation system (#1), structural monitoring points (#5), the power distribution grid (#37), and deep plumbing (#31); a city this reliant on constant structural vigilance plausibly needs a dedicated access layer, not shared with everyday foot or transit traffic.
50. **An emergency backup/secondary access shaft**, distinct from and redundant to the primary elevator system (#18) — the elevators are explicitly established as the *sole* surface connection, which is a stark single point of failure; the same engineering culture responsible for continuous structural monitoring (#5) and general life-critical redundancy (#30) would plausibly insist on at least a minimal manual or emergency secondary shaft, even one far smaller and slower than the primary elevators.

**Civic & Institutional Self-Sufficiency, expanded:**

51. **Dedicated quarantine/isolation medical facilities**, distinct from the general hospital and surgical complex (#21) — the same total-isolation logic that makes evacuation impossible (attribute #15's own governing rationale) means a disease outbreak has to be contained entirely in-house too; quarantine capacity is a specific, distinct need from general medical care, not an extension of it.
52. **Solid waste and industrial scrap handling/recycling infrastructure**, distinct from sewage/wastewater treatment (#23) — heavy fabrication and resource extraction generate solid byproduct and scrap material; given the self-sufficiency instinct already established as generalizing to consumer-goods manufacturing (#25), scrap metal and industrial waste plausibly get sorted and fed back into the fabrication economy rather than simply discarded, since there's no easy way to export waste either.

**Life Support, expanded:**

53. **City-wide centralized time-keeping and scheduling infrastructure** — a population living permanently without natural day/night cues (attributes #2–3) needs some synchronized, city-wide reference for coordinating work shifts, schooling, and daily rhythm; distinct from the lighting management itself, this is the actual timekeeping/scheduling backbone the engineered light schedules would be built around.

**Water and Food, expanded:**

54. **Water storage reservoirs/tanks**, distinct from water production (#11) and water recycling (#12) — the same buffer-stock discipline already established for food (#13) plausibly applies to water too, once the underlying "reflexive" self-sufficiency habit generalizes, per attribute #13's own reasoning, to any critical resource whose production could be interrupted.

**Cold Storage & Food Preservation, expanded:**

55. **Pest and contamination control infrastructure** for the food-growth and food-storage sections — a sealed underground hydroponic environment (#24) and large-scale food storage (#13) create real risk of mold, fungal contamination, or pest infestation with no easy way to bring in outside remediation help; dedicated control measures are a direct consequence of growing and storing food inside a fully enclosed system.

**Surface Operations, expanded:**

56. **Surface snow-clearing and plow infrastructure**, distinct from the meltwater drainage established at the interior/ice boundary (#40) — the surface city and elevator housings (#17–18) sit exposed to the same relentless snow accumulation that buried Byrd's own original station in the first place (Founding section); keeping surface access points clear is a distinct, ongoing physical-maintenance need, not an interior structural one.
57. **Surface lighting infrastructure for the polar-night period** — the surface city (#17) still needs illumination during the 108 days of total polar night for whatever limited operations occur there (elevator access, surface maintenance, weather monitoring); a distinct need from the underground artificial lighting already established (#2), since the surface city sits exposed to genuine outside dark rather than being permanently lightless by design.

**Robot-Specific Infrastructure, expanded:**

58. **Robot-specific diagnostic and firmware/software update infrastructure**, distinct from both the general communications backbone (#46) and the physical maintenance/charging infrastructure (#16) — a fabrication economy building and servicing robots at scale plausibly needs dedicated data infrastructure for software-level diagnostics and updates, a distinct need from the purely physical hardware maintenance attribute #16 already covers.

## Round 5 additions *(added 2026-07-30 — cross-city comparison pass)*

**Method:** a research pass read all 34 other Tepenian cities' own `*_Community_Infrastructure.md` files (Mirny, Halley, Janbogo, Palmer, and Mawson subnets) and catalogued every generalized physical infrastructure type they establish. **Civic & Institutional Self-Sufficiency, expanded:**

67. **A commercial hall/marketplace for distributing in-house-manufactured consumer goods** — a direct physical consequence of attribute #25 (in-house manufacturing of consumer goods); goods produced domestically still need a physical place to actually reach the people who need them.
68. **Communal dining/kitchen facilities tied to guild shift schedules** — a population working staggered industrial shifts across five guilds, combined with the isolation-driven self-sufficiency instinct already established, plausibly relies at least partly on shared, schedule-coordinated dining infrastructure rather than exclusively private household cooking.

**Cold Storage & Food Preservation, expanded:**

**Power Distribution & Industrial Environmental Control, expanded:**

71. **An underground energy storage reserve** (flywheel, battery, or thermal buffer), distinct from power generation (attribute #10) and distribution (attribute #37) — a geothermal source tied to volcanic activity plausibly has variable output, and the redundancy discipline already established (attribute #30) argues for buffered storage rather than relying on generation-to-demand matching alone.

**General Physical Form, expanded:**

## Cross-Referenced Extrapolations: Combining This File's Attributes With Byrd's Existing Plot/Faction-Level Material

### Finding 1: Byrd's own founding story already explains why Upper Earth never found it

**Combining:** the Founding section's own established fact that the discovering expedition itself found "not a station... a field of compacted snow over buried infrastructure, with no surface indication of what lay beneath" + `Byrd_DLC_Reverse_Inferred_Guidelines.md` Guideline 2's own extrapolated requirement that Byrd's survival mechanism needs to be total unknown-ness rather than importance-driven restraint, since that latter logic already produced the wrong outcome once, at Amundsen Station.

**2nd-order effect:** Byrd's own buried construction was already, on its own established terms, invisible from above — genuinely undetectable by surface or aerial observation, since even the Tepenian expedition that came looking for it, guided by old records and maps, found nothing but featureless snow until they physically began probing for tunnels.

**3rd-order effect:** this means the exact survival mechanism Guideline 2 calls for isn't something that needs inventing — it's already sitting in the founding section, simply never connected back to the separate question of why Upper Earth's own targeting never found Byrd during the Long Night War. The same physical fact that made Byrd hard for its own founders to find is a genuinely earned, already-established reason it stayed hidden from an entirely different search decades or centuries later.

**4th-order effect:** worth carrying forward directly into whatever DLC 2 content eventually addresses Byrd's own survival — "we were never targeted because we were never seen" is a stronger, more specific claim than generic remoteness, and it's one this file's own governing facts already support without requiring a new invented justification.

### Finding 2: Independent geothermal power plausibly explains why Byrd's Chamber Works survived the Amundsen Tower's collapse when Mountain Pass's didn't

**Combining:** attribute #10 above (independent, plausibly geothermal power generation, grounded in `Specs/Byrd.md`'s own note that West Antarctic geology is volcanically active in some areas) + the already-established fact (via `Specs/Sinheung.md` and the Mountain Pass history) that Mountain Pass's own chamber-manufacturing capability died permanently because it depended on residual power overflow from the Amundsen Tower's own regulated grid, and that supply ended the moment the Tower was destroyed + Byrd's own confirmed status as one of only two currently-active fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturers, alongside Sinheung.

**2nd-order effect:** if Byrd's own power genuinely is independent of the Tower's regulated grid — geothermal, tapping the same volcanic activity already noted in its own geographic basis — then Byrd's chamber manufacturing surviving the Tower's collapse isn't an unexplained coincidence sitting quietly alongside Mountain Pass's own confirmed death by the identical event. It's the direct, mechanical consequence of the two sites never having shared the same power dependency in the first place.

**3rd-order effect:** this gives Byrd's own "one of only two remaining Cradle sites" status (`Byrd_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` Finding 1) a genuine causal explanation for *why* it's one of the two survivors rather than a third casualty alongside Mountain Pass — power-source independence, not luck or importance, is what kept the chain intact at this specific node.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a load-bearing piece of connective tissue the moment Byrd's own power infrastructure gets developed further — it retroactively answers a question (why did Byrd's Cradle role survive when Mountain Pass's didn't) that no existing file currently poses or answers, using material this file's own attribute list already supplies.

### Finding 3: Not a second wayfinding system — no coherent system at all, by design of how the culture has always handled these encounters *(CORRECTED 2026-07-30)*
