# Byrd / DLC 2 — Reverse-Inferred Guidelines

**What this is, and how it was produced:** a set of guidelines, parameters, patterns, and pressures for what Byrd and its DLC should probably be like — deliberately derived *without* consulting Byrd's own existing City Megasheet, its Cross-Reference Synthesis, or its Enrichment Possibilities file as source material. Instead, this document treats Byrd as the one unknown variable the rest of the game's own already-established patterns should let us solve for by extrapolation: every other region in this project (Concordia's 13 districts, all 5 Subnet Ultra-Megasheets, Amundsen Station, and the Super-Ultra-Megasheet's own cross-regional synthesis) was reviewed, and this document asks what those patterns predict Byrd should look like, purely by the logic of "what else in this world already works this way."

**Deliberately not done here:** no actual Byrd-specific lore, plot points, character names, or story decisions. This stays at the level of guidelines, pressures, and directional pointers — "roughly, loosely, approximately," per the developer's own framing — meant to be checked against Byrd's own actual development whenever that work is picked back up, not to pre-empt it. Written 2026-07-10.

---

## Part One: What the Rest of the Game Predicts About Byrd, by Pure Extrapolation

### 1. Byrd should carry the "uncredited competence" theme at its most extreme intensity in the whole game

Every region examined in this project independently rediscovered the identical civic shape: genuinely consequential work, performed well, credited nowhere. Halley subnet found it in Neumayer's three-century unclaimed engineering legacy. Janbogo/Ross subnet found it in Janbogo's own witness role, quietly bearing the weight of relaying its neighbors' destructions. Mirny subnet found it in Davis's triple-reinforced "enabler" identity and Sinheung's quietly load-bearing chamber-manufacturing role. Mawson subnet found it in the whole subnet's own "connects more than it contains" character. Palmer subnet's own Cross-Reference Synthesis found the same mechanism rediscovered three separate times within a single subnet's own research (Esperanza, Marambio, and Palmer City's Solenne Vaskarre all independently pointing to robots as the actual continuity mechanism, credited by none of the human institutions built on top of them). Concordia found it at district scale, in the four-district Recognition Cascade terminating on Virgo.

**The extrapolation:** this isn't a subnet-specific curiosity — it's confirmed, by independent evidence from literally every other region in the game, as Tepenia's single most defining recurring civic theme. Byrd is also the one region carrying an entire DLC's worth of narrative complexity completely alone, with no subnet neighbors to distribute that weight onto. Put those two facts together and the extrapolation is direct: Byrd shouldn't merely exhibit the "quiet, uncredited, load-bearing" theme the way every other region does — it should be the single most concentrated expression of that theme anywhere in the game. Whatever Byrd turns out to actually do, the guideline is that it should matter more to the rest of Tepenia than anyone standing inside Byrd itself fully realizes.

---

### 2. Byrd needs its own genuinely novel survival mechanism — not a repeat of any of the five already documented

The project has now produced six distinct, non-overlapping regional accounts of how the Long Night War's damage logic actually worked: Halley subnet's 4-category damage-*mechanism* taxonomy (passive age-favoring, active recency-favoring, single-point-of-failure, function-targeted); Janbogo/Ross subnet's 2-principle damage-*logic* (worth-more-alive survival vs. punitive/symbolic destruction); Mirny subnet's single-principle logic (infrastructure value alone, no punitive component); Mawson subnet's own admission that it shares no principle internally, instead sampling one mechanism each from the other subnets' frameworks; Palmer subnet's own independently-derived dual typology (a 4-type strike doctrine — security, efficiency, morale, ideological erasure — paired with a 3-mechanism survival typology — engineered decentralization, strategic irrelevance, isolation); and Concordia's own survival through sheer inland remoteness and elevation.

**The extrapolation:** every region examined so far has produced a genuinely distinct account — no two subnets reused the same explanatory mechanism. This means Byrd's own survival needs its own, not-yet-used justification to remain consistent with the pattern of the rest of the game. Here the extrapolation gets specifically interesting: the Super-Ultra-Megasheet's own Pattern 4 found that the variable protecting a *locally*-important city (remoteness, marginal value, an attacker's restraint) is the exact same variable that got a *nationally*-shared piece of infrastructure destroyed instead — Amundsen Station was hit precisely because of its centrality, not despite it. If Byrd turns out to be nationally load-bearing in some way (see Guideline 3), then "too important to bother destroying" is specifically the one justification this project's own established pattern says should NOT apply to it — that logic already got the wrong answer once, at Amundsen Station. The more consistent extrapolation is that Byrd's survival mechanism should be about total *unknown-ness* — invisibility to whoever was doing the targeting, rather than importance-driven restraint or plain physical remoteness (which Concordia and Vostok/Kunlun already used).

---

### 3. Byrd's central DLC crisis should put direct pressure on whatever makes it nationally load-bearing

The single most cross-regionally-connected thread this whole project has produced runs through Byrd, tying together design work in one subnet, manufacturing capacity in a second, historical manufacturing in a third, and the game's own central catastrophic event (the Amundsen Tower's collapse) in a fourth — six of the game's eight major regions in total, more than any other single thread in the entire project.

**The extrapolation:** a DLC whose main questline doesn't put genuine, direct pressure on Byrd's own load-bearing national function would be leaving the single strongest, most already-earned set of stakes in the entire game completely unused. Whatever the actual central crisis turns out to be, the guideline is that its consequences should reach measurably outside Byrd's own borders — "something here failing would hurt places far away, not just Byrd itself" is the shape the rest of the world's own accumulated logic points toward, not a self-contained local problem with no wider ripple.

---

### 4. Byrd's founding relationship to the rest of the country should surface as a discoverable revelation, not a stated fact

Across every region examined, the recurring shape of a cross-regional debt or connection is the same: it exists in the established facts, but the place carrying the debt doesn't know about it — the connection is something a player (or an in-world character) *discovers*, not something the setting simply states up front. This shape recurs at every scale in this project, from single-city Cross-Reference findings all the way up to the Super-Ultra-Megasheet's own headline Throughways.

**The extrapolation:** whatever Byrd's own founding relationship to the rest of Tepenia turns out to be, the guideline is that it should function as an in-world revelation — something Byrd's own people (and the player) actually learn during the DLC, ideally through a concrete, discoverable artifact rather than pure exposition — echoing the dominant discovery-pattern this whole project keeps producing rather than simply narrating the connection as known background.

---

### 5. Tone and emotional register should lean toward "arriving," not "reaching"

The Dome Fuji/Kunlun theological duality (Mawson/Mirny subnets) — devotion expressed through discovery and stillness versus devotion expressed through construction and striving — was explicitly proposed as general enough to extend past its own religious context into secular sites project-wide, with Concordia (built, argued over, still straining toward something unfinished) and Byrd (found already complete, requiring only the crossing to reach) proposed as the clearest secular pair.

**The extrapolation:** if this duality holds, Byrd's own emotional register should lean toward discovery, reverence, and stillness — closer to Dome Fuji's own register than to Aries' or Aquarius'. This is a texture guideline, not a plot guideline: whatever specifically happens in the DLC, the *feeling* of moving through it should favor quiet, weighted discovery over active construction or striving.

## Part Two: What Maggie Aarden Adds as a Generative Input

The developer explicitly asked that Maggie — the DLC's confirmed recruitable and romanceable companion — be factored into this derivation, since her presence during the DLC is a given regardless of what the story turns out to be.

**Her established personality as a shape-setting input:** Maggie is an SP 5w6 ("The Castle") — the most withdrawn variant of an already withdrawn type, someone who builds security by minimizing her own needs and protecting her private space, who would rather be left alone with her own expertise than responsible for anyone else, but whose w6 loyalty won't let her fully abandon a community that needs her even when every other part of her wants to. This is, structurally, the identical shape Guideline 1 predicts for Byrd itself: quietly, reluctantly load-bearing, competent past the point anyone properly credits, present out of duty rather than desire.

**Her established personal history as a shape-setting input:** Maggie became the robot signatory of the Falkland Treaty under deliberately loaded, coercive circumstances — a historically consequential act she never fully, freely consented to, whose true nature the wider country's own institutions still haven't fully reckoned with (per the Super-Ultra-Megasheet's own Cross-Regional Throughway 3, tracing this same coercion's influence into at least three living Concordia districts). This mirrors Guideline 4 almost exactly: an unacknowledged historical debt or truth, waiting to surface.

**The extrapolation:** a companion whose *personal* arc rhymes with her own city's *civic* arc is a pattern this project's own material actively rewards — Concordia's own district-level work repeatedly ties individual personality to place this way, and it's worth treating as a genuine design target here rather than an accident to avoid. Maggie's own reluctant, withdrawn competence and her own buried historical coercion are not incidental facts sitting beside Byrd's story — by every pattern this project has established, they should be read as a smaller-scale echo of whatever Byrd's own civic arc turns out to be.

**Structural requirements, independent of story content:** Maggie's own romance questline is bound by the project's standing binding laws regardless of what Byrd's central crisis turns out to be — a genuine trade-off with no clean good ending (per the "No Good Endings" law), 2-5 mutually exclusive perks paired with a player perk (per the "Dual-Outcome Companion Perks" law), and companion-mediated access to something exclusive to whichever branch the player chooses (per the "Companion-Mediated Access" law). Given her established history, the shape of that trade-off practically writes itself even without deciding its content: something to do with whether her own coerced past becomes known publicly or stays buried, each option costing something real and irreversible.

## Part Three: Mechanical Constraints, Independent of Story Content

These apply to Byrd's DLC the same way they apply to every other subnet DLC, regardless of what its actual central conflict turns out to be:

- **Scope:** approximately 4-6 hours for the main questline (trigger to completion of the central problem), approximately 10-20 hours of optional side content, per the project's own uniform DLC design standard.
- **Level cap:** +5, like every subnet DLC (2-7); only DLC 1 (Kendra) is the +6 outlier.
- **Intro/end slide structure:** a framed narrated intro sequence establishing background, goals, and stakes, plus end slides detailing the consequences of player choices across multiple factions — the standard model for all six subnet DLCs (only DLC 1 is the structural outlier here, per its own single-character cast). This means Byrd needs genuine internal factions capable of each carrying their own end-slide outcome, substituting for the "multiple cities" every other DLC gets its faction variety from for free.
- **Cross-DLC Bypass Design Law:** Byrd's DLC needs at least three separate forms of bypass-help for each of the other five subnet DLCs (each placed in Byrd's own optional side content, never delivered by a main-questline NPC), plus exactly one guaranteed form of bypass-help specifically relevant to DLC 1's climactic South Pole mission (the Cross-DLC Survival Gifts chain).

## Worth Your Attention

Guideline 2 and Guideline 3, read together, are the most load-bearing pair in this whole document — they're the two places where the rest of the game's own established pattern doesn't just describe Byrd in general terms, but actively rules out one otherwise-obvious answer (survival through importance-driven restraint) while pointing hard toward another (survival through total unknown-ness, paired with a central crisis whose stakes reach measurably beyond Byrd's own borders). The Maggie-Byrd rhyme in Part Two is the most useful single design instinct to carry forward whenever actual development resumes: whatever Byrd's own civic arc turns out to be, her personal arc should very plausibly be read as its echo at individual scale, not a separate story running in parallel beside it.
