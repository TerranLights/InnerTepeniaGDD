# Sanay — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Sanay would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Sanay.md`, `Local_Cultures/Halley_Subnet/Sanay.md`, and `Sanay_
Community_Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred until this
pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30.

**Following `Sanay_Community_Infrastructure.md`'s own precedent:** the source Local Cultures file frames
"Founders' Bedrock" around the real SANAE expedition's own name directly — a GPS-only violation matching
the same issue already caught and routed around for Abowasa, Belgrano, Lazar, and Neumayer. No attribute
below repeats that framing; where founding-era infrastructure is described, it's described by its physical
and structural facts, not by the founding nation's institutional identity.

**Why Sanay's attributes look like nothing else examined so far:** every other Halley subnet city has
either a passive terrain disadvantage (Princess Elisabeth's total wind exposure), a moving foundation
(Halley, Neumayer), or a single defining hazard. Sanay is the subnet's structural outlier in the other
direction — genuine bedrock stability let it take real war damage without being erased, and its economy is
built around function (shipping, logistics, a relay nexus it didn't build for itself) rather than around
managing an ongoing environmental threat.

**Governing facts:** built on Vesleskarvet nunatak, Ahlmannryggen range, Queen Maud Land — genuine bedrock,
inland from the coast; status is damaged but partially operational, the bedrock foundation giving it real
structural resilience without full immunity; Tepenia's actual major shipping/logistics port city (not
necessarily largest by population, but a genuine major hub by function), receiving South African freighter
shipments alongside Belgrano; hosts the Halley subnet's own Arcanet relay nexus, physically located in
Sanay but built and named for Halley, whose residents did the hands-on construction; work is organized
around rotating freighter-arrival crews rather than a fixed day/night schedule; Hwy 7 connects Sanay to the
rest of the subnet and onward to Troll for aviation-based distribution.

## Bedrock Foundation Infrastructure

1. **Structural foundation engineering built directly into stable Vesleskarvet rock**, distinct from any
   ice-shelf or ice-sheet city's own construction constraints — the specific physical advantage that let
   Sanay take real damage without being erased outright.
2. **Vesleskarvet Summit** (established) — the highest point of Sanay's own terrain, panoramic views across
   the Queen Maud Land ice plateau.

## Maritime Shipping & Port Infrastructure

3. **The Shipyard Complex** (established) — the lower city's primary maritime freight infrastructure,
   rebuilt first and fastest after the war per the city's own function-over-comfort priorities.
4. **The Trade Yard** (established) — a physically demarcated port section where incoming Upper Earth
   freighter crews dock and conduct business under defined, bounded protocols, distinct from Palmer City's
   own more open, tourism-facing Upper Earth relationship.
5. **Round-the-clock dockside operations infrastructure**, organized around rotating crews tied to
   freighter arrival and departure times rather than any fixed day/night civic schedule.

## Arcanet Relay Nexus Infrastructure

6. **The Halley Subnet Arcanet Relay Nexus** (established) — semi-distributed across several points along
   the upper clifftops; physically sited in Sanay specifically because Vesleskarvet's bedrock offers a
   stability no ice-founded city could guarantee for permanent critical infrastructure, though built and
   named for Halley, whose residents did the hands-on construction labor.
7. **Bedrock-integrated relay housing and cabling infrastructure**, distinct from any city that would have
   to run comparable infrastructure through or over unstable ice.

## Overland Logistics Infrastructure

8. **The clifftop trucking/shipping depots and import-export business district** (established) —
   coordinating the onward Hwy 7 logistics chain toward Halley and Troll from the upper clifftops, alongside
   the Arcanet nexus itself.
9. **Overland trucking infrastructure via Hwy 7**, distributing goods onward to Halley (which has no docks
   or airstrip of its own) and further to Troll for aviation-based national distribution — the same overland
   chain Belgrano's own attributes describe from its own end.

## War-Damage Infrastructure

10. **Ongoing repair infrastructure calibrated to bedrock-founded damage**, distinct from an ice-founded
    city's own repair challenges — consistent with the established "damage without erasure" status, real
    but non-total harm.
11. **Legible but non-catastrophic damage distribution across the city**, consistent with the established
    "scarred but standing" framing — distinguishing Sanay's ruins-free damage profile from both Princess
    Elisabeth's total destruction and Belgrano's post-war decline.

## Residential & Social Infrastructure

12. **Residential, commercial, and leisure districts stretched along the cliffside** (established) —
    off-shift worker spaces distinct from the shipyard/depot working areas, consistent with the established
    round-the-clock work rhythm requiring genuine off-hours release space.

## Cross-Referenced Extrapolation Findings (Methodology #2)

**What this is:** cross-referencing the 12 attributes above against Sanay's own existing lore —
`Sanay_Full_Extrapolation.md` and `Sanay_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` (which already holds 4 Findings of
its own, written 2026-07-07 — the richest existing base of any Halley subnet city). Sanay is one of this
subnet's three deep-treatment cities per direct developer instruction (alongside Troll and Neumayer), so
this pass runs at Mawson's own depth. Written 2026-07-30.

### Finding A: the residential districts are specifically where Finding 2's own unevenly distributed cost lands

**Combining:** attributes #10–12 (repair infrastructure calibrated to bedrock-founded damage, legible but
non-catastrophic damage distribution, residential/commercial/leisure districts along the cliffside) + the
existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 2 ("function over sentiment" has a real, unevenly distributed
cost — shipyards repaired first and fastest, residential districts deprioritized).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #12's own cliffside residential districts are specifically the physical
geography where Finding 2's already-established social cost lands — a concrete location for an otherwise
abstract civic-values tension.

**3rd-order effect:** gives Finding 2's own genuinely novel social-distinction axis (tracked by workplace,
not national origin or human/robot status) an actual place on the map, rather than leaving it as a stated
demographic pattern with no physical geography attached.

### Finding B: the Trade Yard is the physical zone behind one of this project's most consequential existing Findings

**Combining:** attribute #4 (the Trade Yard) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 3 (Tepenia
has at least two structurally different models for managing Upper Earth contact — Sanay's bounded,
Guangzhou-style zone versus Palmer City's open, individual-tracking model).

**2nd-order effect:** the Trade Yard's confirmed physical existence, arrived at independently through this
Methodology #1/#2 pass, strengthens Finding 3's own broader claim from "plausible proposal" to
"consistent with independently-derived established fact."

**3rd-order effect:** Finding 3 already carries implications reaching beyond Sanay itself — that Tepenia's
Upper Earth policy is locally adapted rather than nationally uniform — so confirming its physical anchor
matters for more than just this one city's own material.

### Finding C: the Relay Nexus building is a third instance of Finding 1's own "does the work, someone else gets the name" pattern

**Combining:** attributes #6–7 (the Halley Subnet Arcanet Relay Nexus, bedrock-integrated relay
infrastructure) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 1 (the uncredited-labor pattern repeating
at two scales — the Arcanet nexus itself, and the subnet-wide naming credit).

**2nd-order effect:** the Nexus building's own everyday name — "the Halley subnet relay," not "the Sanay
relay" — is a third, purely linguistic instance of the identical pattern Finding 1 already traces through
two other domains.

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming as reinforcing evidence for Finding 1's own genuinely open question
(genuine equanimity versus quiet, unvoiced resentment) — a pattern this consistent, showing up unprompted a
third time at the level of ordinary vocabulary, makes the "trained into it repeatedly" reading at least as
plausible as simple contentment.

### Finding D: Sanay's bedrock survival gets the same unremarked-upon treatment as its demographic outlier status

**Combining:** attributes #1–2 (bedrock foundation engineering, Vesleskarvet Summit) + the existing
Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 4 (Sanay's demographic story is ordinary by real-world standards and a
genuine outlier by its own subnet's standards, and the city itself never remarks on it).

**2nd-order effect:** the structural fact of *why* Sanay survived at all — genuine bedrock stability, unique
among damaged Halley subnet cities — receives the identical lack of civic commentary Finding 4 already
identifies at the demographic level.

**3rd-order effect:** extends the Synthesis section's own "notable, unremarked-upon, simply lived with"
throughline to a third domain (after capability-credit and demographic composition) — the very reason the
city still exists doesn't get discussed as remarkable either.

### Finding E: the trucking infrastructure is the concrete mechanism behind Sanay's own material stake in Troll

**Combining:** attributes #8–9 (the clifftop trucking depots and import-export business district, overland
trucking infrastructure via Hwy 7) + the Full Extrapolation's Section II (Sanay's material, not merely
neighborly, stake in Troll's contested airfield control — whoever controls Troll controls downstream
distribution of everything Sanay brings in).

**2nd-order effect:** attributes #8–9 are the literal physical infrastructure implementing that already-
established mutual dependency — the actual trucks and depots moving goods along the route whose endpoint
control is contested.

**3rd-order effect:** worth flagging directly ahead of Troll's own upcoming Findings pass, the same
cross-reference discipline already applied for Abowasa's and Lazar's own Neumayer-facing Findings — this
relationship should be confirmed from both directions, not re-derived independently when Troll's own
material gets cross-referenced next.

### Finding F: the Shipyard Complex carries a double significance — hit hardest, fixed first

**Combining:** attributes #3 and #5 (the Shipyard Complex, round-the-clock dockside operations) + the Full
Extrapolation's Section I (war damage concentrated specifically on the shipyards and dockside infrastructure,
which then received rebuilding priority, fastest).

**2nd-order effect:** the Shipyard Complex is simultaneously the site of Sanay's worst wartime damage and
the site of its fastest post-war recovery — a specific, double-edged significance no other facility in the
city carries.

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming directly as a distinct character trait among this session's damaged-city
attributes — a facility that is both the clearest wound and the clearest proof of recovery in the same
physical location.
