# Halley Subnet — Full Extrapolation (Subnet Ultra-Megasheet, Step 2)

**What this is:** every open question about Halley subnet as a whole given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established across all 8 City Megasheets rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Halley_Subnet_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-10.

---

## I. Each city's own specific role within the subnet's "uncredited load" identity

Mirroring the district-level and Palmer-subnet exercises, but adapted to Halley subnet's own dominant trait — indispensable work that goes uncredited:

- **Halley carries the name, not the function** — the subnet's public face, holding none of its actual technical nerve center.
- **Sanay carries the function, not the name** — the Arcanet nexus, one of Tepenia's largest ports, and none of the credit for either.
- **Neumayer carries the country's whole engineering debt** — the Rastra, the Amundsen Tower, the Cradle chamber, three separate eras, one uncredited city.
- **Troll carries the chokepoint** — the single asset whose control decides how much of the above actually reaches anyone.
- **Belgrano carries the founding act** — the Byrd expedition, the vehicle, the archive, all given away to a city that isn't even in this subnet.
- **Lazar carries the scale** — too large to erase, absorbing the war's worst blows simply by being big enough to survive them.
- **Princess Elisabeth carried total self-sufficiency** — and lost everything the moment the one connection holding it together failed.
- **Abowasa carries the answer** — the bedrock stability its own neighbors' engineering has spent generations working around.

## II. The Rastra's own present-day civic memory — resolved as parallel, not competing, hometown claims

**Proposed:** given the Rastra's own founding chain now spans two cities (Belgrano's need and expedition, Neumayer's redesign), the most honest resolution is that both cities plausibly claim it, for different and equally earned reasons, without either claim actually canceling the other. Belgrano remembers the Rastra as *its own* — the vehicle its crews built, drove, and named, the founding act that gave the city its proudest historical chapter. Neumayer, per its own established precision-first, private-record-keeping culture, would be far less likely to publicly claim the Rastra at all — but its own internal engineering archives, consistent with the same rigor that tracks the Tower and the Cradle chamber, would show the redesign as clearly and completely as anything else the city has ever produced. The Rastra's true origin isn't contested; it's simply asymmetric — one city talks about it, the other one only writes it down.

## III. Whether Troll's own contested control has ever put a Rastra shipment at risk

**Proposed:** yes, plausibly, in the same secondary-freight capacity already proposed for the Cradle's own chamber shipments (Troll Full Extrapolation, Section VI) — Rastra parts and replacement components, manufactured at Belgrano's own established mechanic/fabrication economy, would be exactly the kind of incidental cargo passing through Troll's freight network on the way to other subnets. This gives Troll's own contested airfield one more, very concrete stake: not just goods and not just the literal means of robot creation, but the supply of spare parts for the single most common vehicle in the entire country.

## IV. A Proposed Notable Figure: The Redesign Engineer

**Proposed, new figure:** a founding-era Neumayer engineer — placeholder name **Britta Falk-Yamane** — credited within Neumayer's own internal archive (never publicly, consistent with the city's established character) as the lead figure who reverse-engineered Belgrano's original hauler and drew the schematics that became the Rastra. A natural companion figure to Neumayer's own already-proposed Chief Engineer Ingrid Osei-Falkenberg (the Tower's own designer, a later generation): two engineers, generations apart, both responsible for a piece of Tepenia every citizen depends on, neither remembered outside a single city's own private records.

## Worth Your Attention

Section II is the standout item in this Extrapolation — it resolves what could have been read as a contradiction (which city "owns" the Rastra's legacy) by recognizing that Belgrano's and Neumayer's own established civic characters would naturally produce two different, non-competing relationships to the same shared achievement: one remembers loudly, the other simply keeps the record. That asymmetry isn't a gap to fix. It's Halley subnet's whole defining trait, playing out one more time, in miniature, between two of its own cities.
