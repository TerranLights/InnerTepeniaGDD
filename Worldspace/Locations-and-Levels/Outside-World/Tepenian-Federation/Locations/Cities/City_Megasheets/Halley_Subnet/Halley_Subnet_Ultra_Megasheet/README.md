*The subnet named for a city that gave away its own nerve center — Halley traded real control for naming honor, Sanay does the uncredited work underneath it, and Neumayer's own schematics quietly redesigned the vehicle every Tepenian drives, then built the nation's greatest engineering achievement, then designed its literal robot-creation infrastructure, across three different eras of its own history, without anyone remembering whose design any of it was. ## Who Lives Here, and Why

Halley subnet's demographic story is the most nation-diverse founding pattern of any Tepenian subnet examined so far — British (Halley), German (Neumayer), Norwegian (Troll), Belgian (Princess Elisabeth), Argentine (Belgrano), South African (Sanay), Russian-and-unclaimed (Lazar's Novolazarevskaya/Maitri coalescence), and jointly Finnish-Swedish (Abowasa) founding populations, eight genuinely distinct national or dual-national origins across eight cities. Almost every one of these founding populations was later diluted by the same eventual American-led demographic majority that recurs across most of Tepenia — except Sanay, whose own dilution broke pattern and produced a German plurality instead, and Lazar and Abowasa, whose plural founding stories never had one clean national population to dilute in the first place.

This produces a subnet whose residents arrived for the widest range of original reasons of any Tepenian subnet — scientific mission (Neumayer, Abowasa, Sanay), military aviation (Belgrano, Troll), pure geographic accommodation (Halley, built specifically to move with unstable ice), and sheer administrative overflow (Lazar's Maitri absorption) — and whose people, across nearly every one of these origins, ended up doing something for the rest of Tepenia that the rest of Tepenia doesn't fully credit them for.

## What It Feels Like

Moving through Halley subnet means moving through a landscape defined by unstable ground and the wildly different ways its cities chose to answer that instability. Halley's own ski-mounted modules dig into drifting ice and continuously propel the city toward the mainland, staying perpetually ahead of the shelf's own seaward creep. Neumayer's hydraulic-leg architecture, extended from a single research station's design into a whole city's built character, treats the same instability as a measurement-and-engineering problem rather than a reason to keep moving. Abowasa sits on genuine bedrock, needing neither trick, and has turned that stability into its own quiet export. Princess Elisabeth built its entire survival on total, actively-managed self-sufficiency and lost everything the moment its own internal connections failed rather than the systems themselves.

Then the subnet's economic backbone reveals itself as a genuine, load-bearing chain: freight lands at Sanay's enormous working port, splits toward Troll's contested airfield or directly along Hwy 7, and from there reaches Halley, Neumayer, Abowasa, and the rest of the subnet's interior. Lazar towers over all of it — Tepenia's single largest city, holographic advertisements glowing over a skyline that is simultaneously the subnet's most vibrant district and, in its former-Maitri half, its most visibly war-scarred one. And south of all of it, Belgrano's Boneyard Times quietly persist: an airbase that survived the war intact and lost the peace instead, run today by people whose discipline outlasted the institution that built it.

## Personality (A Whole-Subnet Read)

Halley subnet doesn't reduce to one Enneagram profile any more than Concordia's districts or Palmer subnet's cities did, but its own recurring pattern is unusually legible: a subnet whose defining civic instinct, across at least five of its eight cities, is to do genuinely consequential work and treat drawing attention to that work as a mild civic failure. Neumayer processes its own uncredited national-scale engineering legacy through meticulous private record-keeping rather than public advocacy. Sanay's own faction identity is explicitly named "Competence Without Commentary." Halley itself gave away its own subnet's technical nerve center in exchange for a naming honor and appears to feel no ongoing grievance about it. Belgrano's post-war survival runs on inherited habit rather than institutional pride. Even Abowasa's whole founding story — two nations, quietly working out a shared life at household scale — carries the same undramatic, function-first register. Halley subnet's collective personality, read across all eight cities, is Competency turned inward: capability that consistently prefers to simply keep functioning over being recognized for it.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Queen Maud Land's real multinational research presence

The real Queen Maud Land sector hosts research stations from at least nine different nations within a relatively compact geographic area — Norway, Britain, Germany, Belgium, South Africa, Russia, Finland, Sweden, and others, each pursuing largely independent scientific missions within genuine physical proximity to each other.

**What this gives Halley subnet:** direct grounding for its own unusually high founding-nation diversity relative to other Tepenian subnets — eight genuinely distinct national or dual-national origins across eight cities is a fictionalized but structurally honest reflection of how densely multinational this specific real-world region actually is.

### Secondary: Institutions whose fame exceeds their own name recognition — Bell Labs, JPL, Bletchley Park

Already researched individually for Neumayer, these three institutions apply at whole-subnet scale: each demonstrates that genuinely world-changing work can be performed by an institution the wider public never learns to associate with the result — exactly the pattern recurring across Neumayer, Sanay, and Halley all at once.

### Secondary: Cities and regions defined by contested infrastructure chokepoints — the Berlin Airlift

Already researched for Troll specifically, the Berlin Airlift's core lesson (control of one airfield determining whether an entire population eats) applies at full subnet scale — Troll's own contested control doesn't just determine Troll's fate, per this Ultra-Megasheet's own cross-city Throughways, it determines Halley's and shapes Sanay's distribution reach as well.

### Supporting: Chatham Dockyard and other "survived the disaster, declined in the peace" precedents

Already researched for Belgrano specifically, this pattern is worth naming as a genuine subnet-scale possibility — Belgrano's own Boneyard Times may not be a unique story within Halley subnet so much as the clearest, most fully developed instance of a decline-after-survival arc other lightly-damaged subnet cities could plausibly share in smaller ways.

# Halley Subnet — Full Extrapolation (Subnet Ultra-Megasheet, Step 2)

**What this is:** every open question about Halley subnet as a whole given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established across all 8 City Megasheets rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Halley_Subnet_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-10.

## I. Each city's own specific role within the subnet's "uncredited load" identity

Mirroring the district-level and Palmer-subnet exercises, but adapted to Halley subnet's own dominant trait — indispensable work that goes uncredited:

- **Halley carries the name, not the function** — the subnet's public face, holding none of its actual technical nerve center.
- **Sanay carries the function, not the name** — the Arcanet nexus, one of Tepenia's largest ports, and none of the credit for either.
- **Neumayer carries the country's whole engineering debt** — the Rastra, the Amundsen Tower, the Cradle chamber, three separate eras, one uncredited city.
- **Troll carries the chokepoint** — the single asset whose control decides how much of the above actually reaches anyone.
- **Belgrano carries the founding act** — the Byrd expedition, the vehicle, the archive, all given away to a city that isn't even in this subnet.
- **Lazar carries the scale** — too large to erase, absorbing the war's worst blows simply by being big enough to survive them.
- **Princess Elisabeth carried total self-sufficiency** — and lost everything the moment the one connection holding it together failed.
- **Abowasa carries the answer** — the bedrock stability its own neighbors' engineering has spent generations working around.

---

## II. The Rastra's own present-day civic memory — resolved as parallel, not competing, hometown claims

**Proposed:** given the Rastra's own founding chain now spans two cities (Belgrano's need and expedition, Neumayer's redesign), the most honest resolution is that both cities plausibly claim it, for different and equally earned reasons, without either claim actually canceling the other. Belgrano remembers the Rastra as *its own* — the vehicle its crews built, drove, and named, the founding act that gave the city its proudest historical chapter. Neumayer, per its own established precision-first, private-record-keeping culture, would be far less likely to publicly claim the Rastra at all — but its own internal engineering archives, consistent with the same rigor that tracks the Tower and the Cradle chamber, would show the redesign as clearly and completely as anything else the city has ever produced. The Rastra's true origin isn't contested; it's simply asymmetric — one city talks about it, the other one only writes it down.

---

## III. Whether Troll's own contested control has ever put a Rastra shipment at risk

**Proposed:** yes, plausibly, in the same secondary-freight capacity already proposed for the Cradle's own chamber shipments (Troll Full Extrapolation, Section VI) — Rastra parts and replacement components, manufactured at Belgrano's own established mechanic/fabrication economy, would be exactly the kind of incidental cargo passing through Troll's freight network on the way to other subnets. This gives Troll's own contested airfield one more, very concrete stake: not just goods and not just the literal means of robot creation, but the supply of spare parts for the single most common vehicle in the entire country.

---

## IV. A Proposed Notable Figure: The Redesign Engineer

**Proposed, new figure:** a founding-era Neumayer engineer — placeholder name **Britta Falk-Yamane** — credited within Neumayer's own internal archive (never publicly, consistent with the city's established character) as the lead figure who reverse-engineered Belgrano's original hauler and drew the schematics that became the Rastra. A natural companion figure to Neumayer's own already-proposed Chief Engineer Ingrid Osei-Falkenberg (the Tower's own designer, a later generation): two engineers, generations apart, both responsible for a piece of Tepenia every citizen depends on, neither remembered outside a single city's own private records.

---

## Worth Your Attention

Section II is the standout item in this Extrapolation — it resolves what could have been read as a contradiction (which city "owns" the Rastra's legacy) by recognizing that Belgrano's and Neumayer's own established civic characters would naturally produce two different, non-competing relationships to the same shared achievement: one remembers loudly, the other simply keeps the record. That asymmetry isn't a gap to fix. It's Halley subnet's whole defining trait, playing out one more time, in miniature, between two of its own cities.

# Halley Subnet — Cross-Reference Synthesis (Subnet Ultra-Megasheet, Step 3)

**What this is:** as with Concordia's and Palmer subnet's own Ultra-Megasheets, this step is largely already complete — the cross-city Patterns and Throughways work in this same folder already did the implication-hunting this step exists to produce. The work here is selecting, ranking, and presenting the strongest of that material as Halley subnet's own headline findings. Written 2026-07-10.

---

## Finding 1: One city's uncredited engineering spans the entire subnet's history, in three separate eras

**Combining:** Neumayer's own founding-era redesign of Belgrano's crude first-generation hauler into the vehicle that became the Rastra, in Neumayer's very first years after the Falkland Treaty (`Halley_Cross_City_Patterns.md`, Pattern 2; `Halley_Cross_City_Throughways.md`, Throughway 2) + its later, matured-capacity design of the Amundsen Tower's own schematics + its most recent design of the current-generation Cradle fabrication-synthesis chamber schematic.

**2nd-order effect:** Neumayer isn't simply a city with one uncredited achievement and an unlucky pattern of not getting named for it — it has now produced three, spanning from its own earliest years through to the present day, at three completely different scales (a vehicle every Tepenian drives, the nation's single greatest engineering project, the literal ongoing mechanism of robot creation).

**3rd-order effect:** this makes Neumayer's own established character — quiet, precision-first, allergic to public credit-seeking — not merely a personality trait but a demonstrated, repeated behavior across the city's entire existence, present before it had any reputation to protect and unchanged after it earned one.

**4th-order effect:** Neumayer is, by a wide margin, the single most load-bearing uncredited city in this entire Megasheet project so far — not one of several similar cases, but the clearest possible example of what "does the real work, someone else gets the name" looks like when it isn't a coincidence but a whole city's defining civic behavior, sustained across centuries.

---

## Finding 2: Byrd's founding runs through four cities and two subnets, and Neumayer's own role was previously invisible

**Combining:** Belgrano's need for a working hauler and its own expedition, archives, and all-robot crew (Belgrano Mega-Init) + Neumayer's quiet redesign of that hauler before the expedition ever departed (`Halley_Cross_City_Throughways.md`, Throughway 2 and 3) + Palmer City's and Rothera's own supporting records, shipped across the Weddell Sea (per this project's own Palmer Subnet Ultra-Megasheet).

**2nd-order effect:** Byrd's own rediscovery-story identity, previously understood as owing a debt to three cities across two subnets, now owes a debt to four — Belgrano's own contribution was never singular, it was itself dependent on a fifth city's quiet assistance that no existing record, until this session, connected to the expedition at all.

**3rd-order effect:** this means Byrd's own established culture — which currently reflects none of these debts — is missing not three points of origin but four, and the newest of those four (Neumayer) was invisible even to this project's own prior cross-referencing work until the developer's own direct addition surfaced it.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a genuine demonstration of how this whole Ultra-Megasheet process is meant to work — even a thoroughly cross-referenced chain can still be missing a link, and the right piece of new information can extend it further than the existing synthesis alone would have found.

---

## Finding 3: The Troll-Sanay-Halley dependency chain now has a fourth stake riding on it

**Combining:** Sanay's own established role as the subnet's freight entry point, splitting goods toward Troll's airfield or directly along Hwy 7 (`Halley_Cross_City_Throughways.md`, Throughway 1) + Troll's own fractured, contested control + Halley's total dependence on that same chain, having deliberately forgone its own docks and airstrip + this Ultra-Megasheet's own Full Extrapolation proposal (Section III) that Rastra parts, manufactured at Belgrano, plausibly pass through the same freight network as incidental cargo.

**2nd-order effect:** Troll's own contested control doesn't just determine whether Sanay's imports reach Halley — it now also plausibly touches the supply of spare parts for Tepenia's single most common vehicle, a stake with genuinely nationwide relevance rather than one confined to the Halley subnet's own internal politics.

**3rd-order effect:** this raises Troll's own already-established stakes (DLC 5's central conflict, per Troll's own Megasheet) one level further — a resolution here doesn't just decide Halley's winter or Sanay's distribution reach, it plausibly has some bearing on Rastra maintenance and replacement capacity for cities well outside the Halley subnet entirely.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a concrete way to raise DLC 5's own perceived stakes without inventing anything new — the existing supply chain already reaches this far; the only new step is naming it explicitly.

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across All Three Findings

All three findings trace back to the same underlying shape: things Halley subnet produces reach much further than anyone standing inside the subnet, or even inside this project's own prior research, initially accounted for. Neumayer's engineering reaches across three centuries and every corner of the country (Finding 1). Byrd's own founding reaches across two subnets and now a newly-discovered fourth city (Finding 2). Troll's contested airfield reaches, however indirectly, into the maintenance of a vehicle every Tepenian depends on (Finding 3). Halley subnet's real significance, at every scale this synthesis has examined, keeps turning out to be larger than its own public reputation — the same finding this Ultra-Megasheet's own Mega-Init named as the subnet's defining trait, now confirmed a third and fourth time over.

---

## Worth Your Attention

Finding 1 is the most significant of the three, and specifically because of how it came to be found: it started as a modest addition to an already-completed research pass, and turned out to reveal that Neumayer's uncredited legacy was even deeper and older than this Ultra-Megasheet's own first draft had already established. Worth remembering for the remaining subnets still to come — this kind of synthesis is never fully finished until the developer has had a chance to add the pieces that only they know are missing.
