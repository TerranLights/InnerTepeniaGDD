# Troll — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Troll given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Troll_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-09.

---

## I. Who Controls the Airfield: Nobody, Fully — A Genuinely Fractured Situation

**Proposed:** rather than resolving this into one clear controlling faction, the most useful answer respects that this is explicitly flagged as DLC 5's own working central conflict — a question meant to be resolved through player choice, not predetermined here. What's proposed instead is the *shape* of the problem: multiple claimants currently hold partial, overlapping control — one faction holds the physical runway and control tower, another controls the fuel depot, another controls the trucking dispatch that actually moves freight to and from the field. No single group holds all of it, which is precisely what makes it worth fighting over and precisely why it needs outside intervention to resolve.

---

## II. What Aircraft Remain: A Working Cargo Fleet, Mostly Grounded, Partially Maintained

**Proposed:** a mix of cargo transport aircraft, consistent with the established intermodal freight economy, alongside a smaller number of light passenger and personnel aircraft. Most sit grounded and non-operational post-war, but a genuine handful are kept airworthy through continuous maintenance — because Troll's whole post-war economy depends on at least some ongoing flight capacity, not because anyone particularly wants to invest in aviation for its own sake anymore.

---

## III. St. Roald Veneration: Real, But Quietly Overshadowed

**Proposed:** yes, genuine St. Roald (Amundsen) veneration persists at Troll, a civic tradition tracing back to the 1939 Queen Maud Land claim and the Polar Institute's own founding-era history — but it has been increasingly overshadowed by the airfield-conflict identity in recent generations. The veneration survives as a quieter, more private practice among older residents and those who've kept up the city's older civic traditions, while the city's dominant public identity has shifted almost entirely to "whoever holds the runway." A real tension between private reverence and public identity, not a resolved question either way.

---

## IV. The Two Placeholder Holidays, Developed

- **St. Roald's Landing** — commemorating Amundsen's own historic first arrival at the South Pole, the older, quieter holiday tied to the city's founding Norwegian heritage.
- **Runway Day** — a newer, more civically dominant holiday specifically celebrating the airfield's own founding or a significant expansion, representing the "whoever holds the runway" identity that has increasingly overtaken the older Saint-veneration one. The contrast between the two holidays mirrors the city's own established identity shift directly.

## VI. Fabrication Chambers Passing Through Troll's Freight Network: Plausible, Secondary Use

**Proposed, directly answering the question surfaced in the Mega-Init:** yes, plausibly, but as secondary rather than primary traffic. Soyuz already has its own dedicated, direct aviation route to Dome Fuji specifically for chamber and general supply delivery — that route wouldn't run through Troll. But given Troll's established centrality to the whole Halley subnet's actual goods-distribution logic, it's plausible that Soyuz-manufactured chambers destined for other cities beyond Dome Fuji's own dedicated route would have historically passed through Troll's broader freight network at some point, incidental rather than dedicated traffic layered onto everything else the airfield already moves.
