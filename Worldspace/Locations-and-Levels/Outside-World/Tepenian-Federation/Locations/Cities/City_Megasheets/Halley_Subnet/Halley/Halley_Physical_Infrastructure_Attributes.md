# Halley — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Halley would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Halley.md`, `Local_Cultures/Halley_Subnet/Halley.md`, and `Halley_
Community_Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred until this
pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30. Begins the Halley subnet.

**Why Halley's attributes look like nothing else examined so far:** every other city in this pass sits on
fixed ground, however inhospitable. Halley sits on the Brunt Ice Shelf — moving ice, not bedrock — and its
whole architecture is built around that fact: modular structures actively, continuously propelled toward
the mainland to stay ahead of the shelf's own seaward drift, rather than fixed buildings that occasionally
need repair.

**Governing facts:** floating ice shelf, Weddell Sea, Coats Land coast; status is damaged but partially
operational; the city's own founding design philosophy was to build for relocation rather than resist ice
movement — ski-mounted, tracked platforms that continuously self-propel toward the mainland; deliberately
has no docks or airstrip of its own — a design choice, not an oversight, since fixed maritime/aviation
infrastructure would simply be carried away from any useful position by the ice over time; all resupply
arrives overland, trucked in via a connector road to Hwy 7 from whichever of Belgrano or Sanay currently has
an open shipping passage; Hwy 59 functions simultaneously as a physical highway and the Arcanet cable
corridor south to Amundsen Station, meaning damage to it disrupts both transit and communications at once;
population ~1.09-1.45 million.

---

## Ice Shelf Mobility Infrastructure (the defining, unique feature)

1. **Ski-mounted platform structures with active tracked propulsion** (established) — modular buildings
   that continuously, deliberately move toward the mainland, not simply towed in a crisis.
2. **Dedicated, permanent engineering infrastructure for the track/ski propulsion systems themselves** — a
   genuinely ongoing engineering discipline, unlike any static city's maintenance needs, since the
   structures are never truly "finished" moving.
3. **Snowmobile-based internal transit**, humans and robots riding together across the municipal grounds
   (established) — a fundamentally different internal-transit model from any fixed-road or tram network a
   stationary city would use.

## Deliberate Absence of Fixed Maritime/Aviation Infrastructure

4. **No docks or airstrip of Halley's own** (established, a genuine design choice) — fixed maritime or
   aviation infrastructure would simply be carried away from any useful position by the ice shelf's own
   drift over time, so none was ever built.
5. **An overland connector road to Hwy 7**, receiving goods trucked in from whichever of Belgrano or Sanay
   currently has an open shipping passage — the actual physical resupply chain, standing in for the direct
   maritime access most coastal cities would have.

## Ice Shelf Structural Monitoring

6. **Calving-zone proximity monitoring**, given the real, ongoing risk that sections of the shelf will
   separate — a genuinely distinct hazard class from any land-based city's structural concerns.
7. **Mobility-status assessment infrastructure**, checking whether each ski-mounted module remains movable
   — distinct from ordinary structural damage assessment, since war damage here plausibly left some
   sections physically stuck even where ice dynamics would normally require relocating them.

## Historical & Archive Infrastructure

8. **The Position Archive** (established) — housing accumulated survey records, plausibly Halley's only
   surviving trace of its own lost history.
9. **The Old Positions themselves as accessible physical sites** (established) — abandoned infrastructure
   remaining on the ice at varying distances from the current city, a literal, walkable physical record of
   the city's own movement history.

## Hwy 59 / Arcanet Corridor Infrastructure

10. **Combined highway/Arcanet cable corridor infrastructure** (established) — Hwy 59 carries both physical
    transit and the Atlantic subnet's own communications relay to Amundsen Station in a single corridor,
    the single most strategically critical piece of infrastructure in the whole subnet.

## Collective Decision-Making Infrastructure

11. **Dedicated civic deliberation space for relocation decisions** — given the established fact that when
    and where to move is "a recurring civic conversation rather than a crisis," some physical space for
    this genuinely unusual, ongoing collective process plausibly exists.

## Ice-Reading & Vocational Knowledge Infrastructure

12. **Regional ice-condition monitoring and observation posts**, tracking Belgrano's and Sanay's own
    shipping passages even though neither sits at Halley itself — supporting the established vocational
    tradition of residents "reading ice conditions the way agricultural communities read weather."
