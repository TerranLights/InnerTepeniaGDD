## What It Feels Like

"The Airbase That Never Stood Down" names Belgrano's living, second interwar-era identity precisely: a hard, disciplined, function-before-comfort aviation hub, founded by Argentine Air Force exiles and carried forward almost unchanged by a later demographic majority that never found reason to soften it. This is a genuinely different kind of survival story than most of the Halley subnet's other cities — Belgrano wasn't destroyed in the war. It survived intact, then declined afterward, slowly, into what its own remaining residents call **the Boneyard Times**: not a dead city, but a dying one that stopped dying at some equilibrium point and simply kept existing, run by people who inherited the founding discipline as habit and inheritance rather than as service to an institution that still exists. The city's proudest historical chapter belongs to its earlier, fully-functioning era: Belgrano built Tepenia's first native overland vehicles, "las Arrastradoras" (worn down over generations into the international "Rastra"), and sent an all-robot crew that discovered the buried ruins of Byrd Station — using maps drawn from its own archives, with material support shipped in from Palmer City and Rothera. That same garage-and-engine-repair competence became one of the Boneyard Times' few genuinely load-bearing skills once the founding institution's backing was gone.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Newcastle/Tyneside, England

Genuine shipbuilding industrial heritage paired with a real, still-living working-class rock culture — a city whose civic identity was built on heavy engineering and never abandoned it even as the industry that built it declined.

**What this gives Belgrano:** the tightest possible match for the union of Belgrano's own established traits — a hard, function-first industrial base and a genuine, unpolished rock scene coexisting in the same city, exactly as established in Belgrano's own Garage Rock culture and mechanic subculture.

### Primary: 1960s/70s Birmingham, England

Black Sabbath formed among working-class factory workers in Aston; Tony Iommi's guitar-defining sound traces directly to a factory-press injury forcing him to adapt his playing — heavy metal's entire sonic identity born from an actual mechanic-adjacent industrial accident.

**What this gives Belgrano:** a real precedent for a city's industrial/mechanical culture producing genuine artistic innovation rather than merely coexisting alongside it — a direct echo of Belgrano's own claim that "for anything with an engine, there's reliably somebody in Belgrano who can repair it," extended into the idea that the same hands-on mechanical culture could plausibly produce real musical innovation, not just competent imitation.

### Secondary: Arkhangelsk, Russia

A historic White Sea shipping and timber port, remote and function-first rather than glamorous.

**What this gives Belgrano:** reinforces the remote-frontier-port framing already established at Belgrano's own Weddell Sea coastal position, a real precedent for a city whose importance is purely operational rather than symbolic.

### Secondary: 1970s Detroit, Michigan

Birthplace of garage rock and proto-punk (MC5, the Stooges), genuine rustbelt mechanic culture, and later industrial decline.

**What this gives Belgrano:** perhaps the single closest real-world parallel across this whole research pass — a city whose garage rock scene and mechanic culture were the same culture wearing two faces, later followed by a genuine industrial decline that echoes the Boneyard Times almost point for point.

### Secondary: Cleveland, Ohio

The actual birthplace of the term "rock and roll" (DJ Alan Freed, 1951) and the first rock concert ever held, in a working-class industrial rustbelt city.

**What this gives Belgrano:** reinforces the working-class-industrial-city-as-genuine-rock-birthplace framing, distinct from cities where rock culture is imported or aspirational rather than native to the place.

### Supporting: Chatham, Kent, England (Chatham Dockyard)

A 400-year Royal Navy dockyard town whose 1984 closure caused roughly 23.5% unemployment and a real, documented community decline.

**What this gives Belgrano:** the clearest available real-world precedent for the Boneyard Times specifically — a functional institutional town that didn't die when its founding institution left, but visibly, measurably declined, exactly the shape of Belgrano's own post-war story.

## Worth Your Attention

1970s Detroit is the standout of this pass — its garage-rock/mechanic-culture duality followed by genuine industrial decline isn't just a thematic echo of Belgrano's story, it's nearly a structural blueprint for it: the same culture that builds engines building music, followed by the same culture surviving its own institution's collapse. Chatham Dockyard is the second most valuable pick specifically because it names something the other picks don't — a real, measured precedent for "survived the disaster, declined in the peace," which is exactly the shape of the Boneyard Times.
