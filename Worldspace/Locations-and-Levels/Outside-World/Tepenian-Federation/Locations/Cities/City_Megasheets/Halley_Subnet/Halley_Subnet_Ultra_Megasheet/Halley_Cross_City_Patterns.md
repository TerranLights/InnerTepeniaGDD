# Halley Subnet — Cross-City Patterns

**What this is:** common factors recurring across Halley subnet's 8 City Megasheets (Halley, Neumayer, Troll, Princess Elisabeth, Belgrano, Sanay, Lazar, Abowasa). Several of these patterns are already partially named inside individual cities' own Cross-Reference Syntheses — this document consolidates them into a single subnet-wide picture and extends them where the material allows. Written 2026-07-10.

---

## Pattern 1: Halley subnet contains a complete, four-category taxonomy of how Tepenian cities take war damage — one city per category

Across this Megasheet project's own accumulated framework, four distinct damage patterns have emerged, and Halley subnet alone contains a clean example of every single one:

- **Passive, age-favoring resilience** (older, durably-built cores survive better than newer additions): **Lazar**, whose century-old Novolazarevskaya core took proportionally less damage than its faster-built former-Maitri expansion (Lazar Cross-Reference, Finding 1) — the third confirmed instance of this pattern project-wide, after Port Lockroy and Fort McMurdo.
- **Active, recency-favoring resilience** (a city's own defining resilience mechanism rewards whichever parts were most recently engineered): **Halley**, whose continuously-relocating ski-mounted modules mean newer sections stay mobile and safe while older, less recently maintained sections are stuck and damaged (Halley Cross-Reference, Finding 3) — the sole inversion of the age-favoring pattern.
- **Single-point-of-failure vulnerability** (a strike against one connective chokepoint disables an otherwise redundant system): **Princess Elisabeth**, whose buried energy reserve plausibly survives intact but disconnected, cut off at its integration points rather than destroyed outright (Princess Elisabeth Cross-Reference, Finding 3).
- **Function-targeted damage** (an attacker disables a specific capability, sparing the surrounding population): **Abowasa**, whose research and technical infrastructure took the brunt of the damage while its household-and-neighborhood residential fabric survived largely intact (Abowasa Cross-Reference, Finding 2).

No other single subnet in this project holds a complete instance of all four categories. Halley subnet is, in effect, this whole project's own reference case for how differently a war can land on genuinely similar cities.

## Pattern 2: A recurring "does the real work, someone else gets the name" trait, at four different scales — and Neumayer alone shows it three times

- **Halley** built the Sanay Arcanet relay nexus by hand, then accepted the subnet's own naming honor in place of holding its technical control (Halley Mega-Init, Personality).
- **Sanay** hosts that same nexus and one of Tepenia's largest ports, without seeking or receiving comparable public credit — its own Cross-Reference Synthesis (Finding 1) names this as a lived pattern repeating at two scales already (the nexus, and the subnet's own naming).
- **Neumayer**, in its own very first years after the Falkland Treaty — before it had anything like a reputation to protect — reverse-engineered and redesigned Belgrano's crude first-generation tracked hauler into the vehicle that actually completed the crossing to Byrd, becoming the template for every Rastra built across Tepenia since *(established 2026-07-10)*. Generations later, once its engineering capacity had matured, it designed the Amundsen Tower's own schematics. More recently still, it designed the current-generation fabrication-synthesis chamber used by the Cradle's active manufacturing sites. None of the three — not the Rastra, not the Tower, not the chamber — carries Neumayer's name forward anywhere in the country (Neumayer Local Cultures; Neumayer Cross-Reference, Finding 1).

**Four instances across four cities' worth of scale, but the same single city — Neumayer — accounts for three of them on its own,** spanning from its own founding years through to the present day. Read together with Halley's and Sanay's own instances, this isn't three or four unrelated coincidences. It's Halley subnet's actual defining civic character: a place that keeps producing indispensable, uncredited work, and has done so since its very earliest years.

## Pattern 3: A ground-stability triangle — two cities living with the problem, one city holding the answer

Abowasa's own Cross-Reference Synthesis (Finding 3) already draws this triangle directly: **Halley** (ski-mounted, continuously relocating, built on drifting ice shelf) and **Neumayer** (hydraulic-leg architecture, built on a different unstable ice shelf) both live with the same underlying problem — no fixed ground to build on — while **Abowasa** sits on genuine mainland bedrock and has developed real geological and structural-stability research expertise as its own established economic niche. Abowasa's own file proposes a plausible, currently unstated consulting or trade relationship: the one city in the triangle with stable ground holding research directly relevant to the two cities that don't.

## Pattern 4: Two entirely different shapes of plural founding, both defying the subnet's usual dilution story

Most Halley subnet cities follow the same demographic shape — one founding nation, later diluted to Notable tier by an eventual American-led majority. Two cities break this pattern in opposite directions:

- **Lazar** was never founded by one nation at all — two separate settlements (Novolazarevskaya, the unoccupied Maitri site) coalesced into Tepenia's single largest city, with no clean founding-population story to dilute in the first place (Lazar Mega-Init, "Who Lives Here, and Why").
- **Abowasa** was jointly founded by two nations simultaneously (Finland and Sweden) at Tepenia's smallest, most intimate civic scale — plurality expressed as household-level negotiation rather than demographic mathematics (Abowasa Mega-Init, One-Line Pitch).

**Halley subnet holds both of this project's clearest examples of founding plurality — one at maximum scale, one at minimum scale** — with no other subnet currently showing an equivalent pair.

---

## Pattern 5: Function-first, credit-averse civic character running through most of the subnet

Beyond Pattern 2's specific "uncredited work" instances, a broader function-first character recurs across at least five of Halley subnet's eight cities: Belgrano's whole post-war "Boneyard Times" identity runs on inherited institutional habit rather than active ideology; Sanay's "Competence Without Commentary" faction identity explicitly refuses to frame its own survival as luck or specialness worth discussing; Troll's prosperity shows up as quietly higher-quality goods rather than ostentation; Neumayer processes its own uncredited Tower legacy through meticulous internal record-keeping rather than public advocacy; and Abowasa's whole founding character optimized for balance and quality of life rather than grinding through hardship. Halley subnet's overall civic register, more than any other subnet's, treats visible self-promotion as something close to a mild civic failure.

## Worth Your Attention

Pattern 1 and Pattern 2 are now running neck and neck for the standout of this file. Pattern 1 remains the sharpest structural claim — no other subnet in this project contains a complete instance of every established damage-pattern category. But Pattern 2's newly-expanded Neumayer thread is the more quietly remarkable discovery: one city, across three separate historical moments spanning its entire existence — its own first years, its later maturity, and the present day — keeps producing work that reshapes the rest of the country while its own name goes with none of it. Between the two, Halley subnet has both the clearest taxonomy of how a war can damage a city, and the clearest single example of a city whose real importance nobody outside it has ever fully reckoned with.
