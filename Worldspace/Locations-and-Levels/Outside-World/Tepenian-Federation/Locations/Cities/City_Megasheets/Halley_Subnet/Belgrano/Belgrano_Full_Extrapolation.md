# Belgrano — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Belgrano given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Belgrano_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-09.

---

## I. The Founding-Era Commander: Comodoro Eduardo Villanueva-Krause

**Proposed:** the figure who set Belgrano's operational tone — the one whose choices, more than any single policy, made "function before comfort" the city's baseline rather than merely its founding slogan — was **Comodoro (Commodore) Eduardo Villanueva-Krause**, the ranking Air Force officer during the transition from research/military base to permanent exile settlement. His specific, lasting contribution wasn't discipline itself (that came with the institution) but the decision to extend Air Force command structure to cover the entire civilian population growing up around the base, rather than letting a separate civic government develop alongside it — the choice `Local_Cultures/Halley_Subnet/Belgrano.md` already credits with making Belgrano's institutional culture "contagious" to non-Argentine later arrivals. A German surname paired with a Spanish one reflects the demographic shift already underway even during his own tenure.

## II. Who Shaped the Boneyard Times: Jefa de Mantenimiento Yolanda Petrescu

**Proposed:** if Villanueva-Krause built the institution that outlasted its own founding, the Boneyard Times needed a different kind of figure — not someone who commanded, but someone who organized survival once there was no longer a command structure worth commanding. **Jefa de Mantenimiento (Chief of Maintenance) Yolanda Petrescu**, a maintenance-crew supervisor during the last years of the fully-functioning airbase, is proposed as the person who formalized the maintenance rotations that kept Belgrano from disappearing entirely once institutional backing collapsed — turning the Boneyard Times' "few genuinely load-bearing skills" (already established in the faction file) into an organized, if informal, system rather than scattered individual competence. Whether she is remembered as the era's founder or simply as the person who happened to be capable when it mattered is left open — a natural extension of the faction's own live question about whether "Boneyarders" was a chosen identity or something that simply happened to people.

## III. Where the Byrd-Founding Maps Were Archived: Working Documents, Not a Dedicated Archive

**Proposed:** consistent with Belgrano's established function-first character — an institution that never developed "much of a taste" for using its own network for anything beyond practical coordination — the Byrd-founding maps were never held in a dedicated cultural archive at all. They existed as ordinary institutional recordkeeping: flight charts, aerial survey photography, and meteorological data generated by the airbase's own routine operations, filed the way any working airbase files its working documents. Their historical significance was entirely retroactive — nobody at the time thought of them as an "archive," only as records, until an expedition needed exactly what those records happened to contain. This detail plausibly deepens Salagéa Aparast's own character as a boat-dwelling datashard archivist: her instinct to preserve knowledge before evacuating reads as a genuine continuation of a civic habit Belgrano already had, long before anyone called it archiving.

## IV. Pre-Split-Brain Contact with Esperanza and Marambio: Practical, Not Cultural

**Proposed:** given Belgrano's own established Arcanet culture — practical and operational, never ambitious about being an information hub — the pre-Split-Brain relationship with fellow Argentine-founded Esperanza and Marambio is proposed as logistics-only: shipping schedules, weather advisories, and trade coordination (Esperanza's confirmed role as maritime trans-shipment point for Griselda Alejandra Chávez's instruments is the clearest known example), with no meaningful cultural exchange despite the shared national founding heritage. This is consistent with Belgrano's own character — a city that never had much bandwidth or interest in anything beyond the operational — and avoids inventing a deeper "Argentine solidarity" that nothing in the established files actually supports.

---

## V. Economy Beyond Aviation and Shipping: Spare-Parts Fabrication and Light Vehicle Manufacturing

**Proposed:** the genuine, established garage/mechanic subculture — specialists who can work on Rastras specifically, a direct continuation of having invented the vehicle — plausibly supports a modest spare-parts fabrication and light manufacturing economy beyond pure aviation logistics: producing replacement components for Rastras and aircraft that would otherwise have to be shipped in, a natural extension of "for anything with an engine, there's reliably somebody in Belgrano who can repair it" into "and somebody who can build the part that engine needs." A smaller maritime resource-extraction sector, given the coastal Weddell Sea position, rounds out the remainder.

---

## VI. Municipal Holidays: Two, Both Genuinely Earned

- **Bandera Day** *(proposed)* — a direct adaptation of the real Argentine national holiday honoring Manuel Belgrano, the city's own namesake, as the creator of the Argentine flag; observed even generations after the founding Argentine population was demographically outpaced, treated less as an ethnic-heritage holiday and more as civic pride in the name itself, consistent with the established pattern of founding-nation identity persisting as institutional character rather than living demographic weight.
- **Expedition Day** *(proposed)* — commemorating the departure of the original Arrastradoras and the all-robot crew on the Byrd-founding crossing, Belgrano's proudest historical achievement and the clearest moment where its function-first culture produced something the rest of Tepenia still remembers.

---

## VII. Whether "Boneyarders" Was Embraced: Genuinely Split, Not Resolved

**Proposed:** rather than resolving this cleanly either way, the most honest answer keeps the faction file's own live ambivalence intact — some among the "staying is loyalty" group have partially reclaimed "Boneyarders" as a badge of the choice they made, using it themselves with a kind of grim pride; others still hear it as the older generation's word for them, applied rather than chosen, and avoid using it about themselves at all. No single answer has won out, consistent with the faction's own framing of this as a genuinely unresolved internal argument rather than a settled question.

## Worth Your Attention

Section III is the most quietly valuable proposal here — it resolves the open archive question without inventing an institution Belgrano's own established character wouldn't plausibly have built, and in doing so gives Salagéa Aparast's own established archivist instinct a deeper, more specific civic root than "she just happened to care about preserving knowledge."
