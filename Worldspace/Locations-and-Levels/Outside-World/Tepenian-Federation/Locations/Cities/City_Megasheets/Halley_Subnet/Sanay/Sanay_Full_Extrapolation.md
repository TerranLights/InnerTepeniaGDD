# Sanay — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Sanay given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Sanay_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-07.

---

## I. The War Damage, Located

**Proposed:** the Long Night War's strike concentrated on the shipyards and dockside infrastructure specifically — a legitimate, high-value military target given Sanay's role as a major supply artery, rather than a broad-area strike across the whole city. The upper clifftops — the trucking depots, the business district, and the Arcanet nexus woven through the same bedrock — took comparatively lighter structural damage, both because they sat physically apart from the primary target and because they were built directly into stable rock rather than more vulnerable dockside construction. Consistent with Sanay's established "function over sentiment" civic character (and its Rotterdam parallel from the Mega-Init), rebuilding priority went to the shipyards first and fastest, since the port's continued function mattered more than resident comfort — meaning the residential, commercial, and leisure districts along the cliffside took considerably longer to fully restore, and may still show more visible, lower-priority damage today than the working port itself does.

## II. Sanay's Role Relative to Its Neighbors, and the Stakes at Troll

**Proposed:** Sanay isn't just one of several damaged-but-functioning Halley subnet cities — it's specifically the subnet's supply-chain entry point. Incoming South African freighter shipments arrive here (and at Belgrano), and from Sanay, goods split two ways: trucked directly to nearby cities via Hwy 7, or trucked onward to Troll, whose airfield flies goods out to more remote parts of Tepenia. This creates a real mutual dependency between Sanay and Troll that raises the practical stakes of Troll's contested airfield control higher than a simple neighboring relationship would: Troll has nothing to fly out without Sanay's incoming freight, and Sanay has no way to reach the subnet's more remote destinations without Troll's airfield. Whoever actually controls Troll effectively controls the downstream distribution of everything Sanay brings in — meaning Sanay has a direct, material stake in that contest, not just a neighborly interest in how it resolves.

## III. The Sanay Diaspora in Concordia

**Proposed:** a small but real community, plausibly in the low thousands rather than the tens of thousands — consistent with the established note that Sanay refugees in Concordia are rare given the city's own small population. Likely drawn from dock and logistics workers who happened to be traveling elsewhere in the subnet, or stationed temporarily at another Halley subnet city, at the moment of the war's damage — not a large evacuation, since Sanay's bedrock-founded resilience meant the city never needed to be abandoned the way a fully destroyed city would have. This community would carry the specific, understated resilience Local_Cultures already proposes for Sanay's diaspora character — shaped by having endured real damage without either the raw grief of refugees from destroyed cities or the untouched-exception self-image some other surviving communities might carry.

## IV. The SANAE Research Heritage, Today

**Resolved, drawing directly on the Silicon Valley parallel:** the founding scientific research mission persists almost entirely as institutional memory rather than active daily function — present in place-names, ceremonial vocabulary (the Full Extrapolation's Local_Cultures source already notes SANAE-derived terminology surviving as "institutional/ceremonial vocabulary even among residents with no South African ancestry"), and the Founders' Bedrock holiday, but essentially invisible in what the city's population actually spends its working hours doing. The 10% Technical/scientific share of Division of Industry is best read as a handful of dedicated researchers keeping the tradition genuinely alive in a small, specific way, surrounded by a much larger population for whom the SANAE heritage is inherited history rather than lived work — the same relationship Silicon Valley's residents today likely have to the region's own vanished orchards.

## V. A Formal Zone for Upper Earth-Facing Trade

**Proposed, drawing directly on the Guangzhou research:** Sanay's port maintains a specific, physically demarcated section — proposed name **the Trade Yard** — where incoming South African freighter crews dock, unload, and conduct any necessary business, under defined protocols distinct from the rest of the working port. This isn't hostile or punitive; it's practical, the same logic Guangzhou's own Canton System applied for centuries: welcome the trade, profit from it, but keep the terms of contact clearly bounded rather than granting an Upper Earth crew free rein across a city that, per the project's established Upper Earth trade dynamics, still has real reasons to stay guarded even while doing business. This gives Sanay's Upper Earth-facing trade relationship a concrete, earned procedural answer, distinct in flavor from Palmer City's own Upper Earth relationship (open tourism, visa-tracked but broadly welcoming) — Sanay's version is commercial, bounded, and far less socially porous.

## Worth Your Attention

The most structurally useful proposal here is Section II — tying Sanay's civic stakes directly to Troll's contested airfield through material supply dependency, not just neighborly proximity. It gives Sanay a genuine, active reason to care about DLC 5's central conflict beyond simply being nearby, and it means any resolution of Troll's control question has a concrete, immediate downstream effect on Sanay's own ability to function as the subnet's supply entry point. Section V (the Trade Yard) is the second thing worth carrying forward — it gives Sanay's Upper Earth-facing trade a distinct texture from Palmer City's, which matters if both cities' Upper Earth relationships ever get shown side by side.
