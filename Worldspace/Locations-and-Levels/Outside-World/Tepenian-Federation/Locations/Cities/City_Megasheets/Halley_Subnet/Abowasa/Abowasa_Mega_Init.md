## Who Lives Here, and Why

USA sits Primary, with Germany, France, UK, Russia, and Brazil at Significant tier, and both founding nations — Finland and Sweden — diluted together to the same Notable tier by that same later American immigration. Abowasa's distinctiveness was never about escaping demographic dilution; it's that the founding tension here was never one nation's story to begin with. It was always two, running side by side from day one.

## What It Feels Like

"Two Peoples, One Small Place" names Abowasa's defining civic fact precisely: this is Tepenia's only city founded jointly by two nations at once, inherited from two genuinely separate real-world stations that happened to sit only 200 meters apart. That closeness meant Finnish and Swedish exiles never had the option of remaining separate — the negotiation between the two identities has played out continuously, generation after generation, at a scale small enough that everyone involved knows everyone else personally. Abowasa is small, and has always been small; its whole civic character is intimacy rather than institution, household and neighborhood rhythm rather than any citywide mechanism. Both real founding stations were actually built for year-round occupation, but neither nation ever chose to staff them through the winter — a genuine budgetary choice rather than a structural limit, one that simply stopped mattering the moment exiles needed a permanent home rather than a seasonal outpost. The Long Night War hit Abowasa but didn't erase it: not strategically important enough to be a priority target, not remote or fortunate enough to be spared entirely either, leaving a small, damaged community that's still, genuinely, holding on.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Baarle-Hertog / Baarle-Nassau

An actual town split between Belgian and Dutch administration, the international border running through streets and even individual houses — genuine dual-national governance at the most intimate possible physical scale.

**What this gives Abowasa:** the tightest possible structural match — a real place where two nations' administrations coexist so closely that daily life simply requires working across the boundary constantly, mirroring Abowasa's own founding fact of two stations sitting close enough to have never truly been separate.

### Primary: Sark, Channel Islands

A genuinely tiny self-governing community where governance operates at a near-household scale.

**What this gives Abowasa:** direct precedent for civic life organized at the scale Abowasa's own established culture already claims — a real community small enough that formal institutions give way to the kind of everyone-knows-everyone governance Abowasa's own Social Contract section describes.

### Secondary: Whittier, Alaska

Nearly the entire town's population lives inside a single building, an extreme real-world case of "everyone knows everyone" as the organizing fact of civic life.

**What this gives Abowasa:** reinforces the intimacy-as-organizing-principle theme at its most extreme, a useful real-world anchor for exactly how total a small community's mutual visibility can become.

### Supporting: Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard

A genuinely multinational Arctic settlement where several countries each operate their own small station within the same tiny town.

**What this gives Abowasa:** the closest available real-world parallel for multiple national research programs coexisting within one small Arctic/Antarctic-adjacent settlement, reinforcing that Abowasa's own dual-national founding has genuine present-day real-world precedent, not just historical grounding.
