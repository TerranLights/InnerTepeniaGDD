USA sits Primary (36.36%), with a broad Significant tier beneath it — Brazil, Mexico, UK, and Argentina (the actual founding-heritage nation) all present without any single one dominating. Unlike almost every other Tepenian city, this composition carries none of the usual founding-nation-versus-majority tension, because Esperanza's charter was never national to begin with — "any human whose family had made the sacrifice of exile had a claim on what Esperanza existed to protect," a design principle, not a demographic accident that happened to work out inclusively.

## What It Feels Like

Children, audibly and visibly, before anything else — the immediate, unmistakable marker that this isn't an ordinary Tepenian city. Fathers take their children fishing at the island's edges. Many fathers work Esperanza's mines on rotational shifts — weeks away, then a week or two home — with robots serving as the primary at-home caregivers during those absences, and as the primary teachers at school besides. This isn't read locally as an unusual arrangement or a gap in a "normal" family structure; given Tepenia's real demographics (human exile immigration skewed heavily male, and children are predominantly conceived via now-normalized artificial womb technology), a father-plus-robot-caregiver household *is* Esperanza's normal family unit, grown directly out of the exile's own actual conditions rather than any deficiency. The whole arrangement points forward: this is explicitly a generation being raised to keep pushing further into Antarctica, "in the name of robots and humans hand-in-hand" — one contribution among many Tepenian cities make toward that same continuation, not a unique role reserved to Esperanza alone.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Top-Primary: The Basque baserri tradition, Spain/France

Family farmsteads built and inherited specifically to sustain multiple generations working the same land together. When parents are ready to retire, they choose exactly one child — not necessarily the eldest, not necessarily a son — to inherit and keep the household intact rather than divided.

**What this gives Esperanza:** the single closest structural ancestor to the city's own civic logic. Esperanza's whole charter functions like a baserri scaled up to city size — a household (here, an entire city) organized specifically to continue itself across generations, its succession chosen deliberately (raising this generation specifically to push the frontier forward) rather than left to chance or dilution. The baserri's core principle — continuity through *chosen* inheritance, not assumed default — gives Esperanza's frontier-preparation charter a genuine real-world precedent for what deliberate generational succession actually looks like in practice.

### Primary: Auroville, India

An actual planned "universal town" whose handwritten charter declares it "does not belong to anyone in particular but to humanity as a whole"; delegates from 124 nations attended its 1968 inauguration.

**What this gives Esperanza:** direct precedent for the founding charter's own language — "any human whose family had made the sacrifice of exile had a claim on what Esperanza existed to protect, regardless of where they'd come from" is close to a functional paraphrase of Auroville's own charter. Confirms a genuinely multinational-by-design civic charter, rather than an incidental demographic outcome, is achievable and has real, dated, working precedent.

### Primary: SOS Children's Villages

A global federation of family-style houses led by trained "SOS mothers," emphasizing long-term, family-structured care over institutional orphanages.

**What this gives Esperanza:** direct structural precedent for the specific household shape established in the City Vision Notes — not an institutional dormitory model, but individual family-style units (here, father plus robot caregiver) as the actual mechanism of care, matching SOS Villages' own emphasis on family structure over institutional scale.

### Secondary: Boys Town, Nebraska

An actual official Nebraska village whose entire civic charter is raising at-risk and homeless children.

**What this gives Esperanza:** reinforces the scale of the claim — Boys Town proves a whole *town-level* charter, not just an institution-level one, can genuinely organize itself around child welfare and function as intended.

### Secondary: Pestalozzi Children's Village, Switzerland

Founded 1946 to house and raise war-displaced children of any nation, each nationality given its own farmhouse and foster parents rather than being assimilated into one culture.

**What this gives Esperanza:** real historical precedent for children of different nations being raised together without erasing their distinct backgrounds — echoing Esperanza's own established fact that "no community carries a distinct guest-in-someone-else's-culture feeling, because the culture was built, on paper and in practice, to belong to all of them at once."

### Secondary: Sardinia's Blue Zone, Italy

Multigenerational households are the norm, with elders staying actively engaged as caregivers and teachers rather than retiring from family life.

**What this gives Esperanza:** more a useful contrast than a direct match. Sardinia's model depends on long-lived human elders actively present across generations — exactly what Esperanza's own established demographics can't offer, since human lifespans in Tepenia are only marginally longer than real-world ones. Esperanza substitutes robots for that continuity role instead (robots, not human family lines, are confirmed as the actual carriers of institutional memory here), making Sardinia useful precisely as the real-world model Esperanza's own conditions couldn't replicate, and had to solve differently.

### Supporting: Kibbutz communal child-rearing, Israel

Many kibbutzim raised children collectively in dedicated "children's houses" from the 1920s through the 1980s-90s — a system its own members eventually dismantled because they wanted their children living at home.

**What this gives Esperanza:** a cautionary real precedent worth noting by contrast. Unlike the kibbutz's fully centralized children's-house model (which its own community eventually rejected), Esperanza's structure keeps children within individual household units (father plus robot caregiver) rather than centralizing them communally — a structural choice that may make Esperanza's own arrangement more durable than the kibbutz's own real, eventually-abandoned system.
