## What It Feels Like

The dramatic Lemaire Channel approach and Adelaide Island's mountain backdrop register first, followed by the genuinely unusual experience of a decentralized industrial city — not one dense urban core, but a working landscape spread across a much larger physical scale than any other Peninsula city. Per the developer's own vision session: stretches of fabrication yards dominate, alongside residential areas where humans and robots live, sleep, commune, and share leisure time together, advanced forklifts hauling materials constantly. Rothera makes a broad general-purpose catalog of goods for both home use and construction — nothing specifically famous within that catalog has been decided yet. Humans run 8–10 hour shifts, robots 10–12 hour shifts; when the day ends, everyone stops at one of a variety of local bars and pubs. Humans drink alcohol; robots drink glitch-coolant — and Rothera's own bar scene sits squarely in the working-class, limited-selection-but-stronger-effect category (alongside Byrd), consistent with the city's whole function-over-presentation civic identity. Beneath all of it, large-scale excavated underground vault sections — bigger than the small, iconic shelters typical of post-apocalyptic fiction, made possible specifically by Adelaide Island's abundant size — give the city a third, compounding layer of resilience beyond decentralization alone.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Gary, Indiana

A company town built by U.S. Steel in 1906, home to what was for decades the world's largest steel mill. Gary's employment collapsed catastrophically as automation and offshore competition took hold — over 30,000 workers in the early 1970s, down to roughly 2,246 by 2023.

**What this gives Rothera:** direct precedent for a whole city organized around and defined by industrial production — but Gary's own real history reads as a cautionary contrast rather than a match. Gary's entire identity depended on one concentrated mill, exactly the single-point-of-failure structure that made it vulnerable to both economic collapse and, in Tepenia's own terms, would have made an equivalent city a prime military target. Rothera's decentralized model is a direct structural answer to the precise vulnerability Gary's real history demonstrates.

### Primary: Sweden's Cold War "Bas 90" Air-Basing Doctrine

Aircraft industry built inside hardened factories carved directly into granite mountains, with operational aircraft deliberately dispersed across 20–40 separate hardstands per base specifically so no single strike could eliminate the whole capability.

**What this gives Rothera:** a precise military-doctrine parallel to Rothera's own established survival mechanism — deliberate physical dispersal as the actual strategy for surviving an attack, rather than surviving by luck, terrain alone, or bedrock. Rothera's own civic pride specifically in *why* it survived (decentralization, not chance) mirrors Sweden's own deliberate wartime engineering logic almost exactly.

### Secondary: Magnitogorsk, Russia

Founded in 1929 as part of Stalin's First Five-Year Plan, built to be the world's largest steel production complex — and, remarkably, *planned as a direct copy* of the Gary, Indiana steel mill: a 1928 Soviet delegation traveled to Cleveland specifically to arrange this. Its Ural-mountain-adjacent location also made it strategically safe from German seizure during WWII.

**What this gives Rothera:** an unexpected, genuinely delightful discovery this research pass turned up — Rothera's own two Primary/Secondary picks (Gary and Magnitogorsk) are themselves directly, historically connected, one literally modeled on the other. This gives Rothera's own established industrial layering — inherited infrastructure built on, generation after generation, rather than replaced — a real structural echo: two real-world cities built in direct relation to each other, the same way Rothera's own industrial identity is a layered composite rather than a single clean origin. Magnitogorsk's WWII-era positional safety (survival through geography, not active defense) is also a clean parallel to Rothera's own decentralization-based survival.

### Secondary: Imperial Japan's WWII Dispersed Aircraft Production

After early Allied bombing raids, Japan scattered aircraft manufacturing into small, geographically disparate workshops and private homes — a real historical case where dispersal-without-hardening outperformed the underground factories Japan attempted but never completed in time.

**What this gives Rothera:** an honest, nuanced real precedent showing dispersal alone, even without hardening, can outperform incomplete fortification — relevant since Rothera's own resilience combines *both* (decentralized surface sites *and* completed underground vaults), giving it a stronger real-world combined precedent than either historical case achieved alone.

### Supporting: Norway's Underground NATO Mountain Air Bases

Hangars bored directly into granite mountains since the 1950s, still active today — Bardufoss's mountain hangars were reopened as recently as 2024 to house F-35s.

**What this gives Rothera:** a close cousin to the Swedish system, different country, same underlying logic, and notably still an active, maintained system today rather than a Cold War relic — useful precedent for imagining Rothera's own underground vaults as genuinely maintained, ongoing infrastructure rather than an abandoned wartime remnant.
