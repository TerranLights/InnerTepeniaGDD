# Palmer City — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Palmer City would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Palmer_City.md`, `Local_Cultures/Palmer_Subnet/Palmer_City.md`, and
`Palmer_City_Community_Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred
until this pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30. Begins the
Palmer subnet.

**Following `Palmer_City_Community_Infrastructure.md`'s own precedent:** that file flagged itself as
"floor met, but incomplete" given Palmer City's exceptional cultural density (43-nation composition, a
whole entertainment economy, its own founding myth) and asked for future expansion rather than treating the
pass as finished. The same caveat applies here — this Attributes list derives what's clearly supportable
from established facts, but a heavily cultural, historically dense city like Palmer City plausibly has more
genuine infrastructure than a single first pass will surface.

**Why Palmer City's attributes look like nothing else examined so far:** it's the only Tepenian city
examined in this entire pass where above-freezing temperatures are a regular occurrence — running water,
rain (not just snow), different structural failure modes, potential surface plant life are all physically
possible here in ways no other city needs to engineer for. It's also Tepenia's founding city and its
cultural/entertainment capital rather than its governmental one (that's Fort McMurdo), giving it a civic
identity built on neutrality and celebration rather than any environmental hazard or strategic function.

**Governing facts:** Anvers Island, off the Antarctic Peninsula; status is Destroyed, among the first and
most thoroughly targeted cities in the Long Night War given its extreme accessibility from Upper Earth;
maritime subpolar climate, the most temperate of any Tepenian city, no polar night or midnight sun at this
latitude, frequent rain and wet snow, intense Drake Passage storm systems, persistent fog; Tepenia's first
settlement, founded the same day as the Falkland Treaty itself; cultural and entertainment capital of
pre-war Tepenia (jazz, casino/hospitality economy), not the governmental capital; possessed a unique civic
neutrality as the arrival point every faction's predecessors passed through; home to 100 Miles Davis
Boulevard, the first official address in the Federation, and the found pre-war Antarctica flag (since
relocated to Concordia).

---

## Above-Freezing / Wet-Climate Infrastructure (the defining, unique feature)

1. **Running-water and drainage infrastructure engineered for regular above-freezing conditions**, a
   physical capability no other Tepenian city examined in this pass needs to build for.
2. **Wet-weather structural design distinct from any snow-only city's own engineering** — accounting for
   rain, wet snow, and the icing hazards that come specifically from maritime moisture combined with
   subfreezing temperatures.

## Founding & Arrival Infrastructure

3. **100 Miles Davis Boulevard and the original customs-office site** (established) — the first official
   address in the Federation, the physical threshold where exiled robots and their partners were first
   received.
4. **The Flag Preservation Hall** (established, now empty) — where the found pre-war Antarctica flag was
   displayed before its pre-destruction relocation to Concordia.
5. **Rue de la Réception** (established) — the formal, ceremonial approach street used for visiting
   dignitaries and Upper Earth tourism arrivals, distinct from the informal original arrival point.

## Entertainment & Cultural Capital Infrastructure

6. **The Petrograd Room** (established) — Palmer City's grandest casino/entertainment hall, the physical
   anchor of the city's St. Petersburg-adjacent ambition.
7. **Little Burgundy Quarter** (established) — the district around the original station buildings, the
   densest live-music neighborhood, where the found jazz records were first played.
8. **The Boardwalk** (established) — an elevated walkway purpose-built to let visitors experience the
   city's rare above-freezing, rainy weather, doubling as prime nightlife real estate.

## Storm & Fog Infrastructure

9. **Storm-window civic infrastructure**, coordinating outdoor activity and travel around the frequent
   gale-force Drake Passage storm systems — a materially different rhythm than any katabatic-wind city's
   own wind-window logistics.
10. **Fog-navigation infrastructure**, given the established persistent low cloud and fog banks that can
    drop visibility to near zero without warning independent of wind conditions.

## Civic Neutrality Infrastructure

11. **Whatever civic/administrative infrastructure reflects Palmer City's own established faction-neutral
    status** — a city no single faction could claim because every faction's predecessors passed through it
    first, a genuinely unique civic-legal position among Tepenian cities.

## Ruins-Specific / DLC 3 Archaeology Infrastructure

12. **Whatever survives at the scale consistent with being "among the first and most thoroughly destroyed"
    Tepenian cities**, distinct from any partial-damage or gradual-decline ruins examined so far —
    consistent with DLC 3's own explicit framing as an archaeology of Tepenia at its most vibrant.

## Cross-Referenced Extrapolation Findings (Methodology #2)

**What this is:** cross-referencing the 12 attributes above against Palmer City's own existing lore —
`Palmer_City_Full_Extrapolation.md` and `Palmer_City_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` (which already holds 8
Findings of its own, written 2026-07-06 — the richest existing base of any DLC city examined in this entire
Methodology #2 pass, Byrd aside). `DLC_Overview.md` names Palmer City directly and unambiguously as DLC 3's
central location, so this pass runs at the deepest tier used anywhere outside Byrd, matching Mawson's own
depth. Written 2026-07-30.

### Finding A: the Petrograd Room is the specific building where the Long Night War's inciting incident most plausibly occurred

**Combining:** attribute #6 (the Petrograd Room, Palmer City's grandest casino/entertainment hall) + the
Full Extrapolation's Section III (Solenne Vaskarre plausibly worked in the Petrograd Room specifically, given
the diplomat's own high status would put him in the grandest available venue).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #6 gives the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's own Findings 4 and 5 (the
sovereignty-assertion system's structural blind spot, Solenne as a structurally inevitable collision) an
actual named building, not just an abstract "closed-quarters setting."

**3rd-order effect:** the single most consequential room in Tepenian history — the place the war that
reshaped everyone's world actually began — is a specific, walkable, named location, not a generic backdrop.
Any future DLC 3 content revisiting the inciting incident has a concrete site to build around.

### Finding B: the Flag Preservation Hall's own emptiness is a small, physical monument to a resolved historical question

**Combining:** attribute #4 (the Flag Preservation Hall, now empty) + the Full Extrapolation's Section IV
(the Antarctica flag was relocated to Concordia alongside Solenne, in the same window of pre-destruction
relocations).

**2nd-order effect:** the Hall's own current emptiness, established directly in its own attribute, is
physical confirmation of Section IV's resolved account — a building that once held the flag and now holds
nothing, because the flag genuinely left before the city fell.

**3rd-order effect:** gives Section IV's own quiet resolution a walkable location — a player exploring the
ruins would find an empty preservation hall, and the absence itself tells the story without needing
narration.

### Finding C: Rue de la Réception is the architectural staging ground for Finding 3's own sovereignty theater

**Combining:** attribute #5 (Rue de la Réception, the formal ceremonial approach for dignitaries and Upper
Earth tourists) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 3 (Palmer City's hospitality is
inseparable from an ongoing act of political sovereignty — every visa-tracked Upper Earth visitor a small,
repeated demonstration of Tepenian jurisdiction).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #5's own formal, ceremonial function is the literal architectural expression
of Finding 3's abstract "sovereignty theater" — a street specifically built to stage that assertion,
distinct from Miles Davis Boulevard's own lived-in, historic register.

**3rd-order effect:** gives Finding 3's own rich political reading (hospitality doubling as jurisdiction
demonstration) a concrete stage, literally — every Upper Earth arrival walking Rue de la Réception is
performing the exact submission-to-Tepenian-law dynamic Finding 3 describes.

### Finding D: the storm and fog infrastructure is exactly what the DLC 3 ruins now lack

**Combining:** attributes #9–10 (storm-window civic infrastructure, fog-navigation infrastructure) + the
Full Extrapolation's Section VI (DLC 3's ruins are genuinely accessible but actively dangerous — racing
weather windows and structural decay simultaneously, unlike any cold-preserved interior ruin).

**2nd-order effect:** attributes #9–10 describe the exact pre-war systems that once managed the hazards
Section VI's own ruins-exploration framing now treats as unmanaged, active dangers.

**3rd-order effect:** gives Section VI's own distinctive "racing weather windows" mechanic a direct causal
source — the ruins are dangerous specifically because the coordination infrastructure that once made the
same weather merely inconvenient no longer functions.

### Finding E: the faction-neutrality infrastructure is the institutional expression of Finding 1's doubly-justified cosmopolitanism

**Combining:** attribute #11 (civic/administrative infrastructure reflecting Palmer City's faction-neutral
status) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 1 (Palmer City's cosmopolitanism is doubly
justified — the founding population's relationship-based organizing principle, and the later, independent
43-nation demographic reality, both pointing the same direction).

**2nd-order effect:** a city with two genuinely independent justifications for universal belonging would
plausibly need real administrative machinery to enact that neutrality in practice, not simply cultural
sentiment — arbitration, no-single-claim governance, something attribute #11 already gestures toward.

**3rd-order effect:** strengthens Finding 1's own "over-determined, not fragile" reading — the neutrality
isn't just narratively stated twice, it's institutionally built to be practiced, giving the abstract
cosmopolitan identity real civic teeth.

### Finding F: total ruins scale raises the stakes on Byrd's own possible surviving Palmer City material

**Combining:** attribute #12 (whatever survives at the scale consistent with being "among the first and most
thoroughly destroyed" cities) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 8 (Byrd may hold surviving
primary-source material about pre-war Palmer City that Palmer City's own destruction erased everywhere
else).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #12's own framing (what remains, given near-total destruction) directly sets
up Finding 8's own poetic stakes — the more thoroughly Palmer City's own local record was destroyed, the
more valuable any externally-preserved copy becomes.

**3rd-order effect:** reinforces Finding 8 as a genuinely load-bearing cross-DLC hook rather than a nice
coincidence — DLC 2 (Byrd) may be the single best-preserved source of primary documentation about DLC 3
(Palmer City) precisely because DLC 3's own local archive didn't survive.

### Finding G: Little Burgundy and the Petrograd Room are two different registers of the same tourism economy, and the war's spark came from the higher-status one

**Combining:** attributes #6–7 (the Petrograd Room, Little Burgundy Quarter) + the existing Cross-Reference
Synthesis's Finding 5 (Solenne's occupation places her at the exact intersection of everything Palmer City
is).

**2nd-order effect:** the city's founding cultural soul (Little Burgundy, where the found jazz records were
first played, dense and communal) and its highest-status representational space (the Petrograd Room, where
the inciting incident most plausibly happened) are physically and socially distinct venues serving different
registers of the same underlying economy.

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming directly — the war's spark came from the grandest, most exclusive space
Palmer City had to offer, not from its most communal one, a meaningful detail for any future scene
contrasting the city's warm, democratic self-image with the closed-door setting where its worst moment
actually happened.

### Finding H: evacuating what mattered before destruction is a pattern that recurs at two different scales

**Combining:** attribute #4 (the Flag Preservation Hall, emptied by pre-destruction relocation) + the
existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 8 (Byrd possibly holding evacuated documentary material about
Palmer City, sent generations earlier during the founding-era Byrd Expedition contribution).

**2nd-order effect:** these are two separate instances of the identical civic pattern — Palmer City's most
valuable cultural assets surviving specifically because they were physically relocated elsewhere before
destruction, once in the founding era (records sent to help find Byrd) and once shortly before the war
(the flag sent to Concordia).

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming as a consistent, if unremarked-upon, Palmer City civic instinct —
"evacuate what matters before it's too late" — recurring across nearly 250 years of the city's own history,
from its earliest generosity to its final act of self-preservation.
