## Who Lives Here, and Why

Palmer subnet's population is Tepenia's most demographically varied at the individual-city level, without ever converging on one single dominant founding nation the way some other subnets do. Palmer City itself was founded on a principle that had nothing to do with nationality at all — relationship to robots, not country of origin, was the organizing fact from day one, later expanded to include all 43 master-list nations in some presence. Esperanza's founding charter was explicitly non-national by design, open to "any human whose family had made the sacrifice of exile." Sejong shares one small island with nearly a dozen other national communities in permanent, negotiated proximity. Even the cities with a single, clearly dominant founding-operator nation — Juan Carlos, Marambio, Rothera, and Signy (the last with a unique South African founding-wave presence alongside its founding operator) — each show significant demographic dilution by Census II, USA reliably emerging as Primary or co-Primary almost everywhere in the subnet regardless of who actually founded each city.

This produces a subnet whose real organizing principle was never blood or flag — it was proximity to the rest of the world. Every Palmer subnet city's own founding logic traces back to being reachable: reachable by the first wave of exiles who didn't wait (Palmer City), reachable by South American shipping and Upper Earth tourism alike (Palmer City again), reachable enough to hold Tepenia's first bureaucratic archive (Juan Carlos), reachable enough to serve as a genuine trans-shipment corridor (Esperanza, Marambio). That same reachability is also, unmistakably, why so much of Palmer subnet ended up destroyed — the closer a place is to the rest of the world, the easier it is for the rest of the world to find it when it decides to attack.

## What It Feels Like

Moving through Palmer subnet today means moving through the clearest single record, anywhere in Tepenia, of what the war actually took. Palmer City's own ruins hold flooded lower levels and freeze-thaw structural decay unlike any other Tepenian ruin site, still dangerous in a way no interior city's snow-stabilized wreckage is. Esperanza's birth registry survives only in fragments. Sejong's dense, vertical mini-city — genuinely comparable to Taipei at a fraction of the scale — no longer stands at all.

Then the surviving cities offer three entirely different registers of "made it through." Rothera's decentralized fabrication yards and underground vaults still hum with genuine, deliberately-engineered resilience — a city proud specifically of *why* it survived. Port Lockroy still tends its layered, never-demolished architecture and its slowly-growing communal stone wall, a place that survived by being beneath anyone's notice. Signy still runs its quiet, unhurried, wildlife-paced daily rhythm across two islands joined by one bridge — except now every conversation about the future runs up against a siligel clock nobody in the city can stop.

No other Tepenian subnet holds this specific combination: proof of what thrived, proof of what was lost, and three genuinely different flavors of what it actually means to have survived.

## Personality (A Whole-Subnet Read)

Palmer subnet doesn't reduce to one Enneagram profile any more than Concordia's own thirteen districts did — but it shows its own distinctive whole-subnet pattern, one this subnet's own City Megasheets already partially discovered on their own: at least three of its eight cities (Esperanza, Rothera, Sejong) carry an early Enneagram profile anchored to a secondary, easily-countable administrative fact rather than each city's own richer, later-established civic identity — while Juan Carlos and Signy show the opposite, a profile that genuinely matches. Read at subnet scale, this looks less like a documentation accident repeating three times by coincidence and more like a real trait of Palmer subnet's own history: a place whose cities kept being defined, at least on paper, by what they did *for* other people (logistics partnerships, border facilitation, administrative service) well before anyone properly wrote down what they actually were in their own right. Palmer subnet's whole civic personality, in other words, spent generations being under-described relative to its own depth — right up until the Long Night War made sure several of its cities would never get the chance to be fully described at all.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: The Antarctic Peninsula's own real-world history as the continent's most contested region

The real Antarctic Peninsula hosted the densest concentration of competing national claims and station-building anywhere on the continent — Argentina, Chile, and the UK all asserting overlapping territorial claims across the same stretch of ice for decades before the Antarctic Treaty resolved the dispute diplomatically rather than militarily.

**What this gives Palmer subnet:** direct grounding for the subnet's own established demographic complexity and layered founding tensions — Marambio's founding-tier-now-diluted population mix, Rothera's founding-institution-under-American-majority demographic profile, and Sejong's dozen-nations-on-one-island crowding are all fictionalized versions of a real, historically documented pattern of overlapping presence in exactly this region.

### Secondary: European port cities that absorbed centuries of successive foreign presence — Keelung, Izmir, Vladivostok

Already researched individually for Sejong's own Megasheet, these three cities share a deeper structural relevance to the whole subnet: each demonstrates that a small, geographically exposed gateway location can host wave after wave of distinct national or cultural presence without fully merging into one blended identity — exactly the pattern recurring across Palmer City, Sejong, and (in miniature) every other multinational Palmer subnet city.

### Secondary: Cities that survived a war for reasons that had nothing to do with their own defenses — Kyoto, Gander's inverse

Kyoto's WWII sparing (removed from the atomic target list for its cultural significance) and Gander's real historical rise and fall (mattering enormously, then not at all, purely due to changing aircraft range) both apply directly to Palmer subnet's own surviving cities — Port Lockroy survived by mattering too little to bother with, the precise inverse of Kyoto's "mattered too much to destroy."

### Supporting: Small, extremely isolated real-world settlements — Tristan da Cunha, the Faroe Islands, Newfoundland's outports

Already researched for Signy specifically, these apply at whole-subnet scale to every one of Palmer subnet's island-only cities (Esperanza, Juan Carlos, Marambio, Sejong, Signy) — five of eight member cities, a majority of the subnet, sharing the same basic real-world condition of small, maritime-only-access settlements making do with what reaches them by boat.

## Worth Your Attention

The single most load-bearing fact in this Mega-Init is the subnet's own destruction rate, read plainly: five of eight Palmer subnet cities lost their pre-war function entirely, the highest proportion of any subnet in Tepenia. This isn't incidental to the subnet's own founding character — it's the direct, structural cost of the same reachability that made Palmer subnet flourish first. The subnet that was easiest for exiles to reach in 2564 was, by the same geography, the easiest for Upper Earth to reach two and a half centuries later. Palmer subnet's whole present-day identity — proof of what thrived, proof of what was lost, three different flavors of what survival actually looks like — is the direct, honest consequence of having been Tepenia's own front door.

## I. Sejong's own destruction mechanism — a fourth strike doctrine

The cross-city Patterns work already established three distinguishable Upper Earth strike types across Palmer subnet's destroyed cities: security strikes (Juan Carlos), efficiency strikes (Marambio), and morale strikes (Esperanza). Sejong has never had an equivalent explanation.

**Proposed:** a fourth type — an **ideological erasure strike**. Sejong's whole civic premise was an active, ongoing, genuinely uncertain experiment in whether nearly a dozen nations could share one small island in permanent, working proximity. A successful, celebrated example of exactly that kind of cooperation would be directly inconvenient to Upper Earth's own official narrative that Tepenia's exile population — robots and the humans who chose them — couldn't build anything real or lasting. Destroying Sejong specifically before the experiment could be judged one way or the other doesn't just remove a city; it removes the possibility of Sejong ever becoming proof of anything. This gives Sejong a strike logic distinct from all three of its subnet neighbors' own — not security, not efficiency, not morale, but the deliberate foreclosure of an inconvenient question.

## II. Each city's own specific role within the subnet's gateway identity

Mirroring the district-level "theory of building a true city" exercise, but adapted to Palmer subnet's own established gateway character:

- **Palmer City is the gateway's face** — the negotiated point of contact with Upper Earth itself, tourism and spectacle as the visible surface of a much more careful sovereignty arrangement underneath.
- **Esperanza is the gateway's promise** — proof that what the exile cost was worth it, expressed as a whole city built around protecting the next generation.
- **Juan Carlos is the gateway's memory** — the record of who actually came through, kept so carefully it became the seed of the Federation's entire institutional memory.
- **Marambio is the gateway's throughput** — the volume mover, air and sea both, the physical proof that a gateway has to actually move things, not just exist.
- **Port Lockroy is the gateway's correspondence** — the quieter half of throughput, moving words and relationships rather than goods.
- **Rothera is the gateway's workshop** — building, at real industrial scale, the physical infrastructure the rest of the gateway depends on to keep functioning.
- **Sejong is the gateway's experiment** — the open, unresolved question of whether the gateway's own diversity could actually cohere into something durable.
- **Signy is the gateway's edge** — the point past which the gateway simply stops, isolation as both its identity and, eventually, its cost.

## IV. The Enneagram Sibling-Group Pattern — Read as Subnet Identity, Not Just Documentation Lag

**Proposed:** the recurring "administrative facilitation on behalf of others" trait shared by Esperanza, Rothera, and Sejong (and Juan Carlos and Sejong's own wider sibling group with Amundsen Station and Halley) isn't merely an artifact of when each city's Enneagram profile happened to be written. Read against this Mega-Init's own central thesis — that Palmer subnet's whole identity is about facilitating contact and movement for others — the shared trait is a genuine, if underdeveloped, signal: a subnet whose defining civic function *is* administrative facilitation, at every scale from a single city's logistics partnership up to the whole subnet's role as Tepenia's gateway to the rest of the world. The documentation lag these cities' own Megasheets found wasn't describing something false. It was describing something true about a smaller part of the picture, before the fuller picture — this Ultra-Megasheet's own — existed to place it in.

## Finding 1: Robots are Palmer subnet's actual continuity mechanism, independently, in three unrelated domains

**Combining:** Esperanza's own finding that "being from Hope" identity is carried forward by long-lived robot caregivers rather than human bloodlines (`Palmer_Cross_City_Patterns.md`, Pattern 2) + Marambio's own finding that permanent geology survives the war while the human scholarship interpreting it does not, echoed directly in its dockworker culture's own human-robot relationships (Pattern 2) + Palmer City's own open question of whether Solenne Vaskarre, given established robot lifespans, might still be alive and present nearly 250 years after the war she began (Pattern 2).

**2nd-order effect:** three completely different Palmer subnet cities, addressing three completely different kinds of loss (a founding identity, a scholarly record, a single historical witness), independently arrived at the identical mechanism for what actually survives: not the human institution, not the human memory, but the robot presence running underneath it.

**3rd-order effect:** none of these three cities' own Cross-Reference Syntheses cite either of the other two — this is a genuinely emergent, subnet-scale discovery, only visible once all eight City Megasheets exist to be compared.

**4th-order effect:** this makes Palmer subnet a uniquely strong demonstration, at the city level, of a principle already established project-wide (robots as Tepenia's true carriers of continuous memory) — not stated once and left abstract, but independently, concretely re-derived three separate times by three unrelated pieces of research.

---

## Finding 2: Byrd owes Palmer subnet a double debt neither city's own culture currently reflects

**Combining:** Palmer City's and Rothera's contribution to Byrd's own historical rediscovery and industrial planning respectively (`Palmer_Cross_City_Patterns.md`, Pattern 6; `Palmer_Cross_City_Throughways.md`, Throughway 1) + the fact that Palmer City's own destruction erased its local copies of the very records that helped find Byrd in the first place.

**2nd-order effect:** Byrd, a city in an entirely separate subnet and DLC, is structurally a Palmer subnet creation twice over — shaped by records it received before Palmer City's destruction, and by an industrial template it may have received from Rothera — without its own established culture currently acknowledging either debt.

**3rd-order effect:** because Palmer City no longer exists to hold its own copies of what it once shared, Byrd may now be the only place in present-day Tepenia holding real primary-source material about a destroyed neighbor from a different part of the country entirely.

**4th-order effect:** this is one of the strongest concrete, ready-to-use cross-DLC hooks this entire Megasheet project has produced anywhere — a specific, findable artifact (per this Ultra-Megasheet's own Full Extrapolation, Section V) sitting in one DLC's own setting, capable of resolving an open historical question from a completely different DLC's own destroyed city.

---

## Finding 3: Palmer subnet's fate — destroyed, damaged, or survived — was never random, and the two typologies explain each other

**Combining:** the cross-city Patterns work's own destruction-doctrine typology (security, efficiency, morale, and — per this Ultra-Megasheet's own Full Extrapolation, Section I — ideological erasure, covering all four of Palmer subnet's destroyed cities) + the cross-city Patterns work's own survival-mechanism typology (engineered decentralization, strategic irrelevance, and isolation, covering all three of Palmer subnet's lightly-damaged or intact cities).

**2nd-order effect:** every one of Palmer subnet's eight cities' present-day status — whether destroyed, damaged, or survived — now has a specific, named, non-arbitrary explanation, rather than four destructions and three survivals sitting as separate, unconnected facts.

**3rd-order effect:** read together, the two typologies show the same underlying logic operating in both directions — what made a city valuable enough to strike (a security asset, an efficiency target, a morale target, an inconvenient success story) and what made a city safe enough to spare (engineered resilience, irrelevance, isolation) were never different kinds of fact. They were the same question — how visible, how valuable, how threatening was this city to Upper Earth's own interests — answered two different ways.

**4th-order effect:** this gives Palmer subnet's own status as "the subnet hit hardest by the war" (per this Ultra-Megasheet's own Mega-Init) a fully legible mechanism rather than a simple, blunt statistic — five of eight cities lost their pre-war function not by bad luck concentrated in one place, but because Palmer subnet's own defining trait (reachability) made every one of its cities legible to Upper Earth's own targeting logic, for a genuine variety of different reasons, in a way most other Tepenian subnets' own more interior, harder-to-reach cities never had to face in the same numbers.

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across All Three Findings

All three findings show Palmer subnet's own scattered facts resolving into single, coherent pictures once placed side by side. Finding 1 shows one mechanism (robot continuity) discovered three times independently. Finding 2 shows one relationship (the Byrd debt) that exists but has never been acknowledged from both sides. Finding 3 shows one underlying logic (reachability, for better and worse) explaining every city's present-day status at once, destroyed and survived alike. None of this required new invention — every piece was already sitting in the subnet's own eight City Megasheets, waiting for exactly this kind of subnet-wide comparison.
