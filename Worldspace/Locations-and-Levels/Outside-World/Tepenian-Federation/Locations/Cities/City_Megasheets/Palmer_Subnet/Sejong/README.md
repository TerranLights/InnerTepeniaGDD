## What It Feels Like

Per the developer's own vision session: a bustling "mini-city," something like Taipei at a much smaller scale — dense and vertical rather than sprawling, since King George Island's real land constraints forced Sejong to build up where it couldn't build out. Infrastructure is anchored directly into solid bedrock, a genuine structural contrast with ice-shelf cities like Halley, which must design around ground that's constantly moving. High-rise buildings cluster together, serving a practical function beyond density alone — natural diversionary wind protection. Much of the cuisine is seafood-based, fishing confirmed as a real economic pillar alongside the city's established trade/transit-hub role. Underneath the density, Sejong's whole civic art form is diplomatic coordination — a skill the community built for itself out of sheer necessity, constantly renegotiating the boundary with whichever neighbor happens to be adjacent — a genuinely different skill from the settled, coherent institution Juan Carlos built out of comparative isolation instead, each city developing its own answer independently to a very different set of circumstances.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Keelung, Taiwan

A small port that absorbed successive waves of foreign presence in rapid succession across four centuries — a 1626 fort, a 1642 battle for control of the harbor, a 19th-century treaty-port era, and a major postwar export-logistics boom — while maintaining genuine, organized, distinct community structures throughout: 29 clan associations and 34 province associations still connect residents to their specific ancestral origins today, rather than the city having simply merged into one blended culture.

**What this gives Sejong:** direct precedent for a small place absorbing wave after wave of different presence while maintaining organized, distinct communities rather than fully merging — echoing Sejong's own established "boundary zones" architecture, where each national community's territory remains a real, negotiated, ongoing distinction rather than dissolving into a single shared identity.

### Primary: Izmir (historic Smyrna), Turkey

A cosmopolitan Ottoman-era port where distinct communities coexisted as separately self-governed groups, physically divided into distinct quarters within one city, protected under a broader imperial tolerance for community self-administration.

**What this gives Sejong:** the closest single real-world city match to Sejong's actual situation — a genuinely, physically divided urban area organized into distinct quarters, mirroring Sejong's own established boundary-zone architecture with unusual precision.

### Secondary: Vladivostok

A genuine free-port cosmopolitan city where, at its historical peak, one ethnic group made up less than half the population, with large communities of other origins and roughly 40% of residents being foreign-born as early as the 1870s.

**What this gives Sejong:** a real historical demographic precedent for Sejong's own composition — no single overwhelming majority, genuine cosmopolitan mixing achieved at real historical scale, not merely a worldbuilding conceit.

### Secondary: Gibraltar

A densely populated enclave whose identity was forged almost entirely in relational contrast to a larger neighbor, rather than through any single ancestral lineage of its own residents.

**What this gives Sejong:** reinforces the relational nature of Sejong's own identity formation — its established founding tension is "Sejong-versus-neighbors," not a simple founder-versus-majority story — Gibraltar's own identity, forged through proximity and contrast rather than ancestry alone, mirrors how Sejong's own sense of itself is sharpened rather than diluted by constant neighbor density.

## I. The Other King George Island National Communities — Absorbed, Not Separate

**Proposed:** the smaller national footholds (Argentine, Brazilian, Polish, Russian, Czech, Uruguayan) never developed into fully independent cities of their own — instead, as Sejong grew into the island's largest and most organized settlement, their populations were gradually folded into Sejong's own civic structure, each retaining a distinct neighborhood identity within the city rather than a separate municipal one. This traces directly from the census data itself: every one of these nations appears as part of *Sejong's own* population tiers (USA sole Primary; France, UK, Mexico, Germany, Brazil, Italy, Russia, South Korea at Significant; Argentina, Chile, Uruguay at Notable) rather than as populations of separate, independently tracked cities — Sejong is the only settlement on King George Island the census recognizes at all. *(Corrected 2026-07-13: China removed from this list — its real-world Great Wall Station presence on the island never translated into a demographically tracked Sejong population once operator-identity reasoning was correctly excluded; whatever became of any Chinese-descended community here is a genuinely open question, not a solved one.)*

## II. The Sejong–Janbogo Relationship — A Founding-Era Naming Echo, Not a Living Connection

**Proposed, corrected 2026-07-13:** the original version of this section proposed "a real, maintained connection" between the two cities grounded in shared Korean heritage — that overstates it. Janbogo remained a genuine Korean demographic and cultural center in Tepenia, alongside Sinheung in the Mirny subnet; Sejong's own Korean population diluted to 5.79%, its smallest Significant-tier nation, the same pattern already resolved at Sayowa/Shirayuki. Whatever connection exists is closer to historical footnote than living relationship — perhaps a single shared observance of Hangul Day, maintained more out of founding-era naming symmetry than genuine ongoing cultural kinship, with no meaningful mutual awareness beyond that. Each city's real cultural energy went entirely into its own immediate neighbors — Sejong into King George Island's dense multinational contrast, Janbogo into its own established Ross Sea subnet-hub role.

## III. Did the Coexistence Experiment Succeed? — Yes and No, Genuinely Both

**Proposed:** functionally, yes — the established "boundary zones" being architecturally transitional rather than hard-walled suggests real, practical coexistence was achieved at the level of shared infrastructure and daily logistics. Socially and emotionally, the tension the established lore already names — "the mild climate... paradoxically made it more competitive: multiple communities vying for the same favorable ground" — never fully resolved. This isn't a contradiction; it's success in one domain (the practical, functional level) alongside genuine unresolved friction in another (the emotional, competitive level), giving DLC 3 real dramatic material rather than a flatly settled answer either way.

---

## IV. A Defining Skyline Landmark: The Lighthouse Spire

**Proposed:** a combined lighthouse-and-signal tower, the tallest structure on King George Island, built to serve the same practical maritime-signaling need any dense harbor city requires — but visible from every national community's own footholds across the island, functioning as a literal shared civic landmark none of the dozen communities can claim as exclusively their own. It ties Sejong's two established defining traits into one physical object: the vertical, dense "mini-Taipei" building logic, and the multinational coexistence that required something everyone could equally orient around.

## V. The Two Placeholder Holidays, Developed

- **The Neighbors' Accord** — marking the anniversary of whatever original land-use or resource-sharing agreement the island's dozen communities first negotiated to make coexistence physically workable; a holiday about the *practical* success named in Section III above, deliberately separate from any claim that the underlying social tension was ever fully resolved.

# Sejong — Cross-Reference Synthesis: Gaps, Combinations, and Downstream Effects

## Finding 1: Sejong and Juan Carlos form a genuine natural experiment — one variable changed, two opposite civic outcomes

**Combining:** Sejong's own established structural contrast with Juan Carlos (both South Shetland Islands cities, settled under similar circumstances) + the corrected understanding that neither city's civic character stems from inherited founding-nation culture but from each community's own independent response to circumstance + the one clear variable that actually differs between them: neighbor density (Sejong sharing one island with nearly a dozen communities; Juan Carlos comparatively alone on Livingston Island).

**2nd-order effect:** this makes the two cities a genuinely clean natural experiment — similar founding-era starting conditions, one variable changed, two very different civic outcomes: Sejong's constant, informal, many-sided diplomatic coordination versus Juan Carlos's settled, singular institution built out of comparative isolation.

**3rd-order effect:** this reads as a more interesting and more mechanically legible contrast now that it rests on circumstance rather than heritage — a genuine, teachable case study in how density and proximity shape civic identity, rather than a "these people are like their ancestors" explanation.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a valuable pairing for future DLC 3 content — a recovered document, memoir, or dialogue exchange contrasting the two cities' different developmental paths could double as diegetic worldbuilding that teaches the player this exact "circumstance, not heritage, shapes culture" principle directly.

---

## Finding 2: The proposed lighthouse-spire landmark predicts a concrete, radial city geometry

**Combining:** the Full Extrapolation's proposed lighthouse-spire as Sejong's one shared, neutral landmark that every national community orients toward + the established "boundary zones" as architecturally transitional areas where Sejong's territory meets its neighbors + Izmir's own real quarter system, where distinct self-governed communities organized themselves around shared central infrastructure.

**2nd-order effect:** if the lighthouse-spire is the single most neutral, universally-oriented-toward structure on the island, it's the natural hub for the boundary zones themselves to radiate from, rather than being scattered arbitrarily.

**3rd-order effect:** this gives Sejong's physical layout a concrete, legible shape — a wheel-like arrangement with the neutral lighthouse-spire at the hub and each national community's own territory as a spoke radiating outward, each boundary zone being the seam between adjacent spokes.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a direct level-design implication — the "boundary zone" concept, previously abstract, now has an actual physical logic a level designer could use rather than needing to invent city geometry from nothing.

---

## Finding 3: The shared Enneagram profile with Esperanza may be an underdeveloped truth, not just a documentation lag

**Combining:** Sejong's Enneagram profile ("administrative facilitation," tied to proximity with the Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority) + Esperanza's own shared profile ("equal logistics partnership" with Marambio) + the fact that both cities' own richer, independently established primary identities (Sejong's density-driven diplomatic coordination; Esperanza's founding-compact "Guarded City" identity) are both, at bottom, about negotiating relationships with neighbors — just at very different registers.

**2nd-order effect:** this suggests the "administrative facilitation" trait these two cities share isn't merely a coincidental early-documentation gap — it may be an accurate but underdeveloped signal that both cities' actual civic character is fundamentally about negotiating external relationships as a core skill, expressed at wildly different scales (Sejong's constant multilateral negotiation versus Esperanza's occasional bilateral partnership).

**3rd-order effect:** rather than treating the shared profile purely as an error to correct, it's worth treating it as a genuine, if underdeveloped, signal about a deeper commonality running across all five sibling cities in this Enneagram group (Amundsen Station, Halley, Esperanza, Juan Carlos, Sejong).

**4th-order effect:** this refines the standing methodological check already flagged on Rothera's own Megasheet — rather than assuming every Enneagram-profile lag is simply outdated, it's worth asking, city by city, whether the shared "on behalf of others" trait is the seed of something true that each city's own later development elaborated on differently, rather than dismissing the profile outright.

---

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across All Three Findings

All three findings converge on a single theme: Sejong's real identity is a product of relationship and position, not inheritance — its civic character emerges in direct contrast to Juan Carlos's own, shaped by circumstance rather than heritage (Finding 1); its physical geometry is organized around a shared, neutral point rather than any one community's own center (Finding 2); and even its thinnest documentation (the shared Enneagram profile) turns out to be circling a real truth about negotiation-as-core-skill rather than simply being wrong (Finding 3). Sejong reads, across all three findings, as a city whose whole identity is relational — defined by what surrounds it, organized around what belongs to no single part of it, and even mis-profiled in a way that still gestures at something true about what it actually does for a living.

---

## Worth Your Attention

Finding 1 is the most structurally significant of the three — it takes what could have been a flat "two neighboring cities turned out differently" observation and grounds it in the project's own recently corrected principle that circumstance, not inherited national culture, is what actually shapes a Tepenian city's civic identity, giving Sejong and Juan Carlos genuine teaching value as a paired example of that exact idea.
