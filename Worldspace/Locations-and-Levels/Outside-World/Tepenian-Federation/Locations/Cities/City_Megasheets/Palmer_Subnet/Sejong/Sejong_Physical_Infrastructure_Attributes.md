# Sejong — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Sejong would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Sejong.md`, `Local_Cultures/Palmer_Subnet/Sejong.md`, and `Sejong_
Community_Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred until this
pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30.

**Following `Sejong_Community_Infrastructure.md`'s own precedent:** that file's own Social Cohesion
Mechanisms list is explicitly flagged as too thin for a city this densely multinational, asking for future
expansion rather than treating the pass as complete — worth carrying forward as a live caveat here too,
since the same underlying density argues for more civic infrastructure than a single first pass surfaces.

**Why Sejong's attributes look like nothing else examined so far:** it's the only Tepenian city examined in
this entire pass founded with multiple distinct national exile communities already sharing the same small
island from day one — a dozen separate pre-existing footholds (Korean, Argentine, Brazilian, Chilean,
Polish, Russian, Czech, Uruguayan, and others) coalescing around Sejong specifically, producing an
inter-community coexistence infrastructure with no equivalent anywhere else in Tepenia. It's also, like
Juan Carlos, confirmed to have no highway connection of any kind — genuinely maritime/aviation-access only.

**Governing facts:** King George Island, South Shetland Islands, the most densely, multinationally settled
island in pre-exile Antarctica; status is Destroyed; north of the Antarctic Circle, no polar night or
midnight sun, the mildest climate of any Palmer subnet city; no highway connection exists or is plausible
(160-177km of open water to the mainland Peninsula, far beyond any bridgeable distance established
elsewhere in the project); founded with a dozen separate national exile communities already sharing the
island, requiring genuine boundary-zone negotiation to make coexistence physically workable; Korean founding
population diluted over 130 years of extreme multinational immigration to just 5.79%, its own smallest
Significant-tier nation, a shared founding-era naming heritage with Janbogo rather than an ongoing
demographic one; economy built around King George Island's own favorable, accessible position as a trade
and transit hub.

---

## Multinational Coexistence Infrastructure (the defining, unique feature)

1. **The Boundary Zones** (established) — architecturally transitional, not hard-walled, districts where
   the dozen absorbed national communities' neighborhoods actually met, a genuinely unique civic-planning
   problem among Tepenian cities examined so far.
2. **The Negotiation Hall** (established, figure's own name flagged for future renaming) — where the
   original boundary-zone land-use and resource-sharing agreements between Sejong and its island neighbors
   were actually negotiated, a formal institution no single-nation-founded city needed.

## Shared Landmark Infrastructure

3. **The Lighthouse Spire** (established) — the tallest structure on King George Island, a combined
   lighthouse-and-signal tower visible from every national community's own footholds, the one shared
   landmark none of the dozen communities could claim exclusively.

## Hangul & Cultural Continuity Infrastructure

4. **The Hangul Instruction Halls** (established) — where Hangul literacy and cultural continuity were
   maintained even as the founding Korean population diluted well below its own original demographic
   presence, distinct from Janbogo's or Sinheung's own living Korean-community infrastructure elsewhere.

## Island Isolation & Maritime Access Infrastructure

5. **Maritime and aviation-only access infrastructure**, distinct from every bridge-connected Peninsula
   city examined so far, given the genuinely unbridgeable open-water distance to the mainland.
6. **King George Island Harbor** (established) — the main maritime entry point, well-used given the
   island's own established accessibility as the most reachable part of Antarctica from South America.

## Mild Climate Infrastructure

7. **Structural and drainage engineering calibrated to the mildest, wettest climate of any Palmer subnet
   city**, with above-freezing temperatures and rain common in summer — distinct engineering demands from
   any drier or more southerly city examined in this pass.

## Ruins-Specific Infrastructure

8. **Whatever remains legible of the boundary-zone architecture specifically**, given the established
   coexistence-experiment framing — a genuinely distinct ruins-storytelling register from any single-nation
   destroyed city examined elsewhere, since the central open question here is whether the coexistence itself
   succeeded or produced lasting friction.
