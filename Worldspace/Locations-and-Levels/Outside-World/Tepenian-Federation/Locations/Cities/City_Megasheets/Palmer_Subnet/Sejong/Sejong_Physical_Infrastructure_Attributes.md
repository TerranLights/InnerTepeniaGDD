# Sejong — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Sejong would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Sejong.md`, `Local_Cultures/Palmer_Subnet/Sejong.md`, and `Sejong_
Community_Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred until this
pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30.

**Following `Sejong_Community_Infrastructure.md`'s own precedent:** that file's own Social Cohesion
Mechanisms list is explicitly flagged as too thin for a city this densely multinational, asking for future
expansion rather than treating the pass as complete — worth carrying forward as a live caveat here too,
since the same underlying density argues for more civic infrastructure than a single first pass surfaces.

**Why Sejong's attributes look like nothing else examined so far:** it's the only Tepenian city examined in
this entire pass founded with multiple distinct national exile communities already sharing the same small
island from day one — a dozen separate pre-existing footholds (Korean, Argentine, Brazilian, Chilean,
Polish, Russian, Czech, Uruguayan, and others) coalescing around Sejong specifically, producing an
inter-community coexistence infrastructure with no equivalent anywhere else in Tepenia. It's also, like
Juan Carlos, confirmed to have no highway connection of any kind — genuinely maritime/aviation-access only.

**Governing facts:** King George Island, South Shetland Islands, the most densely, multinationally settled
island in pre-exile Antarctica; status is Destroyed; north of the Antarctic Circle, no polar night or
midnight sun, the mildest climate of any Palmer subnet city; no highway connection exists or is plausible
(160-177km of open water to the mainland Peninsula, far beyond any bridgeable distance established
elsewhere in the project); founded with a dozen separate national exile communities already sharing the
island, requiring genuine boundary-zone negotiation to make coexistence physically workable; **"Sejong" is a
name-only inheritance, not a cultural one — corrected 2026-08-02:** the First Interwar Period (2083-2564) saw
every station but Zhongshan change national hands repeatedly over nearly 500 years, meaning no continuous
Korean population or cultural practice could plausibly have survived from the real-world station's own
naming era through to Tepenian founding; the name persisted purely as inherited toponymic/documentary
record, the way a place can keep a long-ago namesake's name after that namesake's own descendants or culture
are gone, not as evidence of any Korean-descended founding population or institution. A present-day 5.79%
Korean-descent population (Sejong's own smallest Significant-tier nation, per Census II) reflects ordinary
post-founding immigration into Tepenia generally, unconnected to the station's own pre-exile name; economy
built around King George Island's own favorable, accessible position as a trade and transit hub.

## Multinational Coexistence Infrastructure (the defining, unique feature)

1. **The Boundary Zones** (established) — architecturally transitional, not hard-walled, districts where
   the dozen absorbed national communities' neighborhoods actually met, a genuinely unique civic-planning
   problem among Tepenian cities examined so far.
2. **The Negotiation Hall** (established, figure's own name flagged for future renaming) — where the
   original boundary-zone land-use and resource-sharing agreements between Sejong and its island neighbors
   were actually negotiated, a formal institution no single-nation-founded city needed.

## Shared Landmark Infrastructure

3. **The Lighthouse Spire** (established) — the tallest structure on King George Island, a combined
   lighthouse-and-signal tower visible from every national community's own footholds, the one shared
   landmark none of the dozen communities could claim exclusively.

## Island Isolation & Maritime Access Infrastructure

4. **Maritime and aviation-only access infrastructure**, distinct from every bridge-connected Peninsula
   city examined so far, given the genuinely unbridgeable open-water distance to the mainland.
5. **King George Island Harbor** (established) — the main maritime entry point, well-used given the
   island's own established accessibility as the most reachable part of Antarctica from South America.

## Mild Climate Infrastructure

6. **Structural and drainage engineering calibrated to the mildest, wettest climate of any Palmer subnet
   city**, with above-freezing temperatures and rain common in summer — distinct engineering demands from
   any drier or more southerly city examined in this pass.

## Ruins-Specific Infrastructure

7. **Whatever remains legible of the boundary-zone architecture specifically**, given the established
   coexistence-experiment framing — a genuinely distinct ruins-storytelling register from any single-nation
   destroyed city examined elsewhere, since the central open question here is whether the coexistence itself
   succeeded or produced lasting friction.

## Cross-Referenced Extrapolation Findings (Methodology #2)

**What this is:** cross-referencing the 8 attributes above against Sejong's own existing lore —
`Sejong_Full_Extrapolation.md` and `Sejong_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` (which already holds 3 Findings of
its own, written 2026-07-08). Proportionate, moderate depth per this subnet's own weighting — Palmer City
carries the deepest treatment. Written 2026-07-30.

### Finding A: the Boundary Zones confirm Finding 2's own predicted radial city geometry

**Combining:** attribute #1 (the Boundary Zones) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 2 (the
proposed lighthouse-spire landmark predicts a concrete, radial city geometry — a wheel-like arrangement with
each national community as a spoke, boundary zones as the seams between them).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #1, derived independently through Methodology #1 as an established fact,
confirms the existence of exactly the seams Finding 2's own speculative geometry requires.

**3rd-order effect:** gives Finding 2's own level-design implication (a concrete physical logic for the
"boundary zone" concept) a second, independently-derived confirmation — the zones exist as fact; Finding 2
supplies their specific spatial logic radiating from attribute #3's Lighthouse Spire.

### Finding B: the Negotiation Hall is where Finding 1's own "constant, informal, many-sided diplomatic coordination" actually happens

**Combining:** attribute #2 (the Negotiation Hall) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 1
(Sejong and Juan Carlos form a natural experiment — density difference explains their opposite civic
outcomes, Sejong's own constant multilateral coordination versus Juan Carlos's singular archival
institution).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #2 is the concrete physical building where Finding 1's own "constant,
informal, many-sided diplomatic coordination" actually takes place — a genuinely different kind of
institution from Juan Carlos's own single archive.

**3rd-order effect:** gives Finding 1's own rich comparative civic-identity argument a specific location on
Sejong's own side, sharpening the contrast it already draws with Juan Carlos.

### Finding C *(corrected 2026-08-02 — originally about the now-removed Hangul Instruction Halls attribute; see the corrected Governing Facts note above)*: the single shared Harbor is physical evidence for Sejong's own absorption of King George Island's smaller communities

**Combining:** attribute #5 (King George Island Harbor) + the Full Extrapolation's Section I (the island's
smaller national footholds were absorbed into Sejong's own civic structure rather than developing separate
tracked cities, per census data showing only Sejong recognized at all).

**2nd-order effect:** one shared harbor serving what could otherwise have been a dozen separate small-city
ports is consistent with genuine civic consolidation, not merely a demographic-tracking convenience.

**3rd-order effect:** reinforces Section I's own resolved absorption claim with a concrete infrastructure-
level fact — the island's maritime access itself was never fragmented the way a dozen truly independent
cities would require.

### Finding D: the shared isolation profile confirms Finding 1's own "one variable changed" framing at the infrastructure level

**Combining:** attribute #4 (maritime and aviation-only access infrastructure, no highway) + the existing
Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 1 (Sejong and Juan Carlos as a natural experiment, with neighbor density
as the one genuinely differing variable between otherwise similar founding conditions).

**2nd-order effect:** both cities in Finding 1's own comparison share this identical isolation-infrastructure
profile — no highway, maritime/aviation-only access — confirmed independently in each city's own Attributes
file this session.

**3rd-order effect:** strengthens Finding 1's own "one variable changed" claim at the physical infrastructure
level, not just the demographic and civic one — the two cities' connectivity to the rest of Tepenia is
essentially identical, isolating neighbor density as the genuinely singular differing factor.
