## What It Feels Like

Per the developer's own vision session: a genuine two-island structure, using real South Orkney geography. Signy Island itself, the smaller of the two, hosts the one shipping dock on its east side and a substantial radio comms setup; the much larger Coronation Island, connected by a high bridge, is where most residential living and leisure infrastructure sits. The daily rhythm: humans and robots wake (or come back online) and travel south to the docking island to fish, returning in the afternoon to Coronation Island's downtime. Floating extension-platforms expand the island's fishing reach out to sea wherever the geography allows it. Time moves slowly here — nobody's in a real rush, a civic rhythm paced by wildlife cycles rather than urgency. The comms setup is confirmed as radio, not Arcanet — not a contradiction of Signy's established "weakest Arcanet link in the Federation" status, but a direct consequence of it: Signy relies on old-fashioned radio precisely because modern digital networking physically can't reach this far.

Post-war, that same self-sufficiency has both saved and strained the city. Fish and marine resources are abundant enough that a purely marine diet sustains the human population indefinitely. Robots face the opposite problem: siligel, their established nutrition source, isn't something Signy can produce or improvise locally, and with subnet supply lines cut, the robot population faces a genuine, unresolved shortage — the untouched city that still needs help, not because it was bombed, but because it was cut off.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Corfu (Kerkyras), Greece

Historically Venice's fortified "Door of Venice," successfully defended through multiple sieges (1537, 1571, 1573, 1716) via fortification and positioning rather than raw military strength, alongside a traditional artisanal fishing method using floating lights to draw fish toward encircling nets.

**What this gives Signy:** less a scale match than a structural echo of strategic-isolation-as-defense — Corfu's centuries of surviving siege through position and fortification parallels, loosely, how Signy's own remoteness (rather than any deliberate defense) became its actual, unplanned protection against Long Night War targeting.

### Primary: El Hierro, Canary Islands, Spain

A small volcanic island whose wind-pumped-hydro system once ran the entire island on renewables alone for 28 consecutive days, a world record, and currently covers roughly 75% of annual demand.

**What this gives Signy:** genuine, proven real-world proof that a small, remote island can achieve near-total energy self-sufficiency through technology matched to its own local conditions (wind and hydro, not solar) — directly relevant groundwork for imagining how Signy's own radio, dock, and floating-platform infrastructure could be locally power-sufficient despite total isolation from any wider grid.

### Primary: Lofoten Islands, Norway

Arctic cod fishing culture since the Viking Age, the largest cod fishing season on Earth, tight-knit villages now targeting full wind-and-micro-hydro renewable integration by 2030.

**What this gives Signy:** the closest single real-world match to Signy's own established identity — a genuinely major fishing economy built directly on the same kind of maritime richness the Scotia Sea's water-mass confluence provides Signy, at a comparably remote, cold-climate, small-village scale.

### Primary: Tristan da Cunha

The most remote inhabited archipelago on Earth — 242 residents, 2,400km from its nearest neighbor — self-sufficient through subsistence farming alongside an MSC-certified, best-practice lobster and crawfish export fishery.

**What this gives Signy:** the most direct real-world precedent for Signy's own specific post-war situation — genuine, extreme isolation sustained successfully through a combination of local self-sufficiency (echoing Signy's own marine-diet self-sufficiency for its human population) and a well-managed export fishery, a template for isolation as a stable way of life rather than merely endured hardship.

### Secondary: Tenerife, Canary Islands, Spain

Abundant year-round sunlight and rapidly growing solar capacity (combined wind and solar recently exceeding 22% of installed capacity), though notably not connected to any mainland grid, leaving island residents with genuinely higher energy costs.

**What this gives Signy:** a useful contrast rather than a direct match — Tenerife's solar abundance doesn't transfer to Signy's own much higher-latitude, fog-heavy, gale-driven climate, reinforcing that wind, not solar, is Signy's more physically honest renewable analog, consistent with this project's own earlier climate-modeling work on the subject.

### Secondary: Samsø, Denmark

The world's first fully renewable island (2007), powered by community- and farmer-owned wind turbines alongside biomass and solar, its residents now carrying a negative average carbon footprint.

**What this gives Signy:** a genuine best-case precedent for community-owned, rather than utility-owned, renewable infrastructure — relevant to imagining Signy's own small, self-managing population sustaining its own local power generation cooperatively rather than through any centralized authority reaching in from outside.

### Secondary: Shetland Islands, Scotland

Deep fishing heritage alongside the windiest site in the UK, home to the 443MW Viking Wind Farm — though real tension exists between the fishing fleet and offshore wind development.

**What this gives Signy:** an honest, nuanced precedent showing that even a genuinely wind-rich, fishing-rich location can have real internal tension between its two major industries — worth remembering if Signy's own floating-platform fishing infrastructure and any future renewable buildout ever need to share the same limited maritime space.

### Supporting Picks, Condensed

- **Newfoundland outport communities** — historically self-sufficient, consensus-governed fishing villages, sustained by cod and herring until the 1992 moratorium forced mass resettlement. *Gives Signy a real cautionary tale: marine self-sufficiency, while currently working, isn't necessarily permanent or immune to resource collapse.*
- **Orkney Islands** — home to the European Marine Energy Center, the world's leading wave/tidal test site, waters still genuinely shared with active fishing. *Gives Signy a third viable renewable option (wave/tidal, not just wind) worth considering given its own open-ocean, gale-exposed position.*
- **King Island, Tasmania** — a small, ungridded island whose hybrid wind/solar/battery/flywheel microgrid supplies over 65% of annual energy needs renewably. *Gives Signy a genuine hybrid-microgrid precedent, not single-technology, for exactly the kind of small, totally ungridded settlement it already is.*
- **Vestmannaeyjar, Iceland** — Iceland's most productive fishing center, geothermally heated from a 1973 volcanic eruption that nearly destroyed the town. *Gives Signy the general pattern of a natural hazard repurposed into infrastructure — worth remembering even without a matching geothermal resource.*
- **Faroe Islands** — fishing and aquaculture over 90% of exports, historically the entire basis of a self-reliant island society, targeting 100% renewable electricity by 2030. *Reinforces the closest whole-economy match to Signy's own fishing-dominant identity, at national rather than single-community scale.*
