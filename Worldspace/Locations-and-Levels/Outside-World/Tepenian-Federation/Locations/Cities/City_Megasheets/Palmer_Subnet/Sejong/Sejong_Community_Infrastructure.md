# Sejong — Community Infrastructure & Social Life

**What this is:** concrete physical spaces, small institutions, and recurring social rituals that give Sejong's already-established culture a tangible, ground-level presence — the same pass done for each of Concordia's 13 districts, all 23 Mirny, Halley, and Janbogo subnet cities, and Palmer City, Esperanza, Juan Carlos, Marambio, Port Lockroy, and Rothera, extended here. Sejong is destroyed — everything below is framed as pre-war, now among the ruins. **Note:** `Sejong_Full_Extrapolation.md` Section V proposes a "Hangul Day" holiday — rejected directly by the developer ("that wouldn't happen") and not included below. Drawn from `Sejong_Mega_Init.md`, `Sejong_Full_Extrapolation.md`, `Sejong_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md`, and `Local_Cultures/Palmer_Subnet/Sejong.md`. Written 2026-07-30.

## Additions (pre-war, now ruins)

- **The Lighthouse Spire** — the tallest structure on King George Island, a combined lighthouse-and-signal tower visible from every national community's footholds, the one shared landmark none of the dozen communities can claim exclusively.
- **The Boundary Zones** — the architecturally transitional, not hard-walled, districts where the dozen absorbed national communities' neighborhoods met.
- **The Negotiation Hall** *(figure's own name, "Han Ji-woo," flagged for future renaming — not developer-chosen)* — where the original boundary-zone agreements were negotiated between Sejong and its island neighbors.
- **The Hangul Instruction Halls** — where Hangul literacy and cultural continuity were maintained even as the population diversified well beyond its founding nation.

## Small offices for educational training (pre-war)

- maritime signaling and lighthouse/tower maintenance
- boundary-zone negotiation and multinational neighborhood coordination
- Hangul literacy instruction

## Social cohesion mechanisms (pre-war)

**Flagged for future expansion** — a city this densely populated and this multinational (a dozen absorbed national communities sharing one small island) would plausibly support far more social cohesion mechanisms than the single one below; this list should be revisited and expanded, not treated as complete.

- **The Neighbors' Accord** — marking the anniversary of the original land-use/resource-sharing agreement that made coexistence physically workable, deliberately separate from any claim the underlying tension was ever fully resolved.
