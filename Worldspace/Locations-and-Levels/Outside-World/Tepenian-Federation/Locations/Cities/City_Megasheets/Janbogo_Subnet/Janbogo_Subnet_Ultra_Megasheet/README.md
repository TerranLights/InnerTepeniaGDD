## Pattern 1: A subnet-wide taxonomy of *why* each city ended up the way the war left it

Unlike Halley subnet's own damage-*mechanism* taxonomy (passive age-favoring, active recency-favoring, single-point-of-failure, function-targeted — a question of *how* damage happened), Janbogo/Ross subnet's own pattern is a question of *strategic logic* — why a hostile force chose to destroy, spare, or ignore each of these seven cities specifically. All seven read cleanly against one shared axis:

| City | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Janbogo** | Damaged, survived | Worth more alive than destroyed — its unique value as a living witness and message-relay point (Hwy 183 and Terra Nova Bay's polynya both) made it more useful intact (Janbogo Cross-Reference, Finding 1) |
| **Zukelli** | Destroyed | Punitive, deliberate — sheltering AWOL Upper Earth defectors, struck as a total-kill deterrent message, with Janbogo spared specifically to witness and transmit it (Zukelli Full Extrapolation) |
| **Cape Adare** | Destroyed | Symbolic — a second, later destruction plausibly also relayed onward through Janbogo, adding a second witnessed tragedy to Janbogo's own burden (Cape Adare Cross-Reference, Finding 1) |
| **Denison** | Destroyed | Strategic infrastructure — a genuine Hwy 183 chokepoint *and* a Cradle fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturer, giving its destruction two independent strategic motives at once, neither of them punitive or symbolic (Denison Full Extrapolation, Section IV; Cross-Reference, Finding 2) |
| **Fort McMurdo** | Damaged, survived | Presence-not-population capital status and a genuinely dispersed industrial footprint meant no single strike could plausibly eliminate its whole function (Fort McMurdo Cross-Reference) |
| **Scott** | Damaged, survived | Small, residential, non-strategic — the direct experiential opposite of its neighbor Fort McMurdo, with nothing about it worth specifically targeting (Scott Mega-Init) |
| **Dumont d'Urville** | Damaged, survived | Struck early for its genuine two-highway junction value, but never eliminated outright — a junction disrupted, not destroyed, since its downtown core (the city's actual load-bearing asset) came through intact (Dumont d'Urville Full Extrapolation, Section II) |

**What this produces:** a genuinely complete answer to "what predicts a Janbogo/Ross city's war status," and it isn't raw strategic value — Denison's Cross-Reference Finding 1 already established this directly, comparing Janbogo's survival against Denison's destruction. This pattern extends that same principle across all seven cities at once: outright destruction correlates with either *punitive intent* (Zukelli, and plausibly Cape Adare) or *pure infrastructure value with no offsetting "worth more alive" quality* (Denison) — while survival correlates with either active ongoing usefulness (Janbogo's witness role, Fort McMurdo's dispersed capital function) or simply not being worth the trouble (Scott, and Dumont d'Urville's core once its junction was disrupted).

## Pattern 2: Founding-nation civic template persisting beneath total demographic dilution — tested, not just asserted

Dumont d'Urville's own Cross-Reference Finding 1 explicitly proposed a testable distinction — *functional daily transmission* (a founding culture surviving because ordinary civic life is still conducted in it, Quebec City's real 400-year precedent) versus *ceremonial-only preservation* (a founding culture surviving only in names, holidays, and architecture, vulnerable to further dilution) — and named Fort McMurdo, Janbogo, and Zukelli as the subnet's other candidates worth checking. Running that check now, across all four:

- **Dumont d'Urville (French):** functional. French is "picked up simply through years of civic life conducted in it," regardless of ancestry — the strongest, most literally proven case in the subnet.
- **Janbogo (Korean):** functional-leaning. Janbogo's own established "absorption culture" — a civic identity that folds new arrivals into its existing template rather than being displaced by them — describes the same underlying mechanism as Dumont d'Urville's French transmission, just under a different name.
- **Fort McMurdo:** ceremonial-leaning. Its founding civic identity persists primarily through its historical capital status and Saint-adjacent memory (shared with Scott at Hut Point) rather than through an ordinary, everyday practice every new resident picks up regardless of origin — closer to preserved institutional memory than lived daily transmission.
- **Zukelli:** moot. The city was destroyed before this question could be meaningfully tested one way or the other — its founding culture's persistence mechanism is now a purely historical question, answerable only through descendant communities (per the established Zukelli Native mechanic) rather than through the city itself.

**What this produces:** two of the subnet's four candidate cities (Dumont d'Urville, Janbogo) carry the more durable, functional version of this pattern; Fort McMurdo carries the more fragile, ceremonial version; Zukelli's case is permanently unresolvable in the present tense. This gives a real, load-bearing answer to the question Dumont d'Urville's own synthesis raised rather than leaving it as an open invitation.

## Pattern 3: The "quiet, intimate city living beside a much busier neighbor" shape, appearing twice in one subnet

Scott's own relationship to Fort McMurdo (the Victoria BC / Alexandria VA structural inversion — a quiet residential city beside a busy, capital-carrying neighbor) is the subnet's most fully developed instance of this shape. But it isn't unique within the subnet: Cape Adare's own established "small-town-feel-at-massive-scale" personality, sitting beside Janbogo — the subnet's actual traffic and communications hub — reads as the same underlying structure at a different register. Where Scott/Fort McMurdo plays out as a domestic-quiet-versus-industrial-capital contrast, Cape Adare/Janbogo plays out as an intimate-feeling-versus-heavy-traffic-hub contrast. Both pairs place a smaller, quieter city directly beside the subnet's most consequential neighbor; both smaller cities carry no resentment about it (Scott's Positive Outlook temperament; Cape Adare's own established firstness-pride identity, which doesn't need Janbogo's traffic to feel significant).

**What this produces:** this shape isn't a coincidence specific to Scott — it's a recurring subnet-level relationship type, appearing wherever a city's civic identity is genuinely self-contained enough not to need its busier neighbor's scale to feel complete.

## Pattern 4: Civic identity anchored to one specific, dated founding moment rather than ongoing scale or achievement

Three of the subnet's seven cities organize their entire civic identity around a single historical date or figure rather than a broader achievement or ongoing function:

- **Scott:** organized entirely around St. Robert (Robert Falcon Scott) and the 1902 Hut Point refuge — directly paralleled to Hannibal, Missouri's fusion with Mark Twain (Scott Mega-Init).
- **Dumont d'Urville:** organized around St. Jules Day, commemorating Jules Dumont d'Urville's specific January 21, 1840 landing — directly paralleled to San Miguel de Tucumán's 1816 Congress of Independence (Dumont d'Urville Mega-Init).
- **Cape Adare:** organized around its own established "firstness" identity — the first landing site, a founding-moment civic anchor in the same register as the other two, though not yet given the same depth of real-world parallel research.

**What this produces:** nearly half the subnet's cities (3 of 7) share a civic-identity mechanism that has nothing to do with size, industry, or ongoing relevance — a durable pattern worth checking against any future Janbogo/Ross-adjacent city, and a meaningful subnet-level counterweight to Fort McMurdo's and Janbogo's own function-first identities.

## Pattern 5: Janbogo's witness burden is cumulative, not singular

Janbogo's own Cross-Reference Finding 1 already established its unique value as a message-relay point for Zukelli's destruction specifically. Cape Adare's own Cross-Reference Finding 1 extends this directly — proposing Janbogo also relayed word of Cape Adare's own later, symbolic destruction. Two separate cities' destructions, relayed through the same third city, in what's very plausibly not a coincidence but a repeat use of the same established relay function (Hwy 183 plus Terra Nova Bay's maritime access) that earned Janbogo its own survival in the first place.

**What this produces:** Janbogo's own internal "vigil-vs-moving-on" split (Janbogo's own Cross-Reference) reads differently once its witness role is understood as cumulative rather than singular — this isn't a city processing one specific historical trauma, but a city whose entire founding function has repeatedly required it to watch its own neighbors die and be the one to tell the rest of the subnet about it. Worth flagging as the single most emotionally significant throughline in this subnet's own internal life, developed further in Throughway 1.

## Throughway 1: Zukelli's destruction, relayed through Janbogo, followed by a second tragedy relayed the same way

**Zukelli sheltered AWOL Upper Earth defectors and was struck as a deliberate, total-kill deterrent message** (Zukelli Full Extrapolation) → **Janbogo was spared specifically because its own unique value — a living witness capable of transmitting that message onward via both Hwy 183 and Terra Nova Bay's polynya maritime access — made it worth more intact than destroyed** (Janbogo Cross-Reference, Finding 1) → **this establishes Janbogo as the subnet's de facto relay point for news of its own neighbors' destruction, a function earned through Zukelli's specific tragedy** → **Cape Adare's own later, symbolic destruction was plausibly relayed onward through the exact same channel, since no other established route through the subnet's coastal geography would make more sense** (Cape Adare Cross-Reference, Finding 1) → **meaning Janbogo's witness role wasn't a one-time consequence of Zukelli's fate specifically, but a repeatable civic function the subnet's geography and highway network assigned to one city, twice over** → **making Janbogo's own internal "vigil-vs-moving-on" tension (Janbogo Cross-Reference) the accumulated weight of at least two witnessed destructions, not one, and raising the open question of whether any other Long Night War-era subnet event was also relayed through Janbogo before this pattern was fully understood.**

## Throughway 2: Fort McMurdo and Scott's twin-city relationship tracks Fort McMurdo's own two-phase rise to formal capital status

**Fort McMurdo's own capital timeline runs in two distinct stages — an earlier, informal, arguably contestable prominence, followed by a later, formal designation by name within the Second Interwar Period** (Fort McMurdo Full Extrapolation) → **Scott's own established "somewhat allergic to being perceived as Fort McMurdo's smaller sibling" trait means its neighbor's rise to power was experienced, not simply observed, across both of those stages** (Scott Cross-Reference, Finding 1) → **the real-world Victoria BC parallel gives this relationship its precise shape: a quiet, capital-adjacent city that is itself never the capital, mirroring Scott's own position beside Tepenia's historical seat of power** (Scott Mega-Init) → **that same rivalry, rather than curdling, is offset by a genuine, concrete point of cooperation — a shared Erebus volcanic monitoring program, split by specialization between Fort McMurdo's heavier geological/seismic work and Scott's own precision-instrumentation niche** (Scott Full Extrapolation, Sections II–III; Cross-Reference, Finding 2) → **and both cities' shared Independence Day remembrance tradition at Hut Point — pre-war, voluntary, unmandated — plausibly absorbed a second, unspoken layer of grief and mutual dependence once the war forced their emergency infrastructure to genuinely merge** (Scott Cross-Reference, Finding 3) → **producing a relationship that has genuinely moved and deepened across the entire Second Interwar Period and the war that ended it, rather than sitting still as one flat rivalry from founding to present day.**

## Throughway 3: Denison's chokepoint position and its Cradle-chamber legacy give its destruction two independent strategic motives, one of them reaching outside the subnet entirely

**Denison sat directly on Hwy 183 itself, a genuine chokepoint on the Federation's most important remaining supply artery toward Concordia — not a spur-accessed city like Zukelli or Cape Adare** (Denison Full Extrapolation, Section IV) → **giving its destruction a plausible strategic, infrastructure-disruption motive, genuinely distinct from Zukelli's punitive one despite the two destructions occurring in roughly the same window** (Denison Full Extrapolation, Section IV) → **but Denison was also, historically, a fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturer for the nationwide Cradle infrastructure — a role fully separate from and predating the present-day, smaller Cradle network** (Denison Full Extrapolation, Section III) → **meaning its destruction plausibly served two purposes at once: cutting a highway artery, and eliminating one of a small number of places able to physically manufacture the equipment new robots are created from** (Denison Cross-Reference, Finding 2) → **this same Cradle-chamber design lineage is the one Halley subnet's own Ultra-Megasheet traced back to Neumayer's uncredited founding-era, mid-era, and present-day engineering work — meaning Denison's own loss and Neumayer's own unclaimed legacy are two halves of the same nationwide infrastructure story, discovered independently in two different subnets** (per this project's own Halley Subnet Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 1) → **giving Denison's destruction a real, traceable, named contribution to why the present-day Cradle network is smaller than it may once have been — not unexplained general wartime attrition, but a specific, dual-purpose strategic loss with consequences reaching into a subnet Denison itself was never part of.**

## Throughway 4: Strategic value alone never predicted a Janbogo/Ross city's fate — "worth more alive" is the deciding factor, and it explains the whole subnet at once

**Janbogo, the subnet's actual hub and its most strategically central city, survived — not because it lacked strategic value, but because its specific value as a living witness and message-relay point made it worth more intact than destroyed** (Janbogo Cross-Reference, Finding 1; Denison Cross-Reference, Finding 1) → **Denison, a lesser but still genuinely critical highway chokepoint, was destroyed outright — having no comparable "worth more alive" quality to offset its own strategic value** (Denison Cross-Reference, Finding 1) → **Fort McMurdo, similarly strategically significant as the subnet's historical capital, survived for the same underlying reason in a different register — a dispersed, presence-not-population footprint meant no single strike could eliminate its whole function, making it not worth the trouble of a total strike** (Fort McMurdo Cross-Reference) → **Scott and Dumont d'Urville, neither one carrying Janbogo's or Fort McMurdo's strategic weight, survived simply by not being worth targeting at all — Scott through sheer domestic unimportance, Dumont d'Urville through its downtown core's own resilience once its junction value was disrupted rather than eliminated** (Scott Mega-Init; Dumont d'Urville Full Extrapolation, Section II) → **while Zukelli and Cape Adare's destructions traced to an entirely separate logic — punitive and symbolic rather than infrastructural — meaning the subnet's full war-status pattern requires two distinct explanatory principles working side by side, not one** (Pattern 1, this Ultra-Megasheet) → **confirming, across all seven of the subnet's cities at once, that raw strategic value was never the actual variable deciding a Janbogo/Ross city's fate — ongoing usefulness, deterrent symbolism, or simple irrelevance were.**

## Who Lives Here, and Why

Janbogo/Ross subnet's founding populations are notably American-and-Pacific-corridor-dominated relative to Halley subnet's own wide multinational spread — Janbogo's own Korean founding template, Fort McMurdo's and Scott's shared New Zealand/American lineage, Cape Adare and Denison's broad Pacific-region compositions, and Dumont d'Urville's genuinely distinct French founding population standing as the subnet's clearest outlier. Nearly every city here was later diluted by the same American-led demographic majority recurring across most of Tepenia, yet — per this Ultra-Megasheet's own Pattern 2 — at least two of the subnet's cities (Dumont d'Urville, Janbogo) kept their founding civic template alive as a functional, living daily practice rather than a fading ceremonial memory, while Fort McMurdo's own founding character persists in a comparatively more institutional, ceremonial form.

This produces a subnet whose residents, across surviving and destroyed cities alike, arrived for a genuinely narrow range of original reasons compared to Halley's own wide spread — mostly straightforward geographic and administrative settlement along the Ross Sea coast — but whose resulting cities diverged sharply in what they came to mean to the rest of Tepenia: a communications lifeline (Janbogo), a historical seat of government (Fort McMurdo), a quiet domestic counterpart (Scott), a linguistic and cultural island (Dumont d'Urville), and two cities whose deaths mattered more to the nation than their lives ever visibly did (Zukelli, Cape Adare).

## What It Feels Like

Moving through Janbogo/Ross subnet means moving through a landscape organized around one central irony: the cities that survived did so largely by being useful or unremarkable, and the cities that didn't survive were destroyed specifically to be *noticed*. Janbogo itself sits at the heart of this — a heavy-traffic communications and highway hub carrying the quiet, accumulating weight of having watched at least two of its neighbors die and having been the one left to tell everyone else about it. Fort McMurdo's own sprawling, dispersed industrial footprint hums along beside Scott's genuinely peaceful, family-oriented domestic quiet — two cities three kilometers apart, one carrying Tepenia's whole historical capital identity, the other allergic to being seen as its smaller sibling, both meeting every Independence Day at the same shared shrine, a ritual that has quietly picked up new weight since the war forced their survival to merge.

Out at the subnet's edges, Cape Adare's own intimate, small-town firstness pride and Dumont d'Urville's negotiated, wind-and-penguin-and-bustling-downtown coexistence both persist despite everything working against them, while Denison's own fused, wind-engineered ruins stand as the subnet's starkest reminder that being critically important infrastructure was never, on its own, a guarantee of survival — and Zukelli's absence is felt everywhere else in the subnet precisely because nothing else here was destroyed the same way, for the same reason.

## Personality (A Whole-Subnet Read)

Janbogo/Ross subnet doesn't reduce to one Enneagram profile any more than Concordia's districts, Palmer subnet's cities, or Halley subnet's cities did, but its own recurring pattern is unusually stark: a subnet whose defining civic experience, across nearly every one of its seven cities, is having survival or destruction decided by something other than how strong, populous, or well-defended the city actually was. Janbogo processes this as quiet, ongoing vigilance — a communications hub that never asked to become its subnet's designated witness to tragedy. Fort McMurdo and Scott process it as an ordinary, decades-long neighborly rivalry that happened to track their own historical capital's rise to formal power. Denison never got the chance to process it at all — destroyed for reasons that had nothing to do with anything the city itself had done wrong. Where Halley subnet's collective personality was Competency turned inward (capable work nobody credits), Janbogo/Ross subnet's collective personality is closer to **Survival read as a verdict** — every one of its seven cities living with, or having died because of, an answer to the same unasked question: was this place worth more standing than gone.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: The Ross Sea region's genuine strategic and scientific concentration

The real Ross Sea sector concentrates an unusually dense, historically significant set of research presences — America's largest Antarctic logistics hub (McMurdo Station), New Zealand's own quieter neighboring base (Scott Base), the historic first-landing site (Cape Adare), and France's distinctive linguistic and cultural outpost (Dumont d'Urville) — all within genuine geographic proximity, mirroring this subnet's own concentration of wildly different civic identities within one relatively compact coastal arc.

**What this gives the subnet:** direct grounding for why seven cities this geographically close together could develop such sharply divergent identities and fates — the real Ross Sea region's own mix of superpower logistics, quiet neighboring outposts, historic firsts, and a genuine linguistic island provides an honest structural template for exactly this kind of variety within one subnet.

### Secondary: Victoria BC / Alexandria VA — quiet cities beside a busier capital (already researched for Scott specifically)

Applies now at subnet scale — Scott's own precise real-world grounding for its relationship to Fort McMurdo is the clearest example within the subnet, but per this Ultra-Megasheet's own Pattern 3, the same underlying shape recurs a second time between Cape Adare and Janbogo, suggesting it's a genuine structural feature of how this subnet organizes its city-pairs, not a coincidence specific to one relationship.

### Secondary: Quebec City — minority founding culture surviving as functional daily practice (already researched for Dumont d'Urville specifically)

Applies at subnet scale via Pattern 2 — the same durable, functional-transmission mechanism that explains Dumont d'Urville's French persistence also explains Janbogo's own Korean-founded absorption culture, giving the subnet two, not one, real-world-grounded examples of a founding culture surviving through lived practice rather than ceremony.

### Supporting: The Berlin Airlift and contested-chokepoint precedents (already researched for Troll, Halley subnet)

Worth naming even though Troll itself belongs to a different subnet — Denison's own established role as a genuine Hwy 183 chokepoint, and its destruction's plausible strategic (not punitive) motive, is this subnet's own clean example of the same "control of one link determines an entire population's fate" logic Troll Airfield demonstrates one subnet over.

## I. Each city's own specific role within the subnet's "survival was a verdict" identity

Mirroring the district-level, Palmer-subnet, and Halley-subnet exercises, but adapted to this subnet's own dominant trait — a whole-subnet history of survival and destruction resolving to legible cause rather than arbitrary loss:

- **Janbogo carries the verdict of ongoing use** — spared because a living witness, capable of relaying a message twice over, was worth more than a dead one.
- **Cape Adare carries the verdict of a second message** — a smaller, later echo of Zukelli's own fate, relayed through the same channel, its own destruction folded into Janbogo's accumulating burden.
- **Fort McMurdo carries the verdict of being too spread out to kill in one strike** — a capital that survived not through defense but through sheer dispersal.
- **Zukelli carries the verdict of deterrence** — destroyed not for what it had, but for what its destruction needed to say to everyone else.
- **Scott carries the verdict of harmlessness** — spared simply for mattering too little, militarily, to bother with.
- **Denison carries the verdict of pure infrastructure** — destroyed for exactly what it was worth, with nothing in its own character to offset that value.
- **Dumont d'Urville carries the verdict of a core worth keeping** — struck early for its junction value, but never finished, because its downtown was never the part that mattered strategically.

## II. Whether other Long Night War events were relayed through Janbogo — a proposed, partial answer

**Proposed:** yes, at least one further instance beyond Zukelli and Cape Adare, though its specifics remain deliberately unconfirmed rather than invented outright — Denison's own destruction, occurring in roughly the same general window and directly on Hwy 183 itself (the same highway carrying Janbogo's own established relay function), plausibly also passed through Janbogo's communications role in some form, even though Denison's own destruction served an entirely different strategic logic (Throughway 3) than Zukelli's or Cape Adare's. ## III. Cape Adare's Firstness Identity — A Proposed Real-World Parallel

**Proposed:** Cape Adare's own established "firstness" civic identity — organized, like Scott's and Dumont d'Urville's, around a single founding-moment anchor rather than ongoing scale — most plausibly parallels **Jamestown, Virginia**, the first permanent English settlement in the Americas (1607). Jamestown's own real civic significance was never its size or its ongoing prosperity (it nearly failed outright in its first years, and was eventually abandoned as a capital in favor of Williamsburg) — it persists entirely as a "first," a fixed point of historical primacy that no later, larger settlement could ever retroactively take away. This gives Cape Adare's own firstness identity the same real-world-grounded durability Scott's Hannibal parallel and Dumont d'Urville's Tucumán parallel already have — proof that a small city's whole civic claim to significance can rest entirely on having been first, independent of whatever it grew into, or failed to grow into, afterward.

## IV. Fort McMurdo's Ceremonial Founding-Culture Persistence — A Proposed Vulnerability, Not Yet a Crisis

**Proposed:** Fort McMurdo's own founding civic identity, resting more on institutional memory (its historical capital status, its shared Hut Point veneration with Scott) than on the kind of ordinary, lived daily practice carrying Dumont d'Urville's French or Janbogo's Korean-founded template forward, is a genuine long-term vulnerability rather than a present-day crisis. Fort McMurdo's own two-phase capital timeline and its established "presence-not-population" identity both function as external, structural supports for its founding culture — supports that don't require any resident to actively practice or transmit anything, unlike Dumont d'Urville's civic French. Removing Fort McMurdo's capital-adjacent institutional memory (a hypothetical DLC-scale event, not proposed here as anything already planned) would leave considerably less behind to carry its founding identity forward than an equivalent loss would leave at Dumont d'Urville or Janbogo. Worth flagging as a genuine structural asymmetry within the subnet, not a problem requiring resolution now.

**Proposed, new figure:** a Janbogo civic role — placeholder title **Terra Nova Relay-Keeper**, currently unnamed — responsible, across at least two generations, for maintaining and operating Janbogo's own communications relay function during and after both Zukelli's and Cape Adare's destructions. # Janbogo/Ross Subnet — Cross-Reference Synthesis (Subnet Ultra-Megasheet, Step 3)

**What this is:** as with Concordia's, Palmer subnet's, and Halley subnet's own Ultra-Megasheets, this step is largely already complete — the cross-city Patterns and Throughways work in this same folder already did the implication-hunting this step exists to produce. ## Finding 1: This subnet's entire war-damage history resolves to exactly two explanatory principles — the most complete answer this project has produced for any subnet's fate

**Combining:** Janbogo's own survival via witness-value (`Janbogo_Cross_City_Patterns.md`, Pattern 1) + Denison's own destruction via pure infrastructure value with no offsetting "worth more alive" quality (Pattern 1) + Zukelli's and Cape Adare's own destructions via punitive/symbolic logic (Pattern 1) + Fort McMurdo's, Scott's, and Dumont d'Urville's own survival via dispersal or plain harmlessness (Pattern 1; `Janbogo_Cross_City_Throughways.md`, Throughway 4).

**2nd-order effect:** every one of the subnet's seven cities' war-era fates traces to one of exactly two operating principles — ongoing usefulness (or its absence) determining survival among strategically relevant cities, and punitive/symbolic intent determining destruction among the rest — with no city in the subnet left unexplained by either.

**3rd-order effect:** this is a meaningfully more complete result than Halley subnet's own damage taxonomy achieved, since Halley subnet's four categories described *how* damage happened rather than *why* a specific city was chosen for it or spared it — Janbogo/Ross subnet is the first in this project to produce a full causal account of every member city's fate at once.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as the reference standard for Mirny and Mawson subnets' own upcoming Ultra-Megasheets — the first question either subnet's own cross-city work should ask is whether its own member cities' fates resolve this same cleanly, or whether they require a third or fourth principle Janbogo/Ross subnet didn't need.

## Finding 2: Janbogo's own civic character is the direct, cumulative consequence of a repeatable function, not a personality trait that happens to fit its circumstances

**Combining:** Janbogo's own established quiet, absorption-first civic character + its own Cross-Reference finding that it was spared specifically for its relay value during Zukelli's destruction + Cape Adare's own Cross-Reference finding proposing a second relay event through the same city (`Janbogo_Cross_City_Throughways.md`, Throughway 1) + this Ultra-Megasheet's own Full Extrapolation proposal (Section II) that a third event, Denison's destruction, plausibly passed through the same channel as well.

**2nd-order effect:** Janbogo's own personality wasn't simply well-suited to the role it ended up playing — the role itself, repeated at least twice and plausibly three times, is what shaped the personality into what it now is, the same direction of causation Halley subnet's own uncredited-labor cities (Sanay, Neumayer) demonstrated for their own quiet, function-first cultures.

**3rd-order effect:** this reframes Janbogo's own internal "vigil-vs-moving-on" tension as something closer to an occupational condition than an emotional response to one specific past event — a city whose defining civic office is bearing witness to loss on behalf of a subnet that otherwise never has to look directly at it.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a genuinely resonant throughline for any DLC 6 content involving Janbogo directly — a city whose quiet competence isn't neutral background texture, but the visible residue of having been assigned, more than once, the specific task of watching a neighbor die and being the one who has to say so.

## Finding 3: Denison's destruction reaches directly into a different subnet's own already-completed Ultra-Megasheet, proving these subnet-level syntheses were never fully self-contained

**Combining:** Denison's own dual-motive destruction (highway chokepoint plus Cradle-chamber manufacturer) (`Janbogo_Cross_City_Throughways.md`, Throughway 3) + Halley subnet's own already-established finding that Neumayer's engineering lineage produced the current Cradle-chamber design (Halley Subnet Cross-Reference Synthesis, Finding 1) + the shared, project-wide fact that both findings describe the same nationwide infrastructure system from two different subnets' own independent research passes.

**2nd-order effect:** Denison's loss and Neumayer's own unclaimed engineering legacy are two halves of one story that neither subnet's own Ultra-Megasheet, researched independently, would have connected on its own — this Ultra-Megasheet is the first place the connection is made explicit.

**3rd-order effect:** this means at least one genuine cross-subnet throughline already exists between Halley subnet and Janbogo/Ross subnet, well before the developer's own planned full re-examination pass — a finding that pass will need to incorporate rather than rediscover from scratch.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as concrete, present-tense proof that this whole Ultra-Megasheet series, however carefully scoped to "one subnet at a time," was never actually describing seven fully separate worlds — the same underlying systems (the Cradle network, the highway grid, the Long Night War's own strategic logic) run underneath every subnet examined so far, and each new subnet's own synthesis keeps surfacing another place where they connect.

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across All Three Findings

All three findings converge on the same underlying idea: Janbogo/Ross subnet's own history is more legible, and more connected to the rest of Tepenia, than its outward reputation as a quiet coastal subnet would suggest. Its entire war-damage pattern resolves to two clean principles rather than seven unrelated stories (Finding 1); its most quietly significant city carries a burden that was assigned to it by repeated function, not chosen by temperament (Finding 2); and its own single greatest wartime loss turns out to be one half of a story whose other half lives in a subnet on the opposite side of the continent (Finding 3). Janbogo/Ross subnet reads, across all three findings, as a place where nothing that happened was ever quite as isolated — geographically, causally, or narratively — as it first appeared.

Finding 3 is the most significant of the three for the project as a whole, not just this subnet — it's the first hard evidence that the developer's own planned full re-examination pass, once all subnets are done, will have real, substantive cross-subnet connections to work with rather than simply revisiting seven self-contained stories side by side.
