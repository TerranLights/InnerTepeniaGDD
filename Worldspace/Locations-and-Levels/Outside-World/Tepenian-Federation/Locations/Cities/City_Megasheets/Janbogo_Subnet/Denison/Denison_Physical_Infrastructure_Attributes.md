# Denison — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Denison would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Denison.md`, `Local_Cultures/Janbogo_Subnet/Denison.md`, and `Denison_
Community_Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred until this
pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30.

**A note on Cradle-adjacent infrastructure below:** Denison was a confirmed historical fabrication-synthesis
chamber manufacturer during the Second Interwar Period, lost when the city was destroyed — already-
established, national-scope background canon, treated factually here the same way it's treated everywhere
else in the project (real, but unmarked, never a main-quest driver), and explicitly distinct from the
present-day network (Sinheung, Byrd, the Mountain Pass outpost).

**Why Denison's attributes look like nothing else examined so far:** every prior wind-exposed city (Janbogo,
Troll, Belgrano) treats extreme wind as a serious hazard to survive around. Denison is categorically more
extreme than any of them — among the windiest permanent sea-level habitation sites on Earth, wind that's
functionally constant rather than episodic — and its founders responded not by minimizing exposure but by
building the entire city as a single continuous, load-sharing structure and making open pride in that
extremity the city's defining civic character, the explicit opposite of Tepenia's usual "competence without
commentary" ethos.

**Governing facts:** Cape Denison, Commonwealth Bay, George V Land — a rare exposed-bedrock outcrop on an
otherwise ice-buried coast; status is Destroyed; near-constant katabatic wind, average ~80 km/h with gusts
recorded well over 300 km/h, intensifying further in winter, essentially disrespecting normal seasonal
patterns; founders inherited no substantial modern station infrastructure, unlike Casey — only a legendary,
nearly-unsurvivable expedition hut and a reputation for extremity, and built a genuine city there anyway;
the city's most distinctive built feature is functioning as one continuous, aerodynamically coherent
structure rather than separate buildings, city-wide, more comprehensively interlinked than any other wind-
exposed Tepenian city; historically a fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturer.

## Extreme Wind Survival Infrastructure

1. **Near-continuous wind-monitoring infrastructure**, distinct from any episodic-wind city's own warning
   systems — Denison's wind doesn't fully respect season, so monitoring here is closer to constant
   surveillance than event-triggered alerting.
2. **Wind-window logistics infrastructure**, coordinating movement, construction, and outdoor activity
   around the gaps between wind events rather than any clean day/night or seasonal schedule.

## Interlinked City Structure Infrastructure (the defining, unique feature)

3. **The Interlinked City Structure** (established) — a comprehensive, city-wide physical joining of
   buildings into one continuous, load-sharing, aerodynamically coherent system, distinct from Janbogo's or
   Zukelli's own occasional covered connections.
4. **Landmark structures engineered for distant visibility through blowing snow**, giving Denison a
   deliberate, unified skyline legible even in severe weather — a design achievement no other wind-exposed
   Tepenian city has matched.

## Exposed Bedrock Foundation Infrastructure

5. **Structural foundation engineering on the rare exposed Cape Denison bedrock outcrop**, the specific
   geological feature that made any settlement here possible at all on an otherwise ice-buried coast.

## Historical Cradle Chamber Manufacturing Infrastructure (design-only, factual)

6. **Former fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturing infrastructure**, consistent with Denison's
   established historical role producing chambers for the nationwide Cradle network during the Second
   Interwar Period — lost with the city's destruction, distinct from the present-day network's own active
   sites.

## Pride-in-Extremity Cultural Infrastructure

7. **Wind-Sculpture Installation Grounds** (established) — dedicated space for wind-interactive sculpture
   and installation art, a recognized local specialty turning the city's defining hazard into a civic art
   form.
8. **Whatever physical or ritual infrastructure supports the established "reciting wind statistics loudly
   and proudly" social tradition** — a genuine architecture-vs-speech compensation, since the interlinked
   structure itself keeps the wind's severity largely invisible to residents day-to-day.

## Heritage & Founding Infrastructure

9. **The Original Expedition Hut Site** (established) — preserved as the city's founding historical
   landmark tied directly to the Mawson expedition legend, distinct from Cape Adare's own older
   Borchgrevink heritage site elsewhere in the subnet.

## Ruins-Specific Infrastructure

10. **Whatever survives of the interlinked structure's own load-sharing architecture post-destruction** —
    a genuinely distinct ruins-exploration environment, since damage to a continuously joined structure
    would propagate and concentrate differently than damage to a city of separate buildings.

## Cross-Referenced Extrapolation Findings (Methodology #2)

**What this is:** cross-referencing the 10 attributes above against Denison's own existing lore —
`Denison_Full_Extrapolation.md` and `Denison_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` (which already holds 3 Findings
of its own, written 2026-07-08). Proportionate, moderate depth per this subnet's own weighting — Janbogo and
Fort McMurdo carry the deepest treatment. Written 2026-07-30.

### Finding A: the chamber-manufacturing facility is the physical target behind Denison's proposed dual-purpose destruction

**Combining:** attribute #6 (former fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturing infrastructure) + the
existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 2 (Denison's destruction plausibly served two strategic
purposes at once — disrupting the highway chokepoint and eliminating robot-fabrication capacity).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #6 is the concrete physical facility behind the second half of Finding 2's
own dual-purpose theory — a real building distinct from the highway infrastructure itself.

**3rd-order effect:** reinforces Finding 2's own significant claim (Denison's loss touched Tepenia's robot-
population capacity, not just a highway link) with an actual, attribute-level location rather than leaving
the manufacturing target as an abstract economic fact.

### Finding B: attribute #8 is necessarily social infrastructure, not architectural, and that distinction is the whole point

**Combining:** attribute #8 (whatever supports the "reciting wind statistics" social tradition) + the
existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 3 (Denison's verbal pride, not its architecture, is what
actually survived the city's total destruction, carried forward by the diaspora).

**2nd-order effect:** unlike every other numbered attribute in this file, attribute #8 has to be understood
as oral/social practice — gathering customs, conversational habit — rather than a building, since Finding
3's own central insight is precisely that this tradition never depended on physical survival.

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming directly: attribute #8 is the one entry on this list that is, by its own
established nature, un-destroyable in the way the other nine attributes plainly were — a category
distinction the attributes list should carry forward if this file is ever used for level or ruins design.

### Finding C: the visible skyline and the invisible severity are two separate facts, and the landmarks only confirm the first

**Combining:** attributes #3–4 (the Interlinked City Structure, landmark structures for distant visibility)
+ the Full Extrapolation's Section I (the architecture is genuinely withdrawn and invisible from outside;
the verbal tradition exists specifically to compensate for that invisibility).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #4's own established visibility — a deliberate, unified skyline legible even
through blowing snow — clarifies exactly what Section I means by "invisible": the structure itself is
visible, but the wind-severity it survives is not announced by its own shape.

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming as a precise resolution to a potential confusion — Denison's landmarks say
"a city is here," not "and it withstands 300 km/h winds," which is exactly the gap Section I's own verbal
tradition exists to fill.

### Finding D: attribute #10's own framing structurally confirms why Denison had no "worth more alive" quality

**Combining:** attribute #10 (whatever survives of the interlinked structure's load-sharing architecture
post-destruction) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 1 (strategic value alone doesn't
predict destruction versus survival across this subnet — Denison had no comparable "worth more alive"
quality the way Janbogo's living-witness role did).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #10 already assumes total destruction as its starting premise, asking only
what remains — a structural confirmation of Finding 1's own resolved comparative question, since a city with
Janbogo's kind of surviving value wouldn't need an attribute framed around post-destruction remains at all.

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming as a subtle but real piece of internal consistency — the very shape of
this attribute (what's left, not what's still working) is itself evidence for Finding 1's own explanation of
why Denison, unlike Janbogo, didn't survive.

### Finding E: the wind-monitoring infrastructure plausibly had relevance beyond Denison's own city limits

**Combining:** attributes #1–2 (near-continuous wind-monitoring infrastructure, wind-window logistics) + the
Full Extrapolation's Section II (Denison sits genuinely close to Dumont d'Urville, roughly 110km, a close and
frequent relationship distinct from its much more occasional contact with Cape Adare).

**2nd-order effect:** given Dumont d'Urville sits in the same broadly extreme Adélie Land katabatic regime
(per this session's own Dumont d'Urville attributes), Denison's own wind-monitoring data plausibly had
practical relevance for its close neighbor as well, not just for Denison itself.

**3rd-order effect:** worth flagging as a plausible data-sharing relationship between the two closely
positioned cities — the same kind of cross-city infrastructure link established elsewhere in this session
(Mirny's wind-engineering export to Sinheung, Abowasa's bedrock expertise for Halley and Neumayer).
