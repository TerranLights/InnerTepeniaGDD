## I. Pink Lucy's Pre-War Role and Departure

**Proposed pre-war role:** given Dumont d'Urville's established "New Orleans at 1/20th scale" downtown culture — live music in almost every eatery — Pink Lucy plausibly worked within that exact scene, at one of the small, packed, informal venues that define the city's whole downtown identity. This ties her personally to the specific cultural mechanism the city's civic identity centers on, rather than leaving her occupation generic.

**Proposed reason for departure:** the established record already states she left "a damaged but still-standing city, not a dead one" — meaning her departure happened *after* the war damage, not before it. The open question is why she left a city that, however damaged, was still functioning and still inhabited by most of its population. **Proposed:** her own specific venue or neighborhood — not downtown's still-thriving core, but one of the damaged, partially-abandoned sections — was among the parts of the city genuinely lost. This gives her a personal-scale loss distinct from the city's aggregate survival: Dumont d'Urville as a whole endured, but the specific place and scene she belonged to didn't, which is a more precise and more painful reason to leave than simply "the city was damaged."

**On her route:** **resolved 2026-07-12 — Dumont d'Urville → Janbogo** *(this section previously framed the route as "still explicitly undecided" and leaned geographically toward Casey; corrected 2026-07-13, a week after the actual resolution)*. The route was ultimately decided on personality-match grounds, not geographic directness — Janbogo's own hospitality-and-synthesis civic identity closely mirrors her established character read, a stronger fit than Casey's comparatively muted civic character, and Casey's own proposed early, deliberate destruction would have compressed her time there uncomfortably. This section's own geographic observation (Hwy 2 gives a more direct route to Casey; reaching Janbogo via Hwy 183 requires additional travel south through Denison and Concordia) remains accurate as a travel-logistics fact — it just wasn't the deciding factor. See `Worldspace/Characters/Dolls/Still-Present_-_In-Game/recruitable/TBN [FW-25 Pink Lucy]/README.md`.

**On her name's derivation:** left entirely untouched here, per the existing note that "user has leads" already in progress — not an open question this document should speculate on.

## II. The City's Damage Timeline

**Proposed:** Dumont d'Urville was hit relatively early within the Long Night War's single confirmed year (2812), consistent with its established status as a genuine two-highway junction (Hwy 2 and Hwy 183 meeting directly here) — a transportation and coordination node of exactly the kind a hostile force would have strategic reason to strike early, to disrupt Tepenian subnet coordination before it could fully organize a response. This gives the damage a concrete strategic logic (a junction worth hitting early) rather than leaving it as an arbitrary, undated event within the war's single year.

## IV. St. Jules Day's Observance

**Proposed, drawing on the Tucumán research:** the day's central practice is a public reading — held downtown, in the open, regardless of wind conditions — of surviving fragments of Jules Dumont d'Urville's own 1840 expedition journal. The specific point of holding it outdoors, in the wind, rather than in a sheltered space, is that the observance is partly about honoring the conditions the original landing itself was made under — a small, deliberate act of standing in the same wind St. Jules once did, rather than a purely commemorative ceremony held in comfort.

## VI. Demonym: d'Urvillois

Following standard French demonym conventions (the "-ois" suffix, as in "Québécois") rather than an anglicized form — **d'Urvillois** (masculine/plural), **d'Urvilloise** (feminine singular).
