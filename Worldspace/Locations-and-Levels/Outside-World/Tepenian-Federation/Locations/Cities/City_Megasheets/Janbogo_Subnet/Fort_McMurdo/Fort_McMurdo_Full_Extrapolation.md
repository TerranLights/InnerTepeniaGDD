# Fort McMurdo — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Fort McMurdo given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Fort_McMurdo_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-08.

---

## I. Demonym: McMurdans

**Proposed**, dropping the "Fort" prefix for the demonym itself, following the same pattern real-world place names use when the full name is unwieldy as an adjective (a New Yorker, not a "New York-er"). The Specs file's own working placeholder ("Forts McMurdans") already gestures toward this; simplifying to McMurdans keeps the name clean.

---

## II. Industrial Output: Processing Here, Raw Extraction Across the Sound

**Proposed, directly answering the question surfaced in the Mega-Init:** Fort McMurdo itself functioned primarily as a processing and coordination hub — fuel processing, equipment manufacturing and repair, construction-materials production — while the actual raw extraction happened across McMurdo Sound in the Dry Valleys, whose exposed bedrock and sediment (genuinely unlike ice-covered land anywhere else on the continent) offered mineral resources unavailable on Ross Island itself. Ross Island's own volcanic basalt supplied construction aggregate closer to home. This mirrors Calgary's own real division of labor — a headquarters city distinct from the extraction site — giving Fort McMurdo's economy a specific, earned shape rather than "mining happens here" as an undifferentiated blur.

## IV. What "Partially Operational" Means Today

**Proposed:** a functioning core of survivors concentrated in the most structurally intact section of the city — plausibly near the original harbor and pier infrastructure, since heavy port and fuel-storage structures built to withstand geological and volcanic stress would plausibly prove more blast-resistant than lighter residential construction added later. This surviving core continues basic logistics and processing work at a greatly reduced scale, still functionally relevant to the wider Janbogo subnet even in its diminished state — a city-that-was, with real, ongoing work still happening inside it.

---

## V. Cape Evans and Cape Royds: Intact, and a Deliberate Contrast

**Proposed:** both expedition huts survived the Long Night War largely undamaged, protected by their physical distance from the main industrial target and by their own established multi-century preservation in Antarctic cold. In DLC 6, they function as a deliberate atmospheric contrast — genuinely intact heritage sites sitting a few kilometers from an enormous industrial ruin, the Heroic Age of Antarctic exploration preserved almost perfectly beside its industrial-age descendant's destruction.

---

## VI. Erebus's Activity Level: Baseline, Not Heightened

**Proposed:** ongoing but stable — the lava lake's persistent, low-key activity continuing much as it always has, not a heightened eruptive phase. This is consistent with the real Erebus's genuine, well-documented multi-century pattern of stable, non-catastrophic activity, and it keeps the volcano as atmospheric presence and environmental backdrop rather than competing with the war itself as the city's central threat.

---

## VII. The Dry Valleys Operation: Still Running, Lighter and More Resilient

**Proposed:** yes, still ongoing in reduced capacity — since the Dry Valleys extraction was never fully dependent on Fort McMurdo's own damaged infrastructure (accessed via the seasonal sea ice road or by sea, a genuinely separate logistics chain), it could plausibly continue with lighter, more resilient operations even as the main city absorbed the worst of the war's damage.

---

## VIII. Which Sections Survived: The Original Core, Not the Expansion

**Proposed:** the original McMurdo Station-era core — piers, fuel depots, vehicle infrastructure, built to the older, heavier American Antarctic Program standard — survived in better condition than the later Tepenian-era residential expansion built to accommodate a growing civilian population. The oldest, most overbuilt parts of the city outlasted the newer, more lightly built additions around them.

---

## IX. When Fort McMurdo's Capital Status Became Official: After the Amundsen Tower

**Proposed:** the formal designation came in the aftermath of the Amundsen Tower's construction (~2688), not before it. Building the Tower — a national infrastructure project on a scale nothing else in Tepenian history matched — would have made the nation's dependency on Fort McMurdo's industrial output completely undeniable, at exactly the moment its own established "McMurdo Problem" faction was already naming real resentment about the city wielding that much influence with no formal accountability attached to it. Formalizing Fort McMurdo's status wasn't really a change in power at all — it was the government finally attaching legitimacy and accountability to power the city already, unmistakably, held. Making the arrangement official was the response to the resentment, not a cause of it.

---

## Worth Your Attention

Section IX is the most significant proposal here — it gives the newly-clarified de facto-then-official capital timeline an actual earned cause (the Amundsen Tower's construction making Fort McMurdo's dependency undeniable, right as the "McMurdo Problem" faction's resentment was already naming the same fact) rather than leaving the transition as an unexplained date. Section II is the second most load-bearing, since it gives Fort McMurdo's previously undifferentiated "mining and extraction" economy a specific division of labor drawn directly from Calgary's own real precedent. Section VIII is worth flagging directly against Port Lockroy's own Megasheet: both cities show the identical pattern of an older, hardier core outlasting later, lighter-built additions — worth checking in the Cross-Reference Synthesis whether this is coincidence or a genuine cross-city pattern.
