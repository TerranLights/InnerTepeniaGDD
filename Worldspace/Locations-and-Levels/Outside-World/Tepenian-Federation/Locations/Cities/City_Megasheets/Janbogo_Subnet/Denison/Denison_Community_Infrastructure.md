# Denison — Community Infrastructure & Social Life

**What this is:** concrete physical spaces, small institutions, and recurring social rituals that give Denison's already-established culture a tangible, ground-level presence — the same pass done for each of Concordia's 13 districts, all 16 Mirny and Halley subnet cities, Janbogo, and Cape Adare, extended here. **Denison's status is Destroyed** — everything below is framed as pre-war, now among the ruins. Drawn from `Denison_Mega_Init.md`, `Denison_Full_Extrapolation.md`, `Denison_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md`, and `Local_Cultures/Janbogo_Subnet/Denison.md`. Written 2026-07-30.

---

## Additions (pre-war, now ruins)

- **The Original Expedition Hut Site** — preserved as the city's founding historical landmark, tied to the 1911–1914 expedition legend.
- **The Exposed Bedrock Outcrop** — the rare rock exposure that made physical settlement possible at all, a literal and symbolic anchor.
- **The Interlinked City Structure** — the city's own continuous, aerodynamically coherent built form, its most distinctive landmark.
- **The Fabrication-Chamber Works** — the historical site where Denison manufactured Cradle chambers before its destruction, a lost piece of Tepenian infrastructure history.
- **Wind-Sculpture Installation Grounds** — where wind-interactive sculpture and installation art, a recognized local specialty, was made and displayed.

## Small offices for educational training (pre-war)

- wind-engineering and structural craft (fully-interlinked architectural design)
- fabrication-chamber manufacturing, the historical Cradle-infrastructure role
- survival-narrative and extremity literature, the Mawson-legacy literary tradition

## Social cohesion mechanisms (pre-war)

- reciting wind statistics loudly and proudly — the verbal tradition compensating for the architecture's own invisible severity.
- wind-rated gear worn elsewhere in Tepenia as a recognized "quiet flex."
- communal meals doubling as informal wind-safety briefings.
- percussive, rhythmic music meant to be heard "over" the wind rather than in silence.
