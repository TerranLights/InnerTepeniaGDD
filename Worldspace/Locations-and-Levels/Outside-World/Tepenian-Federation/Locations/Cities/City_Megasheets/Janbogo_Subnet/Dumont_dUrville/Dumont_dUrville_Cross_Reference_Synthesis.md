## Finding 1: Dumont d'Urville's French identity persists because it's actively used, not passively preserved

**Combining:** Quebec City's real-world precedent of a minority founding culture surviving centuries through genuine, functional daily use rather than ceremonial preservation + the established fact that "even residents with no French ancestry tend to pick up functional French simply through years of civic life conducted in it."

**2nd-order effect:** Dumont d'Urville's French persistence isn't a fragile, ceremonial holdover kept alive mainly through names and holidays — it's a self-sustaining, functional system, since ordinary civic participation itself is what transmits the language to each new generation of arrivals, regardless of their own ancestry.

**3rd-order effect:** this makes the city's founding tension (a demographically thin founding nation whose civic template nonetheless persists) structurally more durable than a city where the founding language survived only ceremonially — new arrivals here become carriers of the founding culture through ordinary daily life, not through any deliberate act of cultural preservation.

**4th-order effect:** this distinction — functional daily transmission versus ceremonial-only preservation — is a real, checkable difference worth applying to other Tepenian cities carrying a similar founding-nation-demographic-minority pattern (Fort McMurdo, Janbogo, Zukelli among them), to determine which of those cities' founding cultures rest on the same durable mechanism Dumont d'Urville's does, and which are closer to ceremonial preservation that could plausibly erode over further generations.

## Finding 2: the downtown music culture may be load-bearing to the city's actual post-war survival, not just a surviving amenity

**Combining:** San Miguel de Allende's real precedent of an art colony rescuing a town headed toward ghost-town status + Dumont d'Urville's own established damaged post-war condition (some sections abandoned, penguins colonizing the empty spaces) + the already-established "New Orleans at 1/20th scale" downtown eatery-and-music culture.

**2nd-order effect:** the downtown music culture and the city's post-war survival may not be two separate, coincidentally-surviving facts — the culture plausibly functions as a genuine, load-bearing reason people stayed rather than evacuated a place this physically demanding even before the war added damage on top of it.

**3rd-order effect:** if that's true, the actual fragile point in Dumont d'Urville's post-war existence isn't simply its total population figure — it's specifically the downtown core where that culture lives. The already-damaged outlying sections have already been absorbed into the city's ongoing existence without threatening its survival; a comparable level of damage to downtown itself would threaten something the outlying damage doesn't.

**4th-order effect:** this reframes what "Dumont d'Urville's survival" actually depends on for any future story content — not the city's overall footprint or population count, but specifically whether its small, dense downtown core remains intact enough to keep sustaining the cultural life that appears to be holding the whole population's will to stay together in the first place.

## Finding 3: Dumont d'Urville's whole significance rests on modest, disconnected, unglamorous facts rather than any single claim to importance

**Combining:** San Miguel de Tucumán's real precedent of a city chosen for practical geographic position rather than ambition, carrying its entire national significance from one specific historical date + Dumont d'Urville's own founding logic (Petrel Island chosen because its rock was buildable, not because of any expectation of significance) + St. Jules Day's established civic centrality, itself anchored to one specific 1840 date.

**2nd-order effect:** Dumont d'Urville's importance comes from two entirely separate, unglamorous sources that have nothing to do with each other — a practical, accidental reason for its physical location, and a specific, commemorative reason for its civic identity — neither of which involves any claim to scale, strategic vitality, or ambition.

**3rd-order effect:** combined with Finding 1's durable-but-quiet linguistic transmission and Finding 2's unglamorous, load-bearing music culture, a consistent pattern emerges across all three findings: Dumont d'Urville matters through accumulated, modest, practical persistence, never through any single grand or dramatic claim to significance.

**4th-order effect:** this "modest but durable" quality is worth naming as a real throughline distinct from, though fully compatible with, the city's already-established "Negotiated Ground" identity — Negotiated Ground describes *how* the city relates to its hostile environment; this pattern describes *why* the city itself continues to matter at all, despite never having anything dramatic to point to as the reason.

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across All Three Findings

All three findings converge on the same underlying observation: everything genuinely significant about Dumont d'Urville is quiet, functional, and accumulated rather than dramatic or singular. Its language survives through ordinary use, not ceremony. Its culture may be quietly load-bearing to its own survival, not merely decorative. Its whole reason for existing traces to two unrelated, unglamorous facts rather than one grand founding claim. This is a city whose importance is easy to overlook precisely because nothing about it was ever built to be impressive at a glance.

If the downtown music culture really is what's keeping the post-war population together, then downtown itself, not the city's raw population count, is the actual stake in any future conflict or crisis set here — a specific, checkable claim worth testing against whatever DLC 6 content eventually gets built around this city.
