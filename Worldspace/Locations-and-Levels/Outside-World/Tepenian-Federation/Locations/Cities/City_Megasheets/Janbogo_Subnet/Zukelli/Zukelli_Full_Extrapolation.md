# Zukelli — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Zukelli given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Zukelli_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-07.

---

## I. Demonym: Zukellesi

Following the standard Italian city-demonym pattern (Milanese, Napoletani, Torinese) rather than a generic "-ian" suffix — **Zukellesi** (singular and collective plural), phonetically descended the same way "Zukelli" itself descended from "Zucchelli." Used both formally and, per the Cape Adare-style precedent of exported "type of person" descriptions elsewhere in the project, informally across Tepenia to describe anyone carrying Zukelli's specific hospitality-and-warmth civic character, whether or not they were ever a resident.

---

## II. Navigation in a City With No Master Plan

Adopting the Valparaíso research directly: Zukelli's residents needed *some* practical way to navigate a genuinely unplanned, organically-grown street layout, and "you just had to know somebody who lived there" can't have been the *entire* answer for a functioning city of nearly a million people. **Proposed system:** the covered connections and heating-pipe conduits that already thread between Zukelli's buildings (established architecture, built to shelter movement from the katabatic winds) were color- and pattern-marked by neighborhood — not a formal municipal signage system, but an organic, bottom-up convention that emerged the same way the city's streets did, each neighborhood's own residents settling on a scheme over generations without any central authority mandating it. A local reads these markings fluently, the way a resident of any real city reads subtle cues invisible to a tourist; a visitor sees only inconsistent decoration. This gives concrete, livable shape to "you needed to know somebody" without undermining it — knowing somebody was still how you *learned* the code in the first place.

## III. What Janbogo Knew, and When

**Resolved: no advance warning.** The strike that destroyed Zukelli was, per its own established logic, calibrated for absolute certainty of the kill — any warning reaching Janbogo in time to act would have created exactly the kind of gap (an opportunity for the defectors to be moved or extracted) the strike was designed to close entirely. This means Janbogo's survivors did not fail to help in a moment of moral crisis; they had no moment at all. What they had, instead, was real-time witness — watching it happen from across the water with no warning and no ability to intervene, which is its own distinct and arguably worse trauma than a failure to act would have been. This also keeps Janbogo's survival cleanly tied to its intended role (a spared witness carrying a deterrent message forward) rather than complicating it with any question of after-the-fact culpability for not doing more.

## IV. Janbogo's Relationship to the Ruins, Now

**Proposed: both, and it's a genuine, ongoing point of quiet friction within Janbogo itself.** A real but informal minority of Janbogo residents keep something like a vigil — an unofficial, unscheduled practice of crossing to the waterfront at specific personal anniversaries (the day the strike happened, a birthday shared with someone lost, no single fixed civic date) to simply look across the water. This descends directly from the pre-war "Crossing" tradition already established between the two cities (informal visits blurring who was hosting whom), inverted into something that can no longer be reciprocated. A larger portion of Janbogo has, by contrast, made a quiet, private peace with not looking — not out of callousness, but because a city can't function with its whole population in permanent mourning, and ordinary daily life requires not staring at the wound every day. Neither group considers the other wrong, exactly, but there's real, mostly-unspoken tension between them: the vigil-keepers sometimes read the majority's non-looking as a kind of forgetting, and the majority sometimes reads the vigil-keepers' practice as a refusal to let the city move forward. This tension itself, not a single settled civic answer, is the actual living legacy.

---

## V. The Zukelli Diaspora Within Janbogo

**Proposed:** a modest but real population — plausibly in the low tens of thousands, drawn from residents who had reason to be in Janbogo already (family ties, trade, the established cross-city visiting tradition) at the moment the strike occurred, rather than a large-scale evacuation, since the strike's own design (total, sudden, no-warning obliteration) left essentially no window for a deliberate mass exodus. This community sits inside Janbogo's own civic fabric rather than forming a walled-off refugee quarter — consistent with the two cities' already-established closeness — but is recognizable as its own thread by the specific, unusually direct trauma its members carry: not losing a city by report or rumor, the way most of Tepenia's refugee communities did, but having personally watched it happen from a window.

## VI. Favi della Torre — Left Genuinely Open

Unlike every other item above, this one isn't resolved here. There isn't enough established information about Favi della Torre available to this pass to propose an earned connection rather than an arbitrary one — forcing a link between an established Upper Earth character and Zukelli's own Italian civic identity without knowing Favi's own actual story risks contradicting whatever's already true about them. This is flagged as still genuinely open, not quietly decided by default.

## Worth Your Attention

The most load-bearing proposal here is Section III — no advance warning. It's a small, quiet resolution, but it changes the entire emotional shape of Janbogo's survivor guilt: not "we could have done more and didn't," but "there was never a moment where doing more was possible." Section IV's split-community tension over the ruins is the second thing worth carrying forward — it turns "how does Janbogo feel about Zukelli" from a single settled answer into an ongoing, unresolved civic disagreement, which is far more usable for actual character and quest writing than a unified communal position would be.
