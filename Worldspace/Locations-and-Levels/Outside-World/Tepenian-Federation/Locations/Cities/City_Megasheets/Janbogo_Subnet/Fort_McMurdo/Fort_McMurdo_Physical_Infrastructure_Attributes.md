# Fort McMurdo — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Fort McMurdo would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Fort_McMurdo.md`, `Local_Cultures/Janbogo_Subnet/Fort_McMurdo.md`, and
`Fort_McMurdo_Community_Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred
until this pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30.

**Why Fort McMurdo's attributes look like nothing else examined so far:** it's Tepenia's historical national
capital and its largest city by physical/industrial footprint, yet its raw population is capped well below
its industrial-scale peers by Ross Island's limited land area — an "island cap" city whose civic weight has
nothing to do with headcount. It's also the only city examined so far living in the literal presence of an
active volcano with a permanent lava lake, and the only one whose defining civic identity is operational
gravity rather than any single environmental hazard, cultural tradition, or strategic chokepoint.

**Governing facts:** southern tip of Ross Island, in view of Mount Erebus — the southernmost active volcano
on Earth, permanent summit lava lake, a constant atmospheric and psychological presence; status is damaged
but partially operational, its sheer physical scale making complete destruction difficult; historical
national capital (informal-then-formalized, title did not survive the war and has no present-day
successor); reached via a spur road off Hwy 183 across McMurdo Sound rather than a direct main-line stop;
McMurdo Sound freezes solid enough for a seasonal sea-ice road to the Dry Valleys extraction camps in
winter, closing in summer when maritime access opens instead — the same dual-mode seasonal-crossing
mechanic flagged as unresolved for Dumont d'Urville's own Channel Crossing; one of the longest polar nights
of any inhabited Tepenian city (~118 days); pre-exile heritage huts at Cape Evans and Cape Royds sit
preserved nearby, deliberately separate from the working city.

---

## Operational Gravity / Historical Capital Infrastructure

1. **The Comms Coordination Stations** (established) — the staffed, physical infrastructure behind Fort
   McMurdo's own "de facto capital" status, formalizing supply, extraction scheduling, and inter-city
   logistics; the concrete mechanism, not merely a symbolic title, that once made the city indispensable.
2. **Whatever surviving administrative infrastructure remains from Fort McMurdo's formal capital era**,
   distinct from its ongoing operational-logistics role — a historical seat-of-government layer now
   defunct, since the title itself did not survive the war.

## Mount Erebus Infrastructure

3. **The Erebus Monitoring Station** (established) — the long-running volcanology program tracking the
   summit's baseline lava-lake activity, a genuinely unique hazard-monitoring discipline no other Tepenian
   city needs.
4. **Volcanic-gas and ash-fall mitigation infrastructure**, given the established fact that Erebus
   generates its own localized weather (sulfur smell, ash falls during heightened activity) as an ordinary
   atmospheric fact of city life.

## Industrial & Extraction Infrastructure

5. **The Harbor & Pier Core** (established) — the original, structurally intact McMurdo Station-era
   infrastructure, now home to the surviving functioning population; Tepenia's single largest inherited
   industrial footprint.
6. **Fuel processing, equipment manufacturing, and repair infrastructure** (established) — the industrial
   backbone that made Fort McMurdo the city other cities needed to function, including Concordia's own dome
   and corridor construction materials.

## McMurdo Sound Seasonal Crossing Infrastructure

7. **The McMurdo Sound sea ice road** (established) — the seasonal overland crossing to the Dry Valleys and
   mainland, usable only during the solid-freeze period; a highway that exists only in winter, with the
   summer-transition logistics explicitly flagged as unresolved, matching Dumont d'Urville's own Channel
   Crossing flag.
8. **The Dry Valleys Extraction Camps** (established) — lighter, more resilient raw-extraction operations
   across the Sound, still running post-war, distinct from Fort McMurdo's own heavier in-city processing
   infrastructure.

## Pre-Exile Heritage Infrastructure

9. **Cape Evans and Cape Royds** (established) — the preserved Scott and Shackleton expedition huts,
   deliberately kept separate from the working city as a heritage contrast; likely the most intact pre-exile
   structures in the Janbogo subnet.

## Island-Cap & Long Polar Night Infrastructure

10. **Population infrastructure scaled to Ross Island's own limited land area**, distinct from any
    mainland city's own growth pattern — Fort McMurdo does far more industrial work per resident than its
    modest population ranking would suggest.
11. **Extended artificial-light and power infrastructure calibrated to a ~118-day polar night**, among the
    longest of any coastal Tepenian city — roughly six weeks longer than Janbogo's own, a materially
    different sustained-darkness engineering demand.

## War-Damage & Ruins-Scale Infrastructure

12. **Damage-assessment and habitability infrastructure scaled to an enormous, only-partially-collapsed
    industrial footprint**, consistent with the established "pockets of life amid the wreckage" framing —
    a genuinely different ruins-management problem than any smaller damaged city examined so far.

## Cross-Referenced Extrapolation Findings (Methodology #2)

**What this is:** cross-referencing the 12 attributes above against Fort McMurdo's own existing lore —
`Fort_McMurdo_Full_Extrapolation.md` and `Fort_McMurdo_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` (which already holds 3
Findings of its own, written 2026-07-08). Fort McMurdo is one of this subnet's two deep-treatment cities per
direct developer instruction, alongside Janbogo. This pass runs at Mawson's own depth. Written 2026-07-30.

### Finding A: the Harbor & Pier Core is the physical building behind a carefully-distinguished damage pattern

**Combining:** attributes #5–6 (the Harbor & Pier Core, fuel processing/manufacturing infrastructure) + the
existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 2 (Fort McMurdo and Port Lockroy share an identical "old core
survives" damage pattern for two entirely different reasons — Fort McMurdo's core survived through heavy
industrial engineering, not wartime hardening the way Port Lockroy's did).

**2nd-order effect:** attributes #5–6 are the concrete facilities behind Finding 2's own careful distinction
— the Harbor & Pier Core is specifically heavy industrial infrastructure, not military-hardened
construction, confirming exactly the reading Finding 2 argues for.

**3rd-order effect:** reinforces Finding 2's own explicit warning against treating "old core survived" as
interchangeable shorthand across cities — this attribute confirms Fort McMurdo's ruins should read as heavy
industrial engineering, a genuinely different visual and material language from Port Lockroy's own
wartime-hardened core.

### Finding B: the Comms Coordination Stations are where Finding 3's "invisible power" was actually exercised

**Combining:** attribute #1 (the Comms Coordination Stations) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's
Finding 3 (Fort McMurdo's "presence, not population" identity — a hard-to-quantify kind of power — is
exactly what made its informal status eventually intolerable, forcing formal capital designation).

**2nd-order effect:** the Coordination Stations are the concrete, physical location where that historically
invisible influence was actually exercised — supply, extraction scheduling, and inter-city logistics
coordinated from one real place, not an abstract atmosphere of importance.

**3rd-order effect:** the fact that this operational infrastructure eventually needed formal capital-status
legitimacy attached to it, rather than remaining purely functional indefinitely, is itself the physical
expression of Finding 3's own political argument — power exercised from a specific building, for
generations, without formal accountability, until the accountability had to catch up.

### Finding C: the Dry Valleys Extraction Camps independently confirm an already-resolved question

**Combining:** attribute #8 (the Dry Valleys Extraction Camps, still running post-war) + the Full
Extrapolation's Section VII (the Dry Valleys operation continuing in reduced capacity, since it was never
fully dependent on Fort McMurdo's own damaged infrastructure).

**2nd-order effect:** this Methodology #1 attribute, derived independently from first principles, arrives at
the identical conclusion Section VII already reached through direct cross-referencing.

**3rd-order effect:** worth noting as the same kind of independent convergence found elsewhere in this
subnet's Methodology #2 pass (see Janbogo's own Finding C) — two separate analytical passes landing on the
same fact without cross-referencing each other directly.

### Finding D: Cape Evans and Cape Royds create the starkest heritage-versus-ruin contrast examined in this pass

**Combining:** attribute #9 (Cape Evans and Cape Royds, likely the most intact pre-exile structures in the
subnet) + the Full Extrapolation's Section V (both huts survived largely undamaged, functioning as a
deliberate atmospheric contrast to the industrial ruin around them).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #9 confirms Section V's own resolved survival status directly — two of the
best-preserved heritage sites in the entire Janbogo subnet sit only a few kilometers from one of its most
devastated industrial ruins.

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming as a genuinely stark environmental-storytelling juxtaposition — the
Heroic Age of Antarctic exploration preserved almost perfectly beside its industrial-age descendant's
destruction, a sharper physical contrast than any other city examined in this pass offers.

### Finding E: the Erebus Monitoring Station is the evidentiary source behind Section VI's own resolved answer

**Combining:** attributes #3–4 (the Erebus Monitoring Station, volcanic-gas and ash-fall mitigation
infrastructure) + the Full Extrapolation's Section VI (Erebus's activity level confirmed as baseline, not
heightened, ongoing but stable).

**2nd-order effect:** the Monitoring Station's own long-running data record is specifically what confirms
"baseline, not heightened" as an actual observed fact, not an assumed default.

**3rd-order effect:** gives Section VI's own resolved civic-temperament question (the city living calmly
beside an active volcano) a concrete facility that would have generated the evidence behind it.

### Finding F: the sea ice road's shoulder-season gap remains unresolved and directly relevant to Dumont d'Urville's own upcoming pass

**Combining:** attribute #7 (the McMurdo Sound sea ice road, summer-transition logistics explicitly flagged
as unresolved) + Dumont d'Urville's own identical Channel Crossing flag, established during this session's
Methodology #1 pass.

**2nd-order effect:** this shared, currently unresolved dual-mode-crossing mechanic touches two cities in the
same subnet, one already processed here and one still pending in this same Methodology #2 pass.

**3rd-order effect:** worth flagging directly ahead of Dumont d'Urville's own upcoming Findings — whenever
this mechanic is actually resolved, it should be resolved once for both cities together, not independently
re-derived when Dumont d'Urville's own material gets cross-referenced next.

### Finding G: the island-cap population ceiling and the "presence, not population" identity reinforce each other unusually cleanly

**Combining:** attributes #10–11 (population infrastructure scaled to Ross Island's own limited land area,
extended power/light infrastructure for the 118-day polar night) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's
Finding 3 (Fort McMurdo's real significance was never measured by population at all).

**2nd-order effect:** these two independently-derived facts — a hard geographic ceiling on population, and a
civic identity whose importance was never counted in headcount — turn out to reinforce each other unusually
well.

**3rd-order effect:** a city whose importance had depended on population size would have been structurally
capped forever by Ross Island's own limited land area; Fort McMurdo's actual civic-gravity metric
(operational indispensability) is specifically the one kind of significance that geography couldn't cap.
