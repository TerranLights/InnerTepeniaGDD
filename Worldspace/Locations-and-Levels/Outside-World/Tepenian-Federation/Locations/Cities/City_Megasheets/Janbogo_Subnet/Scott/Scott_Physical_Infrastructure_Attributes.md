# Scott — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Scott would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Scott.md`, `Local_Cultures/Janbogo_Subnet/Scott.md`, and `Scott_
Community_Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred until this
pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30.

**Why Scott's attributes look like nothing else examined so far:** it shares Fort McMurdo's entire
geography — same island, same volcano, same climate, same seasonal ice road — but its own civic identity
is defined by scale and pace rather than environment: small, precise, overwhelmingly residential, the
direct experiential counterpart to its neighbor's constant operational churn. It's also the only city
examined so far whose founding Saint veneration (St. Robert) is the single most personally load-bearing
piece of its entire civic identity, and the only one whose physical infrastructure is genuinely,
functionally merged with a separate, distinct neighboring city.

**Governing facts:** Hut Point Peninsula, Ross Island — approximately 3km from Fort McMurdo on the same
volcanic promontory, sharing its climate, Mount Erebus visibility, McMurdo Sound sea ice dynamics, and spur
road connection to Hwy 183; status is damaged but partially operational, its war experience likely
intertwined with Fort McMurdo's given physical proximity and shared infrastructure; overwhelmingly
residential in physical footprint, with a modest handful of public gathering spaces — a genuinely quiet
place to live, not a working city; home to Hut Point itself, Robert Falcon Scott's 1902 discovery hut and
Tepenia's most personally significant Saint-veneration site; one small confirmed industry — collecting
volcanic material fallen from Erebus and forwarding it to Fort McMurdo's trucking network — alongside a
technical/research base, neither of which defines the city's own quiet daily character.

## Twin-City Shared Infrastructure

1. **The Scott–Fort McMurdo Border** (established) — a formally maintained but practically porous line
   between the two cities, observed by both sides even where functionally arbitrary.
2. **Shared Emergency Infrastructure** (established) — physical systems tying Scott and Fort McMurdo
   together, merged out of wartime necessity and still active post-war, distinct from either city's own
   independent civic systems.

## Hut Point / St. Robert Heritage Infrastructure

3. **Hut Point** (established) — Scott's most sacred civic location, the 1902 discovery hut itself, among
   the oldest surviving human-made structures in Antarctica; not a museum but a living remembrance site.
4. **Whatever physical infrastructure supports the established Tepenian Independence Day Hut Point
   Remembrance**, a shared civic ritual belonging to both Scott and Fort McMurdo at once — candles, flowers,
   and personal tokens laid at the site by residents of both cities.

## Residential-Dominant Civic Infrastructure

5. **Housing infrastructure dominating the city's own physical footprint**, distinct from Fort McMurdo's
   own industrial-dominant land use — the direct experiential counterpart to its neighbor's constant
   operational churn.
6. **A modest handful of public gathering spaces (restaurants, general leisure)**, consistent with the
   established "genuinely decent, quiet place to raise a family" civic character.

## Precision Calibration & Monitoring Infrastructure

7. **The Calibration & Monitoring Labs** (established) — precision equipment calibration and
   environmental/geological monitoring for the whole Hut Point Peninsula, including a genuine share of Fort
   McMurdo's own Erebus program.

## Small-Scale Volcanic Material Industry

8. **Volcanic-material collection infrastructure**, gathering fallen Erebus material and forwarding it to
   Fort McMurdo's own trucking network for onward processing — a small, contained operation consistent with
   Scott's established civic identity, real economic activity that doesn't define the city's own daily
   texture.

## War-Damage Infrastructure

9. **Damage-assessment and repair infrastructure calibrated to Scott's own smaller physical scale**,
   distinct from Fort McMurdo's own enormous, only-partially-collapsed industrial footprint — genuinely
   likely intertwined with Fort McMurdo's own war experience given the two cities' physical proximity and
   shared systems.

## Cross-Referenced Extrapolation Findings (Methodology #2)

**What this is:** cross-referencing the 9 attributes above against Scott's own existing lore —
`Scott_Full_Extrapolation.md` and `Scott_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` (which already holds 3 Findings of
its own, written 2026-07-08). Proportionate, moderate depth per this subnet's own weighting — Janbogo and
Fort McMurdo carry the deepest treatment. Written 2026-07-30.

### Finding A: the Calibration & Monitoring Labs are Scott's own physical half of the shared Erebus program

**Combining:** attribute #7 (the Calibration & Monitoring Labs) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's
Finding 2 (Erebus monitoring as the one concrete, functioning example of Scott/Fort McMurdo cooperation,
split by specialization — Scott handling precision instrumentation, Fort McMurdo handling heavier geological
monitoring).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #7 is the concrete building behind Scott's own half of that already-
established shared program, complementing Fort McMurdo's own Erebus Monitoring Station attribute derived
independently in that city's own file.

**3rd-order effect:** confirms Finding 2's own claim with two independently-derived, attribute-level
facilities on each side of the same collaboration — a genuinely earned, specific answer to what Scott/Fort
McMurdo cooperation actually looks like in practice, now anchored on both ends.

### Finding B: Hut Point's physical infrastructure is unchanged, but what happens there now carries different weight

**Combining:** attributes #3–4 (Hut Point itself, infrastructure supporting the Independence Day
Remembrance) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 3 (the shared tradition likely absorbed a
second, unspoken layer of meaning after the war — recent grief and mutual dependence underneath the older,
more distant historical veneration).

**2nd-order effect:** the physical site hasn't changed, but per Finding 3, what the observance actually means
to the people performing it has — the same attribute-level infrastructure now supports two genuinely
different emotional registers depending on whether it's read pre-war or post-war.

**3rd-order effect:** worth flagging directly for any scene set at Hut Point — the ritual a player might
witness there isn't simply an unchanged, preserved tradition, but one carrying real, recent weight the older
veneration alone never had.

### Finding C: the shared emergency infrastructure is the physical mechanism behind wartime coordination overriding peacetime rivalry

**Combining:** attribute #2 (Shared Emergency Infrastructure) + the Full Extrapolation's Section VI (during
the war itself, Scott and Fort McMurdo's experience was functionally coordinated, not separate — the rivalry
paused, since the shared infrastructure didn't care whose city it technically belonged to).

**2nd-order effect:** attribute #2 existed before the war by its own established nature, but Section VI
describes the specific moment it became genuinely load-bearing — the crisis that made peacetime rivalry
irrelevant to actual survival.

**3rd-order effect:** gives the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's own Finding 1 (Scott's rivalry following
a real historical arc) a concrete physical anchor for the exact moment that arc bent — the infrastructure
that didn't care about the rivalry is what actually kept both cities alive when it mattered most.

### Finding D: the Border's own established porousness is a physical expression of Scott's resolved emotional reaction to Fort McMurdo's capital status

**Combining:** attribute #1 (the Scott–Fort McMurdo Border, formally maintained but practically porous) + the
Full Extrapolation's Section VII (Scott's reaction to Fort McMurdo's formal capital designation — "a sting,
not a rupture," mixed but ultimately unbothered).

**2nd-order effect:** a boundary that's real on paper but practically unenforced is a physical expression of
exactly the temperament Section VII describes — neither fully dissolved (Scott still marks its own
distinct identity) nor genuinely defended (the asymmetry was never worth a real fight).

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming directly as architecture matching emotional resolution — the Border's own
established character is Scott's civic psychology made physical, not just a practical administrative detail.

### Finding E: Scott's residential land use and its non-industrial economic niche are the same civic choice, expressed twice

**Combining:** attributes #5–6 (housing-dominant physical footprint, modest public gathering spaces) + the
Full Extrapolation's Section II (Scott's precision/calibration/monitoring economic specialty, deliberately
complementary rather than competing with Fort McMurdo's own heavy industry).

**2nd-order effect:** Scott's built environment and its economic specialization are two separate expressions
of the identical underlying choice — a city that could plausibly have grown into more industrial capacity
given its Peninsula position, but consistently chose scale-restraint instead.

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming as genuine civic consistency rather than coincidence — Scott didn't end up
small and precise by accident; both its land use and its economy independently reflect the same deliberate
choice to stay the quiet counterpart to its much larger neighbor.
