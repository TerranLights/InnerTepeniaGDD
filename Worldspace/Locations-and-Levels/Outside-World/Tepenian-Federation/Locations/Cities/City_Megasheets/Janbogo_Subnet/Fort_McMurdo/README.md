*Tepenia's historical capital, informal at first and later official by name — the biggest built thing in the Federation without being its most populous city, living in the shadow of an active volcano, its real weight measured in decisions that ripple outward rather than people counted at home.*

## Who Lives Here, and Why

China and USA sit co-Primary (19.34%/21.69%), with a genuinely broad Significant tier — Germany, France, Japan, Italy, UK — and an extensive Notable tier beneath that: Chile, Canada, Malaysia, New Zealand, Australia, Indonesia, South Korea, Philippines. Few Tepenian cities carry this many distinct national tiers at once, consistent with the established fact that McMurdo Station was always a multinational working environment, never exclusively American even though Americans founded and named it.

## What It Feels Like

Mount Erebus dominates every skyline view — the southernmost active volcano on Earth, a permanent lava lake at its summit, a constant plume of gas and ash marking the horizon in most weather. Per the developer's own vision session: Fort McMurdo's real mass isn't population or even architecture — it's presence. A visitor immediately senses real business happening, real decisions being made that ripple outward and change what other cities can and can't do. Dedicated comms stations coordinate supply, extraction scheduling, and inter-city logistics — the staffed, physical reality behind its capital status, distinct from Janbogo's own Arcanet relay-nexus role. That status itself unfolded in two phases: an informal, de facto arrangement first, then a later, formal designation by name within the Second Interwar Period, held through to the Long Night War. This gravity is explicitly operational, not political: Fort McMurdo doesn't perform importance or posture in Federation debates, it simply is indispensable, quietly and constantly. The culture itself is working-class, direct, and meritocratic — status earned by what you could do, not where you came from — with transient, seasonal contract labor a founding-era feature of the mining and extraction economy. Living for generations in the shadow of an active volcano gave residents an easy familiarity with forces larger than human or robot agency, something outsiders sometimes find unsettling and residents find clarifying.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Zug, Switzerland

A compact lakeside old town in a canton of roughly 130,000 people, holding over 30,000 registered companies — one of the highest corporate densities in the world, later growing into "Crypto Valley" after the Ethereum Foundation set up there in 2014.

**What this gives Fort McMurdo:** direct precedent for a place whose real significance is concentrated economic density rather than population size — Zug's tiny population carrying an outsized concentration of corporate headquarters mirrors Fort McMurdo's own established "presence, not population" identity almost exactly.

### Secondary: Calgary, Canada

The corporate headquarters hub for Alberta's oil and gas industry — home to roughly 4,300 petroleum and energy-service companies — even though the actual extraction (Alberta's oil sands) happens elsewhere in the province, not in Calgary itself.

**What this gives Fort McMurdo:** a precise structural match — Calgary is where oil *decisions* get made even though the oil itself comes from elsewhere, mirroring Fort McMurdo's own established role as the place where decisions "ripple outward" to shape what other Tepenian cities can do. A genuine coordination hub for industry, not necessarily the extraction site itself.

### Secondary: Geneva, Switzerland

Host to 43 international organizations and 183 UN member-state missions concentrated in a genuinely small territory, alongside one of the world's most sophisticated private-banking sectors.

**What this gives Fort McMurdo:** reinforces the "outsized influence concentrated in small territory" theme, and Geneva's international-organization density echoes Fort McMurdo's own broad, multinational founding population and its coordination role for a whole subnet's logistics.

# Fort McMurdo — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Fort McMurdo given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Fort_McMurdo_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-08.

## I. Demonym: McMurdans

**Proposed**, dropping the "Fort" prefix for the demonym itself, following the same pattern real-world place names use when the full name is unwieldy as an adjective (a New Yorker, not a "New York-er"). The Specs file's own working placeholder ("Forts McMurdans") already gestures toward this; simplifying to McMurdans keeps the name clean.

---

## II. Industrial Output: Processing Here, Raw Extraction Across the Sound

**Proposed, directly answering the question surfaced in the Mega-Init:** Fort McMurdo itself functioned primarily as a processing and coordination hub — fuel processing, equipment manufacturing and repair, construction-materials production — while the actual raw extraction happened across McMurdo Sound in the Dry Valleys, whose exposed bedrock and sediment (genuinely unlike ice-covered land anywhere else on the continent) offered mineral resources unavailable on Ross Island itself. Ross Island's own volcanic basalt supplied construction aggregate closer to home. This mirrors Calgary's own real division of labor — a headquarters city distinct from the extraction site — giving Fort McMurdo's economy a specific, earned shape rather than "mining happens here" as an undifferentiated blur.

## IV. What "Partially Operational" Means Today

**Proposed:** a functioning core of survivors concentrated in the most structurally intact section of the city — plausibly near the original harbor and pier infrastructure, since heavy port and fuel-storage structures built to withstand geological and volcanic stress would plausibly prove more blast-resistant than lighter residential construction added later. This surviving core continues basic logistics and processing work at a greatly reduced scale, still functionally relevant to the wider Janbogo subnet even in its diminished state — a city-that-was, with real, ongoing work still happening inside it.

---

## V. Cape Evans and Cape Royds: Intact, and a Deliberate Contrast

**Proposed:** both expedition huts survived the Long Night War largely undamaged, protected by their physical distance from the main industrial target and by their own established multi-century preservation in Antarctic cold. In DLC 6, they function as a deliberate atmospheric contrast — genuinely intact heritage sites sitting a few kilometers from an enormous industrial ruin, the Heroic Age of Antarctic exploration preserved almost perfectly beside its industrial-age descendant's destruction.

---

## VI. Erebus's Activity Level: Baseline, Not Heightened

**Proposed:** ongoing but stable — the lava lake's persistent, low-key activity continuing much as it always has, not a heightened eruptive phase. This is consistent with the real Erebus's genuine, well-documented multi-century pattern of stable, non-catastrophic activity, and it keeps the volcano as atmospheric presence and environmental backdrop rather than competing with the war itself as the city's central threat.

---

## VII. The Dry Valleys Operation: Still Running, Lighter and More Resilient

**Proposed:** yes, still ongoing in reduced capacity — since the Dry Valleys extraction was never fully dependent on Fort McMurdo's own damaged infrastructure (accessed via the seasonal sea ice road or by sea, a genuinely separate logistics chain), it could plausibly continue with lighter, more resilient operations even as the main city absorbed the worst of the war's damage.

---

## VIII. Which Sections Survived: The Original Core, Not the Expansion

**Proposed:** the original McMurdo Station-era core — piers, fuel depots, vehicle infrastructure, built to the older, heavier American Antarctic Program standard — survived in better condition than the later Tepenian-era residential expansion built to accommodate a growing civilian population. The oldest, most overbuilt parts of the city outlasted the newer, more lightly built additions around them.

---

## IX. When Fort McMurdo's Capital Status Became Official: After the Amundsen Tower

**Proposed:** the formal designation came in the aftermath of the Amundsen Tower's construction (~2629–2639), not before it. Building the Tower — a national infrastructure project on a scale nothing else in Tepenian history matched — would have made the nation's dependency on Fort McMurdo's industrial output completely undeniable, at exactly the moment its own established "McMurdo Problem" faction was already naming real resentment about the city wielding that much influence with no formal accountability attached to it. Formalizing Fort McMurdo's status wasn't really a change in power at all — it was the government finally attaching legitimacy and accountability to power the city already, unmistakably, held. Making the arrangement official was the response to the resentment, not a cause of it.

---

## Worth Your Attention

Section IX is the most significant proposal here — it gives the newly-clarified de facto-then-official capital timeline an actual earned cause (the Amundsen Tower's construction making Fort McMurdo's dependency undeniable, right as the "McMurdo Problem" faction's resentment was already naming the same fact) rather than leaving the transition as an unexplained date. Section II is the second most load-bearing, since it gives Fort McMurdo's previously undifferentiated "mining and extraction" economy a specific division of labor drawn directly from Calgary's own real precedent. Section VIII is worth flagging directly against Port Lockroy's own Megasheet: both cities show the identical pattern of an older, hardier core outlasting later, lighter-built additions — worth checking in the Cross-Reference Synthesis whether this is coincidence or a genuine cross-city pattern.

# Fort McMurdo — Cross-Reference Synthesis: Gaps, Combinations, and Downstream Effects

## Finding 1: The capital's formalization may trace to more than one concurrent mega-project, not the Amundsen Tower alone

**Combining:** the Full Extrapolation's proposed timing for Fort McMurdo's formal capital designation (aftermath of the Amundsen Tower's construction, ~2629–2639) + the established fact that Fort McMurdo's industrial output built "a significant share of Concordia's own infrastructure" — its dome structures, corridor systems, and equipment + the Tower's own established role as the era's single largest national infrastructure undertaking.

**2nd-order effect:** if Concordia's own growth as a hub required a similarly massive, concurrent supply of materials during roughly the same broader era of national construction, Fort McMurdo's formalization may not trace to the Tower alone — it may mark the moment the city became simultaneously indispensable to more than one of Tepenia's largest-ever infrastructure projects at once.

**3rd-order effect:** this reframes the capital's formalization as a response to a broader era of unprecedented, concurrent national construction, rather than one isolated crisis — Fort McMurdo's industrial capacity became critical to multiple different projects at the same time, compounding the pressure toward formal recognition.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a richer causal account for whenever a future Timeline document pins down the exact designation date — not one triggering event, but a convergence of several, all tracing back to the same underlying industrial capacity.

---

## Finding 2: Fort McMurdo and Port Lockroy share an identical damage pattern for two entirely different reasons

**Combining:** Fort McMurdo's proposed Section VIII finding (the original McMurdo Station-era core survived the war better than the later Tepenian-era residential expansion) + Port Lockroy's own established Cross-Reference finding (its 1944 wartime-built core survived better than its later peacetime additions) + the different underlying mechanisms behind each: Port Lockroy's core was deliberately hardened for wartime military use; Fort McMurdo's core was simply built heavier, to the older American Antarctic Program's extreme-environment engineering standard, for reasons that had nothing to do with war.

**2nd-order effect:** these are the same visible pattern — an older core outlasting newer additions — produced by two unrelated causes in two different cities, not one universal Tepenian rule about age and resilience.

**3rd-order effect:** this is worth naming precisely so "old core survived" doesn't become a lazy, self-explanatory shorthand applied identically everywhere — the actual reason behind each city's own resilience should shape what its ruins look like and what remains inside them.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a genuinely useful distinction for level design — Port Lockroy's surviving core would read as hardened military infrastructure; Fort McMurdo's would read as heavy industrial engineering built for an extreme environment, a different visual and material language for what is, on the surface, "the same" kind of ruin.

---

## Finding 3: Fort McMurdo's own "presence, not population" identity may be exactly what made its informal status intolerable

**Combining:** Fort McMurdo's established "presence, not population" identity — real significance that isn't legible through any obviously countable metric + the newly-proposed capital-formalization timing, driven by growing, undeniable national dependency + the established "McMurdo Problem" faction naming real resentment about the city's outsized, informal influence.

**2nd-order effect:** an invisible, hard-to-quantify kind of power is precisely the kind of power that provokes the deepest resentment when left informal — there's no countable way for outsiders to confirm how much influence Fort McMurdo actually holds, only a persistent, uneasy sense that it's substantial.

**3rd-order effect:** formalizing Fort McMurdo's status may have been less about legitimizing power that already had a clear, measurable shape, and more about making visible and accountable a kind of influence that was, by its own established nature, deliberately difficult to see or measure.

**4th-order effect:** this deepens why the "McMurdo Problem" faction specifically existed and specifically drove formalization — not simple resource jealousy, but a genuine structural frustration with a kind of power that couldn't even be properly named until the government finally did so.

---

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across All Three Findings

All three findings converge on the same underlying idea: Fort McMurdo's significance has always been harder to see than it is to feel. Its formalization as capital likely traced to more than one project depending on it at once, rather than a single visible crisis (Finding 1); its own physical resilience shares a visible pattern with Port Lockroy's for reasons that have nothing to do with each other (Finding 2); and its whole civic identity — presence over population, felt rather than counted — may be exactly what made an unaccountable, invisible kind of power impossible to leave informal any longer (Finding 3). Fort McMurdo reads, across all three findings, as a city whose real weight consistently exceeds what any single, countable fact about it can capture.

---

## Worth Your Attention

Finding 3 is the most conceptually significant of the three — it ties Fort McMurdo's own signature "presence, not population" identity directly to the specific historical mechanism (the "McMurdo Problem" resentment) that eventually forced its capital status into the open, giving the city's abstract civic-gravity concept a concrete political consequence rather than leaving it as pure atmosphere.
