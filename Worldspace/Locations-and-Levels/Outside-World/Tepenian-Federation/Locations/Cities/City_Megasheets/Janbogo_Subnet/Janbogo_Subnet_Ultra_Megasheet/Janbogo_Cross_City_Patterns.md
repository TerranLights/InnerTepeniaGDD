# Janbogo/Ross Subnet — Cross-City Patterns

**What this is:** common factors recurring across multiple Janbogo/Ross subnet cities — not causal chains (see Cross-City Throughways for those), but shared traits, structures, or tensions that show up independently in more than one city's own Megasheet. Several of these were already partially self-identified within individual cities' own Cross-Reference Syntheses; the job here is to consolidate and extend that work across the full subnet, following the same process used for Palmer and Halley subnets. Written 2026-07-10.

---

## Pattern 1: A subnet-wide taxonomy of *why* each city ended up the way the war left it

Unlike Halley subnet's own damage-*mechanism* taxonomy (passive age-favoring, active recency-favoring, single-point-of-failure, function-targeted — a question of *how* damage happened), Janbogo/Ross subnet's own pattern is a question of *strategic logic* — why a hostile force chose to destroy, spare, or ignore each of these seven cities specifically. All seven read cleanly against one shared axis:

| City | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Janbogo** | Damaged, survived | Worth more alive than destroyed — its unique value as a living witness and message-relay point (Hwy 183 and Terra Nova Bay's polynya both) made it more useful intact (Janbogo Cross-Reference, Finding 1) |
| **Zukelli** | Destroyed | Punitive, deliberate — sheltering AWOL Upper Earth defectors, struck as a total-kill deterrent message, with Janbogo spared specifically to witness and transmit it (Zukelli Full Extrapolation) |
| **Cape Adare** | Destroyed | Symbolic — a second, later destruction plausibly also relayed onward through Janbogo, adding a second witnessed tragedy to Janbogo's own burden (Cape Adare Cross-Reference, Finding 1) |
| **Denison** | Destroyed | Strategic infrastructure — a genuine Hwy 183 chokepoint *and* a Cradle fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturer, giving its destruction two independent strategic motives at once, neither of them punitive or symbolic (Denison Full Extrapolation, Section IV; Cross-Reference, Finding 2) |
| **Fort McMurdo** | Damaged, survived | Presence-not-population capital status and a genuinely dispersed industrial footprint meant no single strike could plausibly eliminate its whole function (Fort McMurdo Cross-Reference) |
| **Scott** | Damaged, survived | Small, residential, non-strategic — the direct experiential opposite of its neighbor Fort McMurdo, with nothing about it worth specifically targeting (Scott Mega-Init) |
| **Dumont d'Urville** | Damaged, survived | Struck early for its genuine two-highway junction value, but never eliminated outright — a junction disrupted, not destroyed, since its downtown core (the city's actual load-bearing asset) came through intact (Dumont d'Urville Full Extrapolation, Section II) |

**What this produces:** a genuinely complete answer to "what predicts a Janbogo/Ross city's war status," and it isn't raw strategic value — Denison's Cross-Reference Finding 1 already established this directly, comparing Janbogo's survival against Denison's destruction. This pattern extends that same principle across all seven cities at once: outright destruction correlates with either *punitive intent* (Zukelli, and plausibly Cape Adare) or *pure infrastructure value with no offsetting "worth more alive" quality* (Denison) — while survival correlates with either active ongoing usefulness (Janbogo's witness role, Fort McMurdo's dispersed capital function) or simply not being worth the trouble (Scott, and Dumont d'Urville's core once its junction was disrupted).

## Pattern 2: Founding-nation civic template persisting beneath total demographic dilution — tested, not just asserted

Dumont d'Urville's own Cross-Reference Finding 1 explicitly proposed a testable distinction — *functional daily transmission* (a founding culture surviving because ordinary civic life is still conducted in it, Quebec City's real 400-year precedent) versus *ceremonial-only preservation* (a founding culture surviving only in names, holidays, and architecture, vulnerable to further dilution) — and named Fort McMurdo, Janbogo, and Zukelli as the subnet's other candidates worth checking. Running that check now, across all four:

- **Dumont d'Urville (French):** functional. French is "picked up simply through years of civic life conducted in it," regardless of ancestry — the strongest, most literally proven case in the subnet.
- **Janbogo (Korean):** functional-leaning. Janbogo's own established "absorption culture" — a civic identity that folds new arrivals into its existing template rather than being displaced by them — describes the same underlying mechanism as Dumont d'Urville's French transmission, just under a different name.
- **Fort McMurdo:** ceremonial-leaning. Its founding civic identity persists primarily through its historical capital status and Saint-adjacent memory (shared with Scott at Hut Point) rather than through an ordinary, everyday practice every new resident picks up regardless of origin — closer to preserved institutional memory than lived daily transmission.
- **Zukelli:** moot. The city was destroyed before this question could be meaningfully tested one way or the other — its founding culture's persistence mechanism is now a purely historical question, answerable only through descendant communities (per the established Zukelli Native mechanic) rather than through the city itself.

**What this produces:** two of the subnet's four candidate cities (Dumont d'Urville, Janbogo) carry the more durable, functional version of this pattern; Fort McMurdo carries the more fragile, ceremonial version; Zukelli's case is permanently unresolvable in the present tense. This gives a real, load-bearing answer to the question Dumont d'Urville's own synthesis raised rather than leaving it as an open invitation.

## Pattern 3: The "quiet, intimate city living beside a much busier neighbor" shape, appearing twice in one subnet

Scott's own relationship to Fort McMurdo (the Victoria BC / Alexandria VA structural inversion — a quiet residential city beside a busy, capital-carrying neighbor) is the subnet's most fully developed instance of this shape. But it isn't unique within the subnet: Cape Adare's own established "small-town-feel-at-massive-scale" personality, sitting beside Janbogo — the subnet's actual traffic and communications hub — reads as the same underlying structure at a different register. Where Scott/Fort McMurdo plays out as a domestic-quiet-versus-industrial-capital contrast, Cape Adare/Janbogo plays out as an intimate-feeling-versus-heavy-traffic-hub contrast. Both pairs place a smaller, quieter city directly beside the subnet's most consequential neighbor; both smaller cities carry no resentment about it (Scott's Positive Outlook temperament; Cape Adare's own established firstness-pride identity, which doesn't need Janbogo's traffic to feel significant).

**What this produces:** this shape isn't a coincidence specific to Scott — it's a recurring subnet-level relationship type, appearing wherever a city's civic identity is genuinely self-contained enough not to need its busier neighbor's scale to feel complete.

## Pattern 4: Civic identity anchored to one specific, dated founding moment rather than ongoing scale or achievement

Three of the subnet's seven cities organize their entire civic identity around a single historical date or figure rather than a broader achievement or ongoing function:

- **Scott:** organized entirely around St. Robert (Robert Falcon Scott) and the 1902 Hut Point refuge — directly paralleled to Hannibal, Missouri's fusion with Mark Twain (Scott Mega-Init).
- **Dumont d'Urville:** organized around St. Jules Day, commemorating Jules Dumont d'Urville's specific January 21, 1840 landing — directly paralleled to San Miguel de Tucumán's 1816 Congress of Independence (Dumont d'Urville Mega-Init).
- **Cape Adare:** organized around its own established "firstness" identity — the first landing site, a founding-moment civic anchor in the same register as the other two, though not yet given the same depth of real-world parallel research.

**What this produces:** nearly half the subnet's cities (3 of 7) share a civic-identity mechanism that has nothing to do with size, industry, or ongoing relevance — a durable pattern worth checking against any future Janbogo/Ross-adjacent city, and a meaningful subnet-level counterweight to Fort McMurdo's and Janbogo's own function-first identities.

## Pattern 5: Janbogo's witness burden is cumulative, not singular

Janbogo's own Cross-Reference Finding 1 already established its unique value as a message-relay point for Zukelli's destruction specifically. Cape Adare's own Cross-Reference Finding 1 extends this directly — proposing Janbogo also relayed word of Cape Adare's own later, symbolic destruction. Two separate cities' destructions, relayed through the same third city, in what's very plausibly not a coincidence but a repeat use of the same established relay function (Hwy 183 plus Terra Nova Bay's maritime access) that earned Janbogo its own survival in the first place.

**What this produces:** Janbogo's own internal "vigil-vs-moving-on" split (Janbogo's own Cross-Reference) reads differently once its witness role is understood as cumulative rather than singular — this isn't a city processing one specific historical trauma, but a city whose entire founding function has repeatedly required it to watch its own neighbors die and be the one to tell the rest of the subnet about it. Worth flagging as the single most emotionally significant throughline in this subnet's own internal life, developed further in Throughway 1.

## Pattern 6 (brief, cross-subnet echo — not developed further here): Denison's Cradle chamber role connects this subnet to Halley subnet's own uncredited-engineering throughline

Denison's own historical role as a fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturer for the nationwide Cradle infrastructure (Denison Full Extrapolation, Section IV; Cross-Reference Finding 2) is the same infrastructure whose present-day design lineage was traced, in Halley subnet's own Ultra-Megasheet, back to Neumayer's uncredited engineering work. Denison's destruction is therefore not just a Janbogo/Ross subnet event — it plausibly represents a real, named contributing cause to why the present-day Cradle network is smaller than it once was, a fact whose other half lives in a different subnet's own Ultra-Megasheet entirely. Deliberately left brief here, consistent with this subnet's own scope — the full cross-subnet picture belongs to the future re-examination pass, not this document.
