# Janbogo — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Janbogo given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Janbogo_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-08.

---

## I. Who's Running Janbogo Now: A Functional Council, Not a Single Ruler

**Proposed:** a pragmatic, informal council made up of whoever holds practical control over the port, Hwy 183, and the surviving commercial halls — not a single strongman or a Concordia-appointed governor. This traces directly from Janbogo's own established Enneagram character: Compliant, accommodating, synthesizing rather than dominating, the same quality already visible in its Chinese-French fashion fusion and its role as a service-oriented subnet hub. A city whose whole civic identity is about making people feel at home is a poor fit for authoritarian rule, and a genuinely good fit for a working coalition assembled out of necessity.

---

## II. Majyao's Original Teahouse: Preserved as Civic Memory

**Proposed:** the community stepped in to keep the original teahouse intact even after Majyao's own relocation to Concordia — maintained rather than looted or repurposed, functioning today as a site of civic memory even without its original keeper. This follows from the deliberate, unhurried nature of her departure (a relocation, not a flight from chaos) and Janbogo's own established instinct to preserve what makes daily life feel worth living.

## IV. Why the Subnet Nexus Sits in Concordia, Not Janbogo

**Proposed:** a wartime emergency relocation — the Arcanet nexus hardware was moved to Concordia specifically to protect it from further damage once Janbogo's own "damaged, partially operational" status became clear, prioritizing the subnet-wide communications function over keeping the hardware in its historically appropriate hub city. A pragmatic wartime decision, not a peacetime political arrangement.

## V. Tepenia's Three Korean-Founded Cities — Kin, Not Siblings, and Not All Equally Kin

**Revised 2026-07-13:** this section originally proposed a two-city relationship (Janbogo and Sejong only), omitting Sinheung in the Mirny subnet entirely — the same omission bug independently found and fixed at Sejong's own files. Tepenia actually has three Korean-founded cities, and the honest relationship among them isn't a single symmetric bond but three genuinely different distances.

**Proposed:** Janbogo and Sejong's relationship remains what was originally proposed for the pair — limited but real ceremonial contact rather than close, ongoing kinship. Janbogo represents Unified Korea, Sejong represents South Korea specifically, two related but genuinely distinct national origins separated by both geography and political history; the relationship plausibly resembled the way a diaspora with a shared ancestral homeland but different immediate national roots might maintain warm, occasional contact without deep, continuous involvement in each other's daily civic life.

Sinheung sits at a genuinely greater remove from both. It reached its Korean-founded status through an entirely different mechanism — the Jeju-do diplomatic partition that allocated it to Korea alongside Zhongshan (China) and Shirayuki (Japan) — rather than through direct exile settlement on a real-world Korean station the way Janbogo and Sejong were. Geographically it sits in the Mirny subnet, on the opposite side of the continent from both, with no established highway, shipping, or Arcanet connection to either. Proposed: essentially no pre-Split-Brain contact existed between Sinheung and Janbogo/Sejong at all — not estrangement, simply three communities that trace back to the same national origin through such different paths and such genuine distance that they likely didn't think of each other as kin in any active sense, only as a fact any of the three might discover about the others if they ever compared notes.

**The irony worth naming directly:** Sinheung is, by current population, the most heavily and unambiguously Korean city in Tepenia (34.62% Primary) — more so than Janbogo, which built and kept the deepest *institutional* Korean legacy (the teahouse tradition) despite its own Korean population diluting to Significant tier, and far more so than Sejong, where Korean identity survives only as founding-era naming heritage. If these three communities ever do make genuine contact in future DLC content, that contact would surface a real asymmetry: the city that "is" Korea by the numbers is the one with the least developed institutional Korean culture and the most recent, diplomatically-engineered founding story, while the city with by far the deepest lived Korean cultural tradition (Janbogo) is demographically the least Korean of the three going forward.

## VII. The Zukelli Defectors — Deliberately Multi-National

**Proposed:** the defectors were plausibly not from a single nation, but a small, mixed group — perhaps four to seven individuals, few enough to hide, many enough to warrant a decisive response. This actually strengthens the deterrent message's own established purpose: if the warning is meant for "the rest of Tepenia" broadly, a multi-national defector group makes that warning maximally universal, rather than reading as retaliation against one specific national grievance.

---

## VIII. Timing, Warning, and Whether the Message Worked

**Proposed:** the strike came mid-war, once Upper Earth had secured enough operational footing to spare resources for a targeted punitive action rather than needing everything for front-line offense. No genuine warning was possible or intended — the strike's totality depended on surprise, since any advance notice would have let the defectors flee or let Janbogo evacuate, undermining the message's entire point of inescapable retribution. As for whether the message worked: functionally, yes — no other Tepenian city is documented to have sheltered known AWOL Upper Earth defectors after Zukelli's destruction, suggesting the warning was received and heeded, even without a definitive citywide record proving it.

---

## IX. The Shielded Halls' Own Concentration Risk — Real, But Untested

**Proposed, directly answering the question surfaced in the Mega-Init:** the great shielded commercial halls are a genuine concentration risk in principle, but the war's actual damage pattern — Janbogo damaged, not destroyed — suggests either the halls themselves weren't the strike's target, or Janbogo's port and highway infrastructure mattered more strategically than its indoor civic spaces did. This makes the halls' vulnerability real but untested rather than resolved: a latent structural risk the city has simply never had to face directly, not a false alarm.

---

## Worth Your Attention

Section VII is the most narratively valuable proposal here — making the defectors deliberately multi-national, rather than tied to one nation's grievance, gives the strike's own established "message to the rest of Tepenia" logic real internal consistency, rather than leaving an unexamined gap between the strike's stated universal purpose and an unstated single-nation motive. Section I is the second most valuable, since it grounds Janbogo's current governance directly in its own established personality rather than defaulting to a generic post-war authority structure.
