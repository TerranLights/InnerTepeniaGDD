# Dumont d'Urville — Community Infrastructure & Social Life

**What this is:** concrete physical spaces, small institutions, and recurring social rituals that give Dumont d'Urville's already-established culture a tangible, ground-level presence — the same pass done for each of Concordia's 13 districts, all 16 Mirny and Halley subnet cities, Janbogo, Cape Adare, and Denison, extended here. Dumont d'Urville is damaged but standing, not destroyed. Note: "St. Jules" fits the same established Tepenian Saints framework already confirmed for Troll's St. Roald and Cape Adare's St. Carsten — not a GPS-only violation. ## Additions

- **The Downtown Eateries-with-Stages** — the "New Orleans at 1/20th scale" district, live music inside almost every downtown eatery as an ordinary, expected feature.
- **The Adélie Breeding Grounds** — the penguin colonies on the Géologie Archipelago, undisturbed alongside the damaged city.
- **St. Jules Memorial Site** — commemorating the 1840 landing.
- **The Channel Crossing** — the ~5km route between Petrel Island and the continental coast, crossable by sea ice in winter or boat in summer. **Flagged:** the actual logistics of the summer/winter transition haven't been worked out — how the switchover itself happens, what the shoulder-season gap looks like. Same flag as Fort McMurdo's McMurdo Sound sea ice road — worth resolving once as a shared dual-mode-crossing mechanic rather than separately for each city.
- **The Dumont d'Urville Port** — receiving Australian freighter shipments during the seasonal window.
- **Naturalist Observation Blinds** — where the robot naturalist-observation tradition around the penguin colonies is practiced as the city's closest thing to a shared contemplative practice.

## Small offices for educational training

- precision wind-engineering solutions (anchor points, windbreak design)
- naturalist documentation and observational art
- maritime freight logistics, the seasonal shipping window

## Social cohesion mechanisms

- **St. Jules Day** — the public outdoor reading of surviving fragments of the 1840 expedition journal, held in the wind deliberately.
- small, packed, informal live-music venues — the downtown "eateries with stages" culture, ordinary rather than special-occasion.
- unhurried shared meals — one of the few genuinely sheltered social occasions in a city otherwise governed by weather.
- quiet, careful colony observation — the shared contemplative practice extended to visitors and newcomers as a genuine invitation.
