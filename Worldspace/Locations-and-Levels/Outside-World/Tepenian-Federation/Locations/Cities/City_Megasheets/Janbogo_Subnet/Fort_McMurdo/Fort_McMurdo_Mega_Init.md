## What It Feels Like

Mount Erebus dominates every skyline view — the southernmost active volcano on Earth, a permanent lava lake at its summit, a constant plume of gas and ash marking the horizon in most weather. Per the developer's own vision session: Fort McMurdo's real mass isn't population or even architecture — it's presence. A visitor immediately senses real business happening, real decisions being made that ripple outward and change what other cities can and can't do. Dedicated comms stations coordinate supply, extraction scheduling, and inter-city logistics — the staffed, physical reality behind its capital status, distinct from Janbogo's own Arcanet relay-nexus role. That status itself unfolded in two phases: an informal, de facto arrangement first, then a later, formal designation by name within the Second Interwar Period, held through to the Long Night War. This gravity is explicitly operational, not political: Fort McMurdo doesn't perform importance or posture in Federation debates, it simply is indispensable, quietly and constantly. The culture itself is working-class, direct, and meritocratic — status earned by what you could do, not where you came from — with transient, seasonal contract labor a founding-era feature of the mining and extraction economy. Living for generations in the shadow of an active volcano gave residents an easy familiarity with forces larger than human or robot agency, something outsiders sometimes find unsettling and residents find clarifying.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Zug, Switzerland

A compact lakeside old town in a canton of roughly 130,000 people, holding over 30,000 registered companies — one of the highest corporate densities in the world, later growing into "Crypto Valley" after the Ethereum Foundation set up there in 2014.

**What this gives Fort McMurdo:** direct precedent for a place whose real significance is concentrated economic density rather than population size — Zug's tiny population carrying an outsized concentration of corporate headquarters mirrors Fort McMurdo's own established "presence, not population" identity almost exactly.

### Secondary: Calgary, Canada

The corporate headquarters hub for Alberta's oil and gas industry — home to roughly 4,300 petroleum and energy-service companies — even though the actual extraction (Alberta's oil sands) happens elsewhere in the province, not in Calgary itself.

**What this gives Fort McMurdo:** a precise structural match — Calgary is where oil *decisions* get made even though the oil itself comes from elsewhere, mirroring Fort McMurdo's own established role as the place where decisions "ripple outward" to shape what other Tepenian cities can do. A genuine coordination hub for industry, not necessarily the extraction site itself.

### Secondary: Geneva, Switzerland

Host to 43 international organizations and 183 UN member-state missions concentrated in a genuinely small territory, alongside one of the world's most sophisticated private-banking sectors.

**What this gives Fort McMurdo:** reinforces the "outsized influence concentrated in small territory" theme, and Geneva's international-organization density echoes Fort McMurdo's own broad, multinational founding population and its coordination role for a whole subnet's logistics.

The current files don't specify this division of labor.
