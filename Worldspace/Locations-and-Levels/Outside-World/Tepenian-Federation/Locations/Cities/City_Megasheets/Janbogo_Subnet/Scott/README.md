## What It Feels Like

Per the developer's own vision session: overwhelmingly residential in physical footprint — housing dominates, with the rest given over mainly to casual, leisurely business (restaurants, general leisure) and a modest handful of public gathering spaces. Not much is visibly happening here, in the best way: a genuinely decent, quiet place to raise a family, the direct experiential counterpart to Fort McMurdo's constant operational churn three kilometers away. A real technical/research economic base sits quietly underneath this, established but never defining what the city feels like to walk through day to day — confirmed 2026-07-07 to include one genuine contained industry: collecting volcanic material fallen from Mount Erebus and delivering it to a trucking facility across McMurdo Sound for onward processing. At the city's heart is Hut Point — Robert Falcon Scott's 1902 expedition refuge, one of the oldest surviving human-made structures in Antarctica, and Scott's most sacred civic location. On Tepenian Independence Day (June 21), residents of both Scott and Fort McMurdo gather there to lay candles, flowers, and personal tokens — an unmandated tradition that simply happens, year after year, because the Saint belongs to the whole peninsula, not only to the city bearing his name.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Victoria, British Columbia, Canada

A compact, walkable, mild-climate city on Vancouver Island, genuinely quieter and slower-paced than nearby Vancouver across the water — and, notably, British Columbia's actual capital city, despite that quieter character.

**What this gives Scott:** a rich structural inversion worth naming directly. Victoria *is* BC's capital while being the quieter of the two cities (Vancouver, bigger and busier, is not the capital); Scott's own situation is the mirror image — sitting beside Fort McMurdo, the busy, operationally powerful city that holds Tepenia's own historical capital status, while Scott remains the quiet one. Same "quiet city beside a busier neighbor" shape, opposite assignment of which twin carries the formal significance.

### Secondary: Hannibal, Missouri

Branded "America's Hometown," its entire civic identity fused permanently with one resident — Mark Twain — quotes hanging from its streetlamps, nearly every corner paying homage to him.

**What this gives Scott:** direct precedent for a small city whose entire civic identity organizes around one specific historical figure — mirroring Scott's own established relationship to St. Robert with real precision, a city that knows exactly whose name it carries and builds its civic memory around him.

### Secondary: Alexandria, Virginia

A genuinely quiet, walkable historic town just 7.8 miles from Washington D.C., explicitly marketed as the peaceful small-scale alternative to the capital's scale and crowds, yet still a quick trip away.

**What this gives Scott:** the closest single real-world structural match of the three — a genuinely quiet, small-scale historic town sitting in the immediate shadow of a much larger, operationally significant capital city, exactly mirroring Scott's own proximity to Fort McMurdo now that Fort McMurdo's own capital status has been clarified, while maintaining a fully distinct identity of its own.

## II. Economic Character: Precision, Calibration, and Monitoring

**Proposed:** Scott's technical/research economy specializes in precision equipment calibration, maintenance, and environmental and geological monitoring for the whole Hut Point Peninsula — including a share of the ongoing Erebus monitoring program, complementing rather than competing with Fort McMurdo's own established heavy-industry focus. This traces directly from Scott Base's own real-world emphasis on scientific and environmental monitoring, and it gives Scott a specific, non-overlapping economic niche alongside Fort McMurdo rather than a vague "smaller version of the same thing."

---

## III. Scott and Fort McMurdo Today: Still in Active Contact, Cooperation Intensified

**Proposed:** yes, the two cities' surviving sections remain in active contact — their established shared emergency infrastructure and physical proximity meant that, if anything, post-war cooperation intensified out of pure necessity, since neither damaged city could sustain itself with fewer resources working entirely alone. The established "genuinely collaborative and genuinely competitive" relationship persists, but the war stripped away much of the friendly rivalry's earlier luxury.

---

## IV. Hut Point Discovery Hut: Survived Intact

**Proposed:** yes, the hut survived the Long Night War undamaged, protected by its distance from Fort McMurdo's own more heavily targeted industrial infrastructure and by its small, simple, non-strategic physical footprint — the same reasoning already proposed for Cape Evans and Cape Royds surviving near Fort McMurdo. The Independence Day remembrance tradition continues post-war, now carrying even more weight given how much both cities have lost since it began.

## VI. Scott and Fort McMurdo During the War Itself: Coordinated, Not Separate

**Proposed:** the two cities' wartime experience was functionally coordinated rather than separate — their established shared emergency infrastructure meant defense and survival efforts merged out of necessity during the actual crisis, regardless of how politically distinct the two cities normally kept themselves during peacetime. The rivalry paused; the shared infrastructure didn't care whose city it technically belonged to.

---

## VII. How Scott Reacted to Fort McMurdo's Formal Capital Status: A Sting, Not a Rupture

**Proposed, directly answering the question surfaced in the Mega-Init:** mixed but ultimately unbothered. Scott's own established character — "somewhat allergic to being perceived as Fort McMurdo's smaller sibling" — means the formal designation likely stung at first, a concrete, official confirmation of an asymmetry Scott residents already resented being reminded of. But Scott's own established Positive Outlook temperament kept this from curdling into lasting resentment: Scott's civic identity was never built around wanting to *be* the capital in the first place, so a formal title it never sought and never held wasn't really a loss — just an official name finally put on an imbalance that had always existed.

## Finding 1: Scott's rivalry with Fort McMurdo has a real historical arc, tracking Fort McMurdo's own two-phase capital timeline

**Combining:** the Victoria/BC parallel establishing the quiet-city-versus-capital inversion + Fort McMurdo's newly-clarified two-phase capital timeline (informal first, then formally designated) + Scott's own established "somewhat allergic to being perceived as Fort McMurdo's smaller sibling" trait.

**2nd-order effect:** Scott experienced its neighbor's rise to power in two distinct stages without ever holding the title itself — first an informal, arguably contestable prominence, then a formally settled, official designation that removed any ambiguity for resentment to attach to.

**3rd-order effect:** this gives Scott's rivalry with Fort McMurdo a real emotional arc — contestable irritation giving way to settled acceptance — rather than one flat, unchanging dynamic across the whole Second Interwar Period.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a genuinely rich throughline for Scott specifically — its own relationship to its neighbor's power tracks the exact same two-phase history Fort McMurdo's capital status itself went through.

---

## Finding 2: Erebus monitoring may be the one concrete, functioning example of Scott and Fort McMurdo's established cooperation

**Combining:** Scott's newly-proposed economic specialization in precision monitoring and calibration, including a share of Erebus monitoring + Fort McMurdo's own newly-proposed volcanologist figure, credited with that city's own Erebus monitoring program + the established fact that both cities share the identical volcano, skyline, and environmental hazard profile.

**2nd-order effect:** two independently-proposed monitoring efforts at two adjacent cities most plausibly represent one shared program split by specialization, rather than two separate, redundant operations — Fort McMurdo handling heavier geological and seismic monitoring given its industrial infrastructure, Scott handling precision instrumentation given its own established technical niche.

**3rd-order effect:** this gives the two cities' established "genuinely collaborative and genuinely competitive" relationship one concrete, specific, ongoing example rather than an abstract description — a real joint project both proposed extrapolations arrived at independently.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a genuinely earned, specific answer to what Scott/Fort McMurdo cooperation actually looks like in practice — the shared volcano isn't just atmospheric backdrop, it's plausibly the single most concrete point of ongoing collaboration between the two cities.

---

## Finding 3: The Hut Point remembrance tradition likely absorbed a second, unspoken layer of meaning after the war

**Combining:** the established, pre-war Independence Day remembrance tradition at Hut Point, shared by both cities — "the Saint belongs to the whole peninsula" + the newly-proposed wartime coordination (shared emergency infrastructure merging the two cities' survival efforts during the actual war) + the newly-proposed post-war intensified cooperation, born of necessity.

**2nd-order effect:** a shared civic ritual that existed before the war as a pleasant, voluntary tradition now sits atop a much more recent, heavier shared experience — surviving the war together, genuinely dependent on each other's infrastructure.

**3rd-order effect:** the post-war version of the Hut Point tradition plausibly carries a second, unspoken layer of meaning that didn't exist before the war — not replacing the original veneration of St. Robert, but adding quiet, recent grief and mutual dependence underneath the older, more distant historical one.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging as a genuinely resonant detail for any DLC 6 scene set at Hut Point — the ritual a player witnesses there isn't simply an unchanged, preserved pre-war tradition, but one that has plausibly taken on new weight because of what both cities have lived through together since it was last performed in peacetime.

---

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across All Three Findings

All three findings trace the same underlying idea: Scott's relationship to Fort McMurdo, and to its own history, has genuinely changed shape over time rather than sitting still. Its rivalry followed its neighbor's own two-phase rise to formal power (Finding 1); its cooperation with that same neighbor has a real, specific, shared project underneath the general description (Finding 2); and its oldest shared ritual has quietly picked up new meaning from more recent, harder experience (Finding 3). Scott reads, across all three findings, as a city whose quiet, stable surface sits on top of real, accumulated history with its neighbor — not a static contrast to Fort McMurdo, but a relationship that has actually moved and deepened over the Second Interwar Period and the war that ended it.

## Worth Your Attention

Finding 3 is the most emotionally significant of the three — it takes an already-established, moving tradition (the shared Hut Point remembrance) and gives it a genuine reason to carry more weight today than it did before the war, without requiring any invented new ceremony or changed ritual — the same tradition, quietly deepened by what actually happened since.
