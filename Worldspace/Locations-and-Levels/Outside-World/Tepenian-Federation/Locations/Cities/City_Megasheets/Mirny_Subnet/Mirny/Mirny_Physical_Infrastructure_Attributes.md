# Mirny — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Mirny would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Mirny.md`, `Local_Cultures/Mirny_Subnet/Mirny.md`, and `Mirny_
Community_Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred until this
pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30. Begins the Mirny subnet.

**Governing facts:** sits essentially exactly on the Antarctic Circle (66°33'S) — the only Tepenian city
in this genuinely liminal position, experiencing only the minimal form of polar night/midnight sun (4-5
days each, the sun grazing rather than clearing the horizon); the defining environmental hazard is wind,
not cold — sustained katabatic events exceeding 40 m/s, among the most severe in Antarctica; status is
damaged but partially operational; population over 1 million combined; the Mirny subnet's own Arcanet hub,
routing Zhongshan, Casey, Davis, Vostok, and Kunlun to each other; economy is subnet-hub logistics plus a
genuinely major industrial/quarrying operation — raw material processed into eastern-highway construction
materials AND (established, nationally significant) shipped to Sinheung as the near-exclusive supply for
robot-synthesis chamber fabrication; architecture is concentric wind-shedding rings with industrial yards
and residential space integrated together inside, not zoned separately; culture is explicitly
"coexistence, not fusion" between its Russian and Chinese populations; Hwy 110 (the Coastal Cut) is
Concordia's own closest cross-subnet physical link, running through Mirny.

---

## Wind-Resistant Structural Engineering (the defining hazard)

1. **Concentric windbreak-ring architecture** (established) — a smooth-shaped outer ring built specifically
   to shed katabatic wind rather than catch it, sheltering the industrial core inside.
2. **Airlock entries preventing outdoor wind infiltration** (established) — a distinct engineering need
   from a pure-cold city, where the wind itself, not just the temperature, has to be kept out.
3. **Structural reinforcement rated for sustained loading up to 40+ m/s wind events** — genuinely severe,
   among the most extreme wind-engineering requirements of any Tepenian city examined.
4. **Wind-chill-specific protective infrastructure**, distinct from pure cold-rating — established
   Hardcore Mode logic already notes ambient temperature here is milder than Concordia's but effective
   wind-chill is far more severe, meaning protection standards have to account for wind speed specifically,
   not cold alone.

## Antarctic Circle / Solstice Infrastructure

5. **A physical Antarctic Circle marker**, embedded directly in the windbreak's own outer wall (per
   Community Infrastructure's "Circle-Wall Marker Plaza") — civic acknowledgment of the city's own exact,
   liminal position.
6. **A dedicated solstice observation site** (established: the Solstice Grazing Grounds) — a gathering
   space for the winter and summer solstice sun-grazing events, the city's own most significant recurring
   visual phenomenon.

## Industrial & Quarrying Infrastructure

7. **Quarry-to-yard transit infrastructure** (established: Quarry-to-Yard Transit Sheds) — waystations
   along the raw-material supply route from the southern quarries to the sheltered industrial core.
8. **Ore-processing and machining facilities** within the wind-sheltered industrial core, converting
   quarried raw material into finished construction and machinery output.
9. **Construction-materials manufacturing capacity** specifically for the eastern-highway network, a
   direct, established economic role — Mirny didn't just administer the subnet, it physically built the
   subnet's own roads.
10. **Raw-material processing and shipping infrastructure feeding Sinheung's own chamber-fabrication
    supply chain** — a nationally significant, already-established economic role, distinct from and
    additional to the highway-construction materials output.

## Arcanet Hub Infrastructure

11. **The Relay Hardware Vaults** (established) — the physical subnet-hub relay infrastructure routing
    Zhongshan, Casey, Davis, Vostok, and Kunlun to each other.
12. **Redundancy consideration for the relay hardware as a genuine single point of failure** — the
    established fact that its failure would cut the whole subnet off from itself (not from Concordia,
    since that link was already severed by the Split Brain) makes this one of the highest-stakes single
    facilities in the entire subnet.

## Highway & Maritime Infrastructure

13. **Hwy 110 physical infrastructure** (the Coastal Cut) — Concordia's own closest cross-subnet physical
    link, running west toward Davis/Zhongshan and east toward Casey, with an inland spur reaching
    Concordia directly.
14. **Seasonal harbor infrastructure**, vulnerable to closure by katabatic wind events even within the
    navigable open-water season — a distinct, compounding supply-interruption risk beyond ordinary sea-ice
    seasonality.

## Population-Scale & War-Damage Infrastructure

15. **Large-scale civic infrastructure** sized to a population over 1 million combined, among the largest
    of any DLC city examined so far.
16. **Ongoing damage-assessment and repair infrastructure**, consistent with the established "damaged but
    partially operational" status.

## Integrated Residential-Industrial Housing

17. **Residential space integrated directly inside the same inner buildings as the industrial yards**,
    rather than in a separate residential zone (established) — a genuinely different spatial pattern from
    a zoned city like Sayowa, where industrial and residential space are deliberately kept apart.

## Coexistence-Not-Fusion Cultural Infrastructure

18. **Dual-Kitchen Halls** (established) — physically distinct but co-located Russian and Chinese food
    service infrastructure, kept genuinely separate by design rather than fused, matching the city's own
    stated cultural pattern.

## Cross-Referenced Extrapolation Findings (Methodology #2)

**What this is:** cross-referencing the 18 attributes above against Mirny's own existing lore —
`Mirny_Mega_Init.md`, `Mirny_Full_Extrapolation.md`, and `Mirny_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` (which
already holds 3 Findings of its own, written 2026-07-08). Mirny is DLC 7's own namesake and hub city — the
same structural role Mawson played for DLC 4 — so this pass runs at Mawson's own depth, the deepest
treatment in the Mirny subnet, per the same weighting principle established there. Written 2026-07-30.

### Finding A: Mirny is a quiet, load-bearing link in the national Cradle supply chain despite having zero devotional content

**Combining:** attribute #10 (raw-material processing and shipping infrastructure feeding Sinheung's own
chamber-fabrication supply chain) + attributes #7–9 (quarry-to-yard transit, ore processing, construction-
materials manufacturing) + the project's standing Cradle-unmarked-lore rule (real, factual, never a main-
quest driver).

**2nd-order effect:** Mirny — a purely administrative, industrial subnet hub with no religious or symbolic
register at all — is nonetheless a genuine, physically necessary node in the same national
chamber-manufacturing network that Sinheung and Byrd sit at the visible end of.

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming directly: the Cradle network's supply chain has an unglamorous upstream
half (Mirny's own quarrying and ore processing) that nothing in the existing lore has connected to it
explicitly until this Attributes pass — treated factually here the same way Sinheung's and Denison's own
chamber-manufacturing facts are treated elsewhere in this project.

### Finding B: the relay hardware's single-point-of-failure risk compounds across both of Mirny's "doubled" hub roles

**Combining:** attribute #12 (the Relay Hardware Vaults as a genuine single point of failure for the whole
subnet's internal contact) + attribute #10 (the Sinheung chamber-supply shipping route) + the existing
Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 3 (Mirny's hub role is doubled — administrative/Arcanet *and*
industrial/construction, carrying equal weight).

**2nd-order effect:** if the relay hardware ever failed completely, the consequences wouldn't stop at lost
Arcanet contact — coordination for the Sinheung shipping route and the subnet's own highway-material output
plausibly runs through the same administrative hub, meaning a communications failure could stall physical
industrial output too.

**3rd-order effect:** this makes the Relay Hardware Vaults an even higher-stakes single facility than
Finding 3 alone implies — its failure mode touches both of Mirny's doubled hub functions at once, not just
the communications half.

### Finding C: Mirny's residential-industrial fusion is a third variant of the shared Byrd/Mirny architectural instinct

**Combining:** attribute #17 (residential space integrated directly inside the same buildings as the
industrial yards) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 2 (Mirny and Byrd share an identical
Enneagram profile, expressed as opposite physical directions — Byrd downward, Mirny wrapped outward) + this
session's own Sayowa attributes (a clean, deliberate industrial/residential split, the explicit opposite
pattern).

**2nd-order effect:** Mirny's own residential-industrial fusion is a third data point for the "protect the
vulnerable core" instinct Finding 2 already identifies — where Byrd separates by depth and Sayowa separates
by zone, Mirny doesn't separate at all.

**3rd-order effect:** three genuinely different Tepenian answers to the same underlying design question
(how do you keep people safe next to heavy industry) now exist side by side in the project's own accumulated
lore, worth treating as a real comparative design set for any future city facing the same question.

### Finding D: the coexistence-not-fusion principle operates at two different scales at once

**Combining:** attribute #18 (Dual-Kitchen Halls, physically distinct but co-located Russian and Chinese
food service) + the Full Extrapolation's Section VIII (the corrected Sinheung relationship — modest,
functional, minority-to-minority contact rather than sentimental merger).

**2nd-order effect:** the same civic instinct governs both scales — kept genuinely separate at the small,
daily scale (two kitchens, not one fused menu) and kept modest and functional rather than merged at the
larger, inter-city scale (Mirny's and Sinheung's own Russian-descended minorities).

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming as one consistent civic principle rather than two unrelated facts —
Mirny's own "coexistence, not fusion" isn't just a description of its kitchens, it's the city's operating
philosophy at every scale it shows up at.

### Finding E: the Hwy 110/harbor corridor is where Mirny's strongest self-interest and its worst compounding risk meet

**Combining:** attributes #13–14 (Hwy 110's physical infrastructure and the seasonal harbor, vulnerable to
katabatic closure even within its own navigable season) + the Full Extrapolation's Section I (Hwy 110 kept
passable specifically because Mirny's own remaining industry depends on it).

**2nd-order effect:** the exact corridor Mirny has the strongest self-interested motive to protect (Hwy 110)
runs alongside the exact piece of infrastructure most exposed to compounding, wind-driven closure (the
harbor) — the city's own lifeline and its own greatest vulnerability sit on the same route.

**3rd-order effect:** this gives Mirny's post-war repair priorities a specific, high-stakes geography —
plausibly the single most closely watched corridor in the city, maritime and overland halves both, given how
much of Mirny's own established economic identity depends on keeping it open.

### Finding F: the Antarctic Circle marker is more vulnerable than the tradition it commemorates

**Combining:** attributes #5–6 (the physical Circle marker embedded in the windbreak wall, the Solstice
Grazing Grounds) + the Full Extrapolation's Section III (the solstice observation tradition is genuinely
war-proof, requiring no infrastructure at all to continue).

**2nd-order effect:** a real asymmetry exists between the tradition and its own monument — the observance
itself could survive total infrastructure collapse, but the physical marker commemorating it is ordinary
built infrastructure, vulnerable to exactly the kind of war damage the tradition itself is immune to.

**3rd-order effect:** worth flagging as a distinctive, quietly poignant detail — Mirny could lose the marker
entirely and the solstice-watching would continue unbroken regardless, a small, specific illustration of the
city's own established "practical, function-first, unglamorous" civic character even applied to its one
deliberately symbolic object.

### Finding G: Mirny's wind-engineering expertise is a second, quieter export to Sinheung alongside raw material

**Combining:** attributes #2–3 (airlock entries, structural reinforcement rated for 40+ m/s wind events) +
attribute #10 (the established raw-material shipping relationship to Sinheung) + Sinheung's own shared
general coastal exposure (Larsemann Hills/Prydz Bay cluster, per this session's own Sinheung attributes).

**2nd-order effect:** if Sinheung shares meaningful katabatic wind exposure, Mirny's own extreme-wind
structural engineering expertise is a natural, low-cost second export alongside the material shipments
already established — knowledge transfer riding the same route as physical goods.

**3rd-order effect:** worth flagging as a plausible, currently unexamined second dimension of the
Mirny-Sinheung relationship — not just supplier and customer, but a source of genuinely transferable
engineering knowledge between two cities that already have an established, functioning supply link.

### Finding H: the rename question's larger scope is baked into daily operational language, not just signage

**Combining:** attribute #11 (the Relay Hardware Vaults, the subnet's own physical communications
infrastructure) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 1 (the pending rename touches the whole
six-city subnet's own naming convention, not just the city).

**2nd-order effect:** the Vaults themselves are plausibly referred to in routine technical and
administrative language as "the Mirny subnet relay" — meaning the subnet-name entanglement Finding 1
already flags isn't abstract, it's baked into how the city's own most critical facility is actually talked
about day to day.

**3rd-order effect:** reinforces Finding 1's own point with a concrete example — any future rename
conversation has to account for operational vocabulary already in daily use, not just formal naming
conventions.
