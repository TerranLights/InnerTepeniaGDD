# Kunlun — Full Extrapolation

**What this is:** every open question about Kunlun given an actual proposed answer, built as far as possible directly out of what's already established rather than invented from nothing. Not locked canon — meant for review, revision, or rejection piece by piece. Companion to `Kunlun_Mega_Init.md` in this same folder. Written 2026-07-07.

---

## I. The Founding Human Population's Fate

**Proposed, deliberately distinct from Vostok's own resolution:** unlike Vostok's quiet, passive generational decline, Kunlun's human population's end was more direct and more chosen — a consequence of the same reasoning that later produced the established "human presence forbidden as a protective safety measure" policy (2026-07-05). As the cumulative physiological toll of chronic altitude sickness became undeniable across generations — tracked, ironically, by Kunlun's own established altitude-legacy medical infrastructure — the community itself, or a wider Federation safety authority, eventually made the deliberate call to phase out human habitation here entirely rather than let it continue at real, mounting biological cost. Not a fade, but a considered decision, consistent with a population whose whole civic identity already runs on function and evidence rather than sentiment.

## II. What the Observatory Archive Actually Contains

**Proposed, drawing on NOIRLab's own real "small bodies in the Solar System" research focus:** the archive's core content is centuries of continuous near-Earth object monitoring — asteroid and comet tracking, consistent with the City Vision Notes' own tentative lean toward Mars/Moon/near-Earth focus over distant star systems. Layered onto this, and giving the archive genuine dramatic stakes rather than pure scientific abstraction: Kunlun's observatory is also one of the only facilities in Tepenia positioned to have tracked orbital debris from the Amundsen Tower's own collapse — meaning some portion of the "most scientifically significant open question in the GDD" isn't just abstract astronomy, but a direct, still-untapped record of exactly what happened to Tepenia's own space elevator when it came down.

## III. The Cold Record Contest With Vostok

**Proposed, resolved without either city simply overriding the other:** Kunlun likely does experience colder conditions than Vostok in practice, given its higher elevation — but lacks a confirmed surface measurement the way Vostok's record was actually recorded and verified at a functioning station. Vostok holds the *official*, historically verified record; Kunlun's own conditions are colder in the raw physical sense but were never confirmed by an equivalent surface instrument reading. This mirrors the real-world tension itself (satellite estimates of Dome A approaching −93°C, never confirmed at a surface station) and gives both cities a genuine, non-contradictory claim rather than forcing a single winner.

---

## IV. Scientists and Devotees: A Real Internal Distinction

**Proposed:** astronomers and comms specialists organize their time purely around instrument and signal schedules — task-driven, exactly as already established for the population generally. Ice Cold Buddhism devotees layer something additional onto the same conditions: formal, observed periods of stillness during specific astronomical or atmospheric events, most significantly during the −68°C perfect calm of polar night, treated as a devotional practice rather than simply a working condition to schedule around. This gives the two groups a genuinely lived, visible difference in how they relate to the *same* extreme environment — one purely functional, one also reverent — without making them adversarial; a scientist and a devotee can be doing the same task at the same instrument for entirely different reasons, and both readings are true at once.

## VII. The Observatory's Own History of Expansion

**Proposed, drawing directly on CHESS/CHEXS's real pattern of periodic major facility additions:** Kunlun's observatory has undergone at least two significant expansion phases over its roughly five centuries of Tepenian operation, rather than remaining a single unchanged instrument since founding. An early expansion plausibly coincided with the population's own convergence into a genuinely multinational specialist community, as newly-arrived astronomers and comms engineers from different national space-heritage traditions brought new instrumentation approaches with them. A later expansion plausibly followed Tepenia's own achievement of a real orbital-era presence, since the comms-relay function would have needed genuine scaling once there was actual orbital infrastructure worth staying in contact with. This gives "five centuries of accumulated observatory data" real institutional texture — a facility that grew in distinct, datable phases, not a static instrument passively accumulating readings.

## Worth Your Attention

Section I is the most load-bearing proposal here — giving Kunlun's own founding-population resolution a genuinely different shape from Vostok's (a considered, evidence-driven decision rather than a passive fade) keeps the two extreme-isolation cities from reading as the same story told twice, despite sharing the "100%-robot, human population gone" structural fact. Section II is the second most important — tying the observatory archive directly to the Amundsen Tower's own collapse gives Kunlun's still-unwritten DLC story beat a genuine, already-established connective thread to the game's central lore stakes, rather than leaving it as an unconnected astronomy detail.
