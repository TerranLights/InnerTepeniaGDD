# Shirayuki — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Shirayuki would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Shirayuki.md`, `Local_Cultures/Mirny_Subnet/Shirayuki.md`, and
`Shirayuki_Community_Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred
until this pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30.

**Governing facts:** built on the former Bharati Station site (India-built, never Indian-settled — see the
project's own No-Subcontinentals canon), in the Larsemann Hills alongside Sinheung and Zhongshan — a small
ice-free oasis, mild by Tepenian standards, with meltwater lakes and Prydz Bay access; founded not
organically but through a deliberate pre-exile diplomatic allocation (the Jeju-do court), making Japan the
city's Primary-tier population (36.27%) specifically to prevent regional Chinese demographic pressure from
claiming the site by default; status is damaged but partially operational; economy centers on education,
research, and — developed organically over centuries, not part of the founding intention — an outsized
amateur arts, music, and fashion culture; population is large (~730k-1.18M).

---

## Repurposed Research/Educational Infrastructure

1. **The Bharati Gallery Halls** (established) — former research labs converted into gallery and studio
   space, the direct physical throughline from Bharati Station's own original research function.
2. **The Shirayuki Institute of Applied and Fine Arts** (established) — the city's flagship university,
   oriented around highly technical art and design sub-fields, drawing students from across Tepenia.
3. **Inherited 2010s-era Indian-built station infrastructure**, the newest design in the Larsemann Hills
   cluster, repurposed entirely by a founding population diplomatically allocated rather than organically
   settled — a genuine architectural mismatch between the infrastructure's own original design intent and
   the population actually using it.

## Ice-Free Oasis Terrain (shared cluster geography)

4. **Foundation and structural engineering on exposed bedrock and meltwater-lake terrain**, the Larsemann
   Hills' own smaller-scale version of the same ice-free-oasis engineering problem Davis faces in the
   Vestfold Hills.

## Arts, Music & Fashion Infrastructure

5. **The Ashiato Ateliers** (established) — informal studios where residents assemble, alter, and
   personalize homemade Alt-Rock/Alt-Grunge/Alt-Pop garments.
6. **Glacier-Pop Living Rooms** (established) — informal, friends-and-neighbors performance spaces where
   the city's massive amateur music scene actually happens, distinct from formal conservatory space.
7. **A downtown gallery district** (established) — sculptures, murals, and statues populating every
   district, densest in the downtown core.
8. **Deliberate urban-planning and streetscape aesthetics** — the established "clean, orderly streets"
   reading as an outdoor gallery imply real civic investment in aesthetic infrastructure, not purely
   functional city planning.

## Civic & Diplomatic-Founding Infrastructure

9. **The Founders' Garden** (established) — the physical site tied to the observance celebrating the
   city's research, education, and arts identity.
10. **An institutional marker for the city's own diplomatically-engineered founding** — given the
    established civic observance ("The Allocation," commemorating the Jeju-do court's decision), some
    physical civic site plausibly anchors this specific, unusual founding story, distinct from the more
    common organic-settlement narrative most Tepenian cities tell about themselves.

## Cluster Relationship Infrastructure (Shirayuki-Sinheung-Zhongshan)

11. **The Cluster Boundary Walk** (established) — an informal path crossing directly into Sinheung's and
    Zhongshan's own territory.
12. **Shared or border-adjacent communal gathering infrastructure**, consistent with the established
    "Cluster Suppers" tradition — extended, unhurried shared meals crossing into neighboring cluster-city
    territory, physically enacting the slow multi-generational warming between the three cities.

## Education-Driven Migration Infrastructure

13. **Substantial student/transient housing infrastructure**, distinct from purely local-population
    housing — the established pattern of people arriving specifically to study at the Institute means a
    real, ongoing transient population the city's infrastructure has to accommodate.
14. **Institute Placement infrastructure** (established: Institute Placement Nights) — transition support
    connecting graduating students into the city's own Ashiato and Glacier-Pop cultural circles.

## Mild-Climate Infrastructure

15. **Comparatively reduced cold-engineering burden**, given the Larsemann Hills' own mild climate relative
    to most Tepenian cities — freeing real civic resources and design attention toward the education and
    arts economy rather than pure environmental survival infrastructure.

## War-Damage Infrastructure

16. **Ongoing repair infrastructure**, consistent with the established "damaged but partially operational"
    status.
