## Who Lives Here, and Why

Mirny subnet's founding populations arrived through a genuinely wider range of mechanisms than any other subnet examined so far — organic multi-generational settlement (Zhongshan, Davis), a deliberate function-based curated draw (Kunlun), a demographic accident unconnected to any founding legend (Vostok), and pre-exile diplomatic engineering at the Jeju-do Court (Shirayuki *and* the currently-unnamed Korean city, two nations allocated separate sites by the same treaty) *(corrected 2026-07-13 — previously listed the currently-unnamed Korean city under its own separate "genuinely doubled dual founding" mechanism; wrong, its actual founding is singularly Korean via the same Jeju-do allocation as Shirayuki)* — four distinct mechanisms across eight cities, more variety in how a population actually forms than this project has documented in any other single subnet.

This produces a subnet whose demographic story resists a single throughline the way Halley subnet's founding-nation diversity or Janbogo/Ross subnet's Pacific-corridor concentration each did — instead, Mirny subnet's real defining demographic fact is the sheer number of different processes sitting side by side, from Zhongshan's unbroken Chinese continuity (the only Tepenian city where the founding operator nation never lost Primary status) to Kunlun's fully re-resolved, nationality-neutral specialist population, with nearly every other possible mechanism represented somewhere in between.

## What It Feels Like

Moving through Mirny subnet means moving between wildly different registers of extremity and ordinariness within a genuinely short administrative distance. Mirny itself announces the subnet's core industrial character immediately — a fortified windbreak ring wrapped around huge, still-running industrial yards, rough and unapologetically plain. Casey's ruins mark the subnet's one clean loss, a highway junction destroyed mid-transformation, never finishing whatever it was in the process of becoming. Davis sits quietly competent on top of the most varied terrain in Tepenia, content to matter without needing anyone to notice. Then the interior: Vostok's genetics archive too vast for any mind to hold, and Kunlun's observatory-city where the sky and the signal never stop, the two loneliest outposts in the whole Federation, sharing equipment requests and quiet mutual dependency across Hwy 37. And at the subnet's western edge, the Larsemann Hills cluster — Zhongshan's deep, unhurried, singularly Chinese continuity *(corrected 2026-07-13, was "Sino-Russian synthesis" — wrong; Zhongshan's culture is singularly Chinese, never a synthesis with Russia, which was only ever the physical Progress Station's operator, not a founding population)*, the currently-unnamed Korean city's sharp, forward-looking ambition, and Shirayuki's clean, art-filled, genuinely easy warmth — three cities that took a century and a half to grow into one shared neighborhood, now permanently marked by the same wartime strike that hit all three at once.

## Personality (A Whole-Subnet Read)

Mirny subnet doesn't reduce to one Enneagram profile any more than Concordia's districts or the other subnets' cities did, but its own recurring pattern is unusually structural: a subnet whose defining civic instinct, across a genuine majority of its eight cities, is competence that never needed an audience. Mirny's own pride is explicitly unforced. Davis's whole civic identity is triple-reinforced as an "enabler" that serves without asking to be seen. The currently-unnamed Korean city carries genuinely load-bearing national infrastructure behind a modest political profile. Vostok and Kunlun each hold research too vast for any one mind, pursued because the work itself is worth doing, not for recognition. Where Halley subnet's collective personality was Competency turned inward and Janbogo/Ross subnet's was Survival read as a verdict, Mirny subnet's collective personality is closer to **Function outliving the need for credit** — a subnet built, city after city, around doing something genuinely consequential and treating the question of whether anyone notices as beside the point.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Wilkes Land and the Larsemann Hills' genuine multinational density

The real Larsemann Hills sector hosts research stations from China, Russia, and (via the historical Bharati site) India within a strikingly compact area — a genuine geographic and diplomatic convergence point, mirroring this subnet's own Zhongshan/currently-unnamed-Korean-city/Shirayuki cluster and its unusually eventful founding history (organic Chinese settlement confirmed by treaty, and two separate Jeju-do diplomatic allocations to Korea and Japan, all within a few kilometers of each other) *(corrected 2026-07-13, was "dual founding" — see the currently-unnamed Korean city's own corrected founding story)*.

**What this gives the subnet:** direct grounding for why three cities this geographically close together could carry three such different founding stories and still end up genuinely intertwined — the real Larsemann Hills' own multinational compactness is the structural seed this cluster's fictional amalgamation history grows out of.

### Secondary: NOIRLab, ChemMatCARS, and Materials Innovation Platforms (already researched for Kunlun and Vostok specifically)

Applies now at subnet scale — both extreme-isolation research outposts independently picked up real-world precedent for shared, cross-institutional scientific infrastructure, and this Ultra-Megasheet's own Throughway 3 confirms that shared-infrastructure model wasn't just thematic parallel but extended into an actual historical joint venture (Mountain Pass) with direct consequences for the game's own central Tower-collapse lore.

### Secondary: Yakutsk, Nizhny Tagil, and Wolfsburg (already researched for Mirny specifically)

Give the subnet's own hub city concrete real-world grounding for its "windbreak enclosing an industrial core" architecture and its "competence over romance" civic ethos. *(Corrected 2026-07-13: this used to also cite "Volgograd's own precedent for the currently-unnamed Korean city's deliberate, meaning-loaded self-naming" — wrong on two counts: the city never chose its own name, so there's no self-naming precedent to draw on, and the Volgograd pick itself is separately flagged as needing review in the city's own Mega-Init, precisely because its original rationale rested on this same now-corrected premise. Both hub-adjacent cities in this subnet still carry real-world-grounded identities organized around function rather than performance, on their own separate merits.)*

### Supporting: Flagstaff, Memphis, and Reno (already researched for Casey specifically)

Give the subnet's one destroyed city a genuine interrupted arc rather than a flat loss — Casey's own destruction, per this Ultra-Megasheet's own Pattern 2 and Throughway 4, fits cleanly into the subnet's single-principle war-damage logic, but its own Full Extrapolation gives that single strategic fact real emotional weight: a city cut down mid-transformation between two recognizable real-world models.
