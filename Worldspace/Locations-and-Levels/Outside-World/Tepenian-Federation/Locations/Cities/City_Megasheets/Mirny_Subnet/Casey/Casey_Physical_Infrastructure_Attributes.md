# Casey — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Casey would logically have had,
built directly from `Specs/Casey.md`, `Local_Cultures/Mirny_Subnet/Casey.md`, and `Casey_Community_
Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred until this pass is
complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30.

**Casey's status is Destroyed, not damaged-but-operational** — everything below is framed pre-war, per the
same convention `Casey_Community_Infrastructure.md` already established, since this is directly relevant
to Casey's diaspora in Concordia and to any surviving community members the player might meet who hold this
as lived memory, not abstract history.

**Governing facts:** coastal, Budd Coast/Wilkes Land, just north of the Antarctic Circle (66°16'S) — no
true polar night, but very short winter days (~5-6 hours of low-angle light); Casey's whole defining
identity was its position at the junction of Hwy 110 (Concordia/Mirny) and Hwy 2/DCH (Dumont d'Urville) —
the last coastal city before the deep interior drive; economy was built entirely on transit — resupply,
rest, information exchange, not production or destination tourism; the Wilkes Station ruins, a genuine
pre-exile American research site, sat a few kilometers away, already a historical curiosity before Casey's
own destruction; population was large (Census I ~1.5 million), inherited from Australia's largest Antarctic
station; climate combined katabatic winds off the interior with maritime storm systems off the sea, a dual
exposure milder than Mirny's but genuinely distinct from a purely interior or purely coastal profile;
seasonal maritime access ran roughly November-March.

---

## Highway Junction Infrastructure (the defining feature)

1. **The Hwy 110/Hwy 2 junction itself** — the literal geographic and economic center of the city, a
   physical decision point for every traveler moving between the coast and the deep interior.
2. **Waystation Row** (established) — the string of resupply depots and transit lodging strung directly
   along the junction.

## Transit & Waystation Economy Infrastructure

3. **Rapid goods turnover and resupply depot infrastructure**, built for speed and volume rather than
   local production — the whole city's economy ran on things passing through, not things made here.
4. **The Junction Dispatch Office** (established) — where road-condition dispatchers coordinated
   katabatic/wind-window forecasts and travel-safety information subnet-wide, a genuine information-hub
   function distinct from the physical resupply infrastructure.
5. **Fast-turnover hospitality infrastructure**, distinct from a destination city's own hospitality —
   built for efficient, high-volume service to travelers passing through rather than for guests staying to
   experience the city itself.

## Maritime & Seasonal Access

6. **Seasonal harbor infrastructure**, operating on a roughly November-March navigable window — this
   directly determined Casey's own supply and visitor patterns.
7. **Substantial original maritime receiving infrastructure**, inherited from Casey's own pre-exile role as
   Australia's largest, most modern Antarctic station — genuinely capable of handling a wide, early mix of
   exile-era arrivals by sea.

## Entertainment & Social Infrastructure

8. **Splinters** (established) — a massive bar and live-music venue occupying a full city block, with a
   stage, dance floor, and seating along the flanks and rear.
9. **Splinters functioned partly as transit infrastructure in its own right** — given the established fact
   that its fame traveled the whole coastline "the same way everything about Casey travels — by the
   constant flow of people passing through," the venue plausibly doubled as an information and culture
   distribution point, not simply entertainment.

## Historical/Curiosity Infrastructure

10. **The Wilkes Curiosity Walk** (established) — an informal path connecting Casey to the older,
    already-buried Wilkes Station ruins.
11. **Some form of boundary marking or distinction between Casey proper and the Wilkes ruins** — given
    Wilkes predates Tepenia entirely and was already a curiosity before Casey's own destruction, residents
    plausibly maintained some clear sense of where "their" city ended and the older, non-Tepenian ruin
    began.

## Climate-Adapted Structural Engineering

12. **Structures engineered against a dual wind source** — katabatic flow off the interior plateau
    combined with maritime storm systems off the sea, a genuinely different combined-exposure problem from
    Mirny's own purely katabatic-dominated profile.
13. **Infrastructure built around liminal, low-angle winter light rather than full polar darkness** — with
    no true polar night at this latitude, Casey never needed the kind of full artificial-lighting solution
    an interior city like Concordia or Byrd requires, only supplemental lighting for the shortest, dimmest
    days.

## Population-Scale Infrastructure

14. **Large-scale civic infrastructure**, inherited from and scaled to Australia's largest, best-equipped
    pre-exile Antarctic station — genuinely substantial by Tepenian standards even before accounting for
    exile-era growth.

## Present-Day Ruins

15. **The ruins themselves now function as a literal physical obstacle** — since Hwy 2 runs directly
    through the destroyed city, Casey's own wreckage doesn't just mark a loss, it's the actual reason the
    route is severed at its source rather than merely disconnected further down the line.
