# Mirny Subnet — Cross-City Patterns

**What this is:** common factors recurring across multiple Mirny subnet cities — not causal chains (see Cross-City Throughways for those), but shared traits, structures, or tensions that show up independently in more than one city's own Megasheet. Several of these were already partially self-identified within individual cities' own Cross-Reference Syntheses; the job here is to consolidate and extend that work across the full subnet, following the same process used for Palmer, Halley, and Janbogo/Ross subnets. Written 2026-07-10.

---

## Pattern 1: Five distinct mechanisms produce a "multinational" city, all sitting side by side in one subnet

Kunlun's own Cross-Reference Finding 1 explicitly flagged this as worth checking across Tepenia's other multinational cities. Running that check across all eight Mirny subnet cities produces four genuinely distinct mechanisms, not one. *(Corrected 2026-07-13: this section, written 2026-07-10, originally listed "genuinely doubled dual founding" as Sinheung's own separate, fifth mechanism — wrong. The city's actual founding story, resolved the same day this was corrected, is singularly Korean via the Jeju-do court's diplomatic allocation, the identical mechanism already listed here for Shirayuki. The two cities now merge into one shared mechanism category below, dropping the taxonomy from five to four.)*

- **Organic multi-generational immigration:** Zhongshan (a fully-formed founding culture that later arrivals simply integrated into) and Davis (a genuinely dispersed 13-nation Notable tier, no single community concentrated enough to dominate).
- **Deliberate, function-based curated draw:** Kunlun — re-resolved into a 19-nation population selected specifically for real space/astronomy/comms credentials, not organic settlement.
- **Demographic-redistribution accident:** Vostok — its current population arrived generations after founding, via a later administrative resettlement unconnected to the city's own founding legend, with zero ancestral connection to the Lake Vostok research mission itself.
- **Pre-exile diplomatic engineering:** Shirayuki *and* Sinheung — both settled by the same International Court of Diplomacy at Jeju-do before the exile era even began, allocating Japan and Korea their own separate sites specifically to prevent China's regional dominance at adjacent Zhongshan from defaulting into an uncontested third claim. Two cities, one shared mechanism, two different beneficiary nations.
- **(A fifth, hybrid case) Casey's "wide, mixed early wave":** a junction city that received unusually broad settlement from the start rather than one dominant founding population diluted later — distinct from Davis's more gradual dispersal, since Casey's diversity was present from founding rather than accumulating over generations.

**What this produces:** Mirny subnet alone contains a near-complete taxonomy of every mechanism by which a Tepenian city's population composition can form — worth using as the reference set any future city's own demographic-formation story should be checked against, the same way Halley subnet's damage-mechanism taxonomy and Janbogo/Ross subnet's destruction-logic taxonomy now serve their own respective questions.

## Pattern 2: A subnet-wide war-damage logic that resolves to exactly one principle — unlike Janbogo/Ross subnet's two

Janbogo/Ross subnet's own war-damage pattern required two explanatory principles (ongoing usefulness vs. punitive/symbolic intent). Mirny subnet's own pattern is structurally simpler — every documented case traces to strategic infrastructure value alone, with no punitive or deterrent-message component anywhere in the subnet:

| City | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Casey** | Destroyed | A genuine dual highway chokepoint (Hwy 2 running *through*, not just to, the city) — one strike severing two supply corridors at once (Casey Full Extrapolation, Section I) |
| **Zhongshan / Sinheung / Shirayuki cluster** | Damaged, functional | A targeted strike on the shared tri-junction node, aimed at Zhongshan's precision-manufacturing and Sinheung's chamber-manufacturing capacity specifically, not at the cluster's population broadly (Zhongshan Full Extrapolation, Section IV; Sinheung Full Extrapolation, Section V) |
| **Mirny** | Damaged, survived | Genuine subnet-hub value (communications and industrial both), but its own windbreak-ring architecture and dispersed function meant no single strike could eliminate it outright |
| **Davis** | Damaged, partially operational *(corrected 2026-07-14 — this row said "Survived, undamaged," contradicting `Specs/Davis.md`'s own status, corrected 2026-07-03; Davis's own Megasheet files establish no specific damage cause, but its feeder/enabler role plausibly kept it a lower-priority target than a full population center, consistent with the reachability/infrastructure-value logic here — struck, but not worth destroying outright, unlike Casey)* | A feeder/enabler role, secondary to a full population center — raw materials out, no final-stage strategic capacity of its own worth destroying outright |
| **Vostok, Kunlun** | Survived, undamaged | Extreme remoteness and marginal physical reachability — even genuine strategic value (Vostok's archive, Kunlun's comms relay) wasn't worth the resource cost of reaching either one |

**What this produces:** a real, checkable contrast between two subnets' own war-damage logic — Janbogo/Ross subnet needed a punitive/symbolic principle to explain Zukelli and Cape Adare, while every Mirny subnet case explains cleanly through infrastructure value and reachability alone. This is worth treating as a genuine finding about the war's own conduct, not just a coincidence of which cities happened to get hit — Upper Earth's targeting in this subnet reads as more narrowly, consistently strategic than its targeting in Janbogo/Ross.

## Pattern 3: The "quietly load-bearing, uncredited infrastructure" thread recurs a third time, confirming it as project-wide rather than subnet-specific

Davis's own triple-reinforced "enabler" identity (namesake, real-world precedent, current economy all converging independently), Sinheung's own established "quietly load-bearing infrastructure... despite modest political profile" chamber-manufacturing role, and Mirny's own doubled hub function (communications *and* industrial, a heavier load than Janbogo's purely cultural hub role carries) are three separate instances of the same underlying shape already identified in Halley subnet (Neumayer, Sanay) and Janbogo/Ross subnet (Janbogo's witness role).

**What this produces:** this is no longer a subnet-specific curiosity — with a third subnet now producing independent examples, "does genuinely consequential work and receives none of the visible credit for it" is confirmed as one of this entire Megasheet project's real, recurring civic themes, worth checking for in Mawson subnet's own upcoming Ultra-Megasheet as a near-default expectation rather than a surprising find.

## Pattern 4: Deliberate, meaning-loaded renaming as a recurring civic act

Three of the subnet's eight cities carry an active or historical "the name itself carries chosen weight" arc:

- **Sinheung (신흥):** *(corrected 2026-07-13 — this bullet previously said the city "self-named once already at founding, choosing a spacecraft's name over an inherited station name specifically to signal ambition"; wrong. "Soyuz" was never the founders' own choice at all — a leftover label tied to the physical station's Russian namesake. The founders never named their own city, until now.)* Officially named 2026-07-14 — "newly rising/emerging," the city's very first deliberate act of self-naming, not a second one, closing a gap that had stood since founding.
- **Shirayuki:** carried a placeholder name for the entire Megasheet compilation process, then was freshly, officially named (白雪, "white snow") mid-project.
- **Mirny:** flagged for an eventual rename, since its current Russian-namesake name no longer matches its own China-Primary/Russia-Significant demographic composition.

**What this produces:** naming in this subnet is unusually active as a civic and narrative mechanism compared to most of Tepenia, where a city's name is typically just an inherited fact. Sinheung's own Cross-Reference Finding 3 already proposed that its first-ever self-naming should carry real, deliberate weight, precisely because it closes a 130-year-old gap rather than continuing an established pattern — a standard borne out directly once the name itself, Sinheung, was actually chosen — worth extending that same standard to Mirny's own eventual rename, given the two cities sit in the same subnet and now share the same underlying civic principle.

## Pattern 5: Mirny and Byrd solve the identical personality profile in architecturally opposite directions (established finding, carried forward)

Already fully developed in Mirny's own Cross-Reference Synthesis (Finding 2): Mirny and Byrd share the exact same Enneagram profile (Instinctive/Withdrawn/Competency), expressed as a windbreak ring wrapping *around* an industrial core (Mirny) versus a city built *downward* before it ever stood on the surface (Byrd) — the same psychological shape, solved along opposite spatial axes depending on which specific environmental threat each city actually faces. Carried forward here rather than re-derived, since it's already a complete, standalone finding.

## Pattern 6: Vostok and Kunlun's "two loneliest outposts" relationship is a genuine, concrete infrastructure partnership, not just emotional companionship

Already substantially developed within both cities' own Full Extrapolations and Cross-References — a real, mutual resource-sharing relationship (modeled on ChemMatCARS's shared-infrastructure precedent) that historically included a physical joint venture, Mountain Pass, staffed by both cities' own scientists and engineers together. Developed further in Throughway 1 and Throughway 3, below, since this relationship reaches directly into the game's central Amundsen Tower lore and the player character's own personal origin.
