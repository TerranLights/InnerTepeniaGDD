## Who Lives Here, and Why

This city's founding is unlike any other in Tepenia: it exists because Japan, Korea, and China settled the question of who would claim this unoccupied site *before the exile era ever began*, at the Jeju-do court, specifically to prevent China's overwhelming regional presence (immediately adjacent at Zhongshan) from defaulting into an uncontested third claim across the whole Larsemann Hills cluster. Japanese exiles arrived in 2564 inheriting a claim that had already been settled rather than working one out themselves.

That diplomatic engineering held. After a 2026-07-06 correction to the standard population methodology (which had ironically buried Japan behind China anyway, the exact outcome the allocation was meant to prevent), Japan sits solidly Primary at 36.27% — proof, in the numbers themselves, that the Jeju-do decision actually worked as intended across more than a century of exile. This gives the city's whole demographic story a genuinely different shape from its cluster neighbor Sayowa — Tepenia's *other* Japanese city, which inherited its own real JARE station organically and, unlike this city, saw its Japanese founding character diluted by the same regional Chinese pull this city's diplomatic allocation was specifically built to resist.

## What It Feels Like

Clean, orderly streets throughout — cleanliness as genuine civic pride, not just upkeep. Art is everywhere: sculptures, murals, statues, and constructed pieces populate every district, densest in the downtown core, where walking through the city center means walking through an outdoor gallery as much as a functioning city. That art is a genuine mix of civically-commissioned and independent, grassroots work — downtown skews commissioned, residential and informal districts skew amateur.

People dress very fashionably here, but explicitly not in a runway or high-fashion register — Alternative, Alt-Rock, Alt-Grunge, Alt-Pop, and genuinely homemade: assembled, altered, and personalized rather than bought polished off a rack. A massive amateur culture of people making art and music entirely on their own time runs alongside real institutional depth in research and education. The city's schools are excellent, but people come from across Tepenia specifically for the culture and soul of the place, not academic ranking alone. It's genuinely, uncomplicatedly easy to make friends here.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Shibuya, Tokyo

Once rice fields, transformed by a train station (1885) and international attention from the 1964 Olympics into Tokyo's actual birthplace of street fashion — a shift explicitly framed as young Japanese people taking Western-inspired looks and remaking them into something entirely new, rather than simple imitation. Home to the gyaru subculture, itself a direct, visible rebellion against conservative beauty standards (modified school uniforms, bold makeup, deliberate departure from tradition), and to Shibuya-kei, a genre blending retro French pop, bossa nova, and jazz into something "sophisticated yet playful, vintage-inspired."

**What this gives the city:** a real precedent for exactly the transformation this city's own Post-Culture Identity already claims — an externally-introduced influence (Western fashion, in Shibuya's case; a diplomatic decree, in this city's case) reworked by the people actually living there into something authentically their own, not merely borrowed. Shibuya-kei's specific "eclectic fusion, not pure imitation" quality is a concrete texture worth lending to this city's own amateur music culture.

### Primary: Harajuku, Tokyo

Administratively part of Shibuya's own ward, and the real birthplace of kawaii culture — a dense mix of distinct subcultures (Lolita, Gothic Lolita, Kogal, Decora, Visual Kei, and more) that crystallized specifically in the 1990s. Its identity as a fashion district traces directly back to the post-occupation era, when proximity to an American military base introduced Western cultural influence that young Japanese residents then transformed into something distinctly their own — documented and globalized decades later by FRUiTS magazine.

**What this gives the city:** the single strongest structural parallel of all four picks. Harajuku's own origin story — a creative identity born directly from an *external, imposed* circumstance (a foreign military base) that residents nonetheless made completely their own — is close to a one-to-one echo of this city's founding tension (imposed by Jeju-do diplomacy, then organically inhabited over generations). It's also worth noting that Harajuku and Shibuya are themselves two distinct, adjacent creative districts often perceived from outside as a single scene — a small but genuine parallel to this city's own eventual physical merger with Zhongshan and Sinheung into one continuous, if legally separate, urban cluster.

### Secondary: Hongdae, Seoul

Grew directly out of Hongik University's Fine Arts program drawing broke, ambitious young artists into the area's then-cheap rents through the 1990s — Korea's actual indie music scene traces to two specific bands that started there, and a single 1999 legal change (legalizing live performances in bars) turned informal, scattered performance into a real, sanctioned, lasting music scene. Extensive street art and mural culture ("Picasso's Street") grew alongside it, explicitly positioned against mainstream, corporate, sanitized culture.

**What this gives the city:** a concrete origin mechanism for its own established "schools draw people for the culture, not the ranking" trait — Hongdae shows exactly how a respected academic institution can seed a much larger amateur creative scene almost as a side effect, as students (and the broke young creatives drawn by the same institution's reputation and cheap surrounding rents) stay and keep making art long after or entirely apart from their formal studies. Hongdae's specific 1999 legalization moment is also worth adapting directly: a plausible, concrete point in this city's own history where local policy caught up to and formally protected what had already become a genuine grassroots performance culture.

### Secondary: Novosibirsk, Russia

Unofficially "the Capital of Siberia" — a major transport, industrial, scientific, and cultural hub, but the detail that matters most here is Akademgorodok: a dedicated science city, deliberately constructed by Soviet government decision in 1957, now home to over 35 research institutes and universities and the headquarters of the Siberian Division of the Russian Academy of Sciences.

**What this gives the city:** direct, real-world confirmation that a genuinely thriving research and educational culture can emerge from a *deliberately, institutionally planned* origin rather than organic growth — exactly this city's own founding story. Novosibirsk/Akademgorodok is proof that "built by decree" and "became a real, living center of excellence" aren't in tension with each other; they're a documented real-world pattern, directly reinforcing the established Post-Culture Identity theme (*"A Place Decided For You, Made Into a Place You'd Choose"*) with genuine historical weight behind it.

## II. The Jeju-do Allocation, Dated and Characterized

**Proposed:** the allocation decision was made a matter of years, not decades, before the Falkland Treaty (2564) — close enough to be genuinely part of "the same broader diplomatic process," not a separate, much earlier event. **Proposed as not hotly contested at the time:** nothing established suggests a crisis or standoff, and the underlying logic supports a fairly clean, low-friction agreement — China's own claim to Zhongshan was never actually at risk in this negotiation, only the third, still-unclaimed site was in question, meaning Japan taking it cost China comparatively little in real terms, even if it closed off a demographic opportunity China might otherwise have had by default. A resigned, practical acceptance rather than a fought concession is the most plausible reading.

## Finding 1: the city's founding tension is a documented pattern, not a one-time origin fact

**Combining:** the established Post-Culture Identity theme, "A Place Decided For You, Made Into a Place You'd Choose" (city founded by external Jeju-do decree, later genuinely inhabited) + Harajuku's real-world origin story (a fashion identity born directly from an external, imposed circumstance — a foreign military base — later fully claimed by the people who actually lived there).

**2nd-order effect:** this city's whole civic arc — imposed origin transformed into genuinely owned identity — isn't a unique, invented theme specific to this one city. It's a documented real-world pattern this research pass found independent confirmation for, meaning the theme has more structural weight behind it than an isolated creative choice would.

**3rd-order effect:** since this same "imposed, then genuinely claimed" shape shows up at the level of the founding population's national identity, it's worth asking whether the same pattern recurs at other scales within the city's own history — the Full Extrapolation's proposal that the founding community adapted (rather than replaced) the originally research-oriented Bharati Station infrastructure into today's arts-and-education city is itself a second instance of the same shape: inherited, externally-designed infrastructure, later made into something the city actually chose to become.

**4th-order effect:** if "imposed, then claimed" is a genuinely repeating structural feature of this city's history — its founding population, and its physical infrastructure — that raised a real question, at the time this was written, about whether its still-undecided proper name would eventually follow the same pattern. *(Corrected 2026-07-13: it didn't have to — the name was chosen directly, Shirayuki, the very next day, 2026-07-08, rather than emerging gradually the way this pattern would predict. The "imposed, then claimed" shape held for the founding population and the inherited infrastructure, but the name itself broke the pattern by simply being decided.)*

---

## Finding 2: the "schools draw people for the culture" reputation may be self-seeding, not two separate facts

**Combining:** the established fact that this city's schools draw students from across Tepenia specifically for "the culture and soul of the place," not academic ranking + the Full Extrapolation's Hongdae-derived proposal that a respected academic institution can seed a much larger amateur creative scene as a side effect of the people it draws + the already-established massive amateur art/music culture running alongside the city's institutional depth.

**2nd-order effect:** the schools and the amateur culture aren't necessarily two separate, coincidentally adjacent civic features — the amateur culture plausibly grew, at least in part, from earlier generations of exactly the kind of students the schools attract, who stayed on and kept making art and music independent of their formal studies.

**3rd-order effect:** if that's true, the city's own "people come here for the culture" reputation is partly self-reinforcing rather than a fixed, independent fact — the culture that draws new students was itself built by previous waves of students who came for reasons that included, in part, an earlier version of that same culture.

**4th-order effect:** a self-reinforcing loop like this depends on genuine, ongoing retention — some meaningful fraction of nationwide students actually staying in the city after their studies rather than returning home — which is a specific, checkable fact nothing currently established actually confirms or denies. The loop's continued strength as an explanation is only as solid as that unconfirmed retention rate.

**Resolved 2026-07-30:** the retention rate this finding flagged as unconfirmed is now settled — the city's flagship university (the Shirayuki Institute of Applied and Fine Arts, per `Local_Cultures/Mirny_Subnet/Shirayuki.md` Section 7 and `Shirayuki_Community_Infrastructure.md`) is explicitly used by many students, both human and robot, as a deliberate stepping stone toward integrating into the local arts and music culture, not merely a course of study to complete before leaving. This confirms genuine, intentional retention exists, and the self-reinforcing loop this finding proposed holds up as more than a hypothesis.

## Finding 3: this city is one of Tepenia's few real tests of whether deliberately engineered civic outcomes can rival organic ones

*(Corrected 2026-07-13: this finding originally called Shirayuki "the only Tepenian city founded via institutional diplomatic engineering" — wrong. Sinheung was founded via the identical Jeju-do allocation, to Korea rather than Japan. Corrected below to two cities, not one, without weakening the finding's actual argument.)*

**Combining:** the established fact that Japan's Primary-tier demographic status held and was, per the 2026-07-06 correction, explicitly "vindicated" against the regional Chinese pull it was designed to resist + Novosibirsk's Akademgorodok as real-world proof that a deliberately, top-down constructed research/education city can achieve genuine depth and excellence, not just organic ones + the established fact that this city and Sinheung are the only two Tepenian cities founded via institutional diplomatic engineering rather than organic real-station inheritance.

**2nd-order effect:** this city represents a double vindication of engineered outcomes over organic chance — both the demographic allocation itself (Japan remaining Primary) and the resulting civic identity (genuine research and arts depth, not a hollow or purely administrative city) worked out as intended, matching Akademgorodok's own real-world precedent that top-down design can produce authentic excellence rather than a lesser imitation of organic growth.

**3rd-order effect:** since virtually every other Tepenian city traces its founding to organic real-station inheritance, this city and Sinheung occupy a genuinely distinctive position in the setting as a whole — the only two concrete, already-resolved test cases for whether deliberate institutional planning can produce outcomes as real and lasting as organic development, rather than a purely theoretical question. The two cases actually agree, strengthening the finding rather than complicating it: both allocated nations ended up genuinely Primary-tier (Japan 36.27% here, Korea 34.62% at Sinheung) — the same Jeju-do mechanism, applied twice to two different nations, produced the same kind of vindicated outcome both times.

**4th-order effect:** if any broader Federation-level conversation about planning versus organic growth were ever to need a real precedent to point to, this city is the one place in Tepenia that could genuinely be cited as evidence either way — a small, specific point of connection between this one city's own founding story and any larger governance or infrastructure-planning discourse elsewhere in the setting.

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across All Three Findings

All three findings trace back to the same underlying observation: this city keeps demonstrating that things imposed from outside — a founding population, a physical infrastructure, a demographic allocation — can become genuinely, authentically the city's own, not despite their external origin but in some cases directly through processes (like the schools-to-amateur-culture loop in Finding 2) that only work because the external element was there to begin with. Finding 1 shows this at the level of theme and infrastructure. Finding 2 shows a specific, testable mechanism for how it plays out in one civic sector. Finding 3 shows the city functioning as a genuine, documented test case for the same question at a much larger scale.

- **The Shirayuki Institute of Applied and Fine Arts** — the city's flagship university, oriented around highly-technical sub-fields of art and design in their many specific forms; comparable in scope but distinct in focus to Sinheung's smaller university (materials, fabrication, production). - **Glacier-Pop Living Rooms** — the informal, friends-and-neighbors performance spaces where the city's massive amateur music scene actually happens, distinct from its formal conservatories.
- **The Bharati Gallery Halls** — former research labs converted into gallery and studio space, the throughline from Bharati Station's original research infrastructure.

- **The Cluster Boundary Walk** — an informal path crossing into Sinheung's and Zhongshan's own territory.
- **The Battle Halls** *(added 2026-07-31)* — informal, recurring venues built for direct band-vs-band and performer-vs-performer competitive showcases, distinct in spirit from the collaborative, friends-and-neighbors Glacier-Pop Living Rooms.
- **Home Recording Dens** *(added 2026-07-31)* — informal, bedroom-studio-style recording setups scattered across residential blocks, the amateur scene's own lower-stakes counterpart to the Institute's formal production training.
- **Street Corner Stages** *(added 2026-07-31)* — small, semi-permanent busking platforms placed throughout the city, giving beginner performers a genuinely lower-stakes venue than either the Battle Halls or the established Glacier-Pop circuit.
- **The Runway Halls** *(added 2026-07-31)* — dedicated spaces for actually showing finished Ashiato work in front of an audience, a step up in formality from the Ateliers' own workshop-scale garment-making.

## Social cohesion mechanisms

- **The Allocation** — the civic observance commemorating the Jeju-do court's founding decision.

- **Cluster Suppers** — extended, unhurried shared meals crossing into Sinheung's and Zhongshan's own territory, embodying the slow multi-generational warming into today's cluster intimacy.
- **Institute Placement Nights** — informal gatherings, part mentorship and part social introduction, where graduating Institute students are connected into the Ashiato and Glacier-pop circles they came to the city hoping to join.
- **Battle Nights** *(added 2026-07-31)* — the competitive counterpart to Glacier-Pop House Shows, held at the Battle Halls, with genuine local rivalries and reputations at stake between regular performers.
- **Runway Evenings** *(added 2026-07-31)* — seasonal showcases of the Ashiato Ateliers' own finished work at the Runway Halls, garments actually worn and judged rather than simply traded.
- **Corner Stage Debuts** *(added 2026-07-31)* — the informal, low-stakes tradition of a performer's very first public set happening at a Street Corner Stage, long before they're considered ready for the Battle Halls or Glacier-Pop circuit.
- **Album Drop Nights** *(added 2026-07-31)* — informal gatherings marking the release of a Home Recording Den project, treated with real, if modest, ceremony among friends and neighbors.
