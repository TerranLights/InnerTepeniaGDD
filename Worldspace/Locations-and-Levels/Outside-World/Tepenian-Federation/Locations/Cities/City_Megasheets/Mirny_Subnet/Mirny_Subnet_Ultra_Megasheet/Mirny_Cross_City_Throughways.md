# Mirny Subnet — Cross-City Throughways

**What this is:** connecting factors that pass through multiple Mirny subnet cities — not things the cities have in common (see Cross-City Patterns for that), but chains showing how a fact in one city leads through another to a consequence in a third, sometimes reaching outside the subnet entirely. Written 2026-07-10.

---

## Throughway 1: The Cradle chamber-manufacturing network runs directly through this subnet, and through the player character's own origin

**The currently-unnamed Korean city (working title "Soyuz") is one of only two confirmed present-day fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturers in all of Tepenia, alongside Byrd** (the city's own Full Extrapolation, Section III) → **a third site, Mountain Pass Airport on Hwy 37, was a historical joint venture built and staffed by Vostok's and Kunlun's own scientists and engineers together — concrete physical proof of the two cities' established "two loneliest outposts" relationship** (the currently-unnamed Korean city's Full Extrapolation, Section III) → **Mountain Pass's own manufacturing capability depended on residual power overflow from the Amundsen Tower's continent-wide regulated grid, since the outpost was too remote to support dedicated infrastructure of its own** (same source) → **the Tower's destruction during the Long Night War permanently ended Mountain Pass's supply and its manufacturing capability, though chambers already built and shipped before the war still function wherever they ended up** (same source) → **one of those chambers, built at Mountain Pass before the war, is confirmed as the specific chamber in Calethina's own lab that built the player character** (same source, citing `Energy_Grid_Failure_Rationale.md` #11) → **making the Mirny subnet, through Vostok and Kunlun's own quiet, mutual-dependency relationship, a direct, traceable link in the player character's own personal origin — not merely a thematic echo of the Cradle network already established in Halley subnet (Neumayer's design lineage) and Janbogo/Ross subnet (Denison's historical manufacturing), but the specific site whose collapse is written into the player's own backstory.**

## Throughway 2: The Larsemann Hills cluster's three cities arrived at their present-day closeness through three different founding mechanisms, then a shared strike hit all three together

**Zhongshan arrived via ordinary, organic Chinese settlement, confirmed rather than caused by the Jeju-do court, that never lost its own founding-nation dominance** (Zhongshan Mega-Init) → **the currently-unnamed Korean city and Shirayuki both arrived via the same deliberate pre-exile diplomatic engineering at the Jeju-do Court — Korea and Japan each allocated their own separate site specifically to prevent Zhongshan's own Chinese dominance from defaulting into an uncontested third claim across the whole cluster** *(corrected 2026-07-13: this Throughway previously described the currently-unnamed Korean city as arriving via "a genuinely doubled dual founding, two populations together from day one" — wrong; its actual founding is singularly Korean, via the same Jeju-do mechanism as Shirayuki, not a separate mechanism of its own)* (the city's own Mega-Init; Shirayuki Mega-Init) → **these two shared allocations and Zhongshan's own organic claim only warmed into today's established cluster intimacy gradually, tracking the three-stage amalgamation history — legal separation, then de facto merger, then full legal unification** (Shirayuki Full Extrapolation, Section III, citing the Tri-Cities Amalgamation History) → **that shared present-day cluster infrastructure, the tri-junction of Hwy 4, Hwy 110, and Hwy 22, is also where the Long Night War's own strike landed — targeting Zhongshan's precision-manufacturing sector and the currently-unnamed Korean city's chamber-manufacturing capacity specifically, not the cluster's population broadly** (Zhongshan Full Extrapolation, Section IV; the Korean city's own Full Extrapolation, Section V) → **meaning the same infrastructure that took three cities a century and a half to grow together into one shared node, despite two of them sharing an identical founding mechanism and one arriving by an entirely different path, is exactly what a single wartime strike hit all at once — the cluster's unity, hard-won across generations, and its shared vulnerability turned out to be the same physical fact.**

## Throughway 3: Vostok and Kunlun's relationship reaches directly into Tepenia's central Tower-collapse lore, not just their own local isolation

**Vostok's own founding Russian community thinned through ordinary generational attrition, with the current, unrelated population arriving later and inheriting a research legacy that was never theirs to build** (Vostok Full Extrapolation, Sections I–II) → **Kunlun's own founding population was more deliberately phased out, a considered decision once chronic altitude sickness became undeniable, replaced by a re-resolved, curated 19-nation specialist population selected for genuine space/astronomy/comms credentials** (Kunlun Full Extrapolation, Section I) → **both cities, despite arriving at their present-day populations through entirely different mechanisms, ended up sharing the identical "two loneliest outposts" relationship and a real, concrete resource-sharing partnership modeled on shared scientific infrastructure** (Vostok Full Extrapolation, Section VII) → **that partnership's clearest physical expression, Mountain Pass, depended on the Amundsen Tower's own regulated grid for power, tying Vostok and Kunlun's own quiet mutual dependency directly to the Tower's continued operation** (the currently-unnamed Korean city's Full Extrapolation, Section III) → **when the Tower fell, Mountain Pass's manufacturing died with it — meaning the single most consequential thing to ever come out of Vostok and Kunlun's own relationship (a functioning Cradle chamber, later installed in Calethina's lab) was already finished and shipped before the Tower's collapse could touch it, the last thing the two loneliest outposts in Tepenia ever built together.**

## Throughway 4: This subnet's war-damage logic explains itself with one principle where Janbogo/Ross subnet needed two

**Casey was destroyed for a single, clean strategic reason — a genuine dual highway chokepoint, one strike severing two supply corridors at once** (Casey Full Extrapolation, Section I; `Mirny_Cross_City_Patterns.md`, Pattern 2) → **the Zhongshan/currently-unnamed-Korean-city/Shirayuki cluster was damaged for the same underlying reason at a different scale — a shared infrastructure node worth disrupting, not a population worth punishing** (Pattern 2) → **Mirny survived for the inverse reason — real strategic value (subnet-hub communications and industrial capacity both) that its own dispersed, windbreak-ring architecture made too costly to eliminate in a single strike** (Mirny Cross-Reference, Finding 3; Pattern 2) → **Vostok and Kunlun survived entirely undamaged because their extreme remoteness made striking them not worth the cost, while Davis's own lower-priority feeder role plausibly explains why it was struck but never destroyed outright — damaged, not spared** *(corrected 2026-07-14 — this Throughway previously grouped Davis with Vostok/Kunlun as "survived, undamaged"; `Specs/Davis.md`'s own status is Damaged, corrected 2026-07-03)* (Pattern 2) → **producing a subnet where every single documented case of damage or destruction traces to infrastructure value and reachability alone, with no city struck for a punitive or symbolic reason the way Zukelli was in Janbogo/Ross subnet** → **giving the Long Night War's own targeting logic a genuine, checkable regional variation: narrowly and consistently strategic here, split between strategic and punitive one subnet over — a real difference in how the war was actually fought from region to region, not simply an artifact of which cities happened to survive.**
