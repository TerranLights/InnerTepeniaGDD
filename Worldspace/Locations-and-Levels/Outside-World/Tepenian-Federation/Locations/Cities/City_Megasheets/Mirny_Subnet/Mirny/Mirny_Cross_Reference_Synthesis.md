## Finding 1: Mirny's eventual rename is a larger-scope decision than its own flag currently frames it as

**Combining:** Mirny's literal Arcanet-routing role for Zhongshan, Casey, Davis, Vostok, and Kunlun (Local Cultures Section 24) + the entire surrounding region being formally named "the Mirny subnet" in every reference document + the 2026-07-08 flagged rename, currently scoped in `TODO.md` as a question about the *city's* name alone.

**2nd-order effect:** unlike an ordinary city rename, changing Mirny's name would create a mismatch between the city and the six-city regional subnet named after it — something no other flagged Tepenian rename has had to account for.

**3rd-order effect:** this means the eventual rename decision isn't purely cosmetic even under the project's own established "adopt the destination's composition, keep it simple" population-rebalancing philosophy — it requires a second, genuinely separate decision about whether the subnet's own name changes too, or whether "Mirny subnet" persists as a decoupled regional label even after the city itself carries a different name.

**4th-order effect:** worth flagging explicitly for whenever the rename is actually picked up — the real scope of that future conversation is "what do we call this city, *and* does an entire regional subnet's name change with it," not just the single-city question the current TODO entry implies.

## Finding 2: Mirny and Byrd solve the same personality in opposite physical directions

**Combining:** Mirny's concentric windbreak-ring architecture (a fortified outer shell shedding wind, sheltering an industrial-and-residential core inside) + Byrd's own established architecture (built downward before it ever stood on the surface, its true scale hidden underground) + `Distinguishing_Overlapping_Profiles.md`'s listing of Byrd and Mirny sharing the exact same Enneagram profile (Instinctive/Withdrawn/Competency), distinguished only by one line each — "isolation at maximum scale" (Byrd) versus "industrial shelter" (Mirny).

**2nd-order effect:** both cities physically manifest the identical psychological profile as literal architecture turned inward against a hostile environment, but along opposite spatial axes — Byrd goes down, Mirny wraps around — depending on which specific threat each city actually faces (Byrd's isolation and burial-by-snow vs. Mirny's wind).

**3rd-order effect:** this gives the one-line distinction in `Distinguishing_Overlapping_Profiles.md` a far more concrete, visually comparable expression than its own text currently spells out — genuinely useful groundwork for any future concept-art or level-design pass that wants to visually contrast the two cities.

**4th-order effect:** since both cities have now separately picked up real-world industrial inspirations of their own (Mirny via Nizhny Tagil and Wolfsburg's industrial-enclosure model; Byrd via its own Ruhr Valley and Chelyabinsk picks), this cross-reference suggests a genuinely productive future design comparison: two cities sharing one personality, solving "protect the vulnerable core" in architecturally opposite directions.

## Finding 3: Mirny's "subnet hub" role means something structurally different from Janbogo's

**Combining:** Mirny's Specs.md framing as the "Hub of the Mirny subnet" (implying, by name alone, a primarily communications/administrative role) + the 2026-07-05 vision session establishing Mirny as *also* "the subnet's construction engine," with industrial/manufacturing sitting at roughly 20% — on par with, not subordinate to, communications/Arcanet's own 20% — + Janbogo's own, differently-shaped hub role (Janbogo subnet's primary hub, expressed almost entirely through hospitality and trade culture, per its own Local Cultures file).

**2nd-order effect:** "subnet hub" means something functionally doubled in Mirny's specific case — not just where messages route through, but the literal physical source of the construction materials and maintenance machinery that built the roads those messages travel alongside.

**3rd-order effect:** this makes Mirny genuinely distinct among Tepenia's subnet hubs — its administrative role and its physical-industrial role carry equal established weight, rather than one being clearly subordinate to the other the way Janbogo's hub role leans almost entirely on culture and hospitality rather than heavy industry.

**4th-order effect:** worth naming as a real structural variation worth preserving in any future hub-city design work — "hub" doesn't mean the same thing from subnet to subnet in Tepenia; Janbogo's hub role is expressed socially and culturally, while Mirny's is expressed through hard infrastructure on two simultaneous fronts (information and physical construction), a genuinely different flavor of the same underlying administrative function.

## Synthesis: The Pattern Across All Three Findings

All three findings share a common thread: Mirny is a city whose established facts consistently turn out to carry more structural weight, and more downstream consequence, than a first read suggests. Its pending rename touches an entire subnet's naming convention, not just one city's (Finding 1). Its shared Enneagram profile with Byrd conceals a genuinely rich architectural contrast once the two cities' physical solutions are actually placed side by side (Finding 2). And its own "subnet hub" label undersells just how much of that role is expressed through hard industrial infrastructure rather than communications alone (Finding 3). Mirny reads, across all three findings, as a city whose plain, unglamorous self-description consistently hides more genuine structural significance than it advertises — fitting, given its own established civic ethos of "competence over romance," extended here into how the city is documented, not just how it behaves.
