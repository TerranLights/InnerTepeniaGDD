# Mirny Subnet — Cross-City Patterns

**What this is:** common factors recurring across multiple Mirny subnet cities — not causal chains (see Cross-City Throughways for those), but shared traits, structures, or tensions that show up independently in more than one city's own Megasheet. Several of these were already partially self-identified within individual cities' own Cross-Reference Syntheses; the job here is to consolidate and extend that work across the full subnet, following the same process used for Palmer, Halley, and Janbogo/Ross subnets. Written 2026-07-10.

## Pattern 1: Five distinct mechanisms produce a "multinational" city, all sitting side by side in one subnet

Kunlun's own Cross-Reference Finding 1 explicitly flagged this as worth checking across Tepenia's other multinational cities. Running that check across all eight Mirny subnet cities produces five genuinely distinct mechanisms, not one:

- **Organic multi-generational immigration:** Zhongshan (a fully-formed founding culture that later arrivals simply integrated into) and Davis (a genuinely dispersed 13-nation Notable tier, no single community concentrated enough to dominate).
- **Deliberate, function-based curated draw:** Kunlun — re-resolved into a 19-nation population selected specifically for real space/astronomy/comms credentials, not organic settlement.
- **Demographic-redistribution accident:** Vostok — its current population arrived generations after founding, via a later administrative resettlement unconnected to the city's own founding legend, with zero ancestral connection to the Lake Vostok research mission itself.
- **Genuinely doubled dual founding:** the currently-unnamed Korean city (working title "Soyuz," definitely slated for eventual rename) — two populations arriving together from day one, rather than one operator nation joined later by a separate wave.
- **Pre-exile diplomatic engineering:** Shirayuki — the only Tepenian city whose founding-nation allocation was settled by treaty at Jeju-do before the exile era even began, specifically to prevent China's regional dominance from defaulting into an uncontested claim.
- **(A sixth, hybrid case) Casey's "wide, mixed early wave":** a junction city that received unusually broad settlement from the start rather than one dominant founding population diluted later — distinct from Davis's more gradual dispersal, since Casey's diversity was present from founding rather than accumulating over generations.

**What this produces:** Mirny subnet alone contains a near-complete taxonomy of every mechanism by which a Tepenian city's population composition can form — worth using as the reference set any future city's own demographic-formation story should be checked against, the same way Halley subnet's damage-mechanism taxonomy and Janbogo/Ross subnet's destruction-logic taxonomy now serve their own respective questions.

## Pattern 2: A subnet-wide war-damage logic that resolves to exactly one principle — unlike Janbogo/Ross subnet's two

Janbogo/Ross subnet's own war-damage pattern required two explanatory principles (ongoing usefulness vs. punitive/symbolic intent). Mirny subnet's own pattern is structurally simpler — every documented case traces to strategic infrastructure value alone, with no punitive or deterrent-message component anywhere in the subnet:

| City | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Casey** | Destroyed | A genuine dual highway chokepoint (Hwy 2 running *through*, not just to, the city) — one strike severing two supply corridors at once (Casey Full Extrapolation, Section I) |
| **Zhongshan / Soyuz / Shirayuki cluster** | Damaged, functional | A targeted strike on the shared tri-junction node, aimed at Zhongshan's precision-manufacturing and Soyuz's chamber-manufacturing capacity specifically, not at the cluster's population broadly (Zhongshan Full Extrapolation, Section IV; Soyuz Full Extrapolation, Section V) |
| **Mirny** | Damaged, survived | Genuine subnet-hub value (communications and industrial both), but its own windbreak-ring architecture and dispersed function meant no single strike could eliminate it outright |
| **Davis** | Survived, undamaged | A feeder/enabler role only — raw materials out, no final-stage strategic capacity of its own worth striking |
| **Vostok, Kunlun** | Survived, undamaged | Extreme remoteness and marginal physical reachability — even genuine strategic value (Vostok's archive, Kunlun's comms relay) wasn't worth the resource cost of reaching either one |

**What this produces:** a real, checkable contrast between two subnets' own war-damage logic — Janbogo/Ross subnet needed a punitive/symbolic principle to explain Zukelli and Cape Adare, while every Mirny subnet case explains cleanly through infrastructure value and reachability alone. This is worth treating as a genuine finding about the war's own conduct, not just a coincidence of which cities happened to get hit — Upper Earth's targeting in this subnet reads as more narrowly, consistently strategic than its targeting in Janbogo/Ross.

## Throughway 1: The Cradle chamber-manufacturing network runs directly through this subnet, and through the player character's own origin

**The currently-unnamed Korean city (working title "Soyuz") is one of only two confirmed present-day fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturers in all of Tepenia, alongside Byrd** (the city's own Full Extrapolation, Section III) → **a third site, Mountain Pass Airport on Hwy 37, was a historical joint venture built and staffed by Vostok's and Kunlun's own scientists and engineers together — concrete physical proof of the two cities' established "two loneliest outposts" relationship** (the currently-unnamed Korean city's Full Extrapolation, Section III) → **Mountain Pass's own manufacturing capability depended on residual power overflow from the Amundsen Tower's continent-wide regulated grid, since the outpost was too remote to support dedicated infrastructure of its own** (same source) → **the Tower's destruction during the Long Night War permanently ended Mountain Pass's supply and its manufacturing capability, though chambers already built and shipped before the war still function wherever they ended up** (same source) → **one of those chambers, built at Mountain Pass before the war, is confirmed as the specific chamber in Calethina's own lab that built the player character** (same source, citing `Energy_Grid_Failure_Rationale.md` #11) → **making the Mirny subnet, through Vostok and Kunlun's own quiet, mutual-dependency relationship, a direct, traceable link in the player character's own personal origin — not merely a thematic echo of the Cradle network already established in Halley subnet (Neumayer's design lineage) and Janbogo/Ross subnet (Denison's historical manufacturing), but the specific site whose collapse is written into the player's own backstory.**

## Throughway 2: The Larsemann Hills cluster's three cities arrived at their present-day closeness through three different founding mechanisms, then a shared strike hit all three together

**Zhongshan arrived via ordinary, organic Chinese settlement that never lost its own founding-nation dominance** (Zhongshan Mega-Init) → **the currently-unnamed Korean city arrived via a genuinely doubled dual founding, two populations together from day one** (the city's own Mega-Init) → **Shirayuki arrived via deliberate pre-exile diplomatic engineering at the Jeju-do Court, specifically to prevent Zhongshan's own Chinese dominance from defaulting into an uncontested third claim across the whole cluster** (Shirayuki Mega-Init) → **these three genuinely different origins only warmed into today's established cluster intimacy gradually, tracking the three-stage amalgamation history — legal separation, then de facto merger, then full legal unification** (Shirayuki Full Extrapolation, Section III, citing the Tri-Cities Amalgamation History) → **that shared present-day cluster infrastructure, the tri-junction of Hwy 4, Hwy 110, and Hwy 22, is also where the Long Night War's own strike landed — targeting Zhongshan's precision-manufacturing sector and the currently-unnamed Korean city's chamber-manufacturing capacity specifically, not the cluster's population broadly** (Zhongshan Full Extrapolation, Section IV; the Korean city's own Full Extrapolation, Section V) → **meaning the same infrastructure that took three different founding stories a century and a half to grow together into one shared node is exactly what a single wartime strike hit all at once — the cluster's unity, hard-won across generations, and its shared vulnerability turned out to be the same physical fact.**

## Throughway 3: Vostok and Kunlun's relationship reaches directly into Tepenia's central Tower-collapse lore, not just their own local isolation

**Vostok's own founding Russian community thinned through ordinary generational attrition, with the current, unrelated population arriving later and inheriting a research legacy that was never theirs to build** (Vostok Full Extrapolation, Sections I–II) → **Kunlun's own founding population was more deliberately phased out, a considered decision once chronic altitude sickness became undeniable, replaced by a re-resolved, curated 19-nation specialist population selected for genuine space/astronomy/comms credentials** (Kunlun Full Extrapolation, Section I) → **both cities, despite arriving at their present-day populations through entirely different mechanisms, ended up sharing the identical "two loneliest outposts" relationship and a real, concrete resource-sharing partnership modeled on shared scientific infrastructure** (Vostok Full Extrapolation, Section VII) → **that partnership's clearest physical expression, Mountain Pass, depended on the Amundsen Tower's own regulated grid for power, tying Vostok and Kunlun's own quiet mutual dependency directly to the Tower's continued operation** (the currently-unnamed Korean city's Full Extrapolation, Section III) → **when the Tower fell, Mountain Pass's manufacturing died with it — meaning the single most consequential thing to ever come out of Vostok and Kunlun's own relationship (a functioning Cradle chamber, later installed in Calethina's lab) was already finished and shipped before the Tower's collapse could touch it, the last thing the two loneliest outposts in Tepenia ever built together.**

## Throughway 4: This subnet's war-damage logic explains itself with one principle where Janbogo/Ross subnet needed two

## Who Lives Here, and Why

Mirny subnet's founding populations arrived through a genuinely wider range of mechanisms than any other subnet examined so far — organic multi-generational settlement (Zhongshan, Davis), a deliberate function-based curated draw (Kunlun), a demographic accident unconnected to any founding legend (Vostok), a genuinely doubled dual founding (the currently-unnamed Korean city), and pre-exile diplomatic engineering at the Jeju-do Court (Shirayuki) — five distinct mechanisms across eight cities, more variety in how a population actually forms than this project has documented in any other single subnet.

This produces a subnet whose demographic story resists a single throughline the way Halley subnet's founding-nation diversity or Janbogo/Ross subnet's Pacific-corridor concentration each did — instead, Mirny subnet's real defining demographic fact is the sheer number of different processes sitting side by side, from Zhongshan's unbroken Chinese continuity (the only Tepenian city where the founding operator nation never lost Primary status) to Kunlun's fully re-resolved, nationality-neutral specialist population, with nearly every other possible mechanism represented somewhere in between.

## What It Feels Like

Moving through Mirny subnet means moving between wildly different registers of extremity and ordinariness within a genuinely short administrative distance. Mirny itself announces the subnet's core industrial character immediately — a fortified windbreak ring wrapped around huge, still-running industrial yards, rough and unapologetically plain.
