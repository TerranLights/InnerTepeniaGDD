# Sinheung — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Sinheung would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Sinheung.md`, `Local_Cultures/Mirny_Subnet/Sinheung.md`, and
`Sinheung_Community_Infrastructure.md` — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 deliberately deferred
until this pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written 2026-07-30.

**A note on Chamber Works infrastructure below:** Sinheung is one of only two Tepenian cities (alongside
Byrd) that actively manufactures fabrication-synthesis chambers — already-established, national-scope
background canon, consistent with how this project treats the Cradle network project-wide (real, but
unmarked, never a main-quest driver). The attributes below describe the physical facility factually, the
same treatment Byrd's own Chamber Works already receives.

**Governing facts:** Larsemann Hills, Korean-founded via the same Jeju-do diplomatic allocation that
settled Shirayuki (Japan) and confirmed Zhongshan (China); status is damaged but partially operational;
economy is heavy industrial fabrication — raw material trucked in from Mirny via the Zhongshan
tri-junction, finished into goods, plus (nationally significant) chamber manufacturing to Neumayer's own
Mark IV schematic; a functional, smaller airstrip historically flew direct supplies to Dome Fuji; climate
is mild by Tepenian standards, similar to Shirayuki's own Larsemann Hills profile; the cluster culture
treats the roughly 60-day polar night as a genuinely severe seasonal isolation despite the relatively mild
temperatures, adapted to through fermentation/preservation food craft.

## Chamber Manufacturing Infrastructure

1. **The Chamber Works** (established) — one of only two functioning fabrication-synthesis chamber
   manufacturing sites in Tepenia, the city's single most consequential piece of infrastructure.
2. **National-scale freight and shipping infrastructure specifically for chamber distribution** — given
   chambers are shipped nationwide from here, this is a distinct logistics chain from the city's own
   general industrial output.
3. **Specialized fabrication equipment matching Neumayer's own Mark IV schematic** — the chambers built
   here follow an externally-designed spec, meaning the equipment itself is built to match imported
   engineering rather than locally-developed technology.

## Heavy Industrial Fabrication Infrastructure

4. **Raw-material receiving infrastructure** from Mirny, arriving via the Zhongshan tri-junction —
   distinct from and additional to the chamber-specific supply chain.
5. **General fabrication and finished-goods manufacturing facilities**, distinct from the Chamber Works
   itself — the broader industrial base the city's own economy actually runs on day to day.

## Airstrip & Aviation Infrastructure

6. **A functional airstrip**, smaller and less advanced than Troll's own — historically flew direct
   supplies to Dome Fuji, one of two separate aviation lines converging on that isolated city.
7. **At least one damaged, repairable aircraft at the airstrip**, consistent with the established DLC 4
   alternate-access mechanic (a broken plane fixable with the right stat gate and a specific cross-DLC
   part) — a concrete, already-established physical detail about the airstrip's current condition.

## Fermentation & Preservation Infrastructure

8. **Fermentation Halls** (established) — communal kimchi and preservation-craft spaces, adapted to the
   cluster-wide roughly 60-day polar night.
9. **Food-security infrastructure calibrated to a genuinely severe seasonal isolation**, despite the
   Larsemann Hills' own comparatively mild temperatures — the established cultural treatment of the dark
   season as harsher than the numbers alone suggest implies real, deliberate preparation infrastructure
   built around it.

## Civic & Cluster Infrastructure

10. **The Hills Accord Grounds** (established) — the physical site tied to the observance marking ongoing
    coexistence with Zhongshan and Shirayuki.
11. **Production-Line Murals** (established) — public art celebrating the city's own labor, Sinheung's
    civic aesthetic answer to Zhongshan's own ink-painting tradition.

## Educational Infrastructure

12. **The Sinheung Polytechnic** (established) — a university focused on materials, fabrication, and
    production, smaller in scope than Shirayuki's own Institute.

## War-Damage Infrastructure

13. **Ongoing repair infrastructure**, consistent with the established "damaged but partially operational"
    status.

## Cross-Referenced Extrapolation Findings (Methodology #2)

**What this is:** cross-referencing the 13 attributes above against Sinheung's own existing lore —
`Sinheung_Full_Extrapolation.md` and `Sinheung_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` (which already holds 3
Findings of its own, written 2026-07-07, updated 2026-07-14). Kept light, matching the other non-hub Mirny
subnet cities; the Cradle-adjacent content below is treated factually, per the project's own standing
unmarked-lore convention. Written 2026-07-30.

### Finding A: building to an external schematic is itself an instance of "earn it, don't inherit it"

**Combining:** attributes #2–3 (national chamber-distribution freight infrastructure, fabrication equipment
built to match Neumayer's own externally-designed Mark IV schematic) + the existing Cross-Reference
Synthesis's Finding 1 ("earning the claim through output" as a repeated pattern, not a one-time founding
fact).

**2nd-order effect:** Sinheung didn't invent the chamber-manufacturing technology it's known for — it earned
its place in the national network by building to someone else's spec and delivering reliably, the same
demonstrated-output logic Finding 1 already traces through the city's founding and its later economic
specialization.

**3rd-order effect:** extends Finding 1's own pattern to the method of manufacture itself, not just the
fact of it — a third instance of the same civic theme (proving worth through delivery, not through
originating the idea) recurring at yet another scale.

### Finding B: the airstrip's survival is small physical evidence for the war's own targeted-strike logic

**Combining:** attributes #6–7 (the functional airstrip, its one damaged-but-repairable aircraft) + the Full
Extrapolation's Section V (the Long Night War plausibly targeted chamber production specifically rather than
hitting the city as ordinary collateral damage).

**2nd-order effect:** the airstrip's own continued general function, with only a single aircraft actually
damaged, is consistent with a strike that disrupted chamber-specific capacity while leaving the rest of the
city's infrastructure comparatively intact.

**3rd-order effect:** worth treating as a small, concrete piece of physical evidence supporting Section V's
own proposal — not proof on its own, but a detail that fits cleanly with a targeted rather than generalized
strike.

### Finding C: the Hills Accord Grounds commemorate a deal struck before anyone arrived, not neighbors learning to coexist

**Combining:** attribute #10 (the Hills Accord Grounds, marking ongoing coexistence with Zhongshan and
Shirayuki) + the Full Extrapolation's Section VI (the Larsemann Hills cluster's pre-war relationships root
in the same three-way Jeju-do negotiation among Japan, Korea, and the Sinian Federation, not organic
neighborly development).

**2nd-order effect:** the coexistence the Accord Grounds mark isn't a story of three separate communities
gradually learning to get along — it's three parties continuing an agreement their own diplomats had already
struck with each other before any of them set foot on the ice.

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming as a genuinely different emotional register from most Tepenian
cluster-harmony narratives — inherited diplomatic peace, honored generation after generation, rather than
hard-won neighborly trust built from nothing.

### Finding D: Sinheung consistently gets the modest version of parallel cluster infrastructure

**Combining:** attribute #12 (the Sinheung Polytechnic, smaller in scope than Shirayuki's own Institute) +
the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 3 (the Sinheung rename was the city's first deliberate
self-naming, arriving over 130 years after founding, in contrast to Shirayuki's own name being resolved
within a single day).

**2nd-order effect:** the Polytechnic's own comparatively modest, practical scope relative to Shirayuki's
broader arts-and-design Institute mirrors the same asymmetry Finding 3 already identifies at the level of
naming — Sinheung gets the functional, unglamorous version of parallel cluster infrastructure and identity
markers.

**3rd-order effect:** worth flagging as a recurring pattern across multiple scales (naming, education,
economic identity) rather than a single isolated fact — Sinheung's own civic character is consistently the
practical, load-bearing counterpart to Shirayuki's more celebrated one, without either city's own value
being diminished by the contrast.
