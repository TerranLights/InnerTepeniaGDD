# Sayowa — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Sayowa would logically have to
possess, built directly from `Specs/Sayowa.md`, `Local_Cultures/Mawson_Subnet/Sayowa.md`, and
`Sayowa_Community_Infrastructure.md` (plus Course of Events chain #1, "The Point Where Three Roads Meet,"
for the established industrial/residential civic split) — **Methodology #1 only**; Methodology #2 is
deliberately deferred until this pass is complete across all 34 DLC cities. Not locked canon. Written
2026-07-30. This closes out the Mawson subnet (Dome Fuji, Mawson, Sayowa all now have this pass).

**Why this city's attributes look different again:** Sayowa is an island city (East Ongul Island),
damaged-but-functioning like Mawson but much smaller in population (~165k vs. Mawson's ~950k+), and its
defining fact is a specific one none of its subnet-mates share: it sits at a genuine three-way highway
junction — Hwy 4, Hwy 7-ext, and Hwy 37 — that connects the Mawson subnet, Concordia, and the Halley
subnet all at once, and that junction is structurally more nationally important than the city carrying it.

**Governing facts:** East Ongul Island, Lützow-Holm Bay, Prince Harald Coast — sea-ice covered in winter,
icebreaker-accessible in summer, a genuine seasonal dual-mode access city; status is damaged but partially
operational, specifically because the Sayowa Junction was valuable enough to be targeted but too
structurally important to the whole continent's connectivity to fully destroy; population ~165k (Census
II), a fraction of Mawson's; economy is fabrication, trucking/dispatch, and JARE-heritage scientific
research; the city itself is physically split into two matched, deliberately separate halves — an
industrialized fabrication-and-dispatch zone and a residential zone preserving the original JARE research
footprint (established, Course of Events chain #1); the actual three-way junction sits near, not inside,
the city, connected by the dedicated Sayowa Spur road.

## Island/Maritime Infrastructure

1. **Harbor infrastructure at Lützow-Holm Bay** (established) — the primary maritime entry point.
2. **Seasonal ice-road infrastructure** (established: The Seasonal Ice Road) connecting the island to the
   mainland in winter — maintenance equipment, staging areas, and route-marking distinct from any
   maritime-only city's own infrastructure.
3. **Icebreaker berthing and maintenance infrastructure** for the summer access window, when maritime
   transit replaces the ice road.
4. **Seasonal-transition protocols and infrastructure** for the actual crossover period between ice-road
   and icebreaker access — a genuine operational gap (shared with Fort McMurdo and Dumont d'Urville, per
   `Sayowa_Community_Infrastructure.md`'s own note) that the city has to actively manage rather than treat
   as a clean handoff.

## Highway Junction Infrastructure

5. **The Sayowa Junction itself** (established) — the physical three-way crossing of Hwy 4, Hwy 7-ext, and
   Hwy 37, damaged but functioning.
6. **The Sayowa Spur** (established) — a dedicated connecting road linking the city proper to the Junction,
   itself a substantial piece of infrastructure given the Junction sits physically apart from the city.
7. **The Junction Cooperative Maintenance Office** (established) — the physical site where the multi-party
   cooperative arrangement keeping the Junction functional is actually coordinated, distinct from Troll
   Airfield's own open-conflict model for contested infrastructure in the Halley subnet.

## Critical-Infrastructure Protection

8. **Dedicated protective or hardening infrastructure around the Junction specifically**, distinct from
   ordinary city defense — given the Junction was valuable enough to be targeted in the war and remains
   nationally critical today, it plausibly receives a different standard of protection than the rest of
   the city.
9. **Damage-repair prioritization weighted toward Junction functionality above all else** — consistent
   with the established logic that the Junction's own national importance is what kept it from being fully
   destroyed; ongoing repair effort plausibly reflects that same priority order.

## Industrial/Residential Split Infrastructure

10. **A physically distinct industrial fabrication-and-dispatch zone**, separate from the residential zone
    (established, Course of Events chain #1's own Culmination — "a clean split... rather than the two
    simply blurring together").
11. **Preserved JARE research infrastructure, kept intentionally separate from the industrial buildout**
    (established, same chain) — the original scientific footprint protected from being folded into the
    newer industrial apparatus rather than displaced by it.

## Scientific/JARE Heritage Infrastructure

12. **Archival space for Shōwa-era institutional memory**, held privately and generationally within the
    Japanese-heritage community specifically (per `Sayowa_Community_Infrastructure.md`'s own corrected
    framing) — distinct from any public commemorative infrastructure.
13. **Continued ice-core or polar-research facilities** inherited from JARE's own uninterrupted 1957-era
    research tradition, maintained within the residential half of the city's own physical split.

## Logistics & Dispatch

14. **Trucking and freight dispatch infrastructure**, sized for through-traffic as much as local production
    — Sayowa sits directly on the route connecting the Mawson subnet to both the Halley subnet and
    Concordia, meaning much of what moves through the city's own dispatch infrastructure isn't necessarily
    bound for Sayowa itself.
15. **Freight staging and marshalling space** at the industrial half of the city, feeding traffic out
    toward the Junction rather than only serving local fabrication output.

## Climate & Structural

16. **Coastal East Antarctic island structural engineering**, similar in profile to Mawson's own but with
    island- and bay-specific considerations (sea-ice interaction at the shoreline, causeway/spur
    engineering to the mainland) that a non-island coastal city wouldn't need.

## Scale Mismatch

17. **Junction infrastructure scaled to national through-traffic demand, not to Sayowa's own comparatively
    modest population** — a real mismatch between the city's own size (~165k) and the structural weight it
    carries for the whole continent's connectivity, echoing at a smaller scale the same "small city, outsized
    structural burden" pattern this project has already found reason to flag elsewhere.

## Cross-Referenced Extrapolation Findings (Methodology #2)

**What this is:** cross-referencing the 17 attributes above against Sayowa's own existing lore —
`Sayowa_Full_Extrapolation.md` and `Sayowa_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` (which already holds 3 Findings of
its own, written 2026-07-09). Per direct developer instruction, kept deliberately light — Sayowa has the
least content of the three Mawson subnet cities, and this pass adds only a handful of genuinely new
combinations rather than padding the list. Written 2026-07-30.

### Finding A: the seasonal-access gap is a national logistics problem, not just a local inconvenience

**Combining:** attribute #4 (the seasonal ice-road/icebreaker transition gap, a flag shared with Fort
McMurdo and Dumont d'Urville) + attributes #14–15 (dispatch and freight infrastructure sized for continental
through-traffic, not just local production).

**2nd-order effect:** unlike Fort McMurdo's or Dumont d'Urville's own versions of this same flagged gap,
Sayowa's shoulder-season access disruption doesn't just inconvenience local residents — it interrupts a
route the whole continent's Mawson-Halley-Concordia connectivity depends on.

**3rd-order effect:** worth flagging as the highest-stakes version of this shared, still-unresolved
mechanic among the three cities that carry it — whenever it does get resolved, Sayowa's own case is the one
with genuine national consequences riding on the answer.

### Finding B: the Junction Cooperative Maintenance Office is "small vessel, outsized cargo" made architectural

**Combining:** attribute #7 (the Junction Cooperative Maintenance Office) + attribute #17 (Junction
infrastructure scaled to national demand, not to Sayowa's own modest population) + the existing
Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 3 ("small vessel, outsized cargo" as Sayowa's organizing principle at
every scale).

**2nd-order effect:** the Office itself is plausibly a physically modest, quietly-run institution
coordinating something structurally more important to Tepenia than the city hosting it.

**3rd-order effect:** gives Finding 3's own abstract organizing principle a literal building to point to —
the single clearest physical expression of "modest scale, outsized significance" anywhere in Sayowa.

### Finding C: Junction repair priority and Sayowa's own survival trace to the identical attacker calculation

**Combining:** attributes #8–9 (dedicated Junction protection and damage-repair prioritization) + the
existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 2 (Sayowa's survival as strategic value preserved by mutual
restraint, a genuinely new damage-pattern category).

**2nd-order effect:** the same restraint logic that explains why Sayowa wasn't fully destroyed also explains
why post-war repair investment plausibly favors the Junction specifically over the rest of the city —
both outcomes trace back to the identical calculation about what any party, including an attacker, would
lose by letting the Junction fail completely.

**3rd-order effect:** this makes Sayowa's own repair priorities a direct continuation of its wartime survival
logic rather than a separate post-war decision — one consistent principle governing both.

### Finding D: the physical split between JARE heritage and the industrial zone mirrors the demographic split in Finding 1

**Combining:** attributes #11–13 (preserved JARE research infrastructure, kept intentionally separate from
the industrial buildout; archival Shōwa-era memory held privately within the Japanese-heritage community) +
the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 1 (Japan's Tepenian diaspora producing genuinely separate
outcomes at Shirayuki, Princess Elisabeth, and Sayowa).

**2nd-order effect:** the physical separation between the preserved research footprint and the newer
industrial zone is a spatial echo of the same separation already visible in the population data — Sayowa's
institutional memory (JARE heritage) and its demographic majority (China-Primary) occupy genuinely distinct
space, not blended, in both the census tables and the city's own layout.

**3rd-order effect:** worth naming as a small but real piece of design coherence — Sayowa's built environment
and its demographic history are telling the identical story independently, without either fact having been
derived from the other.
