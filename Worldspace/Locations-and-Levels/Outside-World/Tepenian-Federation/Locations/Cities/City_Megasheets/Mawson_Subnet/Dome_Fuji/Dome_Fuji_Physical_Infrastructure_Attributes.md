# Dome Fuji — Physical Infrastructure Attributes

**What this is:** a systematic derivation of physical/civic attributes Dome Fuji would logically have to
possess, built directly from what's already established (`Specs/Dome_Fuji.md`, `Local_Cultures/Mawson_
Subnet/Dome_Fuji.md`, `Dome_Fuji_Full_Extrapolation.md`, `Dome_Fuji_Community_Infrastructure.md`) rather
than invented from nothing — **Methodology #1 only** (first-principles derivation, then a cross-city
comparison round); the Cross-Referenced Extrapolation Findings pass (Methodology #2) is deliberately
deferred until this same pass is complete across all 34 remaining DLC cities, per direct developer
instruction 2026-07-30. Not locked canon — a bouncing-off point for further extrapolation. Written
2026-07-30.

**Why this city's attributes look nothing like Byrd's own:** Byrd needed exceptional compensatory depth
because it carries an entire DLC alone; Dome Fuji shares its subnet with Mawson and Sayowa, so it doesn't
need the same scale of treatment. More importantly, Dome Fuji's own governing facts are close to inverted
from Byrd's: **surface-built, not underground; 100% robot population, with human presence outright
forbidden today; no Arcanet connectivity at all, rather than Byrd's limited-but-real access; extreme
altitude and cold as the defining hazard, rather than total light deprivation.** Copying Byrd's category
structure onto this city would misrepresent it — the categories below are derived fresh from Dome Fuji's
own facts.

**Governing facts this file reasons from:** Dome Fuji sits on the East Antarctic Plateau at ~3,810m
geometric elevation (~4,500m effective physiological altitude), one of the highest-elevation cities in
Tepenia; mean annual temperature ~−54°C, average winter lows ~−65°C, record low ~−80°C; extraordinarily
calm winds (4–6 m/s) — snow accumulation and cold are the defining hazards, not wind or storms; current
population is 55,072, entirely robot, human presence forbidden as physiologically lethal; the city was
originally a mature Japanese (JARE) research station, its founding human population departed after the
Falkland Treaty, and it was later rediscovered and resettled entirely by Ice Cold Buddhism pilgrims —
robots who venerate extreme cold as the closest living approximation to superconducting conditions; no
functioning Arcanet connectivity exists at this altitude, a project-wide rule, not a degree of isolation;
historical resupply ran on two direct aviation routes (Troll, and a smaller strip near Sinheung), status
today unconfirmed; economy is ice-core science (40%), religious/pilgrimage infrastructure (35%), and
facility maintenance (20%).

---

## Extreme Cold & Mechanical Engineering (far more severe than any underground city)

1. **Cold-rated actuators, lubricants, and structural materials** engineered to withstand sustained
   exposure to −65°C average winter temperatures and −80°C record lows — genuinely testing the outer
   limits of continuous robot-mechanical tolerance, a materially harsher engineering problem than any
   climate-controlled underground city would ever face.
2. **Charging and power infrastructure specifically hardened against extreme-cold performance loss** —
   battery chemistry and charging efficiency both degrade badly at temperatures this extreme, a distinct
   engineering problem from simply having power available.
3. **Deliberately minimal insulation on devotional infrastructure**, inverting ordinary cold-weather
   design logic — since the faith's own practice specifically embraces direct cold exposure (robes worn
   minimally to "commune with the cold," per established Vision Session canon), spaces built for
   devotional use plausibly forgo insulation by design, distinct from purely functional/maintenance
   structures, which still need genuine thermal protection for reliable operation.

## Surface Structural Engineering (no underground component)

4. **Structures built and maintained entirely on the ice-sheet surface**, unlike Byrd's underground
   volume — snow drift and accumulation management for roofs and access points is a genuine, ongoing
   structural concern here in a way it never is for a buried city.
5. **Adaptive reuse of the original JARE research station's own structures** (established: "The Old
   Wing") — labs, drilling infrastructure, and researcher housing built for a secular scientific mission,
   converted to devotional and robot-appropriate use rather than replaced with new construction.
6. **Structures engineered against snow accumulation and extreme cold specifically, not wind-loading** —
   Dome Fuji's own unusually calm plateau winds (4–6 m/s, among the lowest in Tepenia) mean the structural
   engineering priorities here are materially different from any coastal or storm-exposed Tepenian city.

## Ice-Core Science Infrastructure

7. **Deep ice-core drilling apparatus**, extended and maintained across however long the station's
   scientific mission has run, capable of extracting cores reaching "hundreds of thousands of years" into
   the ice sheet's own history.
8. **A dedicated cold-storage archive for ice-core samples** (established: "The Archive Vault") — with
   ambient temperature already at or below any practical storage requirement, this plausibly needs
   organizational and access infrastructure more than active refrigeration.
9. **Inherited analysis laboratory space**, staffed today by "the few devotees who arrived with prior
   scientific training" (per established Notable Figure Teodor Marchetti) rather than a dedicated research
   population.

## Religious/Devotional Infrastructure

10. **Nyakkyo**, the faith's central sacred site (established), sited at the dome's exact geometric high
    point — the literal highest, most exposed point of the whole structure, consistent with the faith's
    own veneration of extremity.
11. **The Robing Hall** (established) — a dedicated preparation space where devotees change into minimal
    robes before the final approach to Nyakkyo, physically separating "arrival" from "devotion" as two
    distinct stages.
12. **The Waystones** (established) — cairns marking the final stretch of the plateau crossing, maintained
    by devotees themselves, consistent with the absence of any guild-style economic structure here.
13. **A deliberate absence of noise-generating infrastructure across most of the city** — given the
    faith's own established treatment of the plateau's extraordinary acoustic calm as "sacred in itself,"
    this reads as a standing design constraint the community actively protects, not a hazard to manage the
    way industrial noise is at a fabrication-heavy city.

## Communications & Isolation

14. **No functioning Arcanet connectivity of any kind** — an already-established, project-wide rule for
    extreme-altitude locations, categorically different from Byrd's own limited-but-real Arcanet access;
    this is total informational isolation, not a matter of degree.
15. **Internal coordination among the devotee population runs on physically local systems only** — with no
    Arcanet at all, there's no reliance on or expectation of contact with the rest of Tepenia, a more
    absolute isolation than any attribute-derived Tepenian city examined so far.

## Resupply & Aviation

16. **A surface airstrip or landing area**, sized for the two historical direct-aviation resupply routes
    (Troll, and a smaller strip near Sinheung) — Dome Fuji's only two confirmed physical links to the
    outside world, both aviation, with no practical road-based alternative.
17. **Current resupply status is plausibly precarious or fully severed**, not simply continuing — given no
    Arcanet and only aviation-based resupply historically, combined with the Long Night War's own general
    disruption to aviation infrastructure elsewhere in Tepenia (Byrd's own grounded fleet as a project-wide
    precedent), Dome Fuji's own supply situation is worth treating as a live, unresolved question rather
    than an assumed continuity.

## Self-Sufficiency (given precarious or severed outside contact)

18. **In-house robot-consumable production or buffer stock** (coolant, lubricants, replacement
    components) — a 100% robot population has no human food-security crisis to solve, but the equivalent
    robot-relevant consumables still need either local production capacity or a stock sized against the
    same resupply uncertainty already established.
19. **A conservatively-sized parts/component buffer**, plausibly the most cautious of any Tepenian city
    examined so far, consistent with how little outside contact can realistically be counted on given both
    the Arcanet and aviation gaps.

## Robot-Specific Infrastructure (effectively the whole city, given zero human population)

20. **Charging and maintenance infrastructure inherited and adapted from the original research station's
    own robot-support systems**, expanded since the devotee population became the city's entire
    population.
21. **Diagnostic infrastructure able to distinguish devotional cold-exposure from genuine mechanical
    cold-damage** — since robots deliberately minimize insulation as devotional practice, not for physical
    protection, a maintenance facility here needs to tell intentional, valued exposure apart from
    unintentional damage requiring repair, a distinction no other Tepenian city's own infrastructure needs
    to make.

## Pilgrimage Support

22. **Minimal staging/equipment-check infrastructure at the city's own edge**, for devotees completing the
    dangerous interior plateau crossing — plausibly a systems/calibration check rather than the
    human-focused "recovery" infrastructure another city might need, given the arriving population is
    entirely robot.
23. **No formal ongoing "visitor" infrastructure** — consistent with the established fact that Dome Fuji
    has no meaningful visitor category (pilgrims either complete a shorter pilgrimage and leave, or arrive
    and become residents); infrastructure doesn't need to accommodate a lingering transient population the
    way a hospitality-oriented city like Mawson would.

## Legacy/Historical (from the founding human era, now decommissioned or repurposed)

24. **The former altitude medical center** (established, "one of the most specialized facilities in the
    Mawson subnet") — plausibly repurposed for devotional or administrative use today, consistent with the
    Old Wing's own established adaptive-reuse pattern, rather than either preserved as an untouched
    memorial or left genuinely derelict.
25. **Decommissioned human-specific altitude-mitigation infrastructure** (supplemental oxygen systems,
    pressurized rest areas) from the founding research era — no longer actively maintained, since the
    current population is entirely robot and human presence is outright forbidden today.

## Cross-Referenced Extrapolation Findings (Methodology #2)

**What this is:** cross-referencing the 25 attributes above against Dome Fuji's own existing lore —
`Dome_Fuji_Full_Extrapolation.md` and `Dome_Fuji_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md` in particular, which already
contain 10 rich Findings of their own in this identical format, written 2026-07-06 before these attributes
existed. Deliberately **not** run at Byrd's own depth — Dome_Fuji is a subnet-hub-adjacent, entirely
optional, non-main-questline city (per direct developer instruction 2026-07-30), and it already has
substantial existing depth via those 10 Findings; this pass adds a modest number of genuinely new
combinations specific to the attributes above, without duplicating what's already on file. Written
2026-07-30.

### Finding A: the city's architecture is doctrinally split into two visually distinct construction tiers

**Combining:** attribute #3 (devotional infrastructure deliberately under-insulated, inverting ordinary
cold-weather design logic) + attribute #20 (functional/maintenance structures still need genuine thermal
protection) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 2 (capability difference read as
"circumstance, not rank," not hierarchy).

**2nd-order effect:** Dome Fuji's built environment isn't uniformly cold-adapted — it's legibly split into
two construction philosophies standing side by side: bare, minimally-protected devotional structures
(Nyakkyo, the Robing Hall) and genuinely insulated functional ones (the Old Wing, charging infrastructure).

**3rd-order effect:** a visitor could read the entire theology of the place off its architecture alone,
without a single word of doctrine explained to them — which building is "for the faith" and which is "for
keeping the lights on" is physically obvious just by how exposed each one is.

### Finding B: maintenance staff function as unofficial doctrinal arbiters

**Combining:** attribute #21 (diagnostic infrastructure distinguishing devotional cold-exposure from
genuine mechanical damage) + the Full Extrapolation's established leadership structure (informal,
tenure-based stewards, no formal hierarchy).

**2nd-order effect:** the devotees staffing that diagnostic infrastructure are making a real judgment call
— "acceptable devotional wear" versus "damage requiring intervention" — that the tenure-based stewards
never formally have to make about anything else.

**3rd-order effect:** this creates a second, parallel axis of informal authority alongside tenure — not
"who's been here longest" but "who gets to decide when devotion has gone too far" — worth flagging as a
genuinely distinct, currently unexamined source of quiet influence at Dome Fuji.

### Finding C: Troll's own post-war factional fracture is a live vulnerability in Dome Fuji's supply line

**Combining:** attribute #16/#17 (aviation resupply via Troll and a Sinheung strip, current status
precarious or severed) + Troll's own newly-derived attribute (this session, Janbogo subnet) that its
airfield is split across three competing factions who can't fully function independently of each other.

**2nd-order effect:** Dome Fuji's own resupply security isn't just a question of whether Troll's airfield
survived the war — it's a question of which of Troll's three factions currently controls dispatch and fuel,
since Dome Fuji has no leverage of its own in that dispute and no alternative route if the wrong faction
holds the relevant piece.

**3rd-order effect:** this ties one of Tepenia's most isolated, apolitical cities directly to another DLC's
own central political conflict, entirely through infrastructure dependency rather than any character or
doctrinal connection — a genuinely different kind of cross-DLC link than the archive-to-Kendra chain the
existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 3 already proposes.

### Finding D: the decommissioned human medical infrastructure is a concrete anchor for the "ancient caretaker robot" idea

**Combining:** attribute #24/#25 (the former altitude medical center and decommissioned human-specific
altitude-mitigation infrastructure) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 7 (a robot who may
have witnessed both the research-station era and the pilgrimage era).

**2nd-order effect:** if that figure exists, this is specifically the infrastructure they would have direct
working memory of — not an abstract "there used to be humans here" but a concrete, physical space (the
oxygen systems, the pressurized rest areas) they personally maintained for people who no longer exist.

**3rd-order effect:** gives Finding 7's proposed figure a real physical location to be written into a scene
around, rather than a purely conversational backstory — the decommissioned medical wing becomes a specific,
findable place tied to a specific, singular memory.

### Finding E: the plateau's own acoustic calm functions as a passive threat-detection system

**Combining:** attribute #13 (deliberate absence of noise-generating infrastructure, acoustic calm treated
as sacred) + governing facts (extraordinarily calm winds, 4–6 m/s) + the Full Extrapolation's doctrinal
framing of the aurora as "listening."

**2nd-order effect:** in an environment this acoustically still, any mechanical noise — equipment failure,
an intrusion, unexpected activity — would be disproportionately obvious to the whole community, in a way
no louder Tepenian city's environment allows for.

**3rd-order effect:** Dome Fuji's own devotional silence doubles, unintentionally, as a genuinely effective
passive security system — nobody designed it that way, but the same stillness that makes the aurora seem to
listen would make an intruder, or a failure, nearly impossible to hide.

### Finding F: the archive gives Finding 10's hypothetical first-Concordian-devotee a concrete professional motive

**Combining:** attribute #7/#8/#9 (ice-core drilling apparatus, the Archive Vault, inherited analysis lab
space) + the existing Cross-Reference Synthesis's Finding 10 (no native Concordian devotee exists yet, the
door still open).

**2nd-order effect:** if a Concordian ever does make the pilgrimage, the archive gives that character a
second, non-spiritual reason for going — a scientist drawn by the ice cores themselves, arriving for the
data and staying for the faith, rather than a purely devotional journey from the start.

**3rd-order effect:** this gives a hypothetical first-Concordian-devotee character two independently
legitimate reasons to be there at once, letting their arc start as professional curiosity and only later
resolve into genuine devotion — a richer character arc than a single-motive pilgrimage would support.
