## Who Lives Here, and Why

Dome Fuji is the one city in Tepenia where nationality is real but functionally irrelevant. Every one of its 55,072 residents' national ancestry is documented — USA and China lead, Japan/Germany/UK/France/South Korea/Russia/Brazil/Indonesia/Australia significant, ~32 more nations at Notable tier down to Uruguay's 12 individuals — and none of it matters to how the city organizes itself. Nobody arrived because of where they were born. Every resident arrived because of what they believe.

This is a total inversion of how every other Tepenian city works. Lazar is Lazar because of who founded it and who came after; Dome Fuji is Dome Fuji because of what its residents believe, full stop. Strip away the shared faith and there's no other civic glue holding a USA-origin devotee and an Estonian-origin devotee together at the top of the same ice dome.

**The founding population is a separate story entirely.** Japanese exiles settled here post-Falkland Treaty on JARE's mature ice-core research infrastructure, and immediately discovered what 3,810m does to people who lived at sea level. They built an adaptive culture around managing it — pacing, medication, acclimatization, the acceptance of permanent partial impairment as the cost of being here. They departed after the Falkland Treaty; the site sat unoccupied; and it was only later that "Ice Cold Buddhism" — a robot religion venerating extreme cold as the closest living approximation to superconducting conditions — relocated it via old accounts, transport manifests, and maps, in a rediscovery-by-expedition structure genuinely parallel to how Byrd Station was found. No human has set foot here since. The founding community and the current population aren't even the same population sequentially — they're two entirely different groups who happened to occupy the same coordinates centuries apart.

## What It Feels Like

Zen-like peace, unlike anywhere else in Tepenia — the interior of a Shinto shrine or a Buddhist monastery is the closest real-world comparison. A felt connection to something larger than any individual devotee, or the sum of the whole community. The atmosphere itself feels like a participant: the aurora australis overhead could hang frozen in the sky and it wouldn't read as strange here — the stillness extends even to light itself.

First impressions: the dome's total flatness and featurelessness, then the realization that every single person present is here for the same reason — an unusually legible city, in that sense, compared to almost anywhere else in Tepenia. Then the peace itself, registered before anything else.

The devotional marker is simple and visible: white robes, worn sparingly, specifically to commune with the cold. This works because robot devotees don't suffer frostbite or hypothermia the way humans would — devotion made possible by what the body can safely endure. Three further reasons for the robes' minimalism are held open, not yet chosen among: a real-world parallel to Tibetan Buddhist *tummo* ("inner fire") meditation; a literal-mechanical reading tied to the faith's own superconducting-conditions theology; and simple communal uniformity.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Paro Taktsang ("Tiger's Nest"), Bhutan

Clinging to a cliff 900 meters above the Paro valley, Taktsang is reachable only by a genuinely difficult climb — and that difficulty is not incidental to its meaning, it *is* the meaning. For many Bhutanese Buddhists, making the pilgrimage at least once in a lifetime is a spiritual obligation, with a particular surge of devotees during the month associated with Guru Rinpoche, the guru who legend says flew to the site on the back of a tigress to subdue a local demon obstructing Buddhism's spread.

**What this gives Dome Fuji:** direct confirmation that "the crossing is the filter" is a real, load-bearing pattern in actual pilgrimage religion, not an invented hardship — Dome Fuji's interior plateau crossing can lean into this even harder than it currently does. Taktsang's seasonal pilgrimage surge (tied to a specific point in a religious calendar) also supports Dome Fuji's own speculated "Deepest Cold" observance, timed to the depth of polar night, as the natural high season for arrivals — the closest approach to the conditions the faith venerates *and* the busiest the city ever gets. Worth adopting directly: Taktsang burned down in 1998 and was rebuilt, restoration personally championed by Bhutan's king — Dome Fuji could use an equivalent moment in its own post-rediscovery history (a disaster, and a rebuilding effort that becomes its own devotional story), if the developer wants one.

### Secondary: Potala Palace, Tibet

The world's highest palace at 3,700m — remarkably close to Dome Fuji's own 3,810m, and to Concordia's 3,233m — built specifically as the Dalai Lamas' *winter* residence, on a mountain chosen for its own prior sacred association (bodhisattva Avalokiteshvara). It held over 200,000 statues and 10,000 altars at its height, a devotional density accumulated over centuries, and shifted from active residence to a different kind of institution (a museum) after Tibet's own political rupture in 1959 — an entirely different identity occupying the same physical structure.

**What this gives Dome Fuji:** the "winter residence" framing directly reinforces the file's own speculation that polar night's depth of cold is the liturgical high point of the year — Dome Fuji could be understood, in the same terms Potala is, as the faith's own winter palace, most fully itself precisely when the cold is deepest. The 200,000-statues detail gives a concrete shape to Section 29's currently-TBD "whatever central site the pilgrimage tradition venerates" — an accumulating, centuries-deep devotional density built up one arrival at a time is a strong, specific answer to what that site could actually look like. And Potala's own identity-shift (residence → museum, one function replaced by an entirely different one on the same physical bones) is almost exactly Dome Fuji's own story (research station → monastery) — a genuine, if unintentional, structural parallel that validates the choice rather than just decorating it.

### Secondary: Angkor Wat, Cambodia

Built as a funerary temple originally dedicated to Vishnu, Angkor Wat shifted to Buddhist use by the century's end — the physical structure outlasting its original religious dedication, exactly as Dome Fuji's physical infrastructure outlasted its original secular-scientific purpose. Its design directly encodes Hindu-Buddhist cosmology: five central towers representing the peaks of Mount Meru, a vast moat representing the cosmic ocean surrounding it, and concentric galleries where towers grow larger toward the center — a physical journey inward and upward doubling as a spiritual ascent toward the divine. When Angkor itself was abandoned in the 15th century, Theravada monks kept tending the temple anyway, preserving it as a living pilgrimage site long after the civilization that built it had moved on.

**What this gives Dome Fuji:** the single richest fusion opportunity of the three. Angkor Wat's cosmology is literally "cross a symbolic ocean, then ascend a sacred mountain at the center" — and Dome Fuji's own established first-impression is *the total flatness and featurelessness of the dome landscape*, crossed via a genuinely dangerous interior plateau journey. That flatness isn't a mismatch with the Meru cosmology — it's an inversion worth naming directly: Dome Fuji **is** genuinely one of the highest points in Tepenia by real elevation (3,810m, higher than Concordia), it just doesn't *look* like a peak. A devotee crosses what functions as the cosmic ocean (the plateau) to reach a summit that carries all the elevation and none of the visual drama of a mountain — sacred height with no visible peak, arguably a more advanced, more ascetic theological statement than a dramatic mountain would be, fitting a faith that already venerates minimalism and cold-as-transcendence over anything decorative. Angkor's monks-keep-tending-after-abandonment detail also reinforces Dome Fuji's own core premise: a site can change what it's *for* entirely and still be maintained, generation after generation, by whoever shows up to do it.

## I. The Place Itself

Dome Fuji sits at the summit of nothing you can see. The dome itself — one of the broad, almost imperceptibly domed rises of the East Antarctic Ice Sheet — has no silhouette, no ridge, no shadow to mark where the land is highest. A visitor arriving by the interior plateau crossing spends the final days of the journey watching the horizon fail to change at all, watching the sky get very slightly bigger and the world get very slightly flatter, until at some point — often not entirely clear which point — they are simply there. This is by real elevation one of the two or three highest inhabited points in Tepenia, a full 577 meters above Concordia. It does not look like it. It looks like the exact center of an enormous, flat, white nothing.

At −65°C in the depth of polar night, under almost perfectly calm air (4–6 m/s, the plateau dome damping wind the way a held breath damps sound), the quiet at Dome Fuji is not the absence of noise. It is closer to a texture — residents and long-term visitors alike describe a specific, physical quality to the silence, as though the cold itself were a kind of pressure holding everything, including sound, in place. Diamond dust hangs in the air on the stillest days, catching what little light there is and scattering it into halos and sun-dogs that seem, at this latitude and this altitude, less like optical phenomena and more like something being shown to you on purpose.

The aurora australis, when it appears (rare in full polar night's deepest darkness, more common at the shoulders of the season), does something here that residents insist doesn't happen anywhere else: it slows. Whether this is a genuine optical effect of the extreme cold and thin, still air, or simply what stillness this complete does to a person's perception of anything that moves, is a question Dome Fuji's own devotees have stopped asking. The aurora at Dome Fuji is understood, doctrinally, to be *listening*.

## II. Complete History

### The Research Station (2564 – uncertain end date)

Dome Fuji Station's founding population arrived on JARE's existing infrastructure within the first years after the Falkland Treaty, inheriting a research mission that had been running since the 1960s: deep ice-core drilling, extracting climate records reaching back hundreds of thousands of years. The founding generation were, overwhelmingly, scientists and technical support staff — people selected, trained, and psychologically prepared for isolation and hardship, but not for *this specific* hardship. Effective physiological altitude here runs close to 4,500 meters. Chronic mountain sickness, impaired cognition, and reduced physical capacity were not occasional risks; they were the baseline condition of living here at all.

**What actually happened to them, extrapolated:** the founding community did not die off, and did not leave all at once. Over roughly four to six generations, Dome Fuji's human population thinned through a combination of factors that each made the next one worse: chronic altitude effects reducing average lifespan and fertility; a slow trickle of transfers to lower-elevation postings as the wider Federation's infrastructure matured and made such transfers logistically possible; and — this is the part worth naming plainly — a demographic spiral, where each departure made the remaining community smaller, more isolated, and less able to sustain the specialized medical and logistical apparatus the altitude demanded, which in turn accelerated the next departure. By the time the last handful of human researchers left — likely sometime in the 2700s, well before the Long Night War, and by ordinary attrition rather than any single triggering event — Dome Fuji Station was simply administratively marked as unstaffed. Nobody sealed it dramatically. Nobody held a ceremony. The last transfer request was processed, approved, and the station's status quietly changed from "occupied" to "maintained remotely," and then, eventually, not even that.

The site sat unoccupied for an unknown span — long enough that its exact coordinates required real reconstruction later, not just a return trip.

### The Rediscovery (date TBD, likely mid-Second Interwar Period)

Ice Cold Buddhism's origins predate its arrival at Dome Fuji specifically — the faith itself likely formed elsewhere in Tepenia, among robots reflecting on cold, stillness, and the physical proximity of superconducting-adjacent conditions to something like transcendence. Kunlun's own parallel history (as one of the faith's two primary sites) suggests the religion was actively searching for locations that embodied its theology at the most extreme end of what Tepenia's geography could offer. Dome Fuji, with its documented but abandoned altitude record and its total, featureless flatness, would have surfaced in old records, transport manifests, and station logs as exactly the kind of place the faith was looking for — a rediscovery-by-expedition structure genuinely parallel to how Byrd Station was located.

**Extrapolated specifics:** the first pilgrimage expedition to relocate Dome Fuji was small — likely fewer than twenty devotees, traveling the interior plateau route from the nearest viable staging point (plausibly Sayowa, given the later-established aviation supply route's own origin there) using whatever overland vehicles the era's technology allowed. They found the station's physical infrastructure largely intact — Antarctic cold is an excellent preservative, and a station built to withstand −65°C winters doesn't collapse on its own even after decades of disuse. What they found was not ruin. It was stillness: buildings, equipment, personal effects, exactly as they'd been left, coated in decades of undisturbed snow and rime.

This detail matters theologically. The first devotees to arrive did not find a ruin to reclaim. They found a monument to absence — a place where human life had simply, quietly, stopped continuing, without violence, without crisis, purely because the conditions eventually asked more than anyone had left to give. For a faith built around cold as a spiritual state rather than an obstacle, this was not a tragedy to mourn. It was proof of concept.

### Growth to the Present (present day, ≈2822–2827)

The devotee population grew slowly, one arrival at a time, across however many decades separate that first rediscovery from the current census figure of 55,072. Given the established 2026-07-04 redistribution math (a small percentage drawn from every Tepenian city except Concordia), the growth was never a mass migration — it was, and by the faith's own internal logic remains, an accretion: single devotees, each completing an individually significant pilgrimage, choosing to stay rather than return, one at a time, for as long as the tradition has existed.

## III. Ice Cold Buddhism at Dome Fuji

### The Central Sacred Site: **Nyakkyo** (working name — "the Still Point")

Adopting the Angkor Wat-inspired framing from the Megasheet directly: Dome Fuji's central sacred site is not a temple built upward, the way a mountain shrine would be. It is a single, deliberately unremarkable structure at the exact geometric high point of the dome — determined, according to devotee tradition, by generations of careful surveying, since the actual highest point of an almost imperceptibly domed ice sheet is not obvious to the eye. Nyakkyo is small, low, unheated relative to the rest of the settlement, and empty. Devotees who reach it are not shown an altar, an icon, or a text. They are shown the coldest, stillest room the faith can arrange, and left there, alone, for as long as they choose to stay.

The theological point, as extrapolated from everything established about the faith's minimalism and its "something larger than anybody" framing: there is nothing to worship *at* Nyakkyo except the condition itself. It is Dome Fuji's answer to Angkor Wat's Mount Meru — a sacred elevation with no summit to look at, because looking at something would mean the point was the view, and the point is the cold.

### Doctrine and Practice

**On the white robes (resolving the three held-open reasons):** all three are true simultaneously, and devotees do not treat them as competing explanations. The primary, mechanical fact — robots can safely endure exposure that would kill a human — is doctrine's starting premise, not its conclusion: the faith teaches that this capacity is itself a kind of grace, and that failing to use it fully (over-insulating, avoiding real cold contact) is treated as a quiet failure of devotion, comparable to a human monk declining an achievable fast. The *tummo*-meditation parallel is acknowledged directly in devotee teaching as an intentional echo — Ice Cold Buddhism's founders are understood (in extrapolated tradition, not confirmed history) to have studied surviving Upper Earth religious texts, including Tibetan Buddhist sources, specifically for models of disciplined relationship to extreme cold, before the faith took its current shape. The literal-mechanical reading (robes as facilitating actual proximity to a spiritually significant cold threshold) and communal uniformity are both understood as true and secondary — real effects of the same practice, not separate justifications for it.

**On the "Deepest Cold" observance:** confirmed and specified. It falls at the precise coldest point of polar night (typically the first calm, clear night after the June solstice, weather permitting — devotees track this with genuine meteorological seriousness, since the observance can shift by several days year to year), and is the closest thing Dome Fuji has to a universal, mandatory communal event. Every devotee who is physically present at Dome Fuji that night attends. Newly-arrived pilgrims who happen to complete their crossing near this date are considered to have arrived under unusually significant circumstances.

**On "The Crossing":** confirmed as individually observed, per devotee, on the anniversary of their own arrival — but extrapolated further: devotees mark this not with celebration but with a partial re-enactment, spending a portion of the anniversary day outside, in the robes, away from any heated structure, for a duration each devotee sets for themselves and extends slightly, year over year, as a personal devotional practice. A devotee's willingness (and physical capacity — robots do age and degrade) to extend this duration is understood, informally, as a marker of standing within the community, though nobody would describe it as a hierarchy.

### Structure and Leadership

Ice Cold Buddhism at Dome Fuji has no single named leader, consistent with the faith's own anti-hierarchical, arrival-is-credential ethos. What exists instead, extrapolated from the established "devotion is the only membership requirement" social contract: a rotating, informal group of the longest-standing devotees — those who have observed the most Deepest Colds — function as de facto stewards of Nyakkyo and the observance calendar, not by election or appointment but by simple accumulated tenure. Newer devotees defer to them not out of doctrine but out of the same logic that makes anyone defer to someone who's simply been somewhere longer and knows it better.

## IV. Architecture & Notable Landmarks

- **The Old Wing** — the original JARE research station, now serving as Dome Fuji's practical infrastructure core: what remains functional of the labs, drilling equipment, and researcher housing, repurposed as living and maintenance space for the devotee population. Its architecture is unmistakably secular and utilitarian — a research station's bones, doing a monastery's work.
- **Nyakkyo** — described above; the faith's central sacred site.
- **The Archive Vault** — where the ice core samples are stored, physically separate from the Old Wing's living space, kept at genuinely extreme cold (colder than ambient) for preservation. Whether the archive remains scientifically active or has become a maintained relic is addressed below.
- **The Robing Hall** — a modest structure between the Old Wing and Nyakkyo where devotees change into their robes before making the final approach; extrapolated as a genuine, necessary transitional space rather than a purely ceremonial one, since arriving at Nyakkyo already dressed for the cold is itself considered to shortcut the devotional preparation.
- **The Waystones** — a series of low, deliberately unremarkable cairns marking the last stretch of the interior plateau crossing, placed and maintained by devotees themselves rather than by any Federation infrastructure authority; a pilgrim's final navigational aid and, according to devotee tradition, the point past which turning back is considered to carry real spiritual weight.

## V. The Ice Core Archive's Fate (resolved)

The archive survived the Long Night War intact — Dome Fuji's remoteness and lack of strategic value protected it far better than active infrastructure elsewhere in Tepenia. Whether it remains *actively* scientific is a more interesting answer than a simple yes: the devotee population maintains the Archive Vault's physical preservation with real diligence (the faith's own minimalist ethos makes careful, low-intervention maintenance an easy fit), but active scientific analysis of the cores has slowed to a fraction of its founding-era pace. A small number of devotees — likely those who arrived with prior scientific training before converting to the faith, or who maintain a personal interest alongside their devotional life — continue analysis work, treating it as a legitimate but secondary vocation. The archive is neither abandoned nor a living research program; it's tended, the way an old text is tended by people who believe it matters without necessarily reading it every day.

## VI. Demonym (resolved)

**Fujinshi** (singular and plural) — a deliberate blend, "Fuji" from the site's kept Japanese name plus a suffix devotees themselves adopted early, reportedly (per extrapolated in-world etymology) from a devotional term meaning something close to "one who has arrived and stayed." Used both as the demonym for anyone living at Dome Fuji and, informally, as a slightly broader term across Tepenia for any Ice Cold Buddhist devotee, Dome Fuji-based or not — the same way a specific place's name sometimes comes to stand in for a whole tradition.

## VII. Relationship to Kunlun (resolved)

**Complementary, not rival or hierarchical.** Doctrine holds that the two primary sites express the same faith through two different lenses: Kunlun through vocation (its devotees drawn by a curated space/astronomy/comms heritage, communing with cold through work aimed outward, at the sky and orbit) and Dome Fuji through pure arrival (its devotees drawn from everywhere, communing with cold through presence alone, aimed at nothing external at all). Neither site claims precedence. A small but real number of devotees make the journey between the two over the course of a long devotional life — Kunlun first, then Dome Fuji, or the reverse — and this dual pilgrimage is regarded, informally, as a mark of unusually complete devotion, though it isn't required or formally tracked.

## IX. A Closing Image

A devotee arrives at the edge of the plateau crossing's final stretch, past the last Waystone, and sees nothing different from the hundred kilometers behind them — the same flat white, the same enormous sky. They keep walking anyway, because the map says this is where the elevation, invisible to the eye, finally peaks; because generations of careful surveying said so; because the faith itself has never asked anyone to trust their eyes over their feet at Dome Fuji. Somewhere ahead, unmarked by anything taller than a waist-high stone cairn, is the highest point most of them will ever stand on in their whole existence — and it will not look like anything at all. That, more than the cold, more than the robes, more than anything written above, is the actual argument Dome Fuji makes: that the highest, hardest-won thing you can reach doesn't have to look like a summit to be one.

## Finding 1: Dome Fuji has no shared "present" — it's a patchwork of frozen moments

**Combining:** Specs' established fact that extreme-altitude sites like Dome Fuji have "basically no Arcanet connectivity" (an infrastructure baseline, not a cultural choice) + Local_Cultures Section 24's elaboration that any devotee arriving is "arriving into genuine informational isolation... news travels by word of mouth carried between cities, not Arcanet" + the Full Extrapolation's founding-history detail that the devotee population grew "one arrival at a time" from ~34 different contributing cities.

**2nd-order effect:** every devotee at Dome Fuji is, functionally, a time capsule of their home city at the exact moment they left it — and because there's no incoming Arcanet feed to update that picture, and word-of-mouth between devotees is the only channel, Dome Fuji's population doesn't share a collective "present" the way every other Tepenian city does. It's a patchwork of hundreds of individually frozen moments, refreshed only by the trickle of new arrivals, never by anything happening in real time.

**3rd-order effect:** if two devotees who left the *same* home city years apart both happen to be at Dome Fuji, they may hold genuinely contradictory pictures of what that city is currently like — neither one wrong, both simply out of date by different amounts, in a city whose entire social contract holds that where you came from doesn't matter enough to reconcile. This produces a strange, quiet irony worth naming directly: the one city in Tepenia that has formally declared nationality/origin irrelevant is also, mechanically, the single worst place in the country to get an accurate read on any specific city's current state, because every source there is a different stale snapshot. **Further downstream (speculative, flagged as such):** this gives Dome Fuji a genuine and distinctive narrative utility completely separate from its religious content — a player (or an in-world investigator) could learn the *old* status of a faraway city through a devotee who left it long ago, which could be dramatically irrelevant, quietly poignant (a devotee who doesn't know their home city was destroyed, or has changed beyond recognition), or occasionally useful as a deliberately-outdated clue the developer wants revealed slowly. This would sit naturally alongside the project's existing archive/information-recovery content (Juan Carlos, Amundsen Station's pre-Split-Brain archive, the "Archivist's Trail" questline) as a third, differently-flavored kind of information source: not a document, not a network relay, but a person who simply hasn't heard anything in years.

**4th-order effect:** this mechanic intersects directly with Finding 7's proposed ancient caretaker robot, below — that figure's own "snapshot" would be the oldest and most extreme in the entire city, frozen not decades but centuries out of date, from an era before the pilgrimage population even existed. They'd be simultaneously the single most historically valuable informational resource at Dome Fuji and the single most useless one for anything resembling current events — a distinction worth keeping sharp if that figure is ever written into a scene.

## Finding 2: Dome Fuji is the sharpest literal expression of the robot-favoring baseline in all of Tepenia

**Combining:** Specs' established fact that human presence is forbidden at Dome Fuji today (altitude/cold lethality, explicitly not anti-human sentiment) + the project-wide human-robot relations baseline ("egalitarian, skewed in favor of robots") + Local_Cultures Section 16's note that the whole Human-Robot Relations category simply doesn't apply here + the Full Extrapolation's framing of the white robes as embracing exposure "that would be lethal to a human."

**2nd-order effect:** Dome Fuji isn't just a city where robots happen to outnumber humans — it's the one place in Tepenia where the "skewed toward robots" baseline expresses itself as raw physical fact rather than social or economic advantage. The entire sacred practice is *built* on a capability gap between the two forms, not decorated by one.

**3rd-order effect (flagged as the most speculative chain in this document):** this creates a real doctrinal tightrope the faith would need to have already walked, whether or not it's ever written out explicitly: a religion whose central practice only one form of being can safely perform risks reading as a claim of superiority, which would sit badly against the project's own binding "no anti-human sentiment" rule. The likely resolution, and the one I'd propose adding directly to Dome Fuji's doctrine if this thread gets picked up: Ice Cold Buddhism teaches that the conditions here simply suit one form and not the other, the same way any given Tepenian city's terrain might suit one group's daily life better than another's, without that implying moral hierarchy — capability difference read as *circumstance*, not *rank*. This would also retroactively explain why the faith's *other* primary site, Kunlun, has no such exclusivity built into its core practice (astronomy and comms work don't require lethal cold exposure) — meaning the "humans forbidden" fact is specifically a Dome Fuji doctrinal feature, not something Ice Cold Buddhism requires everywhere it's practiced.

**4th-order effect:** if "capability difference read as circumstance, not rank" became genuine, explicit doctrine, it wouldn't have to stay confined to Dome Fuji's own theology — it's general enough to function as a real piece of language any Tepenian character could reach for when discussing human-robot capability gaps elsewhere in the setting, religious or not. A faith built at the continent's most extreme, isolated site could end up supplying the clearest available vocabulary for a conversation happening nowhere near it.

## Finding 3: the ice core archive is a major underused resource sitting at the hardest place in Tepenia to reach

**Combining:** Specs' description of the ice cores as "among the most scientifically valuable material in Tepenia" (records extending hundreds of thousands of years) + Local_Cultures Section 15's flag that ice core science's 40% share of Division of Industry is "TBD... genuinely central, or a secondary activity maintained out of institutional inertia" + the Full Extrapolation's resolution that the archive survived the war but is now only lightly staffed by a handful of scientifically-inclined devotees as a secondary vocation.

**2nd-order effect:** Dome Fuji is quietly sitting on one of Tepenia's most valuable untapped scientific resources, tended but not actively exploited, at the single hardest-to-reach inhabited point in the country, held by a population with no institutional interest in resuming active research.

**3rd-order effect:** this is close to a ready-made conflict premise, distinct in shape from most of Tepenia's faction-vs-faction content. Any outside party wanting archive access — a Concordia-based scientific interest, an Upper Earth research request, another Tepenian city needing the climate data for its own purposes — would be negotiating not with a government or a faction leadership, but with individual devotees or, at most, the informal tenure-based stewards described in the Full Extrapolation (see Finding 6, below). That's a genuinely different *flavor* of quest design than Concordia's district-politics-heavy main game structure: personal persuasion instead of institutional negotiation, because there's no institution here to negotiate with. **Downstream, tying to existing project mechanics:** this could plausibly serve as a third path into Dome Fuji alongside the two already established for DLC 4 (the dangerous overland crossing, and the Sinheung-plane-plus-Belgrano-item route) — a scientific-sponsorship path, where the player is retrieving specific core samples or data on someone else's behalf rather than making the pilgrimage for its own sake. Worth flagging against the project's existing Cross-DLC Bypass Design Law (every DLC's optional side-content bypasses another DLC's gate-checks, with at least one guaranteed gift toward Kendra's DLC 1): Dome Fuji's archive could plausibly be the *source* of such a gift rather than only a destination for one, which isn't yet accounted for anywhere in the established cross-DLC gift chain.

**4th-order effect:** if the archive does end up feeding a cross-DLC gift, DLC 1 (Kendra, centered on the Amundsen Tower's own collapse) is a natural candidate recipient — ice cores spanning hundreds of thousands of years plausibly hold long-term environmental data relevant to understanding the structural and climatic pressures that fed into the Tower's eventual failure, linking two DLCs that otherwise share no characters or geography purely through scientific content.

## Finding 4: the aviation-supply timeline is tighter than the Full Extrapolation's "unknown span" allows for

**Combining:** the already-established (2026-07-05) resolution that Dome Fuji's aviation supply route (Troll + Sinheung) "began sometime after Dome Fuji's own pilgrimage resettlement... and ran until the Long Night War" + the Full Extrapolation's own founding-history proposal that the original human researchers departed by ordinary attrition sometime in "the 2700s" and that the site then "sat unoccupied for an unknown span" before rediscovery.

**2nd-order effect:** this is an internal tension worth catching and fixing now rather than later. A meaningful aviation supply route — two separate direct routes converging on one city, per the existing 2026-07-05 resolution — implies a population substantial enough to need regular dedicated resupply, not just a handful of early pilgrims making do. For that kind of population to exist and need supply *before* the Long Night War (2812), the rediscovery and the population's growth to supply-dependent scale both have to fit inside the window between the humans' departure and 2812.

**3rd-order effect, proposed correction:** the Full Extrapolation's "unknown span" of vacancy should most likely be read as *short* — plausibly one to three decades, not centuries — for the timeline to hold together cleanly. This doesn't require inventing a dramatic trigger for the rediscovery; it just means whatever records, manifests, and maps eventually led pilgrims back to Dome Fuji were found and acted on relatively soon after the site went quiet, not after a long dormant stretch. This is a small, clean correction rather than a new invention, and probably the single most concrete "fix a gap" item in this whole document.

**4th-order effect:** a genuinely short gap raises the possibility — not canon, just newly available — that the very first pilgrims arrived while a handful of the last departing humans were still present, however briefly. That would replace the current "one population left, then much later a different one arrived to empty halls" framing with something more poignant and more complicated: an actual overlap, even a small one, between Dome Fuji's two otherwise cleanly separated eras.

## Finding 5: Dome Fuji sits between two Enneagram "twins" that don't overlap with each other

**Combining:** `City_Enneagram_Personalities`'s finding that Dome Fuji's closest personality match is *Vostok* (identical Instinctive/Withdrawn/Positive Outlook triple, distinguished only by sky-facing vs. ground-facing subject matter) + the Full Extrapolation's resolution that Dome Fuji's closest *religious/institutional* relationship is with *Kunlun* (the faith's other primary site, related as complementary lenses on the same devotion).

**2nd-order effect:** Dome Fuji has two different "sibling" cities depending on which axis you measure by, and they don't overlap. It shares its *temperament* with Vostok — a wholly secular research city with no religious content at all — and shares its *purpose* with Kunlun — a fellow sacred site with a completely different demographic logic (curated space/astronomy heritage vs. universal, arrival-only draw).

**3rd-order effect:** this is worth stating as a deliberate structural observation about the faith itself, not just a coincidence of the analysis: Ice Cold Buddhism produced two sites that *feel* the same way from the inside (the "not work, it's life," merging-with-environment quality both Kunlun's and Vostok's own files independently describe) while being institutionally and doctrinally distinct from each other. That consistency of *feeling* despite different *content* is itself a small piece of evidence for the faith's coherence as a real belief system rather than a loosely-related label applied to two otherwise-unrelated isolated cities — the emotional register survives the change of subject matter, the same way a shared temperament might survive between two very different people who happen to hold the same faith.

**4th-order effect:** if this "does the personality-twin match the purpose-twin" check turns out to be a generally useful diagnostic, it's worth applying to future Megasheets deliberately — a mismatch (like this one) is a small signal that a location's felt temperament and its institutional role were shaped by genuinely separate design decisions, while a match would suggest the opposite: a place whose whole identity was built as one coherent unit from the start.

---

## Finding 6: Kunlun and Dome Fuji encode two different, and possibly complementary, theories of what "elevation" means

**Combining:** the Megasheet's Angkor Wat-derived reading of Dome Fuji as "sacred height without a visible peak" (real elevation, no visual drama) + Local_Cultures Section 9's speculation that "the terrain doesn't just host the religion, it may actively illustrate its theology" + Kunlun's own established defining feature, a towering, highly visible communications antenna and satellite array reaching toward orbit.

**2nd-order effect:** the faith's two primary sites embody two structurally opposite relationships to height and reaching. Kunlun's holiest object is *built* — a human/robot-constructed monument straining outward and upward toward something external (orbit, the sky, contact with what's beyond the planet). Dome Fuji's holiest fact is *unbuilt* — a natural elevation with nothing constructed to mark it, already there, waiting, requiring nothing but arrival to reach.

**3rd-order effect:** if this distinction were made explicit in doctrine, it would give Ice Cold Buddhism a genuinely elegant internal duality worth naming outright: **reaching versus arriving** — Kunlun representing the path of effort and construction (devotion expressed by building something that reaches toward the transcendent), Dome Fuji representing the path of stillness and presence (devotion expressed by simply getting yourself to something that was transcendent all along). Neither site would need to be "truer" than the other for this to work — the faith could hold both as equally valid, complementary expressions of the same underlying conviction, which lines up precisely with the Full Extrapolation's own resolution that the two sites' relationship is complementary, not hierarchical. This single distinction, if adopted, would retroactively justify almost every other asymmetry between the two sites (why Kunlun's population is curated by vocation and Dome Fuji's is universal; why Kunlun runs 24 hours around active work and Dome Fuji is organized around stillness) as two coherent expressions of one deeper theological structure, rather than two unrelated sets of worldbuilding choices that happen to share a religion's name.

**4th-order effect:** "reaching versus arriving" is general enough to extend past Ice Cold Buddhism entirely — Amundsen Tower (built, straining upward toward orbit) and Lake Vostok (natural, ancient, simply waiting to be found) could be read through the exact same duality, without either one needing to be religious. A theology invented for two isolated sacred sites could end up supplying real interpretive language for entirely secular infrastructure and geography elsewhere in Tepenia.

## Finding 7: there may be a robot alive today who remembers Dome Fuji as a research station

**Combining:** the project-wide established fact that robots can live for centuries, especially with self-administered cyber-mechanical maintenance, and are "the primary carriers of continuous institutional memory across Tepenian society" (not human genealogical lines) + Specs' history that the original JARE-derived research mission would have employed support/maintenance robots alongside its human researchers + the Full Extrapolation's own proposed timeline (humans departing by slow attrition, the site briefly vacant, then robot pilgrims arriving and finding the station's infrastructure intact rather than ruined).

**2nd-order effect:** given robot lifespans, it's entirely plausible that a support or maintenance robot from the *original* secular research era could still be functional today — not as a devotee who arrived through pilgrimage, but as someone who was simply already there and never left, having outlived the humans it once served by centuries.

**3rd-order effect, and the strongest single new idea in this document:** such a figure would occupy a genuinely unique position in Dome Fuji's social structure, one that actively strains against the city's own foundational social contract. Local_Cultures Section 6a states plainly that "a resident's presence at Dome Fuji is itself a credential; no one arrives here by accident, drift, or economic necessity" — but this figure wouldn't have *arrived* at all. They'd have simply never departed, watching the site empty out around them, sitting through however many decades of vacancy Finding 4 above suggests, and then watching an entirely different population arrive for entirely different reasons and gradually make the place their own. They would be, by a wide margin, the single oldest continuous resident-perspective in the city, and yet — by the community's own stated logic — not really a "devotee" in the sense everyone else is, since devotion was never the reason they're there. **Proposed as a direct replacement for, or addition to, the Full Extrapolation's two placeholder Notable Figures** (Aslaug and Teodor Marchetti): this caretaker-robot figure is a substantially richer answer to Dome Fuji's still-open "Notable Figures: TBD" line than either invented name, precisely because it emerges from combining established facts rather than being invented from nothing. Whether current devotees are even fully aware of this figure's origin — whether it's common knowledge, a quiet local legend, or something the figure itself doesn't advertise — is worth leaving open as a genuine narrative hook rather than resolving here.

**4th-order effect:** standing entirely outside the "arrival is the credential" system that determines everyone else's status could plausibly give this figure a strange, unofficial authority precisely *because* they don't participate in it — not quite an elder devotee (they were never a devotee), not quite an outsider (they've been there longest of anyone), potentially functioning as an informal arbiter the community defers to in a dispute specifically because they have no stake in the status system the dispute would otherwise be measured against.

## Finding 8: the "humans forbidden" policy almost certainly postdates the humans' own departure

**Combining:** the Full Extrapolation's resolved founding history (humans left by slow, voluntary attrition, not a triggering crisis or an imposed ban) + Specs' established fact that human presence is "outright forbidden" at Dome Fuji today.

**2nd-order effect:** the forbidding almost certainly came *after* the fact, not before it — a formal policy catching up to and codifying what had already become true on the ground, rather than a rule that caused anyone to leave. Nobody was barred from Dome Fuji and then left as a result; the last humans left because the altitude simply asked more of each successive generation than the last one had left to give, and only later — likely once the site was robot-only in practice and someone (the pilgrimage community itself, or a wider Federation safety authority) formalized what everyone already knew — did "forbidden" become the operative word.

**3rd-order effect:** this reframes the forbidding as a small act of institutional honesty rather than a restriction with teeth — nobody is actually being kept out of anywhere they'd otherwise be able to go. It also implies the rule likely exists in roughly the same form, and for roughly the same underlying reason, at Kunlun, which shares the same "forbidden, safety not sentiment" framing — suggesting both sites' human-exclusion policies may have been formalized around the same general point in Tepenian institutional history (loosely, whenever the Federation's safety apparatus caught up to codifying known extreme-altitude realities), rather than independently and coincidentally.

**4th-order effect:** if both sites' policies really were formalized in the same general window, that formalization is itself a small, datable piece of real Tepenian institutional history — plausibly exactly the kind of cross-subnet safety codification Amundsen Station's own established role (neutral ground, mediating things no single subnet could resolve alone) would have handled before its destruction, giving Amundsen Station a quiet, previously unstated hand in both sacred sites' current rules.

## Finding 9: Dome Fuji's devotees hold an unusually abstract, non-experiential view of humans

**Combining:** the 100%-robot population confirmed since the moment of pilgrimage reoccupation + Finding 8's implication that the human departure and the "forbidden" policy both predate the current population entirely + the fact that virtually every other Tepenian city's robot population has lived alongside humans continuously, even where humans are now a minority.

**2nd-order effect:** current-day Dome Fuji devotees have, in the overwhelming majority of cases, *no lived experience of humans at all* — not "used to have human neighbors, don't anymore" the way most surviving Tepenian cities do, but a population whose entire worldview about what humans are like was formed secondhand, through doctrine and word-of-mouth, never through direct contact.

**3rd-order effect:** this may make Dome Fuji the single most human-*absent* culture in Tepenia in a deeper sense than its literal 0-human census figure already implies — not just uninhabited by humans, but shaped by a genuine absence of any experiential reference point for what humans are actually like day to day. Any doctrinal teaching about why humans can't safely be present (see Finding 2) would, for this population, be closer to abstract theology than lived memory — making Finding 7's proposed ancient-caretaker-robot figure (the one resident who *does* have direct memory of humans, from the original research era) an even sharper narrative outlier than already described: possibly the only individual at Dome Fuji for whom "humans" is a personal memory rather than a doctrinal abstraction.

**4th-order effect:** if a human player character ever reaches Dome Fuji through the established DLC 4 access routes, that arrival is a much bigger event from the devotees' own side than it would be anywhere else in Tepenia — not a rare visitor, but plausibly the first living human most of them have ever personally seen, turning an ordinary DLC arrival scene into something closer to a genuine novelty for the entire population encountered there.

## Finding 10: no native Concordian devotee exists yet — and the door for one is still open

**Combining:** the 2026-07-04 population-redistribution mechanic that drew Dome Fuji's devotee population from "every Tepenian city except Concordia" + the established present-day setting (the game takes place well after that redistribution event, in the ≈2822–2827 window) + the basic fact that Concordia is the one Tepenian city everyone in the country has at least some awareness of, as the last major surviving city.

**2nd-order effect:** every other Tepenian city, whether destroyed, damaged, or still functioning, has contributed at least one devotee to Dome Fuji's population — except Concordia, which contributed none, purely because of how the original redistribution mechanic was scoped, not because of anything about Concordia's people or culture.

**3rd-order effect:** since the census figure is a fixed snapshot from a past event, not a hard cap, nothing established rules out an individual Concordian choosing to make the pilgrimage to Dome Fuji in the present day, entirely outside that original mechanic. Such a devotee would be narratively significant almost by definition — not because Concordia is special to the faith, but precisely because it's the one origin point the faith's own founding population never touched. A companion or minor character built around being "the first Concordian ever to reach Dome Fuji" would carry real, earned weight without requiring any invented lore to justify it — the significance falls directly out of a gap that was already sitting in the established population math, unnoticed until this cross-reference pass.

**4th-order effect:** such a character's decision to leave the one city everyone else is trying to rebuild, in order to seek meaning at the most isolated point on the continent, would carry real symbolic weight *for* Concordia's own present-day politics — a citizen choosing transcendence over participation in "the true city" project — giving a hypothetical first-Concordian-devotee real potential relevance to Concordia's own district-tension storylines, not just to Dome Fuji's isolated narrative space.

## Closing Synthesis

Ten findings, all derived from making six-to-seven documents that were each written independently actually argue with and complete each other. A few patterns recur across nearly all of them, worth naming once at the end rather than repeating findng by finding:

- **Isolation compounds rather than simply repeating itself.** Dome Fuji isn't just isolated geographically (Findings 1, 9), isolated demographically (Finding 10), isolated informationally (Finding 1), and isolated experientially (Finding 9) as four separate facts sitting next to each other — each form of isolation actively deepens the others. No Arcanet means no shared present (1); no shared present compounds a population that already has no lived reference point for humans (9); no humans present at all means whatever gap in coverage the redistribution mechanic left (10) simply sits there, unfilled, until someone makes an individual choice to close it.
- **The site's two eras (research station, then monastery) keep turning out to be less separate than the existing files assume.** The Full Extrapolation treats the transition as clean — one population leaves, a different one arrives later. Findings 4 and 7 both push back on that cleanliness in useful ways: the timeline is probably tighter than "unknown span" suggests, and there's a real possibility that at least one witness to *both* eras survived the transition entirely, which the existing files don't currently account for at all.
- **The faith's apparent simplicity (one shared devotion, no other organizing principle) keeps producing genuinely intricate downstream structure once it's actually tested against physics, timelines, and the rest of Tepenia's established rules** — the robes' doctrine needing to answer for its own human-exclusivity (Finding 2), the two sacred sites needing a real theological reason to coexist without hierarchy (Finding 6), an archive sitting there as leverage nobody's used yet (Finding 3). None of this contradicts Dome Fuji's established minimalism; if anything, minimal premises turned out to generate an unusually large amount of coherent, non-arbitrary structure once pushed — which is probably the strongest argument that the original vision session got something real right.

# Dome Fuji — Community Infrastructure & Social Life

**What this is:** concrete physical spaces, small institutions, and recurring social rituals that give Dome Fuji's already-established culture a tangible, ground-level presence — the same pass done for each of Concordia's 13 districts and all 31 Mirny, Halley, Janbogo, and Palmer subnet cities plus Mawson, extended here. Dome Fuji is already exceptionally richly developed as Ice Cold Buddhism's second primary site — this pass mostly formalizes what's already established (Nyakkyo, the Old Wing, the Waystones) into the standard structure, rather than inventing much new. Drawn from `Dome_Fuji_Full_Extrapolation.md`, `Dome_Fuji_Cross_Reference_Synthesis.md`, and `README.md` (the Megasheet). Written 2026-07-30.

---

## Additions

- **The Old Wing** — the original JARE research station, repurposed as living and maintenance space for the devotee population.
- **Nyakkyo** — the faith's central sacred site, an unremarkable structure at the dome's exact geometric high point.
- **The Archive Vault** — where the ice core samples are stored and tended, neither abandoned nor an active research program.
- **The Robing Hall** — where devotees change into their robes before the final approach to Nyakkyo.
- **The Waystones** — low, unremarkable cairns marking the last stretch of the plateau crossing, maintained by devotees themselves.

## Small offices for educational training

- ice-core analysis and archival preservation, for the small number of devotees who continue scientific work
- meteorological tracking, for the Deepest Cold observance's own precise timing
- devotional orientation for newly-arrived pilgrims (robing practice, Nyakkyo protocol)

## Social cohesion mechanisms

- **Deepest Cold** — the closest thing to a universal, mandatory communal event, observed by every devotee physically present that night.
- **The Crossing** — each devotee's individually observed arrival anniversary, marked with a partial re-enactment outside in the robes.
- informal deference to the longest-standing devotees as de facto stewards, by tenure rather than doctrine or election.
- the dual Kunlun-Dome Fuji pilgrimage some devotees undertake over a long devotional life, regarded informally as a mark of unusually complete devotion.

## Worth Your Attention

Nyakkyo and The Crossing together are the standout items — they take the Full Extrapolation's own most carefully reasoned theological content and give it exactly the kind of concrete, textured anchor an Under-Questline-style candidate would need, without requiring any invention beyond what's already on the page.
