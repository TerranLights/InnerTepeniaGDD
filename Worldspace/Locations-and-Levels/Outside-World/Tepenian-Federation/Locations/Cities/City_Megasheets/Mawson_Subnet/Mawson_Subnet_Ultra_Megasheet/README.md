# Mawson Subnet — Cross-City Patterns

**What this is:** common factors recurring across Mawson subnet's cities — not causal chains (see Cross-City Throughways for those), but shared traits, structures, or tensions that show up independently in more than one city's own Megasheet. ## Pattern 1: Founding-population displacement, in three genuinely different shapes

All three Mawson subnet cities carry a "the people who founded this place are not the people who live here now" story — but each resolves it differently:

- **Mawson:** peaceful demographic dilution. The founding Australian population is now a Significant-tier minority behind a Chinese Primary majority, and the city has made deliberate, genuine civic peace with the gap — "The Name That Outlasted the Founders" as an accepted, even celebrated identity.
- **Sayowa:** institutional weight without demographic weight. Japan, the founding JARE nation, is diluted to Significant tier, but the Shōwa-era name still carries real, felt gravity for the smaller Japanese-heritage community specifically, even as it's functionally just an ordinary proper noun for the demographic majority.
- **Dome Fuji:** total population succession. Not dilution at all — the entire founding human population departed by slow attrition over generations, the site sat briefly vacant, and an entirely different population (100% robot, drawn by religious devotion rather than any national or ancestral connection) arrived later and made the place theirs completely. Nationality isn't diluted here; it's rendered irrelevant as an organizing category altogether.

**What this produces:** a single subnet containing the full spectrum of how a Tepenian city's founding population can be displaced — from Mawson's gentle drift to Dome Fuji's complete replacement — with Sayowa sitting as a genuine midpoint between the two. Worth using alongside Mirny subnet's own five-mechanism population-formation taxonomy (Pattern 1, Mirny Subnet Ultra-Megasheet) as a companion reference for how Tepenian cities' populations change shape over time, not just how they formed in the first place.

## Pattern 2: Japan's Tepenian diaspora experiment gains its most extreme data point in this subnet

Sayowa's own Cross-Reference Finding 1 already established that Japan's diaspora produced three distinct outcomes from three distinct mechanisms — Shirayuki (diplomatically protected, Primary), Princess Elisabeth (organic, co-Primary), and Sayowa itself (organic, unprotected, diluted to Significant). Dome Fuji adds a fourth data point this subnet's own prior research hadn't yet connected: also a JARE-founded, originally Japanese site, its outcome isn't merely diluted — the entire founding population is gone, replaced by a population for whom national origin doesn't apply as a category at all.

**What this produces:** the same national-origin diaspora now has four documented outcomes across the project, two of them (Sayowa, Dome Fuji) sitting side by side in this one subnet — ranging from Shirayuki's fullest protection to Dome Fuji's total erasure of the category itself. Worth flagging directly: this subnet alone contains both the most diluted (Sayowa) and the single most extreme (Dome Fuji) outcome of any Japanese-founded site in Tepenia.

## Pattern 3: No single Enneagram trait unifies the subnet — but every possible pair overlaps on a different axis

Mawson (Feeling/Compliant/Positive Outlook), Sayowa (Instinctive/Compliant/Competency), and Dome Fuji (Instinctive/Withdrawn/Positive Outlook) share no single trait across all three. But every pairing overlaps somewhere: Mawson and Sayowa share Compliant (both civic identities organized around meeting an external need — honeymooners, the highway network); Mawson and Dome Fuji share Positive Outlook (both frame their defining circumstance as something to embrace, not endure); Sayowa and Dome Fuji share Instinctive (both operate through direct, physical, non-analytical engagement with their environment).

**What this produces:** unlike Halley subnet's "Competency turned inward" or Mirny subnet's "function outliving the need for credit," Mawson subnet doesn't reduce to one collective personality even loosely — its three cities' temperaments genuinely diverge, connected only by a shifting web of partial overlaps rather than a shared center. Worth naming honestly as a structural difference from the project's other subnets, not smoothed over into a false unifying theme.

## Pattern 4: This subnet doesn't share one war-damage principle — it samples the project's whole accumulated framework in miniature

Mawson survived via a split outcome (its inter-subnet Arcanet link took the brunt of damage, its intra-subnet connections plausibly intact) — an echo of Fort McMurdo's own dispersed-function survival in Janbogo/Ross subnet. Sayowa survived via a genuinely new mechanism this project hadn't catalogued before its own Cross-Reference Finding 2 — strategic value preserved by an attacker's own calculated restraint, distinct from every damage pattern found in Halley, Janbogo/Ross, or Mirny subnets. Dome Fuji survived via sheer remoteness and lack of strategic value — the same mechanism already established for Vostok and Kunlun in Mirny subnet.

**What this produces:** unlike Mirny subnet's single-principle war-damage logic or Janbogo/Ross subnet's two-principle taxonomy, Mawson subnet's three cities don't share one damage logic at all — each one instead instances a *different* mechanism already established (or, in Sayowa's case, newly established) somewhere else in the project. This subnet functions less as its own self-contained damage-logic case and more as a working sampler of the whole project's accumulated framework, worth keeping in mind as the final comparison point now that all five subnets have been examined.

## Pattern 5: A subnet oriented outward more than inward

All three of this subnet's cities have a stronger, more developed relationship to a neighboring subnet than to each other. Sayowa's entire civic identity is built on bridging to Halley subnet (Princess Elisabeth, the Hwy 7-ext corridor). Dome Fuji's religious and personality life is inseparable from Kunlun, a Mirny subnet city, and its supply chain runs through Troll (Halley subnet) and the currently-unnamed Korean city (Mirny subnet). Mawson itself sits on Hwy 4 toward the same Mirny subnet cluster. None of the three relationships this subnet's own research most developed are with each other.

**What this produces:** Mawson subnet reads less as a self-contained region and more as connective tissue between Halley and Mirny subnets specifically — worth keeping in mind for this Ultra-Megasheet's own Throughways, developed further below.

## Who Lives Here, and Why

Mawson subnet's three cities carry three genuinely different relationships to their own founding populations, spanning the full range this project has documented anywhere: Mawson's peaceful, accepted dilution (Australian founders, Chinese-Primary present, no unresolved tension); Sayowa's institutional-weight-without-demographic-weight split (Japanese founders diluted to Significant tier, the Shōwa-era name still meaningful only to a shrinking few); and Dome Fuji's total population succession (the entire founding Japanese research population gone by ordinary attrition, replaced by a 100%-robot devotee population for whom national origin isn't even a relevant category anymore).

This makes Mawson subnet, more than any other in the project, the place where the question "what happens to a founding population over centuries" gets answered in its most varied and most extreme forms — not a subnet with one demographic story and small variations on it, but three genuinely different endings to the same kind of beginning.

## What It Feels Like

Moving through Mawson subnet means moving between three places that share almost no register with each other. Mawson itself is warm, hospitable, genuinely resort-like — Tepenia's honeymoon capital, a city that decided decades ago to be good news and has stayed that way. Sayowa is quiet, dutiful, and modest — a small city carrying an entire Japanese imperial era in its own name, valued for what runs through it rather than what it is, deliberately spared by an enemy who needed it too much to finish destroying it. And Dome Fuji, reached only through a genuinely dangerous interior plateau crossing, is something closer to a held breath — total flatness, near-total silence, a population of robot devotees who arrived one at a time from every corner of Tepenia and share nothing but the cold they came to commune with. Nothing about moving from one of these cities to the next feels like staying within the same subnet; each one feels like arriving somewhere that could just as easily belong to a different part of the country entirely.
