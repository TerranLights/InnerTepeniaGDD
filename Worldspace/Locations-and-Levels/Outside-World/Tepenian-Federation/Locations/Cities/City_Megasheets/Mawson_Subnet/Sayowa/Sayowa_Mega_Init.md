## What It Feels Like

"The Point Where Three Roads Meet" names Sayowa's defining civic fact precisely: a small, modest, deeply functional city that happens to sit exactly where the Mawson subnet, the inland route to Concordia, and the Halley subnet all physically converge. The city itself reads as split cleanly into two halves — genuinely industrialized fabrication and trucking/dispatch infrastructure on one side, genuine residential neighborhoods on the other — with leisure culture present but visibly secondary, tucked into the residential half rather than commanding any prominent civic space of its own. Things get made here as much as they get routed through: a major fabrication industry paired with a huge trucking and dispatch operation, "the whole continent's connectivity" plausibly depending on the junction this small city happens to occupy. The name itself carries unusual weight — a phonetic evolution of Shōwa (昭和), the Japanese imperial era spanning 1926 to 1989, encompassing war, defeat, and reconstruction, folded into a small Antarctic exile city's proper noun. Sayowa was damaged during the Long Night War but never destroyed outright: too critical to the continent's connectivity, too structurally significant to have been fully erased rather than fought over and kept marginally functional.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Felixstowe, UK

Britain's busiest container port (48% of the UK's containerized trade, roughly 4 million TEUs per year) inside a small, modest town of only ~24,000 people — outsized functional importance carried in a genuinely unglamorous footprint.

**What this gives Sayowa:** the tightest possible structural match — a real, present-day precedent for exactly Sayowa's own established central tension, a small population carrying disproportionate structural weight that has nothing to do with its own size.

### Primary: Trois-Rivières, Quebec, Canada

A quieter industrial/logistics junction city sitting between Montreal and Quebec City, built around freight and processing rather than its own cultural spectacle.

**What this gives Sayowa:** direct precedent for a city whose identity is genuinely built on function rather than fame, reinforcing Sayowa's own established character as a place valued for what it does rather than what it is.

### Secondary: Perpignan, France

A genuine cross-border freight-consolidation and trucking hub near the Spanish border, defined by function rather than fame.

**What this gives Sayowa:** reinforces the freight-consolidation-hub framing already established via Felixstowe and Trois-Rivières, specifically at a cross-border position — a fitting parallel to Sayowa's own genuinely liminal geography, bridging the Mawson and Halley subnets.

### Secondary: Djibouti City, Djibouti

Under one million people, yet handling roughly 85% of its port throughput as trade destined for Ethiopia — outsized strategic logistics importance from a tiny population, purely a function of chokepoint position on the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait (~15% of global maritime traffic).

**What this gives Sayowa:** perhaps the single sharpest real-world parallel across this whole research pass — a real place whose entire strategic significance is chokepoint geography rather than population, output, or fame, mirroring Sayowa's own established gap between its modest size and its outsized structural importance almost exactly.
