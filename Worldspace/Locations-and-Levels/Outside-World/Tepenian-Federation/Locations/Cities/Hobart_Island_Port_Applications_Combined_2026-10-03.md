## 2. What the evidence does NOT give Hobart *(from Part A; read this beside the 38 above)*

- **Not a container or Bass Strait gateway.** 3,035 TEU in FY2025 = **0.5%** of the state's 613,085 TEU; Devonport and Burnie carry about **92%**. Hobart logged 1.75 Mt, 12.2% of the state's tonnage.
- **No on-dock rail** in the sources (TasRail's Hobart terminal moved to Brighton in 2014).
- **Iron, tin, zinc concentrate and woodchips leave through north and north-west ports** (Port Latta, Burnie, Bell Bay), **not Hobart**, as far as the sources show.
- ***Nuyina* cannot bunker at Hobart.** The fuel depot is above the Tasman Bridge (air draft 44 m, beam limit 32.31 m, length limit 185 m); the ship refuels at **Burnie, a 674 km detour costing about A$900k a year**; a Hobart solution is not expected before about 2028.
- **No dry dock or large steel-vessel repair yard** in Hobart or anywhere in Tasmania (next docking: Singapore, mid-2026).
- **Declared berth depths** on the Macquarie wharves are 10.0 to 12.8 m: a limit for deep-draft bulk carriers.
- **The island's heavy industry is shaky:** Bell Bay aluminum lost A$18.5M in 2024 on a one-year power contract; the **TEMCO manganese smelter closed in July 2026**; Nyrstar Hobart cut output 25% in 2025; **Savage River faces a A$300 to 600M impairment** and an unfinanced underground transition.
- **The whole Australian program moves only about 1,600 to 2,190 t of cargo a season** into three stations; statewide "Antarctic" freight was **4,469 t in FY2025**. Hobart's real function is **small-volume and high-value**.

### 3.2 What the overview shows

1. **Hobart's island-specific role is institutional and quality-based, not volume-based.** The best-documented applications are a **gateway** (nearest departure), a **service and knowledge cluster** (AAD, CCAMLR, institutes, the Polar Network), a **biosecurity checkpoint**, and an **air-and-sea passenger and priority-cargo bridge**. The island's own bulk goods (iron pellets, ores, woodchips) leave from **other Tasmanian ports**. This **agrees with your ruling** that the three mainland terminals carry the bulk freight.
2. **The island's real advantages cluster in three places:** (a) **distance and speed** (the nearest departure for the Adélie arc: about 0.9 day against Torquay, 1.6 to 1.7 against Outer Harbor, 2.5 to 2.7 against Bunbury; against 10 to 11 days settled for the mainland routes, Hobart's real 5 to 7 days is about half); (b) **biosecurity** (an island that is already a fruit-fly pest-free area, with a purpose-built Antarctic cargo screening center); (c) **concentrated expertise** (a city where the treaty bodies, the Antarctic agency and the marine institutes already sit).
3. **A pattern worth noticing: the same three advantages point at a different kind of cargo than the mainland terminals carry.** *(This is a reading, not a finding in the sources.)* **Short transit + a clean island + a food surplus (5.9 times consumption) + a screening center** are exactly what **perishable, fresh, high-value cargo and people** would need. **Bulk iron, building materials and grain** need none of those; they need depth, rail and scale, which Hobart lacks and the mainland terminals are being built to have. The overview therefore suggests a **division of labor**: *the mainland terminals carry tonnage; Hobart carries what is perishable, clean, urgent, or human.*
4. **The island setting also exposes a real set of single points of failure** (R-17, R-13): the icebreaker cannot bunker in its own home port; one crane failure stranded half a season's cargo; there is no dry dock; over 99% of freight is by sea; Basslink failed for six months in 2015-16. For a society that depends on Hobart, **the island is a vulnerability as well as a buffer**, and that is a story seed, not only a logistics fact.
5. **Hobart is not a transshipment port in the sources.** No evidence shows bulk mainland cargo staged through Tasmania for Antarctica. P-3 (a bulk-to-ice-ship transfer point) is **only physically consistent** with the berth data; it would be an invention if used.
6. **Several island industries are in decline** (TEMCO closed, zinc cut 25%, Savage River impairment, aluminum on a one-year power contract). **Using Tasmanian metals or iron as a Tepenian supply would mean choosing a future where those survive.** Savage River's pellets (about 2.5 Mt a year) are the one item on the list that could answer the open question of where Tepenia's iron comes from, but they ship from **Port Latta**, not Hobart.
7. **Hobart is also a political place.** R-18 (secession agitation over shipping terms 1924 to 1934, a freight subsidy, port-fee disputes) and R-12 (the Antarctic territory administered from the island but governed by Commonwealth law) show **an island that has argued with its mainland over exactly this kind of trade.** That gives post-war Tasmania a ready-made reason to have its own relationship with Tepenia.

### 3.2b The developer's reading of the overview (2026-10-03, `DR-50`)

**This adopts the "people-and-priority" side of point 3 above** (the air-and-sea bridge, the biosecurity checkpoint, the multi-nation gateway, the polar-services cluster) and **matches the canon corridor table** in `Upper_Earth_Immigration_Composition.md`.
