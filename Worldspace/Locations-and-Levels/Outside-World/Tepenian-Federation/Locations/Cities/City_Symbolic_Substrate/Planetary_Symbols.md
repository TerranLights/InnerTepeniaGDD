# Planetary Symbols — the Nine Planets Plus the Asteroid Belt

**What this is:** a symbol system built around the nine planets (Mercury through Pluto) plus a tenth symbol,
the Asteroid Belt — independent of the Robot Elementals (`Robot_Elementals.md`) and of Platonic-solid
symbolism, a standalone system paired with Elementals only at the point of city assignment
(`City_Symbol_Assignments.md`). Per direct developer instruction, this was invented from scratch rather than
reused from real-world astrology or mythology; each planet's meaning is instead grounded in a real, verifiable
astronomical or physical fact about that specific planet, the same way the two "robot elements" (Electricity,
Electromagnetism) are grounded in real physics rather than any existing tradition.

**Status: official reference framework, promoted from `to-be-integrated/planetary-symbols.md` and
`to-be-integrated/planetary-appended-symbols.md` 2026-08-30** (the two source drafts consolidated into this single file, no
content lost — the appended draft's "one word" and "summary" fields are folded in below). Confirmed working
reference; still open to future revision the way any canon document is, not a frozen final version.

---

## Mercury

Smallest planet in the system, closest to the Sun, with no real atmosphere to buffer it — its surface swings
between extreme heat and extreme cold within a single rotation. Locked into an unusual 3:2 spin-orbit
resonance (not a full tidal lock — a specific, oddly precise rhythm found nowhere else in the system).

- **One word:** Resilience
- **Summary:** Surviving in the face of extreme natural forces
- **Neutral:** Tide-locked in ultra-close proximity to the sun, likely has a very narrow band of habitability
- **Positive:** Endurance at the edge — surviving conditions nothing else in the system has to. A rhythm
  entirely its own, dictated by proximity to something overwhelmingly larger, but never fully surrendered to
  it.
- **Negative:** Being defined entirely by what's next to it. No middle ground, no buffer, no rest —
  everything experienced at full intensity, with nothing held back in reserve for later.

## Venus

Near-twin to Earth in size and mass, yet the hottest planet in the system by far, thanks to a runaway
greenhouse atmosphere. Rotates backward relative to its own orbit, and its day is longer than its year.

- **One word:** Mercilessness (or Brutalism)
- **Summary:** A place of compounded extremes
- **Neutral:** A brutal, unforgiving hellscape where survival may require multiple, possibly extreme, methods
  combined
- **Positive:** Refusing the given direction — turning against the current on principle. A reminder that
  resemblance isn't identity: looking like the safest, most familiar thing doesn't make you it.
- **Negative:** A feedback loop that runs away from itself once started, with no mechanism left to stop it.
  Beauty from a distance concealing something genuinely hostile up close.

## Earth

The only known planet with liquid water, plate tectonics, a magnetic field shielding it from solar wind, and
a large stabilizing moon — habitability as the product of several unrelated systems all happening to work at
once, not any single trait.

- **One word:** Oasis
- **Summary:** A fragile, delicate Garden of Eden
- **Neutral:** Long the only known planet with confirmed life, it's the natural home of everything we know
- **Positive:** Viability built from convergence, not from any one advantage. Resilience that comes from
  redundancy — several different systems each capable of covering for the others.
- **Negative:** A stability entirely contingent on everything continuing to line up at once. Nothing about
  the arrangement is guaranteed to last — it simply hasn't failed yet.

## Mars

Evidence of a past with liquid water and a thicker atmosphere, both long since lost; a rusted, oxidized
surface; the largest volcano and canyon system in the solar system despite Mars being smaller than Earth.

- **One word:** Phoenix
- **Summary:** Revived from a long death
- **Neutral:** Once bearing water (and almost certainly plant life), effectively dead for eons, and colonized
  back to life by the systemwide metacivilization of Greater Tepenia
- **Positive:** Scale that outlasts the conditions that built it — monuments still standing long after
  whatever made them possible is gone. Proof that a small frame can still hold something vast.
- **Negative:** Visible, ongoing decay — oxidation as a slow, permanent record of loss rather than a single
  event. Potential that had its moment and didn't get to keep it.

**Applied sense of "revival" (2026-08-05 clarification):** not limited to literal death-and-rebirth from war
damage — a broader, still pre-war-appropriate sense of "drifting away from an original purpose or condition,
then returning to it" is equally valid. See `City_Symbol_Assignments.md`'s distribution notes for the three
cities this broader reading unlocked.

## The Asteroid Belt

The tenth symbol, confirmed and assigned to Rothera 2026-08-10 (see `City_Symbol_Assignments.md`). Unlike any
single planet, a decentralized scatter of countless separate bodies varying enormously in size, composition,
and orbit, held together by nothing but a shared orbital region. No single point of failure; nothing to
strike that would eliminate it all at once. Filled a genuine gap in the nine-planet set: nothing else in it
captures decentralization itself — strength through distribution, with no single point of failure. Placed
here, between Mars and Jupiter, to match its actual position in the Solar System.

- **One word:** Redundancy
- **Summary:** Strength through decentralized distribution, not concentration
- **Neutral:** Countless separate bodies sharing one orbital region rather than a single mass — nothing to
  strike that would eliminate it all at once
- **Positive:** Resilience through redundancy — losing any single piece doesn't threaten the whole. A
  strength that doesn't require unity, concentration, or a single command point to be real. Presence spread
  widely enough that it's genuinely difficult to eliminate, target, or fully account for.
- **Negative:** No single body carries much weight or authority on its own — significance exists only in the
  aggregate, never individually. A structure with no true center can also mean no one is ever fully in charge
  or accountable. Spread so thin that no single piece is ever strong enough to stand entirely alone, dependent
  on the whole scatter simply to matter at all.

## Jupiter

By far the most massive planet, its gravity well captures and deflects debris that might otherwise reach the
inner system; a storm (the Great Red Spot) that has persisted for centuries without ever resolving; dozens of
captured moons.

- **One word:** Dominance
- **Summary:** Unspoken lordship asserted via mere existence
- **Neutral:** Has no real need to "throw its weight around" because its weight is self-evident through its
  sheer presence
- **Positive:** Sheer scale used to absorb impact meant for something smaller and more vulnerable. Gathering,
  not hoarding — pulling scattered things into orbit and giving them somewhere to belong.
- **Negative:** A storm that never actually ends, just persists — permanence mistaken for stability. Gravity
  strong enough that nothing nearby ever really leaves on its own terms.

## Saturn

Famous for its rings, which are debris, not a solid structure; the least dense planet in the system, light
enough it would float; a steep axial tilt that produces real seasons.

- **One word:** Mystery
- **Summary:** Doesn't care to be fully known
- **Neutral:** Its true nature may perhaps be unknowable, which it's perfectly fine with because it doesn't
  care whether you understand it or not
- **Positive:** Beauty built from fragments rather than requiring wholeness — something genuinely striking
  assembled from what's broken. Lightness as a real trait, not a deficiency.
- **Negative:** Held together only loosely — a structure with no actual cohesion, one disruption away from
  simply dispersing. Impressive from a distance, insubstantial up close.

## Uranus

Tipped almost completely on its side (~98°), likely from an ancient massive collision, so it rolls around the
Sun rather than spinning upright; despite that violent past, it has the coldest recorded atmosphere of any
planet in the system.

- **One word:** Anomaly
- **Summary:** An unintentional "outsider" in its broader environmental context
- **Neutral:** Although it never intended to be fundamentally different from everyone/everything/everywhere
  else, this is nevertheless less of a "role" and more of a condition of its very existence, essentially a
  micro "counterculture" among its surroundings
- **Positive:** An identity built after a defining event, not before it — reorientation as the new baseline
  rather than damage waiting to be corrected. Cold that has nothing to do with distance — proof that the
  obvious explanation isn't always the real one.
- **Negative:** A permanent tilt inherited from something that happened once, long ago, and was never
  anyone's choice. Sideways treated as normal simply because it's been sideways long enough.

## Neptune

The farthest planet, and yet home to the fastest winds recorded anywhere in the system; its existence was
calculated mathematically, from irregularities in Uranus's orbit, before it was ever actually observed.

- **One word:** Diligence
- **Summary:** Quietly willing to share its secrets with anyone willing to do the brainwork
- **Neutral:** Its nature and personality are deduced and extrapolated before being directly observed; does
  not shun, though also does not invite
- **Positive:** Being known through your effect on something else before anyone ever sees you directly —
  real, provable influence that doesn't require visibility. Intensity that has nothing to do with proximity
  to the source of energy.
- **Negative:** Existing, for a long time, only as an inference — trusted to be real before there was any
  direct proof. Being understood entirely through someone else's disturbance rather than on your own terms.

## Pluto

A dwarf planet whose classification is genuinely contested; its moon Charon is large enough, relative to
Pluto itself, that the two arguably orbit a shared point in space rather than Pluto being the sole center;
its orbit is eccentric enough to sometimes cross Neptune's.

- **One word:** Isolation
- **Summary:** Truly alone in the reaches of nothingness
- **Neutral:** In the very outer edges of anything currently explored — if you're coming here, don't expect a
  rescue mission if you find yourself in trouble
- **Positive:** A partnership close enough that "who orbits whom" stops being a meaningful question — real
  mutual gravity, not one-sided dependency. Refusing to stay inside a category someone else drew, on
  principle.
- **Negative:** An identity permanently up for debate, decided by people who aren't actually part of it.
  Orbit patterns that never quite settle, crossing into territory that isn't technically yours.
