# The Eight Elements of Tepenia

Eight symbolic members, paired with a planet at the point of city assignment (`City_Symbol_Assignments.md`).

**Status:** official reference framework. Confirmed working reference; open to revision the way any canon
document is.

**Method.** Each member's meaning is grounded in a **real, verifiable physical fact about that member**,
rather than transcribed from an existing tradition — the same method `Planetary_Symbols.md` uses for the
planets, and the same method behind Electricity and Magnetism. The element *names* come from the Greek and
Chinese systems; the meanings are derived here.

**Source note.** The subnet-scale symbolic layer at `Storyline/DLC-Questlines/Subnet_Symbolic_Associations.md`
carries its own entries for these members and is a separate instrument — see this folder's `README.md`.

⏸️ **The six one-word labels marked *under review* are not settled.** The substance of each entry does not
depend on which word wins.

## Earth

> **Ground is not a material. It is a condition — grains pressing on one another — and the pressing can be
> taken away.**

**① Mostly empty, and the strength is in the contacts, not the grains.** Soil porosity typically runs
**30–45%**, and in loose material the void ratio exceeds 2 — more space than solid. Load is not borne by the
bulk; it is transmitted **grain to grain** through what soil mechanics calls an *effectively stressed
aggregate structure*. No single grain is strong. The strength is in the touching.

**② Terzaghi's effective stress principle: `σ' = σ − u`.** The stress actually holding the grains together is
the total stress **minus the pore water pressure**. Raise that pore pressure until it equals the applied
stress and effective stress falls to zero: the grains **lose contact**, and saturated soil undergoes
**liquefaction**, becoming a fluid mass. Shock and vibration are enough to do it if the water cannot drain.

**③ Where the binder is ice, it is ground only for as long as it stays cold.** Ice-rich permafrost is commonly
**50–80% ice by volume**. Warm it past freezing and the structural ice becomes water: the soil loses **all**
shear strength and bearing capacity, turning to supersaturated slurry, shedding up to **40% of its volume**,
the surface dropping **one to five meters**, foundation pilings torn apart.

- **One word:** Contact ⏸️ *under review — alternatives: Contingency, Bearing*
- **Summary:** Holds because its parts are pressed together, and stops holding the moment they are not
- **Neutral:** Whether it is solid or liquid is not a fact about the material; it is a fact about the
  conditions the material is currently in
- **Positive:** Strength that is entirely relational. It exists in no individual part and is not any part's
  property — a load arrives and is passed from contact to contact across the whole aggregate, borne by
  everything and by nothing in particular. And it answers to being pressed closer: compaction makes it
  stronger, so the remedy for weakness is simply more contact.
- **Negative:** What holds it is not something it owns but a condition it happens to be in, and the condition
  can be removed by things as ordinary as water that cannot drain, a vibration, or a rise in temperature.
  Then the same material — unchanged, none of it gone anywhere — is a liquid, and everything resting on it
  goes down with it. It is the member most able to look permanent while being nothing of the kind: it
  presents as the solid thing others are built on, and it is mostly void held in place by a circumstance
  nobody checked.

## Air

**① A mixture, not a substance — and mostly inert.** Air has no chemical formula, because it is not a
compound but a mixture: **78.08% nitrogen, 20.95% oxygen, 0.93% argon**, and roughly 0.04% everything else,
the noble gases among it chemically inert. By volume it is overwhelmingly the part that does nothing; the
constituent everything depends on is the minority one.

**② Held still it is the best insulator there is. Allowed to move, it strips.** Among common materials **the
thermal conductivity of static air is the lowest there is** — and every insulator that works by air works the
same single way: wool, fur, down and double glazing all **trap air in spaces too small to support
convection**. The insulation is not the fiber. It is the stillness. Let the same air move and it carries the
heat off instead.

**③ It carries what cannot cross a vacuum.** Sound is a mechanical wave — compressions and rarefactions
passed particle to particle — so in a vacuum, with nothing to carry it, there is no sound at all. This is
what separates Air from Magnetism, whose field acts across empty space with nothing in between. **A voice
requires that both ends be standing in the same air. A pull does not.**

- **One word:** Medium ⏸️ *under review — alternatives: Mediation, Conveyance*
- **Summary:** Does nothing itself and determines everything — and only for those who are inside it together
- **Neutral:** Noticed when it moves, or when it is gone, and at no other time
- **Positive:** The condition that makes contact possible without touch, asking nothing of either end except
  that they share it. It shelters by being held still, and that shelter costs nothing but stillness. And what
  it carries it carries in the open: to speak into it is to be audible to whoever else is standing in it, so
  nothing passes through it privately and nothing passes through it selectively.
- **Negative:** It has no preference and cannot acquire one — the warning and the rumor travel at identical
  speed with identical fidelity, and nothing in the medium tells them apart. Its shelter depends on a
  stillness it does not control: the same air that held the heat in strips it away the instant something
  moves it. And it is never a party to anything, only the precondition — so it is noticed exclusively when it
  turns on you, or when it is gone.

## Fire

> **The only member of this set that is not a thing.**

**① A process, not a substance.** Combustion is a **self-sustaining exothermic chain reaction**, and the
standard model of it is a **tetrahedron** of four legs — fuel, heat, an oxidizer, and *the chain reaction
itself*. Remove any one and it stops. The heat it releases is what maintains the reaction that releases the
heat: once ignition temperature is reached it produces its own precondition and needs nothing further but
supply. There is no substance here to point at. Stop the activity and nothing remains that was fire.

**② It eats what allows it to continue.** The same reaction consumes its fuel and its oxidizer as it runs.
Self-sustaining is not self-sufficient — the reaction maintains the *conditions* while destroying the
*materials*, so its duration is set entirely by how much is left within reach.

**③ It does not reliably stop when it appears to.** After a burning droplet's visible hot flame extinguishes,
the droplet goes on burning in a **cool flame** so weak it is invisible to the naked eye and takes an
intensified camera to detect. And the chemistry changes with it: hot flames give off carbon dioxide and
water; **cool flames give off carbon monoxide and formaldehyde.** It went out, it did not stop, and what it
makes now is worse than what it made while you could still see it.

- **One word:** Reaction ⏸️ *under review — alternatives: Persistence, Process*
- **Summary:** Not a thing but an activity — one that produces its own conditions while destroying its own
  supply
- **Neutral:** It needs only to be started; after that the question is never whether it continues but how
  much is left within reach. And what would put it out can be absent precisely where it is most dangerous —
  in the cold it is the single greatest hazard, with humidity near zero, the water all locked into ice around
  it and useless for the purpose, and nowhere to evacuate to.
- **Positive:** The only member that is purely act. No stored form, no reserve, no residue — it is entirely
  what it is currently doing, and it asks nothing of anyone except to be started. Past its threshold it
  sustains itself without further permission, generating the very condition it needs in order to keep going.
- **Negative:** It is sustained by consuming what sustains it, so continuing and exhausting itself are the
  same motion. And it cannot be trusted to have ended: it can drop below visibility while still running, and
  the version you cannot see is not the gentler one — it burns cooler, and what it gives off is more
  poisonous than before.

## Water

**① It concedes shape utterly, and volume not at all.** A liquid has a definite volume and **no definite
shape** — it takes the form of whatever contains it. But water is very nearly **incompressible** *(bulk
modulus ≈ 2.2 × 10⁹ Pa)*, so conforming costs it nothing. And by **Pascal's law**, a pressure change at any
point in a confined incompressible fluid is transmitted **equally and undiminished to every point, in every
direction**. A contained body of water does not absorb what is applied to it. It delivers the whole of it to
every wall at once.

**② Below freezing it stops conforming, and starts taking.** Ice does not wait for water; it pulls it. As a
wet porous body freezes, a negative pore pressure — **cryosuction** — develops at the freezing front and
draws liquid water toward it out of the unfrozen material beyond, where it accumulates as bands of nearly
pure ice called **lenses**. The conduit is a film of water the ice does not freeze: a **quasi-liquid
premelting layer** that persists well below the bulk melting point and stays mobile when everything around it
has set solid. The lens grows for as long as the cold is sustained and water remains reachable, stacks into
further lenses, and splits the body along a plane the ice chose. This — not the 9% volumetric expansion of
freezing water — is what actually breaks rock in cold regions; expansion needs near-total saturation and
rapid freezing from all sides, and those conditions are rare.

- **One word:** Pervasion ⏸️ *under review — alternative: Accretion*
- **Summary:** Gives up its own form to reach every part of what holds it — and what it can reach, it can
  eventually take
- **Neutral:** Needs no cycle, no thaw and no crisis — only cold that does not let up, and something left
  within reach to draw from
- **Positive:** Conforming without conceding. It surrenders its shape completely and its substance not at
  all, so it loses nothing by fitting itself to what it has been given. Force applied to it anywhere arrives
  everywhere, undiminished — nothing is absorbed privately, and nothing is borne alone. And it keeps one path
  open and unfrozen when everything around it has hardened, moving what it needs through that single channel.
- **Negative:** Having no shape of its own means taking the shape of whatever holds it, indefinitely, until
  the container's form is the only form it has. And nothing about it is passive — the suction is real, and
  the quiet is method rather than restraint. It draws from its surroundings without their noticing and leaves
  them hollow; the damage never appears where the water was, only where the ice decided to gather; and the
  surface shows nothing at all until the thing splits along a line it was never consulted about.

## Wood

> **Wood is accumulated history, made structural.**

**① It stands on its own dead.** Only a very thin layer — the **cambium** — is living tissue, laying down new
wood inward and new bark outward. Sapwood carries the sap and holds both living and dead cells. The
**heartwood** at the center is dead outright — retired sapwood, put out of commission as new rings formed —
and it is the structurally significant part. The living portion is a shell around a column of what the tree
has finished with.

**② It records what happened, permanently, and survives to carry the record.** Ring width tracks the year —
narrow through drought, wide through a good one. **Fire scars** are burned patches the tree survived and then
grew new rings over. Floods, insect defoliations, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions and storms all leave
permanent, datable marks. And the whole record can be read out of a living tree with an increment borer,
without killing it.

**③ Pushed, it does not resist — it grows a correction.** A leaning stem forms **reaction wood**: conifers lay
down **compression wood** on the underside, high in lignin, pushing the stem back upright; hardwoods lay down
**tension wood** on the upper side, high in cellulose, pulling it back. Either way the growth is
**eccentric**, with wider rings on the reaction side, so the correction is itself permanently recorded in the
shape of the trunk.

**④ Its strength is entirely a matter of direction.** Loaded along the grain, each fiber acts as a small
hollow column braced by its neighbors; loaded across it, the cell walls simply collapse. Compressive strength
**parallel to the grain runs 8–10 times the perpendicular figure**, and in tension the gap is wider still —
Douglas-fir at roughly **15,600 PSI along the grain against 360 PSI across it**.

- **One word:** Grain ⏸️ *under review — alternatives: Record, Deposition*
- **Summary:** Becomes its own history, and is strong only in the direction that history runs
- **Neutral:** Nothing that happens to it is discarded — it is all still in there, and all of it is
  load-bearing
- **Positive:** It answers pressure by growing rather than by resisting, bending itself back toward upright
  under its own power and keeping the evidence of having done so. What it is finished with is not waste: the
  part it is done living in becomes the part that holds it up. And its entire life stays legible to anyone
  who knows how to look — without it having to be destroyed for the reading.
- **Negative:** Strong in one direction and negligible in every other — a load arriving along the grain is
  carried like a column; the same load across it collapses cell walls at a fraction of the strength. And
  nothing is ever released: every drought, every fire survived, every year it was pushed and compensated for
  is still inside it, still structural, permanently shaping what it can bear and from which direction. **It
  cannot revise its history. It can only add to it.**

## Metal

> **Metal announces its limit by bending — and there are two ways to lose the announcement.**

**① Imposed, by cold.** Above a certain temperature a steel yields before it fails: it deforms, visibly,
absorbing energy, and the deformation is the warning. Below its **ductile-to-brittle transition temperature**
it fractures **without appreciable plastic deformation** — no bend, no notice, and at a markedly lower impact
energy than a ductile failure would need. The mechanism is a race: as temperature falls, **yield stress
climbs steeply while cleavage stress barely moves**, until the metal breaks before it can yield. **Constance
Tipper** identified this in the WWII Liberty ships, whose welded hulls failed in cold water. And crystal
structure decides whether the threshold exists at all — body-centered cubic metals such as carbon steel have
one; face-centered cubic metals such as aluminum, copper and austenitic stainless do not, and stay ductile
down to cryogenic temperatures.

**② Earned, by history.** Every act of shaping — rolling, drawing, forging, bending — drives **dislocations**
into the crystal lattice. The metal becomes harder, stronger, and less able to bend again. This is **work
hardening**, and carried far enough the piece cracks. It is reversible, and only one thing reverses it:
heating above the recrystallization temperature but below melting — **annealing** — which gives the lattice
enough energy for the dislocations to migrate out and the internal stresses to relieve. The accumulated
history leaves only at a temperature just short of destroying the thing that holds it.

- **One word:** Hardening ⏸️ *under review — alternative: Threshold*
- **Summary:** Everything that shapes it makes it stronger, and less able to be shaped again
- **Neutral:** Its limit was never hidden — it is announced by bending. What changes is whether it can still
  bend
- **Positive:** Integrity that holds completely, right up to its limit, with no slow sag and no quiet
  compromise. And nothing it has accumulated is permanent: the hardness driven in by every past shaping can
  be driven back out, the stresses relieved, the capacity to bend restored — though only by heat taken almost
  to the point of ruin. And whether cold can make it brittle at all is a matter of what it is made of, not of
  what it has endured.
- **Negative:** The warning and the strength are the same property, so hardening removes the warning. A thing
  that has been shaped many times is stronger, and nearer to cracking, and gives less notice than it used to.
  The limit itself is silent — nothing sags, nothing yields, nothing announces; the metal is intact, and then
  it is in two pieces, at a fraction of the force that would have been needed while it could still bend. And
  continuity carries the failure: in those same ships a crack ran the length of a welded hull where a riveted
  one stopped it at the edge of each plate. What makes a body whole is what lets one failure travel all of it.

## Electricity

*(Constructed, alongside Magnetism, as a "robot element" — no ancient tradition to draw from.)*

- *Positive:* Animation — the literal spark that turns inert matter into something conscious and active.
  Connection and communication (the electrical basis of any nervous system, biological or otherwise). Power
  and capability. Illumination. Rapid, decisive transformation. The single current that runs through and
  unifies an entire system end to end.
- *Negative:* Danger and destructive potential — shock, short-circuit, electrocution. Instability, where a
  surge and a total blackout are simply two faces of the same underlying fragility. Total dependency — a
  system that is completely helpless the instant its power source is removed. The same current that animates
  a whole system is, withdrawn or overloaded, exactly what collapses it — a direct, literal echo of the
  Amundsen Tower's own story.

## Magnetism

*(Constructed, alongside Electricity, as a "robot element" — no ancient tradition to draw from.)*

**① There is no such thing as one pole.** Cut a magnet in half and you do not get a north and a south — you
get **two complete magnets**, each with both poles, and you may keep cutting down to the atomic level and
still have dipoles. This is not a limit of technique but **Gauss's law for magnetism**, one of Maxwell's four
equations. No monopole has ever been observed.

**② Magnetization is alignment, not addition.** Unmagnetized iron is not short of magnetism. Its **domains** —
regions within which countless elementary magnets are already held parallel by interatomic forces — simply
point in every direction and cancel one another out. Magnetizing it adds nothing whatsoever. The capacity was
already entirely present, and merely disordered.

**③ The Curie point erases the order, and cooling does not restore it.** Heated past that temperature,
thermal energy overwhelms the internal aligning forces; the domains cannot be ordered at all and the material
ceases to be magnetic. It becomes ferromagnetic again on cooling, but it does not come back magnetized. The
capacity returns. The alignment does not.

**④ Hysteresis — it keeps the shape of what was done to it.** Remove the applied field and magnetization does
**not** fall back to zero: the domain walls stay pinned where they were displaced. To bring it to zero you
must **drive** it there, with a field in the opposite direction. Its state is a fact about its history, not
about its present conditions.

- **One word:** Alignment ⏸️ *under review — alternatives: Polarity, Retention*
- **Summary:** Nothing is added to it — everything depends on how much of what it already holds is pointing
  the same way
- **Neutral:** It acts across empty space, on things that never touch it and were never asked
- **Positive:** Its power is not acquired but organized: the whole of it was always there, and the only
  question was ever whether it was pointed together. And it cannot be halved into a part that only attracts —
  whatever draws and whatever repels are one object, and every attempt to separate them yields two whole ones
  instead. Orientation that persists with no maintenance, and holds after the force that set it has gone.
- **Negative:** It holds its alignment after the field is gone, so it goes on pointing where it was last
  pointed long after the reason has passed — and it will not relax back on its own; it has to be driven back
  by an equal force in the opposite direction, which nothing supplies unprompted. Heat it past a threshold
  and the ordering is not damaged but erased, and what returns on cooling is only the capacity, never the
  former alignment. And it acts at a distance, invisibly, on things that never agreed to it.
