# 1. Why this site — the two facts that define the city

| Real-world fact | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **⭐ Lake Figurnoye — the LARGEST FRESHWATER LAKE IN ALL OF ANTARCTICA**, in a lake system reaching ~140 m deep; most Bunger lakes are not ice-covered year-round | **The cheapest water in Tepenia, by an enormous margin** |
| **Ice-free year-round; 450–942 km²** — larger than Davis's Vestfold Hills (~410 km²), the largest such area in East Antarctica | Rock foundations, no ice engineering; genuine room to grow |

## ⭐ The water fact is the whole city, and it should drive everything else

**Every other Tepenian city pays the full latent heat of fusion to turn ice into water.** A2 Water &
Sanitation is a Tier-A survival industry everywhere, scored at **3.9–10.2%** of municipal economies in the
pilot run. **This city draws from standing liquid fresh water instead.**

**Its A2 burden would be the lowest in the nation — and that is a moral condition as much as an economic one.**
In a country where meltwater is metered, registered, allocated and paid for, **a city with a lake is an
anomaly.** The open question is not what it does with the water. It is **what "wasting water" even means in
the one place where water is not scarce**, and how the rest of Tepenia feels about that.

**Combined with ice-free rock, it is the obvious second agricultural region** — which resolves the
"Davis cannot feed the nation alone" problem structurally rather than by inflating Davis past credibility.

**Compare every other founding in the corpus:** Davis was continuously operated. Casey was kept genuinely
functional across the centuries. Abowasa inherited two live adjacent national stations.

> **This city inherited a place that was built, given away, used, walked away from, and left in the dark for

> continent the entire time.

**⭐ And the reactivation's real purpose is a gift to this project specifically: a station woken from four
decades of silence to host machines that run without people.** In a game whose north star is robot
consciousness, that should not be left on the table.

**Three nations' fingerprints — Soviet, Polish, Australian — and no continuous human presence at any of them.
Poland has no other foothold anywhere in the Tepenian city roster.**

# 3.5 ⚠ HIGHWAY ACCESS — researched 2026-09-01, and it corrects a working assumption

**Developer proposal:** a new highway branching off Hwy 110 just outside **Mirny**, running the coast, passing
through or around this city, and rejoining **Hwy 2** just outside **Casey** — on the premise that Hwy 110
*"curves slightly inland in order to avoid mountains as a way of keeping the incline reasonably level."*

**Map check (`Reference/Images/Maps/Antarctica_highway_map_by_topology.jpeg`): the inland bulge is real.**
Hwy 110's green line clearly departs the coast between Mirny and Casey, leaving the entire ~100°E coastal
stretch unserved — exactly where this city sits.

## ⚠ But the obstacle is not mountains

| Real-world feature | Position | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Shackleton Ice Shelf** | **fronts the coast 95°E → 105°E**, ~384 km along-shore | **A floating, calving shelf. No permanent road can be laid on it.** This city at 100°45'E sits *directly behind it* |
| **Denman Glacier** | **66°45'S, 99°30'E** — between Mirny and this city | **11–16 km wide, descends 110 km, heavily crevassed. The deepest point on continental Earth lies beneath it — an ice-filled canyon 3.5 km below sea level** |

**That is a far harder obstacle than mountains, and it is the better reason for Hwy 110's detour.**

## Is this city coastal? **Qualified yes — but shelf-locked**

**"A complex of islands and inlets on the inner continental shelf south of Shackleton Ice Shelf."** The sea
genuinely entered the oasis before ~7.7 ka BP; saline inlets and channels lie just north toward the
**Highjump Archipelago**; **marine inlets do connect to the open ocean.** But it is not open-coast, and the
literature is blunt:

> *"Surrounded by the ice sheet, heavily crevassed glaciers and Shackleton Ice Shelf, which has ensured that
> **sea- and land-based approaches are difficult and rarely attempted**."*

**It was discovered at all only because Lt Cdr Bunger saw it from a seaplane window in 1947.** ⭐ **A genuinely
hidden place** — which sits perfectly with a city founded on a station that had been dark for 43 years.

## ✅ RULED 2026-09-01 — SINGLE-APPROACH, from Casey only

> `[CGRM 2026-09-01 · Path 6 · developer ruling]`
>
> **This city is reachable overland from exactly one direction: east, from Casey.**
> **The Denman crossing is NOT built. There is no western link to Mirny.**

### ⚠ Recording a same-day reversal, per the standing rule that self-corrections get logged

**This was first ruled the other way.** The initial ruling was *both* directions — *"if it's such an enormous
source of food for the country, then it needs to be able to reach in both directions"* — and it was reversed
within the hour, at the developer's request for the counter-argument. **Both rulings are recorded because the
reasoning that flipped it is worth more than either verdict.**

**The two arguments that reversed it:**

1. **⭐ The western leg is a SHORTCUT, not an access route.** **Hwy 110 already runs Zhongshan → Davis →
   Mirny → Casey → Concordia — Mirny is already on it.** So a Casey link does not merely reach Casey; it
   reaches the entire national network *through* the Hwy 110 × Hwy 2 junction, Mirny included. The western leg
   would buy **~350 km direct versus ~1,390 km around** — a real efficiency, **but not access.** The logistics
   premise is satisfied without it. **Nobody goes hungry.**
2. **⭐ Single-approach explains the 43-year dormancy, which the alternative left as a hole.** The site holds
   the largest ice-free ground and the largest freshwater lake on the continent. **If it were also a
   comfortable two-way stop on a coastal through-route, why was it dark for two generations?** Hard access
   answers it — and matches the real record exactly: *"sea- and land-based approaches are difficult and rarely
   attempted,"* at a place not discovered until someone glimpsed it from a seaplane window in 1947. **A hidden
   place should stay a little hidden.**

### What this buys

- **⭐ Strategic exposure becomes real.** Two roads is redundancy; **one road is a knife.** A nation whose
  second agricultural region has exactly one land approach is genuinely exposed — far sharper for the Long
  Night War than a route with a bypass.
- **⭐ The marine inlets stop being decorative.** With land access one-way, **sea shipping through the channels
  near the Highjump Archipelago becomes load-bearing**, and the real geography — a settlement of islands and
  inlets behind an ice shelf — starts doing work instead of sitting in a spec file.
- **A limit, which this nation should have.** Tepenia's canon already carries hard limits: the Tower fell,
  Mountain Pass Airport's chamber plant died with the grid. **"We tried, and the ice won"** is a better fact
  about this country than "we bridged it."

   the western approach would be a considerable location. Not decided.)*
2. ~~Designation.~~ **✅ RULED 2026-09-01 — it is a SPUR, not a numbered highway.**

### ✅ Designation: a spur `[CGRM 2026-09-01 · Path 6 · developer ruling]`

> *"That actually means that {{currently-unnamed city}} actually gets a 'spur', rather than a full highway
> (which is handy, since it means I don't need to draw a new one)."*

**Consistent with existing corpus precedent** — the **Sayowa Spur** and the **Neumayer connector** are both
named, unnumbered connections rather than numbered highways. **No new highway number is assigned, and the
national highway map does not change.**

**Naming follows the established pattern — the spur takes the city's name** *(cf. "the Sayowa Spur")*, so it
resolves automatically once the city is named. **Placeholder: `{{the [City] Spur}}`.**

**⚠ Minor open call — which parent it branches from.** **Recommended: Hwy 110**, since Hwy 110 is the coastal

*(Alternatives: Hwy 2, whose western endpoint is the junction with Hwy 110 at Casey; or the junction complex
itself. All three meet at Casey, so the practical difference is small.)*

> **⭐ And this is the right shape for what the ruling actually says.** A numbered highway is a route *through*
> somewhere. **A spur is a road that only goes one place and then stops** — which is exactly what a
> single-approach city has, and it says so on the map without a word of explanation.

## ⭐⭐ CONSEQUENCE — Mirny becomes the best-fed city in Tepenia

**Developer observation, 2026-09-01, and the distances confirm it is stronger than stated:**

> *"Going in the direction of Mirny, it's actually not extremely terribly far to Davis. This would actually
> make the actual city of Mirny (while being a heavily blue-collar working-class town) a city where food is
> actually comparatively very cheap (relative to the overall country as a whole), since shipping costs would
> factor comparatively low, by national standards."*

**⚠ Revised after the single-approach ruling. The observation holds — its SOURCE changes.**

**Straight-line, Mirny does sit between both producers** *(1° longitude ≈ 45 km at 66°S)*:

```
Davis ──── ~680 km ────► MIRNY ──── ~350 km ────► {{Bunger}} ──── ~443 km ────► Casey
(breadbasket)                    (no road — Denman)   (2nd ag. region)   (spur)
```

**But there is no western road.** By actual route, Mirny → {{Bunger}} is **~1,390 km around via Casey**, not
350. **So Mirny's cheap food rests entirely on Davis — ~680 km on existing Hwy 110 — and not at all on the new
city.**

> ## **The finding survives intact, sourced to Davis alone.** ~680 km to the national breadbasket, on a
> highway that already exists. *(For scale: Vostok is 1,260 km from its own subnet's nearest coastal city.)*

> ### ⭐ And the ruling splits the result in a better way than the original version had it.
> **Mirny is fed by Davis. Casey is fed by {{Bunger}}** — ~443 km up the spur, making Casey the nearest city
> to the new agricultural region and giving it a cheap-food status it did not previously have.
> **Two adjacent well-fed cities, drawing on two different larders.**

**⭐ The contrast is the whole point.** In a nation where food is expensive everywhere — energy-intensive
hydroponics, brutal haulage over lethal distances — **Mirny is a heavily blue-collar, working-class,
industrial town whose people eat cheaply and well.** Threads worth pulling: cheap food is effectively a **wage
substitute**, which changes who moves to Mirny and why *("you don't get paid much at Mirny, but you eat")*;
and a working-class food culture of abundance rather than scarcity — canteens, big portions, no pretension.

### The national food-price gradient this implies — a derivable attribute for all 38 cities

**Two centers of gravity, on opposite sides of the continent:**

| Tier | Cities | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Cheapest** | Davis, {{Bunger}}, **Mirny** *(via Davis, ~680 km)*, **Casey** *(via the spur, ~443 km)* | Sitting on, or one short haul from, the East Antarctic agricultural core |
| **Cheap** | Signy, Juan Carlos, Esperanza *(Palmer subnet)* | **On the Scotia Sea krill grounds** — the other center of gravity |
| **Moderate** | Halley subnet coastal cities | Own fishing, no major producer nearby |
| **⚠ Most expensive** | **Vostok, Kunlun, Dome Fuji** | The plateau — far from both centers, and structurally unable to grow |

**Cheap to compute, highly characterful, and it gives every city a derived economic attribute.** Belongs in
`Division_of_Industry/` once the national food balance is settled. **Not yet applied to any city file.**
