**Climate data source:** *AWS, White Smoke Lake, Bunger Hills oasis (Jan 1992 – Jul 1993) for the annual

**Climate type:** **East Antarctic coastal OASIS, shelf-sealed** — *ice-free basin terrain; **cut off from the
open sea by 64–145 km of ice shelf**, so it carries a continental temperature range at a coastal latitude.*
⭐ **Warm by Antarctic standards** *(−11.2 °C against −17.3 °C in the McMurdo Dry Valleys)*
**Mean annual temperature:** **−11.2 °C** 🔬 *measured on site*
**Prevailing winds:** ⭐⭐ **EASTERLY, and fierce in event — but the annual mean is only 4.6 m/s.** *This site
sits **out of the katabatic drainage**, in a basin behind the shelf.* ⚠ **Compare its neighbors: Mirny
10–14 m/s and "one of the windiest coastal stations in Antarctica," Casey 7–10 m/s.** ***This is one of the
calmest coastal sites in the Federation, and its nearest analogue is Davis at ~5.6 m/s — the other oasis city
out of the katabatic regime.***
**Record extremes:** ⚠ **ESTIMATED — record high approximately +12 °C; record low approximately −39 °C.**
*Bracketed by Davis (+13.0 / −41.8), Mirny (+8.7 / −36.5) and Casey (+9.2 / −37.5); the oasis's bare rock and
low wind argue for high summer maxima, the shelf-sealed continentality for low winter minima.* ⛔ **Not
measured — replace if the 1956–58 or 1986–89 series is ever obtained**
**Temperature range:** warmest month **+0.6 °C**; coldest month **−18.7 °C**; **annual amplitude 19.3 K**
*(against Mirny's 14.4 K — the continentality is the difference)*
**Polar night:** ⛔ **none** — *at 66°15'S the sun rises every day of the year; the June minimum is ~2.7 h*
**Midnight sun:** ⚠ **in minimal form only** — *the site lies **18 arcmin NORTH of the Antarctic Circle**, so
geometrically it gets none; with atmospheric refraction and the sun's upper limb the December disc does not
quite set for a few days around the solstice.* ⭐ *Same marginal condition Mirny records, and even more
marginal here*
**Winter solstice daylight minimum:** approximately **2.7 hours**
**Summer solstice daylight maximum:** approximately **24 hours** *(refracted; ~22.4 h geometric)*
**Annual precipitation:** ⚠ **~92 mm water equivalent — ESTIMATED.** *Anchored between Davis's measured
72.8 mm (the Vestfold Hills oasis) and Casey's 225.1 mm, and far below Mirny's 527.1 mm.* ***An ice-free basin
with extensive dry valleys and little lying snow is an arid site, and the shelf cuts off the moisture source.***

## ⭐ Precipitation regime — what falls vs what lands

**Regime:** **POLAR ARID (oasis)** — *dry, and what falls is largely redistributed rather than retained*
**Falls (reaches the surface):** ~92 mm/yr
**Lands (accumulates and stays):** **~60 mm/yr** *(~65% retention)*
**Lost to sublimation and wind transport:** ~32 mm/yr
⚠ *Retention is higher than a windswept coastal site's would be, because the mean wind here is 4.6 m/s rather
than Mirny's 10–14 — **less scour** — but the summer above-freezing period and the bare warm rock take their
share by melt and sublimation instead.*

> ##### ⚠ WIND vs COLD — which hazard actually defines this city
> ⭐⭐ **NEITHER. The hazard here is the SUMMER.** *At −11.2 °C it is among the mildest sites in the
> Federation, and at 4.6 m/s among the calmest.* ***What it has that almost nowhere else does is **71
> °C-days above freezing** — a real melt season on bare ground.*** **Meltwater, saturated regolith, active
> drainage, freeze–thaw and slush are the engineering problem**, not drift and not scour. *A city here fights
> water, not wind — and it is the only large Tepenian site where that is the primary answer.*
