## Geographic Basis

Cape Denison sits on Commonwealth Bay, George V Land, East Antarctica — a rare stretch of exposed bedrock on an otherwise ice-buried coast, which is precisely why Douglas Mawson chose it as his expedition's main base in 1911. He had no way of knowing until it was too late to relocate that he'd picked what may be the windiest permanent habitation site at sea level on the planet: average wind speeds around 80 km/h, with recorded gusts well over 300 km/h during the worst katabatic events. His own account of the ordeal was titled *Home of the Blizzard*, and the name outlasted the expedition itself.

The wind here is close to constant rather than episodic — more extreme than Janbogo's already-formidable Terra Nova Bay katabatics, and dramatically more extreme than Casey's comparatively moderate coastal regime. Denison's founders inherited no equivalent of Casey's substantial modern station infrastructure to build on; the only prior human history on the site was a small, legendary expedition hut and generations of scientific reputation as one of the most hostile inhabited points on the continent. They built a genuine city there anyway.

**Climate type:** Coastal East Antarctic, defined overwhelmingly by katabatic wind severity rather than temperature extremity
**Prevailing winds:** Katabatic, essentially constant; average ~80 km/h, gusts recorded well over 300 km/h during the worst events — among the most severe wind regimes at any permanently inhabited sea-level site on Earth
**Polar night / midnight sun:** Both occur at this latitude (~67°S), adding a genuine light-cycle rhythm layered on top of the wind-window rhythm — full detail TBD

#### Notable Weather Phenomena

- **Near-constant katabatic wind:** unlike most Tepenian cities, wind here doesn't fully respect season — events occur year-round, intensifying further in winter
- **Wind-window logistics:** movement, construction, and outdoor activity happen in the gaps between wind events rather than on a clean day/night or seasonal schedule

#### Gameplay Notes

- **The interlinked city structure:** Denison's ruins would preserve, in whatever damaged form, a built environment that functioned as one continuous, aerodynamically coherent structure rather than separate buildings — a distinctive exploration space unlike any other ruin in Tepenia
- **The exposed bedrock outcrop:** the rare rock exposure that made settlement possible at all remains a literal and symbolic anchor point for the ruins

## Founding

**Settled:** Post-Falkland Treaty, on the site of Douglas Mawson's Australasian Antarctic Expedition (1911–1914). Founding population: Australian exiles first, joined quickly by the same broad Pacific-corridor mix that would later define Casey (per the Significant/Notable tiers above). Unlike Casey's founders, Denison's had no substantial modern station to inherit — they built a genuine city on a site whose only legacy was a legendary, nearly-unsurvivable expedition and a reputation for extremity.

The founding generation didn't try to talk the wind down. They built around it, named their institutions after it, and made surviving Cape Denison's climate the city's foundational identity from day one — a city founded on a dare already accepted by history: Mawson's expedition had proven a human presence there was possible, barely, and the exiles took that as license rather than warning.

## Character & Culture

Denison's culture is built around a specific, almost defiant relationship to extremity — pride in the specific, named severity of its own environment, performed openly rather than endured quietly. Residents know Cape Denison's wind statistics the way other cities know their founding dates, and reciting them to outsiders is close to a local social ritual. This is the explicit opposite of the "competence without commentary" ethos shared by most other Tepenian cities (see Factions, below).

**Developer vision, established 2026-07-05 — a city as one continuous structure:** Denison takes wind-engineering further than any other Tepenian city by treating its built environment as a single connected system rather than a collection of individual buildings. Structures are physically joined throughout the city — a comprehensive, city-wide interlinking, not just occasional covered connections the way Zukelli or Janbogo use them — so that moving through Denison means moving through one continuous, load-sharing, aerodynamically coherent structure. A handful of landmark structures are distinctive and recognizable even at real distance, visible through blowing snow, giving Denison a skyline that reads as deliberate and unified at a glance in a way no other wind-exposed Tepenian city achieves. Full detail in `Local_Cultures/Janbogo_Subnet/Denison.md`.

**Nationally known for:** Being the windiest city in Tepenia and being proud of it. The Mawson expedition legacy. Wind-engineering expertise. The one Tepenian city that dissents from the rest of the Federation's shared "don't talk about your own toughness" instinct.

## Economy & Industry

Structural/wind engineering was Denison's defining economic sector — an unusually large share of the city's labor, reflecting its central environmental challenge — alongside climate/wind research, general manufacturing, and a modest commercial sector. Full detail in `Local_Cultures/Janbogo_Subnet/Denison.md` Section 15.

**Confirmed 2026-07-07: Denison was a historical fabrication-synthesis chamber manufacturer** — a legitimate producer of the individual apparatus at the heart of the nationwide Cradle infrastructure during its living Second Interwar Period, lost when the city was destroyed. Distinct from the present-day network (Sinheung, Byrd, and the Mountain Pass outpost) — Denison's own general manufacturing strength made it a plausible past producer, but it is not a current one.

## Legacy

The city that endured Mawson's wind for over a century did not, in the end, endure the war. Its diaspora carries the specific grief of having lost not just a home but a place whose whole identity was about *surviving* — and having that identity outlast the environment it was built to withstand, only to be ended by something else entirely.
