## Geographic Basis

**The Bunger Hills oasis, on the Queen Mary Coast of Wilkes Land, between Mirny (93°01'E) and Casey
(110°31'E) — both Mirny subnet.** ⭐⭐ **One of the largest ice-free expanses in East Antarctica: ~450 km² of
bare rock, rising to ~942 km² if the marine inlets and lakes are counted.** *A bounding box roughly 41 km
north–south by 51 km east–west.*

**Rugged low hills, many freshwater and saline lakes, and extensive dry valleys with little lying snow.**
⭐ **The largest and deepest is Algae Lake — Lake Figurnoye — 25 km long and up to 137 m deep**, the largest
body of fresh water on the continent.

**It is walled on every side.** *The Antarctic ice sheet rises against it to the southeast; outlet glaciers
close it to the south and west — among them the **Denman Glacier**, 11–16 km wide, descending 110 km, heavily
crevassed, **with the deepest point on continental Earth beneath it, an ice-filled canyon 3.5 km below sea
level***; and the **Shackleton Ice Shelf** seals the whole northern approach from the open sea.

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ THE TWO FACTS THAT DEFINE THE SITE, AND THEY PULL AGAINST EACH OTHER
> **1 · It has more usable ground than anywhere else in the Federation.** *At the comfortable band it could
> hold 3,375,000 people; at Manila's density, 20,780,100 — **two-thirds of the entire country, on bare
> rock**.* **It carries 947,954, which is 28% of what it could take comfortably.**
>
> **2 · It is the hardest place in the Federation to reach.** *No sea access, one road, and the road runs
> 300+ km east to Casey before it meets anything.* ***Land to spare and no way to ship — the exact inverse of
> Denison's problem.***
>
> ⭐ **That tension is the city.** *It is not remote because it is poor; it is empty because it is sealed.*

⭐ **And it is one of only TWO Tepenian cities that fit entirely on rock** *(the other is Davis, on the
Vestfold Hills)*. **Nothing here is built on ice, and nothing here is on a maintenance treadmill because of
its ground.**

### Annual Climate

**Climate data source:** *AWS, White Smoke Lake, Bunger Hills oasis (Jan 1992 – Jul 1993) for the annual
anchors; monthly distribution constructed against Mirny and Davis — full research record and provenance in
`../Bunger_Hills_City/Climate.md`*

**Climate type:** **East Antarctic coastal OASIS, shelf-sealed** — *ice-free basin terrain; cut off from open
sea by 64–145 km of ice shelf, so it carries a continental temperature range at a coastal latitude.*
⭐ **Warm by Antarctic standards** *(−11.2 °C against −17.3 °C in the McMurdo Dry Valleys)*
**Mean annual temperature:** **−11.2 °C** 🔬 *measured on site*
**Prevailing winds:** ⭐⭐ **EASTERLY, and fierce in event — but the annual mean is only 4.6 m/s.** *The basin
sits **out of the katabatic drainage**.* ⚠ **Compare its neighbors: Mirny 10–14 m/s and "one of the windiest
coastal stations in Antarctica," Casey 7–10 m/s.** ***One of the calmest coastal sites in the Federation; its
nearest analogue is Davis at ~5.6 m/s — the other oasis city out of the katabatic regime.***
**Record extremes:** ⚠ **ESTIMATED — record high approximately +12 °C; record low approximately −39 °C**
*(bracketed against Davis, Mirny and Casey; not measured)*
**Temperature range:** coldest month **−18.7 °C**; warmest month **+0.6 °C**; **annual amplitude 19.3 K**
*(against Mirny's 14.4 K — the continentality is the difference)*
**Polar night:** ⛔ **none** — *at 66°15'S the sun rises every day of the year; the June minimum is ~2.7 h*
**Midnight sun:** ⚠ **in minimal form only** — *the site lies **18 arcmin NORTH of the Antarctic Circle**, so
geometrically it gets none; with atmospheric refraction and the sun's upper limb the December disc does not
quite set for a few days around the solstice*
**Winter solstice daylight minimum:** approximately **2.7 hours**
**Summer solstice daylight maximum:** approximately **24 hours** *(refracted; ~22.4 h geometric)*
**Annual precipitation:** ⚠ **~92 mm water equivalent — ESTIMATED**, anchored between Davis's measured 72.8 mm
and Casey's 225.1 mm, far below Mirny's 527.1 mm. *An ice-free basin with dry valleys and little lying snow is
an arid site, and the shelf cuts off the moisture source.*

#### ⭐ Precipitation regime — what falls vs what lands

**Regime:** **POLAR ARID (oasis)** — *dry, and what falls is largely redistributed rather than retained*
**Falls (reaches the surface):** ~92 mm/yr
**Lands (accumulates and stays):** **~60 mm/yr** *(~65% retention)*
**Lost to sublimation and wind transport:** ~32 mm/yr

> ##### ⚠ WIND vs COLD — which hazard actually defines this city
> ⭐⭐ **NEITHER. The hazard here is the SUMMER.** *At −11.2 °C it is among the mildest sites in the
> Federation, and at 4.6 m/s among the calmest.* ***What it has that almost nowhere else does is **71
> °C-days above freezing** — a real melt season on bare ground.*** **Meltwater, saturated regolith, active
> drainage, freeze–thaw and slush are the engineering problem**, not drift and not scour. *A city here fights
> water, not wind — and it is the only large Tepenian site where that is the primary answer.*

#### Notable Weather Phenomena

**A genuine melt season** — *71 °C-days above freezing, standing meltwater, and lakes that open.* **Fierce
easterly wind events against an otherwise calm mean.** **Freeze–thaw cycling on exposed rock**, which is a
weathering regime almost no other Tepenian city experiences. ⚠ **Perennially ice-covered lakes sit alongside
seasonally open ones** — *the site is marginal for thick lake ice, and glacier contact decides which lakes
keep their cover.*

#### Gameplay Notes

- **The melt season is a calendar** — *ground conditions here change across the year in a way they do not
  anywhere else; December–February is a different place from July.*
- **Wind is not the threat** — *a player conditioned by Denison or Mirny will misjudge this site.*
