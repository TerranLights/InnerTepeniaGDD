## Geographic Basis

Zukelli and Janbogo are neighbors. This is not a metaphor or a loose geographic claim — the real stations on which they are based (Mario Zucchelli Station and Jang Bogo Station) sit approximately 8 kilometers apart on the same bay, the same stretch of Victoria Land coast, sharing the same Terra Nova Bay polynya. In Tepenia, the two cities grew up alongside each other, facing the same water, using the same port infrastructure, experiencing the same katabatic winds off the Transantarctic Mountains, watching the same polynya remain open through winters that froze every other patch of Ross Sea coast.

Zukelli was not a suburb of Janbogo or a satellite settlement. It was a distinct city with its own identity, culture, government, and history. But the two cities knew each other the way cities 8km apart inevitably know each other — as permanent presences on each other's horizon, as partners in the shared infrastructure of the bay, as communities that overlapped at the edges.

The Terra Nova Bay polynya — the persistent stretch of open water maintained by katabatic winds — was a shared resource and a shared landmark. Both cities built around it. Both depended on it. When the Long Night War came, the polynya kept running regardless.

The city is named for Zucchelli — specifically Mario Zucchelli, the Italian Antarctic program director who shaped Italy's presence on the Ross Sea over generations. The Tepenian name is a simplified phonetic adaptation of the original.

#### Gameplay Notes

- **The ruins in Janbogo's shadow:** the most distinctive thing about Zukelli's ruins is that they are visible from a living city; this is unlike every other destroyed city in Tepenia, where the ruins exist in geographic isolation; exploring Zukelli's ruins means being able to see Janbogo's lights from the wreckage, and Janbogo residents can see (or choose not to look at) what remains of their neighbor
- **The shared polynya:** the Terra Nova Bay polynya runs between where Janbogo stands and where Zukelli's ruins are; maritime access to the ruins requires navigating the same waters that Janbogo's port uses
- **Decoding the structural language — established 2026-07-07:** Zukelli's own tangled, unplanned streets were never navigable to outsiders — only to residents who'd learned an informal, organically-emerged system of color- and pattern-marking on the city's covered connections and heating-pipe conduits (see `City_Megasheets/Janbogo_Subnet/Zukelli/Zukelli_Full_Extrapolation.md`, Section II). In the ruins, this becomes a genuine gameplay mechanic: a player with **10 Calculation, 10 Investigation, 6+ Engine, and 6+ Nerve** can examine the surviving markings closely enough to start recognizing real patterns in them — not a full translation, but enough to deduce, at a basic level, what the system meant and get a genuine sense of how Zukelli's residents actually lived day to day. A quiet, no-combat, pure-observation payoff for a specific, demanding stat build, distinct from the more common item-gated or dialogue-gated unlocks elsewhere in the project. **How the player discovers this exists, confirmed 2026-07-07 (see `Game-Mechanics/Core-Mechanics/Interaction_Highlight_System.md`):** the game's standard hold-to-highlight key extends conditionally here — a qualifying player holding it sees walls, stairs, door-frames, lanterns, and other structural-language-bearing elements light up alongside the normal item/NPC highlights; a player who doesn't meet the stat threshold sees only the ordinary baseline highlight set, with no visible hint anything more exists.
- **A social gate-check, confirmed 2026-07-07:** the insight gained from decoding the structural language isn't just flavor — it functions as a genuine, no-combat off-ramp from an encounter that would otherwise be near-guaranteed violence. The most plausible target for this: a Zukelli-descended guardian or diaspora member encountered at or near the ruins, who reacts to an unknown outsider with immediate hostility, reasonably assuming looting or disrespect toward what's left of their home. A player who has already decoded even a basic level of the structural language gets the option to demonstrate genuine, specific, correct understanding of how residents actually lived — proof they've taken the time to actually know and respect the place, not exploit its tragedy — which is what de-escalates the encounter. This lands directly on the already-established "memorial or wound" tension within Janbogo's own relationship to the ruins (`Zukelli_Full_Extrapolation.md`, Section IV — the split between residents who keep an informal vigil and those who've made peace with not looking) — the guardian's own suspicion is exactly the vigil-keeper instinct that tension describes, and earning their trust through real, earned knowledge rather than a scripted password is the whole point.
- **The "Zukelli Native" perk, confirmed 2026-07-07 (see `Game-Mechanics/Perks/Special_Unique_Perks.md`):** once the player has gate-checked past the guardian, he asks for a personal favor — finding out something for him (details TBD). If the player follows through and returns with the information in good faith, he thanks them, admits he "doesn't have much to give in return," but guarantees them safe passage with any of his people. Mechanically, this grants the **Zukelli Native** perk: guaranteed non-hostility with Zukelli natives and descendants, plus unique dialogue options with any NPC — regardless of faction or district — who has a real connection to Zukelli, whether that connection is positive or negative.
- **Hardcore Mode influences:** no unique climate modifiers beyond Janbogo's; the ruins themselves introduce structural hazards rather than environmental ones

## Founding

**Settled:** Post-Falkland Treaty. Italian exiles built on the Mario Zucchelli Station infrastructure, which had been Italy's primary Antarctic presence from 1985. *(Refined 2026-07-25, GPS-purposes-only pass:* through the First Interwar Period, the station was continuously maintained by a rotating succession of national operators — which nations held it, and in what order, isn't relevant to the story. Preserved journals, logs, and orientation manuals left behind across the centuries gave the exiles a real documentary starting point, distinct from being taught by any living institution.)* By the time the exiles actually arrived in 2564, the facility was a mature, substantial, genuinely functional physical footprint the founding population reclaimed and made their own.

**Founding population:** Primarily Italian exiles. From that starting population, Zukelli's exile community built out its own language habits, food culture, architecture, and social tradition on-site over the centuries that followed, shaped by the city's own circumstances rather than transplanted wholesale from anywhere.

The proximity to the founding population at Janbogo created an immediate and permanent civic relationship. Two communities, 8km apart, sharing a bay. The relationship between them — cooperative, competitive, friendly, complicated — developed over centuries.

## Character & Culture

Zukelli had the character of a city that was never alone. Janbogo was always there, visible across the bay, close enough that on a clear day you could see its lights. This proximity shaped Zukelli's identity in the same way that sharing a wall shapes two neighbors: constant awareness of each other, a relationship that was sometimes generous and sometimes territorial, a shared history that accumulated over centuries without anyone formally deciding to share it.

Food culture was the city's defining feature — its own approach to siligel preparation, to communal eating, to the social function of the shared table, developed out of the tangled, densely-built city's own circumstances into Zukelli's most exported cultural product. The city was known throughout the Janbogo subnet for its food. People came from Janbogo for it.

The katabatic winds and the polynya were understood as connected phenomena — the same force that made the bay's water stay open in winter also made outdoor life challenging. The city's architecture reflected this: sheltered courtyards, wind-oriented street layouts, covered connections between buildings that allowed outdoor movement without full wind exposure.

**Nationally known for:** The food culture. The relationship with Janbogo. The polynya. Being the city 8km from the one that survived.

**Developer vision, established 2026-07-05:** A tangled, organically-grown city — built as its needs arose rather than to any master plan, navigable on sight only to people who actually lived there. Beneath the heating infrastructure, elaborate plantlines and elevated "bridger-footroads" gave residents something resembling equatorial nature to walk among, much of it artificial or genetically engineered for the cold but real in effect. A huge, genre-diverse music scene grew out of the city's own food-and-hospitality civic core, with most entertainment happening among friends and in restaurants with dedicated performance stages rather than at standalone venues. Above all else, people describe it as a place with a *soul* — a description that lands with real weight given it was destroyed at 72.5% human retention, near peak, not in decline. Full detail in `Local_Cultures/Janbogo_Subnet/Zukelli.md`.

## Connection to Concordia

Zukelli's connection to Concordia ran through Janbogo — the same subnet hub, the same highway south on Hwy 183. Zukelli refugees who made it to Concordia traveled through or from Janbogo first; the Zukelli community in Concordia is embedded within or alongside the larger Janbogo diaspora community rather than existing as a fully distinct group.

The most immediate legacy of Zukelli's destruction in Concordia is among the people who watched it happen. Janbogo residents and refugees who were in the city when Zukelli was destroyed carry that as a specific kind of memory — the knowledge of what it looks like when a city you can see from your window stops being a city. That is a distinct form of trauma from losing a city you only knew by reputation.

## Current Status / Destruction

Destroyed in the Long Night War. The ruins sit on Terra Nova Bay approximately 8km from Janbogo. The polynya still runs between them.

**Why Zukelli was obliterated while Janbogo, 8km away, was only damaged — established 2026-07-05:** Zukelli was sheltering multiple Upper Earth soldiers who had fully defected and gone AWOL, believing they could find refuge there — aided in hiding by both the local human and robot population. When the soldiers' home military (nationality TBD) discovered where they'd fled, it ordered a strike specifically calibrated for total, absolute obliteration, for two deliberate reasons: (1) to guarantee the defectors could not have survived or been extracted, and (2) to send an unmistakable message to the rest of Tepenia that harboring Upper Earth fugitives would be answered with total, indiscriminate violence.

This is also why Janbogo specifically had to survive, even just partially: a message requires a living witness to receive it and carry it forward. A weapon sized to guarantee total destruction of one specific target (rather than maximum-radius area denial) would plausibly still leave a city 8km away with real but survivable damage — shockwave and secondary effects, not annihilation. Janbogo's waterfront sightline to Zukelli's ruins (see Notable Locations, `Specs/Janbogo.md`) is, in this framing, not incidental proximity — it's the permanent, intended reminder of what the strike was for. Full historical specifics (exact timing within the war, the defectors' nationality/identities, how many, whether the message actually deterred other cities from sheltering defectors afterward) remain open, but the core mechanism — deliberate total destruction as both certainty of the kill and a deterrent broadcast to a spared neighbor — is now the established explanation.

## Legacy

Zukelli's legacy is partly in Concordia (the Italian community that came through Janbogo), partly in Janbogo itself (the survivor-neighbor relationship), and partly in the ruins. Of all the destroyed cities in Tepenia, Zukelli's ruins are the least isolated — they are visible from a living city, across water that is still open, in a location that has not become remote. Someone in Janbogo can look out a window and see them every day. Whether that is a memorial or a wound or both is a question Janbogo has been living with since the war ended. **Added 2026-07-05:** with the reason for Zukelli's total destruction now established (see Current Status, above), its legacy carries a second, sharper edge beyond simple loss — Zukelli didn't just die in the war, it died specifically because its people, human and robot alike, chose to shelter defectors at total cost to themselves. Whether Janbogo's community frames this as a legacy of solidarity worth honoring, a cautionary tale about what protecting the wrong person can cost, or both at once, is open — and likely one of the most emotionally charged threads in the whole subnet's post-war identity.
