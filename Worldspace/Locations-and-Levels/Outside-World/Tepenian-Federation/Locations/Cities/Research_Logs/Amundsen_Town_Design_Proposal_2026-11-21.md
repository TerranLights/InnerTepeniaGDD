## 1 · The idea in one paragraph

Everything at Amundsen Station stands on ice that **moves about 10 m a year** (FIXED, spec L121) and **rises about 0.23 m a year** as snow lands and never leaves (DERIVED in PHYS from the spec's 70–80 mm water equivalent; about 0.44 m a year around buildings, where drifts build). Only two things do not move: **the Pole itself**, and, in the Tower years, **the Tower's shaft, anchored in bedrock about 2.7 km down** (FIXED, TOWER L176). So the town is always being carried away from its own reason for existing and buried under its own feet. **PROPOSAL: make that the defining fact, and let it produce the two settings.** **Above** is the town's *present*: rebuilt and raised again and again so it stays at the Pole and at the Tower, warm, lit by a sky that is either all day or all night, and full of comings and goings. **Below** is the town's *past and its machinery*: cold by necessity, timeless, silent, its walls slowly closing in, and its oldest parts drifting away and sinking as the ice carries them off. The two are fundamentally different not by decree but by physics: one fights burial, the other lives inside it.

**The numbers behind it (DERIVED):**
- Per century: about **23 m** of snow on open ground (about 44 m beside buildings) and about **1 km** of drift.
- Over the Tower's working life (2688 to 2812, about 124 years; TOWER L1084–L1095, L643): the ice at the shaft's collar slides about **1.24 km** past the fixed shaft, and the surface rises about **28.5 m** around it.
- Over the town's whole life (founded after 2564; date open, `DR-16`): up to about **2.5 km** of drift and about **57 m** of burial by 2812, if it was founded early.

## 2 · Three ways to split above and below (choose one, or mix)

| | **A. The present above, the past below** (PROPOSAL, recommended) | **B. The real Pole's split** | **C. A town under the ice** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Above | Homes, dining, clinic, terminal, Tower base, depots, the hostel: the living town, kept raised and kept at the Pole | Living quarters raised on the surface; work above too | Only the Tower base, the road terminal and the depots |
| Below | Relay halls, archive, utilities, cold stores, the shaft, and the town's own older, buried levels drifting away | Power, water, fuel, workshops buried (SOURCED: the real Pole, PHYS §3) | Homes, public life and work, all under the snow |
| What makes them different | Warm against cold, seasonal against timeless, kept against drifting, present against past | Mostly function | Mostly scale |
| Real-world record | B's split plus the record of buried structures that close and are left (PHYS §2.2) | SOURCED, PHYS §3 "Who works where" | Camp Century and Fistclench (SOURCED), but "roughly 10 years" is the longest workable life for an under-snow camp (SOURCED, TR-174); no one has housed thousands this way |
| Risk | Needs the drift and burial accepted as the town's defining fact | The two settings feel like one place on two floors | Fights the record; the town would have to be rebuilt every decade or so |

**Why A:** it keeps everything the record says works (B's split), adds a reason the two settings can never feel alike, and gives the below a history that can be read in its layers. It also matches canon already written: a tunnel network "carved into moving ice" (FIXED, DLC1 L120), the archive "deep within" (FIXED, spec L251), and buried vents and hatches (FIXED, DLC1 L188, L196).

The rest of this file is written for **A**. If the developer picks B or C, sections 3 and 4 still hold as the inventory; only the split changes.

## 3 · ABOVE: the surface town

### 3.1 What it is like
- **Light:** six months of day, six of night, about seven weeks of twilight at each end, a 360° sunrise in September (FIXED, spec L172–L203). Twilight and darkness are the only seasons that exist.
- **Air and weather:** about −27 °C in midsummer to −60 °C in midwinter, records to −82.8 °C (FIXED); calm by Antarctic standards (FIXED, spec L169); the air is thin (pressure altitude about 3,200 m, up to 3,690 m, SOURCED, PHYS §3) and extremely dry, so static destroys electronics (SOURCED, PHYS §3, §7).
- **Rhythm:** the summer half is arrivals, convoys on Highway 22, building, raising and hauling; the winter half is the town closed in on itself under artificial light (PROPOSAL, following the record of every year-round polar station, PEOPLE §1.3).
- **Its enemy is burial.** Snow piles about two stories around buildings in about 17 years (SOURCED/DERIVED, PHYS §3); snow hauling is "a near full-time job" at the real Pole (SOURCED).

### 3.2 Infrastructure (PROPOSAL unless marked)
- **The Tower base and its terminal** (FIXED that it exists, TOWER L630; layout SOFT): the passenger and cargo terminal, the surface collar of the shaft, the Tower's control and maintenance offices. In the Tower years the busiest place in town.
- **The road terminal on Highway 22** (FIXED, the one highway, spec L7): convoy yard, vehicle and Rastra workshops (heated sheds for engines), fuel and cargo staging, the drivers' rest rooms.
- **Raised buildings on jackable legs**, the real Pole's answer (SOURCED, PHYS §3: designed to be jacked, never actually jacked). PROPOSAL: at Amundsen they *are* jacked, on a schedule, and a building that cannot be raised again is cut loose and left to the drift (see section 5).
- **Homes:** single rooms as the norm (SOURCED: single cabins "essential for well-being," privacy the strongest link to feeling restored, PEOPLE §3.1), a personal pinboard or shelf in each (SOURCED, Halley VI, PEOPLE §4.1). Quarters for humans and for robots in the same blocks (PROPOSAL; see 6.3).
- **The hostel for space workers on leave** (FIXED that it exists and is barracks-style, not homes, `DR-64`; about 287 beds in Census I, a working figure, `DR-65`; see the open conflict in 8.3). PROPOSAL: beside the Tower terminal, since its guests come down the Tower.
- **The commons:** a dining hall for the humans (robots eat siligel, FIXED, DLC1 L38), a gym, a sauna, a library, music and craft rooms, a games room, a quiet room for calls home: every year-round station in the record has nearly all of these (SOURCED, PEOPLE §4.4). The forms they take wait for the cultural synthesis.
- **Clinic, fire station, store** (medicine FIXED as a named service, spec L221; fire and store PROPOSAL, both empty in canon, CANON 5.27, 7.1).
- **Snow works:** haulers, blowers and the crews that run them; berms; the yearly raising of buildings, hatches and stair towers. PROPOSAL: one of the largest civilian crews in town, above.
- **Heat rejection:** waste heat from the town, the relay and (in the Tower years) the base must be thrown off *at the surface*, never into the ice below (SOURCED reason: building heat melts the host, PHYS §2.3). PROPOSAL: radiator fields and snow-melt plants on the surface, so the surface town is wrapped in its own warmth and steam (ice fog forms from exhaust below about −40 °C, SOURCED, PHYS §2.4).
- **The Pole marker** (FIXED, its form open, spec L250, L300). PROPOSAL: because the town drifts, the marker is the one surface fixture that is *moved back* every year, while everything else is moved *up*.

### 3.3 Who is mostly above (PROPOSAL)
Humans, because the surface is where heat can be allowed: their homes, the kitchens, the clinic, the store, the offices, the terminal staff, the drivers, the snow crews, the hostel's guests. Robots are above too (terminal, snow works, the Tower collar), in the same buildings.

## 4 · BELOW: the under-ice

### 4.1 What it is like
- **No sky, no seasons.** Time below is kept by shifts, not by the sun (PROPOSAL; windowless work is rated "less pleasant, less stimulating, and more enclosed" even when the measured light is the same, SOURCED, PHYS §2.6).
- **Cold, and it must stay cold.** Snow at depth is about −60 °F (about −51 °C) (SOURCED, PHYS §2.3). Heat brought below melts and weakens the walls (SOURCED), so the under-ice is kept cold on purpose: unheated corridors, warm only in small sealed rooms.
- **Silent and still.** A 100-mph gale overhead "would hardly" be felt (SOURCED, Fistclench, PHYS §2.6).
- **The walls close in.** Trenches in moving ice distort and must be trimmed every year (SOURCED, Camp Century: about 20,000 cubic yards a year; PHYS §2.2); ice pushes up from below (SOURCED, the Pole arches). Trimming crews are a permanent part of life below.
- **It moves.** Everything below, except the Tower shaft, drifts with the ice at about 10 m a year.

### 4.2 Infrastructure (PROPOSAL unless marked)
- **The relay halls** (the relay FIXED, its location open, CANON 1.17, 4.1). PROPOSAL: below, because cold is an asset for machines (the Tower file already reasons the same way for the Tower's own systems, SOFT, TOWER L346–L350) and because the cable comes in underground (Highway 59 "doubles as the Arcanet cable corridor to the Pole relay," FIXED, MREF L283).
- **The archive** (FIXED: "deep within," "protected," spec L251, L276). PROPOSAL: the deepest occupied place in the ice, cold enough to keep anything.
- **The utility tunnel network** (FIXED: carved into moving ice, pre-war, DLC1 L120, L188): water, waste, power and data lines between the buried plants and the surface town, like the real utilidors (SOURCED, PHYS §4).
- **Water from the ice itself:** snow melters and a melted well in the firn (the real Pole's Rodriguez well, SOURCED, PHYS §5.3). Details and the waste side in PHYS §5.3–5.4.
- **Cold stores:** food, siligel stock, spares; the ice is a freezer that costs nothing (PROPOSAL).
- **Warm rooms for charging robots.** A robot's charge draws on the supply, and warming a cold cell to charge it draws on it too (FIXED, national, provisional, `DR-140` item 4; `DR-138` item 8). PROPOSAL: so the under-ice has small, sealed, warm charging bays: the only warm places below, where the robots who work there gather.
- **The Tower shaft** (FIXED that it reaches bedrock; contents SOFT, TOWER L923–L940): the accelerator, power plant and maintenance tunnels. **The one fixed thing below.**
- **The shear ring** (PROPOSAL): where the moving ice meets the fixed shaft, the ice is constantly cut away and re-sealed as it slides past at 10 m a year. A permanent crew works it. It is the most dangerous workplace in town and the one that never ends.
- **The older levels** (PROPOSAL, section 5).

### 4.3 Who is mostly below (PROPOSAL)
Robots, because below must be cold: relay technicians, archive keepers, trimming crews, shear-ring crews, plant operators, cold-store hands. Humans go below in heated suits and cabs, for shifts, and come back up to sleep. **This is a split by physics, not by rule:** every resident may go anywhere, and many do (see 6.3).

## 5 · The joint, and the drift (PROPOSAL)

- **Ways down:** enclosed stair towers, freight lifts and vehicle ramps (all SOURCED as the real connections, PHYS §4), each extended upward as the surface rises. Escape hatches straight up through the snow (SOURCED, Camp Century had 16) are extended too, and are the first thing burial attacks (DERIVED in PHYS §4).
- **The drift:** a building, tunnel or hall that can no longer be raised or trimmed economically is **stripped, sealed and left to the ice**. It keeps sliding away from the Pole at 10 m a year and sinking about 0.23 m a year. Some old levels stay in use for years as cold storage or quiet workrooms before they are closed. So the under-ice has a gradient: **near the shaft and under the present town, the working tunnels; farther out, older and older levels, each from its own decade, closing and darkening, carried off by the ice.**
- **What that gives the place:** the town's history is laid out in space. Walking away from the Pole below the surface is walking back in time. (For later DLC work only: this is a ready-made layered ruin.)
- **Which way the drift runs** is not yet researched (see 8.4).

## 6 · The people (PROPOSAL, built on FIXED totals)

### 6.1 How many and what mix (FIXED)
- Census I: **1,121 humans, 5,736 robots, 6,857** (about 84% robot); Census II: 913, 3,978, 4,891 (spec L14–L15). The town is sized to Census I (`DR-4`).
- A composite of all of Tepenia, on periodic stays (`DR-98`).
- Scientists, engineers, cooks, chefs and other staff are "a solid majority … at any time" (`DR-64`); the named services are kitchens, utilities, medicine, teaching, offices, recreation (spec L221).

|---|---|---|
| Relay operation and upkeep | below | the largest share (the Division of Industry file gives relay 60%, Tower 30%, life support 10%; SOFT and in conflict, CANON 6.10) |
| Tower base, terminal and shaft (Tower years) | above and below | the second share |
| The archive | below | a small, long-staying crew (the archive has no line yet in the Division of Industry, CANON 6.10) |

| Snow works, raising and hauling | above | a large crew |

| Siligel handling and robot upkeep | below and above | robots add maintenance work rather than removing it (SOURCED, PEOPLE §6) |

### 6.3 Humans and robots (PROPOSAL)
- **Same buildings, different days.** Robots live in the same blocks above as humans (so no two-tier town), but most robots spend their working days below and many humans spend theirs above. The difference shows in *whose day is spent where*, not in who is allowed where.
- **Who stays longest:** the record has a permanent core beside rotating crews (for example 42 of 96 operating staff permanent, the rest rotating, at one remote plant, SOURCED, PEOPLE §2.2). PROPOSAL: the archive keepers and the shear-ring crews are that core, the town's old hands.

### 6.4 Terms and turnover (PROPOSAL)
Terms of months to years (FIXED, spec L229); "six months to two years" is SOFT (CANON 6.8). The record suggests a large yearly turnover (Longyearbyen about 21.8% a year, DERIVED; 43% there under two years, SOURCED; PEOPLE §2.1). Staggered rotations so no crew turns over all at once (PROPOSAL; a staggering rule is open, CANON 10.12).

## 7 · What people have (PROPOSAL; real-world basis SOURCED in PEOPLE §4)

**Above:**
- **Issued cold gear**, the station's property, handed back at the end of a term, never sold (SOURCED as the real practice, PEOPLE §4.2).
- **A small personal allowance** of things brought from home (the real allowances run 75 to 140 lb, UNVERIFIED, PEOPLE §4.1): photographs, a pinboard's worth of keepsakes, an instrument small enough to carry, letters to open on set dates (all SOURCED as real habits, PEOPLE §4.1).
- **A store** for small comforts and toiletries (SOURCED as the norm everywhere in the record, PEOPLE §4.3); what it sells and how it is paid for waits on `DR-3` and the culture work.
- **Rationed things:** water (the real Pole allows two two-minute showers a week, SOURCED), fresh food between resupplies, bandwidth home (SOURCED, PEOPLE §4.5). PROPOSAL: in the Tower years water and power are plentiful, so the ration that bites is **bandwidth and seats out**, not water.
- **Shared things:** the commons rooms; instruments, books and tools held in common; things left by earlier crews for the next (SOURCED as a recurring habit: bicycles, painted doors, the ceiling of hats, PEOPLE §4.6). The SOFT "Wall of Home" idea (CANON 7.5) would sit here.

**Below:**
- **Heated suits and cabs** for anyone who is not a robot, and **cell warmers** for the robots (PROPOSAL, from `DR-140` item 4).
- **Trimming tools, ice saws, haulers, survey gear** for watching the walls move (PROPOSAL).
- **Shift logs** kept at every hall and hatch (PROPOSAL; the real record has handover logs at every staffed post, PEOPLE §2.6).
- **Almost nothing personal.** Below is a workplace and a freezer; personal things stay above (PROPOSAL). The exception: what crews leave on purpose in the older levels before they are sealed.

## 8 · Questions for the developer

1. **The split:** A (present above, past below), B (the real Pole's split), C (a town under the ice), or a mix?
2. **The drift as the defining fact:** accept that the town is always being carried away and buried, and is kept at the Pole by raising and rebuilding?
3. **The hostel conflict:** `DR-65` gives about 287 beds in Census I; `DR-69` says there is no space workforce in Census I. Which governs the town's design size (CANON 11.12)? The same question for Tower staff in Census I (CANON 11.11).
4. **Humans and robots:** "same buildings, different days" (6.3), or a sharper split?
5. **Families and teaching:** "teaching" is a named service (spec L221); does that mean children live there, or the teaching of adults (training, courses)?
6. **The archive:** staffed from the start, or only from some later date?
7. **Power before the Tower:** what ran the town in its first century (CANON 5.6)? This changes how plentiful heat, water and power were above.
8. **Ice thickness:** about 2,700 m or about 2,850 m (CANON 1.3)?

## 9 · Research still needed (for a later round; nothing here was searched today)
1. **Which way and how fast the ice at the Pole moves** (direction and any change with depth): it decides which way the old levels drift.
2. **Snow density at the surface and its compaction with depth**, to turn 70–80 mm water equivalent into meters of burial reliably.
3. **How a fixed structure in bedrock survives moving ice around it** (deep boreholes, well casings and shafts through ice sheets; how fast they deform).
4. **Heat rejection on ice:** how real polar plants dump waste heat without melting their footing.
5. **Jacking and relocating large buildings on snow** (the record says designed, never done; are there cases where it was done?).
6. PHYS §9 and PEOPLE §11 hold the full lists.
