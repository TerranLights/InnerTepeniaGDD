## 1. Polar and ice roads as working infrastructure

Already held in earlier files (not repeated): Thur 2012 traverse figures (crew 10 and 8, fleet, 58-day plan, 7 mph, 28 gal/mile), Vostok-Progress 2022-23 figures, Dome Fuji/Concordia traverse notes. This section adds convoy life, stop-over camps, rules, hazards and neighboring roads.

### 1a. South Pole Traverse: convoy life (additions)
- SOURCED (Antarctic Sun, 1 Jan 2006, "Success! South Pole Traverse arrives Dec. 23", PDF read via pdftotext, https://www.southpolestation.com/trivia/00s/traversearrival.pdf): the proof-of-concept traverse arrived "after 43 days and some 1,600 kilometers"; the leader parked the last tractor and told the crew to "form camp and ensure all the vehicles were plugged into electric outlets to keep them warm"; "seven men and one woman ... their Carhartt overalls and jackets blackened with oil and grease" went at once through the standard orientation video in a Jamesway lounge ("the rigmarole that all new U.S. Antarctic Program visitors must"); station staff noted "you arrived a little differently than everybody else." Average age of the field team: 51.5 years (per the leader). Two red modules were used "for berthing and cooking" and stayed in use at the Pole for a couple of days of rest. 250 people were at the Pole when it arrived. Cargo 100,000 kg, "equivalent of 11 LC-130 flights." Route over soft snow ("we call it a swamp"; "three-mile days"; moving one kilometer forward cost several kilometers of shuttling cargo back and forth); the leader: "a lot of the same grind. It's not fun." The 2002-03 first season covered only ~200 km but crossed a 5.5 km shear zone "Swiss-cheesed with 32 crevasses." The lead PistenBully carried a ~6 m boom with ground-penetrating radar.
- SOURCED (Field Safety Coordinator's blog, https://gutzjourney.com/traverse-to-the-south-pole/): "We drag a kitchen module, freezer of food, snow melter, sink shower, and incinalete (poop burner)"; "We all rotate through camp duties such as cooking, melting, cleaning"; "Everyone drives a tractor"; teams are heavy equipment operators, mechanics and a field safety coordinator (crevasse detection, rescue training); one-way "usually takes about 3-4 weeks"; if crevasses are concerning "we re-route"; filling a crevasse to make a snow plug is a last resort.
- SOURCED (blog https://maddymck.wordpress.com/2015/01/06/spot/): "9 people are traveling in each traverse group" (comment reply); a sled "containing the kitchen and two bunk areas"; a second bunk sled nicknamed "Fawlty Towers"; "usually lasts 20-30 days to get from McMurdo to SP." UNVERIFIED as to current official practice (personal blogs).
- SOURCED (search summary of a traverse blog): in whiteout "we are going to shifts - moving the tractors and module every 4-6 hours to keep them from being buried." A 2010 storm stopped a tractor train for about four days. UNVERIFIED (snippet; primary page not opened).
- DERIVED: 9-10 people for ~52-58 days, about three round trips a season (ScienceBlog 2026, already in the 10-29 file) = on the order of 25-30 traverse-person-seasons of crew living in towed modules per summer; nothing in the sources gives a separate relief crew.
- NOT FOUND: any traverse-crew rest day policy, alcohol policy, or labor-agreement shift rules; Antarctic Sun archive pages for the traverse (host dead at the source in the 10-29 file).

### 1b. Tibbitt to Contwoyto Winter Road (Northwest Territories / Nunavut, Canada)
- SOURCED (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibbitt_to_Contwoyto_Winter_Road): about 600 km (as short as 400 km), ~495 km on frozen lakes and 73 km on 64 land portages; 50 m wide on ice; operating "February and March, an average of 67 days per year"; construction "about six weeks" each January by contractors (Nuna Logistics and RTL-Robinson); record "10,922 loads north" in 2007; 2025 about 5,800 northbound loads; "over 700 drivers registered during 2007 with nine accidents and one minor injury"; closed 91.5 hours that season (70 storm, 21.5 incidents); first road to Tundra Mine 1960-61; operated by Echo Bay Mines to 1998; Joint Venture since 1999. Page also says "Construction for 2027 has been canceled" - UNVERIFIED, one source only (a second search found no corroboration).

### 1f. Russian winter roads (Kolyma Highway, Lena crossing)
- SOURCED (Wikipedia R504 Kolyma Highway): 2,031 km Magadan to Nizhny Bestyakh; built by Dalstroy, Sevvostlag prisoners from 1932 to 1953; claims that hundreds of thousands were buried in the road "are unconfirmed"; Kolyma prison records show 10,251 deaths in 1938; settlements listed west to east (Khandyga, Kyubeme, Ust-Nera, Kadykchan, Susuman, Debin, ...); a 2020 death of a teenage motorist who followed navigation onto the abandoned Tomtor section; frozen rivers aid winter crossings on the Old Summer Road; Federal Road status 2008; all-weather gravel road with only ~159 km paved near each end.
- SOURCED (Kaspersky travel blog, 2021-03-15, https://eugene.kaspersky.com/2021/03/15/eats-fuel-and-places-to-stay-on-the-kolyma-highway/): about -50 C; a roadside cafe with filling station, entry with own food 200 rubles, "open all hours; knock hard," one outhouse; "no other café in either direction for around 250 kilometers"; cafes mainly serve long-distance truck drivers ("they come back!"); convoy refuel with one person per car paying once; hotels in Susuman, Khandyga (rented apartments in an old wooden building), Ust-Nera (spartan single rooms, "Lux" rooms sleeping two or three, restaurant next door) were warm; the fiber-optic cable lies on the surface because permafrost makes burying impractical and reindeer antlers snag it. Operators/owners of the cafes: NOT FOUND.
- SOURCED (search summary, Smithsonian-type photo essay): on long night drives drivers sometimes stop in the road, recline seats and sleep with engine and lights on. UNVERIFIED (single secondary).
- SOURCED (Moscow Times, 2 Feb 2026, https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/02/02/crunching-ice-under-my-feet-in-russias-sakha-a-still-unbuilt-bridge-is-a-matter-of-life-and-death-a91836): Lena has no bridge at Yakutsk; winter ice road Yakutsk-Nizhny Bestyakh; Yakutsk "can be cut off ... for up to half the year"; official crossings close in the shoulder seasons; hundreds of people each year cross unstable ice because they cannot afford alternatives; a private hovercraft or icebreaker-assisted ferry costs 5,000-10,000 rubles one way against an average Yakutsk monthly salary of 60,000 rubles; "cars sinking and people dying ... every year" (no figures). **Kolyma Highway, March 2019 (current-era services).**
- SOURCED (T-Zh, 14 Nov 2019, a 17-day hitchhiking trip Magadan to Yakutsk begun 9 March 2019, https://t-j.ru/kolyma/; translation by the fetch tool): distances counted from Yakutsk. ## 2. Remote highway junction towns and road-houses

### 2a. Dalton Highway (Alaska): Coldfoot, Wiseman, Deadhorse, Yukon River
- SOURCED (Wikipedia Dalton Highway): 414 miles (666 km); "only three towns along the route": Coldfoot (pop. 34) mile 175, Wiseman (pop. 12) mile 188, Deadhorse mile 414; "Fuel is available at the E. L. Patton Yukon River Bridge (Mile 56), as well as Coldfoot and Deadhorse"; "about 160 trucks daily in the summer months and 250 trucks daily in the winter." DERIVED from listed points: fuel gaps about 119 miles (mile 56 to Coldfoot) and about 239 miles (Coldfoot to Deadhorse). History: a winter road 1968-69 was abandoned after permafrost thaw; the haul road built in about 5 months from 29 April 1974 (390 miles); fully open to the public 2 Dec 1994 after a court ordered the permit requirement removed. Flooding of the Sagavanirktok River plus melting ice roads forced "weeks-long closures."

### 2h. Observed pattern across 2a-2g (facts only)

### 2l. Whitehorse-Dawson Overland Trail roadhouses, 1902-1920s (afternoon)
- SOURCED (Wikipedia Overland Trail (Yukon), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overland_Trail_(Yukon)): built 1902 for CDN$129,000 after the White Pass and Yukon Route won the mail contract; about 330 miles, 12 ft wide; stage trip five days; sleighs in snow, CDN$125 one way; White Pass fleet 1901-1921: 29 wagons, 54 sleighs including 14-passenger sleighs and 5-ton freight sleighs, about 1,358 horses over the years, most on hand in a season 217 (1909-10); first automobile 1912; White Pass stopped running stages in 1921; motor mail from 1922; a 1955 highway made the trail obsolete.
