## 1 · #88 Arica and the road up to the Altiplano

### Claim check
*Claim to test:* a sea-level port whose highway climbs to well over 4,000 m within a day's drive, carrying a landlocked neighbor's cargo inland by treaty, so the city's economy is the transit itself.
- **Treaty:** CONFIRMED. Treaty of Peace and Friendship between Bolivia and Chile, signed **20 October 1904** in Santiago; ratifications exchanged La Paz 10 March 1905. Chile "recognized the right of Bolivia in perpetuity to commercial transit through its territory and ports," terms "to be regulated by special agreements." The treaty also obliged Chile to build the Arica-La Paz railway (completed 1913). SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Peace_and_Friendship_(1904) (read 2026-11-20). ## 2 · #89 Massawa to Asmara (the coast-to-plateau road)

### Claim check
*Claim to test:* a hot port and a cool highland capital joined by a road (and once a cableway and a railway) that climbs more than 2,000 m in a short distance, so travelers feel the climate change hour by hour.
- **Elevations:** CONFIRMED. Asmara 2,325 m (sixth-highest capital in the world, second-highest in Africa). SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara (read 2026-11-20). Railway's highest point, east of Asmara, 2,394 m. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Railway (read 2026-11-20). Massawa on the coast, annual mean temperature "approaches 30 °C." SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa (read 2026-11-20).
- **Road:** CONFIRMED. Road P-1, Asmara-Massawa, **111 km**, fully paved, high point 2,414 m, "countless curves and hairpin turns," about 2-3 hours without stops. SOURCED: https://www.dangerousroads.org/africa/eritrea/2103-p1.html (read 2026-11-20). JICA gives 110 km: UNVERIFIED (snippet).
- **Railway:** CONFIRMED. 950 mm gauge; begun at Massawa 1887; reached Asmara 1911; Massawa-Asmara section needed 65 bridges (including a 14-arch viaduct over the Obel River) and 39 tunnels (longest 320 m); grades over 3%. SOURCED: Wikipedia Eritrean Railway.
- **Cableway:** CONFIRMED and dated. Built by the Italian firm Ceretti and Tanfani; first 26.6 km section (Ghinda to Godaif near Asmara) opened August 1936; full line completed 1937; about **71.8 km**; up to 30 t per hour each way in about 1,540 small gondolas on roughly 30 cable sections, diesel-powered; freight only (food, supplies and war materials for the Italian army after the 1936 conquest of Ethiopia); longest cableway of its kind at inauguration. After the British took control in 1941 they removed engines, cables and equipment as war reparations; remaining iron towers scrapped in the 1980s. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara-Massawa_Cableway (read 2026-11-20). (The 75 km figure on some pages: UNVERIFIED.)
- **"Climate change hour by hour":** CONFIRMED in substance by the official climate zoning: the country runs from sea level to above 2,400 m; the coastal plain (Semi-Desert zone, mean annual 24-32 °C, July-August highs up to 48 °C in many places, under 200 mm rain) gives way on the eastern escarpment to a **Sub-Humid Zone** (600-2,625 m, 700-1,100 mm, mean 16-27 °C), then the highlands (summer rains, July-August). **The escarpment has two rainy seasons, July-August and November-March; the winter rain comes from northeasterly winds off the Red Sea that drop their moisture on the steep escarpment, making it the wettest part of the country; the remaining forest, the "Green Belt Zone," lies here (forest cover 20% on slopes to 80% in valley bottoms).** SOURCED: State of Eritrea, Ministry of Land, Water and Environment, *Eritrea's Initial National Communication under the UNFCCC*, December 2001, section 2.2, https://unfccc.int/resource/docs/natc/erinc1.pdf (PDF read 2026-11-20). So a traveler crossing in winter meets rain and cloud on the escarpment while both the coast and the plateau are dry. [K+ climate] [P3, P5] Ghinda (928 m on the way) has a tropical savanna climate, 712 mm a year, mean daily highs 28-34.5 °C. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghinda (read 2026-11-20). A timed account of a traveler feeling the change: NOT FOUND.

### A. Snapshot
- **Asmara:** capital of Eritrea; population estimate 1,073,000 (2023, CIA World Factbook, as cited by Wikipedia; Eritrea has had no full census since independence: not checked, so UNVERIFIED); cool semi-arid climate, mean daily maxima 21.5-25.1 °C, minima 4.3-10.8 °C, about 518.6 mm rain a year, roughly 60% in July-August; inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in **2017** as "Asmara: A Modernist African City," described as the first modernist city listed in its entirety. SOURCED: Wikipedia Asmara.
- **Massawa:** Red Sea port on the islands of Base (Batsi) and Taulud and the mainland; population 53,090 (2012 estimate), 21,300 in 1962, about 15,000 in 1928; rainfall around 185 mm; very high summer humidity; "one of the hottest marine coastal areas in the world." SOURCED: Wikipedia Massawa. [P3]
- **Defining feature:** a short, steep link between a hot sea-level port and a cool highland capital, built up in layers (road, rail, cableway) under Italian colonial rule.

### B. What else exists there
- **Massawa port and economy** [K5, K8]: naval base, large dhow docks, rail station; ferries to the Dahlak Islands; exports copper and zinc from the Bisha mine, imports oil and consumer goods; residents say "some months up to 10 ships arrive, and others, none." Salt and cement factories (1993). SOURCED: Wikipedia Massawa.
