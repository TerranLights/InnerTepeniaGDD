## 1 · Lake Suwa's omiwatari, Japan

### A. Other settings
- **Neighborhood communal baths (kyōdō yokujō) as daily infrastructure.** A bath-guide writer (visit Oct 2006) states that Kamisuwa "is a treasure house of communal baths," that most are **for locals only** and few accept outsiders, and that the station tourist office will point visitors to the few that do. One he visited, **Koromo Onsen** (Kowada-minami, Suwa city): a small bathhouse in a residential street beside a river, entrance and pay desk at the **front door of the neighboring private house**, ¥200, 7:00–20:30, closed Thursdays; a regular told him she walks past the bath nearest her home to come here "because the water is different." [K10, K4] [P4] [R: shared bath/rest place] — SOURCED, asahi-net.or.jp/~UE3T-CB/spa/kamisuwa_koromo/kamisuwa_koromo.htm (2006-10 visit; read 2026-10-29 after Shift-JIS decoding).
- **Shimosuwa** (the Lower Shrine's gate town and the only hot-spring post town on the old Nakasendō, per the town's own sheet) has, per the same writer, communal baths "here and there in the residential streets." At **Minami Onsen** (south of Shimosuwa Station, 5:00–21:30, no closing day, ¥220) "the bathers seemed all to be regulars, greeting each other and remarking that so-and-so is late today." [K10] [P4] — SOURCED, asahi-net.or.jp/~UE3T-CB/spa/simosuwa_minami/simosuwa_minami.htm (2006-10 visit).
- The town's own migration sheet (as of 1 Jan 2025): "many public baths (hot springs) you can enter for **¥280–400**, and you can go from **early morning**." **Tanga no Yu** (managed under the town; opening 5:30–22:00, last entry 21:30, open all year, adult ¥240 / child ¥120 at the time of the tourism page; renovated **Dec 2012**; source **52 °C**) traces itself to a Kamakura-era lodging for ascetic monks; the tourism page says wounded samurai bathed there. A tourism listing gives Shimosuwa about **8** communal baths around the Lower Shrine's Akimiya (UNVERIFIED, snippet). [K10, K1] — SOURCED, rakuen-shinsyu.jp Shimosuwa municipal introduction sheet (R7 = 2025); suwa-tourism.jp/archives/000685.php.
- **Who runs the baths and why they survive.** A 2023 geography paper (Tsukuba, on another Nagano spa) summarizes the literature: communal baths are "mainly hot-spring baths for local residents," maintained by municipalities or local management associations, surviving as a **shared resource of residents**; Nagano's are concentrated in the spring-source areas including **Suwa**, and are run mainly by **property corporations, property wards (zaisanku) and neighborhood bodies resting on customary hot-spring rights and common property** (Ishikawa 2012); and on **Kamisuwa specifically** (Kimura 2013): survival depends on each district's own upkeep system securing operating money and on residents' wish to keep the tradition, and the bath works as a **"place" supporting local activity — a space for residents' conversation and discussion**. Same paper: in the 1960s–70s about **40%** of households (in its study area) had no bathtub; home baths spread in the 1980s–90s. [K1, K10, K4] [P4, P7] — SOURCED as the paper's summary of Kimura (2013) and Ishikawa (2012); geoenv.tsukuba.ac.jp/~chicho/nenpo/45/01.pdf (Chiiki Kenkyū Nenpō 45, 2023). Kimura (2013) itself not read.
- **Suwa city subsidizes** barrier-free renovation of communal baths by their **managing organizations**: 50%, up to **¥450,000** (FY2024 call, 1 Apr 2024–31 Mar 2025), stated aim "promotion of community welfare." [K8, K1] — SOURCED, hojyokin-portal.jp/subsidies/47512.
- **Shimosuwa (R1 NOT FOUND resolved):** population **18,470**, **8,714** households, area **66.87 km²** (282/km²), as of **1 Jan 2025**; station, town hall, hospital, supermarkets and banks clustered in the center, walkable; winter mean minimum below **−5 °C**; three nursery schools; a town loop bus and a lake-circling bus at ¥150. [K+, K5] [P3, P4] — SOURCED, rakuen-shinsyu.jp Shimosuwa municipal introduction sheet (2025).
- **The kanten drying fields.** The drying grounds are **rice paddies from spring to autumn**; for the winter, producers buy up straw and lay it between the drying racks for footing and to keep the agar clean, then clear it when the season ends. Nights reach about **−10 °C**. Process: seaweed (tengusa and ogo-nori; about **300 kg makes about 10,000 bars**) is soaked, cleaned, steamed in large kettles about **8 hours**, set in boxes, cut with a special knife, then set outside to freeze, thaw and dry repeatedly; racks face **north to freeze fast, then south for the sun**; producers check the forecast many times a day, stack and cover racks against rain or snow, and some read the clouds over **Mount Moriya**. The spent seaweed used to be prized as field fertilizer; few households keep cattle now, so nobody wants the used straw. [K7, K14] [P8] [R: maintenance-by-weather routine] — SOURCED, interview with the cooperative chair, *Nagano Nippō* (?) page reproduced at reglobal.rcast.u-tokyo.ac.jp …/nagano-kankyoujinshi012.pdf (2024-02-16; newspaper title not printed in the extracted text).

### B. Subcultures
- **Kanten makers.** Confirms R1's open origin conflict in favor of **~200 years**: the trade "has a history of nearly 200 years," begun about **1830** when a peddling merchant from Anayama village (now Tamagawa, Chino) saw agar-making on migrant work in Tanba and brought it home; the method is "almost unchanged." In the **2023–24** season **10 factories** ran in Chino, starting **mid-December 2023**; the **Nagano Prefecture Kanten Marine-Products Processing Cooperative** chair (aged 67, head of a producer firm) cites wild temperature swings, rising seaweed prices as warming seas cut harvests and **seaweed gatherers grow scarce**, and a successor problem. The **prefectural judging** scores gloss, shape, weight, viscosity and strength; there is **no taste category** (the chair would like one). [K14, K7, K9, K12] [SM:competence under pressure] — SOURCED, nissyoku.co.jp/?p=991778 (2024-01-31); U-Tokyo-hosted newspaper interview (2024-02-16). R1's "nearly 350 years" (trade paper, 2020) is not supported by either 2024 source.
- **Lake fishers.** The **Suwa Lake Fishery Cooperative** works with the prefectural **Fisheries Experiment Station Suwa Branch**: it culls invasive fish, drives off cormorants by boat, and stabilizes **wakasagi egg-taking**; catches and egg harvests have fallen. Cormorants counted on the lake reached **301 (Oct 2024)**, a record; a dead cormorant in Feb 2025 held about **100** smelt. A working group (cooperative, station, forestry and environment sections, the Wild Bird Society) was set for FY2025; the paper notes birds hurt the fishery **and the smelt-angling tourist trade**. [K1, K9, K14] — SOURCED, pref.nagano.lg.jp …/vision/documents/3-6_1.pdf (as of R7.4 = Apr 2025).
- On **26 July 2016** about **2,531 kg** of smelt, carp and crucian carp died along the north shore (Kamaguchi sluice, Okaya, to the station's branch, Shimosuwa), most likely from oxygen-poor bottom water rising after a cold, rainy day; the **first report came from a fisher** to the station. [K9] — SOURCED, pref.nagano.lg.jp …/suwakosenmonnka/documents/05-01.pdf.
- **Shrine parishioners (ujiko) as an organized body.** For the 2022 Onbashira the guidelines were issued jointly by the **Upper Shrine Onbashira Safety Executive Committee** and the **Lower Shrine Three-District Liaison Conference**. The Upper Shrine cycle runs: felling → provisional haul-out → **lottery prayers (2 Jan–14 Feb)** → **lottery ceremony** (shrine staff, lottery representatives, the **grand representatives**) → **rope-twisting** by gathered parishioners in each district → shaping the timber → *yamadashi* (with *kiotoshi* and the river crossing) → *satobiki*. Each district fields **kiyari** chanters (including **children's kiyari troupes**), trumpeters and drummers, and the events end in a **naorai** (shared feast). In 2022 only **parishioners living in each district** could haul, and people from outside the Suwa area were asked not to attend or watch at all, "so that the local parishioners can protect and pass on" the rite. [K4, K14, K2, K6] [P7, P9] [SM:competence under pressure] — SOURCED, onbashira.jp …/Kamisya_Onbashira_Guidelines_V4.60.pdf (2021-11-15). The snippet figure of **31** grand representatives — UNVERIFIED (search snippet of the same source family).
- **Newcomers.** Shimosuwa's town sheet: many migrants, "surprisingly many in their 30s–40s," "strong side-ties among migrants," and a run of shops in renovated vacant houses (a bakery, a natural-wine standing bar, a candy-beer-coffee shop with a nursing room); a town-run migration center (**mee mee center Sumeba**) and regional revitalization volunteers. [K4, K13] [P9] — SOURCED, rakuen-shinsyu.jp sheet (2025).

## 2 · The Elfstedentocht, Friesland

**Round 2 focus:** the association's founding and money (R1 NOT FOUND), the lottery and the tour rules (R1 UNVERIFIED), how the province and the media behave in a near-miss year, the safety and crowd machinery, the village ice clubs as places, the province's rule that stops boats so ice can form, and Frisian civic life outside the ice.

### A. Other settings
- **The Elfstedenhal (Leeuwarden)** serves as the registration hall: in March (year before 2017) about **300** people registered there in a **trial run** of the digitized entry procedure, at a temporary desk placed at the association's request; logistics and communication are tested at other times. Lessons go into the **"Elfsteden plan-book"**, issued in an updated version **every 1 December**. ### B. Subcultures
- **Members as the public that pays.** The association **refuses sponsorship**: the roughly **€2 million** a tour costs "comes out of the pockets of the **30,000 members**"; commercial messages are kept out as far as possible, **cloths are hung at popular spots** to forestall opportunistic advertisers, and the broadcaster tries to keep advertising banners out of shot. A marketing consultant quoted in the same piece expects that private sites along the route can be bought or rented by firms regardless. [K8, K6] [P7] — SOURCED, *VARAgids* 1-2017.
- **The lottery system (R1 UNVERIFIED, now sourced for 2014):** start permits were allotted by **date of membership**: members from **before 1986** had an automatic right; later members could skate **every other year** and were **excluded in their first year**; about **half** of all members were excluded for lack of places; younger members called it "unfair and outdated"; the **1997 winner** was told by letter he could not skate the next tour, which drew public criticism; within a month the board gave previous winners guaranteed entry, and wider changes were approved for the following year. [K6, K4, K9] [P7] — SOURCED, nos.nl/artikel/2009972 (2014-12-20). The cap was then raised from **16,000 to 24,000** participants (about 2015) — SOURCED, *VARAgids* 1-2017. [K8, K12, K9] — SOURCED, dutchnews.nl/2012/02/skating_race_fever_continues_d (2012-02-07). Soldiers cleared snow from the ice at **Woudsend**. — SOURCED, *VARAgids* 1-2017.
- **Media as an industry:** the regional broadcaster's reporter in 1997 camped three days in the hall; staff worked overtime with weekend leave canceled; the national broadcaster plans about **250** people, **14–15** outside-broadcast trucks (~**70** cameras), six quads, two helicopters and a relay plane; **3,000** press accreditations, of which the regional broadcaster takes 50–100. ### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Crowd and safety regime:** the **Veiligheidsregio Fryslân** runs a **direction group** (municipalities, the association, emergency services); it planned for **1 to 1.5 million** visitors spread over **36–48 hours** along 200+ km (about **500,000** in 1997); Frisian municipalities use **crowd management** to steer the public to quieter points; if the province's capacity is reached, **trains to Leeuwarden are stopped** (at Zwolle or even Utrecht); by car "you need not even think about it." A crisis-management lecturer: "It won't be 100% safe … that is something we basically accept, because we want this together." [K1, K5, K6] [SM:competence under pressure] — SOURCED, *VARAgids* 1-2017.
- **Anti-gatecrasher procedure:** registrants get a **wristband** on registering and showing ID, exchange it for a **start card**, and on collecting it receive a **vest**; skaters without a vest are visibly unregistered. Many slipped onto the ice unregistered in 1997. [K6] — SOURCED, *VARAgids* 1-2017.
- **When the association starts to worry:** the chair: "We only get restless when it has frozen really well for a longer period, **when the smaller and larger tours have been held**. Only at the end of that path do we begin to talk about the Elfstedentocht." [K2, K7] — SOURCED, *VARAgids* 1-2017.

### K6 · Rules of everyday behavior
- **Boating ban in frost (province-wide, Friesland):** to let ice form, sailing is banned on the **Frisian boezem** (the linked storage waters) and pumping stations are slowed; the decision is made **jointly by the province, the water board, the Friesche IJsbond, Rijkswaterstaat and a meteorologist**. ## 5 · Saudi desert wheat on fossil water

**Round 2 focus:** the aquifer figures (R1 NOT FOUND), the olive festival's organizer and age (R1 NOT FOUND), how the quota wheat season runs now, the rules that bind farms and farm workers, and the harvest festivals of the farming regions.

### A. Other settings
- **The aquifer (R1 NOT FOUND resolved in part):** the **Saq** sandstone aquifer underlies Saudi Arabia from the **Qassim** area to the northern borders (**478,000 km²** of the Saq-Ram's 560,000 km² lie in Saudi Arabia); renewability "low (2–20 mm/yr)". Saudi withdrawals from the Saq system rose from about **890 MCM/yr (1980)** to **8,727 MCM/yr (2005)**, 65% from the Saq itself, the rest from the overlying **Tawil** aquifer and a younger aquifer complex; **more than 80%** of Saq withdrawals take place in the east (**Qassim, Ha'il, Riyadh areas**).
