## 1. Magnetic confinement

### 1.1 ITER (SOURCED unless marked)
- **Current baseline ("Baseline 2024"), announced 3 July 2024** (iter.org press page [5]): full magnetic energy 2036 ("a three-year delay"), deuterium-tritium (D-T) operation 2039 ("the start of the deuterium-tritium operation phase in 2039 represents a delay of four years"). The page describes a first "Start of Research Operation" phase with hydrogen and deuterium-deuterium plasmas and gives no date for it. It replaces the 2016 plan, whose first milestone was a low-energy first plasma.
- **Dates restated 9 September 2026** (ANS Nuclear Newswire [49]): "beginning research operation in 2034," "achieving full magnetic energy in 2036," "starting deuterium-tritium operation in 2039."
- **Cost:** "The additional cost for the ITER Organization amounts to EUR 5 billion... a figure that is still under review by the ITER Members" (iter.org, 3 July 2024 [5]). The 35th ITER Council (concluded 21 November 2024) endorsed "the overall approach" of Baseline 2024 and asked for "continued efforts to reduce risks and optimize costs"; no cost figure on that page [55]. **A total ITER cost under the new baseline: NOT FOUND** (queries 1, 51, 55). A search snippet quoting a figure "over $22 billion" via Gizmodo/Scientific American is UNVERIFIED (not opened).
- **Assembly status:** 29 July 2026 press release: six of nine vacuum-vessel sector modules in the pit, "about two-thirds" of the torus core; final module "by mid-2027, or sooner" (moved up from December 2027) [48]. 9 September 2026: module 9 subassembly expected done by end of November, pit transfer "early December"; a thermal-shield repair begun in 2021 was completed in July 2026; five of 93 port-cell bellows in place; bellows welding "scheduled to last for five years" [49]. The same page's sidebar says that on 7 October 2026 "the ninth and final vacuum vessel segment" was delivered to the site [49] (read from a sidebar; not opened separately).
- **Contribution structure:** "Europe contributes almost half of the cost of construction," the other listed members equally (iter.org, 29 July 2026 [48]).

### 1.2 JET (final D-T campaign)
- **SOURCED** (World Nuclear News, 9 February 2024 [13]): final record 69 megajoules (MJ), "fusion power was consistently produced for 5 seconds," fuel 0.2 mg; previous record 59 MJ over 5 s in 2021; campaign ran seven weeks, August to October 2023; JET's final pulse was December 2023; first D-T experiments 1997.
- **Search snippet only (UNVERIFIED)** [8]: IPFN Lisbon release gives 69.26 MJ in 5.2 s from 0.21 mg; record pulse #104522 on 3 October 2023. Wikipedia (secondary, fetched [79]) says 69.29 MJ over 6 s. The sources disagree on duration (5, 5.2, 6 s); the energy agrees at about 69 MJ.
- **DERIVED:** average fusion power in the record pulse is about 69 MJ / 5 s = 13.8 MW (using 5 s) or 69.26 MJ / 5.2 s = 13.3 MW.
- **No Q was stated** for the 2023 record in the sources read [13][79].
- **Highest tokamak Q:** Wikipedia (secondary [79]) says JET attained "Q = 0.67, producing 16 MW of fusion energy while injecting 24 MW of thermal power" in 1997 (cites ITER "Milestones around the world"), and that this record "endured until 2021." The same page contains an internal inconsistency about 0.33 versus 0.67. I did not find a primary document for the 1997 figure in this session. The repo's earlier file cites 0.64 for JET; the 0.64 versus 0.67 difference is **unresolved** (the 1997 published value is often quoted 0.61 to 0.69 depending on definition; that statement is UNVERIFIED, recalled).

### 1.3 EAST (China) and WEST (France)
- **SOURCED** (Physics World, 23 January 2025, updated 20 February 2025 [14]): EAST sustained a steady-state high-confinement plasma (H-mode) for **1066 seconds**, up from 403 s in 2023; ASIPP's director Song Yuntao: "A fusion device must achieve stable operation at high efficiency for thousands of seconds." Heating power was doubled in recent upgrades (no absolute figure given). Update: "on 18 February... CEA announced its WEST tokamak in Cadarache had maintained a steady-state high-confinement plasma for **1336 seconds**."
- Snippets only (UNVERIFIED) [10]: other reports give WEST as 1337 s, plasma near 50 million degrees C; EAST injected 3.05 GJ.
- Neither device ran D-T fuel in these records (Physics World page does not claim it; W7-X also used hydrogen). No fusion-gain claim is attached to either.

### 1.5 JT-60SA (Japan/Europe)
- **SOURCED** (Fusion for Energy, 13 March 2026 [85]): described as "the world's largest tokamak"; first started "in late 2023 for low-power plasma operations" and was then shut down; integrated commissioning has begun; new campaign "expected to start at the end of 2026," running about six months.

### 1.6 Wendelstein 7-X (stellarator, Germany)
- **SOURCED** (IPP Greifswald, published 3 June 2025, updated 30 June 2025 [12]): on 22 May 2025, the last day of campaign OP 2.3, it "sustained a new peak value of this key fusion parameter" (the triple product) "for 43 seconds"; energy turnover record 1.8 GJ over 360 s (previous 1.3 GJ, February 2023); the 1000 s EAST value was "even slightly exceeded" on turnover; "Wendelstein 7-X thus surpassed the best performances of fusion devices of the tokamak type for longer plasma durations." JT-60U and JET hold the highest triple-product values "but only for plasma durations of a few seconds." The June update adds unpublished JET results of comparable triple product to 60 s, making W7-X and JET "joint leaders" for long durations. Fuel was hydrogen (frozen hydrogen pellets); no Q claim. Plasma beta reached 3% across the full volume; "4 to 5%" is needed for a power plant.

## FINAL

### What the sources support (as of 2 November 2026)
- **No fusion device has produced net electricity or an engineering gain above 1.** The highest scientific gain is NIF's 4.13 (7 April 2025, 8.6 MJ yield from 2.08 MJ laser energy on target); the facility drew about 300 MJ per shot from the grid per secondary sources, so the shot returned roughly 3% of that as fusion yield (DERIVED, 300 MJ UNVERIFIED). NIF has recorded 11 ignition shots; the newest, 20 June 2026, yielded 7.9 MJ.
