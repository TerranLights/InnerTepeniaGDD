## What the setting needs (so you can judge relevance)
A fictional Antarctic nation (the Tepenian Federation) in the years 2564 to 2812, population in the tens of millions, national electricity about 101.7 GW, with an orbital tower (about 324 GW net) and a long-range plan to build near the Sun. Real-world technology of the 2020s is the reference, with modest extrapolation. The questions behind this research: could fusion plausibly power such a nation, at what plant size and fuel logistics; is a treaty that bans nuclear explosives and radioactive-waste disposal compatible with fusion; and is helium-3 a physically defensible reason to go to the inner solar system.

## Unit F3 · Plant design, economics, regulation, treaty status → `Fusion_F3_Plant_Regulation_Treaty_Research_2026-11-02.md`
1. **Published power-plant designs** (EU-DEMO, ARC, STEP in the UK, China's CFETR/CFEDR, K-DEMO, JA-DEMO, US pilot-plant studies): net electric MW, thermal MW, footprint, capacity factor, thermal-to-electric efficiency, heat rejection, cooling water need.
2. **Neutron damage and component lifetime:** first-wall and divertor replacement intervals, activated waste volumes per plant-year, waste classes and half-lives (low, intermediate; the published comparisons with fission waste), decay-heat and the loss-of-coolant argument.
3. **Cost estimates:** published capital costs per kWe and levelized cost (EU, US, UK, IEA, national academies), with their date and assumptions.
4. **Siting:** any published siting criteria, cold-climate or polar siting studies, seismic and coastal-rock requirements.
5. **Regulation:** how fusion is regulated differently from fission (US NRC byproduct-material framework and the 2023 rule/ADVANCE Act, UK, Japan, the EU, IAEA); the legal classification of tritium and of activated components.
6. **The Antarctic Treaty and the Madrid Protocol** (Protocol on Environmental Protection): any text, ATCM paper or scholarly article on nuclear power, nuclear waste, or fusion in Antarctica. Quote Articles I and V of the Antarctic Treaty (the first draft of this brief wrongly said "Article 1(c)", which does not exist; corrected 2026-11-02 after unit F3 reported it) and the Madrid Protocol's provisions on radioactive waste and mining verbatim, and say how the literature reads them for power reactors. (The PM-3A reactor at McMurdo is the known precedent: give its dates and decommissioning.)
7. **Proliferation:** fusion-fission hybrids, breeding of plutonium-239 or uranium-233 in a fusion blanket, and published safeguards proposals (IAEA, academic).

## Unit H1 · Helium-3 supply today and the Moon → `Helium3_H1_Terrestrial_Supply_and_Lunar_Research_2026-11-02.md`
1. **Terrestrial supply:** production from tritium decay of weapons stocks, annual US and world production, stockpile size, DOE Isotope Program sales and prices (dollars per liter or per gram), uses (neutron detectors, cryogenics, medical imaging, quantum computing), the shortage history, current price.
2. **Lunar He-3:** measured concentrations by site (Apollo samples, Chang'e-5 samples, Chandrayaan/Clementine/LRO-based maps), inventory estimates (Schmitt, Kulcinski, Wittenberg), processing rate (tonnes of regolith per gram of He-3), heating temperature, equipment studies and mass/power per tonne, and the published economics.
3. **Interlune and others:** contracts (Bluefors, Maybell, DOE, others), their dates and delivery terms, prototype results, and status as of the latest date you can find; the Chang'e-5 "chang'esite" He-3 findings; India's and China's plans.
4. **Legal status:** Outer Space Treaty Article II, the Artemis Accords, the Moon Agreement, the 2015 US Commercial Space Launch Competitiveness Act, the Luxembourg and UAE laws; what the literature says about whether He-3 can be owned.
