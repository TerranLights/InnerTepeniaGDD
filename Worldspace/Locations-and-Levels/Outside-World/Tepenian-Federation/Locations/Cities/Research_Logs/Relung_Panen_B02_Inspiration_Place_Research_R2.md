## Pick 1 — Shouguang, Shandong (passive-solar winter greenhouses)

**Round 1 threads chased:** greenhouse labor and hired workers; named local dishes; festivals other than the vegetable fair; a primary greenhouse count; electricity use; work through the off-season (summer).

### A. Other settings
- **The greenhouse in summer, the off-season (K7, P4):** in early August 2026, during a heat wave, family growers in Shouguang entered the greenhouses before dawn (one worker, aged 77, from 4:30 a.m.; a 19-year-old college student home for the vacation, helping his mother, from 5 a.m.), left by about 8–9 a.m. when inside temperatures reached 46–47 °C, and went back in around 4–5 p.m. One grower: "太阳一出来，感觉几分钟棚里就升温一度" ("once the sun comes up, the house warms about one degree every few minutes"). The summer tasks named are transplanting seedlings (tomato, loofah), weeding (a man of nearly 80), and watching young plants for damage. — Jimu News (极目新闻), syndicated by ctdsb.net, 2026-08-07 (https://www.ctdsb.net/c1476_202608/2823764.html). **SOURCED.** [K7, K4, P4, SM:competence shown under pressure] A search summary of the same report adds "about 157,000 vegetable greenhouses and 120,000 people engaged in greenhouse vegetable production" in Shouguang and says seedling-planting is done by workers from neighboring villages for the house owners. **UNVERIFIED** (not in the text the fetch returned).
- **Electrified greenhouses (closes a Round 1 NOT FOUND in part):** about 20,000 greenhouses electrified, using supplementary lights and combined water-and-fertilizer equipment; "仅使用补光灯增加作物光照，就能让整个大棚蔬菜产量增加20%" (supplementary lighting alone raises a house's yield 20%); per mu per year, electrified houses use 40% less water, 30% less fertilizer and 20 fewer person-days of labor, about 7,000 yuan less cost. Sanyuanzhu village has a smart-grid demonstration area and an integrated solar-storage-charging station; 4,000+ electric vehicles and 529 public chargers in the city (2020). — 中国能源报 (China Energy News), on the People's Daily paper site, 2021-02-08 (https://paper.people.com.cn/zgnybwap/html/2021-02/08/content_2033907.htm). **SOURCED.** The same report: per-capita disposable income 22,484 yuan (2020); bank deposits 128.89 billion yuan, 80% from farmers and 70% of those from vegetables. **SOURCED.** (Kilowatt-hours per greenhouse: still **NOT FOUND**.) [K8, R]
- **The village buying station (K10, K5):** at a buying station in Daotian Town (稻田镇), weighing "opens" at 4 a.m.; growers from the surrounding villages bring cucumbers on loaded three-wheelers onto an electronic scale; card, weigh, deduct tare, enter the day's price; the station serves 100+ households and up to 100,000 jin (50 metric tons) a day at peak, using a city "vegetable production and sales service platform" built by the city big-data center, agriculture bureau and rural commercial bank. — search summary of a Chinese report (the article opened, China Securities Journal 2022-09-05, did not contain it). **UNVERIFIED.** [K5, K7, K10]

- **Salt workers (K14):** the brine salt-making craft (晒盐技艺, 卤水制盐技艺), national intangible-heritage list, 4th batch, 2014, core areas Guantai and Yangkou in north Shouguang; stages are well-digging, building beds, drawing brine, preparing brine, crystallizing; in the Qing dynasty boiling gave way to sun-drying; in the 1950s animal-powered water wheels and wind pumps, later diesel and electric pumps and plastic-lined pans; the craft stresses "精准观察天气变化" (close watching of weather); passed on "行业和师徒制方式，口口相授" (within the trade, master to apprentice, by word of mouth). Protecting unit: Shandong Shusha Ecological Development Co. — China Intangible Cultural Heritage Network (https://www.ihchina.cn/project_details/14633.html). **SOURCED.** [K14, P8]

### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Cooperatives (K1, K8):** Cuiling West Village (崔岭西村, Daotian Town) set up a tomato professional cooperative in 2014; with Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences researchers it grew a premium cherry-type tomato sold at about 40 yuan/kg; the cooperative now handles nearly 20,000 t a year with sales over 1.1 billion yuan; the CAAS–Shouguang partnership dates from 2013, its Shouguang Vegetable R&D Center from 2019; Shouguang was named the first "National Agricultural Sci-Tech Modernization Pioneer County." — Xinhua 2023-12-25 (https://www.news.cn/20231225/3efa9a367a0c45e5b5c1e24d2a3e408e/c.html). **SOURCED.** The same village's cooperative pays about 0.2 yuan per jin over market price, nearly 30,000 yuan more per household a year; another cooperative (Zhongwang Fruit and Vegetable Professional Cooperative) has 165 members. — China Daily (Chinese) 2022-08-29. **SOURCED.** [K1, K14]
- **Food culture (K12; closes a Round 1 NOT FOUND):** a 2020 list of eight Shouguang foods: **Shouguang tiger-head chicken** (虎头鸡; a Shandong dish recognized in 2002, on the Weifang and Shouguang intangible-heritage lists), **Yangkou salted crab** (female crabs from Laizhou Bay, cured by an old method), **Judian Lake loach**, **Tianma melons** (Tianma township; 150 million kg a year, 30+ varieties), **Shouguang pagu** (扒谷), **gangzitou huoshao** (杠子头火烧, a hard white-flour flatbread from the Wanggao area), **Dongguo whole pig** (东郭全猪), **Laohekou white clams**. — Sina, 2020-04-18 (https://k.sina.cn/article_7031515026_1a31c679200100qpzx.html). **SOURCED.** Origin of tiger-head chicken: cockerels slaughtered in bulk in autumn and winter were hard to keep, so pieces were battered, fried and stored in earthenware jars to keep until the next year. — search summary. **UNVERIFIED.** The hard flatbread's dough is said to be too stiff to knead by hand, so it is pressed with a wooden bar. — search summary (Baidu Baike page 403). **UNVERIFIED.** Pagu: one source calls it a mushroom dish, another a steamed dish of stone-ground soaked mung beans with spinach or green radish; **conflict unresolved, UNVERIFIED.** [K12, P4]

### A. Other settings

### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Total Defence Day, 15 February (K2, K6, P6):** marks the day colonial Singapore surrendered to Japan in 1942; observed since 1984 (40th year in 2024). In February 2024 the first island-wide **Exercise SG Ready** ran 15–29 February with 500+ organizations, simulating food, power and water disruption, disinformation, phishing and drone attacks; in schools on 15–16 February: taps and water coolers shut at one primary school and pupils rationed water from shared pails for handwashing at recess; at a girls' school the canteen closed and students got ready-to-eat emergency meals developed by the Singapore Food Agency and SATS, made with local produce; at a secondary school fans and lights were switched off; a teacher: "There is generally a mentality of abundance, as students have not experienced shortages." — Schoolbag (Ministry of Education), 2024-02-22 (https://schoolbag.edu.sg/story/total-defence-day-are-our-students-ready-for-national-disruptions). **SOURCED.** A long-running school custom: recess of steamed sweet potato and plain rice porridge, ration tickets for snacks sold at inflated prices to show a black market, 1–2 hours of lessons without fans or light, windows blacked out with trash bags. — TheSmartLocal, around February 2020 (https://thesmartlocal.com/read/total-defence-day-singapore/). **SOURCED** (a former student's account). [K2, K6, K12, SM:competence shown under pressure]
- **Singapore World Water Day (K2, K3, K1):** Singapore marks the whole of March for water; Singapore World Water Day 2025 was held 8–9 March with community and corporate partners and the launch of PUB's annual conservation campaign; the Deputy Prime Minister's speech (8 March 2025) recalled that in the 2014 dry spell 25 million gallons a day of NEWater were pumped into reservoirs, and that the Water Efficiency Fund has approved 380+ applications since 2007, saving up to 16 million gallons a day. — PMO speech (https://www.pmo.gov.sg/newsroom/dpm-gan-kim-yong-at-the-singapore-world-water-day-2025/). **SOURCED.** Schools (for example Wellington Primary) run games and booths with shopping malls. — search summary. **UNVERIFIED.** [K2, K3]
- **Rules of everyday behavior (K6):**
  - **Clearing your own table:** from 1 June 2021 diners at hawker centres must return trays and crockery and clear table litter (advisory period 1 June – 31 August 2021; enforcement from 1 September 2021; coffeeshops and food courts followed from Q4 2021); legal basis: littering under section 17(1) of the Environmental Public Health Act; first offence a written warning, second a S$300 composition fine, later offences court fines; reasons: COVID-19 hygiene, an aging cleaning workforce (average age 60), and a tray-return rate of only 35% though 76% of survey respondents said they returned trays; no enforcement against the elderly, the disabled or children under 12 who cannot clear tables. — NEA release, 2021-05-14 (https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/mandatory-for-diners-to-return-dirty-trays-crockery-and-clean-table-litter-at-hawker-centres-coffeeshops-and-food-courts). **SOURCED.** [K6, K10]
  - **Water-efficiency labels:** taps and mixers, dual-flush cisterns, urinal flush valves and washing machines must carry a 0–3 tick water-efficiency label before they can be sold, displayed or advertised (Mandatory Water Efficiency Labelling Scheme). — search summary of PUB documents (PUB info site unreachable). **UNVERIFIED.** [K6, K1]
