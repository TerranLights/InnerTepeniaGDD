## Pick 1 · Singapore [PRIMARY] (PARTIAL, not ticked)

### A. Snapshot (partial)

- **What it is:** an island city-state, a unitary parliamentary republic. Official languages are English, Malay,
  Mandarin Chinese and Tamil. **SOURCED** (secondary): Wikipedia, "Singapore", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore,
  read 2026-10-26.
- **Land area:** 744.3 km². **Density:** about 8,290 per km², which Wikipedia ranks 3rd highest in the world.
  **SOURCED** (secondary): Wikipedia, same page. *Not yet checked against a primary source (SingStat or SLA).*
- **Population, June 2025:** **6.11 million total**, up 1.2% from June 2024. **Citizens 3.66 million** (+0.7%).
  **Permanent residents 0.54 million** (stable). **Non-residents 1.91 million** (+2.7%), growth coming mainly from
  Work Permit holders and migrant domestic workers. The 2020–2025 growth rate was raised by construction work
  permits for projects such as Changi Terminal 5. **SOURCED** (primary): National Population and Talent Division,
  *Population in Brief 2025: Key Trends*, https://www.population.gov.sg/population-in-brief-2025-key-trends/,
  read 2026-10-26.
- **Fertility and marriage, 2024:** total fertility rate **0.97** (unchanged from 2023). Citizen births 29,237
  (+1.2%). Citizen marriages 22,955 (−5.7%). **SOURCED** (primary): same page.
- **Ethnic composition:** Chinese 74.3%, Malay 13.5%, Indian 9.0%, other 3.2%. Wikipedia attributes this to the
  2020 census in one place and labels it 2023 in another. **SOURCED** (secondary): Wikipedia. *The year
  conflicts and needs a primary-source check (SingStat).*
- **History (dates only):** 1819, Stamford Raffles set up a British trading post. 1867, it came under direct
  British rule as a Crown colony. 16 September 1963, it joined the Federation of Malaysia. 9 August 1965, it
  became an independent republic, with Lee Kuan Yew as first prime minister. **SOURCED** (secondary): Wikipedia.
- **Defining feature:** NOT YET RESEARCHED as its own question. Candidates in the sources so far include a
  global transshipment port hub, a very dense multi-ethnic city-state that imports nearly everything it eats,
  and mass public housing. *Nothing is concluded here.*

### B. What else exists there (partial)

**Economy**
- **Headline sectors:** financial services, manufacturing and oil refining. Refined petroleum, integrated circuits
  and computers made up 27% of GDP in 2010. Semiconductors were about 7% of GDP, with over 60 semiconductor companies
  and 11% of the global market share in 2019. **SOURCED** (secondary): Wikipedia. *The figures are dated and need
  current primary data.*
- **GDP 2024, current market prices:** total S$731,436.1 million. Manufacturing S$119,463.3 million, construction
  S$26,063.8 million, utilities S$10,834.3 million. Goods-producing industries total S$156,562.1 million.
  **UNVERIFIED** (search summary only, attributed to MTI *Economic Survey of Singapore 2024*,
  https://www.mti.gov.sg/-/media/MTI/Resources/Economic-Survey-of-Singapore/2024/Economic-Survey-of-Singapore-2024/SA_AES2024.pdf).
  The PDF was not opened. Service-sector rows (finance, wholesale trade) are NOT FOUND yet.
- **Workforce:** about 44% of the workforce is non-Singaporean. **SOURCED** (secondary): Wikipedia.
- **Foreign workforce, June 2026** (Ministry of Manpower): Employment Pass **204,300**. S Pass **177,300**.
  Work Permit total **1,243,600**, which includes migrant domestic workers **323,800** and construction, marine
  shipyard and process (CMP) sectors **490,700**. Other work passes **30,700**. **Total foreign workforce
  1,655,900.** **SOURCED** (primary): MOM, *Foreign workforce numbers*,
  https://www.mom.gov.sg/documents-and-publications/foreign-workforce-numbers, read 2026-10-26.
- **Employment 2024:** total employment excluding migrant domestic workers was 3,736,900 by September 2024.
  Overall unemployment was 1.9% in September 2024 (resident 2.6%, citizen 2.7%). Retrenchments were 12,930 in 2024
  (14,590 in 2023). Foreign employment growth was concentrated in construction. **UNVERIFIED** (search summary
  citing MOM via *The Star*, 2024-12-09 and 2025-01-27). Not opened.
- **Tourism:** 13.6 million international visitors in 2023. Tourism contributed directly about 3% of GDP on average
  in the ten years before 2023. **SOURCED** (secondary): Wikipedia.

**Port and shipping**
- **2025 (record year):** container throughput **44.66 million TEU** (+8.6% on 2024). Vessel arrivals
  **3.22 billion gross tonnage** (+3.5%). Marine fuel (bunker) sales **56.77 million tonnes**, of which alternative
  fuels were 1.95 million tonnes (1.35 million in 2024). **SOURCED** (primary): Maritime and Port Authority of
  Singapore press release, 13 January 2026,
  https://www.mpa.gov.sg/media-centre/details/singapore-posts-record-port-performance-in-2025-and-develops-future-readiness-through-industry-collaborations-for-2026,
  read 2026-10-26.
- **Cargo tonnage 2025:** 614.34 million tonnes, down 1.4% from 622.67 million in 2024. **UNVERIFIED** (search
  summary citing BritCham / *Straits Times* reporting). The MPA page I read did not state it.
- **Tuas Port** (berths, completion date, capacity): NOT FOUND on the MPA release; not yet searched separately.

**Airport**
- **Changi Airport 2025:** **69.98 million passenger movements** (+3.4%), above the 2019 record of 68.3 million.
  374,000 aircraft movements. 2.08 million tonnes of airfreight. Busiest day 20 December 2025, with over 223,000
  passengers. Top five markets: China, Indonesia, Malaysia, Australia, India. **UNVERIFIED** (search summary citing
  TTG Asia 2026-01-23 and *The Star* 2026-01-05). Not opened.
- Changi opened in **1981**, replacing Paya Lebar. **SOURCED** (secondary): Wikipedia.

**How food arrives**
- **More than 90% of food is imported**, from **187 countries and regions** (up from 140 in 2004). Less than 1%
  of land is set aside for farming. By the 1980s Singapore was self-sufficient in eggs, poultry and pork; land
  scarcity later cut farm numbers. Strategic stockpiles include rice (under the Rice Stockpile Scheme), frozen
  protein and canned vegetables. **SOURCED** (primary): SG101 (Government of Singapore), *Singapore's Food
  Challenge*, https://www.sg101.gov.sg/economy/case-studies/sg-food-challenge/, read 2026-10-26.
- **Food policy change:** the "30 by 30" goal (2019) was to produce 30% of nutritional needs locally by 2030.
  It was replaced under **Singapore Food Story 2** (November 2025) by **2035 targets: 20% of fresh leafy and fruited
  vegetables, beansprouts and mushrooms, and 30% of eggs and seafood.** Meats, rice, poultry and alternative proteins
  are excluded from the targets. The four pillars are import diversification, local production, stockpiling and
  global partnerships. **SOURCED** (primary): Ministry of Sustainability and the Environment, oral reply to
  parliamentary question, 13 January 2026,
  https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/oral-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-revised-local-production-targets/,
  read 2026-10-26.
- **2024 local production:** eggs about 34%, vegetables about 3% and seafood about 6% of consumption. 153
  land-based and 72 sea-based farms. Local production worth about S$231 million. **UNVERIFIED** (search summary
  attributed to Wikipedia, *Agriculture in Singapore*). Not opened.
- **Wholesale hubs** (Pasir Panjang Wholesale Centre, Jurong Fishery Port): NOT FOUND yet. The SG101 page does not
  name them.

**Water**
- **Four National Taps:** (1) local catchment (rain captured through waterways and reservoirs), (2) imported water
  drawn from Linggiu Reservoir in Johor, Malaysia, (3) NEWater (high-grade water recycled from treated used water),
  (4) desalinated seawater. **SOURCED** (primary): PUB, *Our Water Story*,
  https://www.pub.gov.sg/Public/WaterLoop/OurWaterStory, read 2026-10-26.
- **Shares of each tap, demand volume, the Malaysia agreement's terms and expiry:** NOT FOUND on that PUB page.
  Not searched further before the stop.

**Housing**
- **78.7% of resident households** live in subsidized high-rise public housing built by the Housing and
  Development Board (HDB). **91%** of resident households own their homes. **SOURCED** (secondary): Wikipedia.
  *A primary HDB source was being searched when the limit hit.*
