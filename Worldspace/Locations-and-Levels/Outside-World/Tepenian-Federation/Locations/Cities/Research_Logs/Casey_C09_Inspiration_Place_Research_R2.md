## 17 · Talkeetna, Alaska

Round 1 covered the climbing system, the Fairview Inn, the Roadhouse, the 2014 economy, Moose Dropping's end, the SPUD and the riverbank. This round goes into the settings, groups, third places and civic machinery underneath, and closes several Round 1 gaps (Spur Road date, the memorial's dedication year, 1980s–90s climber totals, the Bluegrass Festival's end, the incorporation question).

### Round 1 open threads resolved or narrowed
- **Spur Road date:** the Talkeetna Historical Society's history (printed in the Chamber's 2025 visitor guide) gives the Talkeetna Spur Road "connecting Talkeetna to the newly built George Parks Highway in 1964," and calls it the event that "would change the town drastically… changing the way most people reached Talkeetna." SOURCED (talkeetnachamber.org visitor guide 2025 PDF, saved and read locally with pdftotext, 2026-11-21). Sketches of Alaska (2015) adds that the spur had "just been paved" by the early 1970s while town streets were still dirt and gravel (UNVERIFIED snippet; R1 read that page). A local history snippet says the road met a "newly developed gravel" spur in 1963 (UNVERIFIED). [K5]
- **Climbers' memorial dedication:** the NPS 1992 annual mountaineering report states that a memorial to climbers who died in the Alaska Range was dedicated in Talkeetna's cemetery in 1992. SOURCED (nps.gov/dena/planyourvisit/mountaineering-reports-90s.htm, read 2026-11-21). This supports the 1992 date over R1's "began in 1993." [K9][P6]
- **Climber totals, 1980–1999 (Mount McKinley attempts; NPS annual reports):** 1980: 659 · 1981: 612 · 1982: 696 · 1983: 709 · 1984: 695 · 1985: 645 · 1986: 755 · 1987: 817 · 1988: 916 · 1990: 1,002 · 1991: 935 · 1992: 1,070 (record) · 1993: 1,108 (record) · 1994: 1,277 (third straight record) · 1995: 1,220 · 1996: 1,148 · 1997: ~1,110 · 1999: 1,183. Foreign share ran 19% (1985) to 45% (1994); 1990s reports list 21–39 nationalities a year. Guided share 26% (1982) to 42% (1986). SOURCED (NPS reports pages 79-89 and 90s, read 2026-11-21; 1989 not on the page read; 1998 total not stated). [K14][P9]
- **The ranger station's buildings:** in 1990 NPS bought property in Talkeetna to replace a **rented log cabin**; in 1991 a new NPS building opened as seasonal quarters, South District Ranger office and search-and-rescue coordination center; a 5,300 sq ft mountaineering center was built 1995–96 and the new Talkeetna Ranger Station opened in 1997 "and quickly became popular." In 1984 NPS announced that a ranger "will live in Talkeetna year-round." SOURCED (NPS reports, as above). A 1998 tribute records NPS volunteer Mike Vanderbeek, d. 24 May 1998, as the first Denali rescuer to die in the line of duty. SOURCED. [K1][K14][SM:competence shown under pressure]
- **Incorporation:** no record of a 1998 petition found. In March 2012 Borough Mayor Larry DeVilbiss told the Talkeetna Community Council board that Talkeetna should start incorporating because borough services were hard to bring up to residents' satisfaction; incorporation would let the town levy its own (seasonal or year-round) tax as a local bed tax, and might let the borough divest the Talkeetna Water and Sewer Utility. At the same meeting councils debated borough ordinances 11-127 / 11-157 on whether non-property-owners and non-resident landowners may be members of community councils. SOURCED (KTNA, 7 March 2012). The Historical Society's own text (2025 guide) still calls Talkeetna "unincorporated, meaning there is no form of government… no mayor or city manager. The Mat Su Borough oversees the schools, snow removal and road maintenance." SOURCED. [K1][K8][P7]
- **Bluegrass Festival:** its "all-time final hurrah" ended on a Sunday just before 8 August 2011 (so about 7 August 2011); the final performances were a tribute to Doc Schultz, "an Alaska bluegrass pioneer for decades and regular at the Talkeetna festival," who died in early July 2011; the Anchorage Press reviewed its run "over the last 30 years" and "how the event grew away from its origins." SOURCED (ADN Alaska Beat, 8 Aug 2011). Start year: still NOT FOUND (a run of about 30 years implies the early 1980s; not adopted as a date). [K3][K9][K13]

### A. Other settings
- **Downtown and its seasonal population.** The Historical Society/Chamber figure: year-round 1,055; "peaks near 5,000 during the summer months." SOURCED (2025 guide). The historic district is "roughly two by three blocks." SOURCED. Seasonal employees "often occupy all available housing in the town center area" (Chamber, R1 UNVERIFIED; now also in this round's search returns, still UNVERIFIED). [K4][K7][P4]
- **Worker housing.** Talkeetna Alaskan Lodge lists employee housing in which "we all follow guidelines and sign a housing agreement." UNVERIFIED (coolworks.com snippet). J-1 cultural-exchange visas are the usual route by which hotels and restaurants bring foreign temporary workers and house them; in 2021 there were none (State Department put J-1 in the bottom processing tier), so "the lodge is taking up a lot of the local people," "everybody's just trying to work seven jobs," restaurants closed a day a week, and the town childcare center (Sunshine Station) turned children away because state staff-to-child ratios could not be met. Home listings were up 40% and sales up 52% in a year; some buyers planned Airbnb use, which a realtor said meant "fewer long-term rentals and fewer housing options for local workers." SOURCED (KTNA, 4 Aug 2021). [K4][K7][K9]
- **The air-taxi workplace.** Talkeetna Air Taxi pilots must attend a week of ground school in Talkeetna in late April and pass check rides; a ski-press article says TAT guarantees pilots two days off a week; a former base-camp manager lived on the Kahiltna Glacier 2½ months a year. UNVERIFIED (snippets of talkeetnaair.com and The Ski Journal). In 2020 TAT went from 45 employees to fewer than 10. SOURCED (ADN, 23 May 2020). Four companies hold NPS glacier-landing concessions (K2 Aviation, Fly Denali, Sheldon Air Service, Talkeetna Air Taxi), awarded on 10-year terms. UNVERIFIED (snippet). K2 Aviation dates to 1979. UNVERIFIED. [K7][K14]
- **Flight routes the pilots share (a working rulebook in the air):** NPS publishes the common routes out of Talkeetna — the direct Southeast Fork Kahiltna route ("very busy in May and June," keep right at "One Shot Gap," minimum 8,500 ft MSL), the long way round when it is weathered in, the clockwise "around the mountain" tour (report position as "clockwise"; do not cross the range unless weather on the far side is verified), the Ruth Glacier/Mountain House route (stay right in the Great Gorge; overfly Mountain House at or above 7,500 ft), Pika and Coffee glaciers; "always monitor CTAF." SOURCED (nps.gov/dena/planyourvisit/flightroutes.htm, updated 3 Feb 2025). The Denali Aircraft Overflights Advisory Council's Aviation Working Group develops voluntary "best practices" on noise and safety. UNVERIFIED (snippets). [K6][K14][P7]
- **Waiting in town (climbers).** Expedition blogs describe standby days: teams "on standby in Talkeetna," "packing in the calories," checking FAA webcam images of the Kahiltna strip (SOURCED, RMI blog, 12 May 2019); teams lodging together at the Swiss Alaska Inn or renting a house, organizing gear at the airstrip hangar, phoning the air taxi hourly for a window (UNVERIFIED snippets of Mountain Trip/RMI/NPS posts). A 1995 account: climbers coming off the mountain "eager to head out for the solstice bash at Talkeetna's historic Fairview Inn… packed, though it was only 4 in the afternoon… Climbers outnumbered the locals"; they used a bunkhouse for showers and ate at the McKinley Deli. SOURCED (ADN, 23 April 2017, recalling 1995). [K10][K7][SM:competence shown under pressure]
- **The off-road-system residents (Hurricane Turn).** The Alaska Railroad describes the Hurricane Turn as a lifeline for people living off the road north of Talkeetna; winter service runs once a month on Thursdays, letting residents run errands in town on Friday and return north on Saturday's Aurora Winter Train; riders tell the conductor a milepost and wave a flag to board. UNVERIFIED (snippets; the railroad's own pages were not re-requested after R1's SSL error; Smithsonian and CSMonitor pages returned 403). About 200 people live near the tracks (single travel blog; UNVERIFIED). [K5][K4]
- **Homesteads and the 1968–73 land rush.** By 1968 the state "Open to Entry" (OTE) land disposal program "was in full swing," letting individuals stake up to five acres "with few restrictions"; the opportunity ended in 1973; a 1972 recommendation argued five-acre parcels were too small for sustained firewood and lumber; Tannerville homestead was first staked in 1962 under BLM rules with a 10×10 cabin; the chapter mentions a 1980s lawsuit by residents over population density and today's weekender conflicts over trail use. SOURCED (Yukon Don Tanner, *Talkeetna Territory Papers* ch. 7, KTNA, 27 Mar 2018). The Historical Society text: OTE "brought a large influx of people… These people were not always popular with the long-time Talkeetnans"; one old-timer is quoted that the town was "just a bunch of 75-year-old men in the early '70s"; most 1920s–30s old-timers died in the early 1970s. Today the area "is also becoming a bedroom community for Anchorage residents accessing their cabins in the woods." SOURCED (2025 guide). [K4][K9][P9]
- **Schools and youth.** Susitna Valley Jr/Sr High ("Home of the Rams") hosts borough ski championships (classic, mass start, 8 km boys, 6 km girls); its rebuilt building (after the 2007 fire) opened 2010 as Alaska's first LEED-certified high school; girls' basketball reached state five straight years. UNVERIFIED (KTNA/MaxPreps/borough snippets). Denali Junior Nordic teaches children at Talkeetna Lakes Park. UNVERIFIED. Talkeetna Elementary's PTA holds a soup sale with the ice-carving event. UNVERIFIED (KTNA calendar snippet). [K10][K14]
- **Clinic.** Sunshine Community Health Center began in 1987 when a group from Talkeetna and Trapper Creek, "spearheaded by local EMT Gail Saxowsky," got a state grant for a mid-level clinic; it "was open sporadically and struggled continually to make ends meet" under an "impromptu board"; in 1999 two federal grants made it a Section 330 community health center; a 12,000 sq ft building opened January 2004 on the Spur Road; Willow satellite 2003; services now include behavioral health, outpatient drug and alcohol treatment, home health, dental, pharmacy and family support. SOURCED (sunshineclinic.org history post, originally written 2005, posted 2023). It administers a Families in Crisis Fund. SOURCED (Alaska's News Source, Dec 2025). [K1][P4]
- **Fire and rescue.** Talkeetna Fire is part of the borough's Upper Valley emergency services; in 2023 only 38 of 195 calls were fires, with medical and rescue calls rising; Trapper Creek responders are part of Talkeetna Fire; new trucks May 2025; a firefighters–borough agreement on on-duty status. UNVERIFIED (KTNA snippets). A 2009 KTNA item reported District 4 short of volunteers. SOURCED (KTNA, 13 Oct 2009; teaser only). Mat-Su CERT team members train beyond initial certification. UNVERIFIED. [K1][K14]
- **Outdoor spaces.** Talkeetna Lakes Park: just over 1,000 acres, management plan 2004; the Denali Nordic Ski Club is the borough's designated Trail Care Crew, grooming ~70 km multi-use and 2 km ski-only trail almost entirely on volunteer hours. UNVERIFIED (borough and KTNA snippets). The **Village Strip**, the original 1940s airstrip in the middle of town, is on the National Register (2001 per the Historical Society; 2002 per Wikipedia, conflict noted) and "is still used today by locals and Alaskan pilots and is managed by the Talkeetna Airmen's Association," a nonprofit leasing the BLM-owned land. SOURCED (2025 guide) / lease UNVERIFIED (snippet). [K14][K10]

### B. Subcultures
- **Climbers** (seasonal, international; see R1 and the totals above): the summer crowd at the Fairview and Roadhouse, waiting in lodges and rented houses; a solstice party at the Fairview in 1995 where climbers outnumbered locals (SOURCED, ADN 2017). [K4][K10]
- **Glacier pilots and air-taxi crews:** a small set of concessionaire companies, a shared route system and CTAF discipline (SOURCED, NPS routes), ground school each April (UNVERIFIED). The Fairview's walls carry photos "of climbers and pilots in equal proportion." UNVERIFIED (Smithsonian Air & Space snippet). [K14]
- **Seasonal hospitality workers:** J-1 foreign students and others in employer housing; locals filling jobs when they are absent (SOURCED, KTNA 2021). [K4][K7]
- **Homesteaders and off-road residents:** OTE-era settlers and their descendants; the flag-stop households (above). [K4][P9]
- **Old-timers, newcomers and weekenders:** the OTE-era tension quoted by the Historical Society; Anchorage cabin owners as a growing group (SOURCED, 2025 guide). [K9]
- **Veterans:** VFW Post 3836 runs a canteen/bar, the Veterans Day ceremony with a soup-and-bread social, a Fourth of July community picnic and pig roast; it closed for the winter of 2010–11 for lack of money after Moose Dropping's cancellation, because the Historical Society had traditionally shared festival proceeds with the VFW, which "usually covered the winter"; fewer veterans were active. SOURCED (KTNA, 4 Nov 2010; 2 July 2010). [K8][K9][K10]
- **Seniors:** Upper Susitna Community and Senior Center (next to Cubby's): Tuesday lunch 11:30, a third-Thursday lunch added 2025 ($14, $11 members, open to all), bingo, Meals on Wheels (~20 residents served, capacity doubled with a second vehicle); meals funded by the Susitna and Talkeetna community councils, the Upper Susitna Food Pantry, VFW Post 3836 and the Mat-Su Health Foundation; a $5 million appropriation for expansion (2024). UNVERIFIED (KTNA and center-site snippets). [K10][K12]
- **Dena'ina:** the Chamber's history page states the town is on the lands of the Dena'ina Ełnena, "specifically, Dghelay Teht'ana, the Mountain People" (UNVERIFIED snippet); an NPS ethnography describes a mixed Dena'ina–Ahtna band, the "mountain people," using the Talkeetna River drainage (UNVERIFIED snippet); the Knik Tribe has produced a Dena'ina story series for KTNA listeners (UNVERIFIED). No present-day Native institution based in Talkeetna found. [K4][P9]
- **Religious life:** St. Bernard's Catholic Church (F Street), Covenant Bible Church, Talkeetna Assembly of God, Church on the Rock North Campus (Mile 99 Parks Hwy), Talkeetna Baptist Church listed. UNVERIFIED (directory snippets). The Free Box began as a church project (SOURCED, below). [K10]
- **Speech (K11):** English; Dena'ina heritage names. Nothing further.

### C. Leisure industries
- Summer: flightseeing and glacier landings, jetboats, rafting, ziplines, fishing, gold panning; winter: dog sledding, skiing, aurora (Chamber guide listings, SOURCED as listings). Denali Brewing stays open through winter "to keep people employed and feed locals" and loses money doing so; it had 55 year-round staff in 2020. SOURCED (ADN 2020). Winter hours in 2025: Mountain High Pizza Pie noon–8 daily; Denali Brewing brewpub evenings/weekends and tasting room noon–8 daily; Flying Squirrel Bakery (Spur Road Mile 11) reduced days with wood-fired pizza Friday–Saturday nights; Conscious Coffee often closes about a month in winter. SOURCED (Edible Alaska, 23 Nov 2025). An end-of-September KTNA report lists the Roadhouse, Flying Squirrel, Swiss Alaska and Latitude 62 going to winter hours (UNVERIFIED snippet, undated). [K7][K10][K12]
- 2020 shock: Princess and Holland America canceled; three-quarters of 126 surveyed Mat-Su businesses lost half or more of revenue; one bike shop's owner said "my entire business plan doesn't have the word 'tourist' in it anywhere." SOURCED (ADN 2020). [K9]
- **What locals do when visitors leave:** Winterfest, the Ice Festival, Raising Spirits, the Oosik, Jeopardy at the Hangar, ski trails at the Lakes Park, senior lunches, VFW, the library (see D and E).

### D. Third places
- **Sheldon Community Arts Hangar:** the former Talkeetna Air Service hangar, sold by Roberta Sheldon to the Denali Arts Council for one dollar, renovated and renamed; venue for theater, concerts, the Bachelor Auction and Ball, Historical Society Jeopardy, winter plein-air classes, a school winter dance, Raising Spirits, and the January 2026 public meeting on Main Street traffic. A life-size bronze of Don Sheldon was unveiled there at a memorial with polka music and a community potluck (about 31 May–1 June 2025). SOURCED (KTNA, 2 June 2025; KTNA 14 Jan 2026) / other uses UNVERIFIED (calendar snippets). [K10][K13][R]
- **Fairview Inn:** officially a bar, 21+ (UNVERIFIED snippet); the 1995 solstice bash (SOURCED). [K10]
- **Talkeetna Village Park:** Fourth of July parade end-point and VFW picnic; Live at Five free concerts Fridays 5–7 p.m., Memorial Day–Labor Day; the idea came from a resident who saw park concerts in a small Tennessee town; the Chamber pays and the Arts Council supplies musicians and sound; the push came when groups wanted family events back in the park and residents "felt it was time to take back the park." UNVERIFIED (KTNA snippets, c. 2010–12; the article page fetched returned the homepage). [K10][K3]
- **The Free Box:** a moneyless community store "geared toward locals rather than visitors," begun as a church project, later an independent 501(c)(3), then board-run; boarded up for reorganization in August 2014 because people treated it "as a dump" (TVs, computers, bags of dirty diapers); the parade traditionally starts in the parking lot "in front of the free box." SOURCED (KTNA, 12 Aug 2014; 2 July 2010). Current status NOT FOUND. [K10][R]
- **Upper Susitna Community and Senior Center**, **VFW Post 3836**, **Talkeetna Public Library** (Lunch on the Lawn; Strong Women exercise group Tue/Thu 10 a.m.) — SOURCED for "Lunch on the Lawn" as a KTNA interview location (KTNA, 18 June 2019) / others UNVERIFIED. **Conscious Coffee** as a locals' interview spot (SOURCED, same KTNA). [K10]
- **KTNA 88.9 FM:** Talkeetna Community Radio incorporated 1988; five years of grant-writing; on air 17 January 1993 from a tower near Scotty Lake in Trapper Creek and a small green-roofed log cabin on Second Street, with one paid staffer and volunteers; it mattered because residents off the power and phone lines relied on CB radios or bag phones. SOURCED (KTNA 20th-anniversary article). A 1997 national-press piece describes a daily segment sending personal messages to listeners without telephones. UNVERIFIED (CSMonitor snippet; page 403). In 2025 KTNA lost over half its annual budget when federal Corporation for Public Broadcasting funding was rescinded. UNVERIFIED (snippet). [K10][K5][K1]

### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Talkeetna Bachelor Society Auction and Ball** (first weekend of December, within Winterfest): formed 1980; the 45th auction was held in December 2025 at the Sheldon Hangar; 2024 raised $130,000, 2025 goal $160,000; "100% of the proceeds" go to women and children in need, via the Society's Fund for Women and Children in Crisis (administered by the Jessica Stevens Community Foundation through the Alaska Community Foundation), Sunshine's Families in Crisis Fund and others; a board member recalled being recruited by women at the Mountain High pizza shop to a free beer-and-pizza gathering of about ten bachelors who were given a questionnaire and told they would be auctioned for charity; needs named: domestic violence, heating oil, children's winter gear. SOURCED (Alaska's News Source, 7 Dec 2025). 2017 raised $24,338 (UNVERIFIED snippet). [K1][K2][K8]
- **Wilderness Woman Contest** (same weekend, since 1986): single women 21+; at least three timed events chosen yearly (past: hauling firewood, fetching water, driving a snowmachine, harnessing a dog team, catching fish, climbing a tree, shooting, and serving a sandwich and drink to a reclining bachelor watching simulated football); prize a fur hat and plaque. Only single women over 21 and Society members may enter the auction room. SOURCED (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilderness_Woman_Contest, flagged "needs more citations"). [K2][K6][K14]
- **Winterfest:** first Friday/weekend of December: tree-lighting in Village Park, Santa, Parade of Lights, Taste of Talkeetna. SOURCED (2025 guide) / Taste UNVERIFIED. [K2]
- **Talkeetna Ice Festival** (first weekend of February; debuted early February 2022, Denali Arts Council): created because "January, February are pretty tough months for the community"; a professional carver demonstrated, residents carved; one competition entry in 2022. Now: 10-lb blocks carved in Village Park, with Jeopardy, art walk, dog keg pull, skiing, block party, concert and shrimp boil. SOURCED (Alaska's News Source, 7 Feb 2022; 2025 guide). [K3][K10]
- **Raising Spirits** (last weekend of February; first 25 Feb 2023, Denali Arts Council, at the Hangar): billed as Alaska's only spirits festival; majority of proceeds to DAC and the Hangar's operations, 30% to the Emmila Denny Scholarship Fund. UNVERIFIED (KTNA snippet) / timing SOURCED (2025 guide). [K3][K8]
- **Other dated events (2025 guide, SOURCED):** Trio Fatbike World Championship (third weekend of March); Beer Trippin' home-brew competition (April, Denali Brewing); Talkeetna Arts Festival (Memorial Day weekend, free); Live at Five (all summer); Independence Day parade (noon, down Main Street and back); Brew Fest (September). [K3]
- **Fourth of July and the remnants of Moose Dropping:** after 2009, the Mountain Mother contest (a Historical Society event since 1991; timed tasks such as crossing the Susitna on stepping stones carrying two bags of groceries; the 20th edition was 2010) and the Moose on Parade (15–20 decorated wooden moose auctioned for the Artists Guild, Chamber and Historical Society) moved to the Fourth; anyone may walk, bike or ride in the parade. SOURCED (KTNA, 2 July 2010) / contest details UNVERIFIED (snippets). Moose Dropping origin: a 1992 national magazine piece said it would mark its 20th anniversary 10–12 July 1992 (UNVERIFIED snippet), implying about 1972; founder NOT FOUND. [K2][K3][K9]
- **Talkeetna Historical Society** (founded 1972; incorporated 1985; 501(c)(3) 1989, UNVERIFIED snippet): annual Talkeetna Jeopardy (January, Hangar), Salmon Run art auction (decorated wooden salmon), annual meeting at the Roadhouse (Nov 2024). UNVERIFIED (KTNA calendar snippets). [K8][K10]
- **Civic machinery and its fights (K1/K9):** Talkeetna Community Council; Talkeetna Parks Advisory Committee; a Riverfront Management Plan completed 2003 and never enacted; a summer-2009 illegal camp on the riverfront (on railroad property) cleaned up in November 2009 by troopers and a borough worker, with at least 30 trees cut for firewood. SOURCED (KTNA, 6 Nov 2009). Tents as shops: over 2011–13 more Main Street businesses worked from tents at the river end of the street; legal under the SPUD if more than ten feet from the street; "until and unless the SPUD is amended, the Borough cannot interfere"; five people injured crossing water to reach the river when high water blocked the borough path. SOURCED (KTNA, 17 July 2013). A 2025 proposal to close Main Street to cars on six Saturdays was not approved by the borough, which asked for a fuller plan; at a January 2026 meeting residents raised employee parking, access to rental homes and the alleys' inability to carry traffic ("We don't have the infrastructure"); a work group of Chamber, Community Council, public and borough was to be formed. SOURCED (KTNA, 14 Jan 2026). [K1][K6][K9]

### A. Other settings
- **Dry cabins (living without running water):** UAF's international office says Fairbanks is one of the only US cities where people routinely live in dry cabins — no indoor running water, water hauled in containers or delivered, outhouses (UNVERIFIED snippet of uaf.edu ISSS). UAF's centennial history: students began living in rough cabins near campus with wood stoves and no indoor plumbing soon after the college opened in 1922; President Bunnell acquired cabins to recruit students; in 1939 three students lived rent-free in a Bunnell cabin near Noyes Slough in exchange for improvements, stockpiling firewood, caribou, root vegetables and berries; from the 1960s campus buildings with showers eased the problem of unwashed students; in the 1970s the counterculture, back-to-the-land movements and the pipeline housing shortage reinforced the lifestyle; "'Dry cabins' remain popular today." SOURCED (uaf.edu/centennial/uaf100/ideas/dry-cabin.php). Water sources: **Fox Spring**, a free state-owned well about 12 miles up the Steese Highway on a two-acre parcel bought in 1966, used by about 2,000 Fairbanks/Fox residents, busy and social with queues in summer, icy and quiet in winter; in town, the **Water Wagon** and the **Watering Hole** sell water at about three cents a gallon; residents cite affordability and say the routine "forces you to use your time intentionally." SOURCED (AP via ADN, 29 Oct 2019). SOURCED (Military Times, 20 Dec 2019). Later reporting (2022) still placed Wainwright among the Army's highest suicide-rate posts and noted "happy lights" being issued. UNVERIFIED (snippets).
