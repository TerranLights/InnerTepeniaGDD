## 1 · Ladakh ice stupas and artificial glaciers

**Input care note (carried forward):** structure only; nothing about the people transfers, by standing project canon. Facts recorded as real-world facts about the techniques, the villages that run them, and the institutions around them.

**Round 2 headline:** the strongest new material is a peer-reviewed village study of Phyang (Sharma 2019), which answers three Round 1 NOT FOUND items at once: Phyang's size (355 households, one-third Muslim), the outcome of the Phyang–Phey dispute (a written MOU of 24 March 2017 ending the contested ice stupa, ratified 16 February 2018, while a second stupa beside the stream was "cautiously accepted"), and the water rituals (monks pray at the stream head every March; a midsummer purification circuit of the springs). Norphel's own 2013 account answers the maintenance question bluntly: of 10 glaciers his NGO built, only 2 still worked, because maintenance fell to villagers who had not paid in.

### A. Other settings
- **The village as an irrigation unit (Phyang, 16 km from Leh):** 355 households; one-third Shia and Sunni Muslim, the rest Buddhist; the cultivated and inhabited area is divided into **nine mohallas (neighborhoods), each organizing the maintenance of its own channels and its own water distribution**; 22 major irrigation channels (yura), earth trenches lined with alfalfa grass or stones, outlets blocked with old clothes and rocks; a dry fringe (the Thang) outside the watershed, long marginal, used for alfalfa winter fodder. In August 2010 a cloudburst washed away much of the village and all 22 channel headworks; villagers repaired most "with minimal external support" within about a year. SOURCED: Arjun Sharma, "Giving water its place: Artificial glaciers and the politics of place in a high-altitude Himalayan village," *Water Alternatives* 12(3): 993–1016, 2019 (PDF read via pdftotext, 2026-10-29). [K1, K14] [P4] [SM:competence]
- **The winter house:** prosperous families keep two kitchens, a winter one (chansa) and a summer one (yarkhang); the winter kitchen is on the ground floor, a combined kitchen and living room with the stove (thab) in the middle, small windows and low ceilings to hold heat, where families also slept. UNVERIFIED (search summary of thebetterindia/thefederal items; not opened). Families had five summer months (April–September) to grow a year's food; vegetables kept through winter in a dark underground root room (tsothbang) or in garden pits 150–180 cm deep under soil; yak meat, usually prepared at Losar, frozen, cut into strips and hung in dark rooms to dry; a frozen-meat dish (shapchen) served with light green tea; younger people are leaving these practices for the market. SOURCED: Shreya Cheema, Outlook Traveller, "Preserving Summer," updated 2023-03-31. [K5, K12] [P4] [R]
- **Schools in the off-season:** all government and private schools in Leh district close for a long winter break, in 2025–26 from 11 December 2025 to reopening on 2 March 2026, by order of the Chief Education Officer, Leh (corrigendum of 5 December 2025), described as "customary in Ladakh due to extreme cold." SOURCED: Voice of Ladakh, December 2025. In some years the break is extended to 15 March for heavy snow. UNVERIFIED (snippet). [K7, K6] [P4]
- **The frozen river as a road (Zanskar):** when the road is shut, the frozen Zanskar river (the Chadar) is passable about three months a year; children from Zanskar walk about six days down it with family to reach boarding schools in Leh (one father pulled his child on a sled); melting sections, floating slabs and cliff scrambles; ~200 people once sheltered in a cave waiting for passage; army helicopters sometimes rescue; a road was being cut ("Nerak 37 km" marker), a charity planned a hostel in Padum so pupils could study locally. SOURCED: John Zubrzycki, *Christian Science Monitor*, updated 2014-07-15. The route was used for centuries by Zanskari traders carrying butter to Leh. UNVERIFIED (snippets). [K5, K9] [P5] [SM:competence]

### B. Subcultures
- **Monks and the monastery as a water power:** Phyang Monastery's head is the Drikung Kagyu leader Chetsang Rinpoche; the contested ice stupa stood on monastery land; in shortages, monastery land and some high-status families may get priority in the water schedule. SOURCED: Sharma 2019. [K1, K9] [P7]
- **Muslim households in a Buddhist-majority village:** in Phyang one-third of the population is Shia and Sunni Muslim; they sponsor their own special prayers in water shortages; every household, "regardless of religious affiliation," owes labor or cash for the channels. SOURCED: Sharma 2019. [K4, K1] [P9]
- **Seasonal migrant road crews:** workers from Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand and Nepal build roads (Border Roads Organization network; Leh–Padum highway via Chilling) from May to mid-October; Rs 350–600 a day; ~10 hours a day, six days a week; 6–7 men to an 11 × 8.5 ft canvas tent for six months, sleeping on rugs on bare ground; rice, dal and vegetables cooked in the tent; little safety gear, drinking water short; at 11,000–18,000 ft. Some local Ladakhi families also work on the crews. SOURCED: Ritayan Mukherjee, PARI (People's Archive of Rural India), 2017-11-03. About 5,000 laborers come to Leh each year from Jharkhand and Bihar. UNVERIFIED (snippet). Tents re-pitched along the road as work moves; Nepali and Bihari workers share tents. UNVERIFIED (snippet). [K4, K7] [P9] [SM:man-versus-society]
- **Women's organizations:** village mothers' groups (Ama Tsogspa), formed from the early 1990s in Leh, ~5,000 members, in 22 Leh-district villages and several in Kargil; village trash collection (twice a year in some villages), crafts (spinning, dyeing, weaving, knitting), traditional medicine, farm and dairy work, "responsible tourism"; they "take stands against... alcoholism." SOURCED: Peaceful Societies (UNC Greensboro), 2017-03-23, summarizing Business Standard/ANI. Stands against dowry and a role in rangeland-governance meetings: UNVERIFIED (snippets). [K1, K6] [P7]
- **Leaving farming:** watershed committees for the artificial glaciers lack people for monitoring and repair because of tourism and government jobs. SOURCED: Tata Trusts *Horizons*, January 2023. [K9, K7]

### C. Leisure industries
- **Winter is the closed season for most work:** schools shut December–February (above); see ice hockey in Round 1. The Chadar has also become a commercial winter trek (multiple operators). UNVERIFIED (snippets only; commercial pages not opened). [K7]
- **Summer archery as sport and party:** in Leh-area villages archery events and harvest thanksgivings run through summer, with chang (barley beer) and dancing between rounds; "mandatory social code" for villages to take part. UNVERIFIED (search summary of travel/blog pages, not opened). In Kargil's Shakar-Chigtan valley, spring tournaments before sowing, with teams formed under elders; winners hosted feasts; archers paid respects first if someone had recently died; target of sand and black yak skin; bowstrings rubbed with butter; bows of ibex horn or wood (Rs 1,500–3,000); traditionally male only, women forbidden to touch bows as a bad omen; since 2021 a coach in Taksha trains girls (200–300 by 2022); village festivals giving way to UT and national competitions. SOURCED: Murtaza Fazily, *The Federal*, 2025-10-07. [K2, K6, K14] [P4] [SM:competence]

### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Water rituals (answers a Round 1 NOT FOUND):** each March, at the start of the farm season, a retinue of Phyang monks treks to the top of the village where the main stream begins and prays to the village's main sky being (lha tho) at the confluence of glacial streams, for steady glacier water; mid-season, the Buddhist community organizes the **bums khor** ceremony, in which monks circumambulate the village, purify it and pray at each spring's shrine housing the underground spirits (lhu brang). **Polluting or privately encroaching on the springs is taboo.** SOURCED: Sharma 2019. The first Phyang ice stupa was inaugurated with special prayers. UNVERIFIED (snippet, Daily Excelsior, not opened; Daily Excelsior was a dead source in Round 1). Villagers decorated early stupas with prayer flags. UNVERIFIED (snippet). [K2, K6] [P6] [R]
- **Household duty to the channels:** taking part in rituals and in channel upkeep are obligations (thal) of every household as a member of the village community (yul pa); decisions on distribution and maintenance are taken by the village community. SOURCED: Sharma 2019. [K1, K6] [P7]
- **Water order by custom:** fields of the Phyang mohalla (the original settlement, wheat zone 3,500–3,800 m, wheat planted by mid-April) are watered first; downstream **Phey may block only headworks 14 to 22, may redirect water from the main stream only after the summer solstice (21 June) or once Phyang's fields are watered, and makes offerings to Phyang Monastery before taking water.** SOURCED: Sharma 2019. [K1, K6, K9] [P7]
- **Phyang–Phey outcome (answers a Round 1 NOT FOUND):** in March 2017 a delegation from Phey went to Phyang Monastery, objecting to winter water drawn for the Thang stupa and summer pipe-water for a poplar plantation; an MOU of 24 March 2017 between Phey villagers and the monastery said the Thang ice stupa project "will be discontinued from next year (2018) onwards," ratified by the Leh Deputy Commissioner on 16 February 2018; a second stupa built in winter 2017 beside the stream, upstream of Phey, was "not contested and has been even cautiously accepted." The study frames the split as: monastery and developers want the Thang as a showcase; Phyang villagers want it for commercial and residential use; downstream users see it as taking water from ancestral land. SOURCED: Sharma 2019. **Later:** HIAL was allotted 1,076 kanals at Phyang Thang on 5 May 2018; the allotment lapsed 5 May 2019 and was canceled 21 August 2025; in a court affidavit the administration said residents and the Phyang Welfare Society had petitioned since 2018 over broken promises about the university, blocked pasture, and diversion of irrigation streams for ice stupas. SOURCED: *The Daily Pioneer*, 2026-10-22 (reports the administration's affidavit, i.e., one party's claims). [K9] [SM:man-versus-society]
- **Norphel's glaciers, maintenance (answers a Round 1 NOT FOUND):** maintenance is the beneficiary villagers' responsibility; beneficiaries should give 10% of costs in cash or kind, but since they did not contribute they "lack ownership" and avoid unpaid repair; **of 10 glaciers built by the Leh Nutrition Project, only 2 were in good working condition (2013)**; cost Rs 3–10 lakh each depending on terrain and canal length (0.5–2 km). SOURCED: Reach Ladakh interview with Chewang Norphel, 2013-05-04. A later program (Himmotthan, Tata Trusts) pays villagers for their labor; **village watershed committees** pick sites, collect materials, build embankments and maintain them; 13 glaciers in Leh district; pools freeze from late November, melt from about April, giving water for 60–70 days of a 120-day crop cycle; up to 2 million liters per embankment; ~1,800 acres, ~1,200 households; in some villages watering went from every 15–20 days to every 10 days. SOURCED: Tata Trusts *Horizons*, January 2023. [K1, K8, K14] [P8]
- **Dosmoche (end of the lunar year):** two days, 28th–29th of the 12th Tibetan month, usually February, in the courtyard below Leh Palace; monks of Takthok Monastery make a large thread cross (dosmo chenmo) believed to trap evil, burned on day two and carried through Leh Bazaar to an open space near the Polo Ground; dough offerings and masked dances by monks of several monasteries (rotating); games and gambling; organized by the descendants of Leh's original Old Town families, many no longer living in Leh; the Stok royal party historically attended on horseback. SOURCED: Tashi Morup and Rigzin Chodon, Sahapedia (undated). Also described as marking the end of winter. UNVERIFIED (snippet, Free Press Journal). [K2, K4] [P6]
- **Losar:** ~15 days around the winter solstice (December), with prayer for prosperity and crops; families inventory stores and prepare dried foods beforehand. UNVERIFIED (snippets). Yak meat prepared at Losar. SOURCED (Outlook Traveller 2023). [K2, K12]
- **K6 summary:** (a) spring taboo (pollution, encroachment); (b) Phey's solstice rule and offering; (c) household labor-or-cash duty; (d) women not to touch bows (traditional, Kargil); (e) the mothers' groups' stand against drink. All SOURCED as cited above except where marked. [K6]
- **K11 speech:** Ladakhi in the villages (oral histories in Phyang were recorded in Ladakhi); migrant crews from Bihar, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh and Nepal speak their own languages (not stated on a page read). SOURCED in part (Sharma 2019).

## 2 · Siwa Oasis, Egypt

**Round 2 headline:** three Round 1 open items moved. (1) **Population:** the official 2017 census figure is **28,088** (1996: 12,952; 2006: 21,693; 2023 estimate 36,574, of which 27,409 urban and 9,165 rural), which explains most of the 25k–60k spread in Round 1 as different dates and loose estimates. (2) **Who runs the Siyaha:** it is tied to the Madaniyya Shadhiliyya Sufi order, whose head is also described as grand chief of the Siwan tribes; on the fourth day organizers split into crews of butchers, cooks and dishwashers. (3) **The caravan era:** until the 1980s road, camel tracks were the only links, carrying dates and olives out and pilgrims through on a Maghreb–Cairo–Mecca route; travel times were not found. New: the oasis is three populations, not one (Siwi tribes, ~2,000 Arab Bedouin of the Shahibat in their own villages, and scattered Egyptian incomers).

### A. Other settings
- **Scale and spread:** the oasis runs nearly 100 km east–west, 5–29 km wide, with settlements around rock outcrops. SOURCED: Valentina Serreli, "Siwa, the Berber oasis where cultures cross," in *An Atlas of Contemporary Egypt* (CNRS Éditions/OpenEdition, 2023). Population figures above: SOURCED: citypopulation.de (CAPMAS census and 2023 estimate; read 2026-10-29). [P3, P9]
- **The house:** most houses have two floors and a roof terrace used for sleeping in summer and for evening meals and talk; a ground-floor winter room; newlyweds live with the groom's parents, sometimes by adding a story. UNVERIFIED (a 2013 University of Missouri student data sheet quoting the oasis's own former website siwaoasis.com, which now returns HTTP 404). Old Shali was built house on house up the hill, up to five stories, with dark narrow roofed streets. UNVERIFIED (same data sheet quoting Ahmed Fakhry, *Siwa Oasis: Its History and Antiquities*, 1944). [K4] [P4]
- **The gardens and the water crisis as a setting:** ~2,000 wells across ~35 km² of cultivation; private wells hidden in vegetation to avoid restrictions; pump wells draw deeper than the old springs and fields are flooded with water that cannot drain ("the ground in Siwa is sick from too much water"); crop range collapsed toward olives, dates, fodder grass and cattle; fresh produce now trucked from Alexandria; government shared wells were ignored, with non-compliance and corruption cited. SOURCED: *Daily News Egypt*, 2010-03-12 (updated 2015-08-19). Historically water was allotted by rotation from the springs, and the system lost accuracy as land expanded, with complaints of unfair shares. UNVERIFIED (search summary; sources not opened). [K1, K5, K9] [P8]
- **Harvest calendar:** dates in October, pomegranates in November, first olive oil in December. UNVERIFIED (search summary of a travel blog, rambulatory.com, not opened). [K7, K12]

### B. Subcultures
- **The tribes:** nine Siwi tribes, three "Western" (Shihayam, Awlad Musa, Sarahena) and six "Eastern" (Zanayn, al-Hadadin, Lehamudet, al-Jawasis, Sharameta, Aghurmi); the town split "organically" into West and East; tribal processes "often favored over lengthy, costly, and often discriminatory court proceedings"; majority Sunni, with "pre-Islamic customs" still kept; Arabic "imposed in all forms of legislation and schooling." SOURCED: Mona Abdou, *Egyptian Streets*, 2022-05-15. Serreli (2023) counts ten Berber tribes across Siwa and the small El-Gara oasis, and notes a revived Berber allegiance with tourism and Amazigh activism (a Tifinagh letter now marks the name of a bazaar below Shali). SOURCED: Serreli 2023. [K4] [P9]
- **Arab Bedouin:** the Shahibat, a branch of the Awlad Ali, settled in the early 20th century, ~2,000 people in peripheral villages (Bahei el-Din, El-Maraqi, Abu Shruf el-Kafrawi); endogamous, sharing a Bedouin Arabic dialect, considering themselves one family; "There are many mixed marriages" (with Siwans). SOURCED: Serreli 2023. [K4] [P9]
- **Egyptian incomers:** settled for work, from various regions; "do not constitute a close-knit group and are spread among the different villages." SOURCED: Serreli 2023. [K4]
- **Garden laborers (historical):** for centuries date gardens were worked by hired male laborers who trimmed, fertilized, pollinated and harvested; workers in others' service wore a distinct knee-length half-sleeve wool shirt. UNVERIFIED (search summary; data sheet quoting Fakhry 1944). Unmarried men and adolescent boys historically had to live and work outside Shali and could not spend the night in the town. UNVERIFIED (search summary; scribd document not openable). [K4, K6, K7] [P9]
- **Women's working world:** married women "do not work outside the home" within a system of spatial separation of the sexes. SOURCED: Serreli 2023. Environmental Quality International (EQI, founder Mounir Neamatalla), behind the Adrère Amellal ecolodge (~US$350/night in 2010, salt-rock beds, beeswax candles, little or no electricity), "directly and indirectly employs 600 locals," hired and trained Siwans rather than importing staff, and built programs "tailored to women who are unable to work outside their homes." SOURCED: Knowledge@Wharton (Lauder Institute authors), 2010-03-01. [K4, K7, K8] [P8]
- **Local healers:** men and women healers who mix medicines, set bones, do Quranic healing and midwifery. UNVERIFIED (data sheet quoting siwaoasis.com). [K14]

### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Siyaha, who runs it (answers a Round 1 NOT FOUND):** Sheikh Abdelrahman al-Demery is described as head of the Madaniyya Shadhiliyya Sufi order and grand chief of the Siwan tribes, and called the festival "tourism of spirit and asceticism"; two Sufi orders, the Madaniyya Shadhiliyya and the Sanusiyya, pray, chant and eat together for three days; on the fourth day organizers divide into groups of butchers, cooks, dishwashers and others. UNVERIFIED (search summary of Egypt Independent and Scoop Empire; Egypt Independent returned "socket hang up"). Origin as told: the eastern and western people of the oasis were at war; a Sufi sheikh, Muhammad al-Madani, gathered the elders of all tribes "and made them all eat from each other's food"; three nights of men's prayer, devotional singing and dispute settlement on Gebel Dakrour; fourth-day communal meal of fata to which "families support either by food or money," then a procession to a mosque where the imam closes the ceremonies; purpose to "announce the start of a new year with nothing but peace." SOURCED: *Daily News Egypt*, 2019-10-23. **Age remains disputed** (Round 1: 160 vs 700 years; this source says "centuries"). [K2, K1, K12] [P6] [SM:man-versus-society]
- **Dispute settlement:** sheikhs of the respective tribes arbitrate disputes between tribes using Siwan law and the Quran, with sanctions of beatings, alms, fines or banishment from the tribe; each family's sheikh leads a family council of ten respected elders; land disputes go to a special committee that rules from old land papers, some over 100 years old; historically judges ruled under laws written by a learned man, except marriage and inheritance under Islamic law, and "no stranger can enter the town without their permission." UNVERIFIED (data sheet quoting siwaoasis.com and Fakhry 1944; the beatings/alms/fines/banishment line also appears in a search summary). [K1, K6] [P7]
- **Funerals (recorded as fact only; nothing derived, per the deferred mortuary question):** a town crier announces a death through the streets; everyone is expected to attend; men meet at the cemetery and women mourn at the house; the family sits in a shelter at the graveyard to receive condolences, and the crier announces whether they will sit three days or forgive absentees; a meal at the mosque follows, which some families reject as an innovation. UNVERIFIED (data sheet quoting siwaoasis.com). [K6] [P6]
- **Weddings:** seven days and nights with tents (UNVERIFIED, Scoop Empire snippet); a henna night for bride and women guests (UNVERIFIED, snippet); wedding day set by the groom's family, the bride's family may postpone 3–5 days; "common to have 2000 people attending" (UNVERIFIED, data sheet quoting a blog). Round 1 (SOURCED, thezay.org) already records the week-long pre-1980s wedding. **A sheikhs' agreement limiting wedding costs: still NOT FOUND** (English and Arabic queries died at the query). [K2, K6]
- **Birth custom:** mother sleeps on the floor ten days after birth; on the seventh day women friends bring their children for a meal that must include salted fish, linked to a miracle story of Sidi Suleiman; children run through the streets and market calling the child's and father's names. UNVERIFIED (data sheet quoting Fakhry 1944). [K2, K12]
- **Ashura and Ramadan:** torches lit on the night of Ashura while children sing; children light lanterns and give homemade sweets before Eid al-Fitr; during Ramadan, Siwans refrain from music and dance rituals. UNVERIFIED (search summaries; Egypt Today 403; Heidelberg-digitized Bates 1917 returned 403). [K2]
- **K6 summary:** spatial separation of the sexes and married women's work at home (SOURCED, Serreli); tribal sanctions and permission to enter (UNVERIFIED); Ramadan restraint on music (UNVERIFIED); Round 1's alcohol and water rules stand. [K6] [P7]
- **K11 speech:** "the majority of inhabitants are bilingual, speaking Egyptian dialectal Arabic and Siwi"; the Shahibat use a Bedouin Arabic dialect. SOURCED: Serreli 2023.

## 5 · Iquitos, Peru

## 6 · Tristan da Cunha (the Potato Patches)

**Input care note (carried forward):** very small scale (~220–250 people). Names of real people are not used except as sources or as holders of a documented office.

**Round 2 headline:** two Round 1 open threads closed. (1) **Livestock quotas** are **set by the Island Council and policed by the Department of Agriculture**: currently **one adult breeding cow per household** (cut from two, effective end of April 2019); sheep limited to seven per household or three for a single householder (one source) against two per person (another; conflict left open). (2) **Ratting Day's origin:** begun "in the early days when rat poison was not readily available," made into a competition "to add a bit of fun" (snippet level; the government page itself says only that it "dates back many decades"). New: the **Nightingale Island trips**, a long-standing harvest outing (eggs in January, young shearwaters for fat in May, penguin guano for the potatoes in January–February) to family-owned huts, which the islanders defended in 1965 by walking off factory work when an administrator tried to curb it. The **Old Year's Night** masked-men custom (silent, always male, a boys' rite of passage, documented since 1907) is the island's set-piece festival.

### A. Other settings
- **Working day:** government offices close by 3 pm, earlier on Fridays; fishing crews leave between 5:30 and 6 am for a full day; evenings go to the potato patches and processing the day's catch; lobster is the main export (5,000 tagged a season by the Fisheries Department); road maintenance and erosion repair; part-time factory work mainly for pensioners; front doors stay open and visiting needs no invitation. SOURCED: Nick Schönfeld and Julia Gunther, NPR, 2026-04-04. [K7, K6] [P4]
- **Government as the island's workplaces:** departments of Agriculture, Communications, Conservation, Education (St Mary's School and an overseas student program), Fisheries (sets crayfish quotas, licenses), Health (runs the hospital and "engages expatriate doctors and dentists"), Police (also immigration and territorial waters), Post Office (mail and stamps), Public Works (three divisions), Tourism & Heritage (tourism center, museum, visitor coordination), Treasury & Finance (budgets, wages, purchasing, banking), and the Island Store (supermarket); the fishing factory (Ovenstone) is separate but works closely with government; heads named on the page are islanders except in Health. SOURCED: Tristan da Cunha Government, "Government Departments" page (undated; read 2026-10-29). [K1, K7] [P7]
- **Farming as a managed commons:** potatoes grown at the Patches year after year on the same plot with no rotation, without the expected pest build-up; surplus fed to cattle, some sent to St Helena; semi-wild cattle at The Caves and Stony Beach outside the quota; limited numbers at Sandy Point; sheep gathered several times a year for treatment and shearing (Shearing Day generally December or January); unknown numbers of sheep on the mountain; stock fences, cattle grids and two small greenhouses kept by the department. SOURCED: Tristan da Cunha Government, "Farming" page (undated). About 420 single-suckled cows with followers and ~800 sheep on the island (c. 2016–17). SOURCED: Alasdair Wyllie (Agricultural Adviser to the Tristan government from November 2016), *Penguins and Potatoes* blog. [K1, K7, K14] [P8]
- **Nightingale Island as a second settlement-for-a-season:** ~20 small huts (~10 m² each) owned by Tristan families, who treat it as a holiday island. UNVERIFIED (search summary). "Up to 15,000 Great Shearwater eggs, several thousand adults and around 50,000 chicks are harvested every year from Nightingale by Tristan Islanders"; penguin eggs taken too, now only one of two eggs per nest.
