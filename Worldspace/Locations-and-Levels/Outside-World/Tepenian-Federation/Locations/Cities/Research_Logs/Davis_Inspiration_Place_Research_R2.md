### A. Other settings (partial)

- **Neighborhoods as described by one resident (2023):** Yuzhny ("South") — walkable to the center, near hypermarkets, short of good parks; Preobrazhensky — pleasant apart from traffic; Vostochnaya Sloboda ("Eastern Sloboda") — fast-growing new-build area with one school and one kindergarten, long waiting lists, schools working in three shifts; Mys, Lesobaza and Voynovka — remote periphery the author avoids. Older Soviet five-story blocks are being demolished and residents rehoused; new-build volume called "unthinkable"; a three-room 70–80 m² resale flat 15–16 million rubles; one-room rentals from 15,000 rubles. Commenters: bridges are bottlenecks for cross-river residents (up to 1.5-hour peak commutes); one school with 34 first-grade classes; the embankment and Tsvetnoy Boulevard criticized as "granite" with no shade. **SOURCED** — T—Zh, "«Обычно представляют суровый город, жители которого умываются нефтью»: как я живу в Тюмени," 2023-10-06 (t-j.ru/zhivu-v-tiumeni/). Single personal account plus comments; one voice.
- **Settlements absorbed into the city:** 19 formerly separate settlements were merged into Tyumen in January 2014, including Antipino (the refinery's location name), Verkhny Bor (thermal springs, see R1), Kazarovo, Matmasy, Tarmany, Roshchino (airport), Gileva, Komarovo, Utesheva, Plekhanova, Metelevo, Taraskul. Earlier incorporations: shipyard-repair territory with the Vodnikov and Dachny settlements (29 May 1958); Parfyonovo and Novye Yurty villages (15 June 1960). The article gives no descriptions of these areas. **SOURCED** — ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Административное_деление_Тюмени.
- **Work and pay (2023 resident account):** official average 45,000 rubles, "good" from 60,000; examples — shoe-shop clerk up to 40,000; accountant 43,000; city-clinic dentist 70,000; tile-store manager up to 100,000; seasonal bricklayer 150,000 in May, out of work in January; 15% regional coefficient on all incomes; regional minimum wage 18,678 vs 16,242 national; of the author's 15 coworkers only 2 were registered in Tyumen, the rest in neighboring regions (i.e., northern okrugs). **SOURCED** — same T—Zh 2023 piece.
- **Rotation workers based in the south of the oblast (fills an R1 gap, at oblast-south rather than city level):** in YaNAO the number of rotation (vakhta) workers rose from 25,500 (1991) to 133,700 (2020); of these 6,700 came from other countries, 91,100 from other Russian regions — **including 5,700 from the southern districts of Tyumen Oblast** — and the rest from other YaNAO settlements. A 2020 survey of **786 rotation workers living in southern towns and districts of Tyumen Oblast and working in YaNAO**, surveyed at home between shifts, plus focus groups in Nadym and Novy Urengoy. Problems ranked (2005, 2015, 2020): low pay ranked first every time; then poor working conditions, inefficient production organization; "impossibility of being alone" in camps named as important. 81.8% intended to keep working rotation 5+ years (38.3% of them 10+ years); under 4% intended to stop within 3 years (mostly aged 20–25). In a 2005 survey: 76.8% saw a positive effect on family finances, 48.8% a negative effect on raising children, 35.4% on health; "a significant part of wives" view frequent separations negatively. Families in the 2020 southern-oblast sample: complete families, two-thirds with many children, 85% married 10+ years; half "can buy a fridge, TV, furniture without difficulty," a third (mainly rural) cannot. Income tax (NDFL) paid by rotation workers stays with the region (85%) and municipality (15%) where the employer is registered, while the workers spend their earnings where they live. **SOURCED** — A. N. Silin & V. A. Yudashkin, "Вахтовый труд как социальный феномен…," *Vestnik Tyumen State Univ., Socio-economic and Legal Research* 6(4), 2020, pp. 95–109, DOI 10.21684/2411-7897-2020-6-4-95-109 (PDF text read locally). ⚠ The 5,700 figure is southern Tyumen Oblast, not Tyumen city alone; a city-only figure remains **NOT FOUND**.
