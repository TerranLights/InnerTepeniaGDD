### A. Snapshot

**NSF's role in CRISPR** (https://nsf.gov/impacts/crispr, **SOURCED**, accessed 2026-10-26):
- NSF funded Jennifer Doudna's foundational RNA research before the CRISPR breakthrough (award 0950971) and has kept funding her work "to refine CRISPR technologies." Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier received the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for CRISPR-Cas9.
- Timeline on the page: 1987, Japanese researchers first identify short repeated regions in bacterial genomes; about 20 years later French researchers show they act in bacterial immunity; 2012, CRISPR emerges as an editing tool; 2018, NSF-funded researchers use CRISPR on cancer-linked gene pairs; late 2010s, methods to control gene expression without cutting DNA; 2019, startup Soilcea partners with the University of Florida on disease-resistant citrus; 2020, Acrigen Biosciences refines CRISPR with anti-CRISPR proteins; 2021, new Cas12a variants for multiple editing; 2022, groundcherry as a crop model; 2023, the STING-seq method.
- Named NSF programs: Enabling Discovery through GEnomics (EDGE); the Division of Molecular and Cellular Biosciences; the NSF Seed Fund (small-business grants to Acrigen and Soilcea). Named places: Duke University's Center for Advanced Genomic Technologies; University of Florida; startup Tune Therapeutics.
- NSF's 2020 Nobel statement (https://www.nsf.gov/news/statement-nobel-prize-chemistry-2020, **SOURCED**, accessed 2026-10-26): Director Sethuraman Panchanathan said NSF "has long supported the research of Jennifer Doudna and her CRISPR lab," including the Alan T. Waterman Award (2000). Relevant directorates: Biological Sciences and Engineering.

## 2 · Materials Innovation Platforms (MIP) [SECONDARY]

### How this pick was read

MIP is an NSF program that funds a small number of national user facilities, each hosted at one or more universities. Per the brief's rule for an institution rather than a single place, this section covers the program itself, then each platform's site and town, in more depth for the two inaugural (2016) platforms and the two 2020 platforms, and in less depth for the two 2026 platforms.

### A. Snapshot

**The program.** A mid-scale infrastructure program of NSF's Division of Materials Research (DMR). It exists because materials research "requires close collaboration of interdisciplinary and transdisciplinary teams and access to cutting edge tools," and it adopts the Materials Genome Initiative goal to "discover, manufacture, and deploy advanced materials twice as fast, at a fraction of the cost." Every MIP must combine four parts: (1) in-house research run as a "closed loop" of synthesis/processing, characterization, and theory/modeling, each feeding the others; (2) an external user program, with at least 50% of instrument time for outside users; (3) knowledge sharing (tools, codes, samples, data, expertise); (4) training, especially of the next generation of instrument builders and users. Awards are cooperative agreements, six years for Phase 1, $18-30 million. The third competition (solicitation NSF 25-521, posted 2024-12-12, full proposals due 2025-05-15) was limited to alloys, amorphous and composite materials. Earlier competitions: 2015 (first cohort, opened 2016) and 2019 (second cohort, 2020). Source: https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/mip-materials-innovation-platforms/nsf25-521/solicitation, **SOURCED**, accessed 2026-10-26; competition years also https://www.colorado.edu/researchinnovation/nsf-materials-innovation-platforms, **SOURCED**.

## 3 · ChemMatCARS [SECONDARY]

### How this pick was read

ChemMatCARS (NSF's Chemistry and Materials Center for Advanced Radiation Sources) is one specific facility: an NSF-funded X-ray station run by the University of Chicago inside a U.S. Department of Energy synchrotron at Argonne National Laboratory. The "place" is therefore nested: the ChemMatCARS sector, inside the Advanced Photon Source (APS), inside Argonne's fenced site, inside the Waterfall Glen Forest Preserve, near the village of Lemont, Illinois.

### A. Snapshot

- **What it is.** A synchrotron X-ray facility for molecular science and engineering, specializing in chemical and materials crystallography, liquid and soft-matter interface scattering, and anomalous small- and wide-angle X-ray scattering (ASWAXS). Located at Sector 15 of the APS, Building 434D, Argonne National Laboratory, 9700 South Cass Avenue, Lemont, Illinois. Funding: NSF grant CHE-2335833 (operations) and CHE-1836674 (second beamline, with contributions from several NSF divisions). Users apply for beamtime through the APS proposal system, complete training and safety approvals, and the facility runs annual workshops and a User Representative Committee. Source: https://chemmatcars.uchicago.edu/, **SOURCED**, accessed 2026-10-26.
- **Beamlines.** The CARS facilities page lists four ChemMatCARS beamlines (15-ID-A, 15-ID-B, 15-ID-C, 15-ID-D) and a chemistry lab; the ChemMatCARS home page names 15-ID-B, 15-ID-C, 15-ID-D and 15-BM. The two pages differ; the beamline list changed with the upgrade or one page is out of date. Sources: https://cars.uchicago.edu/facilities and chemmatcars.uchicago.edu, both **SOURCED**, accessed 2026-10-26.
- **History, size, people.** Began operations in 2002. Nearly 2,000 scientists and graduate students from about 300 institutions worldwide have used it; over 1,200 peer-reviewed papers. A $17.35M NSF grant (UChicago news 2024-11-20) doubles available beamtime by adding a second beamline. Leaders: Matthew Tirrell (PI and director; dean emeritus, UChicago Pritzker School of Molecular Engineering), Binhua Lin (executive director and project manager; James Franck Institute), Mark Schlossman (co-PI and deputy director; physics, University of Illinois Chicago). Source: https://pme.uchicago.edu/news-events/news/1735m-nsf-grant-will-expand-chemmatcars, **SOURCED**, accessed 2026-10-26.

Benedict's method, small-molecule serial crystallography, captures frames across many crystals at timed points after a reaction starts ("take another crystal, initiate the same reaction and take a frame of data two nanoseconds later"). Source: https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2024/11/NSF-grant-X-ray-synchrotron.html (2024-11-22), **SOURCED**, accessed 2026-10-26.
