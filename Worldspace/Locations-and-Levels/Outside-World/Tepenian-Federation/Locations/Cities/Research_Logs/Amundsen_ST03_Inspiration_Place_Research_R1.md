## 1 · S2-07 Central Government War Headquarters (Burlington), Corsham, Wiltshire, England

**Claim tested:** "An underground town in quarry tunnels built for thousands of government staff, with streets, kitchens, an exchange and its own systems; never used as intended."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with refinements.** Built 1957 to c. 1961 in part of Spring Quarry (a worked-out Bath stone mine) for about 4,000 people for up to three months; it had wide underground roadways, kitchens, a bakery, canteens, a laundry, a hospital, dormitories, the GPO exchange, a BBC studio, its own water, power and air. It was never occupied in an emergency, was run down from 1989 to 1991, emptied of fuel and food, and declassified in December 2004. Refinements: (a) capacity figures differ by source (planned "over 7,000" in the 1957 scheme per one timeline; "4,000 to 5,000" per another; "about 4,000" per the MoD's own booklet); (b) the name "town" is the press's; the MoD booklet calls it an "underground city"; (c) the site was not a new excavation but a wartime underground aircraft-engine factory reused.

### A. Snapshot
- **Location:** beneath MoD Corsham (former Basil Hill Barracks), Hawthorn, near Corsham, Wiltshire; about 28 m (Ringbell) to 37 m / 120 ft (Wikipedia) below ground; between the B3109 Bradford Road and Westwells Road, just south of Brunel's Box railway tunnel. SOURCED (Ringbell; Wikipedia).
- **Size:** about 35 acres (14 ha); over 1 km long; Ringbell gives about 700 m east-west by 275 m north-south. The wider Corsham underground (Spring, Tunnel and adjoining quarries) is far larger: the 2005 DefenceTalk article gives "240 acres of rooms, corridors and roadways"; a 1957 conversion area of about 2,000,000 sq ft is given by the Higgypop timeline. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Ringbell, DefenceTalk, Higgypop); the 60-mile road figure in Wikipedia is UNVERIFIED as to scope (it likely refers to the whole quarry complex).
- **What it is:** the UK's Cold War Central Government War Headquarters (CGWHQ), built for the Prime Minister, Cabinet, civil servants and support staff to run the country after a nuclear attack. SOURCED (MoD booklet; Wikipedia).
- **Capacity:** "some 4,000 Central Government personnel, including the Prime Minister" (MoD booklet); "up to 4,000 people in isolation for up to three months" (Wikipedia); "4,000 to 5,000" (Ringbell); the 1957 scheme provided room "for over 7,000 staff and about 1,000,000 sq ft of office space" (Higgypop timeline, no source named: UNVERIFIED). SOURCED for 4,000.
- **Code names:** Subterfuge (1951-1959), Stockwell (1959-1961), Burlington (1961-1963), Turnstile (1963-1970), Chanticleer (1970-1987), Peripheral (1987-1992), plus "Site 3". Dated sequence SOURCED from Ringbell only; the Higgypop timeline gives slightly different dates (Burlington from 1961, Turnstile from 1963). Treat the exact years as one-source.
- **Defining feature:** a complete, self-supplied underground government town inside an old stone mine that was kept ready for about three decades and never used.

### B. What else exists there (the site and the town above)
**The quarry's earlier lives (D for the bunker):**
- Bath stone (oolitic limestone) quarried locally since Roman times; underground quarrying boomed 1850-1910 after the Great Western Railway and Box Tunnel opened access to the deep beds. SOURCED (MoD booklet). The Box Tunnel opened June 1841; about 4,000 men and 300 horses were deployed at the end; a ton of gunpowder and a ton of candles were used per week; unemployed tunnel masons started the quarrying industry. SOURCED (Corsham Town Council). [K14]
- 1935: the War Office approved three ammunition sub-depots (Tunnel Quarry, Monkton Farleigh, Eastlays Ridge) forming the Central Ammunition Depot; first "district" ready April 1938; Tunnel Quarry had ten districts of about five acres each, an underground standard-gauge railway with two platforms, about 7.5 miles of conveyor belt (from 1940), and fans giving a stable 65°F and 80% humidity. SOURCED (MoD booklet). [K5]
- 1941: an underground barrack block for 300 personnel at Tunnel Quarry, described by the *RAOC Gazette* as "magnificent," with bars and messing areas, a power station with two diesel generators "capable of supplying power to a small town," an underground lake for drinking water, sewerage and flood pumps; "during the Second World War many personnel remained underground for long periods to help keep the installation a secret." SOURCED (MoD booklet). [K4, K5, P4] [R]
- **Spring Quarry underground factory (the shell the bunker reused):** requisitioned 7 Dec 1940 after September 1940 raids on Bristol killed 160 workers; planned at £100,000 and six months; work started April 1941; eventual cost reputedly £19-20 million; planned for the Bristol Aeroplane Company's engine works from Filton, Parnall gun turrets and Dowty undercarriages; Parnall and Dowty declined as raids eased; BAC used only part of its area, for Centaurus engines; BSA made cannon barrels for about 18 months; only about 523 engines are believed to have been built there. Two escalators were taken from Holborn and St Paul's Underground stations and four large passenger lifts installed; gas-sealed ventilation, special lighting, compressors, boiler houses, and a new telephone exchange named Hawthorn. SOURCED (Box People and Places, citing McCamley and the Bath Weekly Chronicle). [K9, K14] [SM: a dangerous or fascinating figure or system]
- **Wartime workforce and housing:** clearance contractor McAlpine "recruited 10,000 navvies, mostly from the Irish Republic"; workers came from Bristol, miners from northern England and Wales. The plan for 14,000 workers called for eight hostels at Rudloe and Corsham (1,000 each), 1,300 married quarters at Hawthorn, and the rest bussed from Bath; Bristol workers refused to move, so only about half the houses were built; prefabricated huts went up in Corsham in 1942 for bombed-out Bristol workers and were later redeveloped as 1950s-60s council estates; bus route 50a from 1944 ran three times daily each way Bath-Box-Hawthorn-Corsham, separate from civilian travel. SOURCED (Box People and Places). The McAlpine/Irish-navvy figure is one-source: a separate search found no second confirmation (UNVERIFIED beyond that page). Corsham's population rose from 3,754 (1931) to 9,268 (1951), a rise Wikipedia attributes to incoming military personnel; no 1941 census. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Corsham). Canteens: Historic England is quoted (search snippet) as giving canteens for "c10,000 workers"; another site gives canteens for 6,000 at a sitting and up to 20,000 users (UNVERIFIED, snippet only). [K4, K5, K7, P9]
- Corsham Regis Primary School opened in 1943 for the children of incoming military workers. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Corsham). [K4]

**The CGWHQ itself (layout by area, as of 1981, per Wikipedia):** Area 1 air filtration (originally GPO area); Area 6 kitchen and bakery; Areas 7 and 12 canteens (12 also a laundry); Area 8 telephone exchange; Area 9 hospital and stores (originally a dormitory); Area 11 water treatment and stores; Area 14 Prime Minister, War Cabinet, Cabinet Secretariat and Chiefs of Staff offices and dormitory; Area 15 Camp Commandant, establishment offices and the Lamson room; Area 16 Central Office of Information, Ministry of Health, Home Office and a BBC studio; Area 17 Ministry of Labor and "special accommodation" suites with private bathrooms; Area 19 workshops and power generation; Area 21 communications center; unnumbered Areas 23 and 24 water and fuel storage. 22 numbered areas in all. SOURCED (Wikipedia, which cites Catford 2012 and Historic England). [P5]
- Plan of 1959 (Higgypop timeline): a wide roadway through the site; north side for the exchange, workshops, air conditioning, catering and domestic facilities; south side for departmental offices and living areas; central east for Cabinet and executive functions; an unoccupied buffer zone separated it from the Admiralty stores in the rest of Spring Quarry. UNVERIFIED (one secondary source, no citation).
- **Systems:** underground lake and treatment plant for water; an underground power station with four generators and twelve fuel tanks for up to three months; air kept at constant humidity and about 20°C; a large butterfly valve to shut off outside air against fallout or gas; the second-largest telephone exchange in Britain; a Lamson pneumatic-tube message system; a BBC studio for the PM to address the nation. SOURCED (Wikipedia; Amusing Planet captions on MoD photos). The 2018 Higgypop write-up of an official MoD video lists two fuel tanks and two large generators in the part filmed. [K5] [R]
- **Communications:** internal switchboard named "Woodland"; given engineering code QQCF so its circuits blended into thousands of others; linked to the Emergency Manual Switching System (about 300 switchboards); a 1968 document lists speech and teleprinter circuits to military communications centers and abroad. SOURCED (Ringbell). [K1, K14]
- **Movement in:** about 4,000 essential staff were to assemble at a checkpoint at Warminster and go on by army lorry; about 210 senior Whitehall officials by special train from Kensington (Olympia) to Warminster, then bus and a 23-mile lorry trip; the PM by helicopter at the last moment. SOURCED (Wikipedia). [K5]
- **Moving about inside:** a fleet of electric buggies, charged in the workshop area (MoD video write-up, SOURCED via Higgypop 2018); visitors in 2007 were driven round on electric carts on roads "not flat or straight" (Gengriz, SOURCED); "American-style" street naming and road signs (Higgypop 2017, Bath Chronicle 2021; snippets only, UNVERIFIED). [K5] [R]
- **Living quarters seen on tours:** dormitory areas with yellow wardrobes for staff belongings; communal washrooms with showers and rows of basins; the PM's dormitory one of the few whitewashed rooms, with a private bathroom; an infirmary with five small wards of one or two beds, a sluice room and its own small kitchen; a main canteen with a 1950s copper automatic coffee machine; a "huge" kitchen; a laundry described in the video as the biggest in Europe at the time; stores of furniture, wrapped swivel chairs, government notepaper; telephone directories kept current until 1989. SOURCED (Higgypop 2018 video write-up; Gengriz 2007). Catford's 2012 book is reported (search snippet) to list a canteen with 225 tables and 2,320 dinner plates: UNVERIFIED (book not read). [P3, P4]

**The town above (Corsham today):**
- Population 13,369 (2021 census); market town and civil parish 4 mi SW of Chippenham and 8 mi NE of Bath; Corsham Town Council (20 councillors); Wiltshire Council unitary authority. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Corsham).
- Employers: the MoD employs about 2,000; MOD Corsham (Basil Hill) holds about 2,100 people, UK Strategic Command's Defense Digital and the Global Operations Security Control Center; Hawthorn site on Skynet Drive supports the Skynet military satellite constellation (managed by Astrium under PFI). SOURCED (Wikipedia, Corsham). In 2001 the MoD gave 1,116 staff on the Corsham estate (DCSA 527, other units 219, Rudloe/JSU 240, plus 130 business partners), projected to rise to 1,380 DCSA plus 194 others plus 280 partners under the Corsham Development Project. SOURCED (Hansard 11 Dec 2001). [K7, K8]
- Underground Bath stone mining continues: Hartham Park mine (now worked by Lovell Stone Group; "the oldest of the still-operational Bath Stone mines") and Monks Park at Gastard (Hanson, reopened after dormancy since 2004). SOURCED from search snippets of stonespecialist.com and Heidelberg Materials (pages not opened: UNVERIFIED). [K14]
- Other underground reuse: Octavian Vaults wine storage at Eastlays near Gastard (former mine and MoD depot), kept at about 12-14°C (sources vary); Corsham Media Park (opened 2001 beside RAF Rudloe Manor) with The Bath Studios sound stages, and a claimed underground studio area in the west of Spring Quarry; Ark Data Centers' Spring Park campus on Westwells Road (first building SQ17 live 2010) on former MoD surface land (not underground). Wine and media park facts SOURCED in part (Wikipedia, Corsham); temperatures and media-park underground claims UNVERIFIED (snippets). Copenacre (Royal Naval Stores Depot from 1937) closed underground stores in 1995, the site in January 2011, sold, housing demolition from 2016. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Corsham).
- Schools: The Corsham School (1,264 pupils, July 2024), four primaries, Heywood Prep (about 260), Wiltshire College site. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Corsham).

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- The mine's stable climate and depth produced a chain of reuses: stone mine, ammunition depot, aircraft-engine factory, naval stores, nuclear HQ, wine vaults, data and archive storage, film space. The MoD booklet lists "storage of data/archive, wine and cold stores" as planned futures. SOURCED. [K+ reuse chain] [R]
- The factory canteens produced the murals, the only art made for the underground workforce; the murals survive because the space was sealed and unused. SOURCED.
- Secrecy shaped the town: the bunker's only public trace was a mound over passenger lift PL1 on Westwells Road (Ringbell, SOURCED); the MoD workforce made Corsham a garrison town (Corsham Civic Society page "Corsham - a Garrison Town" found but its certificate failed, NOT READ).
- Decay: mold, rot, damp and roof falls; scheduled items placed on Historic England's Heritage at Risk Register (Wikipedia says October 2015, SOURCED; Ringbell says mainly because of damp, SOURCED); the 2007 visitor noted radon, rot spores, dust and asbestos, and large areas closed for safety (Gengriz, SOURCED). [K9] [P3]

## 2 · S2-06 Beijing's Underground City (Dixia Cheng) and basement dwellings

**Claim tested:** "A Cold War tunnel network under the capital, and the many people who later lived in basement and air-raid-shelter rooms."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with refinements.** (a) The Underground City (北京地下城) was dug from 1969 to about 1979 in the Sino-Soviet crisis by ordinary residents organized by factories and street committees; it was commercialized from 1978-1980, a foreign-visitor attraction (official opening to foreigners usually dated 2000; a crafts shop operated from 1980), closed in 2008, and **reopened on 12 May 2026** as the Beijing Civil Air Defense History and Culture Museum (北京人民防空历史文化馆). (b) The basement population was mostly **not** in the Dixia Cheng tunnels: it lived in two other kinds of space, ordinary basements of residential buildings (普通地下室) and civil-air-defense works (人防工程) attached to buildings, which every new Beijing building has had to include since the 1950s. Estimates of residents run from 150,000 to 2 million; about 1 million is the figure most often given. (c) Clearances came in waves: a 2010 plan, repeated "three-year actions," the 2017-2020 citywide program to end scattered rental of basements, and the 40-day safety campaign after the 18 November 2017 Daxing fire; rentals partly returned in 2018.

### A. Snapshot
- **Beijing:** permanent population 21.832 million at end-2024 (down 26,000 on the year), urban 19.26 million (88.2%); GDP 4.98431 trillion yuan; per-capita disposable income 85,415 yuan (urban 92,464, rural 39,856); surveyed urban unemployment 4.1%. SOURCED (Beijing Daily, 20 Mar 2025, from the municipal statistics communiqué). The citywide count of non-local ("外来") permanent residents was not in the communiqué; Dongcheng district, where the Underground City lies, had 701,000 residents of whom 148,000 (21.1%) were non-local (search snippet of the Dongcheng communiqué: UNVERIFIED).
- **The Underground City:** in the Qianmen East area of Dongcheng district, south of Tiananmen Square; historic entrance at 62 Xidamochang Street (西打磨厂街62号); since August 2026 the entrance is on Xixinglong Street (西兴隆街), the old Tongle Hutong entrance closed. SOURCED (BJD 2026; HK01 2026). Wikipedia gives a claimed network of about 85 km² at 8-18 m depth with about 90 entrances once hidden in Qianmen shops, about 2,300 sealable ventilation shafts and nearly 70 potential well sites (SOURCED as Wikipedia's claims; scale UNVERIFIED and disputed: a travel guide gives "over 30 km long"). HK01 (2026) says the Qianmen works connected with shelters of the four old inner districts, to subway lines 1 and 2, south to the Temple of Heaven, west to Dashilan and east to Chongwenmen; with a staff canteen, shops, hospital, pharmacy, stores, power station, wells and filtered ventilation (SOURCED as reported). The 2026 museum's underground exhibition space is about 2,500 m². SOURCED (BJD 12 May 2026).
- **Basement housing:** Kim (2014) estimates about one million people in a city then of 23 million; median unit 9.75 m² (below Beijing's 10 m² minimum; citywide average 28.8 m² per person; worker dormitories 6.2 m²); mean rent 436 RMB (about US$70) per month; some complexes up to 600 units below street level; about half one or two floors down, half semi-basement with a small window near the ceiling. SOURCED (Kim, Lincoln Institute). CNN Japan (2015) gives estimates from 200,000 to 2 million (SOURCED). A 2011 China News report, as seen in search snippets, cites Beijing housing-commission data of 2009 of nearly 800,000 people living in about 17,000 ordinary basements plus about 150,000 migrants in civil-air-defense works (UNVERIFIED: the page fetched was garbled).
- **Defining feature:** a capital with a dug Cold War shelter network and a legal duty to build shelter space under every building, whose underground was rented as low-cost housing for a migrant workforce, then progressively cleared.

### B. What else exists there
**How the Underground City was built [K7, K14, P8]:**
- 27 Aug 1969: the central leadership approved the national civil air defense leading group headed by Zhou Enlai; the "deep digging" campaign followed under the slogan "深挖洞、广积粮、不称霸" ("dig tunnels deep, store grain widely, never seek hegemony"). SOURCED (BJD 18 Aug 2026; HK01).
- From 18 October to 18 November 1969 an average of 300,000 people a day across the city joined the "war-preparation labor," per the *Beijing Gazetteer, Military Volume, Civil Air Defense Gazetteer* (《北京志·军事卷·人民防空志》). SOURCED (HK01 citing the gazetteer).
- Ordinary residents of factories and street committees dug with shovels, baskets, picks, winches and shafts. At one gear factory (1971-1973) each workshop sent people in rotation, three shifts round the clock, for nearly three years; factory tunnels were dug in sections from both ends to "meet." Bricks came from demolished screen walls and old city-wall bricks; street committees organized residents to haul brick blanks and fire them in small kilns. SOURCED (BJD 18 Aug 2026). Wikipedia adds that Xizhimen, Fuchengmen and Chongwenmen gates and walls were demolished for material (SOURCED as Wikipedia's claim). [K9]
- "Half a square meter per person" was the building standard of the first phase (1969-1973): shelter area at 0.5 m² per resident, per a participant's recollection. From 1974 new shelters were generally 8 m deep; one eastward passage runs about 15-16 m under today's East Third Ring Road. In 1965 the city completed 17,544 m² of air-defense basements, and 6,673 shelters or trenches were built between 28 Dec 1965 and 26 Jan 1966 (museum display). SOURCED (BJD 18 Aug 2026).

**Peacetime use, 1978 onward [K8, K12, P4]:**
- 13 Oct 1978: Deng Xiaoping told the preparatory meeting for the third national civil-air-defense conference that shelters must be "平战结合" (usable in war and used in peace). SOURCED (BJD 18 Aug 2026 "别有洞天").
- 30 Apr 1980: a street-committee underground hostel in Xicheng opened on trial (former street war-preparation command post); 60,000 yuan invested, employing more than 20 jobless youths, cost recovered in two years; one woman who joined in 1979 after returning from rural rustication worked there 30-plus years until retiring in 2013. UPI's Raymond Wilkinson reported (13 Oct 1980) that 58 shelters had become hostels with 6,000 beds, about US$1 per person per night, six iron beds to a room with running water, toilets and heating. *Beijing Daily* reported on 21 Apr 1983 that underground hostel beds citywide reached 13,931, about 70% of above-ground public-hostel beds. SOURCED (BJD 18 Aug 2026). [K4, K5]
- Other uses reported in the same article: underground restaurants (some feeding surface factories), miles of tunnel rented as shop storerooms, conference halls turned into cinemas and theaters (the Chongwen cinema at the Temple of Heaven east gate, opened 1981, with a 2,500 m² underground hall), a Western restaurant at Xidan, the Tianwaitian small-goods market under Yuetan Park (about 500 million yuan a year in trade), a ring-shaped skating rink under Ditan Park drawing over a thousand young people a day, plus oil and grain stores, mushroom growing and garment work. SOURCED (BJD 18 Aug 2026). [K10, K12, K13]
- 1980: the Qianmen Arts and Crafts Service Department (前门工艺美术服务部) opened in the Qianmen shelter; its entrance plaque "北京地下城" gave the whole area its name; it sold writing materials, silk, porcelain, jade and Tongrentang medicines to foreign visitors, more than 900,000 of whom visited. SOURCED (BJD 2026; HK01). Later the tour showed a working silk workshop in a former staff meeting room (Qianmen Arts and Crafts Center and China Kai Tian Silk Company); entry 20 yuan; bare bulbs; about 18°C year round; signs to Tiananmen and the Forbidden City; rooms labeled as cinemas, hospitals, arsenals; murals of volunteer diggers; areas once used for mushrooms and vegetables, some rooms rented as cheap accommodation. SOURCED (Wikipedia; Hultengren visit report). [K12]
- Closure and return: closed by February 2008 (notice cited a safety inspection; Wikipedia, SOURCED); 2019 micro-exhibition hall at 110 Xixinglong Street; reopened 12 May 2026 as the Beijing Civil Air Defense History and Culture Museum, a joint project of the Beijing National Defense Mobilization Office and the Dongcheng government; Tuesday-Sunday 9:00-17:00, closed Monday; free with online or on-site reservation; four sections on civil-defense history; the main hall (the former assembly hall) displays models of a J-20 fighter, Long March rockets, a Shenzhou return capsule and a lunar rover, plus VR; positioned for national-defense and "red" education. Closed 20 July for entrance works; reopened 1 Aug 2026 with a new shooting-experience item; "tens of thousands" of visitors by 20 July. SOURCED (BJD 12 May 2026; HK01). [K3, K8]

**Basement dwellings: law, landlords, residents [K1, K4, K6, P9]:**
- Rule history per Kim (2014): from 1950 new Beijing buildings had to include common basements and air-defense shelters; for decades official policy encouraged "economic" peacetime use, and residential use was sanctioned (1986 implementing rules, 15 May); August 2010 a three-year plan to evacuate underground tenants and an end to new use permits; evictions began in 2011 in the inner districts. SOURCED. Gizmodo (2014): in the 1980s, with rapid population growth, landlords were allowed to convert basements into residential units. SOURCED (as reported).
- Who lived there: mostly single people or couples without children, staying no more than a few years; largely low-income migrants without Beijing *hukou*, plus some low-income Beijing *hukou* holders (Kim, SOURCED). Huang and Yi (2015) describe "hundreds of thousands" in "crowded, dark basements" and argue that *hukou*, derogatory naming and "purification" of migrant space make housing an institution of exclusion; they call basement renting "vertical spatial marginalisation" (abstract, SOURCED). Occupations in reporting: cleaners, repair staff, shop and service workers, small vendors (Beijing Youth Daily 2012 via Sina, page partly garbled: UNVERIFIED); telemarketers and recent arrivals in their 20s and 30s (2011 report, SOURCED); a bar worker's coworkers lodging together, a chef apprentice sharing a room with three coworkers (SCMP via search snippet: UNVERIFIED).
- Ads (Kim, 2012-13, 3,677 complete of 7,312 ads on Ganji.com): about 25% advertised heating, over 50% internet, about 25% surveillance cameras, under 12.5% guards; units averaged under 11 km from the center and just over 1 km from a subway station; depth made no significant price difference; rent rose about 3.6% per km closer to the center. SOURCED. [K5]
- **A documented basement (Sanhuan Xincheng, Fengtai, 2011):** a two-level underground mall in Building 11 partitioned into 117 units: landlord rooms about 20 m², shared rooms about 11 m², singles 4-6 m²; rent 200-800 yuan a month; more than 1,000 people in the complex's basements; water 10 yuan per person per month; hot water bought by card; showers paid by use (about 2-5 yuan); shoes dried on the first-level stairwell where air moved; clothes hung indoors often stolen; "no clear day or night"; some households there more than three years; a duty office on level 2 posting quiet notices and, at month's end, the names of tenants who had not paid. SOURCED (China Digital Times reprint of a 21 Oct 2011 report). [K4, K6, K10] [R]
- **Subcontracting chain (Qisheng Jiayuan, Changping, 2012):** developer's property manager outsourced 11 buildings' basement bicycle garages (2010) to a trading company, which subcontracted to a property company, which split them among 10 individual contractors, who rented about 350 cubicles to tenants; the district fire-safety committee ruled it illegal (30 May 2011); clearance followed, then rentals revived in early 2012; the civil-defense second basement level stood flooded after the July 2012 rainstorm. SOURCED (NBD, 8 Aug 2012). [K1, K9]

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- The shelter-building duty and the 1978 "use in peace" policy turned shelters into hostels, cinemas, markets, rinks and storerooms (BJD 2026), and later into rental housing. SOURCED.
- Location over light: Kim's data show tenants pay for nearness to jobs and subways, not for depth or windows. SOURCED. [K5]
- The clearances turned some spaces into community rooms, safety halls, storage and shops (SOURCED, see C); others stood empty with weak management (Beijing Daily 2018, SOURCED). [K9]

### E. Structural features
- **Rules of everyday behavior [K6] (current municipal rule, Order 302 of 2021):** where underground space is used, as its plan allows, for hostels, dormitories or other dwelling: at least 5 m² of usable floor per person per room; **no bunk beds**; pest-control, disinfection and waste facilities required; and "using underground space planned for non-residential use as a dwelling is prohibited" (Art. 7). Residential or commercial use of an ordinary basement must be filed with the district housing department before fit-out (Art. 15). Fines: hostels breaking Art. 7 rules, 10,000-30,000 yuan; dormitories or dwellings, 500-1,000 yuan, imposed by district police (Art. 23); exceeding the approved occupancy, 30,000 yuan. Where structure, ventilation or air conditioning is unsafe, occupants must move out, and in emergencies the district government may organize removal "and properly resettle" them (Art. 19). SOURCED (municipal PDF read locally). Earlier layers: Beijing's 2011 rental rule banned renting kitchens, bathrooms, balconies and basement storerooms as bedrooms (fines 5,000-30,000 yuan); a 2013 notice set at least 5 m² per person, at most two per room (family excepted), no partitioned or per-bed letting (snippets of Sina 2011 and Chinanews 2013; the Chinanews page fetched was partly garbled: treat as SOURCED in substance, figures UNVERIFIED). A 2022 municipal "negative list" governs reuse of cleared underground space; a 2026 draft lists 21 banned uses in 13 categories (snippets: UNVERIFIED). Inside one basement: posted quiet notices, surveillance warnings, public naming of rent defaulters (2011, SOURCED). Who enforces: district police, housing, civil-defense, fire and health departments; street offices; property managers and guards (2018 guard duty rosters with two-hourly patrols to stop people living there, SOURCED).
- **Clearance campaigns [K9]:** the 2017-2020 "疏解整治促提升" program set, among ten tasks, ending scattered rental of dwelling space in civil-defense works and ordinary basements citywide, plus underground group dormitories and guesthouses; deputy mayors led tasks; a 10-billion-yuan guidance fund with district matching (rmzxw, 4 Feb 2017, SOURCED). 18 Nov 2017: fire at the Jufuyuan apartments, Xinjian Village, Xihongmen, Daxing: started by an electrical fault during testing of a new cold-storage unit in the roughly 5,000 m² basement; 19 dead (eight children, per Caixin), 8 injured; 305 rooms with more than 400 residents on floors 2-3; the State Council team (seven months, 300 interviews, 2,000 documents) blamed illegal practices and poor supervision; 15 detained including the owner; a deputy district head and 20 other officials disciplined; three firms fined 9.6 million yuan in total (China Daily 26 June 2018, SOURCED). 20 Nov 2017: the Beijing Work Safety Committee ordered a 40-day citywide campaign; notices of two days to a week; water and power cut in places; "thousands" evicted, mostly migrants, in sub-zero weather (Caixin, SOURCED). On 27 Nov Party Secretary Cai Qi said it should not have been done "in a simplistic and hasty manner"; more than 100 intellectuals signed an open letter (Made in China Journal, SOURCED). Fangzhuang, Fengtai, 20-24 Nov 2017: 450 underground rooms closed, 420 (also given as 300-plus) demolished, 6,000-plus m², about 90% of the area's underground space dealt with; rooms had been 4-10 m² with bare old wiring and induction cookers (The Paper, SOURCED). By mid-2018 rentals had returned in several basements; two earlier "three-year actions" had each been followed by rebounds (Beijing Daily via The Paper, SOURCED).
- **Money [K8]:** shelters were financed partly by peacetime rents from 1978; the 2026 museum is free and state-run.
- **Speech [K11]:** Mandarin (Beijing Mandarin locally); migrant residents from many provinces speak their regional varieties. Nothing further recorded.

## 3 · S2-09 Cruise-ship crew areas and crew life

**Claim tested:** "The crew's own hidden town below the passengers': service corridors, crew mess and bars, cabins, a whole multi-origin society on fixed contracts."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with refinements.** (a) Crew live mainly on the lowest decks, below the waterline, in shared cabins of two to four, with their own mess, bars, gym, shop, laundry and offices; on the largest ships (Oasis and Icon class, about 2,200-2,400 crew) these spaces span several decks and are planned as a "neighborhood." (b) The society is ranked, not flat: officers, staff and crew have different cabins, messes and access to guest areas, and ranks correlate with nationality, as several academic studies document. (c) Contracts are fixed-term: about 4-10 months for hotel crew per academic and industry sources; the Maritime Labor Convention caps service before repatriation at under 12 months. Studied here as the tradition itself plus three documented cases: Royal Caribbean's Oasis/Icon-class ships, Holland America Line's Indonesian and Filipino crew system, and the shore side in Liverpool and Manila.

### A. Snapshot (the tradition and the three cases)
- **What it is:** the shipboard workforce of a passenger cruise ship, which lives where it works for a contract of months, in spaces closed to passengers.
- **Royal Caribbean (case 1):** at 31 Dec 2024 the three global brands employed about 106,000 people: about 95,150 shipboard, about 10,800 full-time shoreside; shipboard staff from about 145 countries, the largest groups Philippines 33%, Indonesia 17%, India 15%; about 88% of shipboard employees covered by collective bargaining agreements; 68 ships with about 166,900 berths across global and partner brands. SOURCED (10-K FY2024). Oasis of the Seas: 2,395 crew for 5,592 passengers at normal occupancy (CruiseMapper snippet: UNVERIFIED). Icon of the Seas: 2,300 crew (Cruise Industry News, SOURCED; 2,350 per crew sites, UNVERIFIED).
- **Holland America Line (case 2):** runs training centers in Jakarta and Manila "where a majority of our shipboard employees are hired," with courses that simulate ship facilities (HAL careers page, SOURCED). Search snippets add: up to 70 nationalities aboard; the Jakarta school began in 1981 (as the former ship SS Jakarta per one blog), 10-12 weeks of residential training; a Manila culinary program with the Culinary Institute of America from 2009 (UNVERIFIED: snippets of HAL, Dallas News 2019, Cruise Industry News 2009). Asian crew employed since 1971 per one informal blog (UNVERIFIED).
- **Shore side (case 3):** Liverpool Seafarers Center's cruise-terminal hub (since 2016) and the manning-agency district of T.M. Kalaw Street, Ermita, Manila (see C).
- **Defining feature:** a closed, multinational, ranked workforce living in a vertical town whose lower half is off-limits to the paying population above.

### B. What else exists there
**Spaces (Oasis/Icon class, Royal Caribbean) [P3, P5, K10]:**
- Utopia of the Seas (Oasis class, 2024), from a crew member's video: outdoor crew bar on Deck 6 (late-night parties with a DJ, small dance floor, tables; designated crew smoking area); the helipad on Deck 7 is crew-only on Oasis-class ships; an indoor pub-style crew bar on Deck 5 with games; a crew disco on Deck 3 opened only for events hosted by crew welfare; the crew mess on Deck 2, a single dining room for all crew, serving 1,200-1,300 crew, with theme nights and lunch specials; a crew coffee shop beside it (charges, low prices, fresh juice, smoothies); a small crew convenience store (snacks, ramen, toiletries); the HR center (room issues, sign-offs; limited morning and afternoon hours); crew laundry on Deck 1 (free, detergent paid; often busy); barber shop (two chairs) and a windowless gym on Deck 0; the crew linen room one deck lower (free exchange of towels, bedding and toilet paper; vacuums and cleaning supplies lent; limited hours). SOURCED (Your Mileage May Vary, 14 Oct 2024). [K4, K5, K12] [R]
- Icon of the Seas (2024): Royal Caribbean asked crew what they needed (more storage, privacy); more than 1,000 crew reviewed mockups; the video showed an indoor and outdoor hub, a gym and a hair salon (Cruise Industry News, 5 July 2023, SOURCED). Crew-site and blog snippets add: crew area over four decks; double-occupancy cabins with an L-shaped bed layout, built-in stairs to upper bunks, TV, desk, privacy curtains; a crew store; a game room with seven spaces including consoles and VR; a crew-only patio on Deck 7 beside the crew pub; a crew mess with portholes (UNVERIFIED: snippets only; Royal Caribbean Blog refused, 403).
- **Cabins across lines (2021 crew accounts):** standard crew cabins house 2-4; housekeeping, bar and restaurant staff most likely to be four to a cabin; waiters, bartenders, housekeepers and cooks always share with one other; bunks with curtains; showers timed around cabin-mates; management and "second management" get single cabins (on Norwegian and Princess similar to guest rooms); Disney officers in singles, other Disney crew moving to single cabins, Disney crew cabins with porthole and shower; Viking crew all in inside cabins; AIDA crew normally two to a cabin. SOURCED (Cruise Industry News, 6 June 2021). Dennett (2013): single cabins only for high officers; staff and crew share 2-3 per cabin; cabins grouped by department, occupation and gender so cabin-mates share shift patterns; cabins below sea level, deck height matching rank, crew lowest. SOURCED. [K4]
- **MLC minimums for passenger ships [K6]:** ratings' sleeping rooms at least 7.5 m² for two persons, 11.5 m² for three, 14.5 m² for four (Std A3.1, 9(i)); junior officers 7.5 m² and senior officers 8.5 m² per person where no day room (9(l)); single-berth rooms 7 m² on ships of 10,000 GT or over, though passenger ships may be allowed a reduced area (9(f), 9(g)); every seafarer a separate berth of at least 198 x 80 cm; separate rooms for men and women; laundry facilities; open-deck space for off-duty crew (para 14); "appropriate recreational facilities" (para 17). Guideline B3.1.11 suggests a canteen, TV, films, sports and exercise equipment, a library, swimming where possible, "bars on board for seafarers unless these are contrary to national, religious or social customs," and reasonably priced ship-to-shore phone, email and internet. SOURCED (MLC text, CIL NUS).
- **Logistics [K5]:** Royal Caribbean says Oasis-class ships load about 600,000 lb of food and beverages on turnaround day, about 500 pallets trucked to the terminal, with more than 2,100 crew starting at 6:30 a.m. (Royal Caribbean blog via search snippets; the official page failed with an SSL error and the fan blog refused: UNVERIFIED). Turnarounds in many ports have fallen from 18-36 hours to about eight hours over two decades (Mission to Seafarers official, quoted in a search snippet: UNVERIFIED). Crew internet: one report says Norwegian Cruise Line moved crew from a monthly unlimited Starlink plan to a weekly 24.5 GB plan at US$28 a week or $4 a day (crew-center.com, snippet; page refused: UNVERIFIED).

**People, contracts and work [K7, P9]:**
- Three-tier structure of officers, staff and crew: crew (dining room, custodial, cabin stewards) at the bottom; staff (shop, gym, entertainment); pursers rank as officers; pyramid by department and rank; front-line staff usually on split shifts, so free time is spent with workmates on the same hours. SOURCED (Dennett 2013, citing Lee-Ross 2004, Wood 2000).
- Contract length: "generally 4-9 months for service related staff" (Dennett 2013); ITF data cited there: average stay of hotel/catering crew fell from 3 years (1970) to 18 months (1990) to 9 months (2000); crew contracts 8-10 months with 10-12-hour days, seven days a week, about half a day off a week at a supervisor's discretion (Oyogoa 2016, citing ITF 2002); lower-level crew earned about US$650-2,000 a month for 8-10 hours a day, seven days a week, on half-year or longer contracts (Bloomberg via TBS, 2021). SOURCED as attributed. Note: the ITF 2002 figures predate the MLC (in force 2013).
- MLC limits [K6, K7]: maximum work 14 hours in any 24 and 72 in any seven days, or minimum rest 10 hours in any 24 and 77 in any seven days; rest in no more than two periods, one at least six hours (Std A2.3); repatriation entitlement after service "less than 12 months" (Std A2.5.1); paid annual leave at least 2.5 calendar days per month of employment (Std A2.4); no recruitment or placement fees borne by the seafarer except medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport; visas paid by the shipowner (Std A1.4); minimum age 16. SOURCED (MLC text). The ITF leaflet (2013) tells cruise workers that everyone aboard, including hotel crew, entertainers, shop, spa, photo and casino staff, counts as a seafarer; that they must be paid at least monthly with an itemized statement; that overtime records are kept by the crew office and endorsed monthly; and that complaints may go on board, to the flag state or to a port-state inspector, with a right not to be victimized. SOURCED.
- Before the MLC (ITF 2002, as cited by Oyogoa 2016): recruits commonly paid agencies US$1,500-2,000 for airfare, medical, seafarer's book, visa and fees; companies took an indemnity of about $500 forfeited on breaking contract; wait staff and cabin cleaners could earn about $50 a month in base wage plus tips. SOURCED as cited; historical. [K9]
- Nationality and rank: hotel service crew mostly from Asia and Eastern Europe; Western European officers; North American and British entertainers (Dennett 2013, citing Testa and others; SOURCED). Oyogoa (2016) reports senior officers by line historically Italian (Carnival), Norwegian (Royal Caribbean), British (Princess), Dutch (Holland America), citing Garin 2006; and, after a 1981 strike by 240 Honduran workers, a Carnival executive's statement that the goal was "not to have a ship with 15 percent or more of one nationality." SOURCED as cited. [K9, K4]
- Mess segregation: in some fleets three messes (officers, staff, crew) with different décor, service and food, and rules barring use of messes above one's level; officers eat apart; staff may eat in guest areas, crew may not (Dennett 2013, citing Thompson 2004; SOURCED). A first-hand 2001 account (Zhao) of one ship lists five crew messes stratified by status and nationality (search snippet; Cardiff PDF refused: UNVERIFIED).

### C. Cultural locations (on board and ashore)
- **Crew bar** (on most ships): the one space open to all working ranks; cheap drinks (a waiter in Dennett's study cites bottled beer at $1.50); a daily after-work routine for some; supervisors could warn or "document" crew seen out late before a morning shift; one officer said officers were expected in bed by 10 p.m. SOURCED (Dennett 2013, interview quotes). [K10, K6, K12]
- **Crew mess:** the only place food is prepared for crew; a social space where groups form by occupation, rank and nationality; common complaint of repetitive food on rotation. SOURCED (Dennett 2013). [K12]

Passenger-funded "crew welfare funds" for parties and amenities are described only in passenger forums (UNVERIFIED). ### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- Living where one works produces rules that follow people off shift (supervisors watching the crew bar), cabins grouped by shift, and a crew bar as the single cross-rank space (Dennett, SOURCED).
- Closure and distance produced the 2020 situation: after the mid-March 2020 pause "tens of thousands" of crew stayed aboard for months; crew mostly confined to cabins with timed breaks outside; some lost salary after guests left; repatriation flights booked and canceled; a hunger strike on Navigator of the Seas and a banner on Majesty of the Seas; at least six other crew deaths, mostly suspected suicides, reported among those trapped; Carnival said it spent $300 million on 225 charter flights to over 100 countries and had repatriated 99% by early August; the Norwegian Seafarers' Union said contract limits were repeatedly broken. An October 2019 ITF-affiliated study had found about a fifth of mariners surveyed reporting suicidal thoughts. SOURCED (Bloomberg via TBS, 3 Jan 2021; individual names deliberately not recorded). Across all shipping, the ILO estimated 150,000-200,000 seafarers trapped aboard in June 2020; about 400,000 at the peak (search snippets of ILO and PMC: UNVERIFIED). [K9] [SM: man versus society]
- Short port calls limit shore leave: across 5,879 seafarers surveyed (all ship types, 2024) more than a quarter got no shore leave, about a third got ashore once or twice a contract, average time aboard 6.6 months, 47% of those ashore spent under three hours (WMU, 2 Apr 2025, SOURCED; no cruise-only figure on that page). [K5]

### E. Structural features
- **Rules of everyday behavior [K6]:** MLC hours, rest, leave, repatriation and fee rules (above, SOURCED). Company rules seen in sources: dress code for crew entering guest areas (uniform or smart casual; formal wear after 6 p.m. on certain days) (Utopia video write-up, SOURCED); staff may use guest amenities, crew may not (Oyogoa 2016; Dennett 2013, SOURCED); messes closed to lower ranks on some ships (SOURCED); crew-bar conduct watched by supervisors (SOURCED); Princess reportedly sets tiered blood-alcohol limits (0.00% on duty and 0.04% off duty for safety-critical roles, 0.04% for emergency-critical, 0.05% others) and no alcohol within four hours of a shift; random breath tests common; Costa moved some officers to zero tolerance (crew-center.com snippets; page refused: UNVERIFIED). Guests are barred from crew areas and from physical relationships with crew under Norwegian Cruise Line's guest conduct policy (snippet: UNVERIFIED). - **Money flows [K8]:** wages plus tips or pooled service charges (Princess pools gratuities into compensation and bonuses: snippet, UNVERIFIED); flags of convenience (ITF defines them as open registries with no nationality or crew requirements; Oyogoa notes registry fees were 17% of Liberian government revenue in 2003: SOURCED as cited).

Holland America's Jakarta and Manila centers (SOURCED). [R]
- **Speech [K11]:** English is the working language aboard (stated generally; no source read states it as policy: UNVERIFIED); crews speak many national languages, Filipino/Tagalog, Indonesian and Balinese, Hindi and others, among themselves. Nothing further recorded.

## 4 · S2-13 Capsule and micro-unit housing (Nakagin Capsule Tower; capsule hotels)

**Claim tested:** "Very small private units arranged in large numbers: tight spaces made livable; Nakagin also as a study in decay and dismantling." Care: verify Nakagin's dates (completion, decline, dismantling, preserved capsules).
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with refinements.** Nakagin Capsule Tower: built 1970-1972 (opened 5 April 1972), 140 capsules of 2.5 x 2.5 x 4.0 m (about 10 m²) on two concrete cores in Ginza 8-chome; meant to have capsules replaced about every 25 years, which never happened; hot water cut in 2010; owners voted to sell in 2021; last residents out March 2022; dismantling began 12 April 2022; 23 capsules saved and refurbished. Refinements: (a) Nakagin was marketed first as a businessman's city second home and pied-à-terre, then used as offices, studios, short stays and, in its last years, monthly rentals run by the preservation group; it was never a mass-housing block. (b) The capsule hotel is a distinct, related type: the first, Capsule Inn Osaka (1979), was designed by Kurokawa's office for a men-only sauna; legally it is "simple lodging" with shared space, not housing. (c) In practice capsule hotels and all-night net cafés also serve as long-term homes for some workers without housing (Tokyo survey, 2018). Places studied: Nakagin (Tokyo), Capsule Inn Osaka (Umeda), the 9h (nine hours) chain (Kyoto, Tokyo), plus Tokyo's micro-apartments and net-café residents as context.

### A. Snapshot
- **Nakagin Capsule Tower (中銀カプセルタワービル):** 8-16-10 Ginza, Chūō-ku, Tokyo; architect Kisho Kurokawa, structural engineer Gengo Matsui; construction 1970-1972; floor area 3,091.23 m²; two linked concrete towers of 13 and 11 stories (53.5 m and 47.4 m), joined by bridges at 6F, 9F and 12F; 140 prefabricated capsules, each 2.5 x 2.5 x 4.0 m with a 1.3 m round window and in-unit bathroom, fixed by four high-tension bolts and cantilevered so each could be removed independently; plumbing via a flexible connection about 1 m long; capsules built in a factory 450 km away, delivered 5-8 a night and attached in about 30 days; steel-truss boxes clad in galvanized steel, fireproofed with sprayed asbestos; maximum capsule cost in 1972 about US$14,600. SOURCED (Wikipedia). CNN Japan gives 144 units (SOURCED as stated; conflicts with 140 elsewhere); a property-listing site gives 143 units, self-managed with no manager on site (snippet: UNVERIFIED). Opened 5 April 1972; the first 140 tenants each received a commemorative lamp shaped like the building (MoMA labels, SOURCED). Kurokawa described each unit as just under "six tatami mats" (MoMA, SOURCED); Bunshun says about 4.5 tatami (SOURCED as stated; conflict noted).
- **Capsule hotels:** first was Capsule Inn Osaka, Umeda, opened 1979 (1 Feb 1979 per one Japanese snippet: UNVERIFIED as to day), designed by Kurokawa's office from a 1977 commission for a men-only sauna and bathhouse; double-decker capsule beds for up to 450 guests, each with controls for air conditioning, a TV and an alarm; still operating. SOURCED (MoMA labels; Bunshun 2020). Typical capsule about 1.2 m wide, 2 m long, 1 m high; 2,000-5,000 yen a night; no toilets or baths inside; doors or curtains with no lock "by Japanese legal rules"; luggage in lockers. SOURCED (GO TOKYO).
- **Legal frame:** capsule hotels operate as 簡易宿所営業 (simple lodging: structure mainly shared by many guests), licensed by prefectural governors or city/ward heads under the Ryokan Business Act; dwellings that are a person's base of life fall outside the Act (MHLW overview, SOURCED). Licensed lodging facilities at end of March 2026: 104,846 in all, of which 56,773 hotels and ryokan and 47,603 simple lodgings (MHLW, SOURCED). Capsule hotels are not counted separately: NOT FOUND.
- **Defining feature:** standardized personal units of a few square meters, stacked or racked by the hundred, with daily life outsourced to shared or outside facilities.

### B. What else exists there
**Nakagin: how it was meant to work, and how it was used [K4, K5, P4]:**
- Marketed from spring 1971 to businessmen as buyers, with brochure sketches by a *Car Graphic* illustrator likening capsule ownership to choosing car options; standard 13-inch Sony color TV, alarm clock, Sanyo refrigerator; options of a Sony radio and tape recorder and a Sharp calculator. Housekeeping was to be done by women staff, and meals were assumed to be eaten out. Nakagin's own company moved its headquarters into the plinth between the towers. SOURCED (MoMA labels). Built as mainly a hotel for visiting businessmen with some studio apartments (Wikipedia, SOURCED). Chuo City: it was noted as a small dwelling cheaper than a Toyota Corolla of the time (SOURCED).
- Collaborative design: capsules led by Nobuo Abe (who had worked on Kurokawa's Capsule House at Expo '70), facades drawn largely by Aiko Mogi, cores by Koji Shimosawa, plinth by Kenjiro Ueda. SOURCED (MoMA). [K14]
- Colors: stairwells coral red and orange (Tower A) and peacock blue and turquoise (Tower B); electric-blue capsule carpets; ochre office floors. SOURCED (MoMA). [P3]
- Later use: many capsules sat empty; others used as storage, offices or short-stay rentals for architecture fans (CNN Japan, SOURCED). From October 2018 the preservation group's "monthly capsule" rentals (about 30 capsules leased per SoraNews; 3 types, 8 rooms per a snippet) at 120,000 yen a month; no kitchen and no open flame allowed; the fridge and original tape deck not working; hot water to the rooms cut in 2010 after a pipe burst, leaving a shared shower room; no washing machine (nearest laundromat 16 minutes' walk). SOURCED (SoraNews24, 2021). Netting was hung on the towers to catch falling debris; some long-empty capsules had badly decayed while lived-in ones remained in good condition (MoMA, SOURCED). [K9] [R]
- Decline and decision: capsule replacement every 25 years was never carried out; 2006 estimate about ¥6.2 million per capsule to renovate; demolition needed 80% of owners and first passed 15 April 2007; Kurokawa proposed replacing capsules and keeping the towers (supported by the Japan Institute of Architects), but residents objected on earthquake resistance and land use; Kurokawa died in 2007; the developer went bankrupt in the 2008 recession (CNN Japan); a 2018 sale failed during COVID-19; in March/May 2021 the owners' association voted to sell the site; 20 tenants remained in November 2021; all left by March 2022; dismantling began 12 April 2022 (interiors stripped for asbestos removal first; capsules lifted off by crane; cores torn down; the entrance sign saved). SOURCED (Wikipedia; CNN Japan; MoMA). A Kurokawa-office explanation reported in a snippet says capsules could not be exchanged partly because buildings rose on both sides (UNVERIFIED). [K9]
- Restoration cost to save the whole tower and remove asbestos was estimated at 2-3 billion yen; fundraising stalled in the pandemic; the group shifted to saving individual capsules. A 2021 crowdfunding drive raised 7.69 million yen against a 1 million target; the buyer agreed to let the group take up to 139 capsules. SOURCED (CNN Japan; Bijutsu Techo). **Preserved:** 23 of 140, cleaned of asbestos and refurbished; destinations include the Saitama Prefectural Museum of Modern Art (a former model-room capsule in Kita-Urawa Park), the Museum of Modern Art, Wakayama, two at Shochiku in Ginza, one on a mobile trailer by Yodogawa Steel Works, five proposed as lodging in Yokosuka, SFMOMA (Kurokawa's own capsule A1302), M+ in Hong Kong, and MoMA New York (A1305, exhibited 10 July 2025 to 12 July 2026); a residents' group converting A606 into a "mobile capsule." SOURCED (Wikipedia; Dezeen 2024; MoMA). A 3D laser and photogrammetry archive was made before demolition (Gluon; Yuta Tokunaga with the project). SOURCED.
- **The site now:** Accor plans a Pullman Tokyo Ginza with 145 rooms, restaurant, bar and gym, opening late 2027 (Dezeen, 9 Dec 2024, SOURCED).

**Capsule hotels: how they work and who uses them [K4, K5, K7]:**
- Origin: at the New Japan sauna group in Osaka, many salarymen working late stayed overnight in the sauna's crowded nap room; staff, having seen bunk beds at a Shinbashi facility, proposed a cheap, more comfortable place to sleep; the company commissioned Kurokawa's office; Capsule Inn Osaka opened in 1979 (Bunshun 2020, SOURCED). New Japan's Namba spa building dates from 1952 (Bunshun 2020 snippet: UNVERIFIED). Today Capsule Inn Osaka is men-only, and guests may use the New Japan baths and sauna in the same building (Jalan listing snippet: UNVERIFIED).
- Users: business travelers on tight budgets, people who missed the last train, people wanting a few hours' rest, long-stay visitors; mostly men; some men-only hotels; mixed hotels usually have separate men's and women's floors; women-only examples Nine Hours Woman Kanda, Nadeshiko Hotel Shibuya, Akihabara Bay Hotel; weekly and monthly plans exist. SOURCED (GO TOKYO). Women-only capsule hotels and women's areas grew from around 2014-2016 as "premium" types spread (Bunshun 2017 via snippet: UNVERIFIED for dates).
- Types since the 2010s: high-class (bathing and add-ons), simple (shower-based, stylish, low price, e.g. Nine Hours with shower-only and one-hour nap plans), and cabin "compact hotels" (First Cabin) (Bunshun, 23 Oct 2017, SOURCED). Nine Hours' first site, 9h Kyoto Teramachi, opened 9 Dec 2009 (closed 7 May 2020), with design by product designer Fumie Shibata, interiors by Takaaki Nakamura, signage by Masaaki Hiromura; it won a 2010 Good Design gold award (search snippets of the company's own press release and design sources: UNVERIFIED). Nine Hours runs a sleep-analysis service measuring sleep depth, snoring and apnea (Toyo Keizai 2024, SOURCED).
- Long stays: a Shinjuku capsule hotel manager reported over 100 of 300 capsules filled by one-month guests on a 59,000-yen plan (snippet: UNVERIFIED); a "capsule living" 30-day plan from 40,000-odd yen during COVID (snippet: UNVERIFIED). In late 2025 a Yokohama capsule hotel operator reported occupancy above 80% as Tokyo hotel prices rose, with capsule rooms at 5,000 yen vs business hotels over 15,000 yen (President Online, 26 Dec 2025, SOURCED).
- **People without homes in all-night shops (Tokyo, 2016-17 survey) [K9, P9]:** the survey covered 502 internet cafés, manga cafés, saunas, capsule hotels and similar; about 15,300 all-night users on a weekday; 25.8% had no home and stayed to sleep, about 4,000 a day citywide, of whom about 3,000 (75.8%) were in unstable work (dispatch, contract, part-time); of those without stable housing, 38.6% were aged 30-39 and 28.9% 50-59; 90.1% slept in such shops three or more days a week; 43.8% also slept on the street at times. SOURCED (Tokyo summary PDF, Jan 2018). In April 2020, with net cafés asked to close, Tokyo budgeted temporary housing (500 units reported) and free business-hotel rooms for those displaced (BuzzFeed Japan and others, snippets: UNVERIFIED).
- **Micro-apartments (Tokyo, context):** new one-room flats of about 3 tatami (around 10 m²) with a loft bed, kitchen and separate bath and toilet; the QUQURI series by SPILYTUS (about 1,500 units in the 23 wards, 10-15 m²; one example 82,000 yen a month including fees, no deposit or key money) (President, Newsweek Japan and others, snippets: UNVERIFIED).

### C. Cultural locations
- **Nakagin area:** Ginza 8-chome, the old front door from Shinbashi Station (Japan's first rail terminal, 1872, now Shiodome) (Daiwa Institute PDF snippet: UNVERIFIED); four minutes from Shiodome station and six from Shinbashi (snippet: UNVERIFIED). The tower itself was treated as a cultural site: preservation group tours, a 2015 book (127 pages, per the National Diet Library record), exhibitions and a capsule fund (snippets: UNVERIFIED), the 2014 #SaveNakagin campaign of videos, zines, photos and blog posts (MoMA, SOURCED), and after 2022 the capsules in museums (see B). Residents of the last years included retirees who bought capsules to keep original fittings and collected memorabilia (MoMA, SOURCED; names not recorded). [K13, K3]
- **Capsule Inn Osaka and New Japan:** New Japan is credited with bringing löyly (sauna steam service) to Japan as a paid service; staff fan heat over bathers on a fixed hourly schedule (Bunshun 2020, SOURCED; schedule from snippet). Other men-only sauna-capsule hotels: Sauna & Capsule Hotel Hokuou, Ueno, about 200 capsules, open 24 hours; Sauna & Hotel Karumaru Ikebukuro (onsen.nifty.com, SOURCED). Bathing etiquette in shared baths: wash seated at the shower before entering the tub (GO TOKYO, SOURCED). [K10, K12]
- **Kurokawa and Metabolism:** the *Metabolism 1960* manifesto issued at the 1960 World Design Conference in Tokyo, modeling cities on biological metabolism (snippets of Japanese and English sources: UNVERIFIED as read); Expo '70 Capsule House, hung in the Theme Pavilion's space frame, with three capsule rooms for a man, a woman and a child; the Takara Beautilion (MoMA, SOURCED). Capsule House K (1973, Karuizawa) and the Sony Tower, Osaka (1976, demolished 2006) (Wikipedia, SOURCED). [K13]

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- Units too small for cooking or laundry pushed daily life outward: meals out, outsourced housekeeping (Nakagin's plan, SOURCED), shared baths, saunas and lounges (capsule hotels, SOURCED), the laundromat walk (SoraNews, SOURCED). [K12, K10]
- The plug-in design that defined Nakagin was never used for replacement, so the building aged as a whole, and the only "metabolism" it achieved was its capsules' second lives elsewhere (Wikipedia; Dezeen; MoMA; SOURCED). [K9]
- Unlockable capsules (simple-lodging rules) are tied to the long dominance of men-only, sauna-attached hotels and to separate floors for women (onsen.nifty.com, SOURCED).

### E. Structural features
- **Rules of everyday behavior [K6]:** National simple-lodging standards: where bunk-type berths are used, at least about 1 m between upper and lower berth (Ryokan Business Act Enforcement Order Art. 1(3)); MHLW hygiene guidelines (2003): at least 3.0 m² of floor per berth, or about 4.5 m² per two-tier berth; two tiers only; berths at least 0.9 m wide and 1.85 m long (1.0 x 2.1 m preferred); about 1 m clearance above each berth; lower mattress at least 0.35 m above the floor; curtains to block light; ceiling about 2.5 m or more; a rail of about 0.2 m on upper berths recommended. SOURCED (Kanagawa sheet quoting both texts). Capsules cannot be locked (GO TOKYO; onsen.nifty.com, SOURCED). House rules commonly seen at Japanese lodgings and baths: no outside food, no open flame or incense, no loud noise, phones and cameras discouraged in baths, many hot-spring baths refusing tattooed guests (a 2017 government answer said tattoos alone are no ground for refusal at public bathhouses) (rules of other hotels and spas, snippets: UNVERIFIED; no capsule-hotel house rules were read). Nakagin: no open flame in capsules (SoraNews, SOURCED); demolition needed 80% owner consent (Wikipedia, SOURCED). Who enforces: lodging operators under prefectural or ward licensing and health-center inspection (MHLW, SOURCED).
- **Money [K8]:** Nakagin repair reserve about 395 yen per m² and resale reference of 0.9-1.0 million yen for a 7th-floor capsule (property site snippet: UNVERIFIED). First Cabin and four subsidiaries filed for bankruptcy at the Tokyo District Court on 24 Apr 2020, closing five directly run sites (Tsukiji, Kyobashi, Kyoto Kawaramachi Sanjo, Kyoto Arashiyama, Kashiwanoha) amid competition and COVID closures (Travel Voice, SOURCED); many capsule hotels closed in 2020; Nine Hours survived with an asset-light model (Toyo Keizai, SOURCED). [K9]
- **Speech [K11]:** Japanese (Tokyo and Kansai varieties); international guests in capsule hotels. Nothing further recorded.
