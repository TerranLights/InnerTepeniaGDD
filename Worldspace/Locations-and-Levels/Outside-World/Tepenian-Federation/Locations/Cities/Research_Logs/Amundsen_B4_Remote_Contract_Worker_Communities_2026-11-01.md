**Population (SOURCED).**
- Stars and Stripes 2017-03-12 (https://www.stripes.com/theaters/asia_pacific/2017-03-12/under-the-radar:-life-is-low-tech-at-army-missile-defense-base-in-the-pacific-1523128.html): "About 1,300 inhabitants, mostly Defense Department contractors"; 800 DoD contractors and 50 DoD employees live and work on Kwajalein Island; fewer than 20 soldiers plus 20 Seabees on six-month rotations; about 900 people from Ebeye (pop. 15,000) commute daily; about 100 live on Roi-Namur.
- Army.mil (https://www.army.mil/article/149373/welcome_to_usag_ka): commander responsible for "Kwajalein and Roi-Namur's more than 1,250 residents" - service members, Army civilians, contractor employees, families.
- Military Times 2016-06-28 (https://militarytimes.com/2016/06/28/shortages-at-privatized-commissary-may-point-to-future-headaches): 1,200-1,300 authorized to shop at the store; about 20 permanent-party soldiers, about 50 Army civilians, about 80 family members. DERIVED: 80 family members of roughly 1,250 = about 6 percent were dependents, so the resident population is overwhelmingly working adults.
- School profile 2023-24 (https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/3241/KSS/3474015/23-24_School_Profile.pdf): Marshallese from Ebeye "making up over 60% of the workforce".
- Garrison BQ town hall (https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/403688): "Nearly one-third of Kwajalein's workforce is unaccompanied personnel who live in BQs".
- NOT FOUND: a current (2025-26) official resident count; the number of commuting Marshallese badge-holders today (an undated Marianas Variety snippet said about 1,000 commute daily; UNVERIFIED).

**Contract / residency rule (SOURCED).** Nobody may come onto the island without permission and vetting (Stripes). Newcomers get an on-island sponsor who supplies transport, housing and school information (army.mil). Most workers are on multi-year contracts, some on shorter deployment cycles (https://www.your-poc.com/?p=15638 - secondary, a recruiting-style page). Employer chain in the 2023-24 school profile: the Army Garrison "executes base support operations, through the LOGCAP V contractors" (V2X-Vectrus); mission partners named: MIT/Lincoln Laboratory, RGNext, TribalCo, US Army. Pregnancy rule: no obstetrician; expectant mothers must leave by week 32 (Stripes).

**Jobs (SOURCED, partial).** Fields listed: radar and missile tracking, range instrumentation, communications, aviation maintenance, marine operations, construction, infrastructure maintenance, housing, dining, logistics, security, food service (your-poc.com). Security is a contractor (Chenega Global Protection; Hourglass 2022-09-03, scratch text). Fire department and hospital staff "often work back-to-back shifts with few full days off" (BQ town hall). No itemized job-count table found: NOT FOUND.

**Housing (SOURCED).** Unaccompanied personnel: bachelor quarters (BQs), "studio-style barracks, and most on the island are one-room living spaces" (BQ town hall). Army Family Housing: 1950s Navy-era duplexes; 31 units approved for revitalization July 2021, first (Quarters 436, a two-story duplex) refurbished Nov 2021 (https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/410194). Some dome homes (Stripes). 2016 town hall: 22 new units planned and 30 more from 2022 (https://www.army.mil/article/163590/command_addresses_garrison_community_issues_in_town_halls). Hourglass spring 2026 (search summary only, UNVERIFIED): "first new housing built on Kwajalein in 20 years"; a Cat. D / Cat. C category scheme exists for homes. Residents may not dig deeper than 6 inches in lawns (1944 unexploded ordnance; Stripes).

**Services (SOURCED).** Medical, dental, religious services, K-12 schools, a grocery store, AAFES, housing, recreation, dining, a newspaper, AFN radio/TV, air and sea transport (army.mil). Surfway is the only grocery (contractor-run "privatized commissary" since 2003) plus a small AAFES shoppette (Military Times). Food: Zamperini Dining Facility (meal-card dining, 2022 Hourglass), Burger King, Subway, a pizza place, an espresso/bakery shop, no sit-down restaurant (Stripes). Post office, airport (Bucholz Army Airfield; Dyess Army Airfield on Roi), community center, hobby center, bank (Community Bank ATM on Roi), garrison passport office planned 2021. No cell towers, no traffic lights, no official MWR office per Stripes (note: later documents refer to MWR outlets and Quality of Life funds, so Stripes' "no MWR office" is a 2017 reading; conflict logged). Roi residents lacked fair grocery privileges on Kwajalein (2021 Roi town hall). Medical evacuations in 2021 went to Guam because Hawaii hospitals were full (same). A visiting orthodontist comes periodically; new hospital planned (2016 town hall).

**Schools (SOURCED).** Kwajalein School System: government-owned, contractor-operated, accredited by Cognia; operated by V2X-Vectrus. Junior-Senior High 2023-24: 70 students in grades 9-12 (profile says "approximately 100" in grades 7-12); Class of 2024 = 13 (10 American, 3 Marshallese from Ebeye); 1 full-time principal, 1 counselor, about 20 full-time teachers (ratio about 10:1; all master's degrees); 9 AP courses; 2023 graduation 18 graduated (100 percent), 94 percent to college. Students go home for lunch; Ebeye students get a lunch paid by the RMI government. Elementary: George Seitz Elementary (one first-grade class had 14, Stripes). Clubs: scuba, coral restoration, concert/stage band, soccer, volleyball, basketball, water polo, softball. Childcare facility details: NOT FOUND.

**Governance and rules (SOURCED).** Army garrison commander with quarterly town halls (Roi: Tradewinds Theater, 2021-08-25); Community Relations Council and Joint Labor Review Board for Marshallese workforce (2016 town hall); policy documents named: USAG-KA Regulations 190-10 (Entry and Exit Control), Policy 8-1 (Command Sponsorship) (Hourglass 2022). BQ rules (BQ town hall): no free-roaming pets in BQs, no open flames/candles, no smoking inside BQs or Army family housing, quiet hours, regulated outdoor public intoxication, a revised "strikes" disciplinary plan forthcoming. Bicycles are the only private transport; golf carts and government vehicles exist; rental cars unavailable to the public, "Quality of Life" vehicles rentable hourly/daily. Teens can get a boat (B-boat) license at 16 but cannot rent boats until 18. Private clubs must carry liability insurance, register with the IRS and reimburse utilities (2016 town hall). Pets permitted (Stripes). Guest sponsorship program exists (suspended in COVID, 2022 Hourglass).

**Social life (SOURCED).** Nearly 40 private organizations and clubs; nine-hole volunteer-maintained golf course; Snake Pit bar karaoke Sundays; outdoor movies Sat/Sun; bingo every other Thursday at the American Legion; sailing, snorkeling, scuba, fishing, bowling, dance, pottery, woodworking (Stripes). Kwajalein Yacht Club hosts the annual Swashbuckler's Ball (search summary of a current Hourglass item; UNVERIFIED). Ocean View Club and Golf Club (2022 Hourglass). NASA left 85 bicycles lent out by a volunteer ("Auntie" Sablas; Stripes). Community charity drives for Namu Atoll children (121 children; 2022 Hourglass). Proposed "Battle of the BQs" events and adults-only games (BQ town hall). Complaints recorded: excess alcohol, disruptive conduct (BQ town hall).

**Boat/ferry life (SOURCED).** Marshallese workers commute daily by U.S. Army ferry/barge from Ebeye (about 3 miles; an undated Marianas Variety snippet claimed about 1,000 daily, UNVERIFIED); Roi-Namur is staffed from Enniburr; Dock Security Checkpoint on Kwajalein; priority for C-badge workers on ferries is a recorded complaint topic (2016 town hall). 2004 RNZ report: workers had to clear the dock checkpoint within one hour after shift end (older rule; may have changed). Strike: November 2024, 54 Marine Department workers (Marshallese) struck for two days against contractor V2X, disrupting the ferry for range employees; two American supervisors were dismissed; Army and RMI agreed to create an Ombudsman post (https://marshallislandsjournal.com/kwaj-workers-strike-sparks-action/). Grievances: discontinued meals for crews working outside the base, pay versus expatriate hires, alleged racial discrimination. Masks and distancing were mandatory on the ferry in 2022 (Hourglass).

**Supply and calendar (SOURCED).** Weekly flights and biweekly container barges; perishables about 48 hours from Travis AFB via Pearl Harbor-Hickam (Military Times). Fresh fruit flown weekly from Honolulu on military flights (Stripes). Space-A flights to Honolulu about five hours each way used by residents for produce (Military Times). A United Airlines flight schedule "through October" appears in the 2022 Hourglass (commercial contract flights exist; details NOT FOUND).

**What fails (SOURCED).** June 2016 produce/dairy crisis: shelves nearly empty within two hours of the weekly delivery; reefer containers lost cooling, containers left on the dock reduced barge space; shortages "more than a month"; a 1986 precedent and a 1972 "The shelves are bare" Hourglass line cited. 2017: Surfway dairy cooler broken for months; milk soured, potatoes sprouted; household goods like dish soap short; internet down three weeks for part of the Marshalls (defective fiber cable). Water: ongoing drought and potable water distribution concerns (2016 town hall). BQ concrete spalling, corrosion of window frames and fire doors; broken communal washers/dryers; BQ furniture past lifecycle with difficult shipping. Hot upper-floor BQ rooms; ATM broken awaiting parts (2021). Staff shortages in grounds crews.

Entry Control (Ascension) Ordinance 2026 exists (https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/app/uploads/2026/05/ORD-6-Entry-Control-Ascension-Ordinance-2026.pdf; seen in search results only, text not read). Blog (https://theramblingwombat.com/2017/08/06/leaving-the-island-forever/, secondary): when a contract ends residents must find another job or leave; people who retire or reach 18 without a job must leave.

**Jobs and employers (SOURCED, partial).** Main economic activity: military bases at Wideawake Airfield and the BBC World Service Atlantic Relay station; Mitie manages MoD estate and facilities; ESA Ariane/Soyuz tracking station; US Space Force Eastern Range downrange site; Cable & Wireless (now Sure) communications; Maersk tanker resupplies fuel every two months (Wikipedia). Employment law requires employers to provide free accommodation, medical cover, food or a food allowance and travel (Workmen's Protection Ordinance, Wikipedia). Mitie adverts (search summary): unaccompanied posts, single accommodation at Travellers Hill, three meals at the Combined Mess, 24-month contracts, site allowance and tour bonus; Mitie runs the fire and rescue service for the base and AIG estate. BBC station: a 2023 station booklet says the station is "only manned from 0800 - 1630 hrs" with engineers on routine maintenance; an Encompass-linked source says staffed round the clock (CONFLICT, booklet is newer). A Spectator Australia piece (undated; UNVERIFIED) says "120 Britons" work on the island. No union as of Feb 2021 (Wikipedia). Job-count table: NOT FOUND.

**Housing (partly SOURCED).** Four settlement zones: Georgetown (post office, police station, court, Government House, supermarket, port), Two Boats Village (families, school, community center/pool/small shops per a travel guide), Cat Hill (US base), Traveller's Hill (RAF/contract single accommodation). Free accommodation is statutory for employees (above). Housing stock numbers: NOT FOUND.

**Schools (SOURCED).** Two Boats School (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Boats_School): "currently 100 children and 21 staff" (date unclear); another summary gives 60 pupils aged 4-17 in 2022 (CONFLICT, undated); ages 3/Foundation to Year 11; English National Curriculum on a northern-hemisphere term pattern; IGCSE/GCSE in Years 10-11; after Year 11 pupils go to a UK college, Sixth Form on St Helena, or training with an island employer; COBIS accreditation July 2024 (Tes listing via search summary). Youth groups: Girl Guides, Scouts, twirling, craft, chess. Scouting since Nov 1973.

**Services (SOURCED).** Hospital in Georgetown "can deal with most routine treatment and minor injuries", care is charged (gov.uk travel advice via search summary); two surgeons, a midwife, other staff (Noonsite, undated); one dentist (job listing); medicines supply slowed by long delivery times (German Wikivoyage). Bank of St Helena branch; supermarket; local radio plus relayed St Helena radio, BFBS and US military TV; five-digit phone numbers since 2015; weekly newspaper The Islander (since 1971); roads about 40 km surfaced; no taxi.

**Governance (SOURCED).** Governor of St Helena is Governor of Ascension; appointed Administrator on island; Island Council first took office 1 Nov 2002; 2005: 697 electors chose seven members; 2019: 150 of 518 registered electors voted for five councillors; six of seven resigned January 2007 (all Wikipedia).

**Social life (SOURCED, thin).** Ascension Island Football League with three competitions (league, Knockout Tournament, Flipper Trophy); One Boat Golf Club; Exiles Club began as a Cable & Wireless staff social club (blog, secondary); departure tradition of painting a rock on a plinth near One Boat and leaving sports trophies (blog, secondary); Easter Fair (travel guide). The 2018 blog says the island's only hotel was closed then and that Georgetown has a bar/club and grocery store; a 2025 Wikivoyage copy mentions Obsidian Hotel Wi-Fi (CONFLICT on hotel status). Remoteness "wears on" many contract workers (blog, opinion). Club roster, shop hours and pub rules: NOT FOUND.

**Supply and calendar (SOURCED).** RAF/MOD air bridge from Brize Norton (15 seats per flight for island staff, dependants, visiting family/friends); runway closed to non-mission flights 2016, repaired 2020-23, fully finished May 2023; during repairs a US contractor Boeing 757 combi flew twice monthly to Patrick Space Force Base for US personnel only; monthly SA Airlink via St Helena (suspended April 2020, resumed 26 March 2022); MV Helena cargo-passenger ship (Cape Town - St Helena - Ascension); MV Ascension supply ship served the US facilities; Maersk tanker every two months. Pier at Georgetown with fuel pipeline (all Wikipedia and e-visa FAQ).

**What fails.** April 2017: runway conditions ended twice-weekly flights; a visitor account (secondary) of "decline" and a 2007 parliamentary submission exist but were not opened. Council mass resignation 2007. Hotel closure (blog). Medical capacity limited; insurance mandatory because evacuation is costly. NOT FOUND: a documented shortage episode in the shop.

### 3a Diego Garcia (NSF Diego Garcia, BIOT)

**Population (SOURCED, varies by year).** 2004 UK Hansard (https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written_answers/2004/mar/02/diego-garcia): about 1,400 US military, about 40 UK military, about 1,800 civilian workers, "mainly Mauritian and Filipino". 2017 New Internationalist (https://newint.org/node/27050): about 1,000 US military and almost 2,500 contract workers. 2022 Stars and Stripes/Washington Post (https://stripes.com/theaters/asia_pacific/2022-09-14/filipino-workers-stranded-us-military-base-7331493.html): KBR "employs more than 1,200 mostly Filipino workers". Navy Week/Navy welcome package (search summaries): "no permanent civilian population"; Wikipedia-derived about 3,000 military and contractors (2018). Current 2025-26 figure: NOT FOUND.

**Who lives there / contract rule (SOURCED).** Only military and base employees are allowed; no journalist permitted since establishment (New Internationalist). Non-military staff almost all Filipino, hired mainly through Centerra Parsons; some employed directly by the Navy (NI). Reported terms: base salaries "mandated minimum of $400 monthly" (NI, 2017 era); workers may surrender passports on arrival; fines for leaving early; belief they may not form a union (leaked Navy document, NI). 2022: contractor KBR; many workers earn about $5.25 per hour (four workers per Stripes/WaPo); Philippine government in October 2020 demanded US federal minimum $7.25 per hour; KBR said total compensation including housing, food, medical exceeds it. Charter flights about every 3 months carrying 200-250 workers, reportedly cancelled in 2021-22; "no commercial flights serve Diego Garcia"; some workers had not left in over three years (all Stripes). Accommodation for Filipino workers is at "Camp Thunder Cove" (Stripes).

**Jobs (SOURCED).** Cleaners, warehouse caretakers, construction workers, domestic helpers, engineers, firefighters (NI); clerks, warehouse and other semiskilled jobs (Stripes). A fire service staffed by G4S/Parsons Pacific (NI). Marina manager is a contractor role; MWR Director is a federal post covering dining, retail, entertainment, recreation, sports, fitness (usajobs listing via search summary). Counts by trade: NOT FOUND.

**Services and morale infrastructure (SOURCED, older/secondary).** Workers "describe good accommodations and access to sports facilities and beaches" (Stripes 2022). A 2011 TDY traveler post (https://www.flyingsquadron.com/forums/tdy_gouge/articles/tdy-gouge-diego-garcia-biot-r136/; low authority, 15 years old): base shuttle about every 15 minutes; bikes; outdoor theater with two movies a night; gym; barber; marina; fishing trips; clubs including an O'Club with live music, a Seamen's Club (United Seamen's Service), a pizza-making club, Burgers and Bytes; commissary-type store and a ship's store; internet cafe with waiting times; Wi-Fi via prepaid card; a $1 weekend pass to the plantation area via BIOT police. Ship's Store donated $225K to MWR (DVIDS via search summary; profits go to MWR each quarter). Navy Gateway Inns and Suites is the on-base hotel (search summary). Alcohol: duty-free; "A 12-pack of beer costs $9" and "many workers drink heavily to cope with homesickness" (New Internationalist, journalistic claim; UNVERIFIED). Drinking limits, curfews, medical facilities, schooling (none expected): NOT FOUND.

**Supply (SOURCED).** MV Corsica (SLNC Corsica) calls once a month with the majority of supplies; 2001-built, about 5,548 gross tons (Navy story via search summary; page itself returned 403); earlier MSC-contracted resupply ship through 2010/2014 (contract notice). Air Mobility Command / mail arrangements: NOT FOUND.

**What failed (SOURCED).** Pandemic travel freeze stranding about 1,200 civilian workers; wage dispute, flight cancellation (Stripes 2022); industrial deaths - FCO listed five since 2000, "incomplete" (NI); 2022-era business-humanrights.org item on deaths (search result title only; not opened).

### 3b Pituffik Space Base (formerly Thule Air Base), Greenland

**Population (SOURCED).** Army.mil (https://www.army.mil/article/268995/people_top_priority_on_top_of_the_world): about 650 personnel, about 200 active-duty US Air Force/Space Force, the rest Canadian, Danish and Greenlandic civilian contractors who live and work on base. Stars and Stripes January 2015: about 140 airmen and roughly 500 Danish and Greenlandic contractors. Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituffik_Space_Base): peak about 10,000 around the BMEWS construction (early 1960s; article says 1961); 3,370 by January 1968; about 600 in summer 2010; about 150 US service members permanent in 2025 "down from some 6,000 during the Cold War" (the 6,000 comes from 2025 reporting cited there; the 10,000 and 6,000 are not reconciled; CONFLICT). A Vectrus-linked profile (search summary; undated) says the Thule workforce is Danish 89 percent and Greenlandic 10 percent (UNVERIFIED). A Battelle-style guide quoted at "700+" (search summary only; UNVERIFIED).

**Residency rule (SOURCED).** "Pituffik's remoteness and harsh climate restricts all personnel assigned there to live on base" (army.mil). Airmen serve about a year (Stripes 2015). Greenland's government can appoint a liaison officer and the Greenlandic flag must fly over the base since the 2004 amendment (Wikipedia). Contractor eligibility: since the 2020 framework, bidders must be majority-owned by Danish or Greenlandic individuals and based in Greenland (High North News). Dundas history: 130 residents of Old Thule moved in 1953 about 97 km north to Qaanaaq; Danish Supreme Court 2003 found the move unlawful (Wikipedia).

**Contractors / jobs (SOURCED).** 2022: Inuksuk A/S (51 percent Greenlandic/Danish owned via Permagreen Greenland, 49 percent Vectrus Services Greenland) won a 12-year contract worth up to USD 3.95 billion covering airfield operations, civil engineering, environmental management, food and health services, supply and fuel logistics, seaport, transportation, vehicle maintenance, and community and recreation services (https://en.highnorthnews.com/politics/the-usa-awards-new-contract-for-thule-air-base-reverting-back-to-greenlandic-control/124266). Prior history: Greenland Contractors (MT Hojgaard 67 percent plus Greenland's self-rule government) lost the 2015 award to a US firm, which critics said was the first time since the 1950s that upkeep left Danish/Greenlandic control; estimated economic loss DKK 100-200 million plus jobs, apprenticeships (search summary of arctictoday/ft.dk, sources 403 on direct fetch). Counts by trade: NOT FOUND.

**Housing and services (SOURCED).** Dormitory renovation: 636 existing dormitory rooms under renovation; a three-story 1950s dormitory converted from gang-latrine style to 54 private studios (18 per floor) with kitchenette (army.mil). Medical center/hospital; dining facility; district hot-water heating. 2026 DVIDS (https://www.dvidshub.net/news/572372/year-top-world-leading-pituffik-space-base): personnel share one dining facility, one gym, one community center; polar bears roam the main base; portable radios carried at all times; commander greets arrivals on the flightline. Base elevated on footings due to permafrost; 24 hours of sun March-August, 24 hours of darkness October-January (army.mil). Schools: none expected (airmen unaccompanied); NOT FOUND. Rules beyond on-base residency: NOT FOUND (alcohol, curfews).

**Social / morale (SOURCED).** Summer 2025: seven elders of Avannaata community returned to their birthplace; over 90 villagers visited versus 50-60 expected (DVIDS 2026). Tugboat: the Air Force's only tugboat is stationed at the base (Air & Space Forces headline, not opened). Base morale activities, clubs: NOT FOUND (the DVIDS 2026 article lists only one community center).

**Supply and calendar (SOURCED).** Operation Pacer Goose, annual sealift: delivery within a three-month window planned a year ahead, partners include Military Sealift Command, the Canadian Coast Guard and the base maintenance contractor; "delivery windows ... limited to the port season of July and August" (DLA, search summary); few flights otherwise (DVIDS 2026). A 2024 solicitation sought two annual round-trip voyages from a contractor-chosen Danish port for 2025-2027 (highergov, search summary). Perishables, mail routes: NOT FOUND.

**What failed (SOURCED).** Colonel Susannah Meyers relieved of command 11 April 2025 after an email defending the base following the Vance visit (Wikipedia) - governance event, not infrastructure. Infrastructure: arctic foundation maintenance an ongoing concern (DVIDS 2026). Winter: "deadly snowstorms" (army.mil). Outage/shortage histories: NOT FOUND.

**Contracts and social life (UNVERIFIED, photo essay).** A photographer's account (via search summary): high turnover, workers usually sign two-year contracts, Ukrainian or Tajik miners reportedly receive pay at the end of the contract (UNVERIFIED), "no places to socialize: no restaurants, no bars, no cinema" (this conflicts with SNL listing a hotel restaurant and a canteen/supermarket - CONFLICT of date and scope), social groups split along national lines, no internet until about four years before the essay. A 2009 report: wages about one dollar an hour (old).

**What failed (SOURCED).** 29 August 1996 air crash into Operafjellet killed 141 Russian and Ukrainian miners and family members; 26 October 2017 helicopter crash killed all eight aboard (SNL). Tourism collapsed with the war in Ukraine; Longyearbyen businesses advised against visiting as of 2025 (SNL). Mine fire (Barents Observer headline, not opened).

### 10d Pyramiden

**Population and history (SOURCED, thin).** Purchased from Sweden 1926-27; full mining after WWII; expansion and modernization under the Ninth Five-Year Plan 1971-75 (large cultural center, saltwater swimming pool, imported grass); living standards higher than in Norwegian settlements and workers carefully selected; mining ceased and the town was abandoned in 1998 after the 1996 crash (Svalbard Museum, https://svalbardmuseum.no/en/pyramiden). Peak population: over 1,000 (travel source) or about 1,100 (another); NOT FOUND in a primary source. Today: a hotel (reopened 2013, search summary) and a handful of residents; a summer caretaker figure of six (search summary, UNVERIFIED).

**Services (SOURCED / UNVERIFIED).** Cultural palace with auditorium showing films, swimming pool, school, hospital, cafeteria, basketball/sports hall; own livestock (pigs, cattle, chickens) and a greenhouse (travel sources; counts NOT FOUND; "1,000 cows" is unsupported). Dark-season life, contract length: NOT FOUND.
