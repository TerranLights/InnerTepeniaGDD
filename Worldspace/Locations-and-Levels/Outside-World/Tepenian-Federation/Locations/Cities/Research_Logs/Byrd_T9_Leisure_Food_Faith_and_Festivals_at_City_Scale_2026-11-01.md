### 1.1 Salina Turda (Turda, Cluj County, Romania): a worked-out salt mine run as a leisure park

**Pages read (WebFetch):** Salina Turda, English pages "Turda Salt Mine is the most spectacular underground…" https://www.salinaturda.eu/en/salina-turda-2 and the Rudolf mine page https://www.salinaturda.eu/en/?p=12302 · Salina Turda SA, *Serviciile prestate în interes public în anul 2024* (services provided in the public interest in 2024; PDF, text extracted locally), https://www.salinaturda.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Serviciile-prestate-in-interes-public-2024.pdf · Salina Turda SA, *Regulament de vizitare și acces* (visiting and access rules, updated 10 November 2022; PDF, text extracted locally), https://www.salinaturda.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Regulament-Salina-Turda-actualizat-10.11.2022.pdf · Newsweek România, 22 November 2025, https://newsweek.ro/actualitate/una-dintre-cele-mai-spectaculoase-atractii-turistice-din-romania-sub-sechestru-primaria-se-plange · English Wikipedia, "Salina Turda," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salina_Turda.

- **History. SOURCED (Wikipedia, cited).** The first document naming a Turda salt mine is dated 1 May 1271; table salt was produced *"from the Middle Ages until 1932"*; *"During World War II the mine was used as a shelter, and afterward it was temporarily used as a cheese storage center"*; it *"reopened in 1992 as a halotherapy center and tourist attraction."* A 2008 modernization under the EU's **PHARE 2005** program was worth **€5,888,000**, and *"It was reopened for tourism in January 2010."* (Internal inconsistency logged: Wikipedia says Terezia mining ended in 1880 and calls Rudolf "the last place" mined, while dating the haulage winch to 1881.)
- **What is inside. SOURCED (operator, Rudolf mine page).** *"The trapezoidal dome of Rudolf Mine is 42 meters deep, 50 wide, and 80 long"*; *"172 steps lead you to the heart of the mine. You go down 13 floors,"* each landing marked with the year that level was mined; at *"120 meters underground"* there are *"sports fields, bowling, mini-golf, billiards, and table tennis,"* *"a multifunctional amphitheater that hosts all kinds of shows"* (seat count not given), *"a playground for children, with slides and mini- basketball backboards,"* a panoramic elevator, and *"The panoramic wheel, 20 meters high … probably the only underground panoramic wheel in the world."* Terezia mine: a bell-shaped chamber about 90 m high and 87 m across with an underground lake *"between 0.5 and 8 meters"* deep (Wikipedia); visitors row small boats on it (several travel sites; UNVERIFIED as operator text). The Gizela chamber is a treatment room with natural aerosols (Wikipedia).
- **Rules that show how it runs. SOURCED (visiting rules, 2022).** *"Achitarea serviciilor de agrement din mina Rudolf și mina Terezia se poate face doar numerar"* ("payment for leisure services in the Rudolf and Terezia mines can be made only in cash"); *"Accesul în interiorul Salinei … se realizează pe scări – utilizarea lifturilor este opțională. Vizitarea Salinei nu este condiționată de funcționarea ascensoarelor"* ("access inside the salt mine is by stairs; use of the elevators is optional. Visiting is not conditional on the elevators working"); the company *"își rezervă dreptul de a opri funcționarea ascensoarelor în orice moment, atunci când siguranța turiștilor este pusă în pericol"* ("reserves the right to stop the elevators at any time when tourists' safety is endangered"); ticket price *"NU se poate restitui din orice motive legate de infrastructura specifică unui obiectiv subteran"* ("cannot be refunded for any reasons connected with the infrastructure specific to an underground site"); free guides for organized groups of 20 or more *"în limita personalului disponibil"* ("within the limits of available staff"); companies organize *"diverse activități în amfiteatrul Salinei"* ("various activities in the salt mine's amphitheater"); season passes (*abonamente*) are issued.
- **Who runs it. SOURCED (Newsweek România, 22 November 2025, citing *Ziarul de Cluj*).** *"Societatea este de tip închis și are ca unic acționar Primăria Municipiului Turda"* ("The company is closed and its sole shareholder is the Turda city hall"); **125 people** work there (2024 figures); an average of **122** employees in 2022.
- **Money. SOURCED (operator's 2024 statement).** Turnover **41,161,116 lei** in 2024: tourism services **37,694,505 lei**, sale of goods **3,466,611 lei**. Newsweek România: profit **7.9 million lei** (2024), net profit **7.4 million lei** (2023), turnover **34,060,610 lei** (2022).

Wikipedia: *"about 618,000 … in 2017."* Romania Insider (September 2024, snippet; page returned HTTP 429): about 600,000 a year, and a planned expansion in the Iosif mine projected to raise visits by 20% to at least 750,000 (August 2025): UNVERIFIED.

### 1.2 Gjøvik Olympic Cavern Hall (Gjøvik, Norway): an arena and a shelter in rock

**Pages read (WebFetch):** *Store norske leksikon*, "Gjøvik Olympiske Fjellhall," https://snl.no/Gj%C3%B8vik_Olympiske_Fjellhall · Erik Unaas (board member, Norwegian Olympic and Paralympic Committee and Confederation of Sports), *«Trist bakteppe – men en unik mulighet for norsk idrett»* ("A sad backdrop, but a unique opportunity for Norwegian sport"), slides for the Viken sports-district facilities seminar, Halden, **18 September 2025** (PDF, text extracted locally), https://www.idrettsforbundet.no/siteassets/viken/dokumenter/anlegg/anleggskonferansen-halden-2025/erik-unaas.pdf. **Dead ends:** lokalhistoriewiki.no pages on the hall and on Oddahallen (SSL error; died at the source).

- **The hall. SOURCED (SNL).** *"Hallen åpnet i 1993, og ble bygd til OL på Lillehammer 1994, hvor den var en av to arenaer for ishockeykamper"* ("The hall opened in 1993 and was built for the 1994 Lillehammer Olympics, where it was one of two ice-hockey arenas"); *"Hallen ligger 120 meter inne i fjellet"* ("The hall lies 120 meters inside the mountain"); *"Lengden er 91 meter, bredden 61 meter, og høyden under taket et 24 meter"* ("Length 91 m, width 61 m, height under the roof 24 m"); vault span 61 m; gross area **12,500 m²** (tunnels excluded); volume **135,000 m³**; *"tilskuerkapasiteten 5400"* ("spectator capacity 5,400"). It is the home arena of Gjøvik Hockey and used for sport and cultural events (SNL, paraphrased by the reader). **Capacity conflict:** 5,400 (SNL) vs 5,500 (Norwegian Wikiwand), 5,830 and 6,000 (other wikis, Eliteprospects) (snippets).
- **The shelter. SOURCED (Unaas, 2025).** *"Fjellhallen på Gjøvik, bygget til OL -94, er landets største og mest ukjente tilfluktsrom (til 3570)"* ("The cavern hall at Gjøvik, built for the '94 Olympics, is the country's largest and least known shelter (for 3,570)"). Opening date given as 6 May 1993 by Wikiwand (snippet, UNVERIFIED).
- **The national shelter context, as the same speaker gives it. SOURCED (Unaas, 2025; his figures, not checked against the civil-defense authority).** Norway had no shelters in 1940; building expanded after 1961; *"1998 – noen trodde «det var over», slutt på å bygge tilfluktsrom i Norge"* ("1998: some thought 'it was over', the end of building shelters in Norway"); *"Sverige har 67 000 tilfluktsrom, Norge har 20 000 (19 000 av dem private) – men fra 1998 ikke lenger krav om å etablere slike"* ("Sweden has 67,000 shelters, Norway 20,000 (19,000 of them private), but since 1998 no longer a requirement to build them"); Finland 50,000. Requirement: *"klargjort på 72 timer, idretten vil være mye raskere"* ("ready in 72 hours; sport will be much faster"). His case for peacetime sport in shelters: *"aktivitet som det beste vern og vedlikehold … som gir lys og varme … vann i springen (og rørene) … og innbyggerne med et forhold til anleggene"* ("activity as the best protection and maintenance … which gives light and heat … water in the taps (and pipes) … and residents with a relationship to the facilities"). He says responsibility is split among the justice and defense ministries, the culture ministry, the directorate for civil protection (DSB), the Civil Defense (inspection) and the municipal sector, and that *"ingen vet noe"* ("nobody knows anything"; his own search). Search 4's snippet adds that more than a dozen rock halls for the public were built in Norway between 1972 and 1994 (lokalhistoriewiki, Oddahallen article): UNVERIFIED (page not loaded).

### 2.1 Norilsk: the yearly rhythm as a resident describes it (2026)

**Page read (WebFetch):** T—Zh (*T-Zh*, Tinkoff's personal-finance magazine), Timofei Razov, "Сколько стоит жизнь в Норильске" ("What life in Norilsk costs"), dated **30 June 2026**, https://t-j.ru/norilsk/ (a resident's first-person account, with prices; food prices are in Item 4). Also lenta.ru, 16 January 2023, https://lenta.ru/news/2023/01/16/sun/; World Press Photo, Elena Chernyshova, 2014, https://www.worldpressphoto.org/collection/photo-contest/2014/elena-chernyshova/9.

- **Dark and light seasons. SOURCED (T-Zh, 2026).** *"С 30 ноября по 13 января солнце даже не всходит, в любое время суток ночь"* ("From 30 November to 13 January the sun does not rise at all; it is night at any hour"); *"С 20 мая по 24 июля длится полярный день: солнце светит круглые сутки"* ("From 20 May to 24 July the polar day lasts: the sun shines around the clock"). DERIVED: polar night 30 Nov to 13 Jan = **45 days** (1 + 31 + 13); polar day 20 May to 24 July = **66 days** (12 + 30 + 24). - **Polar-night mood at population scale. SOURCED (Johnsen et al. 2012).** Data: the 6th Tromsø Study survey, **1 October 2007 to December 2008** (July excluded); **12,984** participants, of whom **8,951** aged 30 to 87 answered the questions; response rate **65.7%**.
