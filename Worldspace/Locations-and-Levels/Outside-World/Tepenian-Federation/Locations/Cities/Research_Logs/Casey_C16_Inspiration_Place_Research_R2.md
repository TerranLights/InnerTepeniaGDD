### A. Other settings
- **Walcott School (the town's one school building).** In 2022 it was the Davenport Community School District's **only K-8 school** (the district's others were K-6); the district (which runs from I-74 to Walcott and Blue Grass) lost about 10% of its enrollment in the 2010s (Iowa Highway Ends blog, 2022-09-21, https://iowahighwayends.net/blog/2022/09/walcott-could-lose-half-its-school-grades/, SOURCED). In summer 2022 the district's long-range facilities plan proposed turning it into a junior high (grades 6-8 or 5-8) and busing younger children to Blue Grass or Buffalo. ### B. Subcultures
- **Long-haul drivers as an occupational group (not Iowa 80-specific):**
  - **Pay and the overtime exemption:** truck drivers are excluded from federal overtime protection (the FLSA of 1938 exclusion, recodified in 1966); drivers are paid mostly by the mile, so waiting to load or unload, fueling, parking out bad weather and maintenance are largely unpaid; the first two hours of detention are typically unpaid; DOT OIG (2018) put lost wages from detention above $1 billion a year; BLS median pay about $48,310; turnover at large truckload carriers averaged 94% a year 1995-2017; only about 10% of drivers are unionized (economist Michael Belzer); the "Guaranteeing Overtime for Truckers Act" (H.R. 7517) was introduced 2022-04-14 (FreightWaves, Rachel Premack, 2022-04-21, https://www.freightwaves.com/news/an-84-year-old-law-prevents-truck-drivers-from-getting-overtime-pay, SOURCED). Its passage: NOT FOUND (not searched further). [K7, K9] [SM: man versus society]
  - **Women drivers:** about 7% of drivers per BLS, about 3% of Class 7-8 drivers per ATRI; one in six women drivers faces harassment or discrimination daily; about a third of women report never having been harassed vs. about two-thirds of men; women reported 11 of 12 daily problems more often than men; obstacles listed include lack of safe parking and restrooms and the over-the-road lifestyle (ATRI 2024 report, via FreightWaves, 2024-06-21, https://www.freightwaves.com/news/atri-report-finds-most-women-in-trucking-face-harassment, SOURCED). A 2022 FMCSA study found truck stops the most common place of harassment of women drivers (23-30% of incidents); Women In Trucking's 2025-26 index gives 9.5% women among CDL drivers at 210 member firms (snippets, UNVERIFIED). [K4, K9] [SM: man versus society]
  - **Immigrant drivers:** foreign-born U.S. truckers more than doubled from about 316,000 (2000) to over 720,000 (2021), about 18% of drivers; long phone calls on headsets are a coping practice against isolation, and for immigrant drivers a link to home that the CB-radio era did not give; drivers describe life in the "10-by-10 cab," lack of safe exercise space among parked trucks, and being refused restrooms by businesses (CalMatters, Gagandeep Singh, 2026-08-06, https://calmatters.org/health/mental-health/2026/08/trucker-mental-health-long-phone-calls/, SOURCED; California interviews, not Iowa). Punjabi-run dhabas serve drivers at truck stops on I-80 in Nebraska and Wyoming (e.g., Overton NE, Burns WY, Waco NE); none found in Iowa (search summary, UNVERIFIED). [K4, K12] [P9]
  - **Language rule enforcement (2025-26):** FMCSA restored English-language-proficiency violations to the out-of-service criteria from **2025-06-25** (after an April 2025 executive order reversed a 2016 directive); DOT reports more than 26,000 drivers placed out of service since; a proposed rule to codify it closed comments 2026-10-10 (FMCSA/TT News/FreightWaves snippets and the Federal Register listing, UNVERIFIED as read). How this shows at Iowa 80: NOT FOUND. [K6, K9]
- **Show-truck and antique-truck people:** the museum's restoration work is done by an in-house team, not volunteers (a 1948 Mack armored coin truck restored over four years; a 1955 Mack LTL in progress); some trucks arrive already restored by owners, e.g. a Freightliner COE restored on weekends in a Teamsters Local 25 training yard and donated in fall 2024 (museum site and FleetOwner snippets, UNVERIFIED). ## 4 · #67 Michi-no-eki (道の駅, Japanese roadside stations)

**Round 2 focus:** the people inside the stations (shippers, station staff and station masters, residents' companies), subcultures (car-campers, riders, collectors, rural women's food groups), off-season, third places (baths, seniors' rooms), civic and festive life (Michi-no-eki Day, summit, drills, year-end parties), labor and management friction, and R1's NOT FOUND items (national totals, the council's founding, the stamp-rally start).

**R1 open threads answered:**
- **The national liaison council:** before 2019 it was an **unincorporated voluntary body (任意団体)**; it incorporated as the **General Incorporated Association National Michi-no-Eki Liaison Council** in May 2019 because, as a voluntary body, it lacked "independent, continuous operation and transparency." Its 2019 charter document states that the stations then numbered **1,154** and were used by **"more than 200 million people a year"**; it opened **private-company supporting memberships**, planned a privately run secretariat, and set four lines of work: public interest (disaster relief between stations, fundraising, BCP support; training young people with universities; child-rearing support such as baby corners and expectant-mother parking), management (common cashless payment, customer data), revenue (joint online shops, antenna shops in big cities) and brand (managing the michi-no-eki trademark with MLIT). Its officers are **mayors**: chair the mayor of Minamibōsō (Chiba), vice-chairs the mayors of Teshikaga (Hokkaido) and Iinan (Shimane), with mayors and town heads as auditors and directors (全国道の駅連絡会, 「道の駅による地方創生を更に加速する新たな取組みをスタート」, 2019-05-13, https://www.michi-no-eki.jp/pdf/190513siryou.pdf, saved PDF read, SOURCED). ### A. Other settings
- **The farmers' market from the shipper's side (one documented station):** at **Kawauso-ichi**, the farmers' market of michi-no-eki Kawauso no Sato Susaki (Susaki, Kōchi; opened February 1999; Susaki ~21,000 people, about 40% aged 65+): 50 members at opening; sales passed **¥100 million in its third year**, peaked in 2004 and declined as new markets, another station and an expressway extension opened; **commission 13% of sales, plus ¥1 per barcode label**; **465 members**, slowly falling. Four shippers interviewed in November 2020: women in their **80s and 90s** working alone (pickled radish, vegetables, sacred-tree branches for altars), and a man in his 40s who **gets up at 3 a.m. five days a week** to make country-style sushi. One woman called the market her "place of family-like gathering (団らん)" and goes every day; another said farming is "the only pleasure in my life"; a former office worker said the **sales-report e-mails that arrive several times a day** are her daily pleasure. The market's yearly round: tents outside at the **equinox weeks (彼岸), Obon and New Year** to widen the selling floor; its own stamp card; **study tours to other markets twice a year**; a **December year-end party (忘年会)**; cleaning the moss-phlox beds around the station; an annual pesticide study session. Customers surveyed on a Sunday: half in their 60s-70s; locals visit several times a week, some almost daily (吉成夏紀, Kōchi University of Technology graduation thesis, 2020, https://www.kochi-tech.ac.jp/library/ron/pdf/2020/03/15/a1210561.pdf, saved PDF read, SOURCED; a single-station student study). [K7, K10, K14, K2] [P4, P8]
- **The national pattern behind it (all direct-sales markets, not only stations):** a 2017 survey of 4,250 year-round markets (1,150 replies, 27%) found the average market has **about 200 registered shippers**, of whom 60-80% ship regularly; **commission on fresh produce 15-20% at 47.9% of markets and 10-15% at 37.0%**; the top organizational problems were **"aging of shippers" (89.2%)**, falling shipping volume (57.0%) and falling shipper numbers (48.6%); more members are registered but paused by illness, caregiving or no longer being able to drive; the second most-cited effect of opening a market was "producers' sense of purpose (生きがい)" (63.7%), then "elderly people's sense of purpose" (51.1%); free-text answers mention "many in their 80s" and stations opening nearby as competition (まちむら交流きこう, 『農林水産物直売所・実態調査報告』, November 2018, https://www.kouryu.or.jp/service/pdf/marketsurvey2018.pdf, saved PDF read, SOURCED). [K7, K9] [R: elderly as a working supply network]
- **A station that is a village's town hall, clinic and shop:** Miyama (Nantan, Kyoto; about 3,800 people in 340 km²; no railway; population over 10,000 in 1955, about 5,000 by the 2000s, 50% elderly; five primary schools merged into one in April 2016). When the JA merger closed the local food and daily-goods store in 2000, the **Hiraya district promotion council (平屋振興会)**, one of five residents' self-governing bodies, led talks and in **2002 residents jointly invested to found the limited company "Network Hiraya"**, which took over the store as **"Flat Miyama"**; the town bought and repaired the JA building and **lent it free**; a town official staffs an **administrative counter** in the council's office upstairs; in **2005** the store and the clinic, health center, seniors' community center, post office, fishing co-op, petrol stand and Miyama Milk dairy around it were registered as **michi-no-eki "Miyama Fureai Hiroba"**; it is also an aid station for a cycling event of about 1,000 riders, where residents serve local food (Nantan City Miyama Branch, presentation to MLIT, August 2018, https://www.mlit.go.jp/sogoseisaku/soukou/soukou-magazine/1808nantan.pdf, saved PDF read, SOURCED). [K1, K5, K10] [P7]
