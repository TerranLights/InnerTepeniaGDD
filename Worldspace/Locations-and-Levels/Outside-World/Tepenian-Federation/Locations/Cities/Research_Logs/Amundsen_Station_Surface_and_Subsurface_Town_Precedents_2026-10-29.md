## 1 · Underground or part-underground living towns

For each place the brief asks: what is above, what is below, who lives and works where, how the layers connect, what it costs, and what fails.

### 1.1 Coober Pedy (South Australia)
- **Scale (official census):** ABS 2021 Census, suburb/locality Coober Pedy (SAL40295): **1,566 people**, **57.1% male / 42.9% female**, median age **50**, **1,466 private dwellings**, of which **674 were occupied** (87.8% "separate house," 1.9% semi-detached, 6.1% flat, 3.3% "other dwelling"). **SOURCED** (https://abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/quickstats/2021/SAL40295). Urban center/locality (UCL415010): 1,437 people, median age 49. **UNVERIFIED** (search snippet and Wikipedia citing ABS; the UCL page was not opened). **DERIVED:** 674 ÷ 1,466 ≈ **46% of private dwellings occupied** on census night. **NOT FOUND:** the census does not separate dugouts from surface houses in any table seen; no official count of dugout households.
- **Share living underground:** "About half of them live underground" (of ~2,000 residents). **SOURCED** (NASA Earth Observatory, "Keeping it Cool in the Outback," Image of the Day 2023-11-20, https://science.nasa.gov/?p=1073347). Guinness World Records, "Largest underground mining town" (2014): "around 2,000 residents live in about **1,500 underground homes**," dugouts **2.4–6.7 m (8–22 ft)** below ground, daytime shade temperatures to 51 °C. **SOURCED** (https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/341453-largest-underground-mining-town). Other figures (60%, 80%; populations 1,500–3,500) appear only in travel pieces. **UNVERIFIED**. The figures disagree and are not reconciled.
- **What is below:** homes, hotels and restaurants "dug into" soft porous bedrock; residents carve spaces to their own preferences. **SOURCED** (NASA EO 2023). Underground churches (the Serbian Orthodox Church of St Elijah, and a Catholic church), underground motel rooms, an underground jewelry shop, heritage-listed dugout at 13 Hutchison Street. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia, secondary). St Elijah: built by local Serbian miners, consecrated 1993, ~30 m long, floor ~3–17 m below ground, with a hall, priest's residence and school also dug in. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets).
- **What is above:** a "treeless, rocky desert"; settlement outlines and mining remnants visible from orbit, the dugouts not; an 18-hole golf course with no grass; old opal mines toured. **SOURCED** (NASA EO 2023). Surface buildings, drive-in theater, museum. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia).
- **Climate and the reason for going down:** hottest summer temperatures "reportedly reach 52 °C"; underground "an almost constant 23 °C (73 °F)." **SOURCED** (NASA EO 2023). BoM averages: January mean max 36.7 °C, June 18.4 °C; annual rainfall 144.2 mm; record 48.3 °C. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia citing BoM). Tourism figures for the dugout range from 19–25 °C. **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Ventilation (how the layers connect for air):** "Ventilation shafts run up to the surface to ensure the supply of oxygen and to release moisture"; the dry climate keeps rooms from becoming damp; "low-energy, low-tech climate control through passive cooling." **SOURCED** (NASA EO 2023). **NOT FOUND:** any formal thermal or ventilation engineering study of the dugouts (dead at the query twice).
- **Construction and cost:** a three-bedroom dugout can be excavated into a hillside for a cost similar to a surface house; mechanized mining from the 1970s sped up shafts and tunneling. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia). Tunneling machines made locally, purchase prices ~A$130,000–250,000. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets; the ABC, Business Insider and AOL pages were **dead at the source**: 403 / DNS / 404).
- **Power and water:** a 2017 hybrid project joined solar, wind and battery storage to an existing diesel plant; the grid has "operated on 100 percent renewable power" for over 90 hours at a time. **SOURCED** (NASA EO 2023). Water from the Great Artesian Basin via a council bore ~23–25 km away; a reverse-osmosis plant since 1985 replaced a 1967 solar desalination plant; ageing pipes and losses make charges the highest in South Australia (ABC, 6 March 2019). **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia and snippets).
- **What fails:**
  - **Open shafts at the surface:** more than **250,000 mine-shaft entrances** in the area by 1999 (National Geographic 1999, via Wikipedia). **UNVERIFIED**. Local association estimate "more than 1.5 million." **UNVERIFIED** (snippet). Falls in January–February 2024 (one fall of ~25–30 m, ~22 hours trapped); SafeWork SA warns of shafts hidden under corroded iron, mesh, spinifex or dirt. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets; SafeWork SA and The New Daily **dead at the source**, 403).
  - **Flood:** the town flooded on 10 April 2014 after 115 mm of rain in 24 h. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia citing ABC).
  - **Dugout collapse:** **NOT FOUND** (dead at the query).

### 1.2 Matmata (Tunisia)
- **Form:** a large circular pit dug into the ground as a courtyard, rooms cut into its walls; some homes join several pits by trench-like passages. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia). Pit "about 7 m (23 ft) deep" and "10 m (33 ft) wide"; rooms tunneled "a few meters" in from the pit wall; courtyards "connected to other courtyards with more rooms forming an underground maze." **SOURCED** (naturalhomes.org, https://naturalhomes.org/timeline/matmata.htm; a low-authority page that names no source). Other figures for depth 5–10 m and diameter 7–15 m, a sloping entrance tunnel, two stories of rooms (living and stables below, storage above), 20–22 °C average and a ~15 °C summer difference come only from travel and design snippets. **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Who and where:** in the Djebel Dahar region, ~365 km south of Tunis; rooms "hewn into the walls of an excavated circular courtyard"; an earthen casing protects against summer heat and winter wind; similar homes across the border in Libya. Residents named: a 38-year-old woman living alone in a five-room home in Tijma ("If I leave then the house will be gone"); a family of six letting tourists in for tips; a 65-year-old shopkeeper in Haddej, one of "the few who still know how to build and maintain the homes." Economy: olive farming and tourism. **SOURCED** (Arab News / Reuters, "Last residents hold on in Tunisia's underground houses," 2018-02-24, https://www.arabnews.com/middle-east/last-residents-hold-on-in-tunisias-underground-houses-1253181). Town population 2,116 (2004 census). **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia citing INS).
- **Why people left (abandonment):** rural depopulation; new towns and villages built in the 1960s–70s under Bourguiba's modernization drive (locals suspect an aim to dilute Berber communities); inheritance disputes; drought or heavy rain, which can collapse the houses. Repair is ongoing: "Every time there's rain I come and repair them." Tourism fell after 2011 and the 2015 attacks. **SOURCED** (Arab News / Reuters 2018).
- **What fails — rain:** 1969: rains lasting **22 days** flooded the homes and "caused many to collapse"; a delegation went to Gabès and an above-ground settlement was built, but most residents rebuilt underground and only a few families moved up. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia, flagged as needing citations). The year (1967 vs 1969) differs between sources. **UNVERIFIED**. Remaining dwellings: "500 … now only one lived in full time" vs "~200 families" — **conflict, both UNVERIFIED**.
- **Surface layer:** the hotel Sidi Driss (pit house) was a Star Wars filming location (1976; also 2002). **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia). "New Matmata" (Matmata Nouvelle) on the surface: named in snippets; date and distance **NOT FOUND**.

### 1.3 Cappadocia: Derinkuyu and Kaymaklı (Türkiye)
- **Derinkuyu, scale:** about **280 ft (~85 m)** deep, "at least 18 levels," up to **20,000 people**; more than **360** underground settlements geolocated in Cappadocia. **SOURCED** (History.com, M. de los Ángeles Orfila, 2026-05-12, https://www.history.com/articles/derinkuyu-turkey-underground-city). Wikipedia gives ~85 m, five levels (citing BBC Travel 2022) and 20,000 with livestock and stores. **UNVERIFIED**. Level counts range 5 / 8 / 12–13 / 18 across sources — **conflict, not resolved**.
- **How the town above connects to the city below:** "entrances were hidden, often concealed within masonry houses near the surface." **SOURCED** (History.com 2026). Over **600 entrances**, some under basements of private houses in the town above. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia citing BBC Travel; BBC **dead at the source**, fetch refused). Rediscovery **1963**: a resident expanding his house broke through a basement wall into rooms. **SOURCED** (History.com 2026).
- **Air and water:** a main shaft to the surface plus "more than 50 additional ventilation shafts"; water drawn from the water table "through service wells." **SOURCED** (History.com 2026). A ~55 m shaft used as a well supplying both villagers above and those hidden below; the central shaft never opens on two overlapping levels (Aydan & Ulusay 2013). **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia).
- **Closure and defense:** small chambers in narrow sections "likely held enormous stone doors." **SOURCED** (History.com). Each floor closable separately. **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Uses below:** stables, wine and oil presses, cellars, storage, refectories, chapels, kitchens with tandoor ovens, a vaulted room said to be a school. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia, citing National Geographic and the Nevşehir governorate).
- **Abandonment:** some spaces used until the 19th century before obscurity. **SOURCED** (History.com). Cappadocian Greeks expelled in the 1923 population exchange, after which the city was abandoned. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia). The two statements are not reconciled.
- **Kaymaklı (official):** "It has **8 floors** … **4 floors** are illuminated and opened to visitors"; "opened to the public in **1964**"; contents "wine tanks, water cellars, kitchen and food stores, ventilation shafts, water wells, a church"; "large bolt stones that close the door from the inside." **SOURCED** (Türkiye Ministry of Culture and Tourism museum portal, https://www.turkishmuseums.com/museum/detail/2207-nevsehir-kaymakli-underground-city/2207/4).
- **Kaymaklı, living layer above:** "The people of Kaymaklı (Enegup in Greek) have built their houses around nearly **100 tunnels** of the underground city" and "still use the most convenient places in the tunnels as **cellars, storage areas and stables**," reached from their yards. **SOURCED** (newgoreme.com, https://www.newgoreme.com/about-turkey/cappadocia-region/kaymakli-underground-city-3726/; a regional tourism site). A tunnel of 8–9 km between Derinkuyu and Kaymaklı: **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia citing Martin 2017; other sources say unexcavated).
- **NOT FOUND:** an official muze.gov.tr page for Derinkuyu (dead at the query).

### 1.4 Loess Plateau cave dwellings, yaodong (China)
- **Scale:** 30–40 million people in northern China lived in yaodong as of the early 2000s (Lloyd & Mitchinson 2006; Liu, Wang & Liu 2002). **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia; one source is a trivia book). "An estimated 30 million." **UNVERIFIED** (snippet, The China Project). Falling rapidly with urbanization. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia citing National Geographic 2023).
- **Three forms:** cliffside (dug into a loess slope, open front for light and air; most common); **sunken** (dug down around a central pit courtyard, rooms cut horizontally from its walls, reached by a sloped or cliff-cut entrance); hoop or freestanding (arched, built outdoors, covered by ≥2 m of earth). **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia). Main provinces Gansu, Shanxi, Henan, Ningxia. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet).
- **A sunken-courtyard village:** Miaoshang, near Sanmenxia (Henan): **73 courtyards**, "more than 100 existing caves," method dating to the late Qing; loess keeps courtyards cool in summer and warm in winter; now zoned for tourism. **SOURCED** (China Daily via chinaservicesinfo.com, updated 2018-08-31, https://chinaservicesinfo.com/s/201808/31/WS5b8cf506498ea626bcc56732/shanzhou-cave-dwellings.html). A 2025 field-measurement study (Li et al., *J. Asian Architecture and Building Engineering*) found the earth-sheltered roof the largest effect on indoor temperature. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet). **NOT FOUND:** site-specific measured temperatures (dead at the query).
- **What fails — earthquake:** in the **1556 Shaanxi earthquake**, many residents "lived in yaodongs … which collapsed and buried alive those sleeping inside," and landslides destroyed more; direct deaths ~100,000 by modern estimate, ~830,000 total including famine, plague and migration. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia; no USGS page surfaced). Yaodong fared better than conventional homes in the 1976 Tangshan earthquake but were three times likelier to collapse in Ningxia (harder soil, valley-floor sites prone to landslide; Golany 1992). **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia). **NOT FOUND:** the 1920 Haiyuan earthquake's effect on cave dwellings (dead at the query).

### 1.5 Naours (Somme, France)
- **Form and scale:** about **3 km** of galleries and hiding places, **26 galleries and 300 rooms**, **33 m** below the town of Naours; 9.5 °C; could shelter ~3,000 people and the village's livestock; public rooms, stalls, wells, chimneys, a chapel. **SOURCED** (showcaves.com, https://www.showcaves.com/english/fr/subterranea/Naours.html). Michelin: ~2 km of galleries, three chapels and a bakery. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet). Length 2 vs 3 km — **conflict**.
- **How the layers connected:** smoke from underground fires "was routed to the surface through cottages," chimneys built so smoke dispersed unseen; **entrances hidden in farmhouses and a windmill**. **SOURCED** (showcaves.com).
- **Use over time:** refuge during invasions (dug from the 3rd century per this page; another source says turned into a refuge, "muches," in the 16th century); longest occupation in the **Thirty Years' War (1618–48)**; salt smugglers 1750–1830; rediscovered by the village priest Abbé Danicourt in **1887**; WWI Allied (mainly Australian) visitors left graffiti; British fuel store 1939; German ammunition dump and quarters 1941–44; opened to the public 1949. **SOURCED** (showcaves.com) except the 16th-century and 3rd-century origins, which conflict (**UNVERIFIED**). Thousands of WWI graffiti recorded by archaeologists (2014). **UNVERIFIED** (INRAP page **dead at the source**, 403).

### 1.7 Guadix (Granada, Spain)
- **Scale:** the council says about **2,000** cave houses are preserved; the town has just under 20,000 residents; constant interior **18–20 °C**; a hillside "forest of white chimneys" marks the caves from above; caves are dug "from the ceiling downward." **SOURCED** (eldiario.es, Raquel Sáez, 2025-03-09, https://www.eldiario.es/viajes/municipio-granada-convertido-capital-europea-cuevas-miles-casas-subterraneas-pm_1_12113078.html). ## 2 · Cold-climate towns with an underground (or second-level) network

### 2.1 Montreal: RÉSO, the "underground city" (Québec, Canada)
- **Official figures (City of Montréal):** "quelque **32 km** de galeries piétonnes" covering a large part of downtown; "près de **500 000** personnes" use it daily, whatever the weather; "Frais en été et chaud en hiver"; links "4000 boutiques, restaurants ou aires de services," nearly twenty museums and venues, hotels, offices, schools, universities and the metro. **SOURCED** (montreal.ca, "RESO, le réseau souterrain de Montréal," updated 2026-03-25, https://montreal.ca/articles/reso-le-reseau-souterrain-de-montreal-13713).
- **Tourism office figures and origin:** **33 km**; ~500,000 people a day "as an all-weather transport link beneath the streets"; **1962**: Place Ville-Marie connected by interior passages to the Queen Elizabeth hotel and the central train station; growth tied to the metro and Expo 67. **SOURCED** (Tourisme Montréal, updated 2026-10-27, https://meet.mtl.org/en/blog/underground-city-RESO). Length 32 vs 33 km — **conflict between two official sources, not resolved**.
- **Structure (secondary):** more than **120 exterior access points** (plus ~60 metro entrances outside the official limits); 10 metro stations; ~1,600 housing units in connected buildings; the metro authority offered air rights above metro entrances through emphyteutic (long) leases; most parts open while the metro runs (≈5:30–1:00), some close outside business hours; described as "more of an indoor city" than a subterranean one. Origin of the vision credited to urbanist Vincent Ponte; Place Ville Marie built over a pit of railway tracks. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_City,_Montreal, citing Tourisme Montréal 2017, montreal.ca and press).
- **What failed:** August **2007**: a 7-m fissure in a corridor linking McGill station to The Bay forced closures, halted the Green Line, and took months to repair (blame assigned variously to city workers hitting a pillar and to a bike path). **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia citing Gazette, CBC, CTV 2007–08).
- **NOT FOUND:** ownership and easement structure of the corridors from a primary source (montreal.ca does not state it); the 1991 *Téoros* article was **dead at the source** (bot-check wall).

### 2.2 Toronto: PATH (Ontario, Canada)
- **Official (City of Toronto, modified 2025-01-21):** "more than **30 kilometers**" of mostly underground walkways; "More than **75 buildings**" connected; six subway stations, three department stores, nine hotels, Union Station, City Hall and Metro Hall reachable; "more than **200,000** business-day commuters." **Ownership:** "each segment is owned and controlled by the owner of the property it runs through," about **35 corporations**. **History:** 1900, Eaton's linked its stores by tunnel; five downtown tunnels by 1917; Union Station (1927) tunnel to the Royal York; major growth in the 1970s; **1987** Council made the City the coordinating agency and paid for system-wide signage design; new wayfinding installed spring 2018. **Scale:** 3.7 million sq ft of retail, ~1,200 shops and services, ~$1.7 billion annual sales, ~4,600 jobs, ~$271 million annual tax revenue. **SOURCED** (https://www.toronto.ca/explore-enjoy/visitor-services/path-torontos-downtown-pedestrian-walkway/).
- **Not all underground:** the system includes above-grade enclosed walkways and at-grade links inside buildings. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet). A 2011 city expansion plan (Urban Strategies) toward ~60 km and 45 new entry points. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet).
- **NOT FOUND:** PATH opening hours and any failure record (not searched beyond the two queries logged).

### 2.3 Helsinki: the Underground Master Plan (Finland)
- **Primary source:** City Planning Department, *The Helsinki Underground Master Plan: A city growing inside bedrock*, Helsinki Plans 2009:8 (PDF https://www.hel.fi/hel2/ksv/julkaisut/esitteet/esite_2009-8_en.pdf; text extracted with pdftotext).
  - Underground construction used systematically "since the 1960s"; Helsinki suits it "because its bedrock is hard and located near the ground surface"; "Excavating bedrock is a one-off action." **SOURCED**.
  - "Approximately **9 million cubic meters**, consisting of about **400 separate facilities or tunnels**, have already been built under the city." The plan reserves **40 new rock-resource areas** and **100 new space allocations**. **SOURCED**.
  - "The Helsinki Underground Master Plan is a **juridical plan binding property owners and public officials**"; "As far as is known, no similar town plan regulating underground construction at this scope has been prepared anywhere else in the world." It also guides above-ground zoning; other construction is allowed if it does not conflict with the main underground functions. **SOURCED**.
  - **What is below:** vehicular and rail tunnels, metro sections, airport links (over 30 new traffic tunnels and 10 built); ~30 built and planned parking facilities; an underground service street under Aleksanterinkatu's properties to take service traffic off city-center streets and leave a pedestrian precinct; common-use utility tunnels carrying district heating and cooling and large power and telecom cables, "the total length of the lines running under the Helsinki area is **300 kilometers**"; water management, heating plants, substations, depots, coal and oil storage; the Viikinmäki wastewater treatment plant (1994) "hollowed out of bedrock"; recreation (Itäkeskus swimming hall, Hartwall Arena practice hall, Merihaka sports, an Olympic Stadium sports tunnel); Kamppi, the deepest metro station; facilities "fitted for protective use" as civil-defense shelters; new facilities designed as multi-purpose complexes. **SOURCED**.
  - **Safety principles:** automatic surveillance, alarm and extinguishing systems; "workable smoke exhaust and fire compartmentation"; address systems and "clearly indicated emergency exit routes" for evacuation to the surface; ventilation "designed to also function in fire situations." **SOURCED**.

## 3 · Mining and research towns whose work is below the surface

### 3.1 Kiruna (Norrbotten, Sweden)
- **Layers:** the town on the surface; LKAB's iron-ore mine below and beside it. LKAB says mining had to descend to "a new main level, **1365 meters below ground**," which required moving the former town center; LKAB first informed the municipality in **2004**. **SOURCED** (LKAB, "The moving of Kiruna church," https://lkab.com/en/events/the-moving-of-kiruna-church/).
- **The surface fails because of the work below:** LKAB's updated deformation forecast (presented 2025-08-28) adds "approximately **2,700 homes and 6,000 people**" to the affected area, "largely evenly distributed along the previous estimated impact line"; compensation "over the next ten years"; cost estimate about **SEK 22.5 billion**; "Even if we stopped the mine today, the deformations would continue for many years"; compensation of municipal buildings outside the previous area under discussion. **SOURCED** (LKAB press release, 2025-08-29, https://lkab.com/en/press/continued-mining-affects-more-of-kiruna/). A Q3 2025 report figure of ~SEK 20 billion in increased provisions (~SEK 4 billion for previous mining). **UNVERIFIED** (snippet). Move ~3 km east; new center and city hall (Kristallen) inaugurated 2022; completion expected ~2035. **UNVERIFIED** (secondary snippets). LKAB's own urban-transformation page was **dead at the source** (404); ICE case study 403.
- **Moving a building:** Kiruna Church (a cultural monument), **672.4 t** (a later Q&A says ~600 t), 40 m wide, moved **19–20 August 2025** over a specially adapted **5 km** route, rotated 180° on arrival; its ~100 t belfry and buildings from Björkbacken (including the vicarage) also moved to recreate its setting. **SOURCED** (LKAB church page).
- **What fails below:** **18 May 2020, 03:11** — a seismic event of Mw **4.2** (3.3 on LKAB's local scale; local geophones hit their limit) about **1,146 m** below the zeroing point in the footwall, block 22 into block 26; likely a pillar failure between shafts growing into a rock-mass failure "covering several hundred meters"; "extensive rockbursts and outfall"; the mine was evacuated, **13** underground workers returned to surface, "No one was injured"; small-scale production restarted 21 May, 75% by July; the epicentral area still stopped in August. **SOURCED** (LKAB, 2020-08-03, https://lkab.com/en/news/analysis-of-the-seismic-event-in-kiruna-on-may-18th-completed/). ### 3.2 Longyearbyen (Svalbard, Norway)
- **Company town and the end of mining:** the community grew from the Arctic Coal Company of John Munro Longyear; Mine 1 opened 1906; Store Norske bought the company in 1916 and later ran Mines 2, 3, 5, 7 and Svea (closed 2016); now wholly state-owned; Longyearbyen a "Company Town" where the company owned much of the infrastructure. **Mine 7**: began 1976, ~1 km east of town in inner Adventdalen; ~120,000 t of coal a year recently; 63 employees (50 in production); closed at the end of **June 2025**, after the town's coal power plant closed (decided for fall 2023; diesel as a stopgap). **SOURCED** (High North News, Hilde-Gunn Bye, 2025-06-17, https://en.highnorthnews.com/home/svalbard-the-end-of-the-last-norwegian-coal-mine/189330).
- **A facility below the permafrost — the Svalbard Global Seed Vault:** heavy rain in **October 2016** sent water into the access tunnel; the seeds further inside stayed safe; 2017 measures: heat sources removed from the tunnel, drainage ditches on the mountainside, waterproof walls inside; the tunnel ("Svalbard tube") to be replaced by a water-resistant concrete structure with technical installations moved to a separate service building to avoid unwanted heating; construction from March/April 2018, finished spring 2019; Statsbygg responsible. **SOURCED** (seedvault.no, 2017-11-16, https://www.seedvault.no/?p=2435). Storage at −18 °C; tunnel ~100 m into the mountain. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets).
- **What fails on the surface:** **19 December 2015**, Lia hillside: an avalanche hit **11** homes (one lifted off its base), killed **2** people buried under ≥2 m of snow; wind deposited up to 4 m of snow on the slopes (crown up to 4 m) although the airport recorded only 18 mm of precipitation; insurance costs ~NOK 50 million by early 2017; afterward a new risk map and an NVE avalanche warning service; ruins removed; snow fences "an open question." **SOURCED** (UNIS / Gemini, Nancy Bazilchuk, 2017-02-16, https://www.unis.no/news/force-deadly-polar-bears/). A further avalanche in February 2017 damaged two houses. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet). **NOT FOUND:** pile foundations and permafrost-thaw effects on Longyearbyen houses (dead at the query).

### 3.3 Sudbury and SNOLAB (Ontario, Canada)

### 3.4 Lead and the Sanford Underground Research Facility (South Dakota, US)
- **The mine below:** the former Homestake gold mine; >125 years of mining left >**600 km** of tunnels and shafts from the surface to over **8,000 ft**; **60** levels; donated to the state by Barrick in 2006; re-entry 2007; the Davis Campus on the **4850 Level** (4,300 m.w.e.). **SOURCED** (J. - **Access and services:** two primary shafts, Ross (houses the pumping system; refurbishment 2012–~2017) and Yates (renovated 2012; cage 1.4 × 2.7 × 3.8 m, max 10,000 lb; three science shifts a day); fresh air via the Oro Hondo and #5 Shaft fans, down Ross and Yates; redundant power, fiber and ventilation through both shafts; a 1,500 kVA substation; a standby diesel for up to 24 h for safety systems, including Yates sump pumps. **SOURCED** (Heise 2014). Lab staff maintain ~12 of >370 miles of workings. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet; the SURF overview page was **dead at the source**, 404).
- **What failed — flooding after abandonment:** pumping stopped **June 2003** and the mine flooded to a high-water mark of **1,381 m (4,530 ft)** below surface by August 2008; pumping resumed June 2008; below the 4850 Level by May 2009; held near the 6,000-ft level since April 2012; inflow ~**730 gpm**; pump stations with 700-hp pumps on the 1250, 2450, 3650 and 5000 Levels; surface treatment plant discharges ~2,000 gpm. **SOURCED** (Heise 2014).
- **Davis Campus:** excavated Sept 2009 – Jan 2011 (16,632 t of rock), $15.2M state and private; 927 m² of lab within 2,732 m²; certified for a maximum of **62 occupants** (July 2012). **SOURCED** (Heise 2014).

### 3.5 Wieliczka (Lesser Poland)
- **Official:** 9 levels, **245 km** of galleries, deepest point **327 m**; over 700 years, **26 shafts** and ~**9 million m³** of voids; only **2%** open to visitors; on the first UNESCO World Heritage List, 1978. **SOURCED** (Wieliczka Salt Mine, https://www.wieliczka-saltmine.com/about-the-mine). ### 4.1 Camp Century — the living layout (addendum to A2 §6.1)
- **Plan and buildings:** about two dozen tunnels in a "backbone with pairs of ribs" layout; the main communication trench as backbone, rib trenches for labs, quarters, recreation and storage; tunnels ~**7 m** wide, the four reactor trenches **12 m** wide; most buildings "about the size of a trailer home," assembled from prefabricated wall, floor, roof and door sections; **wooden foundations kept building heat from melting the snow below**. **SOURCED** (Earth Magazine, Jay R. Thompson, 2016-08-23, https://www.earthmagazine.org/article/benchmarks-october-1-1960-camp-century-cold-war-ice-fortress-built/).
- **Public life below:** mess hall, chapel, theater, dispensary, emergency room, hobby shop, recreation spaces, laboratories, crew quarters, storage. **SOURCED** (Earth Magazine 2016). Chapel held in the theater (608 sq ft); barber shop; exchange 500 sq ft; library 672 sq ft; ten-bed infirmary with operating room; "Main Street" over 1,000 ft. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets; DTIC AD0711862 not opened; Atomic Heritage page not re-opened).
- **How the layers connected:** **two ramped entrances** for people and machinery and **16 emergency escape hatches** straight up through the ice. **SOURCED** (Earth Magazine 2016).
- **Construction method and end:** Peter plows cut the tunnels; snow dumped onto steel arches that were removed after it hardened; regular trimming because the moving ice distorted the tunnels; reactor shut down 1963, removed 1964; summer-only from 1964; abandoned 1966; "No one has visited since," buildings likely being crushed. **SOURCED** (Earth Magazine 2016).

### 4.2 Camp Fistclench (Site 2, Greenland ice cap)
- **TIME, 16 September 1957:** "Fist Clench" (officially Site 2), ~200 miles east of Thule; "Only low chimney tops show above the snow"; trenches dug by a **13-ton Swiss-built Peter plow**; snow becomes solid ice about 15 ft down; **24 insulated Jamesway huts**, each 20 × 40 ft, set in trenches roofed with timber trusses and deeply covered with snow; ~**60** scientists and military to winter; residents would hardly feel 100-mph gales overhead; fuel is about three-quarters of the cargo sent to an Arctic base, so underground shelter was expected to save much of it. Roads: a 20-ft-deep trench roofed with curved corrugated metal, covered with snow, metal removed once the snow hardened, leaving a snow arch — ~100 miles of such road said to be under construction between Thule and Fist Clench. An ice warehouse near Thule: a chamber 65 ft square and 25 ft high, 150 ft below the surface and 500 ft back from the glacier face. Engineers hoped for ~10 years of use but "did not know how long they would last." **SOURCED** (TIME archive, https://time.com/archive/6612233/science-fist-clench-under-ice). The 100-mile under-snow road is a contemporary claim, not confirmed elsewhere — **UNVERIFIED as built**.
- **Thule veterans' site:** built 1955, occupied summers of 1958 and 1959; ~220 miles east of Thule; population 96–227; Jamesways within the under-snow complex housed 75 men; **five shallow tunnels** cut by the Peter miller; roofs of bolted timber bents with sheeting or corrugated steel, then covered by milled snow; area figures: quarters 50–102 sq ft per person, kitchen 384, dining 960, exchange 512, plus library, a theater that also hosted chapel services, a club; Jamesways "not thermally effective"; "As the surrounding snow warmed and new snow built up on the roof, the tunnel closed rapidly," the rigid timber roofs took large stresses and several bents cracked; lessons informed Camp Century. **SOURCED** (thuleab.dk, https://thuleab.dk/index.php/other-sites/camp-fistclench; a veterans'/enthusiasts' site, figures not traced to a primary report). Dates conflict (1955 vs May 1957 build; occupation 1957 vs 1958–59) — **not resolved**.

### 4.3 Camp TUTO and its ice tunnel (Greenland ice-cap edge)
- Army research camp 18 miles (29 km) east of Thule, **1954–1966**; built for the Polar Research and Development Center; ~**450** military personnel in September 1958, some seasonal; 2,000-ft runway, a gravel ramp >1 mile onto the ice cap, >100 aluminum-sandwich structures, some linked by tunnels; two doctors and a dentist. A **1,100-ft (340 m)** ice tunnel carved into the foot of the ice cap ~0.5 mile from camp, with temporary living quarters, diesel generators, interior **17 °F (−8 °C)**, officially opened October 1961; the ice camps suffered "gradual deformation, compression, and subsidence, requiring annual repair"; the ice ramp has since collapsed. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_TUTO, general references to NYT 1961, Wager 1962, Wright 1986; no inline citations). Ice mining in Tuto tunnels in the summers of 1955–1958; a self-contained **25-man camp** under construction in the tunnel with occupancy expected 1 February 1961. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets of an AIME paper and a CRREL report). *CRREL's First 25 Years* (nbi.ku.dk) and ERDC SR-44 were **dead at the source** (TLS errors).

### 4.4 New Byrd Station (Antarctica, 1961)
- Commissioned **13 February 1961**; "located underground except for some scientific structures," tunnels connecting facilities; trenches excavated in snow, timber sills on the abutments, roofed with corrugated steel arches, snow backfilled to the arch peak; built by Navy Seabees in 1960–61 and 1961–62 using a Peter snow miller; T-5 prefabricated buildings in the trenches; a 1962 newspaper: buildings "rest in these long tunnels, free from the heavy drifting which is crushing the old Byrd station"; "lasted ten years until it too succumbed to ice, snow and internal moisture." **UNVERIFIED** (snippets; Papers Past returned an empty page; openpolar.no certificate failure; DTIC AD0613051 "Undersnow structures: Byrd Station" not opened). ### 4.5 Neumayer I and II, then III (Ekström Ice Shelf, Antarctica)
- **The tube stations:** Georg von Neumayer Station (**1981**) and Neumayer Station (**1991/92**) were both tubular; "Annual snow accumulation of **80 cm** has buried the Neumayer Station about **15 m** beneath the snow surface"; "Ice movement and pressure have heavily deformed the steel tubes," so safe operation could not be guaranteed. **SOURCED** (IPY news item, 2009-02-20, https://www.ipy.org/news-a-announcements/item/2133-germanys-antarctic-neumayer-station-iii-starts-scientific-operation). Tube dimensions 50 × 7.5 m (1984 AWI report) vs ~90 × 8 m. **UNVERIFIED, conflict** (snippets).
- **The replacement (two layers by design):** a research and operations building on a platform **above** the ice, "connected to a **garage dug into the snow**"; 16 foundation slabs; the whole station raised hydraulically to offset accumulation; 68 × 26 m, 21 m above the ice; 4,473 m² usable (1,850 m² conditioned); 2,300 t; up to **40** people, **9** year-round; ~€40 million; 100 container modules over two summers; 25–30-year life; fully removable; three diesels, a standby generator, a 30 kW wind turbine. **SOURCED** (IPY 2009). Structurae adds: 16 hydraulic columns, annual lift **80–100 cm**, "about 8.20 m" depth under the station, garage and technical equipment in "a subsurface snow cavern at the front," 15 rooms sleeping 40, construction crew ~90, €39 million. **SOURCED** (https://structurae.net/en/structures/neumayer-station-iii). AWI anniversary page **dead at the source** (404); Helmholtz blog 403; TU Delft thesis TLS error.

### 4.6 Alert (Ellesmere Island, Nunavut, Canada)
- **Not under snow — a surface station of many buildings.** "Approximately **90 buildings**, with some **19 major structures** in the core complex," built 1960–1998, ~**22,500 m²**; a 5,500-ft gravel runway, roads, a quarry, landfills, fuel farms, labs, antenna farms; established September 1958; ~28 DND staff and 25 facilities contractors at any time (+ up to 10 sub-contractors), with 50–100 more in summer; "There are no medical doctors or dental care at CFS Alert," medevac decisions by the CF medical provider; weekly sustainment flights from 8 Wing Trenton, seasonal resupply (Op BOXTOP). **Snow:** "The majority of the snowstorms at CFS Alert are the result of **blowing snow, not falling snow**"; walkway and stair clearing must begin within 4 h of a storm's end or before 10 cm accumulates; "Historically, there are **40 events a year**"; priority buildings: three quarters blocks, an operations building, the HAPS center, the gymnasium. Water treatment plant **48,000 m³ a year**; "utility tunnels and cabling" listed among maintained systems. **SOURCED** (Government of Canada, CFS Alert statement of work, 2021, https://canadabuys.canada.ca/documents/pub/att/2021/04/20/994ff5f23c52a5e8976207d32d987f82/alert_sow_en.pdf, read via pdftotext). 817 km from the pole; peak >215 personnel, ~74 by 1997–98. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia snippets).

### 4.7 Jungfraujoch (Bernese Alps, Switzerland) — a station reached through rock
- **Railway inside the mountain:** concession 1894; groundbreaking 27 July 1896; Eigerwand station (2,865 m, inside the Eiger north face) 1903; Eismeer (~3,160 m) 1905; Jungfraujoch (**3,454 m**) reached only in **1912**, "nine years later than originally planned"; **7.6 km** of the route in tunnel through the Eiger and Mönch; max gradient 250‰; electric from the start (three-phase 1,125 V) with the company's own hydro rights; construction "marked by blasting accidents, strikes, and financial problems"; trip ~1¼ h in 1912, 25–35 min today. **SOURCED** (Jungfraubahnen, https://www.jungfrau.ch/de-ch/unternehmen/jungfraubahnen/jungfraubahn-holding-ag/jungfraubahn-ag/).
- **Research station:** 3,450 m between the Jungfrau and Mönch; the Sphinx laboratory at 3,580 m built **1937**; HFSJG founded 1930; "year-round accessibility." **SOURCED** (University of Bern CEP, https://www.climate.unibe.ch/services/services_of_cep/jungfraujoch_research_station/index_eng.html). "The highest research station in Europe that is accessible all year round by rail"; **ten bedrooms**, dining and recreation room, kitchen, library, workshop, "the custodians' apartments"; in cloud ~40% of the time. **SOURCED** (PSI, https://www.psi.ch/de/lac/jungfraujoch-site-old). Station reached "by a short walk from the railway station Jungfraujoch **through a tunnel**"; Sphinx reached by an elevator; research station inaugurated 1931. **UNVERIFIED** (HFSJG snippets; hfsjg.ch **dead at the source**, 403 ×2). ## 5 · Fortified and civil-defense underground cities (published facts only)

### 5.1 Swiss civil-defense shelters
- **Official principle:** "As a rule, every inhabitant in Switzerland should have a place in a shelter"; cantons can close gaps and plan for growth under the obligation to build shelters; types: public shelters, protected premises for management (command posts, rescue units, first-aid posts, hospitals), cultural-property shelters. **SOURCED** (Federal Office for Civil Protection, "Protective structures," 2025-10-28, https://www.babs.admin.ch/en/protective-structures).
- **Scale and practice:** ~**360,000** private underground shelters and ~**2,300** large public shelters; every resident has a place, with an oversupply; underground hospitals with over **50,000** protected beds; communes with enough places pay a replacement fee instead of building; placed end to end, the shelters would form a ~1,200 km tunnel; peacetime uses: wine cellars, hobby rooms, storage; civil-protection clause approved by vote 24 May 1959 (62.3%), federal office founded 1963; during the Schweizerhalle chemical fire (1 Nov 1986) "the shelters were not ready for use." **SOURCED** (Swiss National Museum blog, Jost Auf der Maur, 2022-04-11, mod. 2025-12-17, https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2022/04/shelters-a-swiss-phenomenon/). Replacement fee SFr1,500 per place, two places per three rooms; ~370,000 shelters, ~9 million places. **UNVERIFIED** (swissinfo snippets).
- **Sonnenberg, Lucerne — the largest, and what failed:** two motorway tunnels (~1.5 km) and a **seven-story** cavern between them, completed **October 1976**, SFr 40 million (federally subsidized road-and-shelter project), each tunnel for **10,000** people (about a third of the city); blast doors **1.5 m** thick, **350 t**, designed for a one-megaton burst within 1 km; the cavern held a command post, an emergency hospital with operating theater, a radio studio, multipurpose rooms. **Operation Ant (1987):** a dry run of sheltering a large part of the city — "The doors did not shut properly," and "other shortcomings were never fixed for financial reasons." In **2002** the city decided to disengage the tunnels' protective rooms; the cavern stays a shelter for **2,000**; largest Swiss shelters now 2,000–3,000; ~270,000 other shelters covering 95% of the population (2006). **SOURCED** (swissinfo, 2006-09-02, https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/lucerne-says-goodbye-to-the-cold-war/685910). The Local adds: a staff of **700** to run the logistics center; a prison and a switchboard room; the kitchen served only staff and patients, so residents had to bring **two weeks** of ready-to-eat food; showers only for hospital patients, ~**20 toilets** shared per block; "it would take **two weeks** to close the tunnels" and set up before the public could arrive; water supply limited occupancy to ~two weeks; hospital upkeep 250,000 francs/yr; downsized **2006**. **SOURCED** (The Local, 2016-10-20, https://www.thelocal.ch/20161020/inside-switzerlands-largest-nuclear-bunker-40-years-on). The museum blog: Sonnenberg "fails to fulfill its intended purpose because planners ignored everyday human needs." **SOURCED** (Swiss National Museum). Downsizing date 2002 decision vs 2006 — both stated; reassessed capacity 10,000–17,000 (Structurae). **UNVERIFIED**.

### 5.2 Moscow "Metro-2" (as reported only)

### 5.3 Beijing Underground City (Dixia Cheng) and the underground residents
- **The tunnel city:** built **1969–1979** on Mao's order during the Sino-Soviet border conflict; >300,000 citizens dug it, some sections without machinery; ~**85 km²** under central Beijing, **8–18 m** down (full extent undisclosed); ~**90** entrances hidden in shops along Qianmen streets, many later demolished; claimed capacity all six million residents; restaurants, clinics, schools, theaters, factories, a roller rink, grain and oil stores, a mushroom farm; ~70 possible well sites; **2,300 ventilation shafts** that could be sealed, gas- and water-proof hatches, thick concrete gates; later cheap hotels, shops, business centers; opened to tourists 2000, closed for renovation since at least February 2008 after a safety inspection. **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia, citing CNN 2008, China Internet Information Center 2005, China Daily 2005; globalsecurity.org **dead at the source**, 402).
- **People living below (basements and air-raid shelters):** an estimated **one million** migrant workers in ~**6,000** basements and shelters (about a third of the city's underground space); waiters, hostesses, hairdressers, chefs, guards, domestic workers; rent **300–700 yuan** a month for **7–8 m²** partitioned rooms, sometimes barely wider than a bed; **50–100 rooms** share one bathroom; no windows, chilly drafts, damp and moldy in summer; basements leased for housing from the 1990s, rules loosened 2004, mid-December **2010** regulations effectively stopped lease renewals, with closure planned over three years ("unsafe, dirty and chaotic"). **SOURCED** (Sim Chi Yin, "The Rat Tribe," VII Magazine 2012-07-07 / ChinaFile 2012-10-16, https://www.chinafile.com/node/9226). From 2015, >**120,000** evicted; dwellers occupied 13% of Beijing's underground space (Civil Defense Bureau official); ~150,000 in air-defense shelters plus ~800,000 in ordinary basements; 2017: ~400 people found in a basement owned by a district Civil Defense Bureau. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets; Quartz 403, Shanghai Daily login wall — **dead at the source**). Population figures (one million vs two million vs 950,000) — **conflict, not resolved**.

### 5.4 Singapore's underground planning
- **Official (URA Master Plan 2025, updated 2026-07-27):** rail, road, water, sewage and electricity lines placed underground "to lessen effects on the surface"; **Common Services Tunnels** and utility-specific ducts consolidate utilities with spare capacity; shallower basements for pedestrian links, retail, car parks, utilities; deeper basements for MRT stations and water reclamation plants, with "fire safety" named as a challenge to expanding uses; deep-rock caverns: the **Mandai Underground Ammunition Facility** and **Jurong Rock Caverns**; a cavern for aggregate storage at Gali Batu being explored; Bidadari Underground Service Reservoir and Stamford Detention Tank; a 3D map of as-built structures and piles; feasibility studies on underground battery energy storage. **SOURCED** (https://www.ura.gov.sg/land-planning/master-plan/master-plan-2025/themes/strengthening-urban-resilience/an-underground-of-possibilities/). 3D underground maps piloted for Marina Bay, Jurong Innovation District and Punggol Digital District. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet).
- **Law on who owns the layers:** State Lands (Amendment) Act 2015 — land includes only so much subterranean space as is reasonably necessary, either as specified in the State title or "**30 m below Singapore Height Datum**"; Land Acquisition (Amendment) Act 2015 "allows government purchase of specific layers of underground space below private land." **SOURCED** (Zhou Yingxin, DSTA, "Advances and Challenges in Underground Space Use in Singapore," SRMEG 2017-01-14, after ACUUS 2016, https://srmeg.org.sg/images/Advances_and_challenges_(SRMEG_Networking_nite_2017).pdf, read via pdftotext). Basements typically to ~15 m. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet).
- **Figures (same slides):** Jurong Rock Caverns, S$950 million, opened September 2014, caverns **130 m beneath the seabed**, **1.5 million m³** storage, land saved **60 ha**; DTSS Phase I: **48 km** of tunnels Kranji–Changi, an underground water reclamation plant of **800,000 m³/day**, a 5 km sea outfall, ~60 km of link sewers; Phase II before 2030; Tengah new town with a car-free town center and traffic running underneath, 30,000 public + 12,000 private units (Straits Times 2016-09-09); a studied underground warehousing facility at Tanjong Kling: **65 caverns**, 568,200 m² GFA, **110 m** below ground, ramp access. **SOURCED** (Zhou 2017). Depth 150 m (other press). **UNVERIFIED, conflict**.

- **Cities that put services below and keep people above:** Helsinki (utility tunnels, 300 km of lines, wastewater plant, depots, service street, parking; plus swimming hall, playground, sports halls, karting, SOURCED, §2.3); Singapore (utilities, transport, storage and water below; shallower basements for links, retail, parking; a car-free town center with traffic underneath, SOURCED, §5.4); Montreal and Toronto (shops, food, offices, metro links below; Montreal ~1,600 housing units in connected buildings, SOURCED/UNVERIFIED, §2.1–2.2).
- **Clinics and medical:** Swiss protected hospitals >50,000 beds (SOURCED, §5.1); Alert has no doctor or dentist, medevac by decision of the CF medical provider (SOURCED, §4.6); Camp TUTO two doctors and a dentist (UNVERIFIED, §4.3); Camp Century dispensary and emergency room (SOURCED, §4.1).

### 6.3 Ventilation and air
- Passive vertical shafts: Coober Pedy (oxygen in, moisture out, SOURCED, §1.1); Derinkuyu (main shaft + >50 more, SOURCED, §1.3); Beijing (2,300 sealable shafts, UNVERIFIED, §5.3); Naours (smoke routed through cottages, chimneys built so smoke dispersed unseen, SOURCED, §1.5).
- Mechanical ventilation and filtration: SURF (fresh air via the Oro Hondo and #5 Shaft fans, down two shafts, SOURCED, §3.4); Swedish shelters (air filters and diesel generators being replaced, 2–3 years per upgrade, SOURCED, §2.4); Helsinki (ventilation must keep working in fire, SOURCED, §2.3).
- Heat removal at depth: Mponeng (rock to 60 °C, >6,000 t of ice slurry, giant fans, SOURCED, §3.6); Katarinaberget (200,000 kg of ice stored for cooling, SOURCED, §2.4); SNOLAB ("warm and humid"; pressure +25–30%, SOURCED, §3.3).
- Too much heat underground melts the host: Svalbard Seed Vault (heat sources removed from the access tunnel; plant moved to a separate service building "to avoid unwanted heating," SOURCED, §3.2); Camp Century (wooden foundations kept building heat from melting the snow, SOURCED, §4.1).
- Oxygen added to rooms at altitude: GB/T 35414-2017 (SOURCED, §4.8); Lhasa hotels (UNVERIFIED, §4.8).
- Bad air as a living condition: Beijing basements (no windows, drafts, damp and mold, SOURCED, §5.3).

### 6.4 Light
- Courtyards and open fronts: Matmata pit courtyards (§1.2); yaodong cliff fronts and sunken courtyards "for light and ventilation" (UNVERIFIED, §1.4); Setenil, where one quarter is lit by sunlight reflected off the cliff and the other always shaded (SOURCED, §1.6).
- No daylight: Beijing basement rooms, no windows (SOURCED, §5.3); Naours (electric lighting now, torch advised, SOURCED, §1.5).
- **Perception without windows** (new source): Edit Nagy, *Office Employees' Psychological Reactions to Their Underground and Above Ground Offices*, BRI Research Paper No. 138 (Japan, February 1994): 108 employees (22 underground, 86 above); underground workers wanted windows more strongly; rated lighting "significantly more negatively" though measured lighting was "very similar"; rated their offices "less pleasant, less stimulating, and more enclosed" (p = 0.000); the author attributes this to knowing one is underground without daylight more than to physical conditions. **SOURCED** (https://www.kenken.go.jp/english/contents/publications/paper/138.html). A Singapore cohort (Dunleavy et al., *Building and Environment*, 2020; 329 workers, 12 months, GHQ-12) found no association between working underground and psychological distress after full adjustment, while perceived indoor environmental quality and long hours were associated; Dunleavy's 2021 thesis reports an association with distress and worse perceived sleep. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets; Keele repository 403, Maastricht page without the text) — **conflict, not resolved**. Carmody & Sterling, "Design Strategies to Alleviate Negative Psychological and Physiological Effects in Underground Space": title and abstract fragment only. **UNVERIFIED**.

### 6.5 Fire
- Planning rules: Helsinki (automatic surveillance, alarms, extinguishing; smoke exhaust and fire compartmentation; addressable exits, SOURCED, §2.3); Singapore names fire safety as a challenge to wider underground use (SOURCED, §5.4).
- Events: Kaprun 2000 (§6.1, SOURCED); Old Pole garage fire 1964 and Vostok powerhouse fire 1982 are in A2 §2.1 and §6.4, not repeated.
- **NOT FOUND:** a fire record for Montreal RÉSO, Toronto PATH, Coober Pedy dugouts, or the Jungfraujoch (searched only for the Jungfraujoch; dead at the query).

### 6.6 Water and flood
- Flood from the surface into a below-grade space: Matmata 1969 (22 days of rain, many homes collapsed, UNVERIFIED, §1.2); Coober Pedy 2014 (115 mm in 24 h, UNVERIFIED, §1.1); Svalbard Seed Vault access tunnel 2016 (SOURCED, §3.2); Wieliczka 1992 (aquifer water and rock into Level IV, surface subsidence >2 m, SOURCED, §3.5); Homestake after pumps stopped in 2003 (flooded to 1,381 m below surface by 2008, SOURCED, §3.4).
- **Zhengzhou, 20 July 2021** (new source): **14** died on Metro Line 5 after temporary fences at a rail yard over 3 km from the nearest station collapsed and floodwater entered the tunnel; the yard had been built 30 m from its approved site in a low-lying area with poorly built fences; the driver reversed further down the tunnel, which worsened the passengers' position; >400 commuters trapped; **6** drowned in the Jingguang North Road tunnel, where **247** cars were submerged and the tunnel was closed only at 16:16 with water already surging in; the State Council report found many public facilities and underground tunnels unprepared and many spillways obstructed. **SOURCED** (Sixth Tone, summary of the State Council investigation report released January 2022, https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1009524).
- Water storage and handling designed in: Singapore (Bidadari reservoir, Stamford Detention Tank, DTSS, SOURCED, §5.4); Helsinki (water management facilities underground, SOURCED, §2.3); Derinkuyu wells to the water table (SOURCED, §1.3).

### 6.7 What happened when the layer below failed or was abandoned
- **Ground moves under a town:** Kiruna (deformation from mining; town center and church moved; "deformations would continue for many years" even if mining stopped, SOURCED, §3.1); Lead (1930s buildings moved uphill; subsidence area now in the Open Cut, UNVERIFIED, §3.4); Wieliczka (subsidence >2 m after the 1992 inflow, SOURCED, §3.5).
- **Rock bursts:** Kiruna 2020 (Mw 4.2 at ~1,146 m; evacuation of 13; months of reduced production, SOURCED, §3.1).
- **Ice and snow close the space:** Fistclench (tunnel closed rapidly, timber bents cracked, SOURCED (veterans' site), §4.2); TUTO ice camps (annual repair, UNVERIFIED, §4.3); Neumayer I/II (buried ~15 m, steel tubes "heavily deformed," replaced, SOURCED, §4.5); Camp Century (abandoned 1966, buildings likely being crushed, SOURCED, §4.1); New Byrd ("succumbed to ice, snow and internal moisture" after about ten years, UNVERIFIED, §4.4). (A2 holds Old Pole, the Dome, the arches, Halley.)
- **Shelters not ready when needed:** Sonnenberg (doors did not shut properly in the 1987 exercise; defects not fixed for cost; two weeks to make ready, water for two weeks, SOURCED, §5.1); Schweizerhalle 1986 (shelters not ready, SOURCED, §5.1); Oslo 2023 audit (most shelters not available as required, no maintenance routines, risk of missing the 72-hour deadline, SOURCED, §2.4); Sweden (budget cannot repair all 64,000, SOURCED, §2.4).
- **Below-ground housing withdrawn by policy:** Beijing (lease renewals stopped from December 2010; >120,000 evicted from 2015, SOURCED/UNVERIFIED, §5.3); Matmata (1960s–70s new surface towns, depopulation, heavy rain, SOURCED, §1.2); yaodong (population falling with urbanization, UNVERIFIED, §1.4).
- **Abandoned layers reused:** Derinkuyu and Kaymaklı as museums (Kaymaklı opened 1964, SOURCED, §1.3); Naours as a museum from 1949 (SOURCED, §1.5); Homestake as SURF from 2007 (SOURCED, §3.4); Pyhäsalmi as Callio, with a planned gravity battery (SOURCED, §3.7); Swiss shelters as wine cellars and hobby rooms (SOURCED, §5.1); Katarinaberget and Igeldamm as car parks (SOURCED, §2.4); Beijing tunnels as hotels and shops (UNVERIFIED, §5.3).
