- **Population (figures differ by source and method; recorded as found):**

  - **643,513** inhabitants as of April 2026 "per municipality data" (likely the municipal register of declared residents, a different count from the statistical estimate). **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Demographics of Vilnius" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Vilnius). - **Defining features found in sources:**
  1. **The Old Town (UNESCO, inscribed 1994).** **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Vilnius". Criteria (ii) and (iv): "an outstanding example of a medieval foundation which exercised a profound influence on architectural and cultural developments in a wide area of Eastern Europe" and "a Central European town which evolved organically over a period of five centuries"; core Old Town **74 quarters, 70 streets and lanes, 1,487 buildings**; inscribed property **352 ha**; often described as the largest baroque old town in Central and Eastern Europe. **UNVERIFIED** (search snippet; the UNESCO page returned 403).

Before WWII and the Holocaust, "one of Europe's most important Jewish centers." **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Vilnius".
  3. **Green cover.** "**61%** of its territory as green" (forests, parks, grass meadows). **SOURCED**, EU Covenant of Mayors, "Making European Green Capital: Vilnius' journey" (https://eu-mayors.ec.europa.eu/en/news/making-european-green-capital-vilnius-journey-and-what-other-cities-can-learn). ### D. How the defining features shaped what exists

Stated only as the sources state them:
- The many changes of rule and population left places of worship for many confessions (Catholic, Orthodox, Jewish, Karaite) within the Old Town; a theater founded by Poles in 1913, used as the Russian Drama Theater, renamed in 2022; Polish- and Russian-language newspapers; a Polish culture house. **SOURCED** (see C).
- The Holocaust destroyed the Jewish population (about 7,000 of ~70,000 survived), and Soviet authorities destroyed the old Jewish cemetery above Užupis; the neglected, emptied Užupis quarter was taken over by artists after 1990. **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Užupis" and "Ponary massacre".
- The Soviet period built the micro-districts where ~55% of residents live. **SOURCED**, True Lithuania.
- Post-Soviet reuse of Soviet-era industrial and penal buildings for culture is visible in the city's own nightlife guide: LOFTAS (former Elfa electronics plant), Lukiškės prison 2.0, Utopija (former jail), Dūmų fabrikas (former scythe factory), Kablys. **SOURCED**, Go Vilnius nightlife page.
- The forest share (61% green) is the basis of the Green Capital 2025 award; the Song Celebration's 2024 theme was the forest. **SOURCED**.

### A. Snapshot

- **Location / what it is:** capital of the State of Texas, on the Colorado River (north bank, per the founding account). Established **1839**, first incorporated as "Waterloo"; Mirabeau B. Lamar, then Vice President of the Republic of Texas, recommended the site after a buffalo hunt, citing "the area's hills, waterways, and pleasant surroundings." **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Austin, Texas" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas).
- **Population:** **961,855** at the 2020 census; metro ~**2.55 million**; city area **326.51 sq mi**. **SOURCED**, Wikipedia. City internal estimate for 2024 **1,035,002**; metro (Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos) **2,550,637** on **4,219.8 sq mi**, 25th-largest US metro as of July 2024. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets of a City of Austin document and Census Reporter; census.gov QuickFacts returned 403).

### D. How the defining features shaped what exists

- The live-music identity produced public money for musicians (the hotel-tax Live Music Fund), a formal cultural district for venues (Red River), live music inside the airport terminal (1,087 performances in 2025), and very large festivals (SXSW, ACL) whose impact is measured and reported each year; ACL revenue funds city parks. **SOURCED** (see B/C).
- Demand for the fund outstrips supply (137 grants for 1,000+ applicants) and housing costs rose steeply, with musicians reported moving to outlying towns. **SOURCED** (fund) / **UNVERIFIED** (Music Census moves).
- The university produced long-running student-born traditions (Eeyore's Birthday since 1963) and the Harry Ransom Center and Blanton collections. **SOURCED**.
- "Keep Austin Weird" was coined in **2000** by Red Wassenich during a pledge drive on KOOP Radio's "The Lounge Show"; bumper stickers from July 2000; adopted by the Austin Independent Business Alliance; about two years later Waterloo Records and BookPeople paired it with "Support Local Business" while opposing a proposed Borders store at Sixth and Lamar; the phrase was trademarked by "Outhouse Designs" despite Wassenich's challenge; copied by Portland (2003), Louisville (2005), Indianapolis (2013); the 2010 book *Weird City* traces the culture partly to cheap housing after the 1980s bust and to UT. **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Keep Austin Weird".
- The 1928 plan concentrated Black (and Mexican American) Austin in East Austin, which is where the Victory Grill (East 11th St.) and the Carver Museum (Angelina St., 78702) are; current gentrification there is documented in price and population figures. **SOURCED** (KUT 2023; Carver page) / **UNVERIFIED** (Mexican American relocation).
- A bridge renovation (TPWD: bats arrived "in the early 1980s, shortly after it was renovated"; the year 1980 and the crevice explanation are from snippets, **UNVERIFIED**) preceded the colony; the colony is now a tourist draw with a newspaper-named viewing area and boat tours. **SOURCED** (TPWD).
