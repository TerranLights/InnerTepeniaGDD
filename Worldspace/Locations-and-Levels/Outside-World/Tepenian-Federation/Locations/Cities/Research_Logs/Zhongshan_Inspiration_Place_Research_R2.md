### A. Other settings (neighborhoods, workplaces, outdoor spaces, off-season)

- **Žvėrynas** (old villa quarter on the west bank of the Neris): **2.6 km²**; **14,004** residents (2023), **11,079** (2011 census); a Grand Ducal hunting reserve, later Radziwiłł menagerie (hence the name); subdivided by Russian businessman Vasiliy Martinson at the end of the 19th c.; incorporated into Vilnius **1901** as a resort town; "more than a hundred" wooden cottages and villas, mostly **1890–1910**, plus interwar brick villas and a **1960s** modernist quarter of **16 villas built for Lithuanian musicians**; holds Vilnius's only Karaite **kenesa** (completed **1923**), the Orthodox Church of Our Lady of the Sign (1899–1903), an Orthodox chapel (1871), an unfinished Catholic church (stopped by WWI), Žvėrynas Bridge (1907), the Sholem Aleichem Gymnasium, embassies; "After Lithuania regained its independence in 1990, Žvėrynas became one of the most prestigious neighborhoods" (near the Seimas, Vingis Park). **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Žvėrynas" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDv%C4%97rynas).
- **Šnipiškės wooden houses, nicknamed "Shanghai":** one- and two-story wooden houses of the late 19th–early 20th c. standing among glass high-rises of the business center; "some older homes featuring outdoor facilities" (outdoor toilets); long-term residents, mostly elderly, intend to stay (one woman on Giedraičių Street since **1968**, now with grandchildren and great-grandchildren); land over **€100,000 per are** ("per acre" in the English text, a likely mistranslation of *aras*, 100 m²; recorded as given); 58 municipal properties auctioned the previous year (€36 to €131,000); analyst Šarūnas Tarutis (Citus): "there are many co-owners. The houses themselves have virtually no value"; two municipal heritage sites, **Skansenas** and **Piromontas**; residents call it a disgrace or a heritage; municipality planned community engagement in 2024. **SOURCED**, LRT, 7 Apr 2024 (https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2242991/disgrace-or-heritage-a-unique-district-dubbed-shanghai-of-vilnius). In the 17th–18th c. Šnipiškės was a ceramics district (clay and limestone quarries, brick and tile kilns). **UNVERIFIED** (snippet).
- **Naujoji Vilnia** (railway and factory district, east): **42,545** inhabitants on **39.3 km²** (2023); Lithuanians **29.5% → 47.9%** and Poles **34.2% → 26.4%** from 2001 to 2021 census; grew in the 19th c. as a strip along the Warsaw–St Petersburg and Libau–Romny railways; **10,300** inhabitants by **1903**; the **Possehl scythe factory** (founded **1882** by Emil Possehl) made "1 million scythes a year" by 1886 and over 3 million a year before WWI; after WWII factories for machine tools and agricultural equipment; current construction booms in Pavilnys and Kalnėnai. **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Naujoji Vilnia" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naujoji_Vilnia). Street names recall railway trades (Šaltkalvių "locksmiths," Konduktorių "conductors," Garvežių "steam locomotives," Iešmininkų "pointsmen," Darbininkų "laborers"); "always been known as a settlement of railway and factory workers"; in **May 1991** pro-Soviet Yedinstvo politicians proclaimed a "Polish National-Territorial Region" with its "capital" at Naujoji Vilnia. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets of Neakivaizdinis Vilnius / Wikipedia). (R1's Dūmų fabrikas brewery sits in the old scythe factory here.)
- **Kirtimai Roma settlement ("tabor"), southern edge of the city:** about **6 ha** in three sections; a 2005 municipal count of **511** residents (146 children, 365 adults); **115** homes (2009); origin traced to **1956**, when Soviet authorities required Roma to register a residence and take jobs, with one legal dormitory-type building for eight families and later houses never legalized; drug selling from about the mid-1990s; **281** ambulance calls for intravenous drug use in **2017**, 22 of them deaths; Vilnius Roma Integration Plan from **2016**; residents began demolishing homes in **March 2020**, most structures removed by **August 2020**; **56 of 93** evicted families housed or on rent compensation (cap **€92.50** per person per month vs one-room rents of €210–480, Sept 2020); Institute for Ethnic Studies surveys: over 60% opposed Roma neighbors (2019), nearly 70% would not rent to Roma (2015); Svetlana Novopolskaja directs the Roma Community Center. **SOURCED**, Al Jazeera, 14 Dec 2020 (https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2020/12/14/lithuanias-roma-struggle-to-find-new-homes-after-city-eviction). By 2024: "We have lost our united and active community" (Kristina Saidova, who lived five years with a child and husband in 15 m² temporary social housing); sociologist Agnieška Avin notes the city had named streets in the settlement and sent utility bills there; the city had provided **60** social housing units and **47** rent subsidies to Roma families, with **90** more waiting (city-wide waiting list **1,606**). **SOURCED**, LRT, 21 Jan 2024 (https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2174634/we-lost-our-community-for-nothing-young-roma-laments-demolition-of-vilnius-tabor).
- **Collective gardens (*sodai*, *sodų bendrijos*):** Soviet-era garden plots where city dwellers could keep small plots and build simple structures; described in research as a space to "compensate the suppressed need for private property" and for informal practices; in Vilnius they have been turning into residential zones for over a decade without becoming a typical neighborhood "from legal or practical perspective"; houses rebuilt by owners "Do it yourself." **UNVERIFIED** (abstracts seen in search results: VILNIUS TECH journal *Mokslas – Lietuvos ateitis*, 2020, and Matas Šiupšinskas's dissertation at Vilnius University; not opened).
- **Outdoor water:** **12** public beaches listed by LRT (28 Jun 2024): Lake Balsys (Green Lakes), Žirmūnai (Neris), Valakampiai 1 and 2 (Neris; Valakampiai 2 "has a separate nudist area"), Lake Balžis 1 and 2, Lake Tapeliai (for recreational fishing), Salotė, Gilužis, Vingis Park (pet-friendly), Dvarčionys, Grigiškės; Naujoji Vilnia beach listed but swimming not recommended; facilities: changing rooms, picnic tables, free barbecues, floating piers, volleyball/basketball courts. **SOURCED**, LRT (https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2306755/surviving-the-heat-12-public-beaches-in-vilnius). Go Vilnius (18 Jun 2026) gives **15** public beaches, Blue Flag at Balsys, Valakampiai I and II for a third year, lifeguards **8 a.m.–8 p.m.**, Žirmūnai reachable by river passenger boat. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet). The counts differ by source and year; both recorded.
- **Off-season (winter):** the city counts **25 January 1323** (Gediminas's letter) as its birthday and opens the year with the **Vilnius Light Festival** (late January; 5th edition in 2023 opened the 700th anniversary, 25–28 Jan; "77 projects from 16 countries over four years," "over 200K spectators each time"). **UNVERIFIED** (Go Vilnius snippets, Jan 2023). Sauna use is mainly a cold-season habit (see C).

### B. Subcultures

- **Poles of Vilnius and the Vilnius region:** **85,438** Poles in the city (15.4%) and **45,020** in the surrounding Vilnius District Municipality (**46.8%**), 2021 census; students in Polish-language schools ~11,400 (1990), over 22,300 (2001), 18,392 (2005); Polish-language general schools **75** plus **52** mixed-language (2003), down to **49** and **41** (2011); Związek Polaków na Litwie (Association of Poles in Lithuania) founded **April 1989**; Electoral Action of Poles in Lithuania – Christian Families Alliance (founded **1994**; 4.97% in 2020, 3.88% in 2024 Seimas elections); many who speak a Belarusian vernacular identify as Polish through Catholic faith ("locally perceived as the 'Polish faith'"). **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Poles in Lithuania" (first 100,000 characters read).
  - **Language:** *po prostu* / *mowa prosta* ("simple speech"), an uncodified Belarusian-based vernacular; *polszczyzna wileńska*, a mixed Polish-based urban variety "commonly used by youth in Vilnius." **UNVERIFIED** (abstract of a Lithuanistika article, seen in search). **Tutejszy** ("from here," "local") as a self-label persisted longest in the Vilnius region; in **1989**, among people with Polish nationality in passports, **4%** called themselves *tuteišiai*, 84% Polish. **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Tutejszy". Vilnius University's Krystyna Rutkowska (Center of Polish Studies) on local Polish: Lithuanian institutional loanwords such as *seniūnija* (local administrative ward), *raštas* (document), *mokesčiai* (taxes), older ones such as *kumpiak* (ham, from Lithuanian *kumpis*); phonetic features such as *mówia* for *mówię*; younger people use a mixed "sociolect." **SOURCED**, EFHR, 7 Sep 2016 (https://media.efhr.eu/2016/09/07/rutkowska-polish-language-lithuania-used-kumpiak-now-seniunija). (The fetch summary garbled some glosses; only the four examples above are recorded.)
  - **All Saints' / All Souls' (1–2 Nov) as a Polish-community act:** on **1 Nov 2025** the Social Committee for the Care of the Old Rasos Cemetery ran "Light of Remembrance for Rasos and Bernardine Cemeteries": more than **13,000** candles at Rasos, Bernardine and Antakalnis cemeteries, lit by Polish community members, pupils, scouts and Polish diplomats; the Polish Embassy gave **1,000** red-and-white candles for Antakalnis, which holds **1,626** graves of soldiers from the 1919–1920 fighting for Vilnius; Piłsudski's mausoleum (his heart) at Rasos. **SOURCED**, Polish Radio (https://www.polskieradio.pl/395/7785/Artykul/3602066,13000-candles-light-up-vilnius-cemeteries-in-tribute-to-shared-history).
- **Lithuanian Tatars (Muslim):** brought to Vilnius by Grand Duke Vytautas from Crimean campaigns; settled **Lukiškės** (legend: the land's size set by the flight of two arrows); about **7,000** Tatars in the Grand Duchy in the early 16th c., about **200** in Lukiškės; occupations: carters and porters, gardening, trade and moneylending, military service, translation (Arabic); Lukiškės wooden mosque first documented **1558**, demolished **1968**, cemetery destroyed in the Soviet period; Mečetės (Mosque) and Totorių (Tatar) streets keep the memory. **SOURCED**, LDK istorija, "Tatars in Old Vilnius: From Warriors to Carters" (https://www.ldkistorija.lt/?p=10568). Nemėžis mosque just outside the city (built 1684, rebuilt 1909, restored 1993; one of four working mosques in Lithuania); Vilnius Muftiate founded **28 Dec 1925**. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets of Wikipedia "Nemėžis"/"Islam in Lithuania").
- **Belarusian exiles:** European Humanities University (EHU) moved to Vilnius after Belarus withdrew its license in **2004**; housed in a former Augustinian monastery in the Old Town; Belarus's Supreme Court declared EHU "extremist" on **14 Apr 2026**; at least **60** homes of affiliated people raided in Belarus since June 2026 (Viasna); Belarusian share of students fell from **95% to 74%**; a student: "Most of us simply don't know what to do." **SOURCED**, Novaya Gazeta Europe, Iryna Khalip, 2 Jul 2026 (https://novayagazeta.eu/en/articles/2026/07/02/most-of-us-simply-dont-know-what-to-do-en). EHU enrollment 1,850, about 700 on campus. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet). Belarusian community cafes and centers in Vilnius: **NOT FOUND** (see F).
- **Ukrainians since 2022:** the **Ukrainian Center** in Vilnius opened **11 Jun 2022** (premises from Vytautas Magnus University; led by former ambassador Algirdas Kumža; initiative of Olena Zelenska and Diana Nausėdienė): education, career guidance, psychological help, recreation, concerts; Lithuania had taken in over 50,000 Ukrainians by then. **SOURCED**, Ukrinform (https://ukrinform.net/rubric-society/3504945-eus-first-ukrainian-center-for-displaced-persons-opens-in-vilnius.html). The city library system opened Lithuanian courses for Ukrainians and a Ukrainian literature shelf (June 2022). **SOURCED**, VCB 2022 report (see D). International Ukrainian School (from 2022) and Kharkiv school "Gravity" in Vilnius; 9,640 Ukrainian schoolchildren registered in Lithuania. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets).
- **Roma:** see A (Kirtimai).
- **Artists of Užupis (beyond R1):** founders **Romas Lileikis** and **Tomas Čepaitis**; the "parliament" meets at **Užupio kavinė**, nicknamed the "Barliament"; the republic once had "a navy of four boats and army of ten"; "over 300 ambassadors"; the **Art Incubator** (converted factory on the riverbank) has **40** studios at low rent; rising prices pushing locals out. **SOURCED**, Euronews, 3 May 2022 (https://www.euronews.com/2022/05/03/inside-uzupis-europe-s-independent-art-republic-with-its-own-constitution). Go Vilnius: Art Incubator est. **2002**; Jonas Mekas Visual Arts Center; Vilnius Potters' Guild; "Užupis 1 point" info center ("Curiosity Room"); constitution of **41 articles** in "more than 50 languages." **SOURCED**, Go Vilnius (https://www.govilnius.lt/uzupis-en). Counts conflict across sources (R1: 38 articles, 23 or 40+ translations; Euronews: 43 languages; Go Vilnius: 41 articles, 50+ languages); all recorded, none resolved.
- **LGBTQ+:** Baltic Pride rotates among the three Baltic capitals since **2010**; Vilnius's first march **2010** (about **400** marchers, UNVERIFIED snippet), about 10,000 in 2019 (UNVERIFIED); **2025**: organized by the Lithuanian Gay League (LGL; leader Vladimir Simonko), 2–8 June, march on Gediminas Avenue on **7 June**, slogan "Free to Be (Yourself)," ~**20,000** expected, all events free. **SOURCED**, LRT, 25 Feb 2025 (https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2496514/vilnius-to-host-6th-baltic-pride-festival-expects-20-000-participants). 20,000 actually attended. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet).
- **School-leaver generation:** *Šimtadienis* (100 days before final exams, Feb–March), one of the three major events of the last school year; began at Kaunas Aušra gymnasium in **1926**; a formal part in the school hall (graduates recite, sing, perform) and an informal evening; since the 21st c. the informal part has moved to restaurants, cafes and nightclubs, and certificates have given way to humorous videos that "ridicule their classmates." **SOURCED**, Justė Vasilionytė-Stašaitienė, *Tradicija ir dabartis* (2016), abstract at https://www.lituanistika.lt/content/68153.
- **Basketball fans:** Rytas (Vilnius) vs Žalgiris (Kaunas) is the main domestic rivalry, "some dozen times a season" across leagues; basketball "the second religion of Lithuania"; leagues LKL, NKL (minor pro), RKL (largely amateur third tier). **SOURCED**, True Lithuania (https://www.truelithuania.com/basketball-in-lithuania-33). Post-game shouting and some fights between fans outside arenas. **UNVERIFIED** (Baltic Times snippet).

### C. Leisure industries

- **Tourism:** about **2.2 million** nights in Vilnius accommodation (2023, Statista); most arrivals from neighboring countries (Belarus, Poland, Latvia named). **UNVERIFIED** (snippets; Statista pages are paywalled).
- **Hot-air ballooning:** described as one of "just a few European capitals allowing hot air balloons to fly above its historical center" (Lithuania Travel, **SOURCED**, https://lithuania.travel/en/what-to-do/active-recreation/hot-air-balloons); flights at dawn or in the evening, peak in summer; five operators listed (orobalionai.lt, ballooning.lt, hotairballoon.lt, justballoons.lt, balloon.lt). Other sources say "the only capital in Europe"; christening of first-time flyers "involving champagne and a smidge of dirt." **UNVERIFIED** (snippets). The two claims differ; recorded.
- **Saunas (*pirtis*):** Go Vilnius (16 Jan 2025) names: **Ivãnas Muša Gongã** (center, sauna masters, couples' nude sessions), **Vytautas Muša Gongã** (igloo-shaped sauna with cold bath in the courtyard of the former **Lukiškės Prison**, used 1904–2019), **PACAI SPA** (17th-c. mansion; "Lithuanian sauna ritual, enhanced with seaside sand"), **Bokšto SPA** (Old Town, 25 m pool, 15th–17th-c. cellars), **Pilaitė Mill bathhouses** (three bathhouses by a 1923 wooden mill; three-hour sessions with honey and salt); "around 40,000 saunas across the country." **SOURCED**, Go Vilnius (https://www.govilnius.lt/cold-vilnius-hot-saunas). Tradition: whisks (*vantos*) of birch or oak, salt, about four rounds, temperatures under 70 °C recommended; historically women ran bathhouses and births took place there; most clients come in the cold months, while in summer people go to summer homes and the seaside. **SOURCED**, LRT interview with bathhouse keeper Vereta Rupeikaitė (Rumšiškės open-air museum) (https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2311862/embracing-lithuanian-sauna-traditions-each-country-has-its-own).
- **Film:** **Kino pavasaris** (Vilnius International Film Festival), every March since **1995**, founded by Vida Ramaškienė at cinema Lietuva; main venue Forum Cinemas Vingis, plus Skalvija, Pasaka, Forum Cinemas Akropolis; **114,250** viewers in 5 cities (2017), nearly **130,000** in 2025 in 14 cities; industry event "Meeting Point – Vilnius." **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Vilnius International Film Festival".
- **Summer water leisure:** see A (beaches).

### E. Cultural activities and civic life (new beyond R1)

- **Street Music Day (Gatvės muzikos diena):** started **2007** by musician Andrius Mamontovas, who emailed friends to go out and play; first edition **5 May 2007** with about 600 musicians (295 permits); first Saturday of May; by 2008 more than 3,000 musicians in Vilnius and 31 other towns; copied in several countries. **UNVERIFIED** (snippets; the MFA page returned 403 and Baltic Times is blocked).
- **Užupis Independence Day, 1 April:** bridges "controlled" by border guards who stamp passports; beer from the fountain under the Angel "from morning until night." **UNVERIFIED** (snippets); passport stamps on the bridge **SOURCED** (Go Vilnius).
- **Joninės / Rasos (Midsummer, 23–24 June):** wreaths, bonfires, herb gathering, the search for the fern flower, bathing at midnight and rolling in dew; in **2026** the city's celebration was in **Verkiai Regional Park** from 7 p.m. on 23 June, with an herb market and rituals through sunrise. **SOURCED**, LRT, 23 Jun 2026 (https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2968211/bonfires-and-music-where-to-celebrate-jonines-this-year). The largest celebration nationally is at Kernavė. **UNVERIFIED** (snippet).
- **Vėlinės (All Souls, 2 Nov; All Saints 1 Nov):** cemeteries filled with candles; Rasos, Antakalnis (WWI Polish soldiers' memorial; memorial to the January 1991 TV Tower defenders), Bernardine (1810), the Jewish cemetery north of the center; older customs: places set at meals for the dead, candles by the pillows, beetroot soup and buckwheat dishes after midnight; a folk warning against walking country roads at dusk on 1 Nov. **SOURCED**, In Your Pocket Vilnius, Saulius Žiūra, undated (https://www.inyourpocket.com/lithuania/vilnius/articles/all-souls-day). Polish-community candle action: see B.
- **Kūčios (Christmas Eve, 24 Dec):** twelve meatless dishes; hay under the tablecloth; an empty place with a candle for the dead; herring, smoked eel, **kūčiukai** biscuits with poppy-seed "milk," cranberry kissel; straw-drawing fortune games; meal around 6–7 p.m., traditionally at the first star. **SOURCED**, Wikipedia "Kūčios".
- **Šimtadienis**, **Baltic Pride**, **Kino pavasaris**, **Light Festival**: see B and C.
