## 1 · Nauru and Banaba phosphate

**Round 2 focus:** the settings beyond the mine (the workers' compound, its school, the refugee camps), the groups who lived apart (Chinese, I-Kiribati and Tuvaluan contract workers; refugees; the Banaban diaspora on Rabi), the civic calendar (holidays, sport), and the open threads from Round 1 (present-day alcohol rules, Rabi's economy, workers' housing).

### A. Other settings
- **The Location (Denigomodu District):** an expatriate housing compound that houses all expatriate contract labor; it is the laborers' settlement for the Nauru Phosphate Corporation's mine workers and makes Denigomodu Nauru's largest settlement by population. It has its own **Location School** (years 1 to 8), described as the only school in Nauru that is not English-medium. The same district holds the NPC planning and construction offices, the **Republic of Nauru Hospital (Naoero Medical Center)**, State House (the president's residence), **Eigigu Supermarket**, **Nauru College** (years 7 to 9) and the weather station. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denigomodu (accessed 2026-10-29). *(The page also calls the district "highly dependent on tourism"; recorded as stated, not checked.)* [K4, K7, P4, P5]
- **The workers' compound as a closed space (1948):** in the 7 June 1948 riot, 200 to 300 Chinese laborers barricaded themselves "in their compound"; the compound is the unit of space in every account. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Nauru_riots (citing Nancy Viviani, *Nauru: Phosphate and Political Progress*, 1970). [K4, K9]
- **Refugee settlements on mined and coastal land:** in August 2014 about 50 refugees (mostly from Pakistan and Afghanistan) lived at the **Fly Camp**, next to land damaged by phosphate mining; they reported "clear signals from the local community that they are too different to have jobs or to mix with Nauruans", and outsiders who came to look at them. SOURCED, RNZ, 2014-08-14, rnz.co.nz/news/pacific/252158. Later camps for people granted asylum included **Anibare Lodge** (families) and **Nibok** (converted shipping containers), where locals threw rocks, damaged air-conditioners and burned a refugee's car. UNVERIFIED (search snippets of refugeeaction.org.au and inkl/Guardian; not opened). [K4, K9]
- **Linkbelt Oval:** the country's only active stadium, built by the Nauru Phosphate Corporation, owned by the government and operated by the Nauru Australian Football Association; its playing surface is **crushed phosphate dust**. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_rules_football_in_Nauru. The national association's president said in 2024 that "no one would want to play here, because we don't have grass on the field, it's just gravel"; the Australian Defense Force and Australian aid were backing a grass field, pending purchase of land from private owners. SOURCED, RNZ, 2024-04-11, rnz.co.nz/international/pacific-news/514025. *(Location conflict: Wikipedia puts the oval in Aiwo; the RNZ article says Yangor. Not resolved.)* [K10, K9, P3]

### B. Subcultures
- **Chinese workers and their descendants:** first brought in 1907 by the Pacific Phosphate Company; most were not given permanent residence but renewed their contracts; 180 remained after most were evacuated before the 1942 Japanese invasion, and under Japanese occupation they received smaller rations than Nauruans and were "left to starve"; 1,400 by 1948. 2002: 28 residents identified as Hongkongers; 2011: 152 of Chinese nationality (145 PRC, 7 ROC); 2021: 16. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people_in_Nauru. [K4, K9]
- **The 1948 riot (community funds):** on 7 June 1948 several hundred Chinese workers refused to board the ship home, alleging that the Chinese interpreters would not pay out their share of contributions to the community funds; they armed themselves with spears, clubs and axes; 44 Nauruan constables and 16 European volunteers were sent; 4 dead (2 shot, 2 bayoneted in the jail, allegedly while escaping), 16 wounded, 49 arrested; a Nauruan constable was charged with unlawfully killing two; state of emergency 7 June to 18 July; *Pacific Islands Monthly* criticized "great official secrecy". SOURCED, 1948 Nauru riots and Chinese people in Nauru pages. [K9, K1 (the workers' own community fund), K6]
- **I-Kiribati and Tuvaluan workers:** 2002 census: 2,493 non-Nauruan residents, 1,259 I-Kiribati, 423 Tuvaluans, 491 Chinese; for much of the post-war period non-Nauruans outnumbered Nauruans. SOURCED, ILO Suva, "Labor Migration in Nauru" (title spelling normalized to American) (ilo.org, page undated; 2011 census figures). In March 2005 about 1,000 people from Kiribati and Tuvalu were still on Nauru after the mine slowed, doing "whatever they can for a living" while waiting for "millions in unpaid wages and benefits"; some had gone home on two ships about two years earlier; others believed they had to stay until paid. SOURCED, RNZ, 2005-03-18, rnz.co.nz/international/pacific-news/153980. (Their repatriation in 2006 is in Round 1.) [K4, K7, K9]
- **Nauruans working abroad:** 2007: an estimated 50 to 100 Nauruans recently moved overseas for work (Australia, Fiji), mainly educated and skilled; about 50 expected in New Zealand's and Australia's seasonal worker schemes in 2015-16; only 10% of the labor force in primary production. Net loss of 218 people a year in 1992-2002, about half of them Nauruans. SOURCED, ILO Suva page. [K7, P9]
- **The Banaban diaspora (Rabi):** governed under the **Banaban Settlement Act** (1970), which lets Banabans live on Rabi and gives dual-citizenship eligibility; an elder called it "quite colonial" because of the powers it gives the Fiji-appointed administrator; the Rabi Council of Elders was disbanded under the Bainimarama government, and a later promise to restore it had not been met by September 2023; the administrator had approved renewal of phosphate exploration on Banaba (then on hold), which Banabans opposed in favor of rehabilitation. SOURCED, RNZ, 2023-09-04, rnz.co.nz/international/pacific-news/497242. [K1, K9, P7]
- **Banaba itself:** about 300 people live there as caretakers; the local center is Tabiang (also called Antereen); three villages inhabited in the 2010 census; Buakonikai unoccupied. UNVERIFIED (search snippets of Wikipedia mirrors; not opened). [K4]
- **Banaban dance as protest:** in the 1970s Banaban dancers led protest marches alongside the lawsuits against the British government and phosphate companies; Banaban dance was shown at the first South Pacific Festival of Arts (Fiji, 1971) and programmed for the Sydney Opera House opening (1973); 1979 photographs show Banabans protesting mining and seeking sovereignty. The exhibition *Dance Protest, Project Banaba* (Chau Chak Wing Museum, University of Sydney, to 25 January 2026; fifth in the Project Banaba series, curated by Yuki Kihara, with new work by Katerina Teaiwa). SOURCED, University of Sydney news, 2025-06-02. [K13, K9, SM:man versus society]

### C. Leisure industries
- No tourist industry of any size was found in the sources opened; leisure on Nauru is organized around sport, church and national days. (Absence recorded, not inferred.) Searched; see log.
- **Australian rules football as the main organized leisure:** introduced in the 1910s (a 20-man Nauruan police team in sleeveless guernseys in 1919); the **Nauru Football League** began 4 February 1924, with 250 of about 1,700 people playing, first with locally made pandanus-fiber balls; early matches between Boe and Denigomodu modeled on Carlton and Collingwood; finals from 1931; by the 1950s rugby and soccer had been dropped and it was "the only code that children played". Today an elite league of six clubs tied to districts (Menaida Tigers, Aiwo and Buada; Panzer Saints, Meneng; Blues, Anabar, Anetan, Anibare and Ewa; Ubenited Power, Ubenide; Boe Lions, Boe; Aces, Yaren) and a five-team reserves league; 18 men's and 5 women's clubs, about 680 registered players; the first women's match in 2015; national participation over 30%; record grand-final crowd 3,000 (1999, Menaida vs Panzer), about 30% of the population; several presidents played in the league. SOURCED, Australian rules football in Nauru (Wikipedia). The 2024 local season began in May; players go to Brisbane academy attachments. SOURCED, RNZ 2024-04-11. [K10, K2, P4, SM:competence shown under pressure]
- **Other sport and pastimes:** a traditional wrestling form (eakabarere), weightlifting, volleyball, soccer, softball, tennis, sailing, swimming, golf; basketball (the 1969 Pacific Games team beat Solomon Islands and Fiji). SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Nauru. [K10]
- **Bird catching and fishing:** catching black noddies at sunset as they return from the sea, with men standing on the beach to throw a lasso, is described as a traditional sport; taming frigatebirds is a preserved custom; anglers wait in small light boats for fish. SOURCED, Culture of Nauru (Wikipedia). [K2, K12, K14]

Today's observance includes survivors' testimony and dress: in 2017 a 91-year-old Truk returnee wore a replica of a dress worn at the 1946 repatriation. ## 2 · Vavilov Institute seed bank in the siege of Leningrad

**Round 2 focus:** the city rules that framed the staff's lives (rationing), the open conflicts from Round 1 (who survived; a memorial at VIR), the institute's present-day settings beyond headquarters, and the remembrance calendar (the siege dates; Vavilov's birthday). For a historical institution the "subcultures" are the curators, the evacuees and the soldiers among the staff; for the present, the scientific community that keeps the founder's memory.

### A. Other settings
- **The potato basement and the keeper's walk:** a second account (Novaya Gazeta) gives the basement temperature V. S. Lekhnovich held as +2 °C; he walked 1.5 hours each way along Nevsky Prospect; his leg muscles were so wasted that afterward he needed railings to board a bus; he received weekly bundles of firewood from the house commandant, M. S. Belyaeva, not enough to keep the temperature. SOURCED, Aleksandr Borin, "The feat of 13 Leningraders: for Nikolai Vavilov's 125th birthday", *Novaya Gazeta*, 2012-11-23 (with a follow-up note by Nina Petlyanova), novayagazeta.ru/articles/2012/11/23/52474. [K14, K7, R, SM:competence shown under pressure]
- **Desks as the place of death:** the same article says D. S. Ivanov (rice) died of hunger at his desk among thousands of seed packets, and A. G. Shchukin (peanuts and oil crops) died at his desk with a packet of almonds in his hands; L. M. Rodina (oats) died of hunger. SOURCED, same. *(These are the same three named in Round 1's VIR account; the desk details are the newspaper's, from the author's 1976 meetings with surviving staff.)* [K9]
- **Staff food:** staff lived on ground oil-seed cake and pressed-cake residue; boiled leather was considered a delicacy; one four-day stretch had no bread at all. SOURCED, same. [K12, K9]
- **The defense lines and the front:** A. Ya. Kameraz built fortifications near Vyritsa while keeping the South American potato collection, and was knocked down by a blast but kept working (Novaya Gazeta, SOURCED). He served in the Red Army on the Leningrad Front from November 1941 to July 1945 (123rd division), then returned to VIR, headed its potato breeding and later its distant-hybridization laboratory for tuber crops, retired in 1983, and died in Leningrad on 15 January 1991; Stalin Prize first degree 1949 for "Potato Selection"; varieties including Detskoselsky (a three-species hybrid grown for over 30 years). SOURCED, cyclowiki.org (Tsiklopediya), "Kameraz, Abram Yakovlevich" (accessed 2026-10-29). **This resolves Round 1's conflict for Kameraz: he survived (Seed World 2011's statement that he died is contradicted).** O. A. Voskresenskaya moved into a basement to guard the collection and fell seriously ill with pneumonia in December; her later fate is not given. SOURCED, Novaya Gazeta 2012. [K14, K9]
- **The evacuation route:** deputy director Ya. Ya. Virs took a complete duplicate of the potato collection to Krasnoufimsk in March 1942. SOURCED, Novaya Gazeta 2012. [K5]
- **Present-day settings:** the **Pushkin laboratories** (so named from 1 January 1939) and the Pavlovsk laboratories form VIR's research-and-production base near the city; a **Vavilov garden** at Pushkin takes school excursions. ## 6 · Japan's JA agricultural cooperatives

**Round 2 focus:** the JA's physical settings in a rural town (hospitals, fuel stations, direct-sale markets, shipping sheds), its member groups (youth, women, associate members, the mutual-aid volunteers), its leisure arm (travel, magazines), its internal civic machinery (one member one vote, general or delegate meetings, commodity groups and their discussion meetings), and the rules it imposes on producers. ### A. Other settings
- **Koseiren hospitals (Round 1 NOT FOUND for national totals, now SOURCED):** as of end of March 2022, 33 Koseiren federations in 32 prefectures ran **105 hospitals, 61 clinics**, 21 rural health-screening centers, 28 geriatric health facilities, 96 home-nursing stations, 9 special nursing homes for the elderly, 4 long-term-care medical facilities and 13 nursing schools; about 40% of the hospitals are in areas under 50,000 people, in some places the only hospital in the municipality; about 90% take emergency patients. **Origin:** in 1919, in Aohara village, Kanoashi district, Shimane (now part of Tsuwano), where there was no doctor, farmers pooled small sums to provide cheap medical care; the prewar industrial cooperatives then ran a movement to end doctorless districts and cut medical costs, and the Koseiren carry it on under the Agricultural Cooperative Act. During COVID-19, 33 Koseiren hospitals were designated infectious-disease hospitals and Koseiren held 1,530 beds (over 3% of COVID beds, 4 January 2023). SOURCED, JA Group, *JA Factbook 2023* (org.ja-group.jp, PDF downloaded and read via text extraction). [K1, K5, P4]
- **Fuel stations as lifeline:** the JA calls gasoline stations a lifeline in areas where the oil trade has roughly halved since 1996; **JA-SS stations numbered 2,290 at the end of 2022**, being converted to self-service; JA Zen-Noh imports LPG directly from Middle Eastern producers and runs 77 filling plants for areas without city gas. SOURCED, *JA Factbook 2023*. [K5, K1]
- **Direct-sale markets ("farmers' markets"):** **Mekkemon Hiroba**, run by JA Kinosato (Kinokawa and Iwade, Wakayama), opened **3 November 2000**, described as the first of the large JA direct-sale markets; 968.2 m² of floor; sales ¥2.696 billion and about 778,000 visitors in fiscal 2015, over ¥2.5 billion a year for ten years; handwritten cardboard price signs; staff include JA employees who farm themselves. SOURCED, Nikkei Business Online special, 2016-12-06. It gathers **over 1,500 producers**, who formed a farm-experience group and a Kinokawa pickled-plum promotion council from among themselves; it has an eat-in corner and a food-and-farming experience house; it operates alongside five older direct-sale shops and a roadside station. SOURCED, MAFF Kinki Regional Agricultural Administration Office, award sheet (fiscal 2019, PDF read). ### B. Subcultures
- **Youth organization:** JA youth groups (the JA youth division at each JA) are federated in the **National Council of JA Youth Organizations (JA Zenseikyo)**, founded 1954; members are the youth groups of 46 prefectures, about **60,000 farmers mostly aged 20 to 40**; activities: an annual JA Youth Conference in Tokyo each February, the "JA Zenseikyo Policy Book" of proposals to consumers and government, food-and-farming education at local JAs; its stated concerns are labor shortage, farmland preservation and succession. - **Women's organization and its magazine culture:** *Ie no Hikari* ("Light of the Home"), founded in 1925 by leaders of the industrial-cooperative movement and published since 1944 by the Ie-no-Hikari Association, was read by about a million by 1935; circulation 983,736 (1994), 569,359 (2014-15), then sixth best-selling magazine in Japan; it carried home economics, children's stories and news and promoted cooperative ideals (including a 1933 serial novel about cooperative insurance). SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ie_no_Hikari. The association today runs **"Ie no Hikari conventions"** where readers report activities based on its articles, household-account and life-planning seminars (including writing an "ending note"), cooking and handicraft classes and children's farm schools, and publishes a children's food-education magazine and a home-garden magazine. SOURCED, MAFF GI registration 0064, production-process management rules (created 2017-06-12, updated 2024-04-01; PDF read). [K14, K1, K7]
