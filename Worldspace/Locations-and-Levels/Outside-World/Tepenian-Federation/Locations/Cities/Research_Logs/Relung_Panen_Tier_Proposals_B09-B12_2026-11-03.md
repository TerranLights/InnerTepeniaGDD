## 3 · Corrections the research made to the first-pass reasoning

- **Nauru and Banaba:** "mined bare" is true of Banaba's surface (about 90%) and Nauru's interior plateau (about 80% of land, 15 of 21 km2), not Nauru's inhabited coastal strip, which was never mined. The chain also ran in money: Nauru briefly became very rich from royalties after 1968, then lost most of it.
- **Vavilov Institute:** the collection was the world's, not "a nation's"; staff did not hole up in a vault but worked in unheated rooms at St Isaac's Square under a written guard regime, and also sowed in suburban fields. The death toll differs by source (more than 30; more than 20; at least 19; twelve; nine). The founder Vavilov was not present (arrested 1940, died in prison in Saratov in January 1943). R2: the potato-collection keeper Kameraz survived the war (died 1991).
- **Svalbard Global Seed Vault:** it is a backup, not the primary store (depositors keep sole ownership and sole right of withdrawal); "sealed" fits (no permanent staff, opened about three times a year); and "at one point" is literally true of the duplicates but the vault has been drawn on (ICARDA 2015).
- **IRRI and CIMMYT:** their varieties changed how much of an existing staple a region could grow, not "what whole regions eat." Costs of the seed-plus-fertilizer-plus-credit package are recorded (the Masagana 99 program).
- **Rothamsted:** the question was fertilizer against manure and against nothing, crop by crop; the experiments have been altered over time (sections, rotations, modern varieties), so "continuously" means the plots and treatments run on, not that nothing changed.
- **JA cooperatives:** the "civic frame" claim holds but is shrinking: 12,050 general JAs in 1960 to 510 in 2025, associate members outnumber farmers about 1.7 to 1, and rural JA supermarkets are closing (Hokkaido: about 330 at the peak, 111 now).
- **Grange halls:** "school" is narrowed to adult education in the hall (Lecturer programs, a library, Junior Grange) plus lobbying for school funding; more than 30% of Washington's 200-plus active halls in 2012 were buildings first built as schools, churches or community halls and taken over by a Grange (the traffic often ran the other way). R2 found one hall (Monticello, Maine) that did hold school graduations until 1974.
- **Danish co-ops and folk high schools:** folk high schools were not built by the co-ops (the first, Rødding, 1844, is 38 years older than the first co-op dairy); the causal link between them is debated; both spread along railway-connected parishes (a 2025 economic-history paper).
- **Saskatchewan Wheat Pool:** co-op control was of handling (elevators, terminals), not of marketing (the Canadian Wheat Board from 1935). The elevator-as-town-marker claim holds, with heavy loss since the 1970s.
- **Agricultural shows:** "harvest" is wrong; the Ekka and the Iowa State Fair run in August, Queensland's local shows are spread across the year, and New South Wales shows began as ploughing matches.
- **Tamworth:** the festival was made by a commercial radio station (2TM), not "by the town."
- **Nenana Ice Classic:** "whole territory" narrowed to Alaska (tickets sold only there). A claimed informal pool from 1906 is not confirmed by any other source opened (UNVERIFIED).
- **Lake Suwa:** it is two shrine institutions in sequence (Suwa Taisha 1443-1681; Yatsurugi Shrine 1683 to present), a divination first and a climate record second; today the finding is reported through Suwa Taisha to the Imperial Household Agency and the Japan Meteorological Agency.
- **Elfstedentocht:** it is both a race (about 300) and a mass tour (about 16,000 in 1997); the waiting is national, not only provincial (a 2025 plan expects about 2 million spectators).
- **Aral Sea and Moynaq:** the farming was upstream in the same basin, not remote; climate change is now named as an accelerant; the northern (Kazakh) part has been partly restored by a dike since 2005, the southern part by Moynaq has not.
- **Owens Valley:** the water was first taken from Paiute irrigators by settlers before the city took it from the settlers; the Paiute reservations' water rights are still unrecognized.
- **Saudi wheat:** the phase-out was real but partial; wheat returned on a quota basis (see section 2).
- **Toshka:** the land was not "empty" (old Nubian village territory; Nubians protested in 2016); the name belongs to two places, the desert project and a resettled Nubian village in Nasr al-Nuba.
- **Ord River:** "built ahead of its market" is the critics' framing, and the supporters' view is recorded alongside.
- **Fordlandia:** the failure was both natural (leaf blight, insects) and imposed rules; and the town did not die (run by Brazil's Ministry of Agriculture after Ford left in 1945; a May 2026 court ruling on its heritage).
- **Las Vegas:** the water rules are confirmed strongly; **the "dam's power" claim is corrected**: hydropower is only about 4% of the city's electricity (the source for this is WorldAtlas; the log marks it a weak source), and the dam's role is historical (the 1931-36 boom) and as the creator of Lake Mead. So F12 (power) gets no support from this place.
- **Hungarian tanyas:** the mechanism is made precise (town house plus farmstead; ruling by age, season and week) and the system was deliberately broken by building bans and collectivization (1949-1961).
- **Solo road-travel music:** confirmed as three layers, not one genre; the CB radio class license's 2025 text sets emergency, data and interference rules but no road channel and no language rule.

## 4 · Gaps against facets F1 to F13 (what these 23 do and do not cover)

- **F1 breadbasket not optional:** Saudi wheat, Ord, JA, the Danish co-ops and Rothamsted touch it; none is a region whose output a nation cannot do without.
- **F2 growing without soil:** none of these 23 (this group is about institutions and supply, not soilless growing). Covered by other candidates.
- **F3 lake oasis:** only Suwa (a freezing lake with a town ring), the Elfstedentocht (canals and lakes) and, with river ice, Nenana. None has a lake country walled in by ice.
- **F4 water as hazard:** the group is about water taken or rationed (Aral, Owens, Las Vegas, Saudi), the opposite of Relung's too-much-water problem; nothing covers saturated ground or melt.
- **F5 one door:** Ord (remote), Nenana (rail and road), solo road-travel music (the road itself) bear on it; no place here has one door.
- **F6 walled in:** none. The group has no ice wall.
- **F7 land to spare, no way to ship:** Toshka, Ord and the Hungarian tanya land pattern touch it from different sides.
- **F8 large population in a hostile place:** Toshka (a planned city that counted 2,655 in 2006 against a stated 80,000), Fordlandia, Saudi farm labor.
- **F9 many nations, no shared inheritance:** IRRI/CIMMYT (an international staff community beside Filipino workers), Nauru's workers' compound (Chinese, I-Kiribati and Tuvaluan workers), Saudi farm labor, and the Danish villages that split into parallel institutions.
- **F10 food-export chain:** Wheat pools, JA direct-sale markets, Saudi quota purchases; thin on how a single export route runs.
- **F11 single point of failure:** Nauru (the supply end of the phosphorus chain), Vavilov and Svalbard (reserves), Owens (an outside party controls the land and water).
- **F12 power:** nothing supports it. The Las Vegas hydropower claim is corrected away, and Saudi wheat is about water, not power.
- **F13 calendar of melt and light:** Nenana, Suwa and the Elfstedentocht cover the ice calendar; none is a summer-melt calendar or a short winter day.
- **Governance (spec empty):** JA, Grange, Danish co-ops, Saskatchewan pools, Owens (a utility acting as landlord) give shapes; none was chosen for governance.
- **Arts and music (thin):** Tamworth, solo road-travel music, the Danish folk-high-school songbook (572 songs, R2); a thin facet, and "thin" may be the answer.
- **Food culture as eaten (thin):** the cookery pavilion (R2), JA direct-sale markets, the Thai restaurant and grocery in Longyearbyen; little else.
- **Robot half of the population:** flagged [R] in the logs where a shared-upkeep or repair culture appears (Grange members' donated labor on their halls; the Danish dairy's resident maintainer and the folk high school's shared cleaning and singing; the Vavilov potato-basement keeper; the JA mutual-aid volunteers; Tamworth's temporary camp towns; the Nenana boarding center's buddy rules and sign-ins; the truck-stop chapels and the cab as a place of worship). Flags only; no translation was made.
- **The founding:** left open by ruling; nothing proposed.
