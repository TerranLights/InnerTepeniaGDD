## 1 · Nauru and Banaba phosphate

**Claim to test:** "The far end of the phosphorus chain: islands mined bare to fertilize distant farms (K9)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with a narrowing.** Both islands were strip-mined for phosphate rock that went mainly to Australian and New Zealand farms as superphosphate, at prices below what other buyers paid. On Banaba about 90% of the surface was stripped (1900 to 1979/80) and most of the people were resettled 2,200 km away in Fiji; on Nauru about 80% of the land (the interior plateau) was mined. Narrowing: "mined bare" fits the mined plateau and Banaba's surface, not Nauru's inhabited coastal strip, which was never mined. The chain also ran the other way in money: Nauru briefly became very rich from royalties after 1968, then lost most of that wealth.

### A. Snapshot

**Nauru**
- Raised coral island, 21 km²; highest point Command Ridge, 71 m. A coastal strip 150 to 300 m wide is the fertile, inhabited land; the interior plateau ("Topside") was the phosphate field. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauru (accessed 2026-10-29). [P3]
- Population 11,680 at the 2021 census; 2025 estimate 12,025. Largest district Denigomodu (1,874 in 2021). 14 districts, 8 constituencies, 19-member parliament, no formal parties (15 of 19 MPs independent). SOURCED, same page.
- Independence 31 January 1968 (self-government January 1966). Currency: Australian dollar; Australia handles defense under an informal arrangement. SOURCED, same page.
- Defining feature: about 80% of the land surface strip-mined, leaving "jagged limestone pinnacles up to 15 m" high. SOURCED, same page. RNZ (2019-08-26) puts it as 15 of 21 km² devastated and unusable. SOURCED, rnz.co.nz/international/pacific-news/397506. [K9, P3]

**Banaba (Ocean Island)**
- Raised phosphate island, 6.29 km², highest point 81 m (the highest point in Kiribati), 185 miles (about 300 km) east of Nauru, about 400 km from Tarawa. Part of Kiribati since 1979; its municipal administration runs through the Rabi Council of Leaders and Elders, based on Rabi Island in Fiji. Population 330 at the 2020 census; about 6,000 people of Banaban descent worldwide, mostly in Fiji. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banaba_Island (accessed 2026-10-29). [K9]
- Mining 1900 to 1979 (some sources say 1980): about 90% of the surface removed, 22 million tonnes of land. SOURCED, Wikipedia (as above) and Joshua Macdonald, The Guardian, 2021-06-10, republished pina.com.fj 2021-06-11. [K9]

### B. What else exists there

**How the phosphorus chain worked (verifying the claim)**
- Discovery: Henry Denson found rock on Nauru in 1896 and took it for petrified wood; Albert Ellis identified it in 1899 as phosphate of "the richest quality". 1906: rights transferred from the German Jaluit-Gesellschaft to the Pacific Phosphate Company (£2,000, £12,500 in shares, plus royalties); first shipment 1907. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate_mining_in_Nauru (accessed 2026-10-29). [K9]
- 1919 Nauru Island Agreement (Britain, Australia, New Zealand); the British Phosphate Commissioners (BPC) bought the company's rights on Nauru and Ocean Island for £3,500,000 (agreement 25 June 1920; operations from 1 January 1921) and ran Nauru, Banaba and Christmas Island until 1981. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Phosphate_Commission; Nauru page. [K8, K9]
- Pricing: around 1970, Nauru phosphate sold to Japan at $14 to $15 a ton but into Australia and New Zealand at $12.30. SOURCED, BPC Wikipedia page. The aim of the commissioners was to bring the phosphate price to partner-country manufacturers as low as possible and so cut the price of superphosphate; for most of the twentieth century most of New Zealand's superphosphate came from Nauru rock. UNVERIFIED (search snippet summarizing Papers Past and a 2018 Taranaki Daily News item; not opened). [K8, K9]
- BPC offices in Sydney, Melbourne, Auckland and London; Banaba was "once critical for Australian agriculture". SOURCED (abstract only), Katerina Teaiwa, "Ruining Pacific Islands: Australia's Phosphate Imperialism", Australian Historical Studies 46(3), 2015, pp. 374-391, researchportalplus.anu.edu.au. [K9]
- Superphosphate is still New Zealand's most-applied fertilizer: 788,000 tonnes in 2019. SOURCED, Melanie Newfield, "A rock and a hard place", The Turnstone (Substack), 2022-06-19. [K+ (downstream use)]

**Money flows (Nauru)** [K8]
- 1948: only 2% (A$14,900) of A$745,000 in phosphate revenue returned to Nauruans. 1960: royalties negotiated up to 22%. 1967 the Nauruan government bought the BPC assets; June 1970 control passed to the Nauru Phosphate Corporation (A$21 million for full rights). Revenue about A$100 to 120 million a year into the early 1990s. The Nauru Phosphate Royalties Trust peaked at about A$1 billion and later became "nearly empty" through poor investment and corruption. SOURCED, Phosphate mining in Nauru (Wikipedia).
- Per-capita GDP about $50,000 in 1975; phosphate exports in the 1990s little more than a fifth of the 1970s level; the Trust fell from $1.3 billion (1990) to $0.3 billion (2004). SOURCED, Stephen Howes and Sherman Surandiran, "Nauru: riches to rags to riches", Devpolicy Blog, 2021-04-12.
- Today: in 2019/20 government own revenue was $269 million (from $30 million in 2011/12); revenue tied to the Australian Regional Processing Center (offshore immigration detention) was about 58%; fishing licenses $73 million (from $25 million in 2013/14). The government employed 51% of the Nauruan workforce and the processing center another 15%. An Intergenerational Trust Fund was set up in 2016 (Nauru, Taiwan, Australia). SOURCED, Devpolicy 2021-04-12. [K8]
- Residual phosphate is still mined at small scale by RONPhos (successor to the Nauru Phosphate Corporation). SOURCED, Nauru Wikipedia page. Exports about $65 million in 2023 (fish and residual phosphate). UNVERIFIED (search snippet, Economy of Nauru).
- Unemployment 23%; 95% of the employed work for government (figures as given on the Nauru page, undated there). SOURCED, Nauru Wikipedia page. (These differ from Devpolicy's 51% because they measure different years and bases; both are recorded as stated.)

**Who did the mining (workforce)** [K4, K7, K14]
- 1922: about 80 Chinese mechanics and 490 Chinese laborers worked on Nauru, plus about 360 workers from Pacific Islands. Most engaged for two-year terms; not accompanied by women. SOURCED, UK House of Commons debate, 5 December 1922, api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1922/dec/05/chinese-labouk-nauru.
- Chinese contract workers after 1920 signed three-year contracts with food, clothing and communal housing provided by the employer. UNVERIFIED (event abstract seen in a snippet; the page now returns 404).
- 1983 census: 4,964 Nauruans, plus 2,134 residents from Kiribati and Tuvalu and 263 Europeans, almost all working for the phosphate company. For much of the post-war period non-Nauruans outnumbered Nauruans, with I-Kiribati, Tuvaluan and Chinese workers the bulk of the mining labor. UNVERIFIED (search snippets; Britannica page refused with 403).
- August 2006: several hundred I-Kiribati and Tuvaluan workers and their families, stranded for years after the plant stopped, were repatriated; when the plant was refurbished about 20 were offered new contracts because they were "some of the most skilled operators of the aging phosphate equipment", and a two-month program to train Nauruans in their place had only partial success. SOURCED, RNZ, August 2006, rnz.co.nz/international/pacific-news/163965. [K14, R] *(R flag: maintenance skill held by a small migrant crew on aging equipment.)*
- Banaba: in June 1948 about 1,100 Gilbertese employed on Ocean Island refused to work, demanding £10 a month. SOURCED, Phosphate mining in Nauru (Wikipedia). [K9]

**Institutions** [K1]
- Nauru Rehabilitation Corporation, set up May 1999 to restore mined land. Plan: restore about 25% of Topside over 20 years, the whole job about 100 years, for farming and housing; preparatory work by the Pacific Community over two years, with the New Zealand firm Calibre Consulting evaluating options. SOURCED, RNZ 2019-08-26. Funded from the 1993 settlement: A$120 million from Australia, and A$12 million each from Britain and New Zealand, for pre-1968 damage. UNVERIFIED (search snippet).
- Nauru v Australia: Nauru sued Australia at the International Court of Justice in 1989 ("Certain Phosphate Lands"), settled out of court (1993) to fund rehabilitation. SOURCED, Nauru and Phosphate mining pages; The Conversation, Saleem H. Ali, 2016-07-14.
- Banaba: Tito v Waddell (No 2) [1977] Ch 106, High Court, London, Sir Robert Megarry. The Banabans claimed underpaid royalties and that the 1913 agreement (replant "wherever possible") had not been honored. Held: no enforceable trust; the replanting obligation was binding but limited to what was reasonably practicable; only nominal damages. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tito_v_Waddell_(No_2). The three governments then offered A$10 million ex gratia (announced 27 May 1977), conditional on no further legal action. SOURCED for the condition and amount, Guardian/PINA 2021; the date is UNVERIFIED (snippet of a 1978 Hansard written answer).

**Infrastructure and logistics** [K5]
- Nauru: one airport (Nauru International); Nauru Airlines about 4 flights a week to Brisbane; about 30 km of road; about 4 km of century-old mining railway; Nauru International Port (modernization completed 2025). Water about 32 liters per person per day. SOURCED, Nauru Wikipedia page. One brackish lake (Buada Lagoon) and an underground lake (Moqua Well); solar water purification; a pilot solar farm on Topside to cut diesel use. SOURCED, The Conversation 2016-07-14.
- Banaba: cargo ships come only every few months; village shop stocks run out; one nurse runs the clinic seven days a week. SOURCED, Janice Cantieri, National Geographic Education blog, 2016-08-12. No source of fresh water but rain; droughts every 3 to 4 years. In late 2020 the desalination plant broke down; three months without fresh water until a ship from Tarawa arrived in March 2021 with bottled water and a new plant. SOURCED, Cantieri 2016; Guardian/PINA 2021. In January 2023 the head of the Banaban Council of Elders reported no food or water imports in three months. UNVERIFIED (snippet). Many dwellings are built with asbestos. SOURCED, Guardian/PINA 2021. [K5, K9]

**Health and food (Nauru)** [K12, K9]
- 90% of arable land lost to mining; in 2011 only 13% of households kept gardens; diet mostly imported processed food (rice, sugar). About 97% of men and 93% of women overweight or obese; over 40% with type 2 diabetes; smoking 48.3% (2022). SOURCED, Nauru Wikipedia page. Buada Lagoon historically held milkfish (ibija) farming; its surroundings now grow bananas, pineapples, vegetables and pandanus. SOURCED, same page.

**Rabi Island (where most Banabans live)** [K4, K9]
- Volcanic island in northern Fiji, 66.3 km², 463 m high. Bought by the British government from Lever Brothers in 1941 for £25,000 (paid from the Banabans' own phosphate royalty funds, per banaban.com). 15 December 1945: 703 Banabans (318 of them children), gathered from Japanese wartime labor camps on Kosrae, Nauru and Tarawa, landed from the BPC ship Triona with about 300 Gilbertese, in cyclone season, to an island with no houses ready. Later arrivals 1975-77 and 1981-83. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi_Island; banaban.com (2021); Brookings snippet for the housing (UNVERIFIED for "no houses").
- 2017 census 2,334 people (other estimates around 5,000); 95% Banaban. Four villages named after the destroyed villages on Banaba: Tabwewa (administrative center, about 600), Tabiang, Uma, Buakonikai. Part of Fiji's Cakaudrove Province but self-governed by the Rabi Council of Leaders; residents hold Fiji and Kiribati citizenship and send one member to the Kiribati parliament. As of 2006: two manual phone lines, rudimentary medical care, no wharf, diesel power a few hours each evening. SOURCED, Rabi Island Wikipedia page.

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- Mining took the interior, so settlement, gardens and roads crowd onto the coastal strip; arable land loss underlies the imported-food diet and the diabetes and obesity figures. SOURCED (facts as above). [K9, K12, P4]
- On Banaba, mining destroyed the caves that held drought water, so the island now depends on rain, desalination and irregular ships. SOURCED, Guardian/PINA 2021; Cantieri 2016. [K5, K9]
- The royalty wealth and its loss shaped Nauru's governance: a trust fund that rose and collapsed, then a new trust fund, and a revenue base shifted to detention-center money and fishing licenses. SOURCED, Devpolicy 2021. [K8]
- Legal and memorial culture grew out of the damage: the 1989 ICJ case, the 1970s London case, the Rehabilitation Corporation, Angam-type survival commemoration and the Rabi landing day. [K1, K2, K9]

## 2 · Vavilov Institute seed bank in the siege of Leningrad

**Claim to test:** "Scientists guarded a nation's seed collection and refused to eat it while starving (K14)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED in substance; the popular telling is embellished and the death toll is not settled.** The institute's own account (I. G. Loskutov, VIR) confirms that staff kept the collection through the 1941-44 blockade, that staff died of hunger in the winter of 1941/42, and that "VIR staff did not consider seeds and tubers to be food". Corrections: (1) the collection was "the world's", not only "a nation's": it was gathered on expeditions across the world; (2) the staff did not "hole up in a vault"; they worked in unheated rooms of the institute buildings on St Isaac's Square under a written guard regime (see K6), lived at home, and also sowed the collection in suburban fields under fire; (3) death tolls differ by source: "more than 30" VIR staff died that winter (Loskutov, VIR PDF), "more than 20 scientists and specialists" (Loskutov, Ukhatova, Khlestkina, Genetic Resources journal), "at least 19" (English Wikipedia, Institute of Plant Industry), "twelve" (English Wikipedia, Pavlovsk Experimental Station), "nine" (popular web article, Amusing Planet 2018). The founder, Nikolai Vavilov, was not there: he had been arrested in 1940 and died in prison in Saratov in January 1943. Care note honored: the wartime history is reported neutrally.

### A. Snapshot
- **What it is:** the N. I. Vavilov All-Russian Institute of Plant Genetic Resources (VIR), Saint Petersburg (Leningrad 1924-1991), Russia's national genebank. Headquarters at Bolshaya Morskaya 42-44, beside St Isaac's Square (the wartime address is given as St Isaac's Square 13 and Herzen Street 44, Herzen Street being the Soviet name of Bolshaya Morskaya). SOURCED, Loskutov, "VIR and the blockade of Leningrad", vir.nw.ru/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/blokada3.pdf (Russian, read in full via text extraction); address SOURCED, search snippet of vir.nw.ru, UNVERIFIED for the 42-44 form; Bolshaya Morskaya 44 SOURCED, peterburg2.ru (Vavilov memorial museum page).
- **Lineage:** Bureau of Applied Botany founded 27 October 1894 (UNVERIFIED for the day; the year SOURCED) under the Ministry of Agriculture, by A. F. Batalin and I. P. Borodin; R. E. Regel head from 1905; journal *Proceedings on Applied Botany* from 1908; Regel died of typhus in 1920 and Vavilov succeeded him; institute status 1924; renamed All-Union Research Institute of Plant Industry (VIR) in 1930. SOURCED, I. G. Loskutov, Y. V. Ukhatova, E. K. Khlestkina, "VIR: from a small Bureau in the Russian Empire to the present-day National Center for Plant Genetic Resources", *Genetic Resources* (genresj.org), read 2026-10-29 (article gives data "as of 1 November 2024").
- **Size now:** over 320,000 accessions (64 families, 376 genera, 2,169 species): cereals 137,500; legumes 46,500; forage 32,000; vegetables and melons 52,000; industrial crops 28,500; tubers 8,300; fruit 23,000. Over 750,000 storage units including safety duplicates. About 1,200 staff: some 500 in St Petersburg, over 700 at the stations; 15 branches in 12 regions "from Dagestan to the Arctic". SOURCED, Genetic Resources article. [K14]
- **Defining feature for this pick:** the survival of the collection through the blockade by staff who were starving. SOURCED, as above.

### B. What else exists there (then and now)

**The blockade, as the institute records it** [K14, K9, SM:competence shown under pressure]
- Before the ring closed, a government order to evacuate the institute could not be carried out. In late August 1941 the field collections at Pavlovsk and Pushkin (potato, rye and others) were rushed into the city under shelling, led by A. Ya. Kameraz and O. A. Voskresenskaya of the tuber department; the potatoes went into the institute's basement. SOURCED, Loskutov VIR PDF.
- Many staff were sent to the front, and the rest to build defenses; through the summer and autumn mostly women staff packed the collection for evacuation in two lots: (1) in evacuees' hand luggage, 100 grains each of 20,000 cereal samples and 50 to 200 seeds of other crops; (2) double crates holding 100,000 samples of 20 to 50 g each, about 5 tonnes, in a rail car bound for Krasnoufimsk in the Urals. The train left on 26 August; when the Germans took Mga station on 30 August it came back, sat on sidings for over six months under bombing, and was finally unloaded and brought back to the institute by an army unit. SOURCED, Loskutov VIR PDF.
- Partial evacuation came only in winter: director I. G. Eikhfeld (appointed after Vavilov's arrest in 1940) and some staff left for Krasnoufimsk on 17 January and 19 February 1942, on the first trucks over the ice "Road of Life" across Lake Ladoga, carrying a small part of the collection in their kit bags; about 40,000 seed packets (about half a tonne) were flown to Krasnoufimsk. The largest and most important part stayed in the besieged city. SOURCED, Loskutov VIR PDF.
- Conditions: January and February 1942 were the hardest months, with temperatures down to minus 36 to 40 °C; the bread ration was 250 g (half bran) for workers and 125 g for employees and dependants. No water, no electricity, constant shelling. SOURCED, same.
- Rats: in winter 1942 mice and rats overran the building at Herzen Street 44 and got into the metal seed tins; the weakened staff tied the tins into bundles and packed them between the shelving, filling 18 rooms, working by kerosene lamp because the windows were boarded with plywood after blast damage. SOURCED, same. [K14, R] *(R flag: improvised conservation and repair work under extreme scarcity.)*
- Theft: in spring 1942 there were several thefts of seed by people who broke through boarded windows; losses were small; windows were re-sealed and material moved. SOURCED, same. [K9]
- Named staff who died of hunger in 1941/42 (Leningrad group): L. M. Rodina, curator of the oat collection; D. S. Ivanov, curator of the rice collection and head of the rice section; A. G. Shchukin, curator of the peanut collection; G. K. Kreier, curator of medicinal plants; G. V. Geints, keeper of the book collections and head of the VIR library. "In all, more than 30 VIR staff died that winter." SOURCED, same. *(Names recorded only as the documented holders of the collections, per the caution on real people.)*
- A popular web retelling says an oilseed specialist "Lyudmila Rodina" survived by eating book-binding glue. UNVERIFIED (snippet), and it conflicts with the VIR account, which lists L. M. Rodina (oats) among the dead. Seed World (2011) says Kameraz and Voskresenskaya died; the VIR account names them as the organizers of the potato rescue and does not list them among the dead. Both recorded as conflicts, not resolved here.
- Potato keeper V. S. Lekhnovich (quoted in the VIR account): he guarded the tubers "from rats, frost and starving people", sealed the basement with three different locks and an iron-clad door, walked twice a day from Nekrasov Street to St Isaac's Square (about an hour and a half each way), and burned whatever wood he could find so the basement never went below freezing. SOURCED, Loskutov VIR PDF. [K14, SM:competence shown under pressure]
- Fields under fire: from spring 1942 the collection was sown on state-farm plots in the suburbs, under German artillery fire, to renew seed viability; the potato collection was planted on a suburban farm and guarded against thieves all summer and autumn, every year of the blockade. At the "Predportovy" state farm about 200 cereal varieties were sown on 250 m² (N. R. Ivanov, quoted). SOURCED, same. [K14, K7]
- Help to the city: when the city called for its own vegetable and potato base inside the ring, VIR supplied seed and planting stock, taught courses for vegetable and potato growers and issued growing guidance; it developed a method giving up to 15 cuttings per potato tuber (12 to 15 tonnes a hectare; 2 to 3 kg per cutting for gardeners); in spring 1942 it pushed farms to grow greens (beet, Chinese cabbage) for canteens; it lectured on edible wild plants, because starving people were gathering dandelion, nettle and orache and some were picking poisonous plants. SOURCED, same. [K5, K12, K14]
- After the siege was lifted (February 1944 in the VIR text), staff came back from Krasnoufimsk; 100,000 samples were picked for urgent re-sowing and sent to 6 VIR stations and 72 breeding institutions. Some subtropical and high-mountain samples had lost viability and were replaced after the war. SOURCED, same.

**Vavilov himself** [K9]
- Director December 1920 to August 1940 (SOURCED, peterburg2.ru museum page). Removed from his directorship in 1936 through Lysenko's influence (SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Vavilov; note the dates conflict with the museum page's "to August 1940" and are recorded as given). Arrested in Ukraine in August 1940 while collecting seeds, accused of spying and of ruining Soviet agriculture; sentenced to death July 1941; commuted to 20 years in 1942; died in Saratov prison on 26 January 1943 (records cite pneumonia, dystrophy and edema; some sources say starvation); sentence voided 1955. SOURCED, Nikolai Vavilov Wikipedia page.

**The institute today** [K1, K14, K8]
- Storage: 1976 national seed store at the Kuban station (Krasnodar Territory), 24 underground rooms at +4 °C for 400,000 accessions; 1994-97 renovation with IPGRI and USDA help; 2000 cold rooms at headquarters (+4 °C and −10 °C); 2008 Kuban upgrade (+4.5, −5, −20 °C); 2023 a new −18 °C store for 300,000+; cryobank since 2004 (3,677 accessions: in vitro potato, onion, berries; pollen; cuttings; meristems). Herbarium of cultivated plants (WIR): 141,293 specimens. About 5,000 samples a year sent free to over 200 Russian requests. 2022: National Center for Plant Genetic Resources set up on the basis of VIR; 2024 federal law on bioresource centers and biological collections. SOURCED, Genetic Resources article.

## 3 · Svalbard Global Seed Vault

**Claim to test:** "A remote, sealed reserve that holds a world's food future at one point."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED with three narrowings.** (1) It is a **backup**, not the primary store: it holds free "safety duplicates" of collections that genebanks keep elsewhere, and each depositor keeps sole ownership and sole right of withdrawal (the "black box" arrangement). (2) "Sealed" is accurate in operation: it has no permanent staff on site and is opened about three times a year (February, June, October) for deposits. (3) "At one point" is literally true for the duplicates (about 1.38 to 1.42 million samples from 131 to 135 depositors), but the vault has been drawn on: the first withdrawal (ICARDA, 2015) was to rebuild a collection lost to the Syrian civil war. Care note honored: the town setting (Longyearbyen) is covered here only as the setting of the vault.

### A. Snapshot
- **Location:** inside a sandstone mountain (Platåberget) near Longyearbyen, Spitsbergen, Svalbard (Norway); about 130 m into the rock and about 130 m above sea level; the entrance tunnel is 100 m long. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svalbard_Global_Seed_Vault (accessed 2026-10-29; first 100k characters read). (The mountain's name was not on the part read: UNVERIFIED.)
- **Built:** ground broken 19 June 2006; first seeds January 2008; opened 26 February 2008; cost 45 million kr (about US$8.8 million in 2008). SOURCED, same.
- **Design:** storage at −18 °C inside bedrock at about −3 °C; if cooling failed, warming to 0 °C would take about two centuries (per the page). Floor area about 1,000 m². Capacity 4.5 million samples of about 500 seeds each. SOURCED, same. [K1]
- **Holdings:** 1,378,238 samples at 31 December 2025 from 131 depositors; 46,781 added in 2025 in 167 boxes; 10 new depositing institutions in 2025. SOURCED, NordGen Annual Review 2025, publication.nordgen.org. The official site now shows 1,417,799 samples, 135 depositors, 6,587 species (crossed 1.4 million in June 2026). SOURCED, seedvault.no (accessed 2026-10-29).

### B. What else exists there (the institution)

## 4 · IRRI, Los Baños (and CIMMYT, Mexico)

**Claim to test:** "Crop-breeding stations set beside their own fields that changed what whole regions eat (K14)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with a narrowing of "what … eat".** Both are breeding institutes on their own experimental farms: IRRI on a 252-hectare farm inside the University of the Philippines Los Baños campus; CIMMYT at El Batán (Texcoco) with field stations elsewhere in Mexico, including the Yaqui Valley station at Ciudad Obregón. Their varieties changed how much of the existing staple whole regions could grow (rice across Asia; wheat in Mexico and South Asia) rather than what the staple was. IRRI's IR8 (1966) began what the institute calls the first Green Revolution in rice; by 2005, 60% of the world's rice area was planted to IRRI-bred varieties or their descendants. The same package (seed plus fertilizer, pesticide and credit) also had costs, recorded below. Care note honored: the Philippine material (including a national program) is reported neutrally.

### A. Snapshot

**IRRI**
- International Rice Research Institute, founded 1960 with the Ford Foundation, the Rockefeller Foundation and the Philippine government; headquarters at Los Baños, Laguna, on a 252-hectare experimental farm on land leased from UPLB. 954 staff and a budget of US$56.262 million (2022); offices in 17 countries; over 15,000 scientists trained since 1964; a CGIAR center. 1969 Ramon Magsaysay Award. SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Rice_Research_Institute (accessed 2026-10-29). [K14]
- **International Rice Genebank:** 133,087 accessions in Genesys (126,606 *Oryza sativa*; 1,662 *O. glaberrima*; wild relatives); 38% traditional cultivars or landraces; about 228,000 samples sent to 103 countries over 25 years; 90% of varieties released by national rice programs have IRRI germplasm in their pedigree; safety duplicates at Svalbard and at the US National Center for Genetic Resources Preservation. SOURCED, genesys-pgr.org/wiews/PHL001 (data updated August 2026). [K14, K1]
- Defining feature: a rice-breeding institute and genebank that sits in a university town next to its own paddies.

- Municipality of Laguna province; 117,030 people (2024 census), 54.22 km², 14 barangays; first-class municipality; poverty incidence 6.19% (2023). ## 5 · Rothamsted Research, Harpenden

**Claim to test:** "Field experiments running continuously since the 1840s, with fertilizer as their first question (K14)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** Broadbalk, sown with winter wheat in autumn 1843 (first harvest 1844), was laid out to compare inorganic fertilizers with farmyard manure, with a strip that has received no fertilizer or manure since 1843; it is described as the longest-running scientific experiment in the world. ## 6 · Japan's JA agricultural cooperatives

**Claim to test:** "A farm cooperative that also runs banking, insurance, shops and ceremonies, so it is the town's civic frame (K1, K8, K10)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED for the range of services; "civic frame" is supported but narrowing.** A local JA is a multipurpose cooperative: produce marketing and farm supply, a bank (JA Bank), mutual insurance (JA Kyosai), supermarkets (A-Coop), gas stations, and in some areas hospitals (through prefectural health-and-welfare federations), elder care, funeral services, travel and more; it also runs women's and youth divisions and yearly farm festivals. Narrowings: (1) the scale is shrinking fast: 12,050 general JAs in 1960, 510 in 2025, with 7 prefectures down to a single JA, so a "local" JA often covers many towns; (2) associate (non-farming, non-voting) members now outnumber farmers about 1.7 to 1, so for many members the JA is mainly a bank, insurer or shop; (3) rural JA supermarkets are closing (Hokkaido: about 330 at the peak, 111 now). Care note honored: Japanese nation material reported as institutional fact.

### A. Snapshot (the tradition as an institution)
- **What it is:** Japan Agricultural Cooperatives (JA; the name *nōgyō kyōdō kumiai* was rebranded JA in 1992, UNVERIFIED snippet).
