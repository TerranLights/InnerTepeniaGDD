### A. Other settings (kinds of spaces)

- **Emmeloord shopping core:** main shopping street **Lange Nering**; shops 09:00–18:00, Saturday to 17:00,
  **Friday late shopping to 21:00**, Sunday opening on the **last Sunday of each month**; free parking at De Deel and
  **Paardenmarkt**; cafés cluster around Horecaplein De Deel and **Kettingplein**. Supermarkets listed by the tourist

  https://www.visitnoordoostpolder.nl/en/doing/shopping
- **Weekly market:** **Thursday, year-round, 08:00–14:30, on the Kettingplein**, Emmeloord: produce, cheese,
  clothing, leather goods. — SOURCED, same page. *(Round 1 listed this as NOT FOUND; now found.)*
- **The village "brink":** seven villages (Marknesse, Ens, Luttelgeest, Bant, Creil, Espel, Tollebeek) were laid out
  around an elongated central green ("brink") with shops, houses and public buildings; Ens is built in the
  distinctive Delft School style. — SOURCED-weak (search-result text from Noordoostpolder planning documents; not
  opened in full).
- **Village planning for faith and school:** because of pillarization, each village was to have **two churches and
  three primary schools** (Protestant, Roman Catholic, public); each village was to have a baker and butcher, with
  Emmeloord as the service town for the whole polder; first-generation residents came "from all over the
  Netherlands." — SOURCED, C. L. Vernède, *Plaatsidentiteiten in de Noordoostpolder: Luttelgeest & Tollebeek*,
  master's thesis, Rijksuniversiteit Groningen, May 2010, pp. 3, ~12,
  https://frw.studenttheses.ub.rug.nl/1899/1/Plaatsidentiteiten_Noordoostpolder.pdf (read via pdftotext).
- **What two villages actually contain (2010 survey, same thesis):**
  - **Luttelgeest:** residents name the **greenhouses** ("kassen", "a lot of light from the greenhouses"), the
    **Orchideeën Hoeve** (orchid attraction), farm shop **Verhage**, football club **Tonego**, the **Bosfluiter**
    (the associations' building that houses several clubs), **'t Trefpunt** (the *former* village restaurant), the
    **weighbridge (weegbrug)**, the cold store, "the first houses"; also an **asylum seekers' center (AZC)** that
    Tollebeek residents associate with Luttelgeest. Complaints: "no shop and no hospitality any more," "outflow,"
    "aging," "few activities for youth," "a car is necessary." But also "many and lively associations (more than
    forty)." Luttelgeest has **no supermarket**; residents "seldom to never" shop in their own village. — SOURCED,
    Vernède 2010, pp. 3–4, 44–46.
  - **Tollebeek:** the **fountain on the Sint Hubertusplein** (the shopping street), restaurant **De Goede Aanloop**,
    "the café," "the snack bar," the bridge, a small pavilion ("tempeltje/'t prieeltje") on the quay, the
    **dorpshuis**, "**'t Parochiezaaltje** with the seniors' club (bejaardensoos)," a "wooden shed (houten keet),"
    the **Urkervaart** canal, an **ice rink (ijsbaan)**; many residents come from **Urk**; three primary schools
    (public, Protestant, Catholic); residents shop "often to regularly" in their own village. — SOURCED, same.
- **Lelystad neighborhood form:** the oldest neighborhood is the **Zuiderzeewijk** (first residents September
  1967); the city has **11 wijken** (2025 listing: Atolwijk, Boswijk, Bolder, Havendiep, Kustwijk, Lelystad-Haven,

- **Lelystad as first experienced (1967):** "There was nothing at all, not even a mailbox"; no entertainment, few
  jobs, sparse buses; separated foot, bike and car networks with **elevated bike paths**; no welcoming town center
  at first. A first resident, Henk Hutten, became the city's "first photographer" because his house had the only

### B. Subcultures

- **Urk youth and the "hokken":** a *jeugdhonk* / *hok* / *keet* is a youth-run meeting shed where young people
  gather one or more evenings a week. Urk once had **about 80 illegal youth hokken on the industrial estate**; the
  "Veilig Uitgaan" (safe going-out) project cut this to **19 licensed hokken**; since then youths (especially
  **14–18**) "move to back streets or meet at each other's homes." 2023 youth survey: **23%** drank in the past 4
  weeks, **14%** binge-drank, **24%** of drinking youths got alcohol from their **parents**. Sports canteens named as
  places where youths watch adults drink. About half of Urk residents are **28 or younger**. — SOURCED, Gemeente Urk,
  *Preventie- en handhavingsplan Alcohol 2026–2029* (effective 20 June 2026),
  https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR763169/1. The hokken were moved to the **Zwolsehoek** business estate in
  **Dec 2018**. — SOURCED-weak (search-result text from an Urk zoning document).
- **Urk weekend culture:** in Dec 2022 the mayor (Cees van den Bos) said youth nightlife problems on the industrial
  estate had become "untenable": excessive drink and drugs, dealing, money laundering, sexual misconduct; debt,
  truancy and illness in the group; legitimate bars lose trade and complain the hokken are "unfair competition";
  bar owners say they must be "agent, judge, psychologist and educator"; cameras were placed; police, prosecutors,
  parents, schools **and churches** were enlisted. — SOURCED, *Binnenlands Bestuur*, 1 Dec 2022,
  https://www.binnenlandsbestuur.nl/bestuur-en-organisatie/urk-gaat-uitgaan-onder-jongeren-aanpakken. A Nieuwsuur
  report (Jan 2021) described intense weekday work on the industrial estate followed by equally intense weekends
  ("On weekends anything goes… while the rest of the village looks away"); historian Eva Vriend calls Urkers "loyal,
  always there for each other"; outside authorities (police, doctors, mayors) are regarded as outsiders; the
  phrase "we handle things ourselves." In Jan 2021, after the curfew, youths burned a GGD test site. — SOURCED,
  NOS Nieuwsuur, Jan 2021, https://nos.nl/nieuwsuur/artikel/2365839-waarom-is-er-zo-vaak-onrust-op-urk-er-is-hier-jarenlang-weggekeken.html
- **Urk church life:** "the village of some 20,000 inhabitants counts **no fewer than 24 churches**" — splits of
  the Protestant church (Reformed, Hervormd, Liberated, Restored); "Urk on a Sunday: rest and going to church";
  "hundreds of people dressed in black"; the largest church normally holds ≈**1,000** people. — SOURCED, VRT NWS,
  18 Oct 2020, https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2020/10/18/tot-250-mensen-in-nederlandse-kerken-net-nu-extra-belangrijk-o/.
  A search summary gave "14 denominations, 21 churches" — UNVERIFIED (source not opened); counts differ by date and
  counting method. The oldest building is locally called not by denomination but simply "**HET Kerkje aan de Zee**"
  ("the place is more important: unity in diversity"). — SOURCED, Urk storyline sheet (above).
- **Urk custom:** costume worn with pride on **Urkerdag**; dialect heard daily. — SOURCED, Urk storyline sheet.
  Coffee visits to grandparents called **"bèbe en bessien"** in dialect — UNVERIFIED (search summary only; the page
  quoted was not opened).
- **Urkers in the polder villages:** in Tollebeek most surveyed residents came from Urk; neighbors from Luttelgeest
  associate Tollebeek with "Urk," "big houses with smooth roof tiles like on Urk," "the church," and "Poles." Some
  Protestant respondents still travel back to **church on Urk**. — SOURCED, Vernède 2010.
- **Faith-split schooling (2010):** in Tollebeek, **no** Protestant respondent sent a child to the Catholic school
  and **no** Catholic respondent sent a child to the Protestant school; most Catholics in Luttelgeest used the public
  school. Church attendance: Luttelgeest believers mostly attend the **Protestantse Gemeente Luttelgeest** (21) or
  the **RK Sint Josephkerk** (16); Tollebeek: Protestantse Gemeente Tollebeek (38) and **RK Sint Hubertuskerk** (11).
  — SOURCED, Vernède 2010 (survey sample, not a census).
- **Polish/East European workers:** a Polish delicatessen is listed among Emmeloord's supermarkets (above). A
  Polish-language Mass in Emmeloord or Lelystad: **NOT FOUND** (the Polish Catholic Mission in the Netherlands has
  five parishes in three districts; no Flevoland location surfaced). — SOURCED for the Mission's structure only,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Catholic_Mission (search text).
- **Lelystad origins mix:** first residents were largely state-service staff, Wageningen engineers and polder workers
  from Overijssel and Friesland; many Amsterdammers followed. — SOURCED-weak (search summary of Prometheus book
  extract) / SOURCED for Amsterdammers (NOS 2017). 2025 neighborhood shares: **Zuiderzeewijk** 58% of
  non-Dutch origin (39% non-Western); rural area 15%. — SOURCED, allecijfers.nl (2025).
- **Pioneer identity:** residents' words for their village (2010): "saamhorigheid" (togetherness), "ons-kent-ons"
  (everyone knows everyone), "social control," "growers," "many pioneers," "Urkers." — SOURCED, Vernède 2010.

### 2a. TURPAN

#### A. Other settings

- **The courtyard house:** large rural houses typically have **two courtyards** — one shaded by grapevines with a
  large sitting platform and a water tank, the other for animals; rooms open directly onto the main courtyard and are
  often not linked internally; in summer people put a large bed in the vine-shaded patio and sleep outside, and use

### 2b. AL-AHSA

#### A. Other settings

- **Sect geography of daily life:** "In physical terms, the separation of the two religious groups is almost
  complete." **Hofuf**: mainly Sunni with a Shiite minority; **Mubarraz**: nearly equal; **numerous small villages are
  exclusively Shiite**; Sunnis live in a few larger villages. Separate neighborhoods exist even in the cities; new
  development has made some "mixed" areas, but friendships remain sect- and kin-based, while people meet at **work,
  markets and shops**. — SOURCED, Fahad Al Owdah, *Exploring the effect of sect and gender on language attitudes: A
  case study of Urban Hasawi dialect in Saudi Arabia*, PhD thesis, University of East Anglia, June 2022, §1.3.6.3
  (citing Al-Bohnayyah 2019; Al-Abdulmehsen 2013),

#### B. Subcultures

- **Two sects, separate lives:** separate mosques; Shiites combine noon/afternoon and sunset/night prayers (three prayer
  times); Shiites hold rituals year-round in **husainiyyat** (Prophet's and Imam Ali's births and deaths, **Ashura**);
  men and women attend separately; "**Shiites close their businesses during these ceremonies**"; some subgroups in each
  sect forbid intermarriage and praying together; **Sunni–Shiite marriage is "considered unacceptable"**; tribal Sunni
  families also practice endogamy, which is "virtually non-existent among Shiites." — SOURCED, Al Owdah 2022, §§1.3.6.2,
  1.3.6.4.
- **Dress and name markers:** Shiite men wear the white **ghutra** and not the red **shemagh**; Sunnis wear both (the
  shemagh came culturally from Najd); Shiite scholars wear black or white turbans, Sunni sheikhs do not; women's dress
  is not noticeably different (abaya in public); **personal names with religious meaning are used exclusively within
  each sect**. — SOURCED, Al Owdah 2022, §1.3.6.6.3 (citing Al-Mubarak 2015).
- **Origins as told by each side:** Shiites are "not tribalised, and have traditionally led an agriculture-based,
  non-belligerent lifestyle in small villages" (Holes 2010); Sunnis descend from old Arab tribes and from Najdi families
  who came around 1909 (e.g., Bani Khalid, Al Murrah). A Sunni claim that Shiites were brought in 1669 to serve Sunnis is
  rejected by the scholars cited. — SOURCED, Al Owdah 2022, §1.3.6.1. Population share: sources conflict ("majority
  Shiite" per Al-Hasan 2010 vs "less than one-third" per Wright 2001). — SOURCED (conflict recorded in the thesis).
- **Dialect as identity:** Shiite Hasawi speakers see their dialect as part of their minority identity and as the
  "traditional" Hasawi; Sunni speakers view it negatively and converge toward the Riyadh (Najdi) supra-local variety;
  both sects feel outsiders look down on Hasawi speech. — SOURCED, Al Owdah 2022, abstract. Generations: the old keep
  traditional forms; the **young use emphatic and lengthened sounds to stress local belonging**; the middle-aged use
  dialect less, defining themselves by career and education. — SOURCED (abstract only), al-Salman & al-Faridan,
  *Amarabac* 9/31 (2018): 139–148, https://search.emarefa.net/detail/BIM-852265?index=12

### B. Subcultures

- **The growers ("tuinders") and their speech — "Westlands":** slow, few-syllable words with long, often two-tone
  "sung" vowels ("paaa-aaaard"); explained locally by the isolated, overworked grower who often missed school — during
  **"de krent"** (the **May holiday for thinning grapes**, *druiven krenten*) schooling stopped entirely: "**Brood op de
  plank**" (bread on the table) first, then learning. Examples: "**Mokkermit?**" (what must I do with it?),
  "**Waffere?**" (which one?), "**MÔÔÔgguh**" as a greeting at any hour, with a slight raised index finger at waist
  height; "most as een speer an de gang" (must get going like a spear); religious influence replaced curses with
  inventions like "**krimmeneel**" and "eeuwigste bonk stront"; a careless picker is an "eeuwigste hals"; **"Pluk ie
  al?" — "Ja, blad an 't geveltje"** works as grower code for "how are you?"; "**Wat deeje de tomaten?**" (what did
  tomatoes fetch at the auction?); "**Doe mijn maar een bakkie**" (a coffee, not a tomato tray); "de pad" (the path) in
  many idioms ("van de padje af" = confused); Westlanders are "**groos**" (proud), even of being called the ugliest
  municipality. — SOURCED, Peet Kruit, *Ndus… Jaarrond verhalen voor bij een bakkie*, 2nd ed., Aug 2013, foreword pp. 2–4

### B. Subcultures

- **Thai workers (Isaan):** the Central Arava's Thai population "has equaled the Israelis since the 1990s" — SOURCED-weak
  (European Review of Books interview with Kaminer, paywalled beyond the introduction; ISA abstract via search text).
  Workers organize themselves "by age and seniority"; they reportedly called employers "**mother**" and "**father**,"
  a paternalistic vocabulary that later faded; in Israeli bureaucracy they were once classed as "volunteers"; wages ≈70%
  of the Israeli minimum (2016); foremen rarely controlled the workers' own pace and organization. **Songkran, 13 Apr
  2016:** from just after 9 am, each farm's workers rode **tractor-drawn, flower-garlanded trailers**, drinking beer and
  brandy, dancing to makeshift sound systems, **covering friends with talcum powder**, displaying plastic sports
  trophies; employers cooked **pad thai** for Israeli guests — "a tourist favorite… despite its complete absence from the
  cuisine of Isaan"; **no Israeli audience** came. — SOURCED, Kaminer, MERIP 2016. Kaminer's book: *Capitalist Colonial*
  (Stanford University Press); dissertation *By the Sweat of Other Brows* (Univ. of Michigan, 2019). — SOURCED-weak
  (catalog/review pages; the dissertation page returned 403 twice).
- **After 7 Oct 2023:** Thailand resumed sending farm workers in **June 2024** after an eight-month pause, aiming at
  10,000+ by year-end; ≈30,000 Thai agricultural workers before the war; a bilateral deal for **10,000 Sri Lankan** farm
  laborers; **221 Malawian** laborers flew to Tel Aviv on 25 Nov (2023). — SOURCED-weak (search text from TPS, Bangkok
  Post, FreshPlaza; not opened; Arava-specific figures NOT FOUND).
- **Kibbutz Samar (Hevel Eilot), the "anarchist kibbutz":** founded **1976**; 254 residents (2024); organic dates
  (medjool, nour, zahidi, barhi), ≈350 dairy cows; a 30 m solar tower test plant. — SOURCED,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samar,_Israel. As described in 1997: "Everybody decides for himself where and how much he
  works"; no budget, "everyone draws cash as he wishes"; decisions by dialogue or the assembly; "**there are no locks
  anywhere**"; dining hall voluntary; ≈150 residents (80 members); few had read Kropotkin — the system grew from people
  rejecting "rotten systems." — SOURCED, Meir Turniansky, "Kibbutz Samar" (1997), The Anarchist Library,
  https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/meir-turniansky-kibbutz-samar (*a member's account; current practice not checked*).
- **Osho meditators at Shittim:** a "colorful commune of desert dwellers and Osho meditators"; founder **"Shradha"
  Daniel Netzer**, an ex-tech entrepreneur; silent-meditation weekend on Yom Kippur; tantra weekends; vegetarian/vegan
  meals; weekend packages ≈NIS 870 (tent near Timna lake) to ≈NIS 3,000. — SOURCED, Jessica Steinberg, *Times of Israel*,
