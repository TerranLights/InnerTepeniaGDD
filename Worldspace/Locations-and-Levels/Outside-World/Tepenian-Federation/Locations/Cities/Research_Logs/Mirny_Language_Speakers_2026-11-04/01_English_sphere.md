## Gaps and conflicts

- **Scotland, English skills.** Scotland's Census 2022 asked a four-level English-skills question, but no national percentage was found in two searches, and the census office warns that the English data are mixed with Scots answers. The Great Britain English row rests on England and Wales.
- **Northern Ireland, English proficiency.** Table MS-B14 exists, but its Northern Ireland total could not be read (spreadsheet only). The BEST of 99.1 % uses the England and Wales proficiency ratio as a stand-in.
- **Malaysia, Malay and English.** No census or random-sample survey of language ability exists in what was found; Malaysia's census asked about written language ability in 1970 and 1980, but no results were found and those years are too old to use. Malay (BEST 90 %) and English (BEST 50 %) are structural estimates. English sources conflict widely: 20 % (early 1990s, Oxford Companion via Encyclopedia.com), 57 % (compiled, attributed to EF), over 60 % (University of Hong Kong student page), 72 % (business blog, unnamed source). None is a population measure.
- **Malaysia, Mandarin.** No survey of Mandarin ability among Chinese Malaysians found. The first-language count of 1.02 million (Wikipedia table) and the structural estimate of about 6 million speakers differ by a factor of six because one counts first language and the other counts ability; the structural estimate is used.
- **Malaysia, Malay pupils in Chinese schools.** ISEAS says Malays were 15.3 % of SJKC pupils in 2020; a parliamentary reply reported in July 2026 gives 34,870 Malay pupils, 6.84 % of enrollment, in 2022. Not resolved; it does not change the Mandarin BEST by more than about half a point.
- **Malaysia, Indonesian nationals.** The number of Indonesian citizens among the 2,691,070 non-citizens was not found.
- **Philippines, English.** SWS self-assessment has swung from 32 % (2006) to 46–48 % (2008) to 55 % (2023) of adults who say they speak English; Census 2000 gives 63.71 % of people aged 5+. On a whole-population base the census and SWS 2023 agree at about 55 %. "Functions in business" is stricter than "can speak"; BEST 48 %.
- **Philippines, Cebuano.** The Cebuano household count (about 1.72 million, about 6.5 %) comes from a secondary site; the PSA table was not opened.
- **South Africa, English.** Ethnologue total 19.64 million (about 32 %) vs MAPS 31 million people aged 15+ who speak and understand it (71 % of adults; at least 50 % of the whole population) vs a compiled 50.7 % (Census 2022 native speakers plus Crystal 2003 second-language). The census measures home language only (8.7 %), and the GHS measures the language used most outside the home (16.6 %). MAPS is the most recent and largest measure and is used for BEST.
- **South Africa, African-language second-language ability.** Only Ethnologue (old, rounded, with mixed population bases) and MAPS (isiZulu and Afrikaans only) give second-language figures. Closely related languages (Nguni group; Sotho-Tswana group) blur what "speaks a second language" means. HIGH values for isiXhosa, Sepedi, Setswana and Sesotho are Ethnologue totals and may be too high.
- **New Zealand, population base.** Regional sum 4,993,293 vs recalled national figure 4,993,923; no effect on percentages.
- **Australia, Mandarin as a second language.** The census records home language only; no source for second-language Mandarin was sought or found. BEST 3.2 % is an estimate above the 2.7 % home figure.
