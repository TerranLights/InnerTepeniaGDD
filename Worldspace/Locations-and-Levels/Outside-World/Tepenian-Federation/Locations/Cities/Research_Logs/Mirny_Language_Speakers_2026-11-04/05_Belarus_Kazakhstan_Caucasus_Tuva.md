# G5: Belarus, Kazakhstan, the Caucasus republics, Tuva. Language-speaker shares (real-world data)

*Research agent G5, 2026-11-04. Searches used: 31 WebSearch + 47 WebFetch (23.5) = **54.5 of 60**.*

**How to read the numbers.** "Speaker" here means the developer's definition: native speakers plus fluent second-language speakers who could do business, social talk and general dealings in the language. No census measures exactly that, so every BEST figure is a judgment built from the census/survey figures quoted beside it. The **"what the source measured"** column states exactly what each number is. Where a cell rests on reasoning rather than a direct figure, the note says so and the confidence is marked low.

**Population bases used.** Kazakhstan: 2021 census, language figures are for the population **aged 5 and over** (about 17.2 million; base implied by 6,042.1 thousand = 35.1 % and 14,391,085 = 83.7 %), and 2009 census figures are for the population **aged 15 and over**. Belarus: 2019 census, 9,413,446 residents. Russian republics: census totals as given on Russian Wikipedia (citing Rosstat), 2010 and 2021.

## Gaps and conflicts

1. **Russian in Belarus has no census ability figure.** The 2009 and 2019 censuses both asked for other languages spoken freely, but the counts are only in Belstat PDFs and a Saiku query tool the fetch tool could not read. The cell rests on home-language use (71.4 %) plus reasoning. A conflicting secondary estimate ("Demoskop 2006: 77 % fluent") was not traced to source.
2. **Belarusian ability: sources conflict sharply.** 2009 census native + second-fluent ≈ 67 %; 2019 native 54.1 % (identity) vs home 26.0 %; self-selected Viber poll 59 %; urban online poll 11 % preferring and 44 % using it sometimes. BEST 50 % is a judgment.
3. **English in Belarus has no population figure at all**, only the EF EPI band. The cell is reasoned from Kazakhstan's census figures and EF's relative standing.
4. **Kazakhstan Russian, 2009 vs 2021:** 94.4 % understand spoken (15+, 2009) vs 83.7 % (5+, 2021). The bases differ (2021 includes children aged 5–14), and the primary 2021 table defining "83.7 %" was unreadable. The 2009 "read and write freely" 84.8 % agrees with 83.7 %.
5. **Kazakhstan Kazakh:** census 80.1 % (2021 self-report) vs 49.3 % daily use vs 62.0 % read-and-write freely (2009). Researchers and Eurasianet call the census figure over-reported.
6. **Kazakhstan English:** 35.1 % "know" vs ≈ 9.7 % "speak freely" (2021 census) vs 18.1 % "high proficiency" (journal analysis; definition not seen; the journal page returned HTTP 403) vs EF "very low". BEST 7 % follows the stricter measures.
7. **Six of the seven republics have no regional language-knowledge figure.** Only Dagestan's census table was retrieved. Russian and titular-language cells for Chechnya, Kabardino-Balkaria, North Ossetia, Ingushetia, Karachay-Cherkessia and Tuva are **estimates** from ethnic shares, national speaker counts, two retention figures (Kabardian 87.1 %, Balkar 88.6 %; from a search snippet) and analogy with Dagestan. All are marked low confidence.
8. **Ingush:** the national speaker count (305,868) caps Ingush at ≈ 74 % of Ingushetia's 2010 population, while the ethnic share is 93 %. Likely census undercounting; both bounds are given.
9. **Dagestan 2010 vs 2021:** every language's share fell between censuses (Dargin 14.4 → 9.5 %, Lezgin 12.3 → 8.0 %), most likely from 2021 non-response rather than real change. BEST follows 2010.
10. **Ethnic-share and population figures** for the republics come from Russian Wikipedia tables citing Rosstat; they were not checked against Rosstat directly (Rosstat unreachable).
