## Unit E · The oasis airports (real surfaces, aircraft, range, season)
1. **Every candidate runway surface in or at the Bunger Hills oasis, with every measured dimension:** Transcription Gulf (the epishelf lake ice: usable length and width of each zone, thickness, level, cryoconite cavities, tidal cracks, summer and winter surface, whether the ice persists year to year), the Apfel Glacier blue-ice strip (length, slope, crevasses, ice speed, melt), other lakes and ice in the oasis, and the blue-ice area adjacent to the oasis found in Unit B of the intake research (use its figures; do not repeat its method). Past runways: the 1960s Il-14 and the Il-18 landings (1961-64) and where the "lagoon runway" lay, Australia's 2000-2002 proposed site and whether the C-130 trial was flown, the 2010 CASA 212 damage.
2. **What each surface can carry:** bearing capacity of lake ice and blue ice for wheeled aircraft (the real engineering rules, such as the ice-thickness formulas used for ice runways), real heavy-aircraft precedents on lake, sea or glacier ice (C-17, Il-76, A319, 737, Hercules), and the runway length real jets need on ice (friction, braking).
3. **Range and safe return:** which real aircraft types (freight and passenger) can fly Hobart, Fremantle, Casey or Mirny to the oasis and back with Australian-style reserve fuel (the oasis is about 3,818 km from Hobart, 3,932 from Fremantle, about 440 from Casey, about 350 from Mirny), using published range and payload; label every computed figure DERIVED and show the inputs.
4. **Season and weather at the oasis:** summer melt of lake and glacier surface (temperature thresholds like Wilkins' −5/−7 °C), wind speed and direction frequencies and crosswind exposure (the Oasis station record and the AWS record are in the soil file; find more), blowing snow, cloud and visibility, and the sun and twilight at 66°S.
5. **Freight-airport operations on ice:** how real polar ice airfields handle cargo (Wilkins, Troll, Novolazarevskaya, Union Glacier, Pegasus): fuel storage, ground equipment, staffing, payload per flight, turnaround; any precedent for customs, quarantine or border presence at a remote freight-only airfield.
6. **Two airfields at one Antarctic site:** real examples (McMurdo's sea-ice runway, Pegasus and Williams Field; others) and what they were for.
FINAL: a factual table of what the surfaces can and cannot carry, what aircraft can reach the oasis and return, and what was NOT FOUND. No recommendation.

## Unit F · The ice-front landing and the shelf haul
1. **The Shackleton Ice Shelf front near the Bunger Hills:** cliff height (freeboard) along the front, where the front is low or ramped, the Scott Glacier and Mill Island corner, position and change of the front over time, the shelf surface from the front to the oasis (distance 55-61 km from the western corner, 73-76 km from the east), and any ground or ship survey of that route (the 1948 *Edisto* party, the 2017 and 2021-22 *Akademik Fedorov* operations).
2. **Overland traverses on ice-shelf margins and crevassed ground:** real heavy traverses (McMurdo to South Pole over the Ross Ice Shelf margin, Atka Bay and Neumayer, Novolazarevskaya, Halley, Amery, others): vehicle types, speeds, fuel per tonne-kilometer, how crevasse routes are cleared (ground-penetrating radar, probing, snow bridges), how containers or tanks are moved, season, and failures. Use port file C's figures and do not repeat them.
3. **Satellite and survey data along the corridor:** the Sentinel-1 crevasse map (Surawy-Stepney et al. 2023), ice velocity (ITS_LIVE), surface elevation (REMA), accumulation and melt; from these, derive what the data show about the ground between the front and the oasis (label DERIVED, state dataset, version and method; no route is chosen).
4. **Ice-front landing gaps left by port file D:** berthing loads and cliff stability under cranes, ramp construction, tide and swell effects at an ice front, time and tonnage actually unloaded at Atka, Novolazarevskaya and other fronts, and failures. Only what file D marks NOT FOUND or leaves thin.
5. **Ship-to-oasis by helicopter as the real fallback:** sorties, payload, hours of flight for the 2017 and 2021-22 operations and others; and a DERIVED count of sorties for a stated tonnage (state the tonnage and aircraft).
FINAL: what the data and precedents support and do not support about landing at the front and hauling to the oasis, and what was NOT FOUND. No recommendation.
