## 2. PER-ROW FINDINGS

### 2.0 Shared evidence (Ushuaia as primary; applies to all four rows)

**2.0.1 Ushuaia's share of Antarctic tourism departures (thread 9).**
- IAATO IP 32 rev.1 (2025) [opened, local text]: "Most tourism voyages (98%) operate in the Antarctic Peninsula region during the seven-month austral summer season (from October to April), and depart from Ushuaia, Argentina or, to a lesser extent, from other ports north of the Antarctic Treaty Area." **IAATO publishes no per-port count.** Same paper, 2024-25 actuals (as of 9 May 2025): traditional seaborne Peninsula 47 vessels, 411 departures, **68,968** passengers; air-cruise Peninsula 21 vessels, 122 departures, **10,067**; large ships/cruise-only Peninsula 9 vessels, 21 departures, **36,769**; Ross Sea/continental 1,258; yachts 144.
- Total IAATO visitors 2024-25: **118,491** (2023-24: 122,072) [opened: MercoPress 3 May 2026, quoting IAATO]. (An earlier preliminary estimate was 107,270; the final is higher.)
- Tierra del Fuego Tourism Institute (Infuetur), 2024-25 [opened: ladevi.info]: **565 voyages from 66 vessels, 251,251 visitors (passengers plus crew); Antarctic cruises 422 voyages and more than 105,000 passengers** (about 74.7% of calls). 2023-24: 173,211 passengers, Antarctic cruises 64.4% [snippet]. 2025-26 so far (to 19 Jan 2026): 106,000 visitors, 256 calls, about 250 Antarctic vessels still expected (Dirección Provincial de Puertos) [opened: tiemposur.com.ar].
- **[arith.]** 105,221 Infuetur Antarctic passengers / 118,491 IAATO visitors = **about 89%**. Caveat: two different counting systems (a port counts embarking passengers per call; IAATO counts visitors per season, and its cruise-only ships may call at Ushuaia without embarking there). Treat 89% as an order-of-magnitude cross-check, not a statistic.
- Ushuaia's own tourism secretariat says "El 90% del turismo antártico hace escala en Ushuaia" (turismoushuaia.com [opened]); this is an interested party and gives no method.
- The only real counter-flows in IAATO data: air-cruise passengers (10,067, 8.5% of 118,491 [arith.]) fly from **Punta Arenas**; Antarctica21's Magellan Explorer and Ocean Nova are listed under Chile.

**2.0.2 Ushuaia is NOT "only Argentina's own program" (CONTEXT b; contradicts a first-pass Wikipedia-derived line).** Spain's 2019-20 campaign notice (Ministry of Science) [opened]: "During the campaign, the Hespérides will have 5 ports for logistical supplies and exchange of scientific and technical personnel. Two of them in Punta Arenas and three in Ushuaia, which means crossing the Drake Passage 10 times." The ship serves the Livingston Island base (Juan Carlos I, Hurd Peninsula). Brazil's KC-390 support flights run Rio de Janeiro to **Punta Arenas and Ushuaia** (Brazilian Navy page via search summary [snippet]).

**2.0.3 Crossing time Ushuaia to the Peninsula/South Shetlands (thread 1).**

**2.0.4 Drake Passage width and the Cape Horn to Antarctica conflict (thread 4).**
- British Antarctic Survey GEOMAP 2 bathymetric map record (2016; BAS, Spanish Geological Survey, Korea Polar Research Institute, AWI, US Antarctic Program) [opened]: "The Drake Passage is an oceanic gateway of approximately **850 km** width located between South America and the Antarctic Peninsula." (Different endpoints from the 800 km figure.)
- "About 800 km" Cape Horn to Livingston Island (Wikipedia Drake Passage, first pass); "roughly 800-kilometer band" (antarcticatourism.org, Oceanwide) [opened]; Spanish sources "450 millas náuticas" Cape Horn to South Shetlands [snippet] and "480 nautical miles" [snippet] (operators).
- **[GC-calc] Cape Horn to nearest Antarctic land: Smith Island about 815 to 826 km (440 to 446 nmi); Livingston/Hurd 839; King George 838.** Diego Ramírez (the nearest Chilean land to the Peninsula) to Smith about 800 km.
- **The 650 km figure is not reproducible.** It appears in en.wikipedia Cape Horn ("Antarctica, only 650 kilometres away") and is repeated by travel pages (Swoop, the5capes, a Spanish blog giving "650 km wide") [snippet], and one search summary also gave "645 km narrowest point". But **no pair of land points reproduces it**: Cape Horn is at 55.98°S and the northernmost land of the South Shetland chain that lies near its meridian is Smith Island at about 62.9°S, a latitude difference alone of 769 km [arith.]; even Diego Ramírez (56.5°S) to Smith is at least 712 km along a meridian [arith.]. **Verdict: the best-supported figures are about 800 to 850 km (sea passage) and about 815 to 840 km (land to land); 650 km is an unsupported repetition.** I did not find an IHO text defining Drake Passage limits that gives a width (search 2.0.4-b below).

### 2.1 PERGAMINO (Livingston Island, Hurd Peninsula)

**What the re-run adds.**

1. **Montevideo as a gateway: still not on any gateway list; but it is a real logistics node.**
   - Uruguayan Air Force (CONTEXT b; IAU "FAU" page [opened]): "Actualmente la Fuerza Aérea Uruguaya utiliza los Hércules KC-130H ... eventuales tramos directos entre Montevideo y la Isla Rey Jorge". Presidency note on the first direct run (gub.uy [opened]): departed Carrasco (Montevideo), **about 7 flight hours to King George Island**, King George to Punta Arenas **about 2.5 h** (fuel stop on return), Punta Arenas to Carrasco **about 5 h**, mission about 24 h, 4 Jan 2021; "direct routing was not possible with the previous Hercules model".
   - Older routing (C-130B era; airpressman.com copy of a MercoPress piece [opened]): Montevideo **"via Mar del Plata, Viedma and Comodoro Rivadavia (Argentina) arriving at Punta Arenas in five hours flight time"**, overnight at Punta Arenas to reconfigure cargo, then **2.5 hours** across the Drake to King George (cargo reduced from 130,000 to 118,000 lb for Marsh airfield). A 2015 presidency note also shows Uruguay to Punta Arenas then Chilean Air Force airfield then Artigas [opened].
   - 2025-26: IAU confirms a KC-130 carried the garrison (campaign opened 2 Dec 2025) but gives no route [opened]; Uruguay's KC-130H made "several crossings between Punta Arenas and Base Frei" in the last phase of Campaign 41 [snippet].
   - **Uruguayan ship.** ROU 04 General Artigas: left Montevideo 9 Jan 2026, back 10 Feb 2026 (about 32 days), **"recaló en el puerto de Punta Arenas"** (gub.uy [opened]; visionmaritima.com.uy [opened]: "recalada en el puerto de Punta Arenas"); previous run 15 Jan to 14 Feb 2025 (infodefensa [opened]). Earlier Vanguardia (2014): "importance of the port of Punta Arenas for the logistical support" on the return to Montevideo (armada.cl [opened]); route Punta Arenas, Fuegian channels, **Puerto Williams to await a weather window**, expected return 40 days [snippet, defensa.com page blank on fetch].
   - **Vanguardia decommissioned 2 Sept 2026**: still one source (en.wikipedia, citing Uruguay's Navy site) [opened]; the Navy page itself was not reached. It is consistent with ROU 04 doing the 2026 run.
2. **BAS RRS Sir David Attenborough 2025: Montevideo is on its southbound route.** BAS press text via MercoPress [opened]: "begins a three-week, 6,000-nautical-mile voyage to **Montevideo, Uruguay** and onward to Antarctica" (left Plymouth 17 Oct 2025). A Uruguayan analysis site says the ship called at Montevideo **10 Nov and left 12 Nov 2025**, then Punta Arenas, then Rothera, Halley VI, King Edward Point, Signy, Bird Island [opened; escenariomundial.com, a news blog using vessel-tracking data, no port-authority quote]. Wikipedia's SDA article gives only Stanley (registry) and Punta Arenas [opened].
3. **Cruise ships use Montevideo for provisioning, not as an embarkation gateway.** ANP (Uruguay's port authority) [opened]: "Montevideo como puerto elegido por las compañías para aprovisionamiento y preparación de la temporada de cruceros antárticos"; technical calls from early October; **Scenic Eclipse arrived 21 Oct 2025 with passenger embarkation and crew change**. HX Antarctica & Falklands "Northbound" (MS Fridtjof Nansen, 19 Mar to 6 Apr 2027) **begins at Ushuaia and ends at Montevideo** [snippet, 2 aggregator pages; HX page 404]; a travel-agent page lists a 22-day voyage embarking Montevideo (day 2) and ending Ushuaia, via the Falklands, South Georgia and the Peninsula, no dates [opened]. HX's MS Fram Falklands-South Georgia-Antarctica voyages embark at **Punta Arenas** [opened]. **I found no regular IAATO Peninsula voyage whose Antarctic leg departs from Montevideo.** (Oceanwide and the other IAATO operators in the IP 32 tables are not broken down by port.)
4. **The Livingston station's own logistics ship (CONTEXT b):** Spain's Hespérides, 2019-20: 3 Ushuaia calls plus 2 Punta Arenas calls (section 2.0.2). The Bulgarian station on the same island: not researched.
5. **Distances and times for Pergamino:** [GC-calc] unchanged: Cape Horn 839 km (453 nmi); Puerto Williams 954; Ushuaia 984 (531 nmi); Punta Arenas 1,222; Stanley 1,229; Montevideo **3,100 km (1,674 nmi)**. Time Ushuaia to the South Shetlands about **2 days** (2.0.3). Uruguayan source figure: Artigas Base 1,620 nmi (3,000 km) from Montevideo (MercoPress via search summary [snippet]); [GC-calc] Montevideo to Villa Las Estrellas 3,041 km (1,642 nmi) agrees. Hercules 7 h for 3,041 km = 434 km/h [arith.].
6. **Hurd Peninsula specifics:** Española Cove is used to transfer people and cargo to Juan Carlos I (Wikipedia, via search summary [snippet]); the base is seasonal, November to March.

**Verdict.** Geography of the claim is true (the Río de la Plata faces the open South Atlantic and bears about 7 degrees, north-northeast, from the site); "gateway" is not supported by IAATO or gateway-city sources; Montevideo is a **second-tier logistics node for Uruguay's and Britain's programs and a provisioning port for cruise ships**, never the nearest or fastest. The Fuegian ports are 2,100 to 2,200 km nearer.

### 2.2 PUERTO ABRIGO (Goudier Island, Port Lockroy, Wiencke Island)

**Location facts [opened].** ATS Visitor Site Guideline, Goudier Island: 64°49'00"S 63°29'00"W, "Located in Port Lockroy on the western side of Wiencke Island"; limits: no more than **350 visitors per day**, max 60 at once, 20 per guide, ships max 500 passengers, **3 ships per day**; landing preferred on the west side by the whalers' mooring chains, alternative on the north side (ats.aq). **The approach (thread 10).** What the sources actually establish:
- Ships sail the **Gerlache Strait** and the **Neumayer Channel** to reach Port Lockroy (Oceanwide Neumayer page; coolantarctica.com: Port Lockroy "reached by passing through the towering grandiose cliffs of the Neumayer Channel"; Wikivoyage: ships navigate the Gerlache Strait) [opened]. Damoy Point forms the north side of the entrance to Port Lockroy on Wiencke's west coast; Flag Point is on the north entrance and Lécuyer Point on the south (Wikipedia, search summaries [snippet]).
- Voyage logs show the order: Drake (days 2 to 3), then **Gerlache Strait-side landings on day 4, Port Lockroy on day 5**, then the Lemaire Channel (Oceanwide Plancius PLA26-25 [opened]). Palmer Station is on Anvers Island, **27 km** from Port Lockroy [GC-calc].
