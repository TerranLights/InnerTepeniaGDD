## Pick 1 · Shibuya District, Tokyo, Japan [PRIMARY]

*Scope:* the station district and the ward's residential back-areas (Round 1 covered the ward statistics, the station, Tokyu redevelopment, the main shrines and festivals, Nonbei Yokocho, the record shops, 109 and Miyashita Park). Harajuku/Yoyogi items are held for Pick 2.

### A. Other settings (sub-areas and what they are like)

- **Maruyamacho (円山町), the hillside behind Dogenzaka.** A former geisha quarter: it began around **1887** when a shamisen dealer opened a geisha house in front of the spring-fed bath **Kōbō-yu**; in **1913** it had 24 geisha houses, 60 geisha and 13 teahouses (machiai) and was designated a licensed quarter over 15,000 tsubo; peak in **1921** — **137 geisha houses, 402 geisha, 96 machiai**; about **420 geisha** before the 1923 earthquake; restaurants dwindled by around 1980; now "known as a large-scale love-hotel district"; its local center is the **Dōgenzaka Jizō** shrine. Resident population **2,026 in 1,644 households (2024)**. **SOURCED** — ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/円山町_(渋谷区) (read 2026-10-26). **Four geisha** were still working there in February 2022, among them **Koito**, aged 93, a shamisen player who began lessons at six and kept working through and after the war; low-stepped "geisha stairs" (built for wooden sandals) survive among the hotels and clubs. **SOURCED** — tv-tokyo.co.jp/plus/lifestyle/entry/2022/025449.html (2022-02-12, read 2026-10-26). As geisha and customers declined, teahouses and restaurants converted to love hotels; by 2022 the hotels themselves were in decline (COVID emergencies; better private housing reducing demand; zoning that blocks renovation; exclusion from pandemic relief because they are classed as adult entertainment, not lodging); the area is described as a mix of love hotels, old restaurants, venture companies, luxury housing and music venues. **SOURCED** — gendai.media/articles/-/95851 (Rei Toyama, 2022-06-09, read 2026-10-26). "About 300 love hotels" in Maruyamacho and Dogenzaka: **UNVERIFIED** (search summary of a tokyoweekender page that returned 403).
- **Hyakkendana (百軒店), off Dogenzaka.** A shopping quarter laid out in **1924** by Hakone Land (forerunner of Seibu) under Tsutsumi Yasujirō on a former noble estate, to draw famous shops from earthquake-ruined central Tokyo; it had "over 100" shops at first. Surviving: **Meikyoku Kissa Lion (名曲喫茶ライオン)**, a classical-music café since **1926**, rebuilt after the air raids with its postwar interior and very large vintage speakers kept (2-19-13 Dogenzaka); **Taruya**, a teppanyaki restaurant since **1931**, playing jazz in the dining room (2-20-6 Dogenzaka). **SOURCED** — tv-tokyo.co.jp/adomachi/backnumber/20240330/144767.html (broadcast 2024-03-30, read 2026-10-26). "117 shops including theaters, Ueno Seiyōken, Shiseido, Yamano Gakki"; Lion's ~3 m speakers and 10,000+ records rebuilt 1950; **B.Y.G**, a rock café and bar opened **1969** with a basement live space its manager calls Japan's oldest: **UNVERIFIED** (search summaries of san-tatsu.jp and rurubu.jp).
- **Okushibu (奥渋, "deep Shibuya") — Kamiyamacho and Tomigaya.** The street from the Tokyu main store toward Yoyogi-Hachiman, a quiet residential area next to Shōtō; Kamiyamacho is described as a long-standing quiet residential district where former prime ministers Yoshida Shigeru and Asō Tarō lived; Tomigaya's name is tied to the meeting of the Udagawa and Kōhone rivers' valleys. **UNVERIFIED** (search summaries of jreast.co.jp/funtokyo and Japanese wiki mirrors; the JR East page returned 403). Named independent shops of Okushibu: **NOT FOUND** in a page I could open.
- **Hatagaya and Sasazuka (the ward's western shopping-street neighborhoods).** Hatagaya: **Rokugō-dōri shopping street** north of the station, about **100 shops** (greengrocers, Japanese sweets, restaurants, izakaya); **Nishihara shopping street** to the south with supermarkets, produce shops, a bathhouse and a night-lit golf driving range with rental lockers, plus newer coffee and interior shops; residents range "from single businesspeople to child-raising families"; nearby Hatagaya Hikawa Shrine and the temple Hatagaya Fudōson Shōgon-ji. **SOURCED** — nomu.com/machikara/7542/ (Nomura Real Estate, 2026-05-31; promotional, read 2026-10-26). A basement drinking arcade, **Hatagaya Golden Center**: **UNVERIFIED** (search summary of allabout.co.jp, which returned 403). ### B. Subcultures

- **Gyaru and the "circles."** In the 1990s gyaru gathered after school in Shibuya in "gyarusa" (gal circles) around vending machines and fast-food spots, with **SHIBUYA109** as a live fashion index; magazines **egg** and **Popteen** set trends and made street idols; **ganguro** (deep tan, bleached hair) was the first marked substyle; later hime-gyaru, agejo (nightlife glamour), ane-gyaru; by the late 2000s the style fragmented and moved online. **SOURCED** — scrmbl.com/post/gyaru-heisei-to-reiwa (Ashton Redman, 2026-02-19; a fashion-culture site, read 2026-10-26). egg relaunched in 2019 (web, then print); Reiwa gyaru rarely visit Center Gai and interact online: **UNVERIFIED** (fan-research notion pages, snippets only).
- **Tech workers.** In July 2018 **GMO Internet, DeNA, CyberAgent and Mixi** (all Shibuya-based) founded the **SHIBUYA BIT VALLEY** project; its first conference (Sept 2018) drew 1,000+ students and junior engineers under the theme "working as an engineer in Shibuya is fun," with travel subsidies up to ¥50,000 for students from outside Tokyo; the 2019 edition (Sept 13–14, Hikarie Hall) widened to designers and art students. **SOURCED** — internet.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1174511.html (2019-03-14, read 2026-10-26).
- **Geisha (residual).** See Maruyamacho above: four working in 2022. **SOURCED**.
- **Unhoused people and their supporters.** Tokyo's January 2024 street count found **624** people across Tokyo (372 in the 23 wards' own surveys), with Shibuya among wards showing decreases; the ward-level figure is in an attachment I did not open. **SOURCED** — metro.tokyo.lg.jp/information/press/2024/04/2024042606 (read 2026-10-26). **Mitake Park** (by the ward office) was fenced off on **2022-10-25** with people still living in it; three were directed to shelter, one stayed; the soup-kitchen group **Nojiren** was at first refused entry.
