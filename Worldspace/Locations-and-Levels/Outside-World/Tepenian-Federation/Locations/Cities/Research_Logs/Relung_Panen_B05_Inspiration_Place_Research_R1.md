## 1 · Ladakh ice stupas and artificial glaciers

**Input care note (carried forward):** structure only; nothing about the people transfers, by standing project canon. The facts below are recorded as real-world facts about the technique and the institutions around it.

**Claim to test:** "Farmers in a cold desert store winter water as ice so that the melt arrives when the fields need it (K1, K14)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with two corrections.** (1) There are two distinct techniques, built by two different people: Chewang Norphel's horizontal *artificial glaciers* (from 1987, a chain of channels and mud embankments on shaded slopes) and Sonam Wangchuk's vertical *ice stupas* (from 2013–14, a gravity-fed pipe that sprays water into freezing air to build a cone). (2) The stored ice is not neutral: in at least one documented case it set an upstream village against a downstream one, and the district administration ordered the diversion stopped (see K9). The water gap being filled is specific: sowing is April–June, while natural glacier melt arrives only from late July.

### A. Snapshot
- **Ladakh:** Indian union territory since 31 October 2019; 59,146 km²; population 274,289 (2011 census, then two districts, Leh and Kargil; 8 districts as of 2026). Mostly above 3,000 m; winter lows −20 to −35 °C. Religion: Muslims 46% (mainly Shia), Buddhists 40%, Hindus 12%. Barley and wheat the principal crops. Tourism ~50% of regional GNP in 2000; >500,000 visitors in 2022 and 2023. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh (read 2026-10-29; first 100,000 characters only). [K+ setting] [P3]
- **The problem:** less than 50 mm annual rainfall; farming depends on snowmelt in early spring and glacier melt later; mountain glaciers yield melt only from late July, so the spring sowing window is the shortage. SOURCED: Wikipedia "Ice stupa" (read 2026-10-29); The Quint, Athar Parvaiz, 2018-06-29. Short summer; sowing April–June. SOURCED: Jamnalal Bajaj Award biography of Chewang Norphel, 2010 (PDF, read via pdftotext). [K1] [P4]
- **Norphel's artificial glaciers:** first built 1987 at Phuktse village for Rs 19,000; a larger one near the Chang La pass cost Rs 60,000. Described as a network of channels and mud embankments (*bundhs*) on the upper valley slope; ~2 km long, ~150 ft wide, 3–11 ft deep. Winter stream water that would otherwise run to waste is diverted by pipe and frozen in layers at several altitudes above the village during the 5–6 winter months with no farm work. The glacier nearest the village melts first, in spring, so the melt arrives in sequence through the sowing season. SOURCED: JBA 2010 biography PDF. Water is slowed "to a trickle" in meandering canals so it freezes in shallow layers in pools, October–December; one glacier stores ~1 million cubic feet, irrigating ~380 acres; farmers added potatoes and peas to barley. SOURCED: National Geographic, Ker Than, 2012-02-16. [K1, K14] [P8] [SM:competence]
- **Wangchuk's ice stupas:** prototype ~6 m tall, 150,000 L, built October 2013 in Leh without shade; it lasted until May 2014 in temperatures above 20 °C. A cone offers least surface to the sun per volume stored. SOURCED: Wikipedia "Ice stupa". Stream water from higher ground runs down in buried pipes whose last section rises vertically; pressure from the drop forces it up and out to freeze as it falls; 15–50 m tall; cost up to ~US$1,030 each; spring melt is collected and fed by drip irrigation in April–May. SOURCED: Rural 21 article (read 2026-10-29; author/date not captured by the fetch). The stupas are deliberately shaped to resemble Buddhist stupas. SOURCED: same. [K14] [P8] [SM:humanity-and-technology]

### B. What else exists there
- **Institutions that build and fund:** SECMOL (Students' Educational and Cultural Movement of Ladakh) manages the ice stupa project; HIAL (Himalayan Institute of Alternatives, Ladakh) founded by Wangchuk partly with prize money. SOURCED: Wikipedia "Ice stupa". Norphel worked with the Leh Nutrition Project (NGO) as Chief Project Officer, Natural Resource Management; two glaciers built with Indian Army "Operation Sadbhavana" support, three with the Department of Science and Technology; ten glaciers funded by the State Watershed Development Programme in Mud, Sharasharmos, Igoo, Sakti, Nang, Stakmo, Saboo and Umla, benefiting 1,260 families. SOURCED: JBA 2010 biography PDF. [K1, K8]
- **Village water office (the older institution under all of this):** the *churpon* (also written *chuspon*), "water lord," a village figure appointed annually (historically elected for knowledge, now commonly by rotation) at the start of the farm season, who supervises the rotational schedule giving each household a fixed time slot, keeps watch on the reservoir 8 pm–3 am, maintains the *zing* (community reservoir) feeding the *yura* (canals), and settles disputes. In Sakti village (~400 households) the appointment is marked with prayers, and lamas of Dakthok monastery are consulted for an auspicious date. The role "mostly falls on men." Youth leaving farming threatens it. SOURCED: Village Square, Dawa Dolma, 2023-10-16. Guilty parties apologize to the *goba* (village headman) or the churpon; harder cases go to court; rules set who, when and how, with graded sanctions. UNVERIFIED (search snippets from peacefulsocieties.uncg.edu and an ICAR journal abstract; not opened). [K1, K6, K7] [P7] [SM:competence]
- **Labor:** first-plowing work is done by interfamily reciprocity, families helping plow each other's fields in turn, fed with tea and barley beer through the day. SOURCED: Tricycle, Victoria Andrews, 2023-05-20. Ice stupa crews are village volunteer groups who must take a day-long HIAL workshop. SOURCED: Tribune India, January 2020; Rural 21. [K7, K14]
- **Access:** Ladakh's two road links close in winter. The Manali–Leh highway was closed on November 20 (year per the 2025 newsonair item, snippet only, UNVERIFIED); Zoji La (~11,500 ft) on the Srinagar–Leh road typically closes December to April; the Border Roads Organisation (Project Vijayak, Kargil) clears it; when both roads are shut, the Indian Air Force airlifts supplies. In winter 2025–26 Zoji La stayed open on low snow. SOURCED: Tribune India, 2026-01-14. One reopening came after 73 days. UNVERIFIED (snippet). Road closure limited materials for ice stupa contestants. SOURCED: Tribune India, January 2020. [K5] [P5]

### C. Cultural locations
- **Phyang village and monastery:** Phyang Gompa ~17 km west of Leh, ~100 monks; Phyang Tsedup festival on days 2–3 of the 6th Tibetan month (July 22–23 in 2025). UNVERIFIED (snippets: Wikipedia, utsav.gov.in; not opened). Phyang, ~2,000 inhabitants, was the site of the first planned large stupas. UNVERIFIED (snippet, eco-business/dialogue.earth). [K2]
- **Ice Stupa Competition** (see K3 below). **Ganglas village** (8 km north of Leh) built 10 stupas in 2020 both for the competition and for an ice climbing festival meant to earn income. SOURCED: Tribune India, Jan 2020. At Shara, visitors came for three months to see the stupa and villagers sold refreshments. SOURCED: Rural 21. [K3, K8]
- **Ice hockey on flooded ponds:** started ~50 years ago by the Indian Army in Changthang (eastern Ladakh) as downtime play with improvised kit (blades on ammunition boots, field-hockey sticks, a sand-filled polish tin as puck). Played in January when ice is thickest; Gupuks pond in Leh (~11,562 ft) is the favored skating spot; the CEC Cup draws thousands; its 16th edition had 15 men's and 7 women's teams. SOURCED: Village Square, Dawa Dolma, 2023-01-13. Karzoo pond in Leh is an agricultural pond that locals flood in winter to freeze as a rink; Lieutenant Governor Cup and CEC Cup annually; Ladakh Winter Sports Club; Gupuks village hosts the Khelo India Winter Games 2026. UNVERIFIED (snippets; idronline, reachladakh, outlookindia not opened). [K2, K14] [P4] [SM:competence]

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- **Saka (first plowing) opens the water:** in April, villages fix the date of the first plow by consulting an astrologer or by a meeting of household heads; earth spirits are awakened with Buddhist text readings and offerings of barley flour, butter, juniper and barley beer; dzos (yak–cow hybrids) pull the plow. In Basgo (a ~7 km village on the Leh–Manali highway) the water channels are opened after Saka, and households then plow on their own timelines. SOURCED: Tricycle, 2023-05-20. A child is called to open the field; decorated animals run on the fields; relatives are invited to drink beer. UNVERIFIED (snippet). [K2, K6, K12] [P6]
- **Watering order:** barley fields are watered three times, then the water moves to the wheat, sown after barley; both need about ten waterings to harvest. UNVERIFIED (snippet, source not opened). [K7]
- **Harvest:** Shrubla/Srub-La, before harvest, honoring a goddess of plenty; the Ladakh Cultural Academy Kargil hosts a Srub-La Festival. UNVERIFIED (snippet; ladakh.gov.in page not opened). [K2]
- **The ice-shaped technology carries a religious form on purpose** (stupa shape). SOURCED: Rural 21. [K+ form carrying meaning] [P6]

### E. Structural features (industry, economy, arts, work and life)
- **K1 shared-problem rules are older than the ice:** the churpon's rotation and night watch predate both techniques; the new ice sits on top of the old water-sharing office, not in place of it. SOURCED (Village Square). [K1]

## 2 · Siwa Oasis, Egypt

**Claim to test:** "A salt-lake oasis behind desert, long reached by one route, with its own culture and harvest festival (K2, K12)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with corrections.** (1) "One route" holds for the paved era: the first asphalt road, to Mersa Matruh on the Mediterranean coast (~300 km), came only in the mid-1980s; the other road, to Bahariya (~420 km), requires a permit. Before the road, access was by desert track and caravan (the caravan history itself was not read on a page; see F). (2) The salt lakes are partly a modern product: they are swollen by agricultural drainage from thousands of wells drilled since the 1980s, and that drainage is now the oasis's main crisis. (3) The harvest festival exists (Siyaha, October full moon, at the date and olive harvest) but is first a festival of reconciliation and is tied to a Sufi founder; its claimed age differs between sources.

### A. Snapshot
- About 50 km east of the Libyan border, ~560 km from Cairo (Wikipedia) / ~600 km west of Cairo (NASA); depression down to ~19–20 m below sea level; oasis about 80 km long and 20 km wide; ~200 natural springs; two large salt lakes fed by agricultural drainage. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siwa_Oasis (read 2026-10-29); NASA Earth Observatory, 2023-04-22. Rainfall ~10 mm a year; water from the Nubian Sandstone Aquifer System. SOURCED: NASA. [K+ setting] [P3]
- **Population — sources disagree widely:** ~25,031 (2021, Wikipedia); ~30,000 (AramcoWorld 2006); ~35,000 (snippet, UNVERIFIED); ~60,000 (The National, 2024-02-09). Treat as unresolved. [P9]
- **Defining feature:** an isolated, below-sea-level spring oasis of Siwi (Berber/Amazigh) speakers, culturally separate from mainstream Egypt until the 1980s road. SOURCED: Wikipedia; thezay.org (2021-05-21).

### B. What else exists there
- **Economy:** date palms "by far the largest component," then olives, then handicrafts (basketry); tourism now "a vital source of income." SOURCED: Wikipedia. ~300,000 date palms; five date-packing plants employing hundreds; also lemons, mulberries, almonds, carob, peaches; "no pesticides have ever been used here." SOURCED: AramcoWorld, Sylvia Smith, May/June 2006. Water-bottling plants and rock salt marketed internationally. UNVERIFIED (snippets). [K7, K8]
- **Salt:** evaporation ponds for salt harvesting, buoyant salt pools for visitors. SOURCED: NASA 2023. Summer salt cutting by workers who take donkey carts to the lakes and chop rock salt up to 8 hours a day; one exporter says it processes >900,000 t a year in Siwa and Alexandria. UNVERIFIED (snippets; aquamagazine.com returned HTTP 403; company page not opened). [K7, K14]
- **Water rules (a shared-problem institution):** uncontrolled private well-digging made the salt lakes overflow and threatened edge groves; new drilling banned, individual springs capped, and set times assigned for each garden to draw from the main springs. SOURCED: AramcoWorld 2006. Before the 1980s the 200 springs were the only water; farmers then drilled thousands of wells without drainage, causing waterlogging and salinization; olive and date yields fell ~46% and ~55% from 2000 to 2011. SOURCED (yields): NASA 2023; wells/count UNVERIFIED (snippet). In July 2023 the government announced completion of phase two of a drainage-water project (12 deep wells at 1,200 m; 42 saline surface wells closed). UNVERIFIED (Ahram Online returned HTTP 403). [K1, K9]
- **Governance:** a town council reaching decisions by majority vote; a written Siwan constitution of "rules that govern our conduct toward one another," attributed to an ancestor of a local leader. SOURCED: AramcoWorld 2006. Annexed by Muhammad Ali in 1820; King Fuad I visited in 1928. SOURCED: Wikipedia. [K6, K7]
- **Access and goods:** paved road from Mersa Matruh, no permit needed; Siwa–Bahariya (~420 km) needs a permit arranged with the Tourist Police, with a police escort reported. UNVERIFIED (Horizons Unlimited traveler forum, snippets only). An 800 km road trip from Cairo. SOURCED: The National 2024. Military checkpoints on the route. UNVERIFIED (snippet). [K5]
- **Who lives there:** Siwi speakers, with Arabic needed for dealings outside. "No Siwans ever leave permanently. If they leave to study, they always come back" (a resident quoted). SOURCED: AramcoWorld 2006. [K4, K9]

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things

## 3 · Lake Baikal shore communities (Listvyanka, Olkhon)

**Input care note (carried forward):** Russia nation note. Facts recorded as real-world facts; nothing here is a statement about any nation in the project.

**Claim to test:** "Life organized beside the deepest, largest fresh water, with an ice season that changes how people move (K2, K5)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED and sharpened for Olkhon; weaker for Listvyanka.** For Olkhon the ice does not simply "change" movement; it creates **three access regimes and two cut-off seasons a year**: ferry (15 May–15 December), an official ice road in deep winter, and between them (freeze-up and break-up) only hovercraft. When the ice is late, people and goods are stranded (over 3,000 tourists in February 2026). For Listvyanka, which is on the mainland at the end of a road from Irkutsk, the ice shapes winter tourism (ice festival, skating, ice fishing) more than access. A second strong finding not in the claim: the protected status of the lake shore (national park, fishing ban) governs everyday life as much as the ice does.

### A. Snapshot
- **Olkhon Island:** 730 km², 72 × 21 km, largest island in Baikal; highest point Mount Zhima 1,276 m; population ~1,744, Buryats and Russians; five villages (Yalga, Malomorets, Khuzhir, Kharantsy, Ulan-Khushin); Khuzhir, the main settlement, ~1,200. Residents are fishermen, farmers or cattle ranchers, and increasingly work in tourism. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olkhon_Island (read 2026-10-29). [P3, P9]
- **Olkhon Gate (the strait):** ~8.3 km long, 1.2–2.3 km wide, 30–40 m deep (100 m in the south), strong currents and waves up to 5 m; ferry from the mainland pier (Sakhyurta, also called MRS) 15 May–15 December, operated by the Eastern-Siberian inland navigation company; an ice road in winter; an underwater high-voltage cable laid in 2005 brought the island grid electricity. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olkhon_Gate. [K5]
- **Listvyanka:** urban-type settlement ~70 km from Irkutsk at the point where the Angara leaves Baikal; founded 18th century; population 2,379 (1989), 1,745 (2002), 1,882 (2010); a 2025 report gives 2,156 registered (1,222 working age, 666 pensioners, 277 children). Reached by bus or ferry from Irkutsk. SOURCED: Wikipedia "Listvyanka, Irkutsky District". [P3, P9]

## 5 · Iquitos, Peru

**Claim to test:** "The largest city unreachable by road, where everything arrives and leaves by river or air (K5, K12)."

## 6 · Tristan da Cunha (the Potato Patches)

**Input care note (carried forward):** very small scale (~220–245 people).

**Claim to test:** "A sealed community whose food base is shared plots on scarce ground and whose supply comes on a few ships a year (K1, K5)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with one correction of wording.** The land is **communally owned**, but the plots at the Potato Patches are **held and worked by individual families** (several each, passed down through generations), not farmed in common; all families help one another plant, tend and harvest. Supply: fewer than a dozen ship visits a year from Cape Town (sources give 8–10 voyages, plus a yearly research/supply ship), with no airstrip and a harbor too small for ships. "Sealed" is accurate in law: outsiders may not buy land or settle unless the community changes its law.

### A. Snapshot
- British Overseas Territory in the South Atlantic; the only settlement is Edinburgh of the Seven Seas; population 243 (June 2021), 221 (2026 figure on Wikipedia); about 80 families; descended from ~15 ancestors (8 male, 7 female) arriving 1816–1908; the seven founding male surnames are Glass, Green, Hagan, Lavarello, Repetto, Rogers and Swain; male founders from Scotland, England, the Netherlands, the United States and Italy; female founders of mixed ancestry, believed to be from Saint Helena; three more surnames arrived with English husbands after the 1960s evacuation. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_da_Cunha (read 2026-10-29, two parts). [P9]
- **Potato Patches:** the largest plain on the island, 3.7 km southwest of the settlement, farmed since the 1860s; walled plots shield crops from the weather; reached by road; a "Potato Patches Flier" bus (a 24-seat minibus) carries pensioners. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato_Patches. The plain is ~4 km long and up to 1 km wide, beyond a hill from the settlement; rectilinear walled fields "normally without gates," cropped for "over 150 years"; "every island family owns and cultivates several patches"; "all help to plant, tend and harvest crops." SOURCED: Tristan da Cunha Government/Association website, https://www.tristandc.com/patches.php (undated page). [K1, K7] [P4]

### B. What else exists there
- **Government:** an Administrator; an Island Council (Administrator as president, three appointed and eight elected members); a Chief Islander (Ian Lavarello at time of reading). SOURCED: Wikipedia; Island Council composition UNVERIFIED (snippet). Government jobs — dozens of roles in education, health, administration and maintenance — are a main employer. SOURCED: NPR, Nick Schönfeld and Julia Gunther, 2026-04-04. [K7] [P7]
- **Economy:** commercial fishing and government; exports of rock lobster, stamps and coins; limited tourism; the fish factory burned in 2008 and reopened in July 2009; the fishing season runs July to April; the factory gives part-time work, mainly to pensioners. SOURCED: Wikipedia; NPR 2026. The shipping/fishing concession is run by Ovenstone, whose fishing vessels (MFV Edinburgh; Geo Searcher from 2017) make about eight to nine return trips a year from Cape Town carrying passengers, cargo and mail; the S.A. Agulhas II makes a yearly spring voyage, usually leaving Cape Town in early September. UNVERIFIED (snippets: Wikipedia S.A. Agulhas II, ukota.org; not opened). [K5, K8]
- **Supply (K5):** "typically fewer than a dozen visits a year." SOURCED: Wikipedia. Ships bring fuel, food, tools, gas bottles, diesel, furniture, vehicles and beer from Cape Town; fresh produce runs out quickly after arrival; dogs must be ordered from Cape Town because breeding is no longer permitted. Calshot Harbour is too small for ships, so cargo and passengers come ashore on motorized rafts and boats are lifted in and out by crane. SOURCED: NPR 2026. Grocery orders for the single store are placed a couple of months ahead; the island is reachable by boat only about 60 days a year, and May–September has "very few days" when boats can land. SOURCED: Mary Beth Albright, National Geographic, 2015-06-17. No airstrip; six-day voyage from Cape Town. SOURCED: Wikipedia. [K5] [P5]
- **Services:** Camogli Healthcare Centre (opened 2017, enabling births on the island), usually one resident doctor, government-funded; St Mary's School, children leave at 16, few take GCSEs, post-16 study is in the UK or South Africa; satellite internet from 1998, Starlink from 16 September 2024; no mobile coverage as of 2016. SOURCED: Wikipedia. [K5, K9]
- **Law:** one full-time police inspector and three special constables; no arrests for crime between 1988 and 2010. SOURCED: Wikipedia. [K6]

### C. Cultural locations
- **The Patches' huts:** dozens of huts for tools and seed potatoes, and "camping huts" with beds, stoves, chairs and tables "for a weekend away from the Settlement"; Runaway Beach nearby for rock-pool swimming; small volcanic cones in the plain. SOURCED: tristandc.com/patches.php. Many families keep small cabins there. SOURCED: NPR 2026. [K10, K4] [P4]
- **Prince Philip Hall** (receptions for christenings, birthdays, celebrations); **Albatross Bar**; **St Mary's** (Anglican) and **St Joseph's** (Catholic) churches; about 80% Anglican, 20% Catholic. SOURCED: Wikipedia; NPR 2026. [K10]
- **Customs:** christenings and first birthdays are major community occasions with 20+ godparents; an Old Year's Night tradition. SOURCED: NPR 2026. A three-week holiday beginning before Christmas, opened by "Break-Up Day" celebrations; traditional step dances, waltzes, polkas, mazurkas and schottisches. SOURCED: Wikipedia. [K2, K12]
- **Ratting Day:** an annual public holiday; in 2014 it was opened at 6:00 am on a Friday (2 May) by the Chief Islander ringing the fishing gong; teams named after areas of the Patches hunt rats; at 6:00 pm tails are counted by three judges; prizes for most tails and longest tail (28.8 cm in 2014) given at a 9:00 pm dance; 564 rats killed in 2014; rats threaten the potato stores and nesting Northern Rockhopper Penguins and albatrosses. SOURCED: Peaceful Societies (UNC Greensboro), 2014-05-22, summarizing a tristandc.com news post by Tina Glass. The date is declared each year by the Chief Islander and moved for weather. UNVERIFIED (snippet). Described as a race for most and longest tails. SOURCED: National Geographic 2015. [K2, K6, K1] [P6] [SM:competence]

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- **Scarce flat ground + wind → walled family plots on one plain**, with storage huts and weekend camping huts; seed potatoes must be guarded from rats or taken back to the settlement. SOURCED: tristandc.com. **Rats → a holiday** (Ratting Day). SOURCED. [K1, K2]
- **Equality by rule:** livestock numbers are strictly controlled to conserve pasture and to stop better-off families accumulating wealth. Communal land ownership stated SOURCED (Wikipedia; National Geographic 2015). Exact limits **conflict between sources**: two sheep per resident (SOURCED, NPR 2026); other accounts give two cows per family, four sheep per person, or two sheep per person and one cow per family (UNVERIFIED, snippets). [K1, K6, K8]
- **Isolation and the volcano:** in 1961 an eruption on the flank of Queen Mary's Peak forced the evacuation of 264 people (via Nightingale Island and Cape Town to Hampshire, England); most returned in 1963 after a Royal Society expedition found the settlement had survived. The 2001 storm brought winds of 190 km/h. SOURCED: Wikipedia. [K9]
