## 1 · McMurdo Station as a town

### 1.1 Populations (summer, winter, flow) over time
- **NSF station page (read 2026-11-01, https://www.nsf.gov/geo/opp/ail/mcmurdo-station):** "Up to 1,100 people during a week in the summer season." "Up to 200 people during a week in the winter season." "2,300 people move through the station annually." "147,956 meals are served a year." "approximately 146 buildings of varying sizes"; "a harbor, two airfields and helicopter pads"; annual mean −18 °C, lows −50 °C, highs +8 °C. **SOURCED**.
- **USAP Science Planning Summary, estimated population:** 2024–25: McMurdo **975** (weekly average) summer, **280** ("winter total"); South Pole 105 / 45; Palmer 30. 2025–26: McMurdo **952** (weekly average), **188** ("winter total"); South Pole 170 / 45; Palmer 44 / 20. (https://www.usap.gov/sciencesupport/scienceplanningsummaries/2024_2025/schedules.cfm and .../2025_2026/schedules.cfm, both fetched by curl 2026-11-01; the page says only "weekly average" and "winter total"; what "winter total" counts, a peak or a sum over the winter, is not stated.) **SOURCED**. A 2022–23 value of 1,000 / 140 came back only through a search summary and was not opened (the 2022–23 URL returned 404 by curl). **UNVERIFIED**.
- **USAP Participant Guide 2016–2018, ch. 7 (https://www.usap.gov/USAPgov/travelAndDeployment/documents/ParticipantGuide-Chapter7.pdf):** "Approximately 90% of U.S. Antarctic Program participants reside in or pass through McMurdo Station. The austral winter population ranges from 150 to 200, with the summer population varying between 800 - 1,000." **SOURCED**.
- **COMNAP Antarctic Station Catalog (USA, Aug 2017):** beds **1,200**; peak/summer staff **800**, scientists **200**; off-peak/winter staff **150**, scientists **3**; max at one time **1,200**; area under roof **32,750 m²**; labs 5,439 m²; logistics area 7,788 m²; "eighty-five or so buildings"; six airstrips; medical area 455 m² (8 medical-trained staff summer, **2 winter**; basic capability; aeromedical equipment, hyperbaric recompression chamber, diagnostic X-ray and ultrasound, telemedicine); workshops: ICTS, mechanical, metal, Plexiglas, wood. (https://www.comnap.aq/s/USA_Antarctic_Station_Catalogue_Aug2017.pdf , read via pdftotext.) **SOURCED**.
- **Master Plan 2.1 (15 Dec 2015, https://www.usap.gov/news/documents/McMurdoMasterPlan_2.1.pdf):** "Population limited up to 850 in austral summer with short term surge capacity to accommodate an extra 200 beds during contingencies"; 104 buildings on station, "approximately 45% of them produce waste in one form or another." **SOURCED**. (The 2013 plan's 900 summer and 150–250 winter, and an AIMS slide's ~1,100 / 125–200, came back in a search summary only. **UNVERIFIED**.)
- **Winter population examples:** 2022 (start of winter) "126, including primarily support staff" (MSW Magazine, 2023-05, https://www.mswmag.com/editorial/2023/05/life-at-the-bottom-of-the-water-industry; "In summer months (October to March), the population can be over 1,200"). **SOURCED**. 2018: 133 after the last summer flights, then 171 after a March flight (Antarctic Sun 2018; the Sun domain does not resolve, so only a search summary of it was seen). **UNVERIFIED**. 2009: 153 at the start of winter operations on 23 Feb 2009 (USAP archive, search summary only). **UNVERIFIED**. 2017 Wikipedia infobox: 1,000 summer / 153 winter. **UNVERIFIED** (secondary). ### 1.4 Services that exist at McMurdo (named in primary text)
- **Lodging:** "similar to college dormitories, with a community bathroom down the hall"; at least one roommate; roommates may arrive at any hour because of round-the-clock operations; "Temporary residents are defined as ASC employees staying fewer than 30 days and grantees staying fewer than 15 days." Linens, blankets and pillows provided. (Participant Guide ch. 7) **SOURCED**.
- **Power:** diesel generators at 120 V/60 Hz; **five 1,400 kW Caterpillar AN-8 diesel units** in a central plant plus **three Enercon E33 330 kW wind turbines** on Crater Hill (owned and operated by New Zealand, also feeding Scott Base, which has three 200 kW Caterpillar AN-8 units) (Master Plan 2.1, p. 86). **SOURCED**.
- **Water:** reverse osmosis from seawater. **Telephone:** 24-hour from dorms, calls routed through Denver; radios and pagers from the communications department. **Internet:** "24/7 access to the Internet over a very small (17Mb) link" shared by the whole station (2016–18 guide; the AP 2023 story says NSF was "expanding internet access through Starlink"). **Mail:** post office in Building 140 (with the Movement Control Center); "limited mail service during the winter"; address PSC 769 Box 700, APO AP 96599-9998. **TV and radio:** American Forces Network affiliates, volunteer DJs; an NSF science channel; local channels for dining menu, flight and weather information. **Recreation:** library, clubs, climbing wall, gymnasium, weight room, aerobics room, band room; volunteers organize art shows, chili cook-offs, running races, yoga, dances, music performances, league play, lessons, lectures; rental of DVDs, CDs, board games, costumes, instruments, cross-country skis. **Laundry:** free machines and detergent. **Chaplain:** a military chaplain in the austral summer (Participant Guide ch. 6: "provided by a military chaplain during the austral summer"; ch. 7 phrases it without the season); counseling, "religious and secular." **Meals:** cafeteria style, no charge, a midnight meal in summer, "Snacks, pizza, and leftovers are available at any time"; alcohol not allowed in the dining facility without station-management approval. **Store:** "a limited supply of toiletries, snacks, beverages, and souvenirs. Rationing systems help ensure that all residents have access to items." **Clinic:** walk-in and appointment, six days a week; 911 reaches the fire dispatch; X-ray, lab, pharmacy, nursing; no dentist in season; "limited capability" for dental care in winter. **Vehicles:** official use only. **Waste:** all residents sort their own trash. (All Participant Guide ch. 6 and 7.) **SOURCED**.
- **Named social buildings (Wikipedia, secondary, reading of 2026-11-01). UNVERIFIED:** Southern Exposure (formerly the Chief's Club), Gallagher's (formerly the Erebus Club), the Coffee House (Building 76, formerly the officers' club), the gym (formerly the Acey-Duecy club); Chapel of the Snows (interfaith; Protestant and Catholic services; sobriety groups); a two-lane bowling alley in use 1961–2009, demolished for structural failure and not replaced; Icestock (an annual music festival around New Year's Day, with performers from McMurdo and Scott Base, no citation); a greenhouse built 1989, expanded 1994, demolished 2011; a Toastmasters club (Club 549, 1990s to early 2000s, weekly Monday meetings, 20–25 summer members; Toastmasters magazine, https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2020/feb/sidebars/antarctica-club-549 , **SOURCED**).
- **Scott Base** is about 3 km away and shares wind power (Wikipedia, **UNVERIFIED**; the Master Plan confirms the shared wind turbines).

## 3 · Social and civic life (McMurdo, Pole, others; real events with dates)

### 3.1 Midwinter, sunrise, sunset
- **Scope and history:** "40 research stations representing 21 countries" observe Midwinter's Day; "Most exchange good wishes and greeting cards"; first celebrated 1898 by the Belgian Antarctic Expedition; "The U.S. President has sent a Midwinter greeting to Americans wintering in Antarctica since 1959" (USAP news 2024-06-27, https://www.usap.gov/news/4757/). **SOURCED.**
- **McMurdo:** "Nearly every person on station attends the special dinner" (Courtney Moffatt, a supply technician in her second McMurdo winter; USAP news 2023-06-21, https://www.usap.gov/news/4752). **SOURCED.** Travel-theme dinner (2013), beef tenderloin, roast pig and lobster tails (2014) and shared meals with Scott Base came back only as Antarctic Sun summaries. **UNVERIFIED.**
- **South Pole, by year (southpolestation.com winter pages; SOURCED):** 2010 dinner Fri 18 June (menu border art by a crew member; Obama's greeting letter); 2012 dinner Sat 23 June, the sunset "dinner" run as an all-day "Farmer's Market" with a lunch course in the greenhouse; 2015 manager John O'Connor read the presidential letter aloud; 2018 dinner Sat 23 June orchestrated by head chef Zeke Mills; 2019 dinner Sat 22 June (lobster tail, leg of lamb, plus "sledging biscuits, dried fish, two varieties of pemmican" from the book *Hoosh*); 2022 dinner Sun 19 June plus a **masquerade ball**, and a **sunrise brunch** on Sun 18 Sept (tie-dye theme honoring Johan Booth, who wintered at Pole 14 times); 2023 dinner Sat 24 June with **a special table for the four people "who did not leave Pole alive"** (Casey Jones 1980, Rodney Marks 2000, Thomas Atkins 2014, Andrew Moulder 1966); 2024 dinner 21 June with flowers from the greenhouse on the tables and roast beef among "many course options"; 2025 dinner 20 June, masquerade party after, appetizer spread; 2026 dinner Fri 19 June and a **sunset dinner 20 March**, with winter crests from Medical, Science and Supply. Greeting cards (a composite photo of the crew) are made each year and sent to all stations (2019: designed by two winterers, with a midwinter greeting video).
- **Winter-start film tradition:** "as tradition dictates" crews watch all three versions of *The Thing* when winter begins (IceCube weekly updates, search summaries; also a 2019 essay). **UNVERIFIED.**

### 3.2 The 300 Club, Race Around the World, film festival
- **300 Club (https://www.southpolestation.com/winter/300club.html , read 2026-11-01).** Members run from the sauna outside when the official met-office thermometer reads −100 °F or colder, around the Ceremonial Pole; in 1977 "everyone but Marshall qualified" (the page gives the sauna temperature as not stated; other pages say about 200 °F, search summaries). ### 3.4 Rules, conduct, alcohol, jurisdiction
- **Polar Code of Conduct (Participant Guide Appendix, https://www.usap.gov/USAPgov/travelAndDeployment/documents/ParticipantGuide-Appendix.pdf ; NSF OPP policy v1, July 2018 per USAP news 4371). ## 7 · Municipal infrastructure as a service at McMurdo (who runs it, how many, and what failed, with dates)

### 7.1 Power (plus fuel) as a service
- **Plant:** "five 1400kW Caterpillar AN8 diesel powered electric generation units in a central plant," plus three 330 kW wind turbines on Crater Hill owned and operated by New Zealand that also serve Scott Base (which has three 200 kW Caterpillar units). Distribution is "overhead on power poles," "non-redundant, non-looped, radial," described as "cost effective ... but less than reliable or desirable"; "Servicing overhead power lines, even during the best of weather conditions, is challenging for maintenance personnel." (McMurdo Master Plan 2.1, 15 Dec 2015, p. 86.) **SOURCED.**
- **Fuel for power and heat:** "McMurdo Station requires 800,000 gallons of fuel to produce electricity and another 500,000 gallons of fuel to heat buildings per year"; the plan targeted a cut of about 550,000 gallons (42%). Seven 65 kW Capstone microturbines (combined heat and power) and wood-chip waste-to-heat boilers were planned (2015). **SOURCED.** Whether those were built: **NOT FOUND.**
- **Who runs it:** postings (§1.2): Utilities Supervisor; Power Plant Technician (operator on rotating day/swing/night shifts; two years' plant experience); Lead Power Plant Mechanic; Boiler Mechanic; Utility Mechanic; "linemen" (a 2026 blog says "the linemen were able to fix the power lines"). Count of power-plant staff: **NOT FOUND.**
- **Fuels department:** 24 in peak season, 2 winter-over (2019); stores "a three-year supply of fuel"; moves 1.2–1.5 million gallons of AN-8 a year through the hose line to Williams Field; the tanker offloads about 185,000 gal of motor gasoline, 850,000 gal of JP-5 and 6.7 million gal of AN-8 "every other year" over 72 to 96 hours; supplies the Pole traverse and LC-130s (Aviation Pros, 2019-09-12). **SOURCED.**

### 7.2 Water, wastewater, and solid waste as services
- **Water (reverse osmosis from seawater):** "required to keep at least 160,000 gallons of water in their storage tanks at all times"; maximum storage 204,000 gal, "typically never falls below 170,000"; heat exchangers warm the intake because "the intake/raw water comes from the frigid Ross Sea"; staff in winter 2022: one certified (Grade 2) operator plus two water-plant mechanics; the water-plant story gives winter population 126 at the start of winter 2022 (MSW Magazine, 2023-05). **SOURCED.** Daily production of 40,000 gal (a 2010s museum blog) and 55,000 gal (two field blogs), a recovery of 1 gal fresh per 3 gal seawater and a 74 gal/min intake (PolarTREC entries) all came only through search summaries and conflict. **UNVERIFIED.**
- **Wastewater (Building 199):** macerators, strainer, aeration basins with activated sludge, clarifier, digester, compressor/belt press, UV disinfection, discharge into McMurdo Sound by outfall; solids "sent back to the United States"; "needs a comparatively small staff to operate" (brr.fyi, 2022-10-23). **SOURCED.** The plant was called "the largest plant on the continent," "ran well at about 20,000 gpd," "highest summer flow was 52,524 gpd"; wastewater discharge "ongoing since the 1950s"; the Treaty requires grinding for stations averaging 30 or more people, NSF chose to exceed it; corrosion and pinhole leaks patched in winter with epoxy flown in; "If the grinder pumps failed, untreated discharge could reach the environment within 60 seconds"; soda ash feed "not feasible because large volumes cannot be transported" (Raney, TPO Magazine, 2019-05-16, an interview with an operator who served five months from October 2016). **SOURCED.**
- **Solid waste:** McMurdo "is also the waste management center for much of the U.S. Antarctic Program"; "All USAP refuse – except wastewater – is removed from the continent"; the annual supply vessel carries it out; every resident sorts trash (Participant Guide). **SOURCED.** A waste-to-energy unit was in use for cardboard, wood and burnables (Master Plan 2.1). **SOURCED.** About 3 million pounds of solid waste a year at about 60% recycling (2016) and hazardous waste about 15% of the total, over 40 containers a year (2011) came only through summaries (the Waste360 page returned HTTP 403). **UNVERIFIED.**
- **Fire water:** the plan found the existing system "not capable of delivering the required fire flow," with a 100,000 gallon utility tank at sea level and a proposed 1,000,000 gallons of fire-water storage (two tanks of 500,000) (Master Plan 2.1). **SOURCED.**

### 8.1 Concordia (France and Italy; Dome C, 3,233 m)
- **People:** COMNAP 2017: summer 35 staff + 35 scientists (max 80), winter 8 staff + 5 scientists, 2 medical staff in both seasons ("Basic, Dental, Surgery"); 3,605 m² under roof (prior file §6.3). ESA, 2024–25 text: winter crew "about 12," "up to 80 people share the base" in summer, the ESA doctor stays "for 13 months" (https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/20_years_in_Antarctica). **SOURCED.**
- **Job mix:** ESA (2012-11-23, "Ready for winter"): "requires a minimum of seven people" to run the base in winter: chef, doctor, mechanic, technical manager, plumber, electrician, telecommunications expert; plus eight scientists (chemist, two astronomers, an astrophysicist, a glaciologist named; three unnamed) = at least 15; "minimum eight-month stay," about four months without sunlight. **SOURCED.** The first winterover (2005) had thirteen (eleven French, two Italians; a search summary of a first-winter diary). **UNVERIFIED.**
- **Distinctive:** an ESA-sponsored medical doctor each year who "also leads search and rescue and serves as a research subject"; questionnaires in French, English and Italian; video diaries; chronic low oxygen; the cook "keeps morale up" (ESA pages). **SOURCED.** No greenhouse (COMNAP "No"). **NOT FOUND:** any dated failure, fire, outage or death during a Concordia winter (one search, `Concordia station Dome C winter-over incident accident...`; **died at the query**: general background only).

### 8.2 Rothera (UK; Adelaide Island, sea level)
- **People:** COMNAP 2017: summer 120 staff + 40 scientists (= 160, matching Wikipedia's "maximum population of 160 in the summer"), winter 22 staff + 5 scientists (= 27, matching "average of 27" winter), 136 beds; continuously occupied since 25 October 1975. **SOURCED** (COMNAP) / **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia). A BAS news item gives a "22-member winter team" (Polar Journal, search summary). **UNVERIFIED.**
- **Job mix (winter):** a 2021 account by a former wintering member: mostly non-scientists, "electricians, mechanics, plumbers, carpenters, a chef and a doctor"; BAS recruits a Winter Station Leader inside a management team that includes operations and logistics managers; in winter the Bonner Laboratory is run by three diving-and-boating officers and two marine biologists (Wikipedia). In 2023 a construction team of eight stayed seven months over the winter to fit out a new building (Construction Index, search summary). **UNVERIFIED.**
- **Services and community (Wikipedia):** New Bransfield House (opened 2008) with dining area, kitchen, bar, library and post office/station shop; reverse-osmosis plant in Old Bransfield House; Admirals House with 44 two-person rooms; Dash 7 aircraft with about 20 flights a season; four Volvo Penta generators; the BAS house band Nunatak. **UNVERIFIED.**
- **Failure:** the first Bonner Laboratory was destroyed by fire after an electrical fault in winter 2001 (September 2001 in coolantarctica.com; "gusts over 80 knots"; "Three of five research projects in progress could not continue"; no injuries); rebuilt, reopened 2003/04. **SOURCED** (coolantarctica) / **UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia). Rothera also supplied the Twin Otters for the three winter Pole evacuations (prior file §3.4).

### 8.3 Halley VI (UK; Brunt Ice Shelf)
- **People:** COMNAP 2017: summer 52 staff + 18 scientists, winter 13 staff + 4 scientists (= 17, matching Wikipedia's "17 winter (2017)"), 52 beds, 2,000 m² under roof. **SOURCED** (COMNAP; sums agree).
- **Job mix (2016 winter team, Wikipedia):** chef, doctor, communications manager, vehicle mechanic, generator mechanic, electrician, plumber, field assistant, two electronics engineers, meteorologist, data manager, led by a winter station leader "sworn in as a magistrate before deployment." **UNVERIFIED.**
- **Distinctive:** modular design (eight modules on hydraulic legs with retractable skis, opened 5 Feb 2013, about £26 million); a color psychologist's palette "to offset the more than 100 days of darkness each year," daylight-simulation alarm clocks (Wikipedia). **UNVERIFIED.**
- **What failed:** the "Halloween Crack" was discovered 31 October 2016; the station was moved 23 km (completed February 2017); BAS withdrew all staff from March to October 2017 (88 people on station when the 2017 withdrawal was announced, most summer-only) and "Staff have been removed every winter since"; as of 2020 the base was left unstaffed through winter since 2017 (Wikipedia and press summaries). **UNVERIFIED** for details; the multi-source consistency on "unstaffed in winter since 2017" is high, but the **current (2026) winter status was NOT FOUND**.

### 8.4 Casey, Davis, Mawson (Australia)
- **Casey:** COMNAP 2017: summer 67 staff + 32 scientists (max 99; beds 99), winter 18 staff + 2 scientists; medical staff 2 summer, 1 winter ("Basic, Dental, Surgery"; anaesthesia, blood-transfusion, haematology and X-ray equipment); one airstrip, 20 flight visits a year; hydroponics "Yes"; 8,000 m² under roof; replaced buildings established 1969. AAD living page (last updated 2013-06-19): "About 16–20 people remain on station over winter," "150–160 expeditioners visit Casey" in summer; the Red Shed with gym, climbing wall, library and home theater; bar "Splinters" (pool and darts); store "Woollies"; "Expeditioners dress up for dinner every Saturday night"; bands; hydroponics building; private bedrooms with a bathroom shared by three. **SOURCED.** Winter 2025: "the 33 diverse, skilled, and committed expeditioners of Casey Station Winter 2025" (station leader Andy Warton, AAD news 2025-04-17), with doctor, chef and tradespeople, lay surgical assistant training, search-and-rescue and fire teams, and a hydroponics room (**SOURCED**; conflicts with the 16–20 figure; the 33 may include non-standard staff, not stated). Summer 2022: two doctors typical; a plumbing team (AAD news, March 2022). **SOURCED.**
- **Davis:** COMNAP: summer 62 + 29 (max 91), winter 15 staff; medical 1 and 1. AAD (updated 2022-09-08): "more than 100 expeditioners over summer," "about 20 over winter," own rooms in winter, shared in summer; library, theatrette, small spa and sauna, climbing wall, volleyball, badminton and table-tennis gear, "Woolies" store, a hydroponic shed, chef with "slushy duty"; limited water (summer showers "one every three days" of "three minutes"). **SOURCED.**
- **Mawson:** COMNAP: 53 beds, max 53; summer staff 50; medical 1 and 1; 10 flight visits a year; 240 V, 24 hours. AAD (updated 2020-09-15): everyone lives in the "Red Shed" in single-room "dongas," library and home theater, a gym in the Green Store, "Woolies," slushy duty, a 30 m wind turbine, established 1954, "the longest continuously operated station in Antarctica," ships take 10 to 12 days from Hobart. **SOURCED.** Mawson's own winter count: **NOT FOUND** (COMNAP field blank in the extraction).
- **Contracts and medical:** 4 to 15 months; winter staff complete a winter and summer (AAD recruitment, summary, **UNVERIFIED**). Medical evacuations: Casey 2023 (the icebreaker Nuyina made a 1,800-mile trip); a Davis evacuation (year not shown) (press summaries). **UNVERIFIED.** No Casey, Davis or Mawson fire, outage or death found (one search; **died at the query**).

### 8.5 Neumayer III (Germany; Ekström Ice Shelf)
- **People (Franke et al., *Polarforschung* 90, 2022). SOURCED:** "Nine team members collaborate ... including four scientists, who are responsible for the operation of the scientific observatories, three technicians attending to station maintenance, power supply, and telecommunications, one physician, and one cook. One of the candidates (usually the physician) also acts as the station leader." The 45th team (introduced March 2025) lists: doctor and station leader (Julia Gutting), station engineer and deputy leader (Thomas Schenk), cook (Thorben Koeppen), radio operator and IT engineer ("Alex"), electrical engineer (Regine Kerschner), atmospheric chemist, two geophysicists, meteorologist (Helmholtz blog, https://blogs.helmholtz.de/atkaxpress/en/2025/03/this-is-us/ ). **SOURCED.** A German press item says eight since February 2026 (search summary; **UNVERIFIED**).
- **Rhythm and selection:** 14-month season, about 9 months fully isolated; arrival December, departure end of February; overlap with the outgoing team November–February; 4-month training including firefighting and rescue; required competencies include "high intrinsic motivation, strong communication skills ... emotional maturity, a good sense of humour," with "best leadership practices" that "minimize hierarchies" (Franke et al. 2022). **SOURCED.**
- **History of crew make-up:** more than 370 overwinterers 1981–2022 across the three Neumayer stations; "Mixed-gender teams were not sent until 1996"; an all-female team in 1990 at the Georg Forster station (the first all-female wintering team at any Antarctic station); average crew age 30–40 (Franke et al. 2022). **SOURCED.**
- **Services and life:** a "permanent internet connection," telemedicine; the cook plans food for the whole season; fresh food only November–February except during EDEN ISS years (§4.3); sauna, sports corner, "library in the ice," workshop, balcony, table tennis (45th-team page). **SOURCED.** COMNAP: 2,900 m² under roof; medical facility 56 m² (field misaligned). The station supports an emperor-penguin observation system that the crew must keep running, relocating it when the shelf ice threatened the site (Franke et al.). **SOURCED.**
