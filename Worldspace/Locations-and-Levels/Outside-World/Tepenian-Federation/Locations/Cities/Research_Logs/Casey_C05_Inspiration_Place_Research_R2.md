### A. Other settings (sub-areas and what they are like)
- **The port's terminals as wind-governed workplaces [K7, K6, K1, K14] [R] [SM:competence under pressure]:** a 31 May 2019 report by the local health authority's workplace-safety unit (A.S.U.I.Ts, S.C.P.S.A.L.; Paolo Toffanin, Simon Crocco, Gabriele D'Orlando) for the port's Hygiene and Safety Committee (CIS) was started on 6 March 2019 at the request of the workers' safety representatives (R.L.S.). It notes "numerous" wind accidents over the years (buildings, equipment, goods), says each terminal had built its own rules after its own accidents rather than to a common standard, and reviews seven terminals' procedures. Examples: **Terminal 1 (containers):** a roof anemometer drives three states shown green or red: normal operations below 70 km/h; "empties block" (the empty-container area closed to all vehicles) at 72-90 km/h (5 gusts in 10 minutes) or one gust of 90-110; "yard block" (all yard operations stop) at 90-110 km/h (5 in 10 min) or one gust over 110; a yard block is announced by radio, by **a long blast of the siren**, and by a "yard blocked due to wind" message to vehicle handhelds; quay cranes at the pier head stop at 80 km/h and are pinned; others at 100 km/h; boom movement allowed up to 120 km/h with the coordinator's permission; **work resumes only after 30 minutes with no gust above the limit.** **Terminal 2 (ro-ro and rail):** double-stack roll-trailer moves stop at 55 km/h; train loading and container handling stop at 65 km/h; no one on foot in the double-stack trailer park above 85 km/h; telehandler stacking height falls by tiers (4 high at 50-60 km/h, 3 at 60-70, 2 at 70-80); warning lights around the terminal and an app that notifies staff. **Terminal 3:** software sends SMS alerts to the terminal director, operations manager and two yard foremen: below 72 normal; 72-90 "pre-alarm" (empties stop); 90-110 "alarm" (all containers stop); over 110 "emergency" (total stop). **Terminals 4 and 5** had no anemometer and read public websites (Windguru, the university's meteo page, Windfinder, ISMAR). **Terminal 6 (dry bulk tower):** rail clamps engaged above 70 km/h; after three readings over 70, the suction arm is pulled from the hold and anchored; above 85 the tower is moved to its safe position, pinned and powered down. **Terminal 7:** unloading allowed until the 20-second mean wind reaches 33.5 kn (61.2 km/h). Accidents illustrated: a trailer overturned, an unloading tower collapsed, a quay crane dragged off its rails when a ship's mooring lines broke, a rubber-tired crane overturned, empty containers blown over "by the winter bora." The report's OSMER-ARPA FVG excerpt: bora episodes with hourly mean over 10 m/s for more than 5 consecutive hours "are not at all rare"; gusts "largely exceed 30 m/s," over 40 m/s recorded in the last 20 years; **29 January-12 February 2012: 15 days in which the daily maximum wind never fell below 27 m/s (100 km/h)**; 46.7 m/s (168 km/h) recorded on 11/2 (printed as 2007 in this report; other sources give 2012); an 8 August 2008 westerly front gave 34.4 m/s in Trieste and 45.3 m/s at the Paloma buoy. It proposes common rules for all port firms (area anemometers, alarm systems, storm locks on rail cranes, designated empty-container areas with height limits, training). SOURCED: https://adspmao.it/file/relazione-sui-rischi-derivanti-dal-vento-presso-il-porto-trieste-rev-00-31-05-19.pdf (read by local text extraction). Whether a common port-wide rule was later adopted: NOT FOUND.

### B. Subcultures
- **The Slovene minority and its schools [K4, K9, K11]:** in 2020/21 the Slovene-language and bilingual school network in Italy had 4,301 pupils, **2,435 of them in the province of Trieste** (57%); primary schools in Trieste and Gorizia fell from 56 (1955/56) to 28 (2020/21); network-wide 34 nursery, 29 primary, 10 lower-secondary, 6 upper-secondary schools; all state schools except 4 municipal nurseries in the Trieste area; ministerial curricula plus extra history and geography; textbooks imported from Slovenia or produced locally. Enrollment rose 30% in 2001-2021 because **non-Slovene-speaking families** increasingly choose these schools: among first-year pupils in 2019/20, 27% had Slovene as mother tongue, 41% a non-Slovene mother tongue; 43% speak mainly Italian or another language outside school, 24% mainly Slovene; the community feels "contrasting sentiments" (wider knowledge of Slovene, but Italian replacing Slovene in unguided school activity). About one Trieste-Gorizia child in ten attended a Slovene primary school (2019/20: 10.7%). SOURCED: Norina Bogatec, "Il sistema scolastico sloveno in Italia" (University of Trieste OpenstarTs PDF, read by text extraction; file dated 2022). **March 2026 friction:** the regional school office proposed merging three pairs of Slovene schools in Trieste (Slomšek and Prešeren high schools; Žiga Zois and Jožef Stefan; San Giacomo and Vladimir Bartol comprehensive schools), cutting Slovene head-teacher posts in Italy from 14 to 11 over 2027-2030; opposed by Slovenska skupnost and a letter led by Senator Tatjana Rojc; the same report says a district council rejected a motion for Slovene sections in two new nursery schools. SOURCED: https://www.rtvslo.si/capodistria/radio-capodistria/notizie/friuli-venezia-giulia/trieste-polemiche-per-la-proposta-di-fusione-di-alcune-scuole-slovene/776062 (11 March 2026). The Slovene daily **Primorski dnevnik**: first issue 13 May 1945 (successor to the wartime Partizanski dnevnik), the only Slovene daily published outside Slovenia; regional funding has been cut at times. UNVERIFIED (search summaries of Wikipedia/STA/ANSA). [P9]
- **Karst-village Slovene life, two festivals [K2, K3, K14]:** (1) **Kraški pust** (Karst Carnival), Opicina: its committee is credited with being, in **1967**, the first to bring the area's Slovenes together in one event; 50th edition 2017, 57th 2026; a parade of floats built by teams from Karst villages (Medjevas and Štivan, Padriče and Gropada, and others), at most 23 groups (2017) to keep the parade short; a Friday-evening mask parade at the Prosvetni dom where **masks must be handmade**; the carnival king and queen are chosen by rotation among villages (2017 by lottery among all previous royals); the prefecture requires a security plan; about EUR 23,000 a year from the regional budget (2017). SOURCED: https://www.rtvslo.si/rojaki/italija/priprave-na-pustni-praznik-s-50-sveckami/413738 (30 January 2017; read in Slovene). 2026 date conflicts across listings: UNVERIFIED. (2) **Karst Wedding** (Kraška ohcet), Repen/Monrupino, every two years in the last week of August: a five-day re-creation of 19th-century customs ending in **real weddings** of real couples, valid religiously and civilly (2025: one couple, married 31 August at 11:00 in the church on the Rock of Monrupino): Wednesday opening at the Karst House; Thursday a pre-wedding party where the bride arrives on a cart and dances a last time as unmarried; Friday the groom's serenade of Slovene songs under her window (her mother keeps them apart); Saturday the dowry carried to the new house on an ox cart; Sunday a costumed procession, wedding, and dancing in the square. The organizers choose couples tied to the Repen community; girls do the flowers, young men the decorations, everyone contributes money and labor; a 2022 interviewee from the Slovene minority called it important because it builds a community sense the area is "slowly losing"; it returned in 2022 after a nine-year pause; Slovenia's president attended in 2025. SOURCED: https://www.discover-trieste.it/discover/trieste-and-surroundings/karst/karst-wedding ; https://intrieste.com/2022/08/24/the-importance-of-karst-weddings/ ; https://intrieste.com/2025/08/31/tradition-renewed-karst-weddings-bring-monrupino-to-life/. ### C. Leisure industries
- **Sea bathing as the summer city [K10, K6]:** the **Bagno Marino La Lanterna**, a municipal sea bath near Molo Fratelli Bandiera, official opening 1903 (perhaps earlier), described as the last sex-segregated bathing establishment in Italy: a white wall over three meters high (74 m long, UNVERIFIED) runs into the sea; one entrance for men, one for **women and children under 12**; entering the other side needs a special permit; paid entry from 1938 (EUR 1 in 2015, with monthly and season passes); the wall was demolished once (1959) and rebuilt a few meters further, giving women more space because women and children were the main users; fully restored 2009. In 1943 the newspaper Il Piccolo argued for removing the wall; an informal consultation later found residents overwhelmingly wanted it kept; defenders frame it as free choice. SOURCED: https://www.ilpost.it/2015/08/03/spiaggia-trieste-muro/ (3 August 2015). Disputes over a woman crossing into the men's side and over the burkini: UNVERIFIED (headlines only). The popular name of this bath and its origin are recorded only in the slang file. [K6] [P4] [P7]
