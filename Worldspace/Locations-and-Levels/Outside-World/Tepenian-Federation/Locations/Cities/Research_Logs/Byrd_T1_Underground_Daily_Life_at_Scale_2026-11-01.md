### 1.1 Cappadocia: how the underground settlements were used

**Pages read (WebFetch):** TRT World, "Aliens and priests: The many myths of Cappadocia's hidden world," https://www.trtworld.com/article/18199105 · English Wikipedia, "Kaymakli underground city," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaymakli_underground_city · showcaves.com, Kaymaklı, https://showcaves.com/english/tr/subterranea/Kaymakli.html · showcaves.com, Nevşehir, https://showcaves.com/english/tr/subterranea/Nevsehir.html · Hürriyet Daily News, "Underground city comes to surface," 15 February 2016, https://hurriyetdailynews.com/underground-city-comes-to-surface-95221 · Bursa Uludağ University thesis record, https://acikerisim.uludag.edu.tr/handle/11452/14870.

**How many settlements.**
- **257 underground settlements discovered in Cappadocia. SOURCED (secondary, attributed to a named research group).** TRT World: *"a total of 257 underground settlements have been discovered in Cappadocia."* The article names the mapping body as the Obruk Cave Research Group, which "works with local authorities to map these structures." No primary inventory was read.

**How long people stayed below.**
- **Weeks, not years, and always with a surface economy. SOURCED (specialist opinion, not a measured record).** TRT World quotes Ali Yamaç (speleologist, Obruk Cave Research Group): *"locals could likely shelter underground for weeks at a time,"* would *"eventually need to surface to replenish their resources,"* and *"staying underground for extended periods wouldn't be feasible."* The article: *"it's unclear how long people could remain underground"* and *"One certainty is that a presence on the surface was essential for their survival."* Veronica Kalas (historian and archaeologist): *"There were still crops being cultivated and animals being raised or grazed aboveground."*
- **A dated use as refuge: 1909. SOURCED (Wikipedia citing Dawkins 1916, *Modern Greek in Asia Minor*).** Kaymaklı article: *"a great part of the population at Axo took refuge in these underground chambers."* The duration is not given. The primary book was not read.
- **"Months" claims are inferences from storage volume.** Several popular pages say storage could support people "for several months" (search 1); none cites a record. UNVERIFIED.
- A 2026 Springer book (Ali Yamaç and Ezgi Tok) is described in a search snippet as stating that the shelters were *"estimated to have been constructed by the local population between the 7th and 11th centuries AD to protect themselves against Arab raids"* and that they *"have not been extensively archaeologically studied."* UNVERIFIED (the Springer page redirected to a login; died at the source).

**How levels were used: Kaymaklı.**
- **Wikipedia (uncited description section): SOURCED as Wikipedia's statement, not as a primary survey.** *"Of the four floors open to tourists, each space is organized around ventilation shafts."* *"A stable is located on the first floor."* *"To the right of the stables are rooms, possibly living spaces."* Second floor: *"a church with a nave and two apses"* and *"some living spaces."* Third floor: *"storage places, wine or oil presses"*. Fourth floor: *"The high number of storage rooms and areas for earthenware jars on the fourth floor indicates some economic stability."* Ventilation: *"the design of each room or open space dependent on the availability of ventilation"*; one shaft *"about 80 meters (260 feet) long"* (citing Martin 2017).
- **showcaves.com: SOURCED (secondary).** *"eight levels, big enough to house 5,000 people"*; *"only four are open to the public"*; top level *"used mainly as stables for domestic animals"*; second level *"a christian church and a cemetery, some living rooms, and a huge rock used to block the entrance"*; third floor *"storage of grain, flour and wine, and contains the winery and the flour mill"* (the page spells it "floor"); fourth floor *"a communal kitchen with several food storage areas and ovens."*
- **The settlement and the village are one place.** Wikipedia: *"The houses in the village are constructed around the nearly one hundred tunnels of the underground city"* and *"The tunnels are still used today as storage areas, stables, and cellars."* showcaves.com: *"The top level was used until recent time"* and *"The owners of the surrounding houses created entrances and used it as cellars."* SOURCED (secondary). Opened to tourists in 1964 (Wikipedia, uncited).
- **Capacity conflict:** showcaves.com gives 5,000; popular pages give about 15,000 (search 1). Neither cites a basis. Logged under Unresolved conflicts.

**Nevşehir Castle hill: what a "new underground city" turned out to be.**
- **Found by demolition for housing renewal. SOURCED.** Hürriyet Daily News (2016): *"The underground city was discovered by a Turkish Housing Development Administration (TOKİ) urban transformation project."* *"Some 1,500 buildings located in and around the Nevşehir fortress were demolished."* Özcan Çakır (associate professor of geophysics, 18 March University): *"We think that people who were involved with agriculture in the ancient age carried their products to the city"* through tunnels.
- **Early scale claims vs later classification. CONFLICT.** A search snippet quotes the mayor saying the city lay within 45 ha of a 75 ha project area (search 3; UNVERIFIED, not read in full). showcaves.com, after excavation (2014 to 2020): the site *"has only one (perhaps two) levels below the surface"*; *"The rooms are not interconnected through tunnels"*, so it classes the site as a rock city (*kayaşehir*), not an underground city; *"Each cave has an entrance to the surface."* Partly opened in summer 2020.
- **Before discovery the spaces were ordinary cellars. SOURCED (secondary).** showcaves.com: *"The caves were well known to the inhabitants of the houses above and used as cellars."* *"The city purchased and demolished the houses."*

**Cappadocia's underground space in use today, at scale: naturally cooled rock stores.**
- **SOURCED (master's thesis abstract, primary repository record).** Zeynep Tarımcı, *Nevşehir ili doğal soğutmalı yer altı depolarının mevcut durumu, sorunları ve çözüm önerileri* ("Current status, problems and solution proposals of naturally cooled underground stores in Nevşehir province"), master's thesis, Bursa Uludağ University, 2020 (advisor Cihat Türkben), https://acikerisim.uludag.edu.tr/handle/11452/14870:
  - **1,141 underground stores** in Nevşehir province; **total capacity 1,281,100 tons**.
  - Products: table potatoes, seed potatoes and lemons. Potatoes kept at **4 to 8 °C and 80 to 95% relative humidity for 7 to 8 months**; lemons at **8 to 12 °C and 85 to 95% for 9 to 10 months**.
  - Survey of **90 operators** (face-to-face). **87.8%** of stores belong to their operators; over half the operators have 5 to 20 years in the trade.
  - Problems named: weak organization (*"Yetersiz bir örgütlenmeye sahip,"* "has inadequate organization"); little say over pricing or cash sales; **no insurance** for stored-product losses; operators lack training in storage conditions, pests and diseases, and **proper construction**.
- Other counts in the press and a parliamentary speech: about 1,100 stores and 900,000 t (Grand National Assembly speech), 1,200 stores and 1,150,000 t (Gazete Vatan), 1,321 stores (Anadolu Agency via Bengütürk), 1,300 to 1,400 (Cumhuriyet), "more than 2,000" tuff stores (a DergiPark article). All from search snippets (search 12): UNVERIFIED individually. About 115,000 t of Mediterranean lemons stored in Cappadocia (TRT Haber snippet): UNVERIFIED.
- **Neighbor named:** these stores are the present-day, commercial descendant of the same rock and the same cellar habit seen at Kaymaklı and Nevşehir. They show underground space at a regional scale run as a business of about a thousand small owner-operators rather than a civic project.

### 1.2 Coober Pedy: how the town lives (additions to T10)

**Pages read (WebFetch):** Il Globo, Laura Egan, "Bob Del Tedesco on life in the underground town of Coober Pedy" (about 2020), https://ilglobo.com/en/news/bob-del-tedesco-on-life-in-the-underground-town-of-coober-pedy-54122 · ARENA project page, "Coober Pedy Renewable Diesel Hybrid," https://arena.gov.au/projects/coober-pedy-renewable-diesel-hybrid · InDaily, "Short-term fix for Coober Pedy's long-term problem," 2 February 2023, https://www.indailysa.com.au/news/archive/2023/02/02/short-term-fix-for-coober-pedys-long-term-problem · Baggs and Wong 1987 (PDF, text extracted; see Item 4).

**A dugout from the inside. SOURCED (one long-time resident's account, Il Globo).** Bob Del Tedesco (aged 73; arrived 1967 as a 20-year-old bricklayer; mined opal; first licensee of the Italo-Australian Miners Club):
- Temperature: *"a comfortable, even temperature ranging from 19ºC to 25ºC day and night throughout the year."* Other sources give 23 to 25 °C (search 5, tourism association pages via snippet). Conflict of range only.
- Layout and digging: *"It was a 25-meter-long tunnel with rooms running off it like a rabbit warren"*; dug by *"carving into the ground and wheeling the soil out in wheelbarrows."* Search 5 snippets say newer dugouts are machine-dug and the walls sealed against dust (UNVERIFIED).
- *"The dugouts have all the amenities of traditional houses: internet access, electricity and water."* *"The only difference ... is that the latter have no windows or access to sunlight."* *"It's dead quiet, pitch black, and beautiful and restful."*
- Earlier hardship: *"We were on water rations until the early '70s."* *"The town also had no electricity and locals had to generate their own power."* Distance: *"Port Augusta is our nearest major town, and that's 540 kilometers away"*.
- Social life: *"the Italo-Australian Miners Club, which was established in the 1960s"* run *"thanks to the tireless work of local volunteers"*; also *"the Serbian, Croatian and Greek clubs."* Leisure: *"the local (dirt) golf course provides golfers with squares of carpet for their tees."*
- Composition: *"Around 60 percent of the local community are of European descent"*; newer arrivals *"from South Asian countries such as Pakistan, India and Sri Lanka."* The town is also *"a government center for Indigenous Australians"*.
- **Greek community (snippet only, UNVERIFIED):** Neos Kosmos (2012): Greek migration peaked 1960 to 1970 *"with over 1000 Greeks"*; the community had a kafenio (coffeehouse), a Greek restaurant, a community hall, *"a Greek school for the kids and a Greek Church"*; *"no more than 100"* Greeks today. A Vice guide's "about 600 Greeks out of 4,500 residents" conflicts with the 2021 census total of 1,566 and is treated as unreliable.
- **Serbian community (snippet only, UNVERIFIED):** *"the church, the parish house, a religious school and a community hall - were dug by the Serbian opal miners."*

### 2.6 Neighbor: Japan's underground shopping streets (*chikagai*) and what regulation followed failure

**Page read (WebFetch, PDF text extracted locally):** Keita Hosoya, Ryo Inoue and Hisataka Kobayashi, "Spatial composition and characteristics of plazas in underground shopping malls across Japan," *AIJ Journal of Technology and Design* 29 (73): 1425 to 1430, October 2023, https://doi.org/10.3130/aijt.29.1425 (repository copy at Shimane University). **Why named:** Japan built the largest national family of underground shopping streets and regulated them after two disasters; Sapporo's are part of it.

Plazas host events, sales and rest areas; one has a stage with a large screen, lighting and sound used for regular music events. ## Item 4: Health and mind in windowless and underground life

### 4.1 Light, the body clock and the sense of time without daylight

**Isolation experiments (neighbors: the classic evidence base on people living below ground without time cues).**
- **The Andechs bunker. SOURCED.** English Wikipedia, "Bunker experiment," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunker_experiment: *"The experiment began in 1966 and continued until the early 1980s"*; a windowless bunker in Andechs, Upper Bavaria, with bedroom, kitchen and shower and two doors of which only one could be open at a time; subjects lived there *"for weeks at a time"* cut off from *"all time and light cues"*; free-running rhythms of *"approximately 25 hours"*; core temperature and sleep-wake cycles could drift apart (*internal desynchronization*); intense light shortened the period by about 0.7 to 1 h, and brighter light (3,000 lux) widened the range over which the clock could be entrained; later controlled studies put the intrinsic period at about 24.2 h (Czeisler et al. 1999). Primary abstract, Aschoff, Gerecke and Wever, "Desynchronization of human circadian rhythms," *Japanese Journal of Physiology* 17 (4): 450 to 457 (1967), https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjphysiol1950/17/4/17_4_450/_article/-char/ja: *"Fifty human subjects have been kept in an underground bunker"*; *"average periods of wakefulness and sleep ranging from 23.9 to 50.0 hours"*; in two subjects desynchronization began immediately and in seven *"after 9 to 23 days of confinement."* A snippet gives 418 studies on 447 volunteers between 1964 and 1989: UNVERIFIED.
- **Michel Siffre. SOURCED (Boston Globe obituary, 4 September 2024),** https://www.bostonglobe.com/2024/09/04/world/michel-siffre-scientist-solitude-deep-within-caves-dies-85/: 1962, about eight weeks alone 130 m down in the Scarasson abyss; he believed it was 20 August when it was 10 days later, and said his psychological time *"had compressed by a factor of two"*; 1972, **205 days** in Midnight Cave near Del Rio, Texas, supported by NASA and French bodies including the Defense Ministry (interested in nuclear-submarine crews); on day 160 he wrote *"Desolation overwhelms me"*; 1999 to 2000, 76 days in Clamouse cave, emerging on 14 February 2000 while believing it was 5 February. ### 4.3 Air: radon, carbon dioxide and pollutants, damp

**Radon in dug homes. SOURCED (primary, text extracted from a scanned PDF; OCR quality noted).** S. A. Baggs and C. F. Wong, "Survey of radon in Australian residences," *Architectural Science Review* 30 (1): 11 to 22, March 1987 (AIVC copy):

- Zhang et al., "Investigation of Indoor Air Quality and Staff Satisfaction in Underground Buildings in Xi'an, China," *Polish Journal of Environmental Studies* 30 (4) (2021): 9 sites in underground shopping malls, "snack cities" (food halls) and electronics markets; all pollutants except CO exceeded standards, formaldehyde, PM10, CO₂ and TVOC by **1.26, 1.62, 1.28 and 1.66 times**; *"Pollutions were the most serious in underground shopping malls"*; mall air drew *"the worst satisfaction"*.
- Kim et al., "Indoor air quality and its determinants in underground shopping malls in Korea," *Environment International* 183: 108395 (2024): **10 malls, 128 outlets** (2020); PM2.5 higher outdoors, but aldehydes and VOCs higher in passages and stores; store-to-passage ratios 1.06 to 4.93; formaldehyde and TVOC highest in clothing and accessory stores, PM2.5 in food outlets; most aldehydes and VOCs rose with **CO₂** and with **storefront walls**; advice: monitor CO₂ in stores, optimize air conditioning, design stores without storefront walls.

**Damp and mold in below-grade homes. SOURCED.**
- Jang and Ryu, "Analysis of Housing Environment in Semi Basement Multi-family Housing Units," *Journal of the Architectural Institute of Korea, Structure and Construction* 34 (2): 83 to 90 (2018): sensors in **10 semi-basement units for 13 months**; *"all the housing units under study had dew condensation and mildew"*; summer mean 27.84 °C and 64.91% RH; winter mean 20.6 °C and 40.12% RH; resident satisfaction *"2.03"* on a 1 to 5 scale.

### 4.4 Mind: working and living without windows

- **Underground offices in Japan. SOURCED.** Edit Nagy, *Office Employees' Psychological Reactions to Their Underground and Above Ground Offices*, BRI Research Paper No. 138, Building Research Institute, Japan, February 1994, https://www.kenken.go.jp/english/contents/publications/paper/138.html: 108 employees (**22 underground, 86 above ground**); underground workers wanted windows most (p = 0.000) and rated their lighting much worse although measured lighting was very similar; the underground office was judged *less pleasant, less stimulating and more enclosed* (p = 0.000); layout, artificial lighting, temperature and noise were similar, so the author attributes the difference to psychological factors such as knowing one is underground; Japanese subjects reacted as strongly as Western subjects in earlier studies, read as a sign that *"the need for windows and contact with the outside are universal human needs."*
- **Singapore's underground-workspace cohort. SOURCED.** Dunleavy et al., "Health Effects of Underground Workspaces cohort: study design and baseline characteristics," *Epidemiology and Health* 41: e2019025 (2019), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6815877/: **464 participants (142 underground, 322 above ground)** from two transport companies, a cooling plant and a university, in control-room, office and workshop jobs. At baseline: *"no difference in lux"* (p = 0.233), but underground workers were *"significantly less satisfied with the artificial lighting"* (3.1 vs 3.7, p = 0.002); sleep quality index 5.6 vs 5.4 and poor sleep 43.0% vs 42.2% (no difference); psychological distress 26.1% vs 23.6% (no significant difference); sick-building symptoms 16.2% vs 18.6%; underground staff worked **9.0 vs 8.4 hours a day** (*"36 minutes longer per day"*); median 4.2 vs 3.3 years at the current location. Follow-up results (12 months, 329 workers per a snippet): NOT FOUND.
- **Confinement and the walk in. SOURCED.** Tan et al., "Architectural design, perception, and health in underground working environments," ACUUS 2018, Hong Kong, pp. 538 to 543 (PolyU record): **121 employees in four underground office buildings in Singapore**; perceived confinement in the office lowered self-assessed mental-health functioning, more so in men; the strongest predictors, in order, were the perceived difference between the transition area and the office, the length of the transition area from the surface, the office's spatial dimensions, and thermal conditions.
- **A psychosocial frame. SOURCED.** Lee, Christopoulos, Kwok, Roberts and Soh, "A Psychosocial Approach to Understanding Underground Spaces," *Frontiers in Psychology* 8: 452 (2017), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5368185: four issues: **isolation** (*"a feeling of isolation from aboveground is consistently reported"*), **perceived control** (windowless rooms raise cortisol stress responses; *"even with a virtual window people felt much safer"*; poor landmarks weaken control), **negative cultural associations** (death, burial, poverty, citing Beijing's basement tenants), and **perceived security**. Citing Carmody and Sterling (1990): *"employees with experience of working underground are more positive about working underground in the future."* It also gives Montreal's RÉSO *"over half a million visitors during the long winters."* A January 2026 NTU news item (snippet) reports a survey of over 1,000 Singapore residents in which people with a stronger sense of control were more open to underground offices: UNVERIFIED.
- **Daylight and sleep. SOURCED.** Boubekri et al., *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health* 17 (9): 3219 (2020): 30 knowledge workers, one week in each of two offices (*"40.6 and 316 equivalent melanopic lux"*); with optimized daylight and views they *"slept 37 min longer."* The same paper cites Boubekri et al. 2014, where workers with windows slept about 46 minutes longer than workers without, and windowless workers scored worse on two SF-36 health dimensions (secondary, as cited).
- **Sweden, 1996. UNVERIFIED (search snippet of the abstract; full record unreadable).** Küller and Wetterberg, "The subterranean work environment: impact on well-being and health," *Environment International* 22 (1): 33 to 52 (1996): illuminance about twice as high in above-ground offices; noise somewhat higher underground; afternoon cortisol lower in underground staff.
- **Clinical screening.** The Wieliczka underground resort lists *"Mental illness, especially claustrophobia"* among contraindications (Item 3.3).

### 4.5 What designers and regulators do about it

- **Japan, national guidance for deep underground facilities that people occupy. SOURCED (MLIT, "Guideline outline on promoting barrier-free access and improving amenity," Urban and Regional Development Bureau, deep-underground planning office; press-release folder dated 040719, read as 19 July 2004 from the URL, so the date is DERIVED; PDF text extracted),** https://www.mlit.go.jp/kisha/kisha05/04/040719_2/01.pdf.
  - Characteristics named: *"閉塞感、心理的圧迫感が強い"* ("strong feelings of enclosure and psychological oppression"); *"地上の情報や自然光が入りにくい"* ("surface information and natural light hardly reach"); *"空気が施設内部にこもりやすい"* ("air tends to stagnate inside"); *"風の音等の自然音の欠如"* ("absence of natural sounds such as wind"); labyrinth effects: *"方向感覚の低下"* ("loss of sense of direction"), poor location information, poor sight lines; longer travel to the surface and longer air supply and exhaust runs; leakage under high groundwater pressure.
  - Required measures (講ずべき措置): larger, multiple, faster escalators and elevators; voice guidance including *"地下の深さ・階数等に関する情報提供"* ("information on depth and floor"); signs and floor guidance, electronic displays, **landmarks**, high-contrast colors; air conditioning that jointly manages temperature, humidity, carbon monoxide, dust, odor and airflow, with no spot where air stagnates; lighting quality and quantity; noise reduction; waterproofing and drainage.
  - Desirable measures (望ましい措置): shaping cross-sections and dimensions to suggest breadth; color, lighting, pattern and materials; *"植栽、噴水等 ... による癒し空間の創出"* ("creating healing spaces with planting, fountains and the like"); *"光ファイバー等を通じた自然光の取込み"* ("bringing in natural light through optical fibers and the like"); *"風の音や虫の鳴き声、川のせせらぎといった自然音の活用"* ("using natural sounds such as wind, insects and a babbling stream"); measures against the anxiety of boarding long down escalators; benches and toilets; communications relays so occupants can receive surface information and use mobile phones; trained volunteers and staff to help people with limited mobility.
- **Japan, underground shopping-street plazas.** The 1981 national Basic Policy required open wells for smoke exhaust and daylight in proportion to shop floor, and two or more stairs straight to the surface; managers installed water features for *"openness, ease and moistness"* and to reduce the labyrinth effect (Item 2.6).
- **Japan, skylights in experiments. SOURCED.** Waseda University KAKEN project 23560747 (FY2011 to 2013, principal investigator Tomonori Sano), https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/en/report/KAKENHI-PROJECT-23560747/23560747seika: photos and 3D video of **400 scenes** in underground facilities; *"participants got anxiety and tight feeling from the scene of 'Underground Landscape.' Additionally, the skylight window and the open ceiling space with them reduce these undesirable feelings."* People *"tend to be lost"* because many sights look alike.
- **Artificial skylights. SOURCED (abstract via Europe PMC).** Yasukouchi, Maeda, Hara and Furuune, "Non-visual effects of diurnal exposure to an artificial skylight, including nocturnal melatonin suppression," *Journal of Physiological Anthropology* 38 (2019): **10 healthy men**; daytime exposure to an artificial skylight vs fluorescent light; ratings of naturalness and *"connected to nature"* *"significantly higher with the skylight"*; *"Nocturnal melatonin secretion was significantly greater"* after skylight days; lower cortical arousal; similar work performance. Vendor-reported stress results for "virtual skylights" in hospitals exist but are vendor-published (snippet; not used).
- **Recommendations in the research literature. SOURCED (Lee et al. 2017):** light wells or skylights; more exits and intermediate sloped passages; greenery (windowless-office workers decorate with plants and nature pictures); stronger landmarks and wayfinding; high-end uses to counter stigma.
- **Cities.** Helsinki's master plan names fire compartmentation, smoke exhaust, address systems, marked exits and ventilation that works in fire (Item 2.2); Minneapolis requires *"transparency of skyway walls ... to provide views to the outside"* (Item 2.4); a 2023 Sapporo building joins the underground walkway to the street with a daylit, planted indoor plaza that doubles as a 100-person refuge (Item 2.3).
- **Finnish written design guidance on comfort and lighting in rock spaces: NOT FOUND** (search 53).
