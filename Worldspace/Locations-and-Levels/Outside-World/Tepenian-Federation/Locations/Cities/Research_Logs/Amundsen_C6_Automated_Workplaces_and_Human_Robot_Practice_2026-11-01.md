## 1.1 Rotterdam — ECT Delta (1993) [neighbor added: the historical first version]

## 1.2 Rotterdam — APM Terminals Maasvlakte II (MVII) and RWG
- SOURCED (APMT, https://www.apmterminals.com/en/maasvlakte/about/our-terminal): "the world's most advanced fully automated terminal," automated areas "fenced off from staff"; roughly 80% of crane movements automated, the rest run remotely; dual-spreader cranes; "73 Lift Automated Guided Vehicles (L-AGV)" run on electricity; each AGV runs ~8 hours then drives itself to a station for automatic battery swap. Page does not give headcount.
- SOURCED (Marketplace, Stam): MVII "they still have 600 people working there." Jobs listed in the article: remote crane drivers, mechanics, lashing gangs, supervisors, troubleshooters; "there are plenty of jobs that humans still need to do." Port innovation director Oscar van Veen: goal is "getting the data in" rather than "getting the people out." Albert Veenstra (Erasmus): newer terminals built for ships whose cranes don't allow "a physical person in the cabin." Stam: "Particularly the first phase, it was a strong fight."
- SOURCED (search summary of Maritime Executive/ABB pages; training count only): since early 2014 APMT trained 45 remote operators on simulators; ABB system places STS operators in a control room in the terminal building, first in the world with no one in the crane cab. ABB: control room may reduce fatigue and neck/back stress; cameras often give a wider view than the cab (search summary only).
- SOURCED (ABB operator video, via search summary): operator Ben den Otter described a mental adjustment: after doing everything manually, trusting the crane to do it automatically took a different mindset.
- UNVERIFIED (kitalent.com, promotional-looking, search summary only): "180 specialized automation technicians" at MVII; "40% fewer dockworkers than a conventional terminal."
- RWG (Rotterdam World Gateway, DP World + four shipping lines): SOURCED (Port Strategy, https://www.portstrategy.com/?p=229099, c. March 2015): unions FNV Havens and CNV Vakmensen wanted a collective labor agreement (CLA); RWG said a CLA "is not representative of the workforce at the terminal," would not impose one without employees' "express consent"; unions alleged RWG tried to exclude "various groups including office workers"; striking dockers disrupted RWG. ## 5.5 Mars rover operations teams as a community
- SOURCED (JPL, https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/curiosity-team-switches-back-to-earth-time/, Nov 2012): Curiosity team about 200 JPL engineers and about 400 scientists mostly from other institutions; worked on Mars time for three months because a sol is ~40 minutes longer than an Earth day, often overnight Pacific Time; daily planning cut from more than 16 hours to 12 to make the switch possible; afterward most work within 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Pacific; scientists participate from home institutions across North America and Europe; project manager Richard Cook: "People are glad to be going off Mars time."

## 6.1 Nuclear plant control rooms (search summaries only; NO page was fetched; every claim here is SOURCED-SECONDARY and must be re-read before reuse)
- Licensed minimum crew: 10 CFR 50.54(m)(2)(i): in operating modes two senior reactor operators and two reactor operators on shift; cold shutdown and refueling, one of each (NRC document ML17055D736, https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1705/ML17055D736.pdf, per search summary). An SRO must remain in the control room whenever a unit is in a mode other than cold shutdown or refueling (10 CFR 50.54(k), 50.54(m)(2)(iii)); NRC Information Notice 95-23 (https://publish.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/gen-comm/info-notices/1995/in95023.html) cites violations where operators left the controls unattended. Rule origin: Three Mile Island (NUREG-1791 interim guidance, ML22272A049). IN 91-77 states 50.54(m) covers licensed minimums only and "does not address whether other personnel are available for all tasks." NRC interim guidance for Part 53 reactors (ML25232A023, March 2026 per summary) points to a performance-based, human-factors approach.
- Hours and fatigue: NRC Generic Letter 82-12 (1982) recommended no more than 16 hours straight and a break of at least eight hours between work periods (per summary of https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/gen-comm/gen-letters/1982/gl82012). A 1999 Federal Register petition argued deregulation cut staffing and lengthened hours and that fatigue is under-reported (govinfo FR-1999-12-01, per summary). An older news report on St. Lucie (tmia.com): operators with more than 1,000 overtime hours a year, 12-hour shifts confined to the control rooms, eating at desks, some taking medication to stay alert; a veteran: the schedule "just fatigues you" (UNVERIFIED, undated). Current 10 CFR Part 26 work-hour limits: NOT FOUND (not searched).
- Vigilance and boredom: NRC-sponsored simulator study (MIT DSpace 1721.1/90362, "Operator Performance in Long Duration Control Operations: Switching from Low to High Task Load") treats long, low-task-load periods as typical of nuclear control rooms and says boredom effects matter more as automation grows; field study by Roger Jensen on whether keeping busy helps night-shift alertness (per summary; neither page fetched).
