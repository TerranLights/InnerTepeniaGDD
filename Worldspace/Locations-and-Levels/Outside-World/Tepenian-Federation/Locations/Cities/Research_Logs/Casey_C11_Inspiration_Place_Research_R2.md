## 1 · #85 Magadan and the Kolyma Highway

*Round 1 covered the port, icebreaking, the road, Dalstroy and the camp system, the census, the museums, Bakyldydyak, the gold-panning contest, Zazhirovka, alcohol rules to 2017, bay-ice bans and the road settlements. Nothing below repeats it.*

### A. Other settings (sub-areas, housing, schools, clinics, off-season)
- **Housing stock and condition:** a few Stalin-era buildings and dozens of five-story blocks from the 1980s; permafrost and cold speed concrete decay; a 1955 building's renovated facade collapsed months after the work; in the former camp-era settlements along the road about 40% of apartments are occupied and some are almost unsellable; snow and yard clearing are often left to residents, who sometimes pay for it themselves (SOURCED, Ilya Azar, "Город отложенной жизни," *Novaya Gazeta*, events Oct–Dec 2020, read 2026-11-20). The 1960s–1980s microdistricts were added around the historic center (SOURCED, Anastasia Syrokvash, RT, 27 Mar 2026). [K9] [P3, P4]
- **Satellite settlements inside the city district:** Sokol (airport settlement, 3,892 people in 2021) and Uptar (1,701 in 2021), both shrinking since 2010 (UNVERIFIED, search summary of census data); Uptar, about 50 km out, half-abandoned with boarded houses; Atka on the road resettled, with two families left on wood stoves and outdoor toilets (SOURCED, Azar 2020). The Solnechny and Snezhny microdistricts are the city's main recreation zone, set back from the sea (UNVERIFIED, bigenc snippet; the bigenc page returned HTTP 401 and was not retried). [K9] [P5]
- **Palatka, the district town on the road (about 80 km out, about 4,300 people):** rows of brightly painted five-story blocks, two schools, a school of arts, an Orthodox church and the region's largest swimming pool; five fountains with statues in an eclectic style, entered in the Russian Book of Records in 2019 for fountains per 1,000 residents; the fountains were initiated by Alexander Basansky, a regional Duma deputy and founder of gold-mining companies who has lived there about 40 years (the article does not say who paid); families from Magadan drive out on summer weekends; streets lit until about 23:00; a MAZ-200 truck monument at the entrance marks the wartime motor depot that supplied the Kolyma interior; a jewelry workshop makes pieces from gold nuggets (SOURCED, *Rossiyskaya Gazeta*, 31 Mar 2024, read 2026-11-20). [K8, K10, K13] [P4, P8]
- **New and planned public works (national project):** redevelopment of Magadan Square with a cable-stayed footbridge over the Magadanka River and the Nagaev Bay embankment from Mayak park to Morskoy square (both due autumn 2026); a cruise "Morskoy Tourist Center" (due May 2027); a revived arboretum (from late 2023, after a fire); a maternity hospital, a 1,000-pupil school, a center for gifted children, and a pool complex; a new airport terminal at project stage; a territory of advanced development created inside the city in May 2025, keeping the free-customs regime (ship repair, fish processing, logistics, tourism); a magnitude-5.8 earthquake in March 2026 cracked plaster and roads and cut power in places (all SOURCED, RT, 27 Mar 2026). [K8, K9] [P5]
- **Schools and colleges:** schoolchildren interviewed in 2020 planned to leave after graduation; North-Eastern State University (the region's only university) missed its admission target by about 60 that year; teacher-relocation subsidies are offered (SOURCED, Azar 2020). In one past winter, children stayed home at −52 °C, while at −40 °C they still went to school (SOURCED as a resident's account, Nadya Shushurikhina, T-Zh, 5 May 2023). [K6, K9] [P4]
- **Clinics and health:** health services described as poor; some doctors recruited from the Altai; hospitals kept open but some in very poor repair and under restoration (SOURCED, Azar 2020). [K9]
- **Off-season and seasons:** high season for flights is 15 March–10 April and July–September; many residents leave on summer vacation (UNVERIFIED, search summary of t-j.ru). Spring and autumn last about a week each; white nights in May–June in the northern districts, near-polar darkness in winter (SOURCED, Shushurikhina 2023). [K7] [P3]
- **Dachas:** wooden summer cottages around the city with vegetable plots, mainly potatoes (SOURCED, Shushurikhina 2023). Farm-grown potatoes, carrots, tomatoes, cucumbers and cabbage sell out first (SOURCED, same). Free-hectare program: over 75,000 people in three years across the Far East, 90% locals (SOURCED, *AiF*, 8 Jul 2019, regional figure not given). [K5, K12]

### B. Subcultures
- **Prospector artels (placer gold):** season March–November; washing crews stop at the first frosts, usually late September; bulldozer crews strip overburden until mid-November for next year's ground; 12-hour days with no days off; the artel supplies food, clothing and housing, and the food is deducted from autumn pay; late-2010s pay at one artel: 90,000–120,000 rubles a month for washing-plant hands, 180,000–300,000 for bulldozer drivers; about 3 kg of gold per worker counts as a good season; workers return every spring; plant hands are trained on site, bulldozer drivers need vocational courses, the site foreman an engineering degree; territory guarded but no armed escort; theft by traps set in the washing plant; in 2013 about 2 t of gold and 16 t of silver were stolen from the Karamken refinery and never found; safety rules described as written in blood (deaths under a loader and from rotten supports); a 2,976 g nugget found in 2005 was kept until 2018 and touched for luck by so many people that it lost a couple of grams (SOURCED, Oksana Polyakova, *Vokrug Sveta*, 4 Mar 2025, interview with a former artel director). In the 1970s the same man's Komsomol youth brigade was about one-third Komsomol members and the rest prisoners (SOURCED, same). [K7, K14, K6, K9] [P8] [SM:competence shown under pressure] [R]
- **Gold-panning contest names a trade:** the 2024 winner (0.79 g) was a smokejumper of the regional aerial forest-fire service; the event's Russian name is «Старательский фарт» (9th edition 26 July 2025, Dyukcha park; 157 applications and 60 admitted in 2024; 15 minutes with a wooden tray and pick) (SOURCED, EastRussia, 25 Jun 2025). [K3, K14]
- **Even and other Indigenous communities (2025–26):** the governor's council on Indigenous peoples met on 1 May 2026 (Indigenous Peoples' Day) to review the federal concept for Indigenous development to 2036 (approved December 2025); 44 clan communities get support (boats, tents, spare parts, forest-lease compensation); reindeer herders' monthly payment raised from 8,000 to 12,000 rubles, plus 4,000 for retired brigade members; 250 kg salmon quota for herders and their families in 2025; staff shortage named the main problem; adding Chukchi to the region's official Indigenous list under federal consideration (SOURCED, runews24.ru, 1 May 2026). Herd of over 12,000 reindeer (undated ministerial statement, UNVERIFIED snippet); a traditional herders' rally at the transshipment base of the Irbychan reindeer farm in the Severo-Evensky district, where "hundreds" of herders, veterans and Indigenous representatives meet (SOURCED, EastRussia, 28 Mar 2017). Kolyma Evens practiced taiga reindeer herding of the Tungus type: reindeer as transport, semi-free seasonal grazing, smoke fires against insects (UNVERIFIED, ethnomuseum snippet). [K1, K4, K7, K8] [P9]
- **Indigenous ensembles:** the Ener song-and-dance ensemble of northern peoples, formed 1980, professional from 1993, part of the regional philharmonic from 1997; the Magadan Russian Orchestra founded 1996 by teachers and students of the arts college, "governor's" status from 2014 (UNVERIFIED, bigenc snippet; page refused 401 in Round 1 and not requested). [K13]
- **Muslims:** one mosque, in a former casino building bought by the community; Friday prayers about 1,500 a week, about 5,000 in Ramadan; madrasas for boys and girls; no local government funding, help sent by Tatar and Bashkir relatives elsewhere; the city allotted a section of the cemetery; the imam is ethnic Ingush (SOURCED as reported, Paul Goble, *Window on Eurasia*, 8 Oct 2023, citing Milliard.Tatar; numbers not independently checked). Tajik men reported being held without passports on a remote livestock farm in the region (UNVERIFIED, RFE/RL item via globalsecurity, Nov 2025). Firms bring in migrant workers partly to avoid paying northern supplements (SOURCED as reported, Azar 2020). [K4, K9] [P9]
- **Rotation workers versus residents:** the governor warned (2019) that replacing residents with rotation workers would lose doctors, teachers, engineers and culture workers; many settlements now exist only as rotation camps (SOURCED, *AiF* 2019; Azar 2020). [K4, K7]
- **Leavers and the young:** about 1,000 people aged 14–30 leave the region each year (Agency for Human Capital Development, 2019); average age 41; 64% of 18–24-year-olds and 75% of older respondents in a VTsIOM poll said finding work is hard; the governor names a "deferred-life syndrome" (people living as if about to leave) the main psychological reason for leaving (SOURCED, *AiF*, 8 Jul 2019); about 3,500 left the region in the first eight months of 2020; locals estimate the real city population near 80,000, below the registered 90,000, because many left without deregistering (SOURCED, Azar 2020). Academic work by a Magadan psychologist on students' migration attitudes exists (UNVERIFIED, *RUDN Journal* snippet; page failed with a redirect loop). [K9] [P9]
- **Outdoor hobby communities:** a Magadan mountain club (ice climbing mostly from about March; first ascents in the Kolyma ranges, a 19-person expedition to the Great Rapids Ridge) (UNVERIFIED, VK post and American Alpine Club snippets); a paragliding club using Cape Nyukla, where the first Soviet geological expedition landed; sea-swell surfing in the Sea of Okhotsk; ski touring and snowmobile trips; skiing on the hills into June (SOURCED, Alexei Stroev, Lenta.ru, 20 Oct 2021). Weekend hikes to Batareynaya Bay, Chernyi Klyuch and Kamenny Venets organized through local groups; shrimp trips and paragliding at Nyukla (SOURCED, Shushurikhina 2023). [K14, K10] [P4]

### C. Leisure industries and what locals do
- **Ski and sports build-out:** the Snegorka ski area (about 3.8 km of runs, 3 lifts, 138–456 m, open 11:00–18:00) (UNVERIFIED, skiresort.info snippet); a three-jump all-season ski-jump complex in the Snezhnaya Dolina microdistrict with night lighting, and a universal ski complex named after Elena Vyalbe (born in Magadan, 1968) at the Polet sports base: four trails, roller-ski track, 500-seat stands, 200-place housing next to it, both presented 18 April 2022 and planned for 2025 (SOURCED, EastRussia, 2022); in March 2026 the governor's adviser said the planned chairlift must be heated with a wind deflector; artificial snow to lengthen the season; tourists may use the venues when no competition is on (SOURCED, Tourdom, 26 Mar 2026). [K3, K8] [P4]
- **Visitors:** about 2,000 tourists a year in 2020 (SOURCED as a local figure, Azar 2020). [K8]
- **What residents do:** walk the Nagaev and Gertner bay shores and Mayak park; fish off the military pier (flounder); pick fern shoots, lingonberry, blueberry, cloudberry, crowberry and others; fish and cook over fires rather than eat out; buy from the Urozhai market and two named shops before flying out; sled, tube, ski and snowboard in winter (SOURCED, Shushurikhina 2023). A city half-marathon drew over 450 runners, sponsored by the gold miner Kinross (SOURCED, *AiF* 2019). [K10, K12]
- **Recreational salmon fishing:** an earlier rule set (undated) allowed two pink salmon a day free at the Nagaev and Gertner bays and the Dukcha River, 29 June–5 August (UNVERIFIED, EastRussia snippet). **The 2026 run failed:** about 1,100 t of pink salmon against a forecast of 9,400 t (12%, the worst in 12 years); industrial salmon fishing in Taui Bay was closed almost everywhere; amateur sites in the bay stayed open, but all spawning rivers got "pass days" with no salmon angling; only Indigenous residents could keep fishing in Taui Bay (SOURCED, PRIME, 6 Aug 2026). [K6, K7, K9, K12] [P8]
- **Winter ice fishing:** VNIRO's Magadan branch monitors amateur ice fishing for navaga and smelt in three traditional areas (Ola estuary, Gertner Bay, Nagaev Bay); in 2025–26 no stable ice formed by late January and none was observed until February (SOURCED, VNIRO, 4 Feb 2026). A winter amateur fishing festival, "Klevaya Kolyma," was held in March 2021 (SOURCED as to existence, Rosrybolovstvo photo gallery, 22 Mar 2021; no details given). [K2, K3, K12]

### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Khebdenek, the Even New Year at the summer solstice:** reconstructed and revived in 1997 by specialists of the regional Center for Folk Art; held on a meadow at the Dukcha River with several hundred people a year; rites: greeting the rising sun to jaw-harp music, smoking the meadow and lighting and "feeding" the clan fire, exchanging gifts, leaving a bead or coin for the earth, passing hand in hand through sacred gates and fires, tying a ribbon to a cord between two trees, a youth dance around the fire with ribbons, a newborn rite revived in 2017 (forehead marked with deer blood or clan-fire ash), and the khede round dance lasting hours; contests: throwing the maut, stick tug-of-war, bone carving, best yurt decoration; clan elders now take the ritual role once held by shamans; historically four days, marking the end of winter and the start of the salmon run (SOURCED, Ksenia Murzina, EastRussia, 8 Oct 2019). Listed on culture.ru as a regional Even festival at the Dukcha mouth (UNVERIFIED, snippet; culture.ru pages fail with SSL errors). [K2, K4, K12] [P6]
- **30 October remembrance (2025):** officials told teachers and librarians orally, in chats and meetings, not to hold or post repression-memory events; library events with confirmed guests and tickets could go ahead unpublicized; the official ceremony at the Mask of Sorrow still took place, and the mayor and governor posted online without attending (SOURCED, The Insider, 2 Nov 2025, citing NeMoskva; consistent with Round 1). The international Memorial movement was declared "extremist" and banned by Russia's Supreme Court on 9 April 2026 (UNVERIFIED, The Insider snippet). [K9, K6] [P6]
- **Ener / national groups / Etnokniga:** a library-led "Etnokniga of Magadan Oblast" project by the regional association of culture workers, which includes the Pushkin regional library (UNVERIFIED, culture.ru snippet). [K13]
- **Bears and the city (civic response):** in June 2023 bears attacked people on Nagaev Hill near housing (five hurt, one woman killed); hunting inspectors, Rosgvardia and the regional nature ministry respond; drones with thermal imagers patrol suburbs; officials' advice: do not go into the forest alone or in June, make noise, never feed or film bears, secure garbage (SOURCED, 360.ru, 23 Jun 2023). By 29 September 2026 the regional ministry had 268 reports of wild animals coming out to settlements and proposed changing brown-bear hunting rules (UNVERIFIED, runews24 snippet). A federal program "Bear Land: rules of good neighborliness" started in April 2026 in Magadan, Kamchatka and Sakhalin (UNVERIFIED, snippet). [K6, K1, K9]

### A. Other settings
- **Informal hillside neighborhoods:** the national register of popular neighborhoods (RENABAP) counted 22 informal settlements in Ushuaia in 2020; the largest cluster, "El Escondido," grew 2005–2010 on mountain slopes above the 115 m contour that protects the communal forest (Municipal Ordinance 2.171): about 228,956 m², about 749 dwellings and 825 families, including the Barrio Obrero, El Mirador, Esperanza 2, El Cañadón, Mirador de Ushuaia and Altos de la Cumbre sectors, visible from the center, the airport and the port. Population growth by stage: +500% 1970–1991 (industrialization), +156% 1991–2001, +162% 2001–2015; only 30% of residents were born in Tierra del Fuego (2010 census); 46.15% owned house and land, 18.41% the house only (2010). The municipal plan of 2004 names a local habit of building first and seeking approval afterwards; high rents and tourism growth pushed the 2003–2015 wave north onto wooded slopes (all SOURCED, Diego E. Barrios, "¿Paisaje roto?," *Ayana* 5(1), Dec 2024–May 2025, read from the saved PDF). The Valle de Andorra sector, zoned for farming, grew informally to the east and now holds many named neighborhoods (UNVERIFIED, municipal plan and *Revista de la Asociación Geológica Argentina* snippets). [K4, K9] [P5, P9]
- **Italian-built quarter and the 1948–49 contract:** after the prison closed, the Ministry of the Navy contracted the Bologna firm of Carlo (Carlos) Borsari to bring Italian workers, who arrived on the steamship *Genova* (1948) and the *Ana C* (1949) to build housing, public buildings and town infrastructure; a book, *Los italianos del fin del mundo*, and a documentary marked the 50th anniversary in 1998 (SOURCED, heritage blog by an architect, ushuaiapatrimonio.blogspot.com, 21 Feb 2017); "over 600 Italians" (UNVERIFIED, Italian travel article snippet). A 2023 national bill seeks recognition of the "Almirante Brown" military neighborhood, linked to the Italian immigration, and another the "Luis Piedrabuena" military neighborhood (UNVERIFIED, snippets; the bill PDF failed to load). [K4, K14] [P9]
- **Naval presence:** Ushuaia Naval Base created 13 December 1950, partly on the old prison's buildings; the Southern Naval Area created 16 December 1974 (UNVERIFIED, argentina.gob.ar snippet). An "integrated naval base" project (1,050 m of quay, about 9% built in four years) would move the base out of the center by about 2028 and add staff housing (UNVERIFIED, iProfesional snippet, Aug 2026). [K4, K8]
- **Schools:** Colegio Nacional de Ushuaia, a free university-preparatory school with classical languages, founded March 1994 under the sponsorship of the Colegio Nacional de Buenos Aires to meet population growth (SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org, read 2026-11-20). The Biblioteca Popular Sarmiento, founded in November 1926 by commissioners of the national justice ministry, is described as the oldest library in the province (UNVERIFIED, legislature snippets; the PDF returned 404). [K10]
- **Teachers' strike (2026):** by 2 September 2026 provincial teachers (SUTEF) were in the fifth week of an indefinite general strike; 201 of 202 base-assembly mandates rejected the government offer; demand of a 3,000,000-peso minimum salary against about one-third of that paid; encampments in the three cities and marches to the legislature and Government House in Ushuaia (SOURCED, Tiempo Argentino, 2 Sep 2026). [K9, K1] [P7]
- **Winter in the streets:** the August 2023 storm (the year's largest snowfall) left hundreds of cars and buses stuck, many residents walked to work and school, and the airport closed; the municipality clears a priority circuit first under its "Operativo Invierno" (SOURCED, Diario de Cuyo, 6 Aug 2023); bottled-gas delivery to the upper neighborhoods slows in heavy snow (UNVERIFIED, snippet). [K5, K1] [P4]
- **Housing emergency law:** provincial Law 1536 declared a one-year housing and urban-environmental emergency with a "Citizen Rental Program" (leasing, a trust to build homes for permanent rent), a register of homes in unlicensed temporary rental, a ban on renting provincial-housing-institute homes as tourist lodging, a sworn declaration of lodging address by people entering the province, and fines; in 2026 the legislature asked the executive to report on how it was applied (SOURCED, Legislature Resolution 056/26, read from the saved PDF; the law's own date not seen). In January 2026 the tourism institute reported 750 lodging inspections, over 80 administrative proceedings and 233 sanction files, summer hotel occupancy of 75–80%, tourists buying one or two excursions instead of three, and cruise visits of under twelve hours (SOURCED, Prensa Mercosur, 27 Jan 2026). Average rent about 950,000–1,095,000 pesos a month and 689 active short-let listings in mid-2026 (UNVERIFIED, commercial portals). [K1, K6, K8, K9] [P7]

### B. Subcultures
- **Chilote community:** three migration "ages" in an ethnography (fieldwork 2016–2018): (1) after the prison closed in 1947, Chilote men, many finishing the shearing season in Río Grande, came for logging (riding the old convicts' train into the woods), sawmills, a limestone quarry, and carpentry and cabinetmaking, in which they were esteemed; in the 1950s the town had about 7,000 people and Río Grande 10,000, "70% foreigners (basically Chilean)"; women came later; (2) from 1960, families displaced by the potato blight and the 1960 earthquake settled on the outskirts; in the 1970s oil work (YPF) and the 1972 promotion law brought more; Chilote men were not keen on factory work, but the plants drew women; in the late 1980s land allotments (sometimes with building materials) were offered to settle the outskirts, earlier settlers getting central lots; (3) from 1990, transnational community life: the Solidarity Group, the Gabriela Mistral Chilean Residents' Center (founded 5 September 2000), early settlers' clubs, and the Arturo Prat football team in the local league; Chilean Independence Day (18 September) celebrated with rowing-boat races, curanto, shellfish empanadas, milcao and sopaipillas; festivities in the Vicente de Paul and La Cantera neighborhoods; shops sell Chiloé goods (toasted flour, dried fish, seaweeds, smoked pork); a Chilote restaurant in the center fills when Chile's national team plays (SOURCED, Juan Manuel Saldívar Arellano, *Espacio Regional* 21, Jan–Jun 2024, read from the saved PDF). The consulate counts over 5,000 Chileans in the region (UNVERIFIED, chile.gob.cl snippet). [K4, K7, K10, K12, K14] [P9]
- **Classification by arrival:** residents sort the town's people by whether they were born there, came and stayed, or were posted there and plan to leave (factory managers, public officials, factory workers, sailors), a division a 2011 local newspaper item said left "a very scarce identification with the place" (SOURCED, quoted in Saldívar 2024; the terms themselves are in the separate slang file, not here). [K4, K9]
- **Factory workers (2025–26):** in May 2025 the metalworkers' unions of Ushuaia and Río Grande called a total strike against tariff cuts on imported electronics, saying about 7,000 jobs in the two cities were at risk; the governor and the three mayors opposed the cut (SOURCED, *Buenos Aires Herald*, 16 May 2025). By mid-2026 promoted-industry jobs had fallen to 6,564 against 10,033 a year earlier, and Newsan dismissed 150 in Ushuaia (UNVERIFIED, iProfesional and El Ancasti snippets). [K7, K9] [P8] [SM:man versus society]
- **Port workers (2026):** the national ports agency took over the Ushuaia port in January 2026 citing financial irregularities; over 140 provincial port staff were locked out from 22 January; the 2025–26 season had 495 vessel calls and over 135,000 passengers; workers warned the 2026–27 Antarctic season could not be guaranteed; provincial health officials were refused port access during a hantavirus outbreak linked to a cruise ship that left on 1 April (SOURCED, Cruise Industry News, 12 May 2026). [K9, K5]
- **Yaghan community:** the Comunidad Indígena Yagan Paiakoala, Ushuaia, received legal personality and entry in the national register of Indigenous communities by INAI Resolution 18/2021 (22 Feb 2021, published 4 Mar 2021) (SOURCED, Boletín Oficial). A Selk'nam community, Rafaela Ishton, takes part in official commemorations (UNVERIFIED, Vía País snippet). [K4, K1] [P9]
- **Hobby and sport communities:** a trekking group of about ten graduates of a 2017 Club Andino Río Grande course (doctors, accountants, clerks, armed-forces staff, teachers, ages 30–60) hikes two or three Sundays a month, 10–18 people, 6–12 km, voting on destinations in a messaging group and checking weather apps; some routes are seasonal (one lagoon January–March, another October–March, a valley only in winter) (SOURCED, Silvina Vitale, *La Nación*, 1 Mar 2020). **Ice hockey:** brought in 1979 when the provincial sports authority built a 60 × 30 m outdoor rink and invited players from Buenos Aires' Gimnasia y Esgrima; one instructor stayed; play paused 1981–2003 when Bahía Encerrada stopped freezing safely; the Club Andino restored a natural skating lagoon in 2000; three local clubs now meet in the Copa Fin del Mundo; a coach set up a hockey-stick workshop in town (SOURCED, Cadena 3, 28 Jul 2025). **Football:** only two 11-a-side pitches, both synthetic (natural grass fails); the 11-a-side season is a short spring–summer window set by weather; indoor futsal dominates; wind over 100 km/h halts matches; a pioneer from Chaco gave his name to one stadium (SOURCED, eltrece, 19 Jan 2025). **Snow rugby:** the RugbyXtreme circuit (since 2005) held a six-a-side, two-five-minute-halves tournament at Llanos del Castor with eight men's and women's teams, Ushuaia Rugby Club among them, ending with the customary post-match meal (SOURCED, *La Nación*, 11 Sep 2025). **Sled dogs:** a musher in the Valle de las Cotorras keeps 152 Siberian and Alaskan huskies (about 1.5 t of feed a month, up to six runs a dog a day), began after meeting the Argentine Army's polar dogs removed from Antarctica in the 1990s under environmental rules, takes volunteers, and his cabin was the start of an international sled-dog race suspended in the pandemic (SOURCED, AFP via ABC Color, 13 Aug 2021); the Haruwen center (1987) has held an August sled-dog race since 1993 (UNVERIFIED, snippet). [K14, K10, K3] [P4] [R]

## 3 · #87 Puerto Montt and kilometer 0 of the Carretera Austral

*Round 1 covered the road's construction, the ferries, the German colonization and Williche land, Angelmó and Caleta Anahuac (ZOIT plan), salmon employment and crises, migrants and Alerce in outline, Pampa Irigoin, the San Pedro procession (2002), the Temporales de Teatro and red-tide rules. ### A. Other settings
- **Barrio Puerto (Zona Típica, 3 April 2019):** laid out in 1886 when the engineer Víctor Bordalí subdivided land for incoming colonists, with a lookout promenade and a recreation garden on Cerro Miramar; Calle Miraflores cut in 1909 to link the center with Angelmó; a reception area for German immigrants and for people from the Reloncaví and Chiloé, giving a mixed native-German-Chilote building and food culture; traditional trades named: lathe-turner, tinsmith, seamstress; architecture mixes native-alerce timber houses, rationalist concrete with nautical details (porthole windows, ship's railings) and hybrids of Chilote vernacular and modernism; buildings of two or three stories stepping up the slope. Named places: the **Población Marina Mercante** (a block of paired two-story row houses built for merchant-marine workers, among the first expansions of the old town); Casa Cooprinsem (1930s) and Casa Almonacid (about 1900, with a lookout); the **Parish of San Pedro de Angelmó** (refounded 1939; a 1964 Crucifixion mural by the local painter Manuel Maldonado); the **Cirus Bar** (opened 1962 by the Cárdenas brothers, named by sailors in a raffle contest, one of the first places open to the opposition under the dictatorship); a tinsmith's shop whose owner has worked 50 years and trained most of the city's tinsmiths; the Pueblito Melipulli craft and food market (over 30 southern artists; wool, wood and salmon-leather work); the Miramar lookout. The community and organizations filed for heritage status in 2017 after a four-year participatory process (SOURCED, Consejo de Monumentos Nacionales heritage-education booklet, 2023, read from the saved PDF). About 20 ha; neighbors' concern over decline dates from 2013; the municipal heritage program was later dissolved (SOURCED, Simón Urbina, UACh column, 2 May 2022). [K4, K13, K14, K10] [P3, P4] [R]

### B. Subcultures
- **Women in salmon processing:** about 32% of the industry's 60,000-plus workers are women (INE via Ecoceanos), mainly in farms and processing plants; harvest-season days of 10–12 hours standing in sub-zero temperatures and high humidity; pay 300,000–500,000 pesos plus production bonuses (2019); chronic tendinitis, carpal-tunnel and shoulder injuries, and reported limits on bathroom breaks; employers seeking to lift maternity protection from fixed-term workers (one Puerto Montt case at Salmones Aysén, 2015) (SOURCED, Meritxell Freixas, El Desconcierto, 4 Aug 2019). Women are over 60% of processing-plant hires (UNVERIFIED, Universidad de Los Lagos snippet). A worker letter describes about 300 women at one Puerto Montt plant on 08:00–17:45 shifts (UNVERIFIED, militant source). [K7, K9, K14] [P8] [SM:man versus society]
- **German heritage institutions:** the city's fire service traces to the German Fire Association of Melipulli; the **Segunda Compañía de Bomberos Germania** moved on 6 September 1873 to land on Calle San Felipe donated by the Kassel-born merchant Frederick Schminke (who ran the town's first import house) and handed over by his son; for the 150th anniversary (2023) volunteers re-enacted the march, unveiled a plaque and laid flowers at Schminke's grave (SOURCED, Semanario Local, 11 Sep 2023). Six fire companies existed in 1960 (UNVERIFIED, snippet). ### A. Other settings
- **Self-built working-class neighborhoods:** **Barrio 18 de Septiembre** began on 10 January 1953 when the municipality (mayor Felicia Barría Vera) handed 168 lots to low-income families; within days residents formed a "Comité Pro Adelanto," the first of its kind in the city; a Methodist-run first-letters school opened 7 May 1955 (an "emergency school" from 1958); the population was named on 27 September 1956; the club "18 de Septiembre" founded 22 January 1957; a football league founded 28 December 1963 in a resident's house; a bomb attack on the Nuestra Señora de Fátima parish on 6 October 1984 under the dictatorship; today about 25,000 people and about 170 active businesses, mostly family-run (grocery stores, bakeries, barbers, workshops); a heritage route made with residents (Neighborhood Council No. 20) (SOURCED, Ministry of Cultures, 16 Dec 2019). **Población Calisto:** land of a Chilote couple's dairy smallholding (bought 1945) sold off in lots from about 1948; houses built with no water, sewers, gas or electricity; residents formed a committee in the 1950s, opened streets, then raised money for electricity and water by holding parties; one resident waited seven years for water, fetched by bucket from a municipal standpipe; heating was "pure firewood"; the neighborhood council (No. 14 "Independencia") was formed under the 1968–70 neighborhood-council law (decree 1460 signed by President Frei Montalva), its leaders were persecuted or appointed after 1973, and it re-democratized about 1991; the council hall was a shack on blocks until the 1990s; children crossed open land to school in the dark at 8 a.m. in winter (SOURCED, Sergio Reyes Soto, *Historia de la Población Calisto y la Junta de Vecinos #14*, Calafate Ediciones, March 2022, municipally funded, read from the saved PDF). ### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **The FOM attack (resolved as far as sources allow):** the FOM was founded in 1911; on 27 July 1920, after a "patriotic" march by public employees and men of the Magallanes Battalion during the mobilization against Peru, organized by the Patriotic League and a "White Guard," which the FOM had declined to join for lack of notice to consult members, the marchers went to the FOM hall; with the complicity of police and local authority the hall was burned and members killed: the authorities counted four dead, the FOM's own records about thirty; deaths came from gunfire and the fire, and firefighters were forced not to fight the blaze under threat of jail or ill-treatment (fire report No. 8, 29 July 1920); the 1968 monument to the FOM martyrs is by the sculptor Miodrag Zivkovic (SOURCED, Museo Regional de Magallanes, centenary dossier, 2020). ### A. Other settings
- **The Hydrostone:** built after the 6 December 1917 explosion to replace destroyed housing in the North End, completed 1920: some 325 fireproof dwellings of concrete "Hydro-stone" block with slate roofs, in a restrained Tudor Revival style, facing wide treed park-like courts with service lanes behind, and a row of shops on Young Street; master plan by the town planner Thomas Adams, buildings by Ross and Macdonald; described as the country's first public housing project; National Historic Site 1994 (SOURCED, Parks Canada directory). Built as medium-income rental housing for displaced working families by the Halifax Relief Commission (UNVERIFIED, *Acadiensis* and Canadian Encyclopedia snippets). [K4, K9, K1] [P3, P5]
- **Black communities on the city's edge:** North Preston (formerly New Road; renamed in the 1970s, officially 1989), East Preston and Cherry Brook / Lake Loon were settled by Black Loyalists (1783–84) and Jamaican Maroons, who were brought to build fortifications on Citadel Hill after a 1796 rising; many residents had no legal title to land passed down for generations until the Land Titles Initiative (2017) and the Land Titles Clarification Act (April 2021); almost 260 North Preston residents had received clear title since 2017; North Preston has the highest concentration of African Canadians in Canada and high home ownership (SOURCED, Canadian Museum of History teachers' page). [K4, K9, K1] [P9]

About 600 people sleeping rough in 2024 and 1,211 people reporting homelessness in May 2025 (UNVERIFIED, snippets). ### B. Subcultures
- **Lebanese community:** the first documented settler from the northern Lebanese village of Diman arrived in 1894; later waves in the 1950s and during the civil war of the 1970s–80s, continuing by family sponsorship; early men worked as peddlers, then storekeepers, cleaners, bartenders and factory hands; families bought and renovated derelict houses, rented rooms to lodgers, and became property developers who now lead many large downtown projects; a Lebanese bakery opened in 1962 now makes about 200,000 bags of pita a day; the Canadian Lebanon Society was founded in 1938 and the Diman Lebanese Center opened in 1973; by some estimates as many people of Diman origin live in Halifax as in the village (650–700 registered); Halifax's share of Lebanese Canadians is nearly four times the national average (SOURCED, Canadian Museum of Immigration at Pier 21 article, undated). **Our Lady of Lebanon** Maronite parish: founded 1979 on Vatican instruction; a 500-seat church and parish hall built 1981–82, consecrated 4 November 1984, bell tower 1988, enlarged 1996–97; groups include a Cedar Festival committee, a dabke dance group, choirs and the Knights of Columbus (SOURCED, parish history page, 2017). Two Lebanese festivals a year (UNVERIFIED, snippet). [K4, K8, K12, K10] [P9]
- **African Nova Scotian church life:** the African Baptist chapel founded in 1832 by Rev. ### A. Other settings

- **Malay choirs:** the Cape Malay Choir Board (85th year in 2024) was started by a UCT professor to uphold Afrikaans; choirs are men's social or "sporting" clubs (one founded 1938 by five families); red fezzes, suits and ties; the signature item is the Dutch-derived folk song; before Covid 30 choirs, then fewer (13 competing in 2024); competition from City Hall, finals at the CTICC (16–17 November 2024) and a grand finale at Athlone Stadium (30 November); about 20 choirs parade on New Year's Eve; the board president said the sung Afrikaans helps children keep the language (SOURCED, People's Post / Nova News, 12 Nov 2024). - **Xhosa-speaking township families and the Eastern Cape:** each December tens of thousands travel "home" to the Eastern Cape: in 2015 about 45,000 were expected to leave from the Joe Gqabi bus facility in Philippi, over 3,000 a day between 10 and 23 December; the City checks buses at Joe Gqabi, Bellville, central Cape Town and Mfuleni, unloading any bus with defects (SOURCED, GroundUp, 7 Dec 2015). **Initiation:** two seasons (winter May–July, Xhosa only; summer 1 November–31 January); 680 practitioners (traditional surgeons, carers, administrators) trained in 2025; the province supplies protective and fire equipment and water tanks to registered initiation schools under the Customary Initiation Act 2 of 2021 (SOURCED, Western Cape Government statement, 31 Oct 2025). 51 initiation schools approved for summer 2024 and a City agreement to support registered schools (UNVERIFIED, snippets). [K2, K7, K1, K5] [P9]
- **Fishers:** Kalk Bay was founded about 1742 as a small port; its fishers descended from enslaved Indonesians and Malaysians, deserters, British settlers and their enslaved people, and Filipinos from about the 1850s; handline fishing; railway 1883, harbor 1910; mosque about 1846; under Group Areas the fishing families of Die Land were allowed to stay near their boats while the rest of the village was declared white in 1967 and non-fishers were forced out; snoek is sold from the backs of pickup trucks; a folk belief about a boxfish forecasting weather; overfishing and quotas push boats farther out; rising property values threaten the remaining families (SOURCED, Atiyyah Khan, Daily Maverick, 14 May 2021). In Hangberg (Hout Bay), small-scale fishers describe quotas favoring industrial firms; abalone and rock lobster poaching for export pays divers about R10,000 a trip against R4,000 a month on an abalone farm; poaching deaths at night in rough water; gang control of the informal trade (SOURCED, Fiona Anciano, Daily Maverick, 14 Aug 2018); small-scale fishers 1.5% of the hake quota (UNVERIFIED). [K7, K9, K14, K12] [SM:competence shown under pressure] [R]
- **Khoisan revivalists:** most active in Cape Town: protests, ceremonies, community language courses, history workshops, medicinal practices, tourism and fashion; a Griqua museum in Elsies River; demands for land, recognition, a census option, return of remains and an official language; many with Khoisan ancestry still identify as "Colored," the apartheid classification that fed a myth of extinction; most revivalists reject the 2019 Traditional and Khoi-San Leadership Act (SOURCED, Rafael Verbuyst, Africa Is a Country, 23 Jan 2023). The Act took effect 1 April 2021 and was later struck down by the Constitutional Court for lack of public participation (UNVERIFIED, snippets). [K4, K9] [P9]
- **Minibus-taxi operators:** the August 2023 strike (3–10 August) by the national taxi council over impoundments left tens of thousands of commuters stranded, kept over 450,000 pupils and 17,500 teachers from schools in its first days, saw at least five buses burned and five people killed, 120 arrests; impoundment continues for driving without a license, off route or unroadworthy, and the council agreed not to call a strike in the middle of a working day (SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org).
