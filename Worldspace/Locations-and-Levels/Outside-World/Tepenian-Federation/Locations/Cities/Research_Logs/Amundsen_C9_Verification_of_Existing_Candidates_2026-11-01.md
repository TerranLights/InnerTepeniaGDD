### 1a · IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the IceCube Lab (ICL)  (row 1)

Primary source read in full by text extraction: Aartsen et al., "The IceCube Neutrino Observatory: Instrumentation and Online Systems," JINST 12 (2017) P03012, arXiv:1612.05093 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.05093; PDF saved and converted with pdftotext). Secondary: CERN Courier, "The coolest job in physics" (https://cerncourier.com/a/the-coolest-job-in-physics/, fetched); IceCube "Life at the Pole" week-13 post (https://icecube.wisc.edu/news/life-at-the-pole/2014/04/2014-week-13-at-pole); southpolestation.com 2005-06 photo page (https://southpolestation.com/0506/06photos9.html).

**Verified (what the detector and building are)**
- SOURCED (arXiv 1612.05093, section 1.1.4): "The IceCube Laboratory (ICL), located at the surface in the center of the array, is the central operations building for the experiment." Surface cables "are routed up two cable towers on either side of the structure and into a server room on the second floor." The server room "houses the South Pole System ... including all data acquisition and online filtering computers. Power is supplied by the South Pole Station generators."
- SOURCED (same): the building is "temperature-controlled to a target of 18 C; continuous airflow through the server room is important to avoid overheating"; the second floor is "shielded against electromagnetic interference with a metal mesh"; "Because of the low humidity, strict protocols to minimize electrostatic discharge when working on ICL equipment are necessary."
- SOURCED (same, section 5.1): 86 boreholes, about 60 cm in diameter, to 2,500 m; 5,160 DOMs total (arXiv 1710.01191 snippet: 60 DOMs per string, 86 strings, about 1.5 to 2.5 km depth); DOM diameter 35 cm.
- SOURCED (same, section 6.2): the South Pole System (SPS) "comprises 19 racks of computing and network hardware." Table 7 machine counts: DOMHubs 97 (one per in-ice string plus 11 for IceTop), other data acquisition 4, monitoring 3, event filtering 24, system infrastructure 8, other 6 (sum 142, DERIVED: 97+4+3+24+8+6 = 142). Servers "typically upgraded every three to four years." Network backbone 10-20 Gbps.
- SOURCED (same): "Total power usage of the detector and online systems, including computing servers, is approximately 53 kW" (DOMs 30.6 kW at 5.7 W each; DOMHubs 12.4 kW at 128 W each; 20 filter-client servers at 300 W = 6 kW). UPS covers "station power outages of up to 15 minutes." Severe problems "result in a page to the IceCube winterover personnel via the station's Land Mobile Radio (LMR) system."
- SOURCED (same, section 6.6): station data traffic by geosynchronous satellites with steeply inclined orbits; "four to six hours of communications once per sidereal day" per satellite; "a window of about 12 hours of connectivity with bandwidth of 250 Mbps for uni-directional data transfer and bandwidth of 5 Mbps for bi-directional internet connectivity"; Iridium for the rest, "up to 2.4 kbps of bandwidth per modem." IceCube's own messaging system (I3MS, deployed 2015) uses Iridium modems and gives "low-bandwidth secure shell (ssh) connectivity to the South Pole."
- SOURCED (section 6.1-6.5): data-taking runs are split into 8-hour periods; configuration changes "at most once per year (typically in May)"; about 15% of events pass the online filters to go by satellite (line "a subset of events (about 15%)"). Uptime target above 99% (section 6.8 text: "detector uptime of greater than 99%"); "clean uptime" of 97-98% in recent years against a 95% target.
- SOURCED (section 4): surface cables 300 m to about 800 m long; a 1 m deep trench was dug between the ICL and each IceTop station.
- SOURCED (section 6.2): a scaled-down copy, the South Pole Test System, sits in Madison, Wisconsin, with servers and DOMHubs identical to those at the Pole and "a small number of DOMs in chest freezers."

**Drilling (SOURCED, same paper, section 5.1 and Tables 5-6)**
- 5 MW Enhanced Hot Water Drill (4.7 MW thermal + 0.3 MW electrical); water 88 C, 760 L/min, 7,600 kPa; maximum drill speed 2.2 m/min, ream 10 m/min; fuel (AN-8) 21,000 L per hole on average (15,000 L at peak); average hole cycle 48 h, fastest 32 h; "seven field seasons (approximately 21 months total time)"; peak 20 holes in 2009-10 (early November to mid-January).
- SOURCED: the drill system was two sites: the Seasonal Equipment Site (generators, water tanks, pump and heating buildings, a central control building, mechanical and electrical shops, spare-parts storage, and the Rodriguez well) and the Tower Operations Site (drill tower with attached operations building, hose and cable reels); maximum reach between them 450 m. Firn (the top 50 m of compressed snow) was drilled separately by a copper-tube hot-water cone at 2 m/h.
- SOURCED: "The drill crews worked in three shifts per day of ten people per shift." Season startup took about four weeks; shutdown began about two weeks before season end with propylene-glycol flushing and compressed-air blowout. "IceCube had only four reportable drilling-related safety incidents in approximately 52 on-ice person-years."
- DERIVED: three shifts of ten = 30 drill-crew people on shift at a time (arithmetic from the sentence above; the total drill-season headcount including support is NOT FOUND in this paper).

(This is a single sample crewmember, not an average, correcting a search-snippet paraphrase.) **WAIS Divide field processing (SOURCED, Souney et al. 2014 unless noted)**
- Site: 79 28.058 S, 112 05.189 W, elevation 1,766 m, mean temperature about -30 C, about 1,640 km from McMurdo; ice 3,465 m thick; deep coring in austral summers 2007/08 through 2011/12 with the DISC drill; final depth 3,405 m on 1 December 2011. The NSF "operated a seasonal field camp at WAIS Divide to support both the WAIS Divide ice-core project and other West Antarctic field science activities"; resupply mainly by LC-130 aircraft.
- Layout: "two abutted steel arch structures: an unheated 30.5 m x 9.1 m x 8.2 m drilling arch, and an actively refrigerated 25.6 m x 9.1 m x 5.0 m core-handling arch with an 18.3 m x 3.7 m x 3.7 m underground basement for core storage." Four Carrier industrial freezer units kept the core-handling arch "below -25 C"; the drilling arch was left unheated for the drillers' productivity and to ventilate drilling-fluid vapor (70% Isopar-K, 30% HCFC-141b). Both arches are "completely buried by accumulation and drift" (Fig. 2 caption, photo January 2006 vs end 2012/13 after seven winters).
- Operating rhythm: designed for "a 24 hours per day, 6 days per week drilling operation producing 4 m of 122 mm" core per run; "Throughout the project, drilling and processing rates consistently reached >30 m of ice core per day."
- Workflow in the field: core transfer truss, receiving and logging station with a Fluid Evacuation Device (FED), 1 m logging and cutting, 8-12 hours in drying booths, then packing into boxes on wood skids, loaded onto Air Force pallets; goal "to assign a precise depth to the ice, cut the ice into 1 m long sections, measure its electrical properties to obtain a preliminary depth-age scale, and safely prepare the ice for transport." "Only minimal processing and sampling of the ice core was carried out in the field."
- Brittle ice: from about 580 to 1,310 m depth a different method was used, and the brittle ice from the 2008/09 season was left in the underground basement over the 2009 winter to relax; the report on the 2007-08 season and the paper give the basement as 18.3 m x 3.7 m x 3.6-3.7 m (one source writes 3.6 m, the paper 3.7 m; immaterial).
- Staffing (SOURCED, search-result paraphrase of a UNH/IDPO source, not opened at its primary page, UNVERIFIED as to wording): "10 drillers, up to 9 core-processing personnel, 15-20 camp staff and other science groups." The Souney paper itself gives only the Denver figure (below) and thanks "the dozens of core handlers."
- 2007-08 field report (SOURCED): NICL field team of two (Hargreaves, Bencivengo) trained all core handlers "starting with core reception / logging station, 1m measurement logging and cutting station, database operation, cart loading, core packing, loading core boxes onto wood skids, gantry / hoist operation and loading skids onto air force pallets." New equipment: computers in "warm boxes," digital Balluff measuring sticks, the FED, roller racks moving core 16 m at a time ("it does take two people to move a rack safely"); the logging database failed (an extra page broke tracking counters) and the crew "simply reverted to logging into paper notebooks"; the refrigeration "kept the working area below 15 C" (the minus sign is not in the extracted text; UNVERIFIED sign) "but were noisy and the defrost cycle left lots of snow on the floor, which made the floor slippery"; frost from defrost cycles stuck the hoist on its I-beam. Drying booths dried carts "in 12 hours or less."
- Transport chain (SOURCED, same paper): about 18,000 km retrograde: WAIS Divide to McMurdo by cold-deck LC-130 (about 3.5 h flight; cores in freezers within 2 h of landing at Pegasus), McMurdo to Port Hueneme, California by cargo vessel in February ("the longest and riskiest leg"), then by truck about 1,600 km to Denver. Three purpose-bought 12.19 m "SAFECORE" containers with redundant cooling and generators, run at -30 C, telemetry on location and temperature, capacity about 1,440 m of ice per season; a refrigeration technician accompanied them from McMurdo to Denver.

**NICL / NSF Ice Core Facility, Denver (SOURCED)**

### 2.14 · Row 14: Ascension Island residency rules now

Primary text read: Entry Control (Ascension) Ordinance 2007, Revised Edition of the Laws 2017 as printed with amendments to 2026 (PDF from https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/documents/entry-control-ascension-ordinance/, converted with pdftotext; the printed text shows the amendments "A5 of 2015, A1 of 2017, A5 of 2017, A6 of 2026"), and the Entry Control (Ascension) (Amendment) Ordinance 2026, No. 6 of 2026 (https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/app/uploads/2026/05/ORD-6-Entry-Control-Ascension-Ordinance-2026.pdf, converted). B4 section 2 already has the Wikipedia statement of "no right of abode" and the three-month contract rule; this adds the statute.

**What the law says (SOURCED)**
- Section 4(1): "Subject to this Ordinance, there is no entitlement for any person to land or remain on Ascension." Section 4(2): landing or remaining without "a landing permit issued by an immigration officer or an entry visa issued by the Administrator" is an offense, penalty "A fine of GBP 5,000 or imprisonment for 12 months, or both." (Same penalty for masters of vessels who fail to get permission, s.3, and for staying after a permit expires, s.9.)
- Section 6: entry visas are written applications to the Administrator; fee set by Gazette notice (visa fees revised by Gazette Notice 49 of 20 April 2017); the Administrator may grant "unconditionally or with such conditions ... or may refuse"; "if no period is specified, the permit is valid for 30 days from the day on which it is issued"; the Administrator may vary or delegate to the Chief Immigration Officer. Section 7: landing permits for a time specified. Employment-linked residence is therefore a visa with a period and conditions, not a status (the ordinance contains no word for residency or abode).
- Section 8: grounds for revocation, including a breach of condition, conviction for an offense punishable by six months or more, lack of "adequate means of support," a security risk, illness "likely to require medical treatment for which the medical facilities available in Ascension are inadequate," refusal of a medical examination, fraud, and "continued presence ... undesirable in the public interest"; also revocable "where there has been a material change in circumstances."
- Section 10: removal orders can provide for arrest, detention "until a suitable vessel is available," and forfeiture of money or property to defray the cost of removal. Section 12: written representations to the Governor are allowed but "does not operate as a postponement or stay" unless the Governor orders it.
- Section 5(2) exemptions: (a) active armed forces members; (b) people "in the service of the Crown in right of Her Majesty's Government of Ascension when visiting Ascension for 28 days or less" (the phrase "when visiting ... for 28 days or less" was inserted by Ordinance A6 of 2026); (c) UK, Falkland, St Helena and Tristan da Cunha government staff on official duty; (d) US government personnel and contractors "as provided for by Article XII of the Bahamas Long Range Proving Ground Agreement dated 25 June 1956"; (e) NATO staff on official duty; (f) exempt dependants: spouse or long-term partner, or a child under 18 normally resident with a person in (a), (c), (d) or (e), "in respect of whom that person's employer provides transport to Ascension, living accommodation, and repatriation"; (g) others the Governor appoints by Gazette notice (notices of 18 June 2013 and 16 November 2020 are referenced). Section 5(2A): crew on a commercial agreement may remain until their ship or aircraft leaves.
- Section 5(1): deemed visa for people in lawful custody, required by law to remain, landed through misadventure, or unable to leave through certified illness or injury, "which expires when the reason ... ceases."
- Amendment No. 6 of 2026: "to reduce the total exemption provided to employees of Ascension Island Government from immigration control to only those persons attending for periods less than 28 days." Enacted 20 May 2026; the text says it comes into force on 19 May 2026 while the cover says date of commencement 20 May 2026 (an internal inconsistency in the printed ordinance). Saving provision (s.3): a person exempt immediately before commencement "shall continue to be so exempt for so long as that person remains in continuous employment" of the Ascension government, and the exemption ceases when that employment ends or continuity is broken. (The saving provision text says "Her Majesty's Government," the title says "His Majesty's": wording as printed.)
