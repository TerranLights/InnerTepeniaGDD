## 1 · S3-07 Pompeii and Herculaneum

**Claim to test:** towns stopped in a single event, with daily life preserved in place; how they are found, read and presented in first person. **Verdict:** CONFIRMED in substance, NARROWED on two points. (a) Both towns were buried by the AD 79 eruption of Vesuvius, Pompeii mainly under ash and pumice, Herculaneum under some 20 m of pyroclastic material that charred (rather than burned) wood, furniture and papyrus scrolls; both preserve streets, houses, workshops, food outlets and human remains. (b) "Presented in first person" is NOT how either park presents its dead: the 2026 Pompeii casts exhibition explicitly uses no first-person victim narratives and frames the casts as "not art"; it is a memorial approach (see C). (c) The date: the park's current position (Dec 2024) is that 24 August AD 79 cannot be ruled out; the October date proposed in 2018 is not settled. See A.

### A. Snapshot
- **The eruption date debate** [K+ chronology]. Pliny the Younger's letters (written c. AD 106-108, manuscripts lost, copies vary) give the traditional 24 August. In Oct 2018 the park announced a charcoal inscription in a Regio V house under renovation, read as the 16th day before the Kalends of November (17 October), and argued an eruption around 24 October; autumn fruits (pomegranates, figs, walnuts) were also cited. In Dec 2024 the park's own E-Journal (Zuchtriegel and colleagues) reported an experiment: a test charcoal inscription on the same wall stayed legible about ten months, so the 2018 graffito need not date from the last weeks; with Pedar Foss's 2022 manuscript study (every date other than 24 August is a later invention without basis in the manuscript tradition) the authors conclude "al momento non ci sono sufficienti elementi per scartare la data del 24 agosto"; Zuchtriegel: "Forse abbiamo sottovalutato la tradizione letteraria." INGV and other researchers continue to argue for autumn. — SOURCED: National Geographic, E. Blakemore, 7 Jan 2026 (https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/mount-vesuvius-eruption-pompeii); MeteoWeb 12 Dec 2024 (https://www.meteoweb.eu/2024/12/ultimo-giorno-pompei-non-si-puo-escludere-data-24-agosto-79-d-c/1001713124/). UNVERIFIED (snippet only): the wording of the park E-Journal PDF itself (URL returned 404) and the INGV counter-position.
- **Pompeii, ancient:** walled city of about 64-67 ha, of which 17 of 66 ha remain buried (Zuchtriegel, AP, 20 Feb 2022, SOURCED https://wjla.com/news/offbeat/pompeii-rebirth-of-italys-dead-city-that-nearly-died-again). Population estimates range roughly 8,000 to 20,000, most often 10,000-12,000 — UNVERIFIED (snippets: worldhistory.org, berksarch.co.uk; no primary demographic source read).
- **Herculaneum, ancient:** about 4,000 inhabitants; walled area about 20 ha; on a plateau over the sea with streams on east and west; Roman municipality after 89 BC; Augustan rebuilding; damaged by the AD 62 earthquake — SOURCED: Parco Archeologico di Ercolano, "The story" (https://ercolano.cultura.gov.it/the-story/?lang=en, accessed 2026-11-21). Buried under about 20 m of volcanic material (SOURCED, park page "From discovery to archaeological park", https://ercolano.cultura.gov.it/from-discovery-to-archeological-park/?lang=en). The modern town of Ercolano sits directly on unexcavated parts, limiting excavation (UNVERIFIED, encyclopedia snippets).
- **Human remains.** Pompeii: over 1,000 victims found; 104 plaster casts made with Giuseppe Fiorelli's method (1863: liquid plaster poured into the voids left by decayed bodies); none of 26 casts CT-scanned or X-rayed contains a complete skeleton; casts were "considerably manipulated and likely creatively restored." A 2024 ancient-DNA study (Pilli et al., Current Biology 34(22), 7 Nov 2024) recovered genomes from five casts: all genetically male, including the "mother" with the golden bracelet; the four "family" victims of the House of the Golden Bracelet are not related; ancestry largely from recent eastern-Mediterranean immigrants — SOURCED (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11627482/). Herculaneum: discovered 1980, some 300 skeletons in nine of the twelve vaulted chambers (fornici) on the ancient beach, "probably used as warehouses or boat shelters," with jewelry, coin purses, house keys, amulets, tools and a surgeon's set; women and men, young and old, free and enslaved — SOURCED (park page "The escapees", https://ercolano.cultura.gov.it/i-fuggiaschi/?lang=en). Excavation phases 1980-85 (~150), 1997-99 (~75), 2008-12 (~104), 340 individuals in total per a 2020 Antiquity study — UNVERIFIED (snippet). Herculaneum has no body casts; remains are skeletons (UNVERIFIED, secondary snippets).
- **Defining feature:** sudden burial that froze an inhabited town mid-routine (houses under renovation, bakeries working, shops stocked). [P3, P6]

### B. What else exists there (then and now)
**The ancient working town (what the excavations show)** [K7, K12, K14, P4, P8]
- **Bakeries:** 39 excavated bakeries documented by Nicolas Monteix (Univ. of Rouen); by the 1st century AD domestic ovens in large houses gave way to commercial bakeries across the city; some had hydraulic grain-soaking systems; bread was still not general for the poor, who ate cereal porridges — SOURCED (Archaeology, July/Aug 2019, https://archaeology.org/issues/july-august-2019/collection/pompeii-industry-bakeries/digging-deeper-into-pompeiis-past/).
- **Mill-bakery as prison:** Regio IX, insula 10, announced 8 Dec 2023: a house under renovation with a Fourth Style residential part and a bakery whose mill room had no door to the street, its only exit into the atrium, small high barred windows; semicircular grooves in the basalt floor guided blindfolded donkeys (and enslaved workers) around the millstones; three victims found. Zuchtriegel: the most troubling side of ancient slavery, the owner "felt the need to restrict" movement — SOURCED (park press release, https://pompeiisites.org/en/?p=50244). [K9, SM:dangerous system]
- **Food outlets:** counts of street food counters (thermopolia) and inns vary by survey: 69 thermopolia and 146 cauponae (Eschebach), or 89 and 120 (Consorzio Neapolis database) — SOURCED (Ellis, Internet Archaeology 24, page updated 30 Jun 2008, https://www.intarch.ac.uk/journal/issue24/4/2.1.2.html). [K12]
- **Enslaved household at Civita Giuliana** (suburban villa 600 m north of the walls; excavated 1907-08, again since 2017): room "c" with three beds that doubled as storage (2021); room "a" with one plain bed and one costlier bedstead, two cupboards, amphorae, an iron hoe, suggesting a hierarchy among the enslaved; rodents in the vessels; no shackles, bars or padlocks found, control appears to have worked through the household's internal organization and permitted (legally unprotected) families — SOURCED (park press release 20 Aug 2023, https://pompeiisites.org/en/?p=47281). [K4, K7, K9]
- **Herculaneum organic finds:** charred household shrines, cupboards, tables, a bed, a cradle, the polychrome ceiling of the House of the Telephus Relief and a boat hull found on the beach; preserved because the >20 m pyroclastic mud cut off oxygen; Maiuri's 1920s paraffin consolidation; now in a permanent display at the Antiquarium from 10 Apr 2025 in two rooms set out as a Roman house, under continuous microclimate control, restored with the Packard Humanities Institute — SOURCED (Finestre sull'Arte, Apr 2025, https://www.finestresullarte.info/mostre/ercolano-antiquarium-esposizione-legni-romani-conservati-dall-eruzione). [R maintenance of fragile material]
- **The papyrus library (Villa of the Papyri, Herculaneum):** excavated from 1752 by Karl Weber's tunnels for the Bourbon king, yielding more than 1,800 carbonized rolls; in 1753 the king commissioned Antonio Piaggio, who built a machine that unrolled them slowly with gravity and silk threads (about fifty unrolled, some destroyed); most are in the Biblioteca Nazionale "Vittorio Emanuele III," Naples; six given to Napoleon (1802, now Institut de France), 18 to the future George IV (1815); the villa was partly excavated 1992-97 and may hold more rolls — SOURCED (Il Post, 20 Oct 2023, https://www.ilpost.it/2023/10/20/storia-papiri-carbonizzati-ercolano-scavi/). The Vesuvius Challenge (public, donation-funded, co-founded by Brent Seales, Nat Friedman, Daniel Gross) reads unopened rolls by X-ray microtomography and machine-learning ink detection; first word read 2023 ("purple"); 2023 Grand Prize text from PHerc. Paris. 4; PHerc. 1667 unrolled digitally end to end (announced June 2026) — SOURCED (https://scrollprize.org/firstscroll); date of PHerc. 1667 announcement UNVERIFIED (snippet). The Officina dei Papiri Ercolanesi joined the national library in 1910 (UNVERIFIED, snippet of cultura.gov.it). [K14, SM:humanity and technology]

**The present-day institutions** [K1, K8]

- **Visitors (Director's report 2025, table 1, SOURCED):** Pompeii 4,153,910 (2024) and 4,000,090 (2025); all sites 4,267,233 and 4,118,926 (-3.48%). Ticket income booked at €54,767,770.51 for 2025. Peak day 36,480 on 6 Oct 2024 (UNVERIFIED, snippet of MiC data).
- **Herculaneum Archaeological Park:** autonomous institute since 2016 (separate from Pompeii; the Pompeii page says 2017); visitors 521,426 (2024) and 553,611 (2025, +6.2%) — SOURCED (https://ercolano.cultura.gov.it/herculaneum-archaeological-park-visitor-growth-in-2025-6-2-compared-to-2024/?lang=en). - **Herculaneum Conservation Project (HCP):** founded 2001 by agreement with the Packard Humanities Institute (operating in Italy through the Packard Institute for Cultural Heritage), first long-term public-private partnership of its kind in Italy, described as UNESCO-recognized best practice — SOURCED (https://ercolano.cultura.gov.it/herculaneum-conservation-project/?lang=en). ### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- Burial created the archaeology industry that is now the towns' main employer and draw: >4 million paid visitors a year at Pompeii, ~550,000 at Herculaneum; the modern town of Pompei was itself founded later around a sanctuary and gained autonomy in 1928 (SOURCED above). [K8]
- The kind of burial shaped what can be "read": Pompeii's ash left voids for casts; Herculaneum's hot deep flows left skeletons, charred wood and a carbonized library that is now read by X-ray and machine learning (SOURCED above). [K14]
- Living beside a still-active volcano: Pompei and Ercolano are both wholly in Red Zone 1 of the Vesuvius emergency plan (18 municipalities entirely in zone 1; 25 in all with partial ones) — SOURCED (Napolitan, 18 Jan 2017, https://napolitan.it/2017/01/18/59071/vesuvio-3). [K1, K6]
- **Vesuvius emergency plan** [K1, K5, K6]: national plan dates from 1996, updated 2001 (18 municipalities, total evacuation before an eruption, each town twinned with a host Region); Red Zone enlarged to 25 municipalities (Campania resolution 250, 26 Jul 2013; Prime Minister's directive of 14 Feb 2014). Phases: I attention, II pre-alarm (rescuers progressively garrison the zone; families with somewhere to go may leave by their own car after telling the municipality where, and may not move their domicile back until phase I is declared again; entry "gates" may restrict access), III alarm (everyone left is moved out within 72 hours, the assumed time to clear the whole zone; private traffic barred from the main exit roads; by own car or by Region buses to meeting areas and on to the twinned Region). A cultural-heritage plan (census of buildings and archaeological areas, priorities, protection techniques) is led by the Ministry's regional crisis unit with the fire service and the Carabinieri heritage unit — SOURCED (Protezione Civile, "Indicazioni per l'aggiornamento delle pianificazioni di emergenza … zona rossa dell'area vesuviana," 20 Jan 2015, PDF read locally). About 670,000-700,000 residents in the zone — UNVERIFIED (Sky TG24, Fanpage snippets).

### E. Structural features (industry, economy, arts, work and life)
- **Money flows** [K8]: from 30 Apr 2025 (Decree UDCM 150) 30% of Pompeii's net ticket income goes to the national "Fondo luoghi della cultura" (€15,316,950 in 2025); 0.1% of ticket income goes to the INPS welfare fund for painters, sculptors, musicians, writers and dramatists (€54,767.77); €700,000 to the Unità Grande Pompei for its upkeep; €2.2 million for staff overtime on the PNRR special project — SOURCED (Director's report 2025, pp. 24-25). The EU-funded Great Pompeii Project (~€105 million, to be spent by 2016) followed the 2010 collapse of the gladiators' training hall (Schola Armaturarum), which "nearly cost" the UNESCO listing; deterioration dated from the 1970s; 17 of 66 ha remain unexcavated — SOURCED (AP, 20 Feb 2022, WJLA).
- **Work friction** [K7, K9]: 24 Jul 2015 the site was closed from 9 a.m. for an assembly of four unions (CISL, UNSA, FILP, FP) with long lines outside; Naples Chamber of Commerce: "We are making a fool of ourselves on the world stage" — SOURCED (ANSA, https://www.ansa.it/english/news/lifestyle/arts/2015/07/24/pompeii-closure-makes-a-fool-of-italy_daf0fefb-a6cf-42fc-85a4-4aa9265ec397.html). A 2014 closure kept an estimated 1,100 visitors out 08:00-11:00 (UNVERIFIED, snippet).
- **Crowd control** [K6, K9]: daily cap of 20,000 and named tickets from 15 Nov 2024 (to "reduce human pressure" and stop illegal touts; Zuchtriegel: "zero tolerance" on touting); on the free Sunday of 4 May 2025 entries were suspended for hours after the cap; waits up to 45 min; booth staff handwrite names at 4-5 min per visitor for group vouchers; licensed guides and about a dozen unions object; Zuchtriegel cited staff shortages for not reorganizing entrances — SOURCED (The Art Newspaper, 5 Jun 2025, https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/06/05/pompeii-tour-guides-decry-lengthy-queues-as-visitor-numbers-soar). Time slots from 16 March and reduced access to some houses from 4 p.m. after 15 October — SOURCED (park home page).
- **Everyday behavior rules (K6)** — YES, formal rules for visitors (Pompeii visitor regulations, linked Decree 446 of 23 Dec 2025): personal named tickets with ID checks; groups 5-35, over 15 must use whisper sets; bans on eating outside designated areas, touching frescoes, climbing walls, picking fruit or flowers, entering behind barriers, scant or bare-chested dress, loud talk, running, banners, stickers, smoking outside designated spots, leaving fountain taps running, bags over 30x30x15 cm, unauthorized drones and professional equipment, bicycles and scooters; dogs ≤10 kg, leashed, carried inside buildings; visitors told not to shelter under trees in storms and to keep away from stray dogs. **Enforced by** security staff (ID checks, removal for refusal, violence, harassment or indecency) and park staff; tour leaders answer for their groups and can be temporarily barred for repeated breaches; Director General sets slots and group caps; fines referred to but not quantified; legal base for imaging Legislative Decree 42/2004 arts. 106-108 — SOURCED (https://pompeiisites.org/en/visiting-info/regulations-for-visitors/). Residents' rules: the Vesuvius plan's phase rules (above). No customary rules among residents found.
- **Heritage crime** [K9]: at Civita Giuliana a long-running clandestine dig (tunnels from a modern house) was uncovered by the park, the Torre Annunziata prosecutor and the Carabinieri from 2017; those involved were prosecuted criminally and civilly — SOURCED (park release 2023). Looting damage put at almost €2 million — UNVERIFIED (AFP snippet).
- **War damage** [K9]: Allied bombing in Aug-Sept 1943 (Operation Avalanche, aiming at nearby roads and rail) "badly damaged" the ruins and struck the site museum; many structures rebuilt after the war; recent collapses involved postwar reconstructions — SOURCED (Getty, https://getty.edu/art/exhibitions/pompeii/destruction.html). 160-165 bombs in nine raids and unexploded bombs in the foundations — UNVERIFIED (Irish Times, Open Culture snippets).
- **K11 (speech, loose):** Italian; the Neapolitan language/dialect is in everyday use across the Vesuvian area (general knowledge, not researched — UNVERIFIED).

## 2 · S3-08 Prypiat and the Chernobyl Exclusion Zone

**Claim to test:** a young town built for a technical installation, emptied in days and left with its belongings; who still works or lives in the zone. **Verdict:** CONFIRMED, with corrections. Prypiat (Ukrainian spelling; Pripyat in older English) was officially founded 4 Feb 1970 as the satellite town of the Chornobyl nuclear power plant, gained town status nine years later, and was emptied in a single afternoon (27 April 1986), not "in days"; residents were told to pack for about three days and many never returned for their belongings, which were later partly looted. The zone is not empty: before 2022 several thousand people worked there on rotation, about 100 elderly returnees still live there, and since 2022 soldiers are garrisoned in it. Organized tourism (from 2011) ended with the 2022 invasion.

### A. Snapshot
- **Founding and growth:** official founding date 4 Feb 1970; built as the plant's satellite town under plant director Viktor Briukhanov; city plan by Moscow architects; planned for about 12,000 residents by 1975 and at most 18,000 after units 3-4, but nearly 50,000 by 1986 with projections to 85,000; average age 26; about 1,000 births a year; construction declared a Komsomol project, recruiting young workers from across the USSR; eighteen dormitories for single residents, apartments mostly for young families; microdistrict 3 built at nine stories instead of five — SOURCED (dev.ua, O. Onopriienko, 25 Apr 2025, https://dev.ua/news/velyka-istoriia-mista-do-richnytsi-chornobylskoi-trahedii-1745481824). First buildings (dormitories, canteens) 1971, about 3 km from the plant (UNVERIFIED, snippet).
- **Population 1986:** about 49,000 inhabitants, about 3 km from the reactor; 45,000 residents evacuated on 27 April; by 14 May some 116,000 people within 30 km evacuated; a further 220,000 resettled in later years — SOURCED (World Nuclear Association, "Chernobyl Accident 1986," https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/safety-and-security/safety-of-plants/chernobyl-accident.aspx). The figure 49,360 (IAEA FAQ) is UNVERIFIED (page returned 402).
- **The accident:** unit 4 exploded at 01:23 on 26 April 1986 (SOURCED dev.ua). Two workers died in the explosions; 28 people (six of them firemen) died of acute radiation syndrome within weeks; about 200,000 cleanup workers in 1986-87 (the legal category of "liquidators") — SOURCED (WNA).
- **The evacuation:** residents were first told to stay indoors with windows shut and given potassium iodide; on the Saturday evening they were told a bus would collect them on 27 April at 2 p.m. to move them away "for three days"; a loudspeaker announcement spoke of an "unfavorable radioactive atmosphere"; they were advised to take documents, a few necessities and some food — SOURCED (The Moscow Times, A. Odynova, 22 Apr 2011, https://themoscowtimes.com/archive/the-day-a-soviet-paradise-stood-still; note the article misdates the explosion's weekday). Evacuation began about 13:30 on 27 April; vehicle counts vary between sources (about 1,100 to 1,390 buses, ~250 trucks) — UNVERIFIED (Soviet documents in the Wilson Center archive seen only as snippets; the archive pages failed: DNS error and 403). Hydrofoils from the river station carried key workers and researchers (SOURCED dev.ua).
- **The zone today:** roughly 30 km across (Al Jazeera); initial 30 km zone of 2,800 km², later extended to 4,300 km² (WNA, SOURCED; WNA's figure includes later extensions). Chornobyl Radiation and Ecological Biosphere Reserve: created by Presidential decree 174/2016 of 26 Apr 2016 on 226,964.7 ha; reduced by decree 835/2024 of 5 Dec 2024, which moved a 45 m-wide, 695.2 ha strip along the Belarus border to the State Border Guard Service for fortifications and monitoring, leaving 226,269.5 ha — SOURCED for the 2024 transfer and areas (Ukrainska Pravda, 6 Dec 2024, https://www.pravda.com.ua/news/2024/12/6/7488009/); 2016 decree text UNVERIFIED (president.gov.ua returned 403).
- **Defining feature:** a single-industry young town abandoned within hours, inside a radiation exclusion zone that is still a workplace. [P3, P6, SM:humanity and technology]

### B. What else exists there
**Prypiat before 1986 (institutions, services, supply)** [K4, K5, K12, P4]
- Five primary schools with 15 parallel classes of over 30 pupils each; the "Energetik" Palace of Culture with an 800-seat hall, rare-wood floors and marble; two swimming pools (one fit for international competition) and a later ice rink; a department store among the first in the Ukrainian SSR; shops that sold cheese and sausage amid Soviet shortages; a city hospital whose chief doctor said in 1982 that the birth rate, not illness, was his main concern; a river station with hydrofoils reaching Kyiv in about two hours — SOURCED (dev.ua 2025).
- A concert hall, a large hotel, a restaurant, and an amusement park with a Ferris wheel delivered for the May 1986 holidays; a direct water route to Kyiv and rail to Moscow — SOURCED (Moscow Times 2011). 25 shops and 27 cafes and restaurants seating 5,500 at once; scarce goods otherwise found only in Moscow — UNVERIFIED (hobby-site snippet, chernobyl.one).
- **Alcohol control (K6):** Prypiat City Council Order No. 6 of 18 Jan 1986 declared a "Month of Sobriety" (20 Jan-20 Feb 1986) banning sale of wine and liquor in all shops and catering; such months were held regularly; supply was planned centrally through the Departments of Workers' Supply (ORS); about 40 dormitories housed laborers from across the USSR — SOURCED as read on a blog that reproduces the order (@chernobyl_archive, Teletype, 3 Jul 2022, https://teletype.in/@chernobyl_archive/pripyat-alcohol-eng, translating a LiveJournal post); the primary order itself not seen.

**The zone as a workplace** [K1, K7, K14, R]
- **Chornobyl NPP (state specialized enterprise):** units were RBMK-1000 reactors; unit 2 shut after a turbine-hall fire in 1991, unit 1 in Nov 1996, unit 3 in Dec 2000 (SOURCED WNA). Decommissioning in four stages; stage 1 ended 2015 (fuel moved to used-fuel storage); stage 2 (final shutdown and conservation of units 1-3) under way, preparatory work 94% (unit 2) and 44% (unit 1), unit 3 physical works from 2027, prep to end 2028, safe storage from 2034, then dismantling; done with the plant's own personnel — SOURCED (World Nuclear News, 2 Sep 2026, https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/decommissioning-of-chernobyl-units-progressing). A solid radioactive waste processing complex entered industrial operation after a license amendment of 20 Dec 2024 — UNVERIFIED (snippet, chnpp.gov.ua).
- **Workforce figures (by date):** 2013: about 4,000 serviced the zone and 3,500 operated the plant; workers had shorter hours, early retirement and 56 vacation days a year (versus 24); quarterly or annual medical exams; all personnel carry dosimeters — SOURCED (Kyiv Post, 25 Apr 2013, https://archive.kyivpost.com/lifestyle/chornobyl-exclusion-zone-full-of-life-gossip-work-323765.html). 2021: about 7,000 people came in and out to work; more than 4,000 on shifts of 15 days a month or four days a week to limit dose; about 100 scientists in the zone's laboratories; guards at the Prypiat entrance on 12-hour shifts; workers sandblast contaminated metal for decontamination and resale (good pay, high risk) — SOURCED (National Geographic, J. ### D. How the defining feature shaped those things

[K7, K8]

## 3 · S3-09 Hashima Island (Gunkanjima)

**Claim to test:** an extremely dense residential and industrial island for one enterprise's workers, emptied in 1974 and left to decay. **Verdict:** CONFIRMED, with precision added. Hashima (Takashima area, now Nagasaki City) was a Mitsubishi undersea coal mine and company town; peak population 5,259 in 1959 (Burke-Gaffney) or 5,267 in 1960 (Gotō and Sakamoto 2005, as cited by He 2018), on about 6.3 ha; the mine closed 15 Jan 1974 and the last residents left 20 Apr 1974. It was left to decay until 2009, when Nagasaki City opened a fenced visitor route; since 2018 the city has run a 30-year, ~14.7 billion yen preservation program, so "left to decay" is now only partly true. Wartime forced labor of Korean and Chinese workers is documented by death records, testimony and Japan's own 2015 statement, and its presentation remains disputed (see E).

### A. Snapshot
- **Location and form:** about 17.5 km from Nagasaki port, about 5 km off the Nagasaki Peninsula; originally a sandstone reef about 320 m by 110 m, enlarged by six reclamations (1897-1931) to about 6.3 ha (about 2.8 times the original); about 480 m by 160 m, enclosed by a seawall about 1,200 m long and up to 20 m high; some 50 concrete structures — SOURCED (He Yunyan, doctoral thesis, Nagasaki University, March 2018, pp. 46-48, read locally from the repository PDF, https://nagasaki-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/28167/files/SKK49_Ka.pdf). The battleship name dates from about the Taishō era (same).
- **Ownership and mining:** coal first taken by local fishermen around 1810; shaft mining under the Fukahori branch of the Nabeshima from 1887; bought by Mitsubishi in 1890 for 100,000 yen; shafts 1895 and 1898; slag used to reclaim land; production about 150,000 t/yr around 1916, peak about 410,000 t in 1941; about 16.5 million t over 84 years — SOURCED (Brian Burke-Gaffney, "Hashima," hosted at https://www.uwosh.edu/faculty_staff/earns/hashima.html, undated). He (2018, citing the 1989 Mitsubishi "Takashima Coal Mine History") gives about 15.7 million t in 1890-1974, the No. 2 shaft deepened to 616 m by 1936 (then Japan's deepest), workings reaching about 1,000 m below sea level, and the closure reason as exhaustion of safely minable coal — SOURCED. Coal was strongly caking bituminous coking coal for steelmaking, low in ash and sulfur, gassy and prone to spontaneous combustion; a 1956 gas outburst killed three miners — SOURCED (PRESIDENT WOMAN Online, Fūraidō, 10 Nov 2024, https://president.jp/articles/-/87999). A 1964 underground fire that injured ten and killed miners is cited as the beginning of the end — UNVERIFIED (snippet, same series).
- **Population and density:** over 3,000 by 1916; peak 5,259 in 1959; about 835 people per ha island-wide, about 1,391 per ha in the residential district — SOURCED (Burke-Gaffney). He (2018) gives 5,267 in 1960 and a density more than nine times Tokyo's wards (SOURCED, citing Gotō and Sakamoto 2005).
- **Closure:** officially announced 15 Jan 1974; all residents gone by 20 Apr 1974; dismantling of mine facilities to year's end — SOURCED (He 2018; Burke-Gaffney). Residents left belongings and stray cats behind (Burke-Gaffney, SOURCED).
- **Defining feature:** a total company town on a walled rock, every service inside the seawall, entirely dependent on boats. [P3, P5, SM:dangerous or fascinating system]

### B. What else exists there (in its working life)
**Housing and the company's provision** [K4, K8]
- 1916: a six-story reinforced-concrete block (Building 30), reportedly Japan's first large concrete building; units of one six-tatami room with shared bathing, cooking and toilets; two years later a nine-story E-shaped block, then Japan's tallest building; more than thirty concrete buildings by the end; the manager's wooden house on the summit of the original rock — SOURCED (Burke-Gaffney).
- **Housing by rank:** unmarried miners and subcontractors in one-room units; married Mitsubishi workers in two six-mat rooms with shared kitchens, toilets and baths; senior staff and teachers in two-bedroom flats with kitchens and flush toilets — SOURCED (Burke-Gaffney). A former resident's family of eight lived in a 6-mat plus 4.5-mat pair of rooms; they cooked on a wood-fired stove until her high-school years — SOURCED (PRESIDENT WOMAN Online, 20 Oct 2024, https://president.jp/articles/-/87298).
- **Company provision:** free housing; water and power about 10 yen a month in 1959 (mainland 6-mat flat without bath about 3,000 yen); up to two free propane cylinders; near-universal TVs, washing machines and refrigerators; a 1972 Hashima worker earned about 200,000 yen a month against 50,000-60,000 for a new graduate — SOURCED (Newsweek Japan reprint of PRESIDENT Online, 24 Jan 2025, https://www.newsweekjapan.jp/stories/culture/2025/01/533751_3.php). Residents rotated cleaning and maintenance duties; no motor vehicles; corridors and stairs linked buildings — SOURCED (Burke-Gaffney). [K1, R shared maintenance]
- **Work rhythm (K7):** after a union formed in 1946, eight-hour days on three shifts; the company promoted an image of harmonious labor relations and a family-like single-mine community — SOURCED (Newsweek Japan, 24 Jan 2025). Miners descended a vertical shaft and rode underground man-cars to the face (SOURCED PRESIDENT, 10 Nov 2024).

**Water, food and supply (K5)**
- No water source: from 1891 distilled water from a salt-making apparatus was rationed for drinking and seawater used otherwise; water boats from the Shōwa era (the Mishima Maru, later three boats) never ended shortages; an undersea pipeline of two steel pipes about 6.5 km from the Nomozaki Peninsula, begun 1956, opened about a year later supplying about 1,000 t a day to every home — SOURCED (Newsweek Japan reprint, 24 Jan 2025, https://www.newsweekjapan.jp/stories/culture/2025/01/533751_4.php). Burke-Gaffney gives 1957 for the pipeline (SOURCED).
- Fresh meat and vegetables arrived twice daily on the boats Hiradoguchi Maru and Yūgao Maru from Nagasaki; in rough seas residents fell back on canned food and instant noodles; peddlers from Nozukushi port (Takahama village) crossed daily and set up open-air stalls on the main street (fish 11:00-16:00, greens about 9:00-11:00, unsold stock consigned to the purchasing society); paydays brought more peddlers; with high wages and little to buy, luxury cosmetics and made-to-order clothes sold well; flowers sold well on an island without greenery — SOURCED (PRESIDENT WOMAN Online, 3 Nov 2024, https://president.jp/articles/-/87741). Storms that stopped boats for more than a day caused shortages (SOURCED Burke-Gaffney). [K12]
- **Shops and services:** a shopping street in Building 57 (liquor, sundries, clothing, food, restaurants, tobacco, greengrocer, fishmonger; ground rent 5 yen per tsubo paid to Mitsubishi); Building 16 pawnshop that also sold school uniforms, bookstore, general store; Building 30 wage window, small shops and a restaurant; the Hashima Purchasing Society in the basements of Buildings 59-60 (largest market, below-market prices, ticket system); a consumer co-op office with a milk shop; rice stored and sold in a Building 65 basement; an ice room selling ice candy — SOURCED (PRESIDENT, 3 Nov 2024). Facilities overall: primary and junior high school, playground, gymnasium, pinball parlor, cinema, bars, restaurants, 25 shops, hospital, hairdresser, Buddhist temple, Shinto shrine, brothel; rooftop planting from 1963 with imported soil — SOURCED (Burke-Gaffney). A nursery on the Building 65 roof and a seven-story school — UNVERIFIED (snippet).
- **Bathing (K6, K10):** before the pipeline, fresh water was boiled for baths and seawater used in droughts or rough seas (residents then brought their own fresh water for rinsing); after 1957, heated fresh-water baths at any hour; private baths only in managers' housing (Building 5), the staff club (Building 7), the Seifūsō inn and Building 3 (1959, every unit); communal baths in Buildings 8, 31, 60 and 61 (the last underground, about 20 adults); free to miners and families but only 3-8 p.m., crowded throughout; women's changing rooms larger than men's because working miners bathed at the pithead; baths were the social hub where children arranged to meet after school — SOURCED (Newsweek Japan reprint, 15 Jan 2025, https://www.newsweekjapan.jp/stories/culture/2025/01/532695_5.php). [R shared rest ritual]

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- Island scarcity drove the firsts: concrete high-rises for typhoon resistance and density; distilled water and water boats, then an undersea pipeline; rooftop gardens on imported soil; everything inside one company's walls, with housing and services allocated by rank (SOURCED above). [K1, K4, K5]
- Total company provision (free rent, near-free utilities, high wages, ticketed company store) shaped consumption toward luxury goods and made the island a single-employer community (SOURCED above). [K8]
- Total dependence on boats meant storms meant shortages; today it means tourism is weather-gated (E). [K5]

### E. Structural features
- **Wartime forced labor (K9), reported neutrally:**
  - **Koreans:** Koreans first worked in the Takashima field in late 1917 (334 at Takashima by May 1918, 70 underground at Hashima); mobilization to Japan from September 1939; per the Hashima miners' union records, 500-800 Korean laborers in 1943-45; one or two days' training before underground work; Koreans brought from Sakhalin in August 1944; in summer 1943, 350 Hashima Korean laborers were exchanged for 350 Chinese prisoners of war and sent to Mitsubishi's Nagasaki Shipyard (and exposed to the August 1945 atomic bombing); workers crawled 600-1,000 m through narrow passages and worked wet over 12 hours a day; heavy quotas, poor food, daily violence; seaside dormitories flooded; escape attempts using planks or crates rarely succeeded; a 1935 gas explosion killed more than 20, nine of the 17 with recorded birthplaces Korean — SOURCED (Encyclopedia of Korean Culture, entry by Jung Hye-kyung, revised 17 Jun 2025, https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0081607; the entry notes it reflects the author's views). The 2012 Korean government commission study of death records identified 122 Korean deaths (120 in the Nagasaki cremation records plus two), an estimated 49 of them forcibly mobilized; the total cannot be determined (same).
  - **Death records (Japanese-side civic research):** cremation and burial permits 1925-45 list 122 Korean deaths (58 from illness, 63 unnatural: suffocation, crushing, head injury, drowning, explosion); Koreans estimated at about 500 at any time in 1939-45; their average death rate in 1944-45 was 3.1% against 2.6% for Japanese; survivor Suh Jung-woo: soybean-dregs and brown rice, beatings until one agreed to work, "a prison island that one could never escape"; survivor Kim Sun-ok: Chinese workers were treated worse and contact with them was forbidden — SOURCED (He 2018, pp. 59-60, citing the Association's publications 1982-2016).
  - **Chinese:** 204 Chinese laborers arrived at Hashima in June 1944 (the first Chinese brought to Nagasaki Prefecture); 15 died; they were called by number, packed 40-50 to a low dark wooden room, given one set of thin clothes a year and a loincloth a month, fed bean-cake gruel and small steamed buns, worked 12-hour days with quotas enforced by beatings, and were barred from learning Japanese beyond numbers and work terms; Korean and Chinese dormitories stood at opposite ends of the island to keep them apart; Chinese workers left on 19 Nov 1945 under occupation escort — SOURCED (He 2018, pp. 60-63, 93-94, citing survivor testimonies and the Mitsubishi company history).
  - **Conflicting totals:** Burke-Gaffney states about 1,300 laborers died on the island by August 1945 (SOURCED as his statement; it is far above the death-record counts and no source for it is given). Global Voices (Nevin Thompson, 21 Aug 2021) gives at least 800 Koreans sent, 134 reportedly died (SOURCED as its statement, https://globalvoices.org/2021/08/21/japans-battleship-island-hides-history-of-wartime-forced-labor/).
  - **The presentation dispute:** at the 2015 inscription of the "Sites of Japan's Meiji Industrial Revolution" (23 sites including Hashima) Japan's ambassador stated that in the 1940s many Koreans and others were "brought against their will and forced to work under harsh conditions," and pledged an information center; the foreign minister then said "forced to work" did not mean "forced labor" — SOURCED (He 2018, p. 52; Global Voices 2021). In July 2021 the World Heritage Committee expressed "strong regret" over insufficient explanation; in September 2023 it acknowledged new measures (including a section at the Tokyo center honoring workers who died) and asked for a report by 1 Dec 2024 — SOURCED (Arab News Japan, 15 Sep 2023, https://www.arabnews.jp/en/japan/article_103641/). Japan's report of 31 Jan 2025 kept materials asserting that Korean workers' conditions matched Japanese workers' and placed victims' testimonies in a Korean-language collection on a bookshelf; Seoul expressed regret — SOURCED (Korea Times, 1 Feb 2025, https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/foreignaffairs/20250201/korea-voices-regret-after-japans-unesco-report-again-fails-to-reflect-forced-labor). In 2017, after the Korean film "The Battleship Island," Nagasaki's mayor said life on Hashima differed from the film and that former residents described it as being like one family — SOURCED (He 2018, p. 52). Former residents' groups have released material rebutting forced-labor claims (UNVERIFIED, 2026 Segye snippet).
- **Everyday behavior rules (K6) — YES:** historic: bath hours 3-8 p.m.; Chinese and Korean workers barred from contact with each other and with Japanese; Chinese barred from learning Japanese (SOURCED above). Today: landing only on boats of licensed operators; visitors confined to the walkway on the island's south (outside it, entry is barred by the 2008 ordinances); landing prohibited under the "Hashima Visitor Facility Use Prohibition Standard" in high wind, waves or poor visibility; a trial since 22 Apr 2023 allows landing if the Iōjima offshore gauge read ≤0.5 m during the previous boat's berthing (cap 0.6 m), wind from east through north to west, ship anemometer ≤5 m/s, visibility ≥500 m, and the captain judges it safe — SOURCED (Nagasaki City reply, 18 May 2023, updated 5 Feb 2025, https://www.city.nagasaki.lg.jp/teian/31312.html; ordinances SOURCED via He 2018, p. 51). Enforced by Nagasaki City and the boat captains.
- **Reopening and tourism (K3, K8):** Mitsubishi gave the island free to Takashima town in 2001; Takashima merged into Nagasaki City in 2005; ordinances in 2008; on the World Heritage tentative list January 2009; landings open from 22 Apr 2009 on a southern route; five licensed operators, two trips each a day; 4,601 landed in the first month, 59,000 in the first year, 167,342 in FY2013, over 500,000 cumulative by April 2014, 90% from outside the prefecture; a local think tank put the economic effect at about 6.5 billion yen over three years; national historic site October 2014; World Heritage July 2015 — SOURCED (He 2018, pp. 50-52, citing Yomiuri and Nishi-Nippon Shimbun). Tour prices about 3,900-5,500 yen (UNVERIFIED, booking-site snippets).
- **Preservation money (K8, K14):** the city's 30-year program from FY2018 (about 14.7 billion yen) repairs seawalls and coal-production facilities; on 16 Apr 2026 a 52 m² wooden research base built and co-run by Shimizu Corporation opened on the north side as a lab and emergency shelter, the first building placed on the island in decades; wind, rain and salt are accelerating decay — SOURCED (Kumamoto Nichinichi/Kyodo, 16 Apr 2026, https://kumanichi.com/articles/1981309; note it dates the last building "before the 1970 mine closure," which conflicts with the 1974 closure). ## 4 · S3-10 Pyramiden, Svalbard

**Claim to test:** a company town at the edge of habitability, closed in 1998 with much left in place, now kept by a few caretakers. **Verdict:** CONFIRMED, with refinement. Pyramiden (78°39' N, Billefjorden) was a Soviet, then Russian, coal-mining settlement of the state trust Arktikugol, about 1,000 people at its 1980s peak; coal mining stopped in spring 1998 (last coal 31 March) and the town was left over a few hectic summer months with most buildings, equipment and household goods in place. "Caretakers" is accurate for winter (about three people in 2014; one to five reported since), but since 2007 Arktikugol has restored infrastructure, reopened a hotel (2013) and runs summer tourism with 20-30 staff, and Russia now promotes the town as a science-and-tourism outpost.

### A. Snapshot
- **History:** a 1910 Swedish expedition (financed by Jernkontoret and Grängesberg-Oxelösund) claimed the field; Soviet Russia bought it in 1926 (the Polar Institute cruise handbook says 1927, via Russkij Grumant); Trust Arktikugol founded 1931, acquired Barentsburg and Pyramiden rights 1932; development from 1939, first wintering 1940-41; in 1941 the 99 people there were evacuated with Barentsburg and Grumant Russians to Arkhangelsk by British forces, who burned coal and fuel stores and machinery; rebuilt after WWII as the intended Soviet "capital" on Svalbard (first consulate there); 1,712 workers and 28,000 t of equipment sent in 1950; first coal shipped 1956; about 1,000 inhabitants in the 1980s — SOURCED (Arealplan for Pyramiden, planbeskrivelse, adopted 16 Dec 2014, prepared by LPO arkitekter for Trust Arktikugol, Sysselmesteren site, PDF read locally: https://www.sysselmesteren.no/siteassets/miljovern/arealplanlegging/gjeldende-arealplaner/pyramiden/planbeskrivelse-pyramiden.pdf; its history section cites Arlov 2003 and Andreassen, Bjerck and Olsen 2010). About 9 million t produced 1955-1998, about 1 million burned locally in the power plant — SOURCED (Norwegian Polar Institute cruise handbook, updated May 2015, https://cruisehandboka.npolar.no/en/isfjorden/pyramiden.html).
- **Geology and closure:** Carboniferous seam, up to 30° dip, broken by faults, making mining costly; Soviet collapse cut funding; the 1996 air crash (141 Russian and Ukrainian passengers and crew) and the 1997 Barentsburg explosion (23 miners killed) worsened matters; mining stopped spring 1998; the town was abandoned over "a few hectic summer months," leaving most buildings, installations and "large quantities of equipment and household goods" — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014). Last coal 31 March 1998 — UNVERIFIED (encyclopedia snippets).
- **Workforce origin:** technology and labor largely from central and southern regions; at times a majority of Ukrainian miners; contracts of two to three years — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014); two-year contracts, a growing share of women and children in the 1980s — SOURCED (cruise handbook). Miners mostly from the Donbas and Siberia with families — UNVERIFIED (Mentoday lifestyle article, 14 Nov 2022, read, but not a scholarly source). Skilled miners historically from the Donetsk region and service staff from Volyn (said of Barentsburg) — SOURCED as reported (The Moscow Times, Russian service, K. Egorova, 18 Jul 2026, opinion piece with composite characters, https://ru.themoscowtimes.com/2026/07/18/polyarnoe-solntse-russkogo-doma-kak-shpitsbergen-zhivet-vopreki-geopolitike-a200577).
- **Defining feature:** a complete, planned, prestige company town at about 79° N, frozen at its 1998 abandonment. [P3, P6, SM:dangerous or fascinating system]

### B. What else exists there
**The working town (1956-1998)** [K1, K4, K5, K7, K12, R]
- **Layout:** a linear town from the harbor in the east to the culture center in the west; mine and mine buildings to the north, food production (barns, greenhouse) to the south; the fullest part grouped around a square named for the 60th anniversary of the October Revolution, with housing, school and kindergarten, hospital, culture and sports buildings; 1950s wooden houses with traditional Russian detailing; 1970s-80s brick blocks from Soviet Arctic town designs; people lived in dormitory rooms and small flats and most ate in the canteens; planning suited to workers on two-to-three-year stints — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014).
- **The power plant as the town's heart:** the coal-fired plant powered mine and town; its surplus hot water ran in pipes to heat all houses, the saltwater swimming pool and the stalls of cows, pigs and hens; manure fertilized greenhouse soil for cucumbers, parsley, tomatoes, onions, lettuce, peppers and ornamental plants; in 1975 local output was 35,000 kg meat, 48,000 l milk, 110,000 eggs and 5,700 kg vegetables; ash from the plant was made into bricks at a local brickworks, used for many later buildings — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014). Food free from 1958; greenhouse 1985; library of over 50,000 titles; nightly cinema; museum — SOURCED (cruise handbook). [R shared energy, K12]
- **Culture and leisure:** new "culture palaces" in Barentsburg and Pyramiden in the early 1970s; indoor sports halls and swimming pools in the late 1980s (Pyramiden's pool opened 1987, reported as "The Northernmost"); color TV from Moscow from 1985; first permanent indoor plumbing from Elsabreen to Pyramiden 1957 — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014; Aleksandr Portsel, "The Poliarnaia Kochegarka newspaper as a source on the Soviet mining on Spitsbergen in the 1930-80s," Nordlit 2020, https://septentrio.uit.no/index.php/nordlit/article/download/4992/4730/17363, read locally). About 1,000 people incl. about 100 children in the mid-1980s; school, cinema, plays, concerts — SOURCED (National Geographic, J. Lafferty, 2019/2023, https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/singing-ghosts-svalbard).
- **Work culture (K7, K14):** postwar rebuilding by five-year plans and competition between workers, shifts and sites, with prizes and bonuses; best workers named on the board outside the administration building and in speeches — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014). Competitions for the best-kept dormitory room (prizes: a radio, gramophones, furniture in 1948; 300/200/100 ruble bonuses in 1960) — SOURCED (Portsel 2020). Only top workers sent, wages roughly doubled, pay banked in savings books while goods were bought with the trust's own vouchers — UNVERIFIED (Mentoday 2022; numismatic catalog snippets show Arktikugol vouchers of 1979 "for internal settlements," pages not loaded).
- **Information:** the trust newspaper "Poliarnaia kochegarka" (1948-1991, renamed "Shakhter Arktiki" in 1989), printed in Barentsburg three times a week; its Pyramiden correspondent worked from a room in the hotel and sent copy by helicopter or radio — SOURCED (Portsel 2020).

**The present settlement** [K1, K5, K8]
- **Operator and plan:** Trust Arktikugol is plan owner; maintenance since 2007, partly with the Governor (summers 2011-2012); buildings locked to stop souvenir-taking; land-use plan adopted 16 Dec 2014 to allow tourism and research in existing buildings (AARI coordinates research there with the Norwegian Meteorological Institute) — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014). Arktikugol's Center of Arctic Tourism set up in 2015; about 65% of the trust's revenue now from non-mining activities; target 50,000 tourists a year by 2040; restoration begun in Barentsburg and Pyramiden; Rosgidromet's "Typhoon" institute monitors pollution in spring and summer — SOURCED (D. S. Shumkov, "Ugol'" 2026 no. 4, pp. 37-40, DOI 10.18796/0041-5790-2026-4-37-40, PDF read locally).
- **Buildings in use (2014):** the container hotel at the quay and Hotel Tulipan (with a campsite using its toilets, showers and kitchen); the second floor of the service building and one Finnish house as staff housing; in winter only the service building is used; the hotel is set to "rest mode" at 10 °C in the dark season — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014). Hotel reopened 2013 (UNVERIFIED snippet); only two residential buildings in use in 2026, a hotel with restaurant and a dormitory (SOURCED as reported, MT 2026).
- **People now:** "normally three people" living there through the polar night to run the technical infrastructure (SOURCED, Arealplan 2014); about 20 in summer and no more than five in winter, staff living in the hotel with guests, symbolic rent deducted from wages (SOURCED as reported, Mentoday 2022); 20-30 in tourism in summer and 1-3 in winter (SOURCED as reported, MT 2026); a few permanent residents plus about 20, mostly scientists, in summer (UNVERIFIED, Izvestia snippet; page 403). One Ukrainian caretaker was in 2019 the driver, gardener, janitor and de facto hotel manager; staff live without phone signal or internet — SOURCED (NatGeo 2019/2023). Statistics Norway reports Barentsburg and Pyramiden together, so no official Pyramiden-only count was found (SOURCED, SSB table 07430 extract).
- **Utilities (2014):** drinking water from the dammed Gusinoe lakes, now trucked to a 150 m³ steel tank (the old pipelines intact except at the Bertilbreen river crossing); waterborne heat from a coal boiler in the service garage, coal stock from the mining era judged enough for at least 15 years, a diesel boiler in reserve; quay about 7 m deep, ships to 20,000 t; heliport for year-round helicopter access (flights strictly limited by concession); all waste shipped to Barentsburg for sorting — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014). [K1, K5, R]
- **Access (K5):** day boats from Longyearbyen from about May (when the Billefjorden ice allows) to mid-October, daily in summer; March-May by snowmobile, dog sled or ski over fjord ice (recent poor sea-ice winters have made this harder); helicopter year-round — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014). Since early 2025 the research vessel "Professor Molchanov" sails from Murmansk to the Russian settlements, mostly carrying staff and cargo (SOURCED as reported, MT 2026; 10 trips planned for 2026, TourDom, 4 Mar 2026, https://www.tourdom.ru/news/arktikugol-rasschityvaet-prinyat-na-shpitsbergene-ne-menee-10-tysyach-turistov-v-2026-godu.html). Russian-settlement visitor numbers: about 12,000 in 2024 and about 7,000 in 2025 (scope unclear), forecast at least 10,000 in 2026; 2025 tourism revenue a record (SOURCED, TourDom 2026).

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- Distance and the polar night made the town a closed loop: one power plant supplying light, heat, pool, barns, greenhouse and bricks; its own farm and canteen food; its own newspaper; organized culture and sport to fill the dark season; two-to-three-year contracts as the work rhythm (SOURCED above). [K1, K7, K10, R]
- Prestige under the 1920 Svalbard Treaty: Pyramiden was meant as the Soviet showcase; the same logic now drives its reuse for tourism and a proposed international science center (Turkish TÜBİTAK MAM team visited 21 Jul 2024, three months after Turkey joined the Treaty on 11 Apr 2024) — SOURCED (The Barents Observer, T. Nilsen, 23 Jul 2024, https://www.thebarentsobserver.com/arctic/turkish-science-team-explores-russias-svalbard-plans/126008; Shumkov 2026). [K8]
- Abandonment created new uses: kittiwakes nest on window sills of the empty administration buildings (UNVERIFIED, Nat Hab blog snippet); a tame family of Arctic foxes lived under the hotel in 2019 (SOURCED NatGeo).

### E. Structural features
- **Everyday behavior rules (K6) — YES:**
  - **Polar bears:** anyone traveling outside the settlements must carry suitable means of scaring off polar bears; the Governor recommends firearms — SOURCED (Sysselmesteren, "Weapon," updated 9 Mar 2026, https://www.sysselmesteren.no/en/weapon/). In Pyramiden visitors and staff carry rifles to leave the buildings; bears have entered the hotel through open windows (SOURCED as reported, MT 2026); "no one goes outside without at least one gun" (SOURCED NatGeo). Pyramiden counts as a settlement; tour businesses operating outside settlements must use approved Svalbard guides (rule announced for 1 Jul 2027 in one snippet, later reported postponed) — UNVERIFIED (snippets of Governor and government pages; regjeringen.no returned 403).
  - **Buildings:** locked to prevent souvenir-taking (SOURCED Arealplan 2014); entry without permission not allowed (UNVERIFIED snippet).
  - **Heritage law:** under the Svalbard Environmental Protection Act § 39 all cultural remains from before 1946 are automatically protected with a 100 m safety zone; at Pyramiden this covers Swedish-period remains (1910-27) in the harbor area, remains of Arktikugol's first phase (1930-45) on the south side of Pyramiden mountain (exploratory drilling, early mines and transport, the oldest buildings), and the graveyard; works in the zone beyond maintenance need approval from the Directorate for Cultural Heritage (§ 44); post-1946 structures (most of the town) are not covered by the Act's chapter V — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014, based on Dag Avango and Sander Solnes's 2013 survey).
  - **Environment:** 2024 amendments to Svalbard environmental law include a drone ban in protected areas and on ice from 1 March, a 5-knot speed limit near shore April-August to protect birds, permits for camps, and limits on landings for tourists — SOURCED as summarized (Shumkov 2026, citing the Act as amended 20 Dec 2024).
  - **Other reported rules:** cats banned on Svalbard (a 1988 regulation on importing animals cited by fact-checkers; a Pyramiden cat famously registered as an Arctic fox) — UNVERIFIED (snippets; Mentoday). Mentoday also reports that dying and burial "are not allowed," citing permafrost, and that the seriously ill and pregnant are sent to the mainland — recorded as a reported claim only, UNVERIFIED and not confirmed by any official source (see the deferred mortuary caution).
  - **Wartime-era rule:** Norwegian authorities cannot ban visits to Pyramiden but advise against buying from the Russian side (SOURCED as reported, MT 2026).
- **Friction (K9):** 1996 Vnukovo Airlines crash into Operafjellet (29 Aug 1996), all 141 aboard killed, mostly miners and families returning from leave (SOURCED as stated in Arealplan 2014 for the death toll; date UNVERIFIED snippet). On 9 May 2023 Trust Arktikugol staged a Victory Day vehicle parade in Pyramiden (car, snowmobile, bulldozer, excavator; at least 11 people) under Russian, Soviet and company flags, with a "Donetsk People's Republic" flag on the bulldozer — SOURCED (The Barents Observer, A. Staalesen, 15 May 2023, https://thebarentsobserver.com/en/security/2023/05/russian-separatist-flag-waved-svalbard); a larger military-style parade with a low-flying Mi-8 the same day in Barentsburg (same; and R. Stange, spitsbergen-svalbard.com, 10 May 2023). In 2022 Norway stopped two containers (~7 t) of supplies for Barentsburg at the Storskog border under sanctions, then let them continue on a Norwegian truck and ship via Tromsø — SOURCED (The Moscow Times, 6 Jul 2022, https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2022/07/06/norway-greenlights-blocked-goods-for-russian-arctic-miners-a78225). After 2022 Ukrainian citizens in the Russian settlements were reportedly told to take Russian passports or leave (SOURCED as reported, MT 2026, an opinion piece).
- **Pollution and cleanup (K1, K9):** many contaminated-ground sites from the mining era; PCB-containing materials in several buildings; diffuse emissions from facades — SOURCED (Arealplan 2014). Coal dust and ash have changed local moss flora toward acid-loving species; permafrost thaw is deepening the active layer and threatening foundations — SOURCED (Shumkov 2026). Volunteers of the "Clean Arctic" project with Arktikugol prepared the first 30 t of scrap metal from Pyramiden for shipment (TASS, 15 June, year not stated; SOURCED, https://tass.com/economy/2146505/amp). [K14]
- **Work and pay now (K7, K8):** guides 70,000-90,000 rubles a month, captains about 160,000; housing and meals provided but deducted from pay (said of the Russian settlements generally) — SOURCED as reported (MT 2026). Before 2022 the Russian and Norwegian communities often celebrated holidays together in a modest way (same).
