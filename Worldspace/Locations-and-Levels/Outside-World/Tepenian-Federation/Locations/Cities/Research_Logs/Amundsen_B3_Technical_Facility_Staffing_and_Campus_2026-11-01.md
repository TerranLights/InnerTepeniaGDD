## 3. Large particle accelerators and synchrotrons (Fermilab, SLAC, DESY, J-PARC, ESS)

## 7. Archives and vaults staffed on site

### 7.1 Svalbard Global Seed Vault
- Staffing: SOURCED (NordGen FAQ, https://www.nordgen.org/our-work/svalbard-global-seed-vault/frequently-asked-questions/, read): "no permanent staff on site"; NordGen staff travel there "when the Seed Vault is opened for deposits"; Statsbygg, "based in Longyearbyen", provides "continuous surveillance" and servicing; "no individuals apart from authorized personnel, are allowed inside the Seed Vault due to enhanced security measures" (media and guest visits have ended); only NordGen personnel handle seed boxes; depositors can withdraw only their own seeds; "Seed boxes are scanned at the airport to ensure they contain seeds and nothing else" using Longyearbyen airport's equipment.
- Facts: SOURCED (Wikipedia, secondary, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svalbard_Global_Seed_Vault): opened 26 Feb 2008; 130 m inside a sandstone mountain; storage rooms at -18 C; 1,355,591 accessions as of June 2025; 127 depositors (March 2025); tripartite agreement among Norway, the Crop Trust and NordGen; "black box" deposit arrangement (depositor owns contents, no one has access to anyone else's seeds); Wikipedia quotes The Independent that COVID-19 did not pose a risk "as there are no permanent staff at the Svalbard facility."
- Opening frequency: UNVERIFIED (a 2016 magazine item via search summary says three times a year; NordGen FAQ gives no number).
- What failed: SOURCED (news via search summary, UNVERIFIED in primary text): in May 2017 meltwater entered the access tunnel and froze there (no seeds damaged) because permafrost "had not developed as projected"; officials said it was meant to operate "without the help of humans but now we are watching the seed vault 24 hours a day"; upgrade (about 4.4 million USD per a WorldArchitecture headline) removed heat sources (transformer moved outside the tunnel), added drainage and waterproof walls, and "additional security".
