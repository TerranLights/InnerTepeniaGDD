## 1 · #60 Kashgar (Kashi, Qeshqer), Xinjiang, China — Round 2

### A. Other settings (neighborhoods, housing, workplaces, schools, clinics, outdoor spaces, off-season)
- **The 19th-century serais of Kashgar (closes Round 1's "caravanserais in Kashgar itself: NOT FOUND").** The British *Report of a Mission to Yarkund in 1873* (vol. 1; Toyo Bunko copy digitized by NII's Digital Silk Road) lists eleven serais under the heading "SERAIS KASHGHAR": Badshahi Kona (31 rooms; "The Zakatehi resides here" as OCR-printed, probably the zakat (tax) collector; that reading is mine, not the source's), Badshahi Nawa (30; "in the Chowk"), Alum Akhun (23), Ahmed Akhun (31), Ameen Akhun Bai (17), Zak Mullah Bai No. 1 (21) and No. 2 (63, "very small rooms"), Mahomed Yusuf Bai (28), Yusuf Bai (30), Khola Yar Beg (40), Ismail Beg (22). The best charged **5 tangas a room a month**, second-class serais 3 tangas; the first-class serais held merchants and traders, while the rest held no merchants but **sheltered people with no residence of their own, such as camelmen**. The same page lists Yarkand's fourteen serais (the first four "chiefly occupied by Andijani merchants"; one earning about 4,000 tangas a year in rents; others named Hindoo, Bajaori, Khoten, Oil Serai) and nine at Aksu. SOURCED: dsr.nii.ac.jp/toyobunko/VIII-1-B-17/V-1/page-hr/0632.html.en (OCR text; Aksu room counts unclear). Naming pattern worth noting: serais named after their owner-founders (Akhun, Bai, Beg) and after the trading community or product they served. [K4, K5, K8] [P5] [R: rest-house tiers by class]
- **Neighborhoods (mahalla).** The Old City (*kona shähär*) is described as narrow lanes of mud-brick homes, mosques and shops organized in *mähällä* neighborhoods; anthropologist Jay Dautcher's work (cited by the article; his fieldwork was in Ghulja/Yining, not Kashgar) describes the mahalla as a key unit of Uyghur social identity. Under the reconstruction plan, residents moved into multi-story compounds with **shops on the ground floor and flats above**; one resident complained the upper floors were very hot in summer, another (a taxi driver) liked the running water, electricity and air conditioning; the author's interviews (2012-13) found Uyghur opinion divided, the main grievance being exclusion from decisions. SOURCED: Liam Powers, openDemocracy, 28 Mar 2014. A Heidelberg journal article states kinship networks are central and that close friends are treated as relatives whether or not related by blood. UNVERIFIED (snippet only; page blocked by a bot wall, see log). [K4, K9] [P4]
- **Old City reconstruction — actual numbers (Round 1 NOT FOUND, partly closed).** China Daily (18 Sep 2015) gave about **CN¥7 bn, 65,000 households and 220,000 residents**, completion planned for 2017; an ifeng report (2015) says retired regional cadres declared the core-area reconstruction finished and the area made a 5A scenic site. UNVERIFIED (search returns; pages not opened). Earlier figures: residents could choose rebuilt traditional-style houses or high-rises; early movers went to suburban high-rises (CECC, UNVERIFIED snippet). An official count of people actually relocated and returned: still NOT FOUND. A 2025 RFA Uyghur-service report says the regional housing department plans to demolish and rebuild **258 "old residential areas" in 2025** (over 76,000 residents; 118 in the south), after **6,888 renovated in 2019-2024**; the list of sites is not published and Kashgar is not named among them. SOURCED: rfa.org/uyghur ... 02132025172819 (19 Feb 2025, citing *Xinjiang Daily*). [K9]

- **Old City residents' new livelihoods (K8):** over **5,000 merchant units** in the Old City; many residents "do tourism business at their own doors"; the first Old City homestay owner recalled houses without gas, water carried from far off and rubbish taken far away; a sixth-generation herbal-tea seller said his annual income rose from about CN¥40,000-50,000 to CN¥300,000-400,000. SOURCED: CNR via yzwb.net, 19 Sep 2025. **Costume photography shops:** from 2 in 2020 to **over 200** in 2023, over 1,000 sittings a day; mostly Uyghur costumes, also Kazakh and Tajik; Old City tailors sew the costumes on contract and props (pottery, instruments) come from Old City workshops; the scenic-area management committee organized the shops into a photography association that issued service standards ("competition moved from price to service"). ### B. Subcultures
- **Pakistani traders and their Xinjiang families (K4, K7, K9):** AFP (Mar 2018) reported that merchants from Gilgit-Baltistan kept wives and children in Kashgar and followed a yearly rhythm: **each autumn they crossed the Karakoram Highway to winter in Pakistan and returned in spring**; from 2017 many wives and children disappeared into "re-education" centers (one wife taken March 2017; the husband turned back at the border in July); a trader said any contact from Pakistan was treated as a threat; the Gilgit-Baltistan assembly passed a protest resolution; China's foreign ministry said the matter was being discussed and denied the centers existed. SOURCED: Dawn (AFP), 25 Mar 2018. In 2019 most wives were reported released under conditions (no travel for three months, monitoring). UNVERIFIED (Arab News snippet). [SM: man versus society]
- **The Kashgari diaspora in Pakistan (relation, K4):** Alessandro Rippa (LMU Munich working paper, 2014) explains that Uyghur migrants in Pakistan are called "Kashgaris" because Kashgar was and is the hub of China-Pakistan trade, though most did not come from Kashgar; migrations from the 1940s (hajj pilgrims stranded after 1949; traders' families), settling in Parri, then Gilgit and Rawalpindi; after the Karakoram Highway opened to civilian traffic in **1982**, about 1,200 Xinjiang pilgrims crossed into Pakistan for the hajj in 1985 carrying goods to pay their way; two donated free pilgrims' hostels in Rawalpindi's China Market, **Kashgar House and Khotan House (opened 1986; bunk rooms around a courtyard with a prayer ground)**, closed after the 2006 China-Saudi direct-flight agreement and became traders' warehouses; from the late 1990s Uyghur and Kashgari traders lost the highway trade to Han companies and Pashtun traders. SOURCED: Rippa, *From Uyghurs to Kashgaris (and back?)*, Munich Working Papers in Social and Cultural Anthropology 7 (2014), read from the saved PDF. [K4, K5] [R: free rest-houses for travelers]
- **Kashgar Prefecture minorities:** Tajik about 33,600 (0.99%) and Uzbek about 2,500 (0.07%) in a census table (year not shown in snippet). UNVERIFIED. No source found on a present-day Uzbek or Andijani quarter in Kashgar.
- **Youth (football):** Kashgar became a national campus-football city in 2011 (all 43 schools) and a "characteristic city" in 2015; the Shenzhen-funded youth football base (completed 2023, operating July 2024) is southern Xinjiang's first with board, lodging and training; about **400 elite players on 20 teams** (U8-U16; girls' teams being formed); coaches teach in schools on weekdays, players train at the base at weekends and in winter/summer camps; 13 Kashgar players have reached professional clubs since 2011; a coach with 22 players (two girls) says parents drive children to training daily; a woman coach born 1998 trained in Guangzhou from age 12 and returned in 2020. SOURCED: The Paper, 13 Nov 2026 (state media tour). Yopurga County football association founded July 2025; a "Kashgar Cup" U16 international youth tournament (second edition at Yopurga). UNVERIFIED (search return). [K3, K14]
- **Bingtuan (XPCC) settlers:** the Third Division (Tumxuk city) near Kashgar; the "first plow" of military land reclamation is commemorated at Caohu, 41st Regiment (spring 1950). UNVERIFIED (search returns). Workforce origins and numbers: NOT FOUND.
- **Cadre "relatives" (K4, K6):** the "Pair Up and Become Family" program placed over 1.1 million cadres (by Sept 2018) with Uyghur and Kazakh households, at first a "Becoming Family Week" (Dec 2017), then stays **every two months, five days at a time**; cadres attended naming ceremonies, circumcisions, weddings and funerals and recorded each family member's ideology, religion, social activity and income; families received CN¥20-50 a day for shared meals; families said they could not pray or wear religious dress while cadres were present. A Kashgar baker was quoted in People's Daily praising hygiene know-how learned from his cadre. SOURCED: Al Jazeera (AP), 30 Nov 2018. [K6, K9]

### D. Third places
- **The century-old teahouse (Ostangboyi):** owner Mamet Osman (50 in 2017) took it over from his grandfather; opens 9:30 a.m.; first customers mostly older men; serves teas from China and abroad, the favorite being **Fu brick tea** (10 yuan a pot, about 250 pots a day, valued for digestion in a fatty diet); naan 2 yuan; **naan and a pot of tea is a common Kashgar lunch**; **Friday is the busiest day, as worshippers come after Friday prayers at Id Kah**; young people prefer modern teahouses with WiFi. SOURCED: Xinhua, 25 Feb 2017 (before the 2017 mosque restrictions described by RFA). [K10, K12] [R: shared energy/rest ritual — warm platform and tea]

### E. Cultural activities, civic life and rules (K6)
- **Hospitality rules (K6, K12), general Uyghur, not Kashgar-specific:** step over the threshold, not on it; shoes off; bring a gift (fruit, eggs, a few naan, brick tea); wash hands three times and do not shake off the water; the host seats the guest and the best seat is the guest's; snacks set out before a larger meal; **naan is served whole and broken in front of guests, never thrown away (hard naan dipped in tea, spoiled naan put out for birds)**; tea in bowls, never let a guest's bowl run empty; bowls rinsed by passing a little tea from bowl to bowl; no chipped bowls; two hands to give and receive; stand when someone enters; return a food gift's plate with something on it; avoid politics and religion. SOURCED: Josh Summers, farwestchina.com, "Uyghur Customs: 26 Rules" (2014, page footer 2024; a long-term resident's guide; no regional distinction). Never place naan upside down. UNVERIFIED (travel sites). [R: shared meal rituals]
- **Festivals:** Corban/Qurban (Eid al-Adha) and Roza (Eid al-Fitr) at Id Kah square (above); Nowruz on the equinox, with travel sites naming the Old City, Yarkand and Yengisar as strongest for street music and household rituals. UNVERIFIED (travel site). Qingming grave-sweeping spread to ordinary Uyghur families in April 2024, with videos showing Chinese-only gravestones without Uyghur script or verses (region-wide; Kashgar not named). SOURCED: RFA Uyghur service, 8 Apr 2024. [K2, K9]
- **Ramadan rules (K6) since Round 1's 2011 material:** Peyziwat (Jiashi) schools made students and parents sign pledges not to fast (RFA 2018, UNVERIFIED); a Kashgar police officer said restrictions eased in his township from 2020 (RFA 2021, UNVERIFIED); **in 2022 village officials in Kashgar and Hotan prefectures received notices that only 10-50 Muslims per unit could fast, and fasters had to register**; in Tokkuzak township a policeman confirmed "Ramadan measures"; elsewhere eligibility was limited to the elderly and adults without school-age children. SOURCED: RFA, 1 Apr 2022. Enforced by village cadres and neighborhood committees. 2023-2026 Kashgar-specific rules: NOT FOUND.

### B. Subcultures: the people of city hans
- **Who lived in Istanbul's hans (mid-19th c.):** two pious-foundation registers list **593 urban hans and bachelor rooms (418 "han," 175 "bachelor room")** in 26 districts (Galata and the Bosphorus excepted); the 175 bachelor-room houses held 688 rooms (about four each); the Valide Han had 248 rooms; occupants were **single men who had left families behind — boatmen, porters and similar guild workers, workshop journeymen, masters and apprentices, seasonal soldiers, students and travelers** — living outside the family-based neighborhood order and so treated as a marginal group; bachelor rooms clustered by the Unkapanı port, between Tavukpazarı and Beyazıt, and at landing stages (Aksaray, Atpazarı, Hasköy, Üsküdar Balaban, Beşiktaş). ### A. Other settings
- **Living over the shop.** In 1864 the fair had **3,391 shops, each serving as workplace, home and dining room**; above each shop in the rows were rooms where merchants lived with their staff (some Moscow merchants brought 30 clerks and boys); at the peak, people slept "in attics, closets, cellars," hotels being few; **rich merchants breakfasted in their shops and dined in restaurants**; Kyakhta and Moscow tea merchants dined "only at 5-6 o'clock at the fashionable Nikita's, where all the foreigners gather," with champagne and truffles but no non-Orthodox fast-breaking food; many merchants slept in their shops beside their goods for fear of theft. **Eating and drinking places in 1864: 21 taverns, 51 cookshops, 38 half-cookshops, 77 drink stalls, 80 wine cellars, 4 porter houses, 4 confectioners, 68 coaching inns; in 1889: 78 taverns, 20 wine cellars, 11 buffets, 12 beer shops.** Melnikov's description: a near-European bazaar and Empire-style Main House beside, across the canal, wooden houses, low taverns, cookshops and inns "mixed with expensive restaurants glittering with luxury, unpaved streets and mud to the knee." ### E. Rules, policing and civic life (K6, K1)
