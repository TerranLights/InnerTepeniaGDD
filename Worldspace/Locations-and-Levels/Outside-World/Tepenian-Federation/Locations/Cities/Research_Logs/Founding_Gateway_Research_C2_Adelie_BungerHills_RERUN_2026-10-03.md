2. **Fetches.** About 90 WebFetch reads. **Important quality note carried over from the first pass:** WebFetch passes each page through a small summarizing model, so page text is paraphrased, not verbatim. I handled this three ways: (a) where a figure matters I fetched a second page or a different language or publisher; (b) I flagged every figure that rests on one summarized page as **single-source**; (c) for four documents I got the **actual PDF** and extracted the text locally with `pdftotext`, so those are verbatim primary text: the **MEXT JARE-66 action plan** (Japanese ministry document with the Shirase and Umitaka Maru schedules), the **NGA Pub. 151 "Distances Between Ports"** (11th ed., 2001), the **DOSM "Current Population Estimates, Malaysia, 2025"** table, and the **Geoscience Australia CEAMARC V3 voyage report** (Beaman 2009).

4. **Distances.** All great-circle distances in this file are **my own computations**, now done **two ways** (haversine, R = 6371.0088 km, and Vincenty on the WGS84 ellipsoid) from coordinates cited per site, so the first pass's single method is independently cross-checked (section 6). 5. **What is still thin:** the SCAR Composite Gazetteer's own web entry (placenames.aq is a JavaScript app and could not be read; the AADC gazetteer, which feeds it, was read instead); any peer-reviewed sea-ice climatology specific to 64° to 66°S, 95° to 105°E (only sector-wide statements found); Indonesian official polar documents (only university, press and one foreign-agency item found); Indonesia's actual treaty-accession status after November 2025 (one source asserts an announced intent; the Treaty Secretariat list does not show accession); any Southeast Asian ship route to Antarctica (none exists in any source found); Peninsular Malaysia and Sumatra populations from primary statistical tables (the Malaysian one is now primary; the Indonesian ones are secondary or snippet).

**Resolution of the first pass's conflict:** English Wikipedia's "Antarctic gateway cities" lists Japan under **Cape Town** (re-fetched: "the programs of Russia, Germany, Belgium, Norway, and Japan also reach Antarctica via Cape Town"; it does not mention Fremantle). The ministry plan shows why: Cape Town is real but is the **air route for the advance party**, not the ship route. For the Shirase the gateway is **Fremantle**. The first pass's "no Hobart call found in any Japanese ship page" is **corrected**: **Hobart is a regular Japanese call through the Umitaka Maru (Feb 2024, Feb 2025)**, and Hobart also hosted Japan's Umitaka Maru in the 2007 to 2008 CEAMARC survey (next item).

**Japanese vessel contact with Adélie Land (CONTEXT (b)):** one documented case. **CEAMARC** (Collaborative East Antarctic Marine Census), austral summer 2007 to 2008: Aurora Australis (Australia), **Umitaka Maru (Japan)** and L'Astrolabe (France) surveyed the waters off **Terre Adélie and George V Land**; Umitaka Maru's part was February 2008, "on and north of the continental shelf" (Geoscience Australia CEAMARC V3 report, verbatim PDF; https://www.antarctica.gov.au/magazine/issue-13-2007/feature/collaborative-east-antarctic-marine-census/ ; https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/90244). The Aurora Australis leg of that survey left **Hobart** (see Row 2). I found **no Japanese station, aircraft or JARE field presence in Adélie Land**, and no JARE use of Dumont d'Urville.

**Other Japanese Antarctic work on the East Antarctic coast (CONTEXT (b)):** Shirase carried out a three-week survey off the **Totten Glacier (Sabrina Coast, about 116°E)** in JARE-61 (Dec 2019, Feb/Mar 2020), JARE-66 (5 to 25 Mar 2025) and JARE-67 (early 2026); it works from **Fremantle**, not Hobart. Totten is about 680 km east of Bunger Hills and 250 km east of Casey (my computation).

### 2.2 Thread 2 (Row 1 part): sourced Hobart to Dumont d'Urville distance and transit times

**Distance (S):** "approximately 2,700 km" Hobart to Dumont d'Urville, with "4 to 5 rotations during the 120 days of the austral summer, November to February" (TAAF, https://taaf.fr/collectivites/lastrolabe/ ; also IPEV and several French maritime-press pages). Computed great circle 2,682 km (haversine) / 2,685 km (Vincenty), 1,448 / 1,450 nm. **The published figure and the computed one agree within 1 percent.**

**Transit times (S), AAD shipping schedules (Hobart to station, as scheduled/run):**

| Season | L'Astrolabe voyage | Left Hobart | Macquarie Island | At Dumont d'Urville | Back in Hobart |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023-24 (VR1) | https://www.antarctica.gov.au/antarctic-operations/travel-and-logistics/shipping-and-air-schedules/2324/ | 6 Dec | 9 Dec | 12 to 16 Dec (6 days out) | 22 Dec (6 days back) |
| 2024-25 (VR1) | .../2425/ | 30 Nov | 3 Dec | 5 to 9 Dec (5 days out) | 18 Dec (9 days back) |
| 2025-26 (VR1) | .../2526/ | 13 Dec | 15 Dec | 20 to 30 Dec (7 days out) | 8 Jan (9 days back) |
| 2026-27 (VR1, scheduled) | .../2627/ | 4 Dec | none listed | 11 to 19 Dec (7 days out) | 24 Dec (5 days back) |
| 2026-27 (VR2, scheduled) | .../2627/ | 30 Dec | none listed | 4 to 16 Jan (5 days out) | 21 Jan (5 days back) |

So the **Hobart to Dumont d'Urville passage is 5 to 7 days outbound and 5 to 9 days back**; the first rotation of a season is the slow one because the ship works through pack ice for about half the route (A2PasDuPole, https://a2pasdupole.fr/2021/11/29/lastrolabe-le-passeur/ : "5 days on average but can be longer depending on the pack"; Mer et Marine: first rotation "5 days normally, about 10 days or more"). **Caveat:** the AAD pages list only one or two L'Astrolabe voyages per season, while IPEV/TAAF state 4 to 5 rotations; the AAD list is evidently a subset (the voyages with AAD cargo or passengers). L'Astrolabe cruises at 14 knots (A2PasDuPole), ice class "IB 5" (about 70 cm ice), carries about 1,200 t and up to 60 persons (TAAF). 2,700 km at 14 knots is 4.3 days, consistent with the 5-day best case.

**Ice and weather delays (S):** 30 Dec 2024, L'Astrolabe arrived off the station but a katabatic storm with gusts above 70 knots stopped berthing at the Lion airstrip; three days of patrol, berthed 2 Jan (TAAF district blog digest, https://terreadelie-antarctique.blogspot.com/2025/12/encore-une-semaine-bien-chargee-en.html ; **SNIPPET ONLY**). First season of the new ship (2017): sea ice "stopped the ship 40 km from its destination", cargo went by two AS350 helicopters and over the sea ice (Mer et Marine, https://www.meretmarine.com/fr/l-astrolabe-tenu-a-distance-de-dumont-d-urville-par-la-banquise). Station access season **October to March** (IPEV, https://institut-polaire.fr/en/antarctica/the-dumont-durville-ddu-station/). Historical: 1976 to 77 about 35 days; failure in January 1949 (French Wikipedia, first pass).

**Personnel routing (CONTEXT (b)):** IPEV says personnel fly Paris to Hobart or Christchurch, then board L'Astrolabe, or connect by Twin Otter or Basler from McMurdo or Casey. AAD's Wilkins page says the aerodrome also facilitates access for France, Italy and China.

### 2.3 Thread 4 (Row 1 part): wind and sea-ice climatology at Dumont d'Urville (CORRECTS the first pass)

- **Mean wind:** IPEV: "35 km/h on average"; a French climatology digest gives "9.2 m/s (33 km/h) over the last 30 years, and gusts of 100 km/h on 118 days a year" (https://terreadelie.sblanc.com/pages/vent-catabatique.htm and a Terre Adélie blog; **SNIPPET ONLY** for the 118-day figure).
- **Peak gust (CORRECTION):** the first pass quoted French Wikipedia's "record gust 320 km/h on 16 June 1972." A Météo-France climatologist at the Terre Adélie district, writing on the official TAAF district blog (https://terreadelie-antarctique.blogspot.com/2026/08/le-vent-dumont-durville-mythe-et-realite.html), states that anemometers before 1981 "systematically overestimated extreme gusts", that Météo-France takes 1981 as the baseline, that the 1972 event "is no longer recognized as an official record", and that the **official record is 214.9 km/h on 23 May 1988** after recalculation to the 3-second gust standard adopted in June 2026 (an older figure of 244.8 km/h for the same day "should be forgotten"). A second search digest repeats the 214.9 km/h figure. **Treat 320 km/h as a legend, not a record.** IPEV still says katabatic gusts "close to 250 km/h" (promotional wording); AAD says katabatic winds "reached 327 km/h along coastal slopes" (continent-wide, first pass). **Two angles agree on the 214.9 figure; both are the same TAAF/Météo-France origin, so call it single-origin.**
- **Sea ice at the coast (SNIPPET ONLY, review-paper digest):** in the 90°E to 160°E sector landfast ice forms from April, peaks mid to late September, breaks up in November, and "most coastal zones are free of fast ice from December to March"; near Dumont d'Urville breakup ranges "from early November to early January" (Fraser et al. 2023 review, https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2022RG000770; not opened). This fits the Oct to Mar access season and the December to January AAD sailing dates above.

### 3.2 Thread 2 (Row 2 part): sourced sea transit and operator itineraries

- **Aurora Australis, CEAMARC V3 (S, verbatim Geoscience Australia report):** "sailed from Hobart at 19:00 hrs" **16 Dec 2007**; "arrived on the Antarctic shelf to commence CEAMARC surveying on **22 December 2007**"; the voyage narrative places the ship "on the outer George V shelf **50 nm north of Commonwealth Bay**" around 22 Dec; the ship returned to Hobart 27 Jan 2008 "after 43 days at sea". That is **6 days Hobart to the shelf off the bay in an open-water year (before B09B)**.
- **Mawson, 1911 to 12 (historical):** Aurora left Hobart 2 Dec 1911, Macquarie Island 13 to 24 Dec, entered Commonwealth Bay 8 Jan 1912 (36 days with the Macquarie stop; first pass).
- **L'Astrolabe, Oct to Dec 2006 (S):** left Hobart 20 Oct 2006 on an AAD heritage mission, back 24 Dec (AAD news above).
- **Aurora Expeditions, centenary voyage, Jan 2012 (S):** "sailed into the bay on the evening of 10 January 2012"; fast ice "too thick to make it all the way in", within **10 nm** of the hut; Zodiac cruising along B9B's "150 kilometer long" wall (https://www.aexpeditions.co.uk/blog/aurora-expeditions-reaches-commonwealth-bay-100-years-after-sir-douglas-maw).

## 4. ROW 3: RELUNG PANEN (Bunger Hills oasis)

### 4.1 Thread 5a: which coast (RESOLVED)

- **AADC (Australian Antarctic Gazetteer; also listed in the SCAR Composite Gazetteer) entry for Bunger Hills** (https://data.aad.gov.au/aadc/gaz/display_name.cfm?gaz_id=694): "Located at **the western end of Knox Coast in Wilkes Land**; area of several hundred square km of ice-free rock; first observed from David Island 1911 to 14, photographed in Operation Highjump 1946 to 47; named for Lt Cdr D. E. Bunger USN." Coordinates in the entry: **66°10′00.1″S 100°53′00″E**; the entry says Russia and the USA also recognize the name. This is a different point from Wikipedia's 66°17′S 100°47′E (the hill range spans about 65°58′ to 66°20′S, 100°20′ to 101°28′E), so use "about 66°10′ to 66°17′S, 100°45′ to 100°53′E" depending on which anchor point is wanted.
- **Boundaries:** Queen Mary Coast ends at **Cape Hordern 100°30′E**; Knox Coast runs 100°31′E to 109°16′E (Hatch Islands) and is part of Wilkes Land (Wikipedia, Knox Coast and Wilkes Land, re-confirmed in a search digest; the AADC Wilkes Land entry is gaz_id 133705). The AADC **Shackleton Ice Shelf** entry (https://data.aad.gov.au/aadc/gaz/display_name.cfm?gaz_id=731) places the shelf "off the coast of **Queen Mary Land**" at 65°59′46″S 100°30′14″E, "extending up to 170 km northwards" and eastward "from Junction Corner (94°45′E) to at least Denman Glacier (99°E), with some Soviet charts indicating 105°E".
- **Verdict:** the oasis is on the **Knox Coast of Wilkes Land (western end)**; Mindat and Polish Wikipedia describe it as at or near the Queen Mary/Knox boundary, and the shelf in front of it is a Queen Mary Land feature. The label "Queen Mary Land" for the oasis itself is **wrong by the Australian gazetteer**. The SCAR placenames.aq entry itself could not be read (JavaScript portal; fetch showed only the title).

### 4.2 Thread 5b: shipping access to the shelf front and the oasis (CORRECTS the first pass)

**A ship route to the ice-shelf front exists and was sailed in 2025 (S):**
- **RSV Nuyina, Denman Marine Voyage:** left Hobart **1 Mar 2025**, at the Shackleton Ice Shelf/Denman area within about a week, returned Hobart **2 May 2025** (AAD schedule 2024-25: "Hobart (18 Feb to 1 Mar) to Burnie (25 to 27 Feb) to Denman Glacier (5 Mar to 23 Apr) to Hobart (2 May)"; ACEAS/UTAS agree on 1 Mar and 2 May; one AAD news page summarized "9 May" and is treated as a misread). **Distance to Hobart logged by the voyage updates: 1,518 nm on 7 Mar, 2,060 nm on 14 Mar, 2,049 nm on 21 Mar** (https://www.antarctica.gov.au/news/2025/denman-marine-voyage-update-7-march-2025/); my computed Hobart to the Denman/Shackleton front is 2,035 to 2,160 nm, so the log sits right on the great circle. Conditions logged at the shelf: air −3.7 °C, sea −1.15 °C, wind 39.5 kn, fog and large icebergs; a digest says "a week's transit each way", "almost 4,000-kilometer journey", "had to break a lot of sea ice to get there", pancake ice, about 60 scientists (https://www.antarctica.gov.au/news/2025/denman-marine-voyage-returns-after-landmark-voyage-to-glacier/ ; https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-04-03/nuyina-scientists-collect-antarctic-sea-creatures-denman-glacier/105130020).
- **AAD 2026-27 schedule:** "Denman Glacier arrive 4 Feb, depart 7 Feb" on voyage V3 (Hobart 24 Jan; Burnie 25 to 26 Jan; then Kombi Mooring, Mawson 13 to 27 Feb) (first pass and re-fetch).
- **Ponant:** Hobart to Cape Town cruise passes the Shackleton Ice Shelf on day 13 (digest, above).
- **Russia:** Akademik Fedorov sails from **Cape Town** and services Molodezhnaya, Progress, Mirny and Vostok and does "geological and geophysical works in the **Oasis of Bunger** and oceanographic works in the Mawson Sea and Prydz Bay" (64th Russian Antarctic Expedition, https://www.waponline.it/the-64th-russian-antarctic-expedition/ ; the work is by the ship's helicopters and parties, no berth at the oasis is stated).
- **The oasis itself is not on the coast.** It is separated from the sea by the Shackleton Ice Shelf (Wikipedia: "a 50 km strip"); AAD's field-sites page (first pass) says Edgeworth David Base is "85 km inland from the Shackleton Ice Shelf" (the two figures use different reference points and I could not reconcile them). **No ship berths at Bunger Hills.**

**How Edgeworth David Base (Australian, summer) is supplied (S, CONTEXT (b)):** AAD helicopters (two BK117) are flown from Hobart by RAAF C-17A ("nearly five hours in the air" to Wilkins; HeliHub, https://www.helihub.com/2023/12/05/royal-australian-air-force-delivers-helicopters-for-remote-science-campaign/), equipment goes over snow the **70 km Wilkins to Casey**, then by Basler to Bunger Hills (a digest gives "240 nautical miles, a 1.5 hour flight" **SNIPPET ONLY**; my computed Casey to Dobrowolski is 237 nm, 439 km, consistent). "Edgeworth David base camp at Bunger Hills, about **450 km west of Casey**" (AAD, https://www.antarctica.gov.au/news/2024/bunger-hills-camp-set-up-and-ready-for-denman-terrestrial-campaign/), 27 scientists plus two pilots and two engineers in the 2023-24 season (HeliHub; Wikipedia says "up to 8", unreconciled).

**How Dobrowolski/Oazis was and is supplied (S):** the Soviet Oazis station (1956 to 1958) was supplied by air from Mirny (about 346 km west by my computation); Poland took it over in Jan 1959, last regular Polish team 1979, dormant for "lack of funds and the high costs of air transport", reopened Jan 2022 (Wikipedia). The 2022 Polish team "set sail on a Russian icebreaker from South Africa in early December", left **Mirny on New Year's Day**, and "the last 100 kilometers were covered by helicopter" (Notes from Poland, https://notesfrompoland.com/2022/01/10/polish-antarctic-research-station-emerges-from-43-year-hibernation/). So the **oasis was reached in 2022 by Cape Town, Russian ship to Mirny, then helicopter**, i.e., not from Hobart.

### 4.3 Thread 5c: sea-ice climatology on this coast

- **Shackleton polynya and fast ice (SNIPPET ONLY, Wikipedia-derived digest and a KBA fact sheet):** "the ice shelf blocks the westward transport of sea ice, resulting in heavy sea ice cover to the east and a large and persistent polynya to the west of the ice shelf" (the polynya produces about 123 Gt of sea ice a year); a "fast ice region to the northwest of the Denman tongue"; summer warming "to less than −1 °C near the Denman ice front". The shelf protrudes about 180 km in the west and about 80 km in the east (digest) against the AADC "up to 170 km northwards". Sector landfast calendar: see 2.3 (April to November, free December to March). **No site-specific climatology for 64° to 66°S, 95° to 105°E was found.** The practical datum is the Nuyina's March 2025 arrival (breaking sea ice, pancake ice, icebergs, fog).
- **Bunger Hills** is "notably ice free throughout the year" (Wikipedia and several digests); its lakes include Algae Lake (Lake Figurnoye), 25 km long, up to 137 m deep, with some lakes salty because they connect under the shelf (digest).

## 5. HOBART AS THE PRIMARY GATEWAY (cross-row verification; Thread 9)

**Population (S, ABS):** Greater Hobart 255,250 (30 Jun 2025); Tasmania 580.7 thousand (31 Mar 2026).

**Distances to the three sites (two methods; Pub. 151 coordinates for Hobart, 42°53′S 147°21′E, differs 1.9 km from Wikipedia's):** Dumont d'Urville **2,682 / 2,685 km (1,448 / 1,450 nm)**, bearing 187°; Cape Denison **2,698 / 2,701 km (1,457 / 1,458 nm)**, bearing 184.5°; Bunger Hills (Dobrowolski) **3,820 / 3,829 km (2,063 / 2,067 nm)**, bearing 211°. Published checks: Hobart to Casey 3,443 km (Wikipedia) vs 3,431 km computed; Hobart to Davis 4,838 km (Wikipedia) vs 4,828 km; Hobart to Mawson 5,475 km (Wikipedia) vs 5,464 km; Hobart to Dumont d'Urville "approximately 2,700 km" (IPEV/TAAF, S).

**Role (S):** Hobart is one of the five Antarctic gateway cities and "services the national Antarctic programs of Australia, France and China" (Wikipedia; ABC 26 Nov 2024 adds the US Coast Guard's Polar Star and nine ice-class vessels approved to pass under the Tasman Bridge). The Tasmanian Antarctic and Southern Ocean sector employs about 1,000 people and contributes more than A$183 million a year (ABC and Tasmanian/Serco releases; secondary). **Nuyina** is Hobart-based; the federal government committed **A$188 million over four years** (Oct 2024) for a new home-port wharf and Tasmania gave **priority access to Macquarie Wharf 6 for 30 years** (ABC; gCaptain; PM release). **Operational nuance (S, ABC):** since 2023 Nuyina has refueled at **Burnie** (north-west Tasmania) because the Harbor Master refused transit under the Tasman Bridge to Hobart's fuel depot; every AAD voyage 2023-24 to 2026-27 lists a Burnie stop after Hobart; ABC reported in May 2026 that the refueling issue was unresolved.

**Voyages by season (S, AAD schedules):** 2023-24 Nuyina V1 to V4 plus commissioning voyages, L'Astrolabe VR1; 2024-25 Nuyina V1 to V4 (V3 = Denman Marine Voyage) plus trials, L'Astrolabe VR1; 2025-26 Nuyina V1 to V4 plus trials, L'Astrolabe VR1; 2026-27 (scheduled) V1 to V4 plus trials, L'Astrolabe VR1 and VR2. Hobart to Casey by Nuyina: 8 days outbound in each of the last three first voyages (8 to 16 Oct 2024; 26 Sep to 4 Oct 2025, with a Burnie stop; 19 to 27 Oct 2026 scheduled); Hobart to Mawson 15 days (10 to 25 Feb 2026, with a Burnie stop; 24 Jan to 13 Feb 2027 scheduled, with Denman and Kombi stops); 12 days in 1999 to 2000 by Aurora Australis (digest).

**Air bridge (S):** Airbus A319 (Skytraders) and RAAF C-17A flights Hobart to **Wilkins Aerodrome** (66°41′27″S 111°31′35″E, about **70 km southeast of Casey**), October to March; 11 A319 and 6 C-17 services in 2025-26, 5 A319 and 5 C-17 services listed for 2026-27; Hobart departures at 07:00, returns 13:30; about **4 to 4.5 hours** by A319 (ABC 2018: 4.5 hours; Wikipedia: four hours) and "nearly five hours" by C-17 (HeliHub); "around half of the 500 people Australia takes south each season" use the flights; Wilkins also serves Italian, French and Chinese programs (AAD, https://www.antarctica.gov.au/antarctic-operations/travel-and-logistics/aviation/intercontinental-operations/wilkins-aerodrome/). **The air bridge serves Casey and, by Basler or helicopter, Bunger Hills (Row 3); it does not reach Cape Denison (1,347 km) or Dumont d'Urville** except through McMurdo or Casey connections that IPEV mentions for French personnel.

**Which row Hobart serves best on the evidence:** Row 1 (an everyday French sea route, 5 to 7 days) and Row 2 (a sea route that has worked in some summers and failed in others) are **sea** gateways; Row 3 is a **sea route to the shelf front plus an air bridge from Hobart to Casey** to the oasis, and the one recorded reach of the oasis by a non-Australian team (Poland, 2022) came from Cape Town via Mirny.

## 7. CHANGES FROM THE FIRST PASS

1. **Japan's gateway resolved.** Shirase main body: **Fremantle** (MEXT JARE-66 plan, NIPR JARE-67 blog); advance party: **Cape Town** by DROMLAN air route; **Umitaka Maru: Hobart on return, every season recently** (Feb 2024, Feb 2025). The first pass's "no Hobart call found" is wrong; the English Wikipedia Cape Town listing is explained, not contradicted.
2. **Japan and Adélie Land:** the first pass found no contact; one exists: the Umitaka Maru in the 2007 to 08 CEAMARC survey off Terre Adélie and George V Land. No Japanese station presence found.
3. **Hobart to Dumont d'Urville** now has a sourced distance (about 2,700 km, IPEV/TAAF) and a multi-season transit table (5 to 7 days out, 5 to 9 back).
4. **Hobart to Cape Denison published-distance conflict sorted:** AAD 2006 2,600 km and NMA 2,670 km match the computed 2,700 km; "4,000 km south of mainland Australia" (AAD history site) is not geometrically usable; 3,000 km (Wikipedia) is an overstatement.
5. **Sea transit Hobart to Commonwealth Bay sourced:** 6 days to the outer shelf 50 nm off the bay (Aurora Australis, Dec 2007); operator itinerary 24 days Hobart to Dunedin with the bay on days 14 to 15.
6. **Commonwealth Bay access is intermittent, and a voyage failed in December 2025 (22 nm out).** The first pass said only that B09B had blocked L'Astrolabe in 2011 to 13.
7. **Wind figures corrected.** Cape Denison mean **about 70 to 75 km/h** (AAD 71 km/h average daily maximum; AAE 19.4 m/s; 1995 mean 20.9 m/s), not Wikipedia's 80 km/h. **Dumont d'Urville's "320 km/h record" is not official**: the Météo-France record is **214.9 km/h** (23 May 1988, 3-second standard); mean about 33 to 35 km/h.
8. **Bunger Hills coast resolved:** AADC gazetteer says **western end of the Knox Coast in Wilkes Land**; "Queen Mary Land" is not correct for the oasis (it is correct for the Shackleton Ice Shelf in front of it).
9. **First pass said "no ship route to Bunger Hills was found."** **Corrected:** a ship route to the **Shackleton Ice Shelf front** was sailed by Nuyina in March 2025 (about a week from Hobart; 2,060 nm logged), and Hobart-embarking cruise ships pass the shelf; the **oasis itself** (about 50 to 85 km inland of the shelf) is reached by air (Hobart to Wilkins to Casey to Bunger by Basler), by helicopter from Mirny (Russia, Poland 2022), never by berthing.
10. **Indonesia and Malaysia polar activity documented** (the first pass found "nothing"): Malaysia has a funded program (YPASM 2012, NARC 2022; SCAR full member 2008; Treaty party 2011) working with BAS, INACH, KOPRI, NZ Scott Base, none in East Antarctica, always by air to a gateway city; Indonesia has had three small participations (AAD 2002, JARE-58 2016 to 17, Russia 2024) and is **not a Treaty party or SCAR member**; both of the East Antarctic participations on a national program's ship were routed through an Australian gateway: the AAD Aurora Australis in 2002 (to Davis, returning to Hobart) and the JARE Shirase boarded at Fremantle in 2016 to 17; an unconfirmed Ukrainian-agency item says intent to accede was declared in 2025.
11. **Real sea distances from Sumatra and Malaysia now have a primary basis** (NGA Pub. 151 legs); the first pass's own estimates (8,600 km Penang, 8,100 km Port Klang) are reproduced; digest-level figures that looked authoritative (Fremantle-Hobart 1,494 nm, Port Klang-Fremantle 5,248 nm) were traced to the table and are **wrong attributions**.
12. **Malaysian population now primary (DOSM 2025p; Peninsular states about 27.8 million).** Australian figures re-verified at the ABS pages. Sumatra figures still secondary.
13. **Great-circle distances independently confirmed** (Vincenty; maximum 0.3 percent difference; published checks within 0.3 percent).
