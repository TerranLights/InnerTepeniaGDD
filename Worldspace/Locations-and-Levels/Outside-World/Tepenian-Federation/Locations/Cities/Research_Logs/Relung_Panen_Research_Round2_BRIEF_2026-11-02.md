# Relung Panen research, round 2: brief for five research units (2026-11-02)

**Why this exists.** The developer approved eight proposals for Relung Panen's open items (`Relung_Panen_Open_Items_Proposals_For_Approval_2026-11-02.md`, this folder) and asked for **real research wherever the current data is thin**. Round 1 (2026-10-25 to 2026-10-29) left the gaps listed under each unit below. Each unit fills those gaps with real-world data. **Research is the input-data layer of the worldbuilding: a recalled fact is a guess in a confident tone, and a wrong fact is worse than a missing one. Research fully. A source searched once is not exhausted. Query every point from more than one angle, and go back to points already logged as thin.**

## What Relung Panen is (so you can judge relevance)
A fictional city of about one million people (humans and robots) on the real **Bunger Hills oasis** (Knox Coast, Wilkes Land, East Antarctica, about 66°17'S 100°47'E): 450 km² of ice-free rock and lakes, sealed from the sea by the Shackleton Ice Shelf (64 to 145 km). It is the country's second agricultural region (stacked indoor hydroponics, about 105,000 food workers). Its only road is a spur east to the real Casey station (about 440 to 470 km; corrected 2026-11-02, the brief first said 350 to 400). The setting is the year 2564 to 2812 of a fictional Antarctic nation, so **real-world technology of the 2020s is the reference, with modest extrapolation; do not assume technology beyond what exists or is in demonstration now.** The proposals being tested: cargo airport on Transcription Gulf lake ice; passenger airport on the Apfel Glacier blue-ice strip; a winter-only, air-only intake airport on Drygalski Island (about 92 km north of Mirny); a seasonal pair-of-ships sea gateway; no conventional port (goods go by road to Casey); a grid power line along the road; a cold-chain workforce.

## Rules for every unit
1. **Facts only. No recommendation, no design, no ranking.** The developer decides. Label every claim: **SOURCED** (you read it at the source page or document in this session), **DERIVED** (your arithmetic or analysis on sourced figures; show the working), **UNVERIFIED** (seen only in a search snippet, recalled, or inferred), **NOT FOUND** (looked for, with the queries listed, and not found). Quote key figures with the source's own wording where short.
2. **Log every search**: the exact query string, the tool, the result (hit, partial, miss), and for a dead end whether it died at the query or at the sources (paywall, 403, DNS failure, refusal). Put the log at the end of your file. **Do not work around a refusal or a block**; log it and move on. If the search tool itself refuses, write a STOP note naming where you stopped.
3. **Budget:** about 60 to 80 web calls per unit. Stop earlier if the questions are answered; do not pad. Prefer primary sources (agency reports, papers, operator documents, datasets) over summaries.
4. **Write one file only**, at the path named in your unit, in this folder. Do not edit, move or delete any other file. Do not write to the scratch of another unit. Put scripts and downloads in your own scratch folder, not in the repo.
5. **American English** in everything you write. **No slang or nicknames collection**; if you meet slang, ignore it.
6. **Do not use or read the graph index (`graphify-out/`) or any `Local_Cultures`, `Local_Robot_Culture`, `City_Megasheets` or other city-culture file.** You do not need them. Read only the existing research files named in your unit.
7. **Structure of your file:** a title and one-paragraph scope; a PROGRESS checklist (tick items as you finish); one section per question with findings; a **FINAL** section ("what the sources support / do not support / NOT FOUND"); then the search log. Tick nothing you did not finish. Date and time-stamp your start and end.
8. When done, reply with a **short** summary (under 250 words): the file path, which questions were answered, which are still NOT FOUND, and anything that surprised you.

## Unit R1 · Power (the biggest gap) → `Relung_Panen_R1_Power_Research_2026-11-02.md`
Read first: `Relung_Panen_Soil_Research_2026-10-25_F_Growing_Without_Soil_Precedents.md`, `Relung_Panen_Soil_Research_2026-10-25_E_Oasis_Ground_Water_and_Light.md` and `Relung_Panen_Oasis_F_Ice_Front_and_Haul_2026-10-29.md` (this folder).
Questions:
1. **Electricity demand of stacked indoor hydroponics / vertical farming**: kWh per kg of produce (leafy greens, tomatoes, staples such as potatoes/wheat/rice if anyone grows them indoors), kWh per m² of growing area per year, lighting efficacy (µmol/J) of current LEDs, and how demand splits (lighting, HVAC and dehumidification, pumping). Best published numbers and their range; the effect of cold outdoor air on cooling versus heating load.
2. **Scale**: the electric power needed to feed N people entirely from indoor hydroponics. Use published per-person food mass and energy (calories, kg) and the kWh-per-kg numbers. **Compute** for 100,000, 750,000 and 1,000,000 people (and show the result in average megawatts). Check against real vertical-farm sizes (the largest operating farms and their megawatts). Is feeding hundreds of thousands people this way real anywhere or only a calculation? Say what the sources say.
3. **Antarctic and polar indoor-growing precedents**: EDEN-ISS (Neumayer III): power, yield, hours; McMurdo and South Pole hydroponics; Chinese, Russian, Indian and Australian station greenhouses; Svalbard and Greenland greenhouses. Power per kg where stated.
4. **Transmission**: long overhead or buried high-voltage lines in polar, permafrost or ice-sheet conditions (Siberia, Alaska, Canada, Greenland, Svalbard, Iceland, Norway's Arctic). Longest and most relevant; voltage; ice loading; blizzard and icing failure rates; HVDC for 300 to 400 km; cable-laying on ice sheets. Any power line or cable longer than a few kilometers on Antarctic ice. Typical cost per km.
5. **Local resources at the Bunger Hills**: measured wind and solar (automatic weather station records, the Soviet Oasis station 1956–58, Australian and Russian data, reanalysis), including seasonal solar by month at 66°S, snow/ice albedo effects, and any wind-turbine experience at Antarctic stations (Casey, Mawson, Mario Zucchelli, Princess Elisabeth, Concordia, Davis; Ross Island wind farm). Capacity factors where measured.
6. **Alternatives, as precedents only**: small reactors at remote polar sites (PM-3A at McMurdo; Arctic and Siberian floating and land units; current microreactor/SMR demonstrations and their status), geothermal absence at the site, and fuel logistics for diesel generation in East Antarctica (liters per person per year at stations). No ranking.
NOT FOUND from round 1 to retry: any energy study for the oasis; wind-resource number for the oasis.

## Unit R2 · Drygalski Island and Mill Island (the winter-intake candidates) → `Relung_Panen_R2_Drygalski_and_Mill_Island_Research_2026-11-02.md`

Questions for **Drygalski Island (about 65°45'S 92°30'E)**, then the same for **Mill Island (65°30'S 100°40'E)**:
1. **Ice cap facts**: thickness (Bedmap, radar, ice cores), surface elevation, flow speed and direction (ITS_LIVE, MEaSUREs velocity, published), crevasse fields (imagery, published surveys), snow accumulation, melt and surface water, wind regime, fog/visibility. **Mill Island has ice cores and camps (2008–2010): find the papers and their field data.** For Drygalski, find the 1960 Soviet "Mir" station account and any later visits.
2. **Coast and sea access**: ice-edge height and character, fast-ice presence and thickness, depth, landing records by ship, helicopter or aircraft, iceberg grounding, sea-ice season at each island.
3. **Aircraft use**: any landing on either island (aircraft, helicopter), any survey of a possible runway there, and any report of flights from Hobart/Perth/Davis/Mirny reaching them.
4. **Comparable island ice-cap runways or bases**: ice-cap airstrips on islands or ice rises elsewhere (Antarctic ice rises, Svalbard, Greenland, Franz Josef Land, Severnaya Zemlya) with surface type, length, and how they were prepared.
5. **Distances**: great-circle distances from each island to Mirny, Davis and Casey; to Hobart and Perth.
NOT FOUND from round 1 to retry: reliable ice speed on both domes; Drygalski crevasses and accumulation; any ship landing at Drygalski/Bowman/Mill/West Ice Shelf islands.

Questions:
1. **Transcription Gulf (Zaliv Transkriptsii; Russian "залив Трёшникова" / "Transkriptsii")**: the 2019 Russian survey (ice thickness 2.95–3.2 m, cryoconite zones): find the original paper or report; the length of runway free of cryoconite pits; summer surface and meltwater behavior; how the 1963 Il-18V landings were done (strip preparation, load, dates, crew accounts); every later aircraft use (Il-14, Twin Otter, CASA 212, others); epishelf-lake science (freshwater layer, depth, tidal connection under the shelf, ice thickness history).

4. **Heavy aircraft on lake or blue ice**: published bearing-capacity rules (Gold formula, Canadian ice-road standards, US Army CRREL guidance), and real heavy-wheeled landings on Antarctic blue ice or lake ice (Novolazarevskaya, Union Glacier, Wilkins, Phoenix, Pegasus, Lake Vida/Lake Bonney area, Squaw Lake) with aircraft types, weights and surface conditions. What limits wheeled freighters (767, A330F, 777F) on ice, from any source.
5. **Fuel at the oasis**: how fuel has been stored or flown in for oasis operations (Australian and Russian seasons); typical fuel store sizes at comparable stations.
NOT FOUND from round 1 to retry: pit-free length of the gulf; Apfel ablation and thickness; Figurnoye winter thickness; Gibson and Andersen 2002 (try alternative hosts); the 1963 Il-18 details.

## Unit R5 · The sea gateway, bulk haul to Casey, and the cold chain → `Relung_Panen_R5_Sea_Gateway_and_Cold_Chain_Research_2026-11-02.md`

Questions:
1. **Sea gateway**: documented cases of two ships working as a pair in polar sea ice (icebreaker plus support ship; Australian, Russian, Chinese, Indian and Japanese resupply operations); the *Nuyina* 2024–2025 operations at the Bunger Hills and Denman: helicopter sortie counts, passengers carried, weather days; ship-to-shore passenger rates by helicopter (people per day); immigration/customs/biosecurity clearance done at sea or at an ice edge anywhere; polar-code or IMO text on ship pairing.
