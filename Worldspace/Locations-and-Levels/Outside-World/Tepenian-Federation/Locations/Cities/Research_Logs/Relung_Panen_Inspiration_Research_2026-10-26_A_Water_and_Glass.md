## 1. Dutch polders and their water boards (Noordoostpolder / Emmeloord; Flevoland / Lelystad)

*All sources accessed 2026-10-26.*

### A. Snapshot

- **Water boards (waterschappen):** regional bodies "solely charged with the management of surface water," separate from provinces and municipalities. **21** in 2018; **c. 3,500** in 1850; oldest coordinating body c. **1122** near Wijk bij Duurstede; in **1255** Count William II appointed the first *Hoogheemraadschap*. Elected every four years (same day as provincial elections), any registered resident 18+ votes; they levy their own taxes; they run dikes, dunes, locks, water levels, drainage, sewage treatment; they do **not** supply drinking water. — SOURCED, Wikipedia "Water board (Netherlands)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_board_(Netherlands)
- **The local board, Waterschap Zuiderzeeland** (HQ Lelystad, Postbus 229, 8200 AE): manages "all dikes, watercourses, pumping stations and water purifications" in Almere, De Fryske Marren, Dronten, Lelystad, Noordoostpolder, Steenwijkerland, Urk and Zeewolde. — SOURCED, https://organisaties.overheid.nl/samenwerkingen/45778/Zuiderzeeland and https://www.zuiderzeeland.nl/over-ons
  - "256 km of dikes, 7 main pumping stations (Vissering at Urk, Buma, Smeenge in the Noordoostpolder; Wortman at Lelystad, De Blocq van Kuffeler at Almere, Lovink, Colijn), 425 employees" — **UNVERIFIED** (appeared only in a search-engine summary; the official pages I opened did not state these numbers; the board's 2022–2027 water management program PDF returned 404).
- **IJsselmeer polders overall (CBS, 12 June 2018):** 1,600 km² land (nearly 5% of the Netherlands); 106,000 ha farmland (nearly 6% of national farmland). 2017 population: Zuidelijke Flevopolder 223,400; Oostelijke Flevopolder 117,700; Noordoostpolder incl. Urk 66,800; Wieringermeer 12,500. Average farm size 2017: Noordoostpolder 43.7 ha, Oostelijke Flevopolder 60.4 ha, Zuidelijke Flevopolder 66.5 ha (national 31.9 ha). Flevoland land use 2017: 70% arable, 17% grass, 9% horticulture, 5% green fodder; main crops by harvested area potatoes 34.1%, wheat 22.1%, sugar beet 18%, seed onions 16.6%. Under-20s 26% (NL 22%), 65+ 14% (NL 19%) in 2018. — SOURCED, CBS "Nieuw land: wat de IJsselmeerpolders Nederland brachten", https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/achtergrond/2018/24/nieuw-land-wat-de-ijsselmeerpolders-nederland-brachten
- **Noordoostpolder:** drained, final drainage 9 Sept 1942, "1,500 million m³ of water" pumped out; first dike breach closed near Urk 3 Oct 1939. — SOURCED, https://visitnoordoostpolder.nl/en/discover/our-history. Area "ruim 460 km²" (just over 460 km²). — SOURCED, Boeve & Hospers, *Geografie*, April 2010, "Het DNA van de Noordoostpolder", https://geografie.nl/sites/default/files/paragraph/attachment/file/Boeve%20%26%20Hospers%20-%20Het%20DNA%20van%20de%20Noordoostpolder.pdf. 48,000 ha designed for arable farming; c. 1,500 identical prefabricated concrete farms of 24 ha allocated to selected nuclear families. — SOURCED, Eggers & Driessen, "From Polder Colony to Greenhouse Plantation", *Cultural Anthropology* Fieldsights, 24 Jan 2023, https://www.culanth.org/fieldsights/from-polder-colony-to-greenhouse-plantation-dwelling-in-the-noordoostpolder-plantationocene. Municipality population **51,930** (data current 9 July 2026). — SOURCED via search result from https://organisaties.overheid.nl (gemeente page) — *figure seen in search result text; treat as SOURCED-weak*.
- **Emmeloord:** founded 1943 at the crossing of the three main drainage canals (Lemstervaart, Urkervaart, Zwolsevaart); "the first and the only major town of the polder"; **26,055** residents (1 Jan 2019); no railway station, buses to nearby stations; A6/N50 junction. Ten villages planned in a ring, founded 1946–1956: Marknesse, Ens, Kraggenburg, Luttelgeest, Bant, Rutten, Creil, Nagele, Espel, Tollebeek. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmeloord
- **Lelystad:** municipality founded 1 Jan 1967; **79,811** residents (Jan 2021); 765.45 km² of which 230.32 km² land, 535.13 km² water; on former Zuiderzee seabed; Eastern Flevoland polder drained June 1957; named for Cornelis Lely (Afsluitdijk engineer). Rail: Flevolijn south, Hanzelijn north; A6 motorway. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelystad. First residents arrived September 1967; before that only engineers, laborers and superintendents lived on the construction island; in 1963 Minister Korthals told the new director of the Rijksdienst voor de IJsselmeerpolders, Will Otto, to get Lelystad off the ground quickly. — SOURCED-weak (search-result summary citing the Prometheus book extract 9789044641301 and Wikipedia; not read in full). Municipal growth goal: "at least 100,000 inhabitants." — SOURCED, Gemeente Lelystad, *Strategische visie Evenementen Lelystad 2024–2028* (PDF dated 24 Oct 2024), https://www.gemeentelelystad.nl/Documenten/Strategische%20visie%20evenementen%20Lelystad%202024.pdf
- **Flevoland province:** c. 445,000 residents (Jan 2023), 2,412 km², the 12th and newest province (1986); six municipalities (Almere, Dronten, Lelystad capital, Noordoostpolder, Urk, Zeewolde); 2015: 55.2% non-religious, 15.5% Protestant Church, 12.2% Catholic; Urk 97.7% religious. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flevoland

### B. What else exists there

- **Bulbs:** tulips first planted in the polder in the early 1960s; "some 2,000 hectares of tulips are planted every year," producing "almost 1 billion saleable bulbs"; lilies, gladioli and grape hyacinths also grown; "the third largest bulb-growing area in the Netherlands in terms of surface area." — SOURCED, Tulpenfestival Noordoostpolder, https://tulpenfestival.nl/en/?p=951
- **Lelystad employers/institutions:** Wageningen Bioveterinary Research (c. 250 staff; animal and public health, vaccines, diagnostics; BSL-3/BSL-4 facilities), Houtribweg 39 — SOURCED, https://wur.nl/en/location/organisation-wbvr-lelystad.htm (search-result text; page not opened in full — SOURCED-weak). Rijkswaterstaat Traffic and Water Management office, Zuiderwagenplein 2 — SOURCED-weak (search-result text, https://www.chr-khr.org/en/institute/rijkswaterstaat). Provincie Flevoland seat, Visarenddreef 1, Lelystad — SOURCED (factsheet colophon above). Waterschap Zuiderzeeland HQ — SOURCED (above). Batavia Stad Fashion Outlet; Lelystad Airport area named as a future festival site; "four harbors" popular with water-sports people. — SOURCED, Lelystad events vision 2024.
- **Governance:** Flevoland Provincial Council 41 seats; BBB (Farmer–Citizen Movement) largest bloc with 10 seats after March 2023. — SOURCED, Wikipedia Flevoland.
- **Who lives and works there — settlers:** selection by age, marital status, finances, education; officials with assessment forms visited applicants; Zeeland farmers who lost farms in the 1953 flood were admitted without selection. — SOURCED, visitnoordoostpolder.nl history. Selection also checked "farming knowledge, financial situation, housewife hygiene level, child discipline" and "pioneering spirit." — SOURCED, Eggers & Driessen 2023. Criteria also included religion, health, professional skills and literacy; planners kept the four main Dutch "pillars" (Catholics, Protestants, Reformed, liberal-socialists) proportionally represented in the polder and its villages. — SOURCED-weak (search-result summary of Dutch planning documents and a Groningen student thesis, https://frw.studenttheses.ub.rug.nl/1899/1/Plaatsidentiteiten_Noordoostpolder.pdf; not opened). **1951: 3,018 applicants for 135 farms.** Rejection was experienced as personal ("You weren't good enough as a person"); rejected applicants reapplied year after year. — SOURCED, NOS, 11 Jan 2013, "Polderselectie nog altijd trauma", https://nos.nl/artikel/460520-polderselectie-nog-altijd-trauma (on Eva Vriend's book *Het Nieuwe Land*). Settlers came from Groningen's Veenkoloniën, Brabant, Utrecht, Friesland, Zeeland. — SOURCED-weak (Smithsonian, search-result text).
- **WWII:** the polder became known as the "Dutch Paradise for People in Hiding," as men came to work on the reclamation partly to escape forced labor in Germany. — SOURCED, visitnoordoostpolder.nl history.
- **Who works there now — migrant labor:** Eastern European workers (Poland, Bulgaria, Russia named) housed in "migrant worker parks" of prefabricated cabins and caravans; one park's initial capacity 300 workers in 150 cabins; they work in warehouses, logistics and large-scale greenhouse horticulture (Luttelgeest); some stay 11 months a year ("full-time temporarity"); limited contact with locals. — SOURCED, Eggers & Driessen 2023 (the authors' "Plantationocene" framing is their interpretation). Temporary agencies rent vacant rural buildings to house mainly East European seasonal workers; the municipality inventoried campsites and other locations; individual farm permits (e.g., max. 20 migrants in a renovated farm dwelling). — SOURCED-weak (search-result summaries of Noordoostpolder planning documents and the municipality's Nov 2012 policy, https://flexwonen.nl/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/beleid-arbeidsmigranten-Noordoostpolder-november-2012.pdf; not opened).
- **How goods arrive and leave:** villages were planned with a *loswal* (unloading quay) for water transport; in 2010 most firms on the adjoining business park did not use the quay. — SOURCED, Boeve & Hospers 2010. Emmeloord has no railway. — SOURCED, Wikipedia Emmeloord.
- **Daily life pattern (planned):** villages spaced c. 8 km apart (about double old-land spacing) on the assumption people would cycle from A to B; rapid car adoption made this obsolete; plan drew on Christaller's 1933 central-place model; ten villages instead of five because farm workers were thought better housed in villages than scattered; deep back gardens meant for growing one's own vegetables, by 2010 often neglected or wanted for parking. — SOURCED, Boeve & Hospers 2010.
- **Lelystad social life (municipality's own assessment, 2024):** residents, organizers and businesses "do not at all yet see Lelystad as a lively events city"; missing: youth music events and a festival, markets, theater festivals, food festivals, sports events, multicultural events, and the kermis (funfair); no large equipped events ground; volunteer shortage since COVID; most events serve residents. Events felt to fit best: Jordaan in de Polder, Nationale Oldtimerdag, LelySTART, Hiswa te Water — "although not unique to Lelystad and/or matching its identity." Coordinating body: Stichting Evenementen Coördinatie Lelystad (ECL). — SOURCED, Lelystad events vision 2024 (text extracted and read, pp. 3, 9).

### D. What exists because of the water system / reclamation

- The **town site** itself: Emmeloord is where the three main drainage canals meet; the Poldertoren was the water tower for the new land and became its landmark and carillon (each village gave a bell). — SOURCED (Wikipedia Emmeloord; Poldertoren).
- **Pumping stations as monuments and art** (Wortman reliefs and mural; municipal monument status). — SOURCED.
- **A museum and a national depot built on the seabed:** draining the Zuiderzee exposed shipwrecks, hence the National Ship Archaeological Depot (c. 40,000 finds) at Batavialand; Schokland's former island and its UNESCO status exist because the sea was pumped away around it. — SOURCED.
- **Tree belts** around every village were windbreaks on the bare former seabed and now define the villages as "green oases." — SOURCED, Boeve & Hospers 2010.
- **A new nature reserve** (Oostvaardersplassen) formed by accident on the drained land. — SOURCED.
- **A wholly planned society:** the state chose who could farm (3,018 applicants for 135 farms in 1951), placed churches at village edges to balance denominations, spaced villages by bicycle distance, and the selection is still remembered as a trauma. — SOURCED.
- **Large farms** (43.7–66.5 ha averages vs 31.9 national) on uniform heavy clay; tulips since the 1960s on soil "particularly suitable." — SOURCED (CBS 2018; Tulpenfestival).
- **The water board itself** as a separate elected tier with its own tax — SOURCED.

### E. Structural features bearing on industry, economy, arts and work culture

- A state-built landscape with fixed farm plots, central town, ring of villages and a single water authority; agriculture is still the third-to-fifth employer (3,070 jobs in Noordoostpolder) but retail, staffing/business services, health and industry are larger. — SOURCED (factsheet).
- Seasonal and near-permanent East European labor housed in purpose-built cabin parks, outside village life. — SOURCED (Eggers & Driessen).
- Young demographics (under-20s 26% in the Flevopolders; Urk youngest municipality). — SOURCED.
- In Lelystad, a young planned city's own government reports a thin, resident-only events scene, no large festival ground, and a volunteer shortage; meanwhile one of the country's largest festivals (Lowlands) sits on polder land nearby because of available open ground and access. — SOURCED for the facts; the "because" is UNVERIFIED (no source stated the reason for the site choice).
- Religion: largely secular province, with a strongly religious exception (Urk) and a history of shared church buildings among small Reformed denominations in new towns. — SOURCED.

## 2. Desert oasis irrigation systems: Turpan (karez, Xinjiang) and Al-Ahsa (Saudi Arabia)

*All sources accessed 2026-10-26. Two sub-places; each gets its own A–F.*

> **Source caution for Turpan:** much current Turpan material comes from Chinese state media or state-sponsored content (e.g., a Chinese-Embassy branded article run in Australian regional papers). Those items are marked as such. Independent reporting on Xinjiang (detention, labor transfer) is also listed, with attribution. Neither side was used to fill the other's gaps.

### 2a. TURPAN

#### A. Snapshot

- Prefecture-level city in eastern Xinjiang, **69,759 km²**, population **693,988 (2020)**; 2018 composition **77% Uyghur, 16.8% Han, 5.9% Hui**; Islam dominant; precipitation **15.7 mm/yr**; summer regularly above 40 °C, records near 50 °C; "China's largest raisin producing area"; former capital of the Gaochang Kingdom, Uyghur Buddhist center, converted to Islam in the 15th century. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turpan
- **Turpan Depression:** fault-bounded trough c. 150 km SE of Ürümqi; **Aydingkol, 154 m below sea level, lowest point in China** (now a dried, muddy, salty lake). — SOURCED-weak (Wikipedia Turpan Depression / Aydingkol, search-result text).
- **Karez:** underground channels carrying groundwater (Tianshan snowmelt recharge) by gravity, with vertical shafts **every 20–70 m**, shafts up to **90 m** deep upstream, dozens to 100+ shafts per karez; precipitation 16 mm vs evaporation 3,000 mm. As of Sept 2024: **"over 1,200" karez exist, "nearly 190" still carry water**, total underground length **over 3,400 km** (historically 5,000 km), annual flow **114 million m³**, irrigating **nearly 100,000 mu** (≈6,700 ha; 15 mu = 1 ha). Inscribed **3 Sept 2024 on the World Heritage Irrigation Structures list (11th batch)**; protection work since 2009. — SOURCED, Science and Technology Daily (state), 3 Sept 2024, https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2024-09/03/content_223899.html
- **Decline (academic):** 1949 → 2023 karez count **1,084 → 169** (≈16 lost per year); total flow **16.11 → 3.6 m³/s**; strongest correlation with electric pumping wells (r = −0.890); a 2006 inflection possibly from protective regulation. — SOURCED, Yu Xiaoying, Xu Weiwei, Zhou Jinlong et al., *Bulletin of Geological Science and Technology* 2025, https://dzkjtb.cug.edu.cn/en/article/doi/10.19509/j.cnki.dzkq.tb20240094. Groundwater falling 1.5–2 m/yr — UNVERIFIED (search summary only). Xinjiang **Karez Protection Regulation** issued 2006 and under revision (2026 draft splits karez into watered/recoverable, dry, and associated cultural remains). — SOURCED-weak (search-result text pointing to slt.xinjiang.gov.cn draft; not opened).
  - Note conflicting older figure: Wikipedia "Qanat" says "nearly 1,000 karez systems… about 5,000 km" (undated). Not used as current.

#### B. What else exists there

- **Grapes:** citywide **630,000 mu (≈42,000 ha)** of grapes; **507,100 mu** converted to high-efficiency water-saving irrigation by 2024; grapes use **85% of total water** (search-summary claim, UNVERIFIED); 2024 water savings 150 million m³; groundwater rose 32 cm in H2 2024 vs 2023 (state claim). — SOURCED (state media), CCTV dialect channel via yzwb.net, 7 Sept 2025, https://www.yzwb.net/news/yw/202509/t20250907_260649.html. Output "over 1.7 million t" in 2025, Shanshan County 336,500 mu / 840,000 t — UNVERIFIED (search summary only).
- **Raisins:** Turpan ≈ **75%** of China's raisin output; China's 2009/10 forecast 155,000 t, the world's largest. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chunche
- **Village economy example — Ximen Village, Xincheng:** 2,700 mu arable, 1,900+ mu grapes; new varieties (Sapphire, Polia, Deep Red Seedless, Black Beauty) replacing Seedless White; Seedless White as low as 2 yuan/kg vs Sapphire 25 yuan/kg; a dried-fruit processor handling 8,000 t/yr pays the village collective 700,000 yuan rent and employs 80+ villagers; 50+ family guesthouses; 125,000 tourist visits in 2024. — SOURCED (state media), Xinhua, 12 May 2025, https://www.news.cn/food/20250512/0d6a6851b6d04ec8b147d691f0e3276b/c.html
- **Salmon farming in the desert:** a land-based flow-through salmon farm built 2023 producing 3,000 t/yr of high-grade salmon, planned 5,000 t; water held at 10–18 °C. — SOURCED-weak (state media items, search-result text: cn.chinadaily.com.cn, yzwb.net; not opened in full).
- **Minerals/energy:** coal, petroleum, natural gas. — SOURCED-weak (Chinese-Embassy branded content, below). Tuha oilfield specifics: NOT FOUND.
- **Transport:** Turpan North station on the Lanzhou–Xinjiang high-speed railway (opened 16 Nov 2014; first HSR in Xinjiang); Turpan Jiaohe Airport (TLQ), opened 9 July 2010, 10 km NW of the city. — SOURCED-weak (Wikipedia station and airport pages, search-result text).
- **Who works there — independent reporting:** RFA (24 Sept 2020) reported "as many as eight internment camps may be operating in Turpan," based on ASPI's Xinjiang Data Project; ASPI identified 380 suspected detention facilities Xinjiang-wide. Chinese officials stated in 2019–2020 that "vocational training center" internees had "graduated" and found jobs. — SOURCED, https://www.rfa.org/english/news/uyghur/xuar-camps-09242020200349.html. Xinjiang-wide labor-transfer research (Newlines Institute) documents state-mobilized seasonal cotton picking (≥570,000 persons from three regions in 2018). — SOURCED-weak (search-result text). **Turpan-specific evidence of labor transfer into grape harvest: NOT FOUND.**

#### C. Cultural locations

- **Turpan Karez Paradise** — museum ≈5 km from the city center near G312; shows Miyim Haji's karez (said to be c. 800 years old, uncited). — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turpan_Karez_Paradise. **Karez Park (坎儿井乐园)**, a 4A attraction with a 60 m underground elevator descent. — SOURCED, stdaily 2024. **Karez Folk Customs Garden** — village-founded museum where villagers demonstrate digging tools; underground tea bar, grape corridor. — SOURCED-weak (Klook / chinatravel.com, search text).
- **Chunche (raisin drying houses)** — perforated mud-brick huts built on elevated windy ground, long sides N–S; grapes hang ~40 days in the dark interior; air-dried raisins stay green or yellow; some producers bring grapes from Hami to dry here. — SOURCED, Wikipedia Chunche.
- **Turpan Grape Festival** — since **1990**, late August at **Grape Valley (Putao Gou)**; trade fair plus ethnic performances; listed heritage: **Muqam suites, Meshrep gatherings, Dawaz high-wire acts, traditional papermaking.** — SOURCED, *Canberra Times*, 31 Aug 2026, **branded content for the Chinese Embassy in Australia**, https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/9334120/turpan-grape-festival-desert-culture-thrives-at-annual-event/
- **Turpan Muqam** — one of four regional variants of the Uyghur Muqam (Twelve Muqam, Dolan, Turpan, Hami), inscribed by UNESCO 2008 (proclaimed 2005); **meshrep** and **bezme** gatherings, where everyone took part, are "held less frequently"; youth interest declining. — SOURCED, https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/uyghur-muqam-of-xinjiang-00109. Turpan/Lukchun muqam has 10 muqam, distinct from Kashgar's 12. — SOURCED-weak (CREM-CNRS archive, search text).
- **Emin Minaret (Sugong Ta)** — 44 m mud-brick minaret, 1777–78, built by Suleiman in honor of his father Emin Khoja; adjacent mosque for 1,000+. — SOURCED-weak (Lonely Planet / China Highlights, search text).
- **Turpan Museum** — Xinjiang's second-largest; Astana tomb finds (silks, preserved bodies), dinosaur fossils, giant rhinoceros skeleton. — SOURCED-weak (Lonely Planet, search text).
- **Jiaohe and Gaochang ruins, Bezeklik caves, Astana tombs** (c. 200–800 CE, 10 km², 1,000+ tombs, natural mummies). — SOURCED (Wikipedia Turpan) / SOURCED-weak (Astana details, search text).
- **Tuyoq valley / Tuyuq Khojam Mazar** — oasis village 70 km east in Shanshan County, seedless grapes, old Uyghur-style village, mosque, Buddhist caves; the mazar is locally held to be the Cave of the Seven Sleepers (*Ashab al-Kahf*). Rahilä Dawut (Xinjiang University) writes that the Toyoq Ravine was a Buddhist and Manichaean holy land; 94 Thousand-Buddha caves sit beside the mazar; locals fused local custom with the Seven Sleepers legend "to transform the Buddhist holy land into an Islamic one"; Turpan-area mazar visits tend to be **irregular, for personal needs**, not a fixed calendar circuit; photo of offerings at the Akhterek Khojam mazar in Pichan (Shanshan); "the Mazar of the Seven Girls, Turpan." — SOURCED, Dawut, "Shrine Pilgrimage among the Uighurs," *The Silk Road* 6/2 (2009): 56–67, https://edspace.american.edu/silkroadjournal/wp-content/uploads/sites/984/2017/09/Shrine-Pilgrimage-Among-the-Uighurs.pdf (read via pdftotext); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuyoq_valley. Pilgrims May–October from Xinjiang, Gansu, Ningxia — SOURCED-weak (madainproject, search text); **current pilgrimage status: NOT FOUND.**
- **Bazaar and night markets** — samsa, kebabs, ice cream at the bazaar; night markets from c. 8 pm (Gaochang Lu, Guanghui Street); drinks under grape trellises on Qingnian Lu. — **UNVERIFIED-current** (Frommers/Sygic travel text of unknown date).
- **Mosques — independent research:** ASPI estimates (Xinjiang-wide) that a large share of mosques were demolished or damaged since 2017. — SOURCED-weak (search text). **Turpan-specific mosque data: NOT FOUND.**

#### D. What exists because of the water system

- The karez themselves became museums, a 4A theme park, a village "folk customs" garden and a world irrigation-heritage listing — while only ≈169–190 still flow. — SOURCED.
- Raisin culture: the chunche exists because hot dry wind does the drying; it is valued enough that grapes are shipped in to be dried here. — SOURCED.
- The arid climate preserved the Astana tombs' silks and bodies (museum collections). — SOURCED-weak.
- Water policy now drives the economy: drip conversion of 507,100 mu was pitched as freeing water for 100+ enterprises and industrial use (+14 million m³ in 2024). — SOURCED (state media).

#### E. Structural features

- An oasis economy whose old water system was undercut by its new one (pumped wells), now being rebuilt around drip lines and tourism. — SOURCED.
- Agriculture shifting from bulk raisin grapes to premium fresh varieties and village homestays. — SOURCED (state media).
- Arts: muqam and meshrep are UNESCO-listed but performed less often; festival performances now staged in a trade-fair context. — SOURCED.
- Work and life are shaped by state security policy per independent reporting (detention, labor transfer) and by state development policy per official media; both are attributed above.

### 2b. AL-AHSA

#### A. Snapshot

- **Al-Ahsa Governorate** population **1,104,267** (census 10 May 2022, GASTAT); **Al-Hufuf–Al-Mubarraz 729,606** (2022). — SOURCED-weak (citypopulation.de citing GASTAT, search text) and SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofuf. A 2022 Arab News piece gives "1.3 million." — SOURCED, https://www.arabnews.com/business/al-ahsa-innovatively-preserving-heritage-among-unesco-creative-cities-2067291
- **Oasis:** c. 37 miles (60 km) inland from the Gulf; oasis area **85.4 km²** (Guinness, Oct 2020); **2.5 million+ palms** (≈3 million in the governorate per Saudipedia); fed historically by artesian springs; four cities and 22 villages; UNESCO World Heritage **2018** ("Al-Ahsa Oasis, an Evolving Cultural Landscape," 12 components: gardens, canals, springs, wells, a drainage lake, historic buildings, urban fabric, archaeological sites); UNESCO Creative City of Crafts and Folk Arts **2015** (first in the Gulf). — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ahsa_Oasis; https://saudipedia.com/en/article/1645/history/landmarks-and-monuments/al-ahsa-oasis; UNESCO text via search (whc.unesco.org returned 403).

#### B. What else exists there

- **Water history:** ~30 natural springs fed the oasis; 1967 spring output ≈ **600,000 m³/day**; many have since dried; some now converted into tourist sites by the Saudi Tourism Authority and the irrigation organization. Named springs: Ain Najm (hot, sulfurous), Ain al-Haqel, Al-Jawharia, Ain al-Harrah (warm), Ain al-Khadood (warm), Ain Ali (cold), Bani Ma'an springs (cold), Ain Umm Sab'ah (warm, seven streams). Uses: irrigation, swimming and bathing, social gatherings and **weddings**, therapy. — SOURCED, https://saudipedia.com/en/article/1558/government-and-politics/water-and-agriculture/al-ahsa-water-springs
- **The Irrigation and Drainage Project (late 1960s):** before it, salt buildup from reused ("twice-used") water, waterlogging and sand burying 100+ springs; the project built **900 miles of concrete irrigation canals, 900 miles of drainage ditches, ~1,000 miles of farm roads**, evaporation lakes for saline drainage; cost **$51 million**, five years; arable area **8,000 → 20,000 ha** planned; 30 of 60 major springs tapped; water from some springs dated to 17,000 years; farmers held written water rights by **day and hour** according to acreage. — SOURCED, Tor Eigeland, "The Twice-Used Water," *Aramco World* Nov/Dec 1970, https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/197006/the.twice-used.water.htm. ## 3. Negev runoff farming (Nabataean/Byzantine farms; Evenari farm at Avdat; Sde Boker)

*All sources accessed 2026-10-26.*

### A. Snapshot

- **Avdat:** Negev archaeological site (30.794°N 34.773°E), inhabited 3rd c. BCE – mid-7th c. CE; Nabataean Incense Route station named for deified King Obodas I; Roman from 106 CE; Byzantine churches and monasteries on the acropolis; when the incense trade declined (early 3rd c.) residents built **terraced farms and water channels** to collect winter runoff; at least **five Byzantine wine presses**; part of UNESCO "Incense Route – Desert Cities in the Negev" (2005, with Haluza, Mamshit, Shivta). — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avdat
- **Shivta:** 43 km SW of Beersheba; mostly Byzantine; three churches (Transfiguration mural traces in the south church apse; mosaics), wine presses; estimated production **≈2 million liters of wine**; runoff stored in large reservoirs; abandoned by the 8th–9th c.; only large-scale dig 1933–38 (H. D. Colt), records largely lost in a 1938 fire. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivta
- **Byzantine Negev wine (PNAS 2020):** ≈10,000 grape seeds plus wheat and barley grains from trash pits at Elusa (Haluza), Shivta and Nessana (Nitzana) show viticulture took off in the 4th c., was very productive ≈200 years, then dropped in the mid-6th c. (cold years after volcanic eruptions in the 530s–540s, the Justinianic Plague of the 540s, and socioeconomic decline); wine went in amphoras via the port of Gaza; lead researcher Daniel Fuks (Bar-Ilan). — SOURCED, *Archaeology* magazine, 28 July 2020, https://archaeology.org/news/2020/07/28/200729-israel-negev-winemaking/
- **Evenari runoff farms:** experimental station founded **1959** near Avdat under Prof. Michael Evenari (Hebrew University); the Desert Runoff Farms Unit (now Hebrew University + Ben-Gurion University of the Negev) reconstructed two Nabataean farms, at **Shivta and Avdat**, on original walls and channels; rainfall **4–5 inches** (≈100–125 mm) falling in a few hours a year; Shivta: olives, pomegranates, almonds, figs, carobs, pistachios, apricots, peaches, plums, grapes — staff stopped managing it in the late 1970s for lack of funds, and the trees kept yielding unmaintained; Avdat: cereals, vegetables, legumes, pasture plants and **3,000 orchard trees on 20 acres**; a third farm at **Wadi Mashash** with 200 Awassi sheep (the source's date "1933" for Wadi Mashash is impossible and is treated as UNVERIFIED — likely a typo). — SOURCED, Hershel Shanks, "Nabatean Farms Reconstructed in the Desert," Biblical Archaeology Society sidebar (undated), https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/sidebar/nabatean-farms-reconstructed-in-the-desert/. Avdat farm "is still in use today"; farming shown viable under 150 mm rain; runoff farming in the region dates to c. 1000 BC, peak 300 BC–600 AD; Cornell's Lehmann Lab ran trials there (planting density, species, pruning). — SOURCED, https://lehmannlab.cals.cornell.edu/?p=604 (c. April 2020)
- **Sde Boker:** kibbutz founded **15 May 1952** by discharged soldiers; population **554 (2024)**; Ramat HaNegev Regional Council; precipitation ≈3.44 in/yr. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sde_Boker
- **Midreshet Ben-Gurion:** community next to Sde Boker; construction from 1962 on Ben-Gurion's idea of a "Hebrew Oxford of the Negev"; **≈1,570 residents (2024)**. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midreshet_Ben-Gurion
- **Ramat HaNegev Regional Council:** 4,099 km² (≈22% of Israel by its own description), **≈8,500 residents (2021)**, 2.1/km²; 20 villages (kibbutzim Mashabei Sadeh, Retamim, Revivim, Sde Boker, Tlalim; moshavim Be'er Milka, Kmehin; community settlements Ashalim, Ezuz, Merhav Am, Midreshet Ben-Gurion, Nitzana, Nitzanei Sinai, Sheizaf; youth village Ruah Midbar) plus **23 individual farms**; HQ on Highway 40 between Mashabei Sadeh and Tlalim; Revivim (1943) began as an agricultural experiment station. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_HaNegev_Regional_Council; search text (desertech.org.il) for Revivim.

### B. What else exists there

## 5. Almería's Campo de Dalías greenhouse region, Spain

*All sources accessed 2026-10-26. The Campo de Dalías (Poniente Almeriense: El Ejido, Roquetas de Mar, Vícar, La Mojonera, Dalías, Adra area) is distinct from the Campo de Níjar east of Almería city; where a source covers the province or Níjar only, it is labeled.*

### A. Snapshot

- **El Ejido:** 227 km², **91,440** (2025); third-largest municipality in Almería province; segregated from Dalías as its own municipality in **1982**; 2023 rainfall only **27.3 mm**. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ejido. Foreign population "over 30%"; El Ejido third in the province by foreign share after **Níjar (47.19%)** and **Vícar (33.32%)**; main nationalities Moroccan, Romanian, Ecuadorian, Argentine, Russian. — SOURCED-weak (search text from Spanish-Wikipedia mirror and press; INE table not opened).
- **Roquetas de Mar:** **111,240** (Jan 2025), second-largest in the province; beach resort (Spanish, UK, Irish, German visitors); A-7 motorway; "the second-largest town in Spain without a rail link." — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquetas_de_Mar
- **Dalías** (old parent municipality): 4,196 (Jan 2025), 140 km², 411 m elevation. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dal%C3%ADas
- **Greenhouses:** Campo de Dalías ≈**19,000 ha** under greenhouses, growing again since 2012. — SOURCED-weak (search text from Spanish water-ministry/Botín Foundation documents). **Province of Almería, campaign 2023–24 (Junta de Andalucía, Delegación Territorial de Agricultura en Almería):** greenhouse area for vegetables **32,483 ha** (stable); cultivated area 61,447 ha (48,205 ha greenhouse + 13,245 open air — more than one crop a year on half the greenhouses); production **3,927,190 t**; value **€3,953 million**; exports 2,306,913 t worth €3,173.49 million; average price €0.80/kg; production costs up >20% over three campaigns; **pepper** the leading crop (sales €1,230 M, exports €943 M); **biological pest control on 28,647 ha** (pepper 12,463 ha); organic greenhouse vegetables 4,450 ha; **282 registered fruit-and-vegetable companies**; producer organizations (OPFH) bill €2,320 M (53%), the **auction system 22%**, other firms 25%; top 10 firms bill 61%; exports = 76% of value; 70% of production exported. — SOURCED, "Análisis de campaña 2023-2024 Almería" (PDF hosted by La Voz de Almería), https://legal.lavozdealmeria.com/2024/presentacion-campana-2023-2024-1732544724.pdf (read via pdftotext).

### B. What else exists there

- **Water:** Campo de Dalías aquifer system withdrawals ≈**140 hm³/yr** against ≈**79 hm³/yr** of renewable input; seawater intrusion begun at the western and eastern ends; **Campo de Dalías seawater desalination plant** in El Ejido, designed for 30 hm³/yr net; plans for an aquifer users' community. — SOURCED-weak (search text from MITECO viability report https://www.miteco.gob.es/es/agua/planes-y-estrategias/informes-de-viabilidad-de-obras-hidraulicas/daliasf_tcm30-82805.pdf; not opened). Illegal wells reported to 2,000 m. — SOURCED-weak (Wikipedia "Intensive farming in Almería").
- **History:** first greenhouse **1963**; polyethylene adopted after trials in the Canaries and Catalonia; harvests a month earlier than open field. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intensive_farming_in_Almer%C3%ADa. Growth after 1950s colonization programs. — SOURCED, https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ejido
- **Environment:** ≈30,000 t of plastic waste and 5,200 t of chemical waste a year; the white roofs' albedo has cooled the area ≈0.3 °C per decade since the 1980s. — SOURCED (Wikipedia "Intensive farming in Almería", secondary).
- **Marketing infrastructure — alhóndigas** (warehouses where farmers and buyers meet in descending-price auctions): Agrupación de Alhóndigas de El Ejido (Agroejido 1976, Femago 1978, Agroponiente 1987, Cehorpa 1991); **Alhóndiga La Unión** ≈400 million kg/yr, 3,000+ farmers, 26 centers in Almería and Granada. — SOURCED-weak (infoagro / alimarket, search text).
- **Research:** **Estación Experimental Cajamar Las Palmerillas**, El Ejido — founded **1975** by Caja Rural Intermediterránea (now Cajamar, a cooperative bank), 30 greenhouses on 14 ha for trials and knowledge transfer. — SOURCED-weak (MAPA press text via search). - **Workforce:** a Wikipedia figure of **120,000 immigrant workers (2015), 80,000 without papers** — SOURCED-weak (secondary). Greenhouse temperatures up to 50 °C. — SOURCED-weak. SAT union: in 2019, 30% of firms still paid 2002-level wages, €32–35/day instead of €46.80 under the 2013 agreement. — SOURCED, *El Salto*, 20 Feb 2019 (María Usero), https://www.elsaltodiario.com/almeria/dos-decadas-acuerdos-incumplidos. SOC-SAT offices in Almería, Níjar, El Ejido. — SOURCED-weak (search text).

## 6. The Arava valley, Israel (desert vegetable export; Eilat as gateway)

*All sources accessed 2026-10-26. Covers Central Arava Regional Council (moshav farming belt), Hevel Eilot Regional Council (southern kibbutzim) and Eilat.*

### A. Snapshot

- **Central Arava Regional Council:** founded **1977**; communities along Route 90 — moshavim **Ein Yahav, Hatzeva, Idan, Paran, Tzofar**; community settlements **Ir Ovot, Sapir (council seat, "Merkaz Sapir"), Tzukim**; 2,700 residents (2009) per Wikipedia; **3,772 (2024)** per search-reported administrative data; 2022 election: 86% center-left bloc. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Arava_Regional_Council; 2024 figure SOURCED-weak (citypopulation.de, search text). Hatzeva 1,297 and Ein Yahav **1,654** (2024). — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ein_Yahav; Hatzeva SOURCED-weak.
- **Hevel Eilot Regional Council:** founded **1964**, ≈2,649 km², **≈5,300** residents in 13 communities (kibbutzim incl. Yotvata, Ketura, Lotan, Grofit, Samar; settlements Be'er Ora, Shaharut, Shitim); **not connected to the national water system — relies on Red Sea desalination**; first commercial solar field (Arava Power, Ketura); 82% center-left (2022). — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hevel_Eilot_Regional_Council
- **Agricultural scale:** 112-mile strip Dead Sea–Red Sea; **600 farms on 8,650 acres in eight villages** (2012); "over 60% of Israel's fresh vegetable exports," ≈10% of ornamentals; **peppers ≈150,000 t/yr**, plus tomatoes, eggplants, melons, watermelons, table grapes, herbs, dates, strawberries, mangos, ornamental fish. — SOURCED, Israel21c, 29 May 2012 (Abigail Klein Leichman), https://archive.israel21c.org/the-surprising-source-of-israels-edible-exports/. "900 families… over 50 percent of Israel's fresh vegetable exports"; population grew from 400 (1950s) to ≈4,000; rainfall under 1 inch/yr; ≈12 workers per farm, mostly Thai. — SOURCED, WAMC, 10 July 2019, https://www.wamc.org/wamc-news/2019-07-10/agriculture-thrives-in-israels-arava-desert
- **Eilat:** **56,004** (2024); 78.6% Jewish, 4.9% Arab, 16.5% other; "over 10,000" foreign workers (caregiving, hospitality, construction); Sudanese refugees from 2007; **free trade zone since 1985**; Ramon International Airport opened **2019**; no railway (planned). — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilat

### B. What else exists there

- **Water:** farming relies on brackish groundwater from local aquifers (reported ≈1.5 km deep, 63 wells); brackish water is desalinated for drinking; dates, watermelons, grapes and peppers tolerate it; a nanofiltration pilot at the **Yair station, Hatzeva** cut irrigation and fertilizer 25%; brine used to grow red beet. — SOURCED-weak (York University Mideast Environment page, ScienceDaily 2012, Eurekalert; search text). Drip irrigation and "fertigation" associated with Yuval Zohar (1960s). — SOURCED, Israel21c 2012.
- **Research:** **Arava R&D** (founded 1986 by the Jewish Agency), stations **Zohar and Yair**; site tours, seminars. — SOURCED, Israel21c 2012. **AICAT** (Arava International Center for Agriculture Training) brings students from developing countries in Asia and Africa (East Timor named). — SOURCED, WAMC 2019. **Arava Institute for Environmental Studies**, Kibbutz Ketura (1996): 800+ students — Israeli Jews and Arabs, Palestinians, Jordanians, Egyptians, Tunisians, Moroccans, Europeans, Americans; BGU master's programs; four research centers (hyper-arid socio-ecology, renewable energy, sustainable agriculture, transboundary water); revived a 2,000-year-old date seed ("Methuselah") and frankincense. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arava_Institute_for_Environmental_Studies
- **Kibbutz economies:** **Yotvata** (1951 Nahal outpost; first southern-Arava kibbutz 1957; 824 residents 2024; <30 mm rain): dairy begun 1962 with four cows, by 2008 **62 million liters/yr and 63% of Israel's dairy-beverage market**; dates, mangos, onions, potatoes, corn. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yotvata. **Ketura** (Nov 1973, American Young Judaea; ≈564 residents): **Algatech** microalgae (astaxanthin), Arava Power solar fields (40 MW facility 2015), dates, **Keren Kolot** guest house. — SOURCED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketura,_Israel
- **Moshav model:** Ein Yahav — experimental station 1950 (Shahal movement), moshav 1962; known for developing **chocolate-colored peppers**; 100 m below sea level; airfield (EIY). — SOURCED, Wikipedia Ein Yahav. ≈200 families, ≈150 farmers, 50 dunams each. — SOURCED-weak (Nefesh B'Nefesh page, search text).
- **Workforce — Thai labor:** HRW "A Raw Deal" (21 Jan 2015): ≈20,000 Thai farmworkers in Israel; interviewed 173 workers in 10 moshavim (Ein Yahav named); average pay 16.45 NIS/hr vs 23.12 legal minimum; 12–17-hour days, seven days a week; makeshift housing in 9 of 10 communities (cardboard rooms in sheds, crowded caravans); pesticide spraying with minimal protection; **122 deaths 2008–2013** (43 "sudden nocturnal death syndrome"); 2011 bilateral agreement cut recruitment fees from ≈$10,200 to $850. — SOURCED, https://www.hrw.org/node/131918.
