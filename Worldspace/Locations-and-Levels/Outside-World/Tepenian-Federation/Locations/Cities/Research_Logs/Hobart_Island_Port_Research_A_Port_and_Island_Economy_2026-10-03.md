## 2. Per-thread findings

### 2.1 Thread 1 — The port of Hobart as a physical port

#### 2.1.1 Operator and structure
- Operator: **TasPorts (Tasmanian Ports Corporation Pty Ltd)**, a state-owned corporation formed 1 January 2006 by merger of four port companies (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Hobart **[tertiary]**). TasPorts also provides pilotage, towage, vessel traffic services, fuel distribution at Hobart/King Island/Flinders Island, slipways at Hobart/Strahan/Flinders/King Island, and (through a 50/50 JV with Qube Ports) the **Southern Export Terminals (SET)** log export business on the Hobart wharf precinct (TasPorts Annual Report 2021-22 and 2024-25, https://tasports.com.au/volumes/documents/Annual-Reports/TasPorts-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf and `...TasPorts-Annual-Report-2122.pdf`) **[2 angles, same publisher]**.
- TasPorts regulates, pilots and tows at Port Latta but **does not own or operate infrastructure there** (Annual Report 2024-25, directors' report). Port Latta is Grange Resources' own port (Grange AGM deck, May 2026, slide 13: "Grange has 100% ownership of all infrastructure").
- TasPorts employed 338 staff in FY2025; net profit after tax A$11.9 million; 14.3 Mt freight; 131 cruise visits statewide (TasPorts 2024-25 annual report news page, https://tasports.com.au/news/tasports-2024-25-annual-report).
- Hobart-based fleet: two ASD tugs (**Mount Florance** and **Yandeyarra**, 65 t bollard pull, 12.6 kn), two pilot launches (Kelly, Paringa), a barge and workboats (https://tasports.com.au/meet-the-fleet-hobart). Pilotage is compulsory at all Tasmanian ports for ships of 35 m LOA or more (https://tasports.com.au/marine-services). Ports Procedures Manual: two ASD tugs of at least 45 t bollard pull to be kept in Hobart, at least one escort-capable.

#### 2.1.2 Layout (sources: TasPorts berth pages; Ports Procedures Manual s10.1)
Hobart's port limits are defined in three zones: **Zone A** (River Derwent from latitude 42°55.5'S up to beacons about 450 m south of the Tasman Bridge), **Zone B** (from there to the Bowen Bridge, i.e., through and above the Tasman Bridge), **Zone C** (the whole D'Entrecasteaux Channel and its bays, down to the line Partridge Island to Southport Island). The Macquarie and Princes wharves sit in Zone A, **south of the Tasman Bridge**; Risdon (zinc smelter wharves) and Selfs Point (oil wharf) are in Zone B, **above** the bridge (Ports Procedures Manual June 2019, https://tasports.com.au/volumes/documents/Port-Procedures-Manual/TasPorts-Ports-Procedures-Manual.pdf; berth list at https://tasports.com.au/hobart-port-information).

#### 2.1.3 Vessel limits
- **Maximum draft for Hobart: 14.1 m**; Zone C approach (outer channel) has no length limit and an 8.6 m draft at lowest astronomical tide before tide-restriction (Ports Procedures Manual s10.1.5, s10.2.3; June 2019 edition).
- **Macquarie 4 and 5**: after added arch fenders, assessed to berth vessels of up to **50,000 t displacement and 300 m LOA** (HMI 23-27, re-issued 28 Sept 2023, https://tasports.com.au/volumes/documents/HMI-Hobart/HMI-23-27-Port-of-Hobart-Macquarie-Points-Berth-4-and-5-Issued-28-September-2023-valid-until-cancelled.pdf).
- **Zone B, through the Tasman Bridge:** maximum LOA 185 m; maximum beam **32.31 m** (set to match the pre-expansion Panama Canal beam, the design limit of the product tankers that regularly go up to Selfs Point; HMI 22-58); **maximum air draft 44 m** (manual and HMI 22-58; Wikipedia gives a design clearance of 46 m below the bridge); escort tug required for vessels of 130 m LOA or more, and for 110-130 m vessels drawing 7 m or more; current limits for 150-185 m vessels (defer transit if current at the vessel's draft exceeds 1.0 kn or approach-side profiler exceeds 0.75 kn; also deferral triggers tied to Meadowbank Dam overflow, spring tides, ebb, southerly sea breeze; HMI 22-21, https://tasports.com.au/volumes/documents/HMI-Hobart/HMI-22-21-Port-of-Hobart_Tasman-Bridge-Current-limits_Issued-13-September-2022-valid-until-Cancelled.pdf). **[3 angles: manual, two HMIs; Wikipedia for the 46 m design figure]**
- Nine ice-class vessels have approval to pass under the Tasman Bridge, including China's Xue Long and Xue Long 2; RSV Nuyina is barred (ABC, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-11-26/china-antarctic-icebreaker-invited-to-use-hobart-as-gateway/104642498).
- Cruise: Hobart took **87 cruise calls in FY2025** (its strongest season; statewide 131 visits, second largest on record) (TasPorts Annual Report 2024-25). FY2024: 83 cruise visits at Hobart (Annual Report 2023-24, Port of Hobart panel). A search-engine summary of a TasPorts cruise page said two cruise vessels can be accommodated at once up to 349 m LOA **[snippet only, page not opened; the cruise page I fetched did not contain that figure]**.
- Other TasPorts ports' limits (for flat reference, same sources): Port Latta, per Ports Procedures Manual: LOA **165-230 m**, max beam 36 m, max draft = 15.25 m plus tide minus 1.6 m under-keel clearance, air draft 15.3 m under the loader (Panamax vessels must arrive in heavy ballast). Bell Bay max draft 11.5 m (2019). Devonport max draft 9.0 m (2019); declared depths 1 July 2026: outer channel 9.5 m, inner channel 8.8 m, turning circle 9.2 m (HMI 26-18). Burnie turning circle 360 m diameter, least depth 10.0 m (HMI 25-35).

#### 2.1.5 The Antarctic gateway role: physical facts
- **RSV Nuyina:** 160.3 m LOA, 25.6 m beam, 9.3 m draft, 25,500 t displacement; cargo capacity 1,200 t plus 1,900 m3 cargo fuel (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSV_Nuyina **[tertiary for these specs]**). From the AAD IEE: cruising 12 kn, endurance 90 days, range 16,000 nm, operating 200-260 days a year with about 100 days south of 60°S; the standard season runs October to April (https://documents.ats.aq/EIES/EIA/02687enIEE%20-%20RSV%20Nuyina%20-%20Operations%20and%20Science%20Systems%20-%20Final%20-%20October%202024.pdf) **[primary]**. Port of registry Hobart.
- **Home berth:** Macquarie Wharf 6. In 2021 TasPorts and AAD jointly spent A$3 million on bollards and fenders for the 160 m ship (https://tasports.com.au/news/nuyina-officially-home-at-the-port-of-hobart). The wharf is described as "riddled with concrete cancer" (ABC, 2 Aug 2024). TasPorts' initial 30-year cost estimate of A$515 million was rejected by the federal Environment Minister as not commercially reasonable (ABC, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-08-02/hobart-macquarie-wharf-upgrade-for-antarctic-program-and-nuyina/104169152).
- **Redevelopment (Macquarie Wharf Redevelopment, stage 1 = Macquarie Wharf 6):** Federal funding A$188 million (announced Oct 2024) for construction **and ongoing maintenance**; a 30-year agreement with priority access for Nuyina; scope = demolish and rebuild Wharf 6, shore power for Nuyina, upgraded portside facilities, **a dedicated Antarctic precinct**, and later upgrades to cargo handling capacity at Wharves 4, 5 and 6, "expanding Tasmania's export and cruise capacity". Expressions of interest 2 Apr 2025 (six received, three shortlisted); contract to Hazell Brady Joint Venture (Hazell Bros Group with Brady Marine & Civil) 19 Dec 2025; early works Feb 2026; demolition began Aug 2026 (12,900 t of structure over five months, 280 t crawler crane); **practical completion on track for August 2028**. Sources: https://tasports.com.au/news/hazell-brady-jv-awarded-contract-to-redevelop-macquarie-wharf-6 ; https://tasports.com.au/news/deconstructing-a-giant-macquarie-wharf-redevelopment-reaches-major-milestone ; https://tasports.com.au/news/project-team-assembles-to-deliver-macquarie-wharf-6-redevelopment ; https://tasports.com.au/news/tasports-opens-expressions-of-interest-for-macquarie-wharf-6-redevelopment ; https://tasports.com.au/news/port-of-hobart-secured-as-australias-antarctic-gateway **[3+ angles, same publisher plus ABC and the search results from premier.tas.gov.au / dcceew.gov.au]**. TasPorts' board report calls this its largest infrastructure project (the earlier QuayLink at Devonport cost A$241 million; the Port Master Plan page says the two projects together exceed A$400 million).
- **Macquarie No. 2 wharf** is Hobart's dedicated Antarctic and cruise terminal and houses AAD's **Cargo and Biosecurity Center**; **further investment is planned at Macquarie Point for year-round lay-up of the next generation of Antarctic resupply and research vessels**; **AAD headquarters is in Kingston**, south of Hobart; CSIRO's and UTAS's marine laboratories (IMAS) are on the Hobart waterfront (Antarctic Tasmania, Wayback copy: https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://www.antarctic.tas.gov.au/hobart_a_gateway_city/hobart_world-class_research_hub_and_science_gateway). Hobart City Deal partners are "working to progress" an **Antarctic and Science Precinct** at Macquarie Point (Hobart City Deal page, Wayback copy; the live page is 403) **[2 angles, both Tasmanian Government]**.
- **Hobart Airport:** home of the AAD's Airbus A319 intercontinental air link to Wilkins Aerodrome (about 400 passengers a season, limited priority cargo) and the RAAF C-17A (72 t per heavy-lift flight; can airdrop year-round including winter) (AAD aviation page, https://www.antarctica.gov.au/antarctic-operations/travel-and-logistics/aviation/) **[2 angles with Antarctic Tasmania]**. The AAD schedule shows A319 flights to Christchurch as well (positioning for McMurdo support; Antarctic Tasmania says the A319 also serves US McMurdo runways). The airport's runway upgrade to take A350/787 was completed Aug 2025 (Wikipedia **[tertiary]**); a 2024 closure had blocked 26 planned Antarctic flights in Oct-Dec 2024 (ABC, 2 Aug 2024).
- **Foreign programs through Hobart:** France (IPEV/TAAF's L'Astrolabe runs from Hobart to Dumont d'Urville 4-5 times a summer; MoU first signed 2014, renewed 2019), China (Xue Long ships; five port calls in the season before COVID; Tasmanian MoU 2013 expired; Tasmanian Premier invited the ships back in Nov 2024), the US Coast Guard's Polar Star (ABC, Nov 2024). Other nations that have used Hobart: Italy, Japan, USA, USSR (search-result summary of Wikipedia **[snippet only]**). Antarctic Tasmania: Hobart is one of five recognized gateway cities; the Wikipedia gateway page says Hobart "offers the least transportation to Antarctica among the gateway cities, but has the highest number of Antarctic scientists" **[single-source, tertiary]**.
- **Economic size of the Antarctic sector:** A$183 million a year (Tasmanian Polar Network chair, ABC Nov 2024); "over A$200 million a year" and a 70-plus-member Tasmanian Polar Network (ABC, 11 May 2026) **[2 angles, different dates]**.
- **Historical:** Hobart used by Biscoe (1831), Dumont d'Urville (1839), Borchgrevink, and Amundsen, whose Fram reached Hobart on 8 Mar 1912 to break the news of the Pole (Antarctic Tasmania history page, Wayback copy: https://web.archive.org/web/20251123090931/https://www.antarctic.tas.gov.au/why_is_tasmanias_antarctic_sector_important/history).

#### 2.1.6 Ship repair, slipping and dry-docking
- TasPorts' **Queens Domain slipyard** in Hobart: slips of **1,200 t, 180 t and 25 t**; services include marine engineering, boiler-making, blasting and painting (https://tasports.com.au/slipyards). TasPorts also provides slip services at Strahan, Flinders Island and King Island. TasPorts won a 2025 Biosecurity Tasmania award for marine-pest training for slip operations (Annual Report 2024-25).

Currently under construction: three 129 m battery-electric ferries for Molslinjen (Hulls 102-104) and a total of five large sustainable aluminum vessels in the pipeline; Hull 096 (130 m, "China Zorrilla", the world's largest battery-electric ship) left Hobart in August 2026; Incat was named 2026 Tasmanian Exporter of the Year (https://incat.com.au/company-profile/ ; https://incat.com.au/incat-named-tasmanian-exporter-of-the-year-2026/) **[primary, company-published]**. Battery size 41.2 MWh (Corvus system) is from a search snippet of several news sites **[snippet only]**.
- Tasmanian Vessel Replacement Taskforce (2021): "**There are no shipyards or precincts in Tasmania that are set up to fit out large steel vessels**"; Bell Bay and Hobart ports "identified as having potential", with major additions needed (https://www.treasury.tas.gov.au/Documents/Vessel%20Replacement%20Taskforce%20Final%20Report%20-%20Redacted.pdf, Executive Summary and Chapter on fit-out) **[primary]**.

#### 2.1.7 Rail, road and Bass Strait
- **Rail:** TasRail runs a 1,067 mm network; 2022-23 freight was 485 million net metric ton-km, **2.85 Mt**; terminals at **Brighton, Burnie, Devonport, George Town (Bell Bay), Conara and Launceston**; the Brighton Transport Hub (north of Hobart) began operating in 2014 "following TasRail's relocation from the Hobart CBD" (https://tasrail.com.au/freight-terminals ; https://tasrail.com.au/about-us). The Hobart wharves are **not** on TasRail's terminal list **[inference from absence, flag]**; the Hobart (Macquarie Point) rail yard is now historical (Wikipedia, Rail transport in Tasmania).
- **Bass Strait:** more than 99% of Tasmania's total freight by volume moves by sea; about 89% of Bass Strait freight is for Tasmanian and mainland Australian markets and 11% is export; domestic container and trailer freight about 550,000 TEU in 2019-20, projected 642,000-808,000 TEU by 2034-35; three operators: Toll (Burnie, two ships of 700 TEU each, 6 nights a week), SeaRoad (Devonport, up to 455 TEU, 6 nights), TT-Line (Devonport, 7 nights; moving to Geelong's Corio Quay). Services are "built into nearly all Tasmanian freight supply chains" under a "make-and-ship model", with minimal warehousing within Tasmania. Government freight-cost relief: Tasmanian Freight Equalisation Scheme, up to A$855 per TEU (Vessel Replacement Taskforce Report, March 2021) **[primary]**. Geelong to Devonport 242 nm (448 km), 9-11 hours (Wikipedia, Spirit of Tasmania **[tertiary]**).
- Hobart is not a Bass Strait port. The Bass Strait shipping lanes are served from Burnie and Devonport; Bell Bay is a Tamar River bulk and industrial port.

#### 2.1.8 Thread-1 findings that plausibly translate into a Hobart-specific application
Stated flat, no ranking:
- A deep-water southern port with the largest declared berth depth in the Macquarie group (12.8 m at Macquarie 4) and a 300 m / 50,000 t berth, close to the Southern Ocean, with an Antarctic cargo and biosecurity facility on the wharf.
- A designated polar-vessel home port with a 30-year priority berth, shore power and a planned year-round lay-up and Antarctic precinct.
- A bulk and fuel import port for southern Tasmania (1.0 Mt imports a year) and a log export port.
- A small-vessel slip and an aluminum fast-ferry builder on the same river.
- A port that is **not** the container or Bass Strait gateway (0.5% of statewide TEU).

### 2.2 Thread 2 — Tasmania's own resources and industry

#### 2.2.1 Iron ore: Savage River and Port Latta
- **Owner and operator:** Grange Resources Limited (ASX: GRR), 100% of Savage River; Burnie head office; the CEO is Weidong Wang; the board includes a representative of Jiangsu Shagang Group (board appointment notice, 2 June 2026). Savage River is "Australia's longest-running magnetite operation" and "57 years" old (AGM deck, 13 May 2026, https://grange.blob.core.windows.net/public/bea9a42c-aec8-404d-8564-6904ed6512be.pdf).
- **Chain:** open-cut mine (North Pit, Center Pit) → beneficiation (concentrate over 65% Fe) → **~85 km slurry pipeline** → **Port Latta pelletizing plant** (5 vertical shaft furnaces) → bulk carrier. Capacities: mining 7-10 Mtpa, beneficiation about 5-7 Mtpa, pipeline about 2.85 Mtpa, pelletizing about 3.3 Mtpa, Port Latta about 4.5 Mtpa (AGM deck slide 11).
- **Output:** about 2.5 Mt a year sold (AGM deck slide 7 shows sales of 2.3-2.6 Mt a year over 2016-2025); about 2.6 Mt shipped in calendar 2023 at an average US$140 FOB (Grange market update, 22 Dec 2023, https://grange.blob.core.windows.net/public/9546b357-7d51-423f-b3ed-15bcd73170fe.pdf); **Q2 2026: concentrate 586,906 t, pellets produced 590,883 t, pellet sales 580 kt, pellet stockpile 303,403 t, realized price US$114.32/t FOB Port Latta** (Grange quarterly report, 28 July 2026, https://grange.blob.core.windows.net/public/35e8286d-f68d-4eac-81d8-f75864698170.pdf). Pellet premium about 18% over the 65% Fe benchmark in 2025; 2025 cash cost A$165/t (AGM deck slide 6). **[3 angles: three Grange documents; the two quarterly/AGM documents agree.]**
- **Reserves and mine life:** December 2025 statement: Mineral Resource **464 Mt at 44% DTR**; Ore Reserve **96 Mt at 44.7% DTR** (open-cut plus underground), depletion of 3.8 Mt in the year (AGM deck slide 12; the full statement was released to ASX on 2 April 2026). Feasibility study (Dec 2023): underground block cave, 64 Mt ore, 28 Mt concentrate over 15 years to 2037. Deepest drilling shows mineralization continuing to more than 1 km depth.
- **Status risks (all primary, Grange):** the North Pit Underground Project has over 5 km of development completed, an independent technical expert review found no fatal flaws, and mining-lease renewal legislation passed unanimously in March 2026; **but** financing and a final investment decision are pending, the project capital is reported as about A$890 million (up to A$1.2 billion total investment, Tasmanian Government statement via search result), and on **30 July 2026 Grange flagged a likely non-cash impairment of A$300-600 million** on Savage River because the carrying value depends on obtaining external financing and on long-term price assumptions (https://grange.blob.core.windows.net/public/2ceca8ed-b7b9-4dce-b04e-5821010c8ca5.pdf). The mine is operating normally; it is not in care and maintenance.
- **Context:** Australia's total iron-ore mine production in 2024 was **954 Mt** (Geoscience Australia AIMR 2024 preliminary tables, https://www.ga.gov.au/scientific-topics/minerals/aimr/preliminary-tables), so Savage River is about 0.26% of the national total; Grange's contribution to Tasmania in 2025: A$10 million royalties, A$99 million wages, A$6 million payroll tax, over A$159 million spent with local suppliers (AGM deck slide 8). Grange also owns the undeveloped **Southdown** magnetite project (1,257 Mt at 33.8% DTR) near Albany, Western Australia, and is seeking an equity partner (AGM deck slide 21).
- **Port Latta is not Hobart.** It is on the north-west coast (west of Burnie). Pellets are shipped by bulk carrier (165-230 m LOA, draft to about Panamax). For a Hobart-centered question the relevance is that **Tasmania's iron comes off the north-west coast**, not through the Hobart wharves.

#### 2.2.3 Smelting: zinc, aluminum, ferro-alloys
- **Nyrstar Hobart (Risdon Zinc Works), Lutana:** capacity up to **280,000 t** of zinc a year; roast-leach-electrowin process; by-products cadmium, copper sulfate, gypsum, sulfuric acid; ~520 direct employees (about 650 with contractors); operating in Hobart since 1916-18; in 2024 contributed A$513 million to Tasmania's economy, A$292 million direct spend, about 1,891 FTE jobs, 304 local businesses; **from April 2025 production cut by 25% (about 70,000 t a year)** because of negative treatment charges and raw-material market conditions; much of its concentrate comes from Tasmania's west coast mines (https://www.nyrstar.com/operations/metals-processing/nyrstar-hobart ; https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-03-13/tas-nyrstar-hobart-to-reduce-zinc-production/105045094) **[2 angles]**. A modernization with a federal contribution (A$50 million of an estimated A$400 million in 2022, Wikipedia) is reported in Wikipedia only; the Nyrstar page says a new cellhouse project "is being reviewed" **[the larger number is single-source tertiary]**.
- **Why it matters for the port:** Risdon is in Zone B (above the Tasman Bridge, 8.7 m max draft, vessels up to 185 m LOA, 32.31 m beam, 44 m air draft). In 1975 the 140 m bulk carrier **Lake Illawarra**, bound for Risdon with 10,000 t of zinc concentrate, struck the bridge; the span collapsed and 12 people died (Wikipedia Tasman Bridge **[tertiary]**; ABC 4 June 2026 states the death toll of 12).
- **Aluminum, Bell Bay (Rio Tinto):** nameplate **178,000 t/yr** (Wikipedia, citing a 2006 Rio Tinto data book **[dated, tertiary]**); built in 1955 partly because of "available hydroelectric power and deep water facilities"; lost A$35 million in 2023 and A$18.5 million in 2024; its 10-year Hydro Tasmania power contract expired at the end of 2025 and the state extended it by a year; the smelter uses "about a third of the state's total electricity generation" and employs about 550 people (RenewEconomy, 5 Nov 2025, https://reneweconomy.com.au/tasmania-gives-rio-tinto-smelter-a-power-lifeline-but-expects-federal-subsidy/) **[2 angles on the power-use share: RenewEconomy "a third"; Wikipedia "more than 25%" from a 2014 submission]**. The A$2 billion federal Green Aluminum Production Credit may or may not apply.
- **TEMCO / Liberty Bell Bay (manganese alloys, George Town/Bell Bay):** opened 1962 (BHP), ferromanganese and silicomanganese, "the only commercial ferroalloy operation in Australia", sold to Liberty/GFG in 2021; ore supply crisis after Cyclone Megan damaged GEMCO's infrastructure (March 2024); phased shutdown from May 2025; voluntary administration early 2026; **ceased operations 16 July 2026, 217 workers** (ABC, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-07-16/liberty-bell-bay-closure-devastates-community-northern-tasmania/106922774; Wikipedia Bell Bay) **[2 angles]**. TEMCO therefore does not currently operate.
- **Hydrogen and ammonia at Bell Bay:** **not researched** (see §0 and §4).

#### 2.2.6 Energy: hydro, wind, interconnection, island security
- **Hydro Tasmania:** "one of the most complex hydropower systems in the world, with **30 hydropower stations and 54 large dams**", a gas power station (Tamar Valley), a 25% share in three wind farms, and hybrid renewable systems on King Island and Flinders Island that cut diesel use by two-thirds or more; "Tasmania has the highest proportion of renewable energy of all the states" (https://www.hydro.com.au/our-energy-system/our-power-stations ; https://www.hydro.com.au/our-energy-system/how-energy-works). Installed hydro about **2,286 MW** (2022) and about 80% of 2021 electricity from hydro; wind about **564 MW** across five main-grid wind farms, Granville Harbor capacity factor 41.6% in 2021 (Wikipedia, Energy in Tasmania **[tertiary, single-source for the numbers]**).
- **Basslink:** the only interconnector, 370 km, **500 MW**, owned by APA; Dec 2015 fault left Tasmania disconnected from **21 December 2015 until 13 June 2016** (Wikipedia **[tertiary]**; Hydro Tasmania independently says its High Reliability Level storage target is meant to cover "a 6-month Basslink outage" in dry weather **[2 angles]**). In the two financial years from mid-2023 to mid-2025 Tasmania had drought with inflows of 6,200 and 6,700 GWh and a Basslink net import of 1,910 GWh (Wikipedia, Hydro Tasmania **[single-source, tertiary]**).
- **Marinus Link:** proposed 750 MW HVDC cable, stage 1 final investment decision made 1 Aug 2025, construction from 2026, completion about 2030 (Wikipedia **[tertiary]**). Pumped hydro at Lake Cethana (750 MW) is under consideration (Hydro Tasmania).
- **Large loads:** the Bell Bay aluminum smelter uses about a third of generation (§2.2.3).

#### 2.2.8 Thread-2 findings that plausibly translate into a Hobart-specific application
Stated flat, no ranking:
- Everything on this list is **already produced on the island** and could in principle be sold to a trading partner directly from Tasmanian ports without passing through the three mainland terminals: iron pellets (Port Latta), zinc and aluminum (hydro-powered smelters), tin concentrate, logs and sawn/framing timber, salmon, dairy, beef, vegetables, fruit.

- Hobart-built aluminum fast catamarans (Incat, 30-130 m, battery-electric capable) are a distinctive local industrial product.

- **To Casey and Davis:** Hobart is shorter for Casey than every mainland port in the table and every north-coast Tasmanian port; Bunbury is **shorter than Hobart to Davis (4,577 vs 4,815) and to Mawson (5,063 vs 5,448)**, which is also visible in the AAD table (Perth 4,736 vs Hobart 4,838 to Davis; 5,223 vs 5,475 to Mawson).
- **Other Tasmanian ports:** Strahan (west coast) and Triabunna (east coast) are closer to the Antarctic stations than Bell Bay, Burnie, Devonport or Port Latta, but **neither is a commercial deep-water port of any size in the sources**: the Ports Procedures Manual lists Strahan at 35 m max LOA and Spring Bay (Triabunna) among secondary ports (Spring Bay limits not extracted). The practical Tasmanian southern departure port is Hobart.

#### 2.3.3 Thread-3 findings that plausibly translate into a Hobart-specific application
Stated flat, no ranking:
- For the Adélie Land and Commonwealth Bay coast, a Hobart departure is about 0.9 day closer than the Victorian terminals, about 1.6-1.7 days closer than Outer Harbor, and about 2.5-2.7 days closer than Bunbury (computed, 12 kn).
- For the Bunger Hills coast the Hobart advantage is about 0.6-0.8 day versus the Victorian and South Australian terminals and roughly nil versus Bunbury.
- The Tasmanian departure is the natural first leg for Macquarie Island (1,542 km).
- Real transit times from Hobart are published: 5-7 days to Dumont d'Urville (L'Astrolabe) and 8 days to Casey (Nuyina, including some operations).

### 2.4 Thread 4 — What the island setting changes: biosecurity, fuel, security of supply

#### 2.4.1 Marine biosecurity and quarantine
- **Tasmanian internal border:** Tasmania states that it has "some of the world's strictest biosecurity laws" (Biosecurity Act 2019), requires inspection of regulated goods imported from the mainland (Registered Quarantine Place scheme), and lists **77 known introduced marine pests** among Australia's more than 400 introduced and cryptogenic marine species (https://nre.tas.gov.au/biosecurity-tasmania/biosecurity-basics ; https://nre.tas.gov.au/invasive-species-site/Pages/Invasive-Marine-Species.aspx ; https://nre.tas.gov.au/biosecurity-tasmania/plant-biosecurity) **[3 pages, same department]**.
- **Antarctic cargo screening at Hobart:** all cargo for the Australian Antarctic Program passes through AAD Supply Chain Operations biosecurity screening at the **Kingston warehouse and the Cargo and Biosecurity Center**: internal inspection and fogging of cargo transport units, external inspection and fogging of containers and break-bulk cargo, a rodent-detection dog ("Nui"), pest control; timber must be free of bark, dirt, deep cracks and borer holes; waste must be returned to Australia; polystyrene beads, radio-isotopes and pesticides are prohibited without a permit (https://www.antarctica.gov.au/antarctic-operations/travel-and-logistics/cargo-and-freight/biosecurity/) **[primary]**. The AAD ships page says personal effects and cargo "may be inspected by Quarantine Tasmania on behalf of the AAD" (https://www.antarctica.gov.au/antarctic-operations/travel-and-logistics/ships/). The IEE says the Center holds an Approved Arrangement to manage biosecurity risks, hull inspections are done annually, and ballast water is managed on voyages **[2 angles]**.
- **TasPorts:** received the 2025 Biosecurity Tasmania Industry Award for its marine-pest training program for slip operations (Annual Report 2024-25).
- **Statement by the sources, flat:** the biosecurity regime is a feature of Hobart as a **Tasmanian** port (state law plus the AAD facility on Macquarie 2); I found no source that says the island setting by itself makes marine biosecurity easier or harder at Hobart (the hull-borne marine-pest risk applies to ships from all Australian ports, per the IEE).

#### 2.4.2 Ship fuel and the Tasman Bridge
- **The problem:** Hobart's marine fuel depot is at **Selfs Point**, about 4 km from Macquarie Wharf and **above the Tasman Bridge**. In 2023 the harbor master refused RSV Nuyina permission to transit the bridge, because of its "directional stability" (109 computer simulations, the ship "hit" the bridge's central pylons 4 times, and would have hit 10 more times if simulations had not been called off; Nuyina's width across bridge wings 35 m against 73 m between central pylons per the ABC; its rounded hull and large surface area are wind-sensitive) (https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-06-04/tas-nuyina-under-the-tasman-bridge/106755940 ; https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-06-05/nuyina-refuelling-options-explainer/106761314) **[2 angles, same journalist]**. Note the conflict between the 35 m wing width reported by the ABC and the 25.6 m beam in Wikipedia/IEE; the ABC article says "35 meters across bridge wings", which may refer to bridge wings (bridge-deck projections) rather than the hull beam. Flag, unresolved.
- **The workaround:** the ship sails to **Burnie** (north-west Tasmania) to bunker, a **674 km** detour (ABC; earlier ABC reports said 660 km; Wikipedia says 360 nm, about 670 km), costing **almost A$900,000 a year** (earlier FOI figure A$875,000; ABC Nov 2024 says "almost A$1 million") (https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-11-08/aad-foi-reveals-cost-blowout-tasman-bridge-nuyina-safety-ban/103073662 **[snippet only]**; https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-10-09/icebreakers-costly-refuelling-saga-yet-to-be-resolved/105868524 **[snippet only]**). In the 2026-27 AAD schedule Nuyina bunkers at Burnie on 18-19 Dec 2026 and 25-26 Jan 2027, with **MGO (marine gas oil) and SAB (Special Antarctic Blend diesel)** (AAD schedule, above) **[primary]**.
- **Quantities:** about **4.2 million L** of ship fuel for a 90-day voyage plus **1.9 million L** of cargo fuel for resupply (ABC, 5 June 2026). A 2023 report considered 64 road tankers at two hours each, more than five days of continuous 24-hour work (ABC, 11 May 2026).
- **The commitment and the miss:** the 2024 Commonwealth-Tasmania agreement tied the **A$188 million** to a "refuelling solution" available by the 2026-27 season; a **A$2 million milestone payment** depended on the Commonwealth agreeing a solution by **31 July 2026**; by June 2026 the Commonwealth had rejected the state's interim solution of road tankers as "far from" acceptable and the state confirmed it would **not** have a permanent solution by the deadline; internal documents said most options need 24-36 months. Three options remain "live": a barge, a holding tank (storage at Macquarie Wharf not available until 2028), and re-assessing the bridge ban (the AAD formally withdrew its 2024 request when told the decision was unlikely to change unless the hull was modified) (ABC, 11 May, 4 June and 5 June 2026) **[3 angles, same journalist; plus the 2024 ABC article about the barge EOI]**.
- **Ice-class ships that can use the bridge:** nine, including Xue Long and Xue Long 2 (ABC Nov 2024).
- **Fuel supply in general:** Tasmania's fuel and gas "flow primarily through Bell Bay, Burnie, Devonport and Hobart" (TasPorts Annual Report 2023-24); TasPorts itself distributes fuel at Hobart, King Island and Flinders Island (Annual Report). The oil wharf at Selfs Point has a declared depth of 13.8 m.

#### 2.4.3 Security of supply: freight and electricity
- **Freight:** over 99% of Tasmania's total freight task by volume moves by sea (Vessel Replacement Taskforce Report, citing Department of State Growth) **[primary]**; goods made on the island go out under a "make-and-ship model" with minimal warehousing (same report). Government freight-cost relief up to A$855 per TEU (TFES).
- **Electricity:** one interconnector (Basslink, 500 MW, 370 km). Basslink was out of service **21 Dec 2015 to 13 Jun 2016** during a drought (Wikipedia **[tertiary]**); Hydro Tasmania's reserve rule is built around surviving a 6-month Basslink outage in dry weather (hydro.com.au, "How do we keep energy secure?") **[2 angles]**; Marinus Link (750 MW) is under construction for about 2030.
- **The bridge disaster as a hazard record:** the 1975 *Lake Illawarra* collision (see §2.2.3).

#### 2.4.4 Thread-4 findings that plausibly translate into a Hobart-specific application
Stated flat, no ranking:
- A biosecurity-screened export point for goods going to a protected or sensitive destination (the AAD facility on Macquarie 2 is the working example).
- An upriver oil depot behind a bridge with hard ship-size limits (air draft 44 m, beam 32.31 m, LOA 185 m): a physical chokepoint that constrains which ships can bunker locally and has pushed an expensive workaround to a different port.
- An island with sea-dependent freight (more than 99%) and a single power interconnector: security-of-supply texture for a trading partner.

## 3. Flat candidate list: possible Hobart-specific applications (possibilities only; unranked)

Each item is tied to the thread it came from. None is a decision. Several are in tension with each other; that is deliberate (the file records what the evidence could support, not what to pick). Items that the evidence cuts against are in §3.2.

### 3.1 Possibilities the evidence supports

1. **The nearest-departure port for the Adélie Land / Commonwealth Bay coast.** A Hobart-side departure is about 0.9 day closer than the Victorian terminals and about 1.6-2.7 days closer than Outer Harbor and Bunbury (computed, 12 kn); real transits are 5-7 days to Dumont d'Urville (L'Astrolabe). (Thread 3.)
2. **The nearest-departure port for Macquarie Island and the southern ocean.** 1,542 km from Hobart; Nuyina's V4 voyage goes there each autumn. (Thread 3.)
3. **A transfer point from bulk freighters to smaller or ice-capable ships.** Hobart has a 12.8 m / 300 m LOA / 50,000 t berth (Macquarie 4) and the Antarctic cargo facility on Macquarie 2; this role is not documented anywhere as existing, only physically consistent with the berth data. (Thread 1; Thread 3.)
4. **A polar-vessel home port and service base.** A 30-year priority berth for the national icebreaker, shore power, a "dedicated Antarctic precinct", and planned year-round lay-up for next-generation Antarctic vessels at Macquarie Point; a Tasmanian Polar Network of 70+ firms supplying goods and services to Antarctic programs. (Thread 1.)
5. **A multinational gateway for foreign Antarctic programs.** France (L'Astrolabe, Hobart to Dumont d'Urville), China (Xue Long ships), the US Coast Guard; foreign cargo handled through the AAD's cargo system and facilities. For a coast that includes a French-named station and a Commonwealth Bay site, this fits as a "foreign flag" supply route. (Thread 1.)
6. **A combined sea-and-air gateway: people by air, freight by sea.** The A319 and C-17 flights from Hobart Airport to Wilkins Aerodrome run beside the sea resupply; the A319 carries about 400 passengers a season and little cargo; the C-17 takes 72 t and can airdrop in winter. (Thread 1.)
7. **A biosecurity-screened export point.** AAD's Cargo and Biosecurity Center (container fogging, rodent-detection dogs, bark-free timber rule) on the wharf, inside a state with strict biosecurity law. (Thread 1; Thread 4.)
8. **An island-protected supply buffer that can ship food directly.** Tasmania produces 5.9 times the food its residents eat; A$4.42 billion of net interstate food sales; salmon A$1.28 billion; dairy, beef, vegetables, fruit. Food that does not come from the mainland terminals. (Thread 2.)
9. **A direct source of hydro-powered metals.** Aluminum (Bell Bay, 178 kt nameplate), zinc (Nyrstar Hobart, 280 kt capacity, cut 25% in 2025), tin (Renison, 11.0 kt of Australia's 11.3 kt). Iron pellets (Savage River/Port Latta, about 2.5 Mt a year) from the north-west coast. (Thread 2.)
10. **A timber and log supply port.** Hobart's Southern Export Terminals handle logs; statewide forestry cargo is 3.49 Mt a year; bulk building materials are named as one of the mainland terminals' freight classes, and Tasmanian framing timber is one candidate. (Thread 2; Thread 1.)
11. **An aluminum fast-ferry and electric-vessel construction base.** Incat at Prince of Wales Bay builds 30-130 m aluminum catamarans, with five large battery-electric or hybrid hulls in the pipeline; a Hobart-built vessel type could serve coastal and inter-station runs. (Thread 2.)
12. **A slip-based repair point for small and medium vessels.** TasPorts' Queens Domain slip (1,200 t / 180 t / 25 t). (Thread 1.)
13. **A fuel-handling node with an upriver oil depot.** The Selfs Point Oil Wharf is 13.8 m deep, the deepest declared berth at the port; the bridge limits are a real operational constraint. (Thread 1; Thread 4.)
14. **A cruise and passenger port.** 87 cruise calls in FY2025 at Hobart, up to the bridge-free Macquarie berths. (Thread 1.)
15. **A cold-climate science, logistics and expertise hub.** AAD headquarters at Kingston; CSIRO and UTAS marine laboratories on the Hobart waterfront; Antarctic sector worth A$183 million to over A$200 million a year to Tasmania; Tasmanian firms supplying engineering, food, fuel and traverse systems loaded at the wharf. (Thread 1.)
16. **A southern-island redundancy route that does not depend on the Bass Strait ferry system.** More than 99% of Tasmania's freight is by sea, but the Antarctic and Hobart-based flows (fuel, logs, Antarctic cargo) are not Bass Strait container traffic. (Thread 1; Thread 4.)
17. **A seasonal rhythm.** Nuyina's operating period runs mid-October to mid-April; voyages are scheduled in four numbered legs with turnarounds in Hobart; winter is airdrop-only by C-17. A port whose Antarctic traffic has a summer season and a winter silence. (Thread 3.)

### 3.2 What the evidence does **not** give Hobart (flat, so the list above is not read as the whole picture)

- Hobart is **not** the container or Bass Strait gateway: 3,035 TEU in FY2025, 0.5% of the statewide total; Devonport and Burnie carry 92%.
- Hobart has **no** on-dock rail connection in the sources; TasRail's Hobart terminal moved to Brighton in 2014.
- Iron, tin, zinc concentrate and woodchips move through **north and north-west** ports; Hobart's wharves are not their shipping point as far as the sources show.
- Nuyina **cannot** bunker at Hobart: it bunkers at Burnie (a 674 km detour), and a Hobart solution is not expected before about 2028.
- **No** dry dock or large steel-vessel repair yard was found in Hobart or anywhere in Tasmania (Vessel Replacement Taskforce, 2021).
- Hobart's declared berth depths (10.0-12.8 m on the Macquarie wharves) are limits for deep-draft bulk carriers; the 14.1 m maximum draft figure is for the port (June 2019 manual) and the declared berth depths are what the Harbor Master publishes.
- Upper-river berths (Risdon, Selfs Point) lie above a bridge with a 44 m air draft, 32.31 m beam limit and 185 m LOA limit.
- The island's smelters and aluminum are at risk: Bell Bay aluminum lost A$18.5 million in 2024 and its power contract was extended only one year; TEMCO closed in July 2026; Nyrstar Hobart cut output 25% in 2025; Savage River faces an A$300-600 million impairment and unfinanced underground transition.
