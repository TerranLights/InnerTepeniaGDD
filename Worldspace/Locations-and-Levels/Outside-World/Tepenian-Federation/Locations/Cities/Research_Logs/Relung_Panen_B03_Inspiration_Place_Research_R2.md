### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Lá an Phátrúin, Inis Oírr, 14 June (St. Caomhán):** the island's patron saint; his 10th-century church, Teampall Chaomháin, sits below the surrounding ground in blown sand; it "is usually covered by drifting sands, but it is cleared each year in order to hold a service at his tomb"; an open-air Mass is said there each year; the feast was moved to 14 June in the 19th century from an original 3 November; a tradition of spending the vigil praying at his grave (Leaba Chaomháin), where cures were reported. **SOURCED** (Wikipedia "Caomhán of Inisheer," read 2026-10-29; the cures claim is marked "citation needed" there). I was very against that because I knew it would be divisive — and it was" (Dara Molloy). Only one household caught COVID-19 on Inis Mór. **SOURCED** (*Irish Times*, Feb 2021). [K1, K6, K9, P7]

### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Niñopa (R1 lead, now read):** image made in 1573 at the San Bernardino monastery, 51 cm, about 600 g; kept for one year at a time by a mayordomo family rather than in a church; "The waiting list to host the image extends past 2040 and families have waited as much as fifty years"; if a listed person dies first, "the privilege is inherited by another member of the family." Yearly cycle: **2 February (Candlemas)** — custody passes to the next family, seeds and candles are blessed; **30 April (Día del Niño)** — the mayordomo feeds guests; **16–24 December (Posadas)** — the image goes each day to a different neighborhood with chinelo dancers, music and fireworks; "Almost every day, the image leaves to attend Mass and to visit families, the sick and hospitals." Other venerated child images: Niño Dormidito, Niño de Belén, Niño Tamalerito, Niño Grande, Niño de San Juan. **SOURCED** (Wikipedia "Niñopa," read 2026-10-29). The family wakes the image "each morning with music before being dressed for the day"; hosting is "a serious and expensive undertaking," families "prepare for years, even decades," usually renovating a room; they pay for dancers and food. In 2020 home visits were stopped and the 2 Feb transfer streamed online. **SOURCED** (*Mexico News Daily*, L. Thelmadatter, 13 Jan 2021). [K2, K4, K8, P6, P7]
- **Ley seca (alcohol-sale bans) during fiestas — K6:** the borough publishes alcohol-sale bans in the Gaceta Oficial de la Ciudad de México for each pueblo or barrio's patron-saint fiesta. Aug 2026: Barrio Caltongo 5–7 Aug; Santa Cruz Acalpixca 6 Aug; San Lorenzo Atemoaya 7–15 Aug; Barrio San Lorenzo 8–11 Aug; Barrio La Asunción 14–16 Aug; San Luis Tlaxialtemalco 15–25 Aug. Applies to liquor stores, grocery stores, supermarkets. Fines for businesses 40,000 to 300,000 pesos plus closure; for individuals 2,000 to 3,400 pesos, 25 to 36 hours' administrative arrest, 12 to 18 hours' community work. **SOURCED** (adn40, 29 Jul 2026). Oct–Nov 2026: San Francisco Tlalnepantla 2–12 Oct; La Huichapan 9–12 Oct; San Lucas Xochimanca 13–25 Oct; "Santa María Tepan" (as rendered by the fetch; spelling not checked) 25 Oct; San Gregorio Atlapulco 28 Oct–2 Nov. **SOURCED** (adn40, 5 Oct 2026). These dates also map the fiesta calendar. [K6, K2, K1, P7]
- **Trajinera rules — K6:** after a 20-year-old drowned in Sept 2019 crossing between two trajineras while drunk, the borough announced life jackets, drug-free certification for every rower ("Todo remero va a tener que estar certificado, libre de consumo de drogas"), alcohol limits and a ban on loudspeakers and generators (boats are recreation vessels, "not dance venues"). **SOURCED** (*La Razón*, 2 Sep 2019). The rules published in the city Gaceta Oficial 29 Jan 2020 (in force 30 Jan): per group one 1-liter bottle of spirits, or beer at most three 355 ml cans per person; radios, speakers, generators and loud sound players banned; max 18 passengers aged 4+; no standing at bow or stern, no jumping between boats, no trash; operators must "suspender el viaje y regresar al embarcadero" (end the trip and return to the dock) if a passenger is drunk, with no refund; violations go to the civic court. **SOURCED** (*Chilango*, 29 Jan 2020). Michelada and beer stands removed from the embarcadero stairways after fights and falls — **UNVERIFIED** (snippet of adn40). [K6, K1, K9, P7]

### B. Subcultures
- **Lotus-fiber women (occupational, religious rules — K6):** women are the main fiber extractors. "In the past old days, fiber extractor must be single, unmarried women. ## FINAL — Round 2 summary (2026-10-29)

**Most useful new finding per pick (each place on its own terms):**
1. **Aran Islands** — the island court sits three times a year in the 1966 community hall (Halla Rónáin), hearing drink, public-order and pier-parking cases in Irish and English, with the judge's stated logic that offenders face "their neighbors"; Inis Oírr's islanders dig their patron's 10th-century church out of blown sand every year for a 14 June Mass; a 2016 ferry-levy dispute measured off-season isolation; the Inis Meáin development company used surplus profit to offer a rent-free house to an Irish-speaking family (1,300+ applied).
2. **Xochimilco** — no official count of chinamperos exists; estimates run from about 500 to about 3,400 (producers' own estimate 2,000–2,400 men and 600–1,000 women), with 596 marigold growers in 2026; alcohol-sale bans are published in the city gazette for each pueblo's patron fiesta (with fines, arrest and community service); a written boat code since 2020; Niñopa custody waits past 2040 and is inherited within the family; chinampas are being converted to soccer fields.
3. **Inle Lake** — the 2018 Helvetas/BioTrade study gives the lotus-weaving origin (Daw Sa Oo, Kyaing Khan; revived as a cooperative), work terms (daily-wage, piece, apprentice; weaver about US$2/day) and the extractors' purity rules (formerly unmarried women only, not during menstruation, bathing and the five precepts); Phaung Daw U trustees banned new gold leaf in 2019, and only men could apply it before; conscription and displacement now shape the villages; the hospitality school closed Dec 2024.
4. **Uros** — Uros Chulluni is a delegated municipality (about 2001), declared an indigenous people by Puno's council in 2016; each island has a president and about four families; Puno's 5 November founding-myth reenactment starts on an Uros island; boatmen are organized in an association; no Uros-run festival found.
5. **La Geria** — island D.O. registry (1,827 growers, 1,864 ha, 34 wineries, 2023) with harvest inspectors and grape-origin checks; the La Geria hermitage festival (15 Aug) reenacts the camel harvest; the island romería (mid-September) commemorates lava said to have stopped in 1736.
6. **Ifugao** — Imbayah (since 1979) rests on an old feast of a commoner's rise to the elite, made annual in 2015 "to remind our people especially our young generation"; Gotad ad Ifugao is the June provincial festival built on a post-harvest celebration; Batad's Tinawon Festival (June); no high school in Cambulo pushes the young out, while the village school teaches terrace-wall building.
