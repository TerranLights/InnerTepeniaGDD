### A. Other settings (sub-areas, schools, worker housing, off-season)
- **Secondary schools and who boards:** Coláiste Naomh Éinne (Inis Mór) had 62 students in Dec 2015; its principal said the school "boasts frequent winners of national contests in dance and sean-nós singing" and has produced "two handball world champions and four county football players." Coláiste Naomh Eoin (Inis Meáin) opened as a pilot in 2001. Coláiste Ghobnait (Inis Oírr) opened in 1985. Before the island secondary schools, island students had to leave at about 12; a former boarder: "We were lucky as we could go back in at least every second weekend, but it does break your connection with home." A scholarship scheme for Irish learners places about 10 mainland students on each island with host families, and demand "often outstrips supply." **SOURCED** (*Irish Times*, "Island education," 1 Dec 2015). [K4, K7, P4, P9]
- **Inis Oírr host-family year:** Coláiste Ghobnait had 35 students in 2026, 10 of them mainland Transition Year students living for the school year with island host families; host-family stipend €37 a night; the scholarship covers food and lodging; a separate Department of Education remote-areas grant of up to €5,000 a year; students usually travel home every two weeks; program running 20-plus years. The principal: students hear Irish "as a living language; it's not just inside the classroom. When they leave school every evening, they hear the language in the local shop." **SOURCED** (*Farmers Journal*, 3 Jun 2026). Coláiste Laichtín Naofa, the island's summer Irish college, had 180 students on a July course — **UNVERIFIED** (search summary; page not opened). [K4, K8, P4, P9]
- **Housing scarcity and a co-op answer (Inis Meáin):** in July 2022 Comhlacht Forbartha Inis Meáin (the island development company, est. 2016) offered a house rent-free for a school year to a family with school-age children; more than 1,300 applied by 10 Aug 2022; Irish speakers prioritized ("Gaeilge is the language of our schools, of our community … we will be prioritising people who can function in the language," chairwoman and secondary principal Mairéad Ní Fhátharta); population about 183 (2016); the island has a preschool, primary and secondary school; the company did it with surplus profit "returned to the community." **SOURCED** (*Irish Times*, 8 Aug 2022). Two families (from Meath and Limerick) chosen; total applications later above 1,600; one family stayed permanently — **UNVERIFIED** (search summaries of *Irish Times* 23 Aug 2022 and IrishCentral). [K1, K4, K8, K9, P9]

### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Lá an Phátrúin, Inis Oírr, 14 June (St. Caomhán):** the island's patron saint; his 10th-century church, Teampall Chaomháin, sits below the surrounding ground in blown sand; it "is usually covered by drifting sands, but it is cleared each year in order to hold a service at his tomb"; an open-air Mass is said there each year; the feast was moved to 14 June in the 19th century from an original 3 November; a tradition of spending the vigil praying at his grave (Leaba Chaomháin), where cures were reported. **SOURCED** (Wikipedia "Caomhán of Inisheer," read 2026-10-29; the cures claim is marked "citation needed" there). In the 1980s the islanders excavated the ruin, cleared the dunes and built a low wall against the sand — **UNVERIFIED** (search summary). An annual community act of digging out a building that the ground keeps burying. [K2, K14, P6, R]
- **Pátrún, Inis Mór:** three days, sports, currach and hooker racing, tug-of-war (R1). Date given in a listing as 27–28 June — **UNVERIFIED**. The Aran Sweater Market blog on it is 404 (dead at source, again).
- **Island court (K6):** the District Court sits in Cill Rónáin three times a year for all three islands, in the community hall; cases: assault, harassment, public order, drunk driving, road traffic, environmental and planning enforcement, pier parking; heard in Irish and English; the judge travels by the 45-minute ferry. Judge Mary Fahy: "If people do misbehave, they are coming before their neighbours." Inis Mór has the only Garda station of the three islands (one sergeant, two gardaí). **SOURCED** (*Irish Times*, 9 Sep 2023). [K6, K1, P7]
- **Policing history:** from June 1967 the Department of Justice posted only unmarried gardaí to the islands, each for two years, to avoid the cost of moving pregnant or sick wives to the mainland; one garda paid £10 toward a £35 lifeboat trip for his pregnant wife. **SOURCED** (*Irish Times*, 30 Dec 2005). [K6, K7, K9, P7]
- **Visitor trouble:** August bank holiday 2004, Inis Oírr: about 40 visitors off the Doolin ferry brought alcohol; bonfire, doors ripped off the campsite toilet and shower blocks, rave music; the co-op manager spoke of "a real atmosphere of fear"; one garda on the island for the weekend, then a sergeant and three more sent from Salthill. **SOURCED** (*Irish Times*, 5 Aug 2004). [K6, K9]
- **Pandemic self-rule:** in early summer 2020 Inis Mór islanders voted by phone message on whether to reopen ferry and plane routes to visitors; about 80% wanted to stay closed; "We ended up having a vote through phone messaging. I was very against that because I knew it would be divisive — and it was" (Dara Molloy). Only one household caught COVID-19 on Inis Mór. **SOURCED** (*Irish Times*, Feb 2021). [K1, K6, K9, P7]
- **Craiceann** bodhrán summer school: first held 2001 at Áras Éanna; continued yearly except for the pandemic years; over 1,000 students from 28 countries; 2022 edition 24–28 June. **UNVERIFIED** (search summary of craiceann.com; its history page 404). [K3, K13]

### A. Other settings

  - So: no official census of chinamperos; estimates run from about 500 to about 3,400. **The range itself is the finding.**
- **Who farms, by age and sex:** most Mexico City farmers are between 45 and 60 — **UNVERIFIED** (search summary of an *Ecoosfera* piece). Historically "Antes eran los hombres quienes sembraban y cosechaban. Las mujeres nos encargábamos de la comercialización, del traslado y de la venta, pero no de la tierra misma" (Before, it was men who sowed and harvested. We women handled marketing, transport and sale, but not the land itself), Rosalba del Valle, co-founder of the Lintlali cooperative; "Ser mujer chinampera implica doble esfuerzo: trabajar la tierra y sostener la casa" (Being a woman chinampa farmer means double effort: working the land and keeping the house). Women's participation in whole crop cycles rising, notably in agroecological farming in San Gregorio. **SOURCED** (*Milenio*, 4 Jan 2026). [K4, K7, P9]
- **The chinampa turned into a sports ground:** a young farmer (32) who bought her chinampa in 2020 describes "a massive increase" in chinampas converted to soccer fields over the previous two to three years; owners also convert to food stands and kayak-tour businesses because tourist uses can earn in "a couple of weekends" what farming earns in a year (US$5,000 to 10,000); "People don't want to farm anymore." A researcher says the chemical load from the fields is "very, very large." **SOURCED** (*Christian Science Monitor* / AP, 4 Nov 2024; *VOA*, 10 Nov 2024). The Cuemanco embarcadero area has about 10 islands with soccer fields reached by trajinera — **UNVERIFIED** (search summary of a 2016 *Chilango* list). [K9, K10, P4, P8]

### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Niñopa (R1 lead, now read):** image made in 1573 at the San Bernardino monastery, 51 cm, about 600 g; kept for one year at a time by a mayordomo family rather than in a church; "The waiting list to host the image extends past 2040 and families have waited as much as fifty years"; if a listed person dies first, "the privilege is inherited by another member of the family." Yearly cycle: **2 February (Candlemas)** — custody passes to the next family, seeds and candles are blessed; **30 April (Día del Niño)** — the mayordomo feeds guests; **16–24 December (Posadas)** — the image goes each day to a different neighborhood with chinelo dancers, music and fireworks; "Almost every day, the image leaves to attend Mass and to visit families, the sick and hospitals." Other venerated child images: Niño Dormidito, Niño de Belén, Niño Tamalerito, Niño Grande, Niño de San Juan. **SOURCED** (Wikipedia "Niñopa," read 2026-10-29). The family wakes the image "each morning with music before being dressed for the day"; hosting is "a serious and expensive undertaking," families "prepare for years, even decades," usually renovating a room; they pay for dancers and food. In 2020 home visits were stopped and the 2 Feb transfer streamed online. **SOURCED** (*Mexico News Daily*, L. Thelmadatter, 13 Jan 2021). [K2, K4, K8, P6, P7]
- **Ley seca (alcohol-sale bans) during fiestas — K6:** the borough publishes alcohol-sale bans in the Gaceta Oficial de la Ciudad de México for each pueblo or barrio's patron-saint fiesta. Aug 2026: Barrio Caltongo 5–7 Aug; Santa Cruz Acalpixca 6 Aug; San Lorenzo Atemoaya 7–15 Aug; Barrio San Lorenzo 8–11 Aug; Barrio La Asunción 14–16 Aug; San Luis Tlaxialtemalco 15–25 Aug. Applies to liquor stores, grocery stores, supermarkets. Fines for businesses 40,000 to 300,000 pesos plus closure; for individuals 2,000 to 3,400 pesos, 25 to 36 hours' administrative arrest, 12 to 18 hours' community work. **SOURCED** (adn40, 29 Jul 2026). Oct–Nov 2026: San Francisco Tlalnepantla 2–12 Oct; La Huichapan 9–12 Oct; San Lucas Xochimanca 13–25 Oct; "Santa María Tepan" (as rendered by the fetch; spelling not checked) 25 Oct; San Gregorio Atlapulco 28 Oct–2 Nov. **SOURCED** (adn40, 5 Oct 2026). These dates also map the fiesta calendar. [K6, K2, K1, P7]
- **Trajinera rules — K6:** after a 20-year-old drowned in Sept 2019 crossing between two trajineras while drunk, the borough announced life jackets, drug-free certification for every rower ("Todo remero va a tener que estar certificado, libre de consumo de drogas"), alcohol limits and a ban on loudspeakers and generators (boats are recreation vessels, "not dance venues"). **SOURCED** (*La Razón*, 2 Sep 2019). The rules published in the city Gaceta Oficial 29 Jan 2020 (in force 30 Jan): per group one 1-liter bottle of spirits, or beer at most three 355 ml cans per person; radios, speakers, generators and loud sound players banned; max 18 passengers aged 4+; no standing at bow or stern, no jumping between boats, no trash; operators must "suspender el viaje y regresar al embarcadero" (end the trip and return to the dock) if a passenger is drunk, with no refund; violations go to the civic court. **SOURCED** (*Chilango*, 29 Jan 2020). Michelada and beer stands removed from the embarcadero stairways after fights and falls — **UNVERIFIED** (snippet of adn40). [K6, K1, K9, P7]
- **La Flor más Bella del Ejido:** R1 covers it; nothing new.
