## 1 · The entries, in the picks file's format

```
##### Relung Panen (candidates #27 to #50)
- Virgin Lands Campaign, northern Kazakhstan [PRIMARY] // a 1954 state program to plow the steppe for grain: over 300,000 Komsomol recruits plus student, soldier and driver brigades, 24,000 specialists of whom 14,000 left, and a net 322,000 new state-farm workers (1954 to 1957) against 1.8 million m² of housing; the erosion it caused produced its own grain institute at Shortandy, and a regional super-government (the Virgin Lands Territory) ran 1960 to 1965
- Beidahuang state farms, Heilongjiang, China [SECONDARY] // cold wetland reclaimed into a grain base by state-sent people in four streams (140,000 demobilized soldiers, 100,000 college graduates, 200,000 inland frontier-support youth, 540,000 urban educated youth); the farms ran their own courts, schools and hospitals until local governments took over from 2019, and about three quarters of the Small Sanjiang Plain's wetlands were lost between 1950 and 2000
- FELDA land schemes, Malaysia [SECONDARY] // a state agency that since 1956 settled landless rural poor in planned tree-crop villages, each with a pre-built house on a quarter-acre plot and 4 to 5.7 ha of rubber or oil palm paid off over about 15 years; a 1960 law keeps each holding undivided, so the second generation leaves or crowds the house lots, and 69% of the 112,635 settlers were aged 61 to 79 in 2019
- Snowy Mountains Scheme and Cooma, Australia [PRIMARY] // over 100,000 workers from more than 30 countries built the dams and tunnels in 1949 to 1974, about two thirds migrants, many on two-year directed-labor contracts; camps and company towns (Cabramurra houses only employees and families, 37 residents in 2016), towns drowned and moved, and an Avenue of Flags at Cooma that still flies the 27 or 28 flags of 1959
- Murrumbidgee Irrigation Area and Griffith, Australia [SECONDARY] // an irrigation district from 1912 first settled mostly with returned soldiers, about two thirds of whom abandoned New South Wales settlement holdings; Italian, then Punjabi Sikh and Pacific seasonal workers made it a food region (22.4% of Griffith claim Italian ancestry in 2021); alcohol was prohibited in the area until 1924 and the Italian community built its own church in six months of volunteer labor (1940)
- Imperial Valley, California [SECONDARY] // a desert farm region made by one canal: the 1905 break sent the whole Colorado River into the Salton Sink for about two years, and the replacement All-American Canal (1942) is described as the valley's only water source; much of the labor commutes daily from Mexicali, water has been sold to coastal cities since 2003, and the shrinking Salton Sea exposes dust linked to children's reduced lung function
- Shouguang, Shandong, China [PRIMARY] // coal-free passive-solar winter greenhouses (heat-storing north wall, night blanket) begun by a village in 1989 now supply about a third of Beijing's market vegetables (2018) and send 8,000+ technicians across China (2025); a 2018 typhoon damaged about half of all the houses and uninsured farmers waited nearly a year to rebuild; newer houses are motorized and sensor-controlled, with their electricity use not quantified in the sources
- Singapore's food and water security [SECONDARY] // imports over 90% of its food from 187 countries and regions and runs water as a strategic system (Four National Taps, a 99-year import agreement expiring 2061); the enclosed-farm push largely failed (7 of 16 tendered rooftop car-park sites remained in November 2025, under 1% of local output) and the "30 by 30" target was dropped on 4 November 2025; a national Total Defense Day (15 February) drills a shortage
- Qatar's food build-out after the 2017 blockade, Baladna dairy [SUPPORTING] // the land border closed on 5 June 2017 and fresh milk largely vanished; an air- and sea-lifted herd at a farm that already existed made the country self-sufficient in fresh milk within about a year, but the immediate gap was met mostly by rerouted imports and stocks, and vegetables stood at 39% self-sufficiency in 2024 after 24% in 2018
- West Berlin's Senate Reserve and the Airlift [SECONDARY] // a federally paid, Allied-ordered reserve to keep the city going for at least 180 days (about 1950 to 1990; about 4 million t on 700+ mostly secret sites); residents met it as cheap sales of rotated old stock, and in July 1948 power came four hours a day, so hairdressers, doctors, X-rays and cinemas moved to the night
- Hong Kong's Dongjiang water and food lines [SUPPORTING] // one 83 km line has supplied about 70 to 80% of fresh water since 1965 (dug largely by hand by 10,000+ workers in about a year); three daily express freight trains carried live animals from 1962 to 2010, and today fresh food comes by road through a few crossings, where a driver shortage in 2022 showed the fragility
- Wake Island, Ascension and Chōfu hydroponic farms [SECONDARY] // Pan American grew vegetables in tanks on coral-atoll Wake Island (1937 to 1941) for about 35 residents resupplied by ship every six months; a US military farm on volcanic clinker fed about 2,000 servicemen on Ascension (1945 to 1946); Chōfu's occupation farm (126 ha, from 1946) existed for clean salad vegetables, not for lack of access
- Aran Islands, Ireland [PRIMARY] // fields made of sand, seaweed and scraped clay on bare limestone, described by Synge (1898 to 1901) and an 1890 report; the same seaweed shore also made kelp ash sold by iodine content until about 1948; island services run through development co-ops with no local government, and an island court sits three times a year in the community hall
- Chinampas of Xochimilco, Mexico [PRIMARY] // made ground built in shallow lake water from staked enclosures filled with canal mud and plant matter, with canals as the circulation network; not floating, most of the roughly 2,215 ha is not in active cultivation, no official count of growers exists (estimates run from about 500 to about 3,400), and alcohol-sale bans are gazetted for each pueblo's patron fiesta
- Inle Lake floating gardens, Myanmar [SECONDARY] // stilt-house lake villages whose families farm floating beds anchored with bamboo; about 90% of the beds are tomatoes with heavy chemical inputs and account for most of the lake's lost open water since 1935; lotus-fiber weaving was revived as a cooperative, and conscription and displacement now shape the villages
- Uros floating reed islands, Lake Titicaca, Peru [SUPPORTING] // islands built of cut totora root-mass blocks under criss-crossed reed layers that must be renewed every weeks to months; each island has a president and about four families, and tourism has been the main livelihood since the islands moved near Puno after storms in 1985 to 1986
- La Geria, Lanzarote, Spain [SUPPORTING] // vines in hand-dug pits through the 1730 to 1736 eruption's ash down to the buried older soil, behind semicircular dry-stone wind walls, the ash working as a moisture-trapping mulch; the core zone legally allows only pit cultivation, and the island wine registry listed 1,827 growers on 1,864 ha in 2023
- Ifugao rice terraces, Philippines [SECONDARY] // terraces built up mountain slopes, watered from family-owned forest woodlots through shared canals with assigned watchers, with the farming calendar marked by ritual specialists; the "2,000 years" age is contested (wet-rice terracing expanded within about 400 to 500 years) and many terraces are abandoned
- Subak, Bali, Indonesia [PRIMARY] // irrigation schedules set by consensus at subak meetings held at regional water temples; a subak's boundary follows where its water stops, not village borders, and a member's water share rises with the dues and labor given; historically the supreme temple was also landlord and tribute collector, and kings imposed schedules where water was short
- Aflaj of Oman [PRIMARY] // water time shares are deeded, inherited, rented yearly and auctioned weekly; at Falaj Daris 74% of shares belong to endowments and the state treasury, unwritten drought rules cut what can be traded, and a village meeting hall paid for by endowments can rotate guest-hosting in step with the falaj rotation
- Ghadames, Libya [SUPPORTING] // an oasis town of seven clan quarters with covered streets for men and a roof level for women; a timed five-channel water division is attested only in an Arabic encyclopedia entry and a 2025 review (no scholarly source was read); the spring went unmaintained from 1971 until a 2006 restoration, and a Tuareg community has been displaced since 2011
- Valencia's Water Tribunal and Murcia's Council of Good Men, Spain [SECONDARY] // Valencia's irrigators' court sits Thursdays at noon at the cathedral door, entirely oral and in Valencian, with no lawyers and no appeal; the canal guard is usually the accuser and fines run a few hundred to a thousand euros, stated in the guard's daily wage; in the October 2024 flood the guards and syndics measured 108.88 of 265 km of l'Horta Sud canals damaged
- Acequias of New Mexico, United States [SECONDARY] // an elected mayordomo (appointed if the post falls vacant) under state statute enforces bylaws that tie labor, voting and water access to acreage and good standing; in one acequia the spring cleaning costs two hours per acre per cleaning day, with $15 for each unmet hour, and the related festival is San Isidro's day, about 15 May
- Bisses of Valais, Switzerland [SUPPORTING] // irrigation channels hung on cliffs, kept up by compulsory shared duty with fines for absence and a spring rebuild by about 200 workers at Savièse; a 1934 eyewitness account of the last cliff water-in describes families sending workers, Mass for protection from accidents, and wine afterward
```

## 3 · Corrections the research made to the first-pass reasoning

- **#27 Virgin Lands:** "volunteers from many nations" is too wide. The recruits were mostly from Ukraine and the Russian republic, and a population deported to the same region before 1954 (Volga Germans, Chechens, Ingush and others) was already there. The settlement made the Kazakh population a minority in the north (29% by 1961/62, per one newspaper article's claim). Many nations, but not "no shared inheritance."
- **#28 Beidahuang:** the workforce came in at least four named streams plus political exiles; the 1968 to 1976 Production and Construction Corps was a military border project. Several machine summaries mistranslated Chinese 万 and 亿 units; the log corrected the figures against the Chinese text.
- **#29 FELDA:** settlers came from many places but were selected as landless rural poor and were overwhelmingly Malay; mixed-ethnicity schemes existed mainly in the earliest (Lurah Bilut, 1959). The land is a debt repaid over about 15 years, and from 1985 settlers got shares, not titles. The "collective organization" was a state-supervised structure (scheme committee, block system, cooperatives).
- **#30 Snowy Mountains:** "tens of thousands" is too low (over 100,000 from over 30 countries), and "no shared inheritance" needs care: many were displaced people on two-year directed contracts, and old enmities (Serbs and Croats) persisted.
- **#31 Murrumbidgee:** sequence correction. Planned for returned soldiers, who mostly abandoned it; migrants filled the gap. The Griffith Sikh Games' first year is unresolved (1985 or 1995).
- **#32 Imperial Valley:** widened. The single source also ties the valley to interstate Colorado River allocations, the 2003 water transfers to coastal cities and the Salton Sea.
- **#33 Shouguang:** "earth-walled" fits the original type only; later walls are brick plus foam and then aerated concrete. "Low-power" is narrowed: seventh-generation houses use sensors and motorized blanket rollers, electricity use not quantified in the sources read. The 1989 greenhouse was a village initiative, not a state design. Shouguang is also a brine, salt, bromine and chemical town.
- **#34 Singapore:** see section 2.
- **#35 Qatar:** see section 2.
- **#36 West Berlin:** the reserve was publicly known but its sites and contents were secret.
- **#37 Hong Kong:** the express food trains ended in 2010; the water line is the standing half. Staples come mostly from Southeast Asia, not the mainland lines.
- **#38 Wake, Ascension, Chōfu:** Wake was a commercial airline stopover, not a military base. Chōfu was about hygiene (produce fertilized with night soil), not lack of access.
- **#39 Aran:** widened (the shore also made a cash product, kelp ash); today the made-soil landscape is managed mostly as protected grassland and farming is a part-time supplement to tourism.
- **#40 Chinampas:** not floating, despite the "floating gardens" label; "markets on the water" today is mostly mobile vendors serving tourist boats, with the main produce markets on land; most of the area is not cultivated.
- **#41 Inle:** "whole villages farm" is too broad; lake villages also live by fishing, crafts and boat transport and (until 2020 to 2021) tourism. The floating beds are largely a chemical-heavy tomato monoculture.
- **#42 Uros:** one scholarly line (Orlove 2002, via Tiner 2014) suggests the floating islands date from the 1800s, not deep antiquity.
- **#43 La Geria:** corrected on "where no soil existed." The pits go down through the ash to a buried older soil.
- **#44 Ifugao:** the "2,000 years" age is contested; the water rules are tied to private-but-shared forest ownership (the muyong), not one water board; much of the ritual apparatus has weakened.
- **#45 Subak:** narrowed. The consensus is real, but historically the supreme temple was a landlord and tribute collector, and kings imposed schedules in water-short areas.
- **#46 Aflaj:** sharpened; no named falaj festival was found (the log found the meeting hall and Eid pit ovens instead).
- **#47 Ghadames:** see section 2.
- **#48 Valencia and Murcia:** precision only; the Murcia council's decisions were held unreviewable by a 2003 ruling.
- **#49 Acequias:** the mayordomo is elected by members under state statute (commissioners may appoint to a vacancy), not simply "elected"; the cleaning is worked by members or paid substitutes, with a fine for absence.
- **#50 Bisses:** confirmed. The cliff section was dynamited after a 1935 tunnel replaced it. The 1311 deed recording twelve men killed repairing a channel is secondhand (the deed itself was not seen).
