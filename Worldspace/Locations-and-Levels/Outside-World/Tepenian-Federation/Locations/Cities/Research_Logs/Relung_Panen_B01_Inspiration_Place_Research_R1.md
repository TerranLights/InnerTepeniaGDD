## Pick 1 · Virgin Lands Campaign, northern Kazakhstan (Tselinograd)

**Claim to test:** "A state-mobilized program to close a national grain deficit by settling a vast hostile region with volunteers from many nations at once (K1, K7, K9)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with two corrections.** (1) The "volunteers" were one stream among several: Komsomol recruits, specialists, demobilized soldiers, seasonal student and driver brigades, and a population of people deported to the same region before 1954 (Volga Germans, Chechens, Ingush and others) who were already there and were drawn into the work. (2) "Many nations" is true but the arrivals were mostly from Ukraine and the RSFSR; the effect was to make the existing Kazakh population a minority in the north. The grain-deficit aim is confirmed; so are an early boom (1954, 1956), a drought failure (1955), and a dust-storm crisis (1960 to 1963).

### A. Snapshot
- **What it was:** a Soviet program, launched by a 1954 party plenum and decree, to plow unseeded steppe in northern Kazakhstan and the Altai/West Siberia for grain. [K1] SOURCED: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Lands_campaign; soviethistory.msu.edu/1954/virgin-lands-campaign/ (both read 2026-10-29). The decree "On increasing grain production by developing virgin and fallow lands" is dated 27 March 1954 (UNVERIFIED, Russian search snippet, kazpravda.kz / regional sources).
- **Scale:** 19 million ha plowed in 1954 and 14 million more in 1955 (soviethistory.msu.edu, SOURCED); Wikipedia gives 30 million ha plowed by early 1955 and states 90% of the USSR's sown-area increase 1954 to 1960 came from these lands (SOURCED). The two sources' plowing figures differ; both recorded.
- **People:** "more than 300,000 people, primarily from Ukraine and the RSFSR, were recruited by the Komsomol" in 1954, plus seasonal students, soldiers, truck and combine drivers (soviethistory.msu.edu, SOURCED). 24,000 specialists sent 1957 to 1960, of whom 14,000 left because of conditions (Wikipedia, SOURCED).
- **Machines and farms:** 200,000 tractors sent by the start of 1955; 425 new state farms (sovkhozy) by early 1955; in Kazakhstan by 1959 only one tractor per 218 ha (Wikipedia, SOURCED).
- **Center:** Akmolinsk (founded 1830 as Aqmola) became the transport hub; on 20 March 1961 it was renamed Tselinograd, from the Russian word for virgin land (Wikipedia "Astana" via search; Tselinny Krai article, SOURCED). Population: 102,276 (1959), 233,638 (1979), 277,365 (1989) (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Astana, SOURCED). It became Kazakhstan's capital on 10 December 1997 and is now Astana (UNVERIFIED snippet, Wikipedia "Astana").
- **Defining feature:** a single national objective (grain) imposed on a dry, wind-exposed steppe at speed, with people and machines shipped in before housing, storage and roads existed.

### B. What else exists there
- **Regional super-government [K1, P7]:** Tselinny Krai (Virgin Lands Territory), 26 Dec 1960 to 19 Oct 1965, capital Tselinograd, joining five oblasts (Kokchetav, Kustanai, Pavlodar, North Kazakhstan, Tselinograd); 21% of the Kazakh SSR's land and 31% of its population. Dissolved about a year after Khrushchev's removal. SOURCED: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tselinny_Krai.
- **Research institution built to manage the one big problem [K1, K14, P8]:** the grain research institute at Shortandy, about 60 km from Akmolinsk, founded 14 Feb 1956 (USSR Council of Ministers resolution No. 253) on an existing experimental station; raised to an All-Union institute in 1961; directed by Alexander Barayev 1957 to 1985. It developed the soil-protective (anti-erosion) farming system after wind erosion hit "millions of hectares" in the early 1960s: Order of the Red Banner of Labor 1967 for tillage methods; Lenin Prize 1972; USSR State Prize 1988 for anti-erosion machinery; 200+ varieties submitted for state testing; now the A.I. Barayev Scientific and Production Center of Grain Farming, part of the S. Seifullin Kazakh Agrotechnical University since 2020. SOURCED: baraev.kz/about/1790-history-of-development.html. Barayev's 1956 visit to Canada and the stubble-mulch idea: UNVERIFIED (search snippet, baraev.kz news page).
- **Housing shortfall [K4, K9]:** state-farm workforce grew by 322,000 net (1954 to 1957) while only 1.8 million m² of housing was built (Wikipedia, SOURCED). Early settlers lived in "tents with holes, sheds and barracks"; one group of 300 lived in a former stable (Novaya Gazeta, R. Khasanov, "Ostorozhno, khleb idet!", 4 Mar 2021, SOURCED). Dormitories described as cramped, low-ceilinged, six workers to a room with two folding beds, men sleeping on benches (Qalam, 24 Nov 2024, citing period complaints, SOURCED).
- **Storage and transport gap [K5]:** only 10 million t of grain storage in Kazakhstan by 1960 (Wikipedia, SOURCED); a specialist recalled "up to 50% of grain" lost on some farms for lack of workers, equipment and storage (Novaya Gazeta, SOURCED).
- **Today's economy [K5, K8]:** Akmola region sowed 5.5 million ha in 2024 with a grain forecast above 7 million t (UNVERIFIED snippet, Astana Times / gov.kz). In the May 2024 to Sept 2025 marketing year 6.5 million t of grain were shipped from Akmola region by rail (+91%), 5.2 million t exported, "almost half of the total grain shipments nationwide" (railways.kz news 2025, SOURCED).
- **Who lives there [P9]:** by 1961/62 ethnic Kazakhs were 29% of the northern population after about two million settlers arrived (Novaya Gazeta, SOURCED as the article's claim). Kostanay region 1954 to 1957: over 150,000 people of 40 nationalities and over 100 new state farms and settlements (Novaya Gazeta, SOURCED). Germans in Kazakhstan, mostly descendants of Volga Germans deported in 1941: 659,751 (1959), 957,518 (1989), 353,441 (1999), 178,409 (2009), 226,092 (2021); 63.1% fall 1989 to 1999 through emigration (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans_of_Kazakhstan, SOURCED).

### C. Cultural locations
- **Palace of the Virgin Land Workers** (Dvorets Tselinnikov), Kenesary St 32, Astana: built 1961 to 1963 by Riga architects Peteris Fogels, Olgerts Krauklis, Daine Danneberg on the site of the 1950s "Rodina" cinema; opened 6 Nov 1963; originally about 2,500 seats; rebuilt 1998 as Congress Hall; renamed Concert Hall Astana in 2016; now about 1,600 seats. SOURCED: andreas-praefcke.de/carthalia (Carthalia theater database). The idea arose after Khrushchev's 1961 visit found no suitable hall; 2,340-seat hall and 150-seat cafe; party meetings, concerts, film screenings, New Year events; listed as a republican monument 1982 (UNVERIFIED, search snippet of inform.kz; page returned 403).
- **Other 1960s civic buildings:** a Palace of Youth, a House of Soviets, a new airport, several sports venues, and the first three high-rise housing districts begun 1963 (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Astana, SOURCED).
- **Monument to the first Virgin Land workers**, Astana, remembered as a meeting point; a monument near the museum at Barayev Street and Republic Avenue (sxodim.com nostalgia article, SOURCED; exact names and dates NOT FOUND).
- **ALZhIR Museum-Memorial Complex**, Malinovka (now Akmol/Tonkeris), southwest of Astana [K9]: a separate, earlier history in the same region. The Akmolinsk camp for wives of "traitors to the Motherland," established August 1937, closed early 1950; over 18,000 women held at some point, about 8,000 served full sentences; memorial opened 31 May 2007; includes a monument to ethnic Korean women inmates. SOURCED: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALZhIR. Exhibits: a transport wagon, replica guard post, prisoners' photos and possessions (UNVERIFIED snippet, Lonely Planet).
- **Small and temporary cultural life [K10, K13]:** evening singing in a small trailer with accordion and guitar (Novaya Gazeta settler memoir, SOURCED). In a 1956 MSU student harvest brigade, a faculty member produced a wall newspaper of brigade news on drawing paper, and a graduate student shot a film of the brigade; in 1957, football, volleyball, birthday celebrations with wreaths of field flowers, after-work net fishing in flood ponds and fish soup and talk at the campfire; a closing rally at the farm's central estate where returning students received the state medal "For the Development of Virgin and Fallow Lands" (N. N. Ugarova, "My byli na tseline," memoir in MSU Chemistry Faculty collection, 2026, chem.msu.ru/rus/history/tselina/003-Ugarova.pdf, SOURCED).

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- **Single objective, so the harvest set the calendar [K7]:** shifts "from 6 AM to 8 AM the next day," "we worked around the clock" (Novaya Gazeta, SOURCED); tractor drivers 10 to 12 hours, some shifts 24 hours or more (Qalam, SOURCED).
- **Labor imported seasonally [K4, K7, K14]:** 1956 MSU brigade of about 30 to 40 traveled almost two weeks by freight train, fed army field-kitchen meals at sidings; lived in two construction trailers (one for women, one for men) at a field camp; nearest well 40 km away so water canisters had to be watched; work on the threshing floor: women raked grain to the winnowers, men unloaded combine trucks and loaded cleaned grain for the state procurement depot, often to late evening. 1957 brigade (Kustanai region, near the RSFSR's Kurgan oblast) slept two months on hay inside a large army tent; meals cooked by two on-duty students; one student bought food by farm truck in Kurgan city because village shops had none; a rival brigade fed itself on 63 kopecks per person per day; work included replastering cowsheds with a clay and manure mix (Ugarova memoir, SOURCED).
- **Farm layout [P5]:** a state farm had a central estate (administrative buildings) and outlying branches (otdeleniya), with field camps in the steppe; a branch foreman visited on horseback to assign and accept work (Ugarova memoir, SOURCED).
- **Erosion crisis produced its own institution [K1, K9]:** spring 1960 dust storms hit over 4 million ha of spring crops; in 1963 about 20 million ha of cultivated reclaimed land in Kazakhstan was affected (UNVERIFIED, search snippet citing an article; the source PDF was not read). The Barayev institute's anti-erosion system was the response (SOURCED, above). A 20 million t grain purchase from Canada in 1963 (UNVERIFIED snippet; figure not confirmed on a read page).
- **After 1991 [K9]:** Soviet funding ended, diesel deliveries and paychecks stopped; about 34.6 million acres of Kazakh farmland went fallow for about 20 years (National Geographic, SOURCED; this article's case-study farm, Chilinka, is in southeast Kazakhstan, not the Virgin Lands zone, so only the national figure is recorded).

### E. Structural features
- **Political friction over who the region belonged to [K9]:** in June 1979 (16 June per Wikipedia snippet, UNVERIFIED) young Kazakhs in Tselinograd and Atbasar protested a central plan for a German autonomous region in northern Kazakhstan; the plan was dropped; participants were not formally prosecuted but were blocked from university entry (e-history.kz, SOURCED). Wikipedia places the proposed center at Ereymentau (SOURCED).
- **Ethnic friction in the campaign years [K9]:** "ethnic and racial friction and even pogroms" (soviethistory.msu.edu, SOURCED; no specific incidents named on that page).
- **Pay and supply [K8]:** mechanics complained of being paid in grain contaminated with impurities (Qalam, SOURCED).
- **K6, rules of everyday behavior:** none found as a formal code; Komsomol brigade organization (elected commander, duty rosters, socialist competition between pairs) is documented in the Ugarova memoir (SOURCED). Alcohol or curfew rules: NOT FOUND.
- **K11 (very loose):** Russian was the working language of the farms and brigades; Kazakh, German, Ukrainian and the languages of deported peoples were present (inferred from the populations above; no source on language use was read). The 1957 central estate is described by the memoirist as otherwise a Kazakh village (SOURCED).
- **K12:** brigade food from cereals and tinned goods, no refrigeration; sunflower oil, eggs, tomato paste, vegetables, melons bought in town; fish soup from floodplain ponds (Ugarova, SOURCED). A Kazakh farm (1956, actually an orchard farm) gave each student two crates of Aport apples (SOURCED).

## Pick 2 · Beidahuang state farms, Heilongjiang ("Great Northern Wilderness")

**Claim to test:** "Reclaimed cold wetland turned into a national grain base by demobilized soldiers and youth sent from across the country (K7, K14)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and widened in three ways.** (1) The workforce came in at least four named streams (soldiers, college graduates, inland "frontier-support" youth, urban educated youth), plus political exiles sent to army farms and labor-reform camps in the same region. (2) The reclamation was also a military border project (the 1968 to 1976 Production and Construction Corps faced the Soviet border). (3) The cost is measured: about three quarters of the Sanjiang Plain's wetlands were lost 1950 to 2000. Data caution: several machine summaries mistranslated Chinese 万 and 亿 units; the figures below were checked against the Chinese text.

### A. Snapshot
- **Where and what:** the reclamation region of northern and eastern Heilongjiang, including the Sanjiang (Three Rivers) Plain where the Songhua, Heilong (Amur) and Ussuri meet, bordering Russia; formerly "trackless swamps" and brush; 57,600 km² of state farms by the 2007 article's figure (Beijing Review, 2007-06-13, bjreview.com/special/2007-06/13/content_66443.htm, SOURCED).
- **Timeline [K1, P7]:** 1947, Army cadres from Yan'an and Nanniwan founded the first state farms; 1958, 100,000 (10万) demobilized officers and soldiers moved in, starting the large-scale phase (rmzxw.com.cn, 2018-09-30, SOURCED; the 24 Jan 1958 directive date is UNVERIFIED, search snippet). June 1968, the Heilongjiang Production and Construction Corps was formed under the Shenyang Military Region, for reclamation and border reinforcement (Pan Mingxiao/Michel Bonnin, "The Lost Generation," ch. 5, aisixiang.com/data/99813.html, SOURCED). 1976, Heilongjiang Agricultural Reclamation Bureau (UNVERIFIED snippet). 2018, renamed Beidahuang Agricultural Reclamation Group; 2019, its management bureaus became branch companies and administrative duties passed to local governments (Wikipedia "Jiansanjiang," SOURCED; search snippet for 2018 date).
- **Who came [P9]:** 140,000 (14万) demobilized officers and soldiers, 100,000 (10万) college graduates, 200,000 (20万) inland frontier-support youth, 540,000 (54万) urban educated youth (12371.cn, 2021-04-08, SOURCED). In May to June 1968 the Shenyang Military Region moved about 260,000 (26万) youth to Heilongjiang and 55,000 to Jilin, which required two special trains a day for five months; only youth of "good" or ordinary family background were accepted (Pan Mingxiao ch. 5, SOURCED; the machine summary misread 26万 as 26,000, corrected here). Many came from Beijing and Shanghai (Beijing Review, SOURCED). About 345,000 educated youth sent to Heilongjiang in 1968 (UNVERIFIED snippet, China Daily 2015; conflicts in scope with the 260,000 figure; both recorded).
- **Defining feature:** a cold, flooded, sparsely settled plain drained and plowed by people sent there by state assignment, then turned into a mechanized grain base run for decades as a self-contained state system.

### B. What else exists there
- **Output [K8]:** starting grain output 0.048亿斤 (4.8 million jin, about 2,400 t); 100亿斤 in 1995, 200亿斤 in 2005, 300亿斤 in 2009, 400亿斤 in 2011, 421亿斤 in 2017 (rmzxw.com.cn, SOURCED; 400亿斤 = about 20 million t, conversion mine). Stable above 400亿斤 for ten years, enough for 160 million people's annual grain (12371.cn, SOURCED). Cultivated area over 43 million mu (about 2.9 million ha) (rmzxw, SOURCED). Group manages 3 million ha; about 23 million t in 2021 (UNVERIFIED snippet, China Daily).
- **Mechanization [K14]:** 35,000 harvest machines (2018); field mechanization over 99% (2017) (rmzxw, SOURCED). Baoquanling farm: 11,000 machine sets on 580,000 mu, 99.9% mechanized (12371.cn, SOURCED).
- **Self-contained society [K1, K4, P7]:** the farms historically ran their own public administration, procuratorate, courts, culture, education, health, transport, telecom, poverty relief and aid (Li Yining, Caijing, 2015-12-15, via aisixiang.com/data/95138.html, SOURCED). National policy set the end of 2018 for state enterprises to hand hospitals and schools to local authorities (UNVERIFIED snippet, Yahoo/SCMP).
- **Workforce makeup [K4, K7]:** formal workers, temporary workers and migrant laborers, and farmers and their families living inside farm territory; nearby farmers allowed to farm small plots inside state farms, the origin of the "family farm" arrangement (Li Yining, SOURCED). A Suihua man moved to Qixing Farm in 1995 and rented 24 more hectares of rice land in his second year (UNVERIFIED snippet, China Daily 2020).
- **A farm town [P5]:** Jiansanjiang, an agricultural reclamation district under the Group across Fujin, Tongjiang, Fuyuan and Raohe counties: about 270,000 residents, 15 state farms, about 8 million t of food a year; airport opened Oct 2017; rail to Harbin via Jiamusi; China Agricultural University rice experimental station (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiansanjiang, SOURCED). Jiansanjiang branch has over 660,000 ha of rice; harvest begins late September; 21,000 machines prepared in 2021 (China Daily, 2021-09-22, SOURCED). Qixing Farm: 1.22 million mu, 1.05 million mu of it paddy (rmzxw, SOURCED).
- **Logistics [K5]:** NOT FOUND in detail this round (rail via Jiamusi and the 2017 airport are the only items read).

### C. Cultural locations
- **Beidahuang Museum** (北大荒博物馆), 175 Hongqi Street, Xiangfang District, Harbin: built by the provincial Land Reclamation Bureau; photographs, dioramas, a large sand table, bronze sculptures, reliefs; a pine-wood wall running through two floors carved with more than 12,000 names of pioneers who died in or gave their lives to the reclamation (rmzxw.com.cn, SOURCED for the wall; other details UNVERIFIED snippet, ctrip). Opening year NOT FOUND.
- **Beidahuang Development and Construction Memorial Hall** (北大荒开发建设纪念馆), Dangbi town, Mishan, on Xingkai (Khanka) Lake; statue of General Wang Zhen in front; free entry, closed Mondays, 8:00 to 16:00 (UNVERIFIED, search snippets).
- **Beidahuang school of printmaking [K13]:** begun at the end of the 1950s by amateur artists among the demobilized soldiers, with an army cultural section; first-generation artists Chao Mei, Zhang Zuoliang, Hao Boyi (UNVERIFIED snippets, Ashmolean, China Daily 2012). Hao Boyi came in 1958 with the army, self-taught, with woodworking skills; he ran the Art Creation Class from 1971 to 1991, which trained sent-down youth; in the 1980s it moved from oil-printed woodcut to screenprint and experimental methods; the group won the 1983 Creative Collective Award of "Printmaking World" magazine (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hao_Boyi, SOURCED).
- **Xingkaihu labor-reform complex [K9]:** in 1958 Wang Zhen proposed army farms in Beidahuang; they became a destination for Beijing intellectuals labeled "rightists"; Xingkaihu labor camp was "the most notorious labor reform complex established by the Beijing police authority" (Ning Wang, "Banished to the Great Northern Wilderness," UBC Press/Cornell, 2017; publisher page SOURCED for the book's scope; Xingkaihu quote UNVERIFIED snippet). Pan Mingxiao notes many Corps farms were built on former labor camps (SOURCED).
- **Hezhe (Nanai) people [K9, K11]:** the Indigenous fishing people of the Sanjiang Plain; 5,354 in 2010 (4,245 in 1990); Hezhe ethnic townships at Jiejinkou, Bacha and Sipai; winter ice fishing with spears; fish-skin clothing; language critically endangered, under 50 speakers, none of them children (factsanddetails.com, SOURCED). How reclamation affected them specifically: NOT FOUND.

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- **Wetland converted, wetland lost [K9]:** 73.6% of the Small Sanjiang Plain's wetlands lost to agricultural development 1950 to 2000; consequences: waterfowl and plant decline, larger water-level swings, drainage cutting river-floodplain links, more flooding (Liu Hongyu et al., Ambio 33(6), 2004, abstract SOURCED). In one sub-region wetland fell from 64.83% of the area (1954) to 16.22% (2000) while cultivated land rose from 0.68% to 39.38% (UNVERIFIED snippet).
- **Early conditions [K4]:** pioneers lived in makeshift huts and semi-underground dwellings (12371.cn / search snippets; names of hut types not copied here). Sowing used horse-drawn seed drills, four horses to a drill (12371.cn, SOURCED).
- **Corps losses [K8, K9]:** by 1973 the Corps system nationally had large accumulated losses and Heilongjiang was losing close to 2 billion yuan a year; after militarization cadre staffing doubled and only 8% of cadre posts managed production (Pan Mingxiao, SOURCED; the 11.1 billion yuan national figure depends on a unit the summary may have misread, so it is UNVERIFIED).
- **Deaths:** "around 50,000 young people lost their lives" (UNVERIFIED, search snippet; source not read).
- **Official ethos [K+ slogan]:** "arduous struggle, bold pioneering, serving the collective interest, selfless dedication" is the formal statement of the Beidahuang Spirit (12371.cn, SOURCED).

### E. Structural features
- **A state system that was also the government:** farms ran courts, police, schools, hospitals until the 2010s reforms (Li Yining, SOURCED); local governments took over from 2019 (Wikipedia, SOURCED).
- **Work rhythm [K7]:** rice harvest from late September (China Daily 2021, SOURCED). Season lengths and winter work: NOT FOUND this round.
- **K6:** recruitment screened by family background in 1968 (SOURCED). Everyday behavior rules: NOT FOUND.
- **K11 (very loose):** Mandarin with the Northeastern dialect group as the working language; settlers from Beijing, Shanghai and other provinces; Hezhe language near extinction (factsanddetails, SOURCED for Hezhe; the rest UNVERIFIED general).
- **K12:** NOT FOUND this round.

## Pick 3 · FELDA land settlement schemes, Malaysia

**Claim to test:** "Planned agricultural settlements where settlers from many places were given land, housing and a collective organization (K1, K4, K8)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with corrections.** (1) Settlers did come from many places, but selection targeted the landless rural poor, overwhelmingly Malay; mixed-ethnicity schemes existed mainly among the earliest (Lurah Bilut, 1959). (2) Land, a pre-built house on a quarter-acre village plot, and a scheme-level committee were given; but from 1985 new settlers got shares in the land rather than titles, and the land and house were a debt repaid over about 15 years. (3) The "collective organization" was a supervised state structure (scheme committee, block system, settler cooperatives, a national settler investment cooperative), and the scheme's long-run problem became inheritance and the second generation, because the 1960 land law keeps each holding undivided. Care note honored: structure only.

### A. Snapshot
- **What it is [K1, P7]:** Federal Land Development Authority, established 1 July 1956 under the Land Development Ordinance, "to handle the resettlement of rural poor into newly developed areas" and organize smallholdings for cash crops (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Land_Development_Authority, SOURCED). Settlers' Day (Hari Peneroka) is 7 July each year, a date set by founder Tun Abdul Razak (UNVERIFIED, Sinar Harian snippet).
- **First schemes:** Air Lanas, Kelantan, March 1957, 400 settlers on 1,680 ha (The Edge Malaysia, "Cover story: Evolution of Felda," 2015 content, SOURCED). Lurah Bilut, 19 km from Bentong, Pahang: 12,920 acres (5,228 ha); first batch arrived by bus from Datuk Keramat, Kuala Lumpur, on 2 Aug 1959; 10 acres (4 ha) each; Malays, Chinese, Indians and Orang Asli (Wong Chun Wai, "GE13: Felda scheme with a potent voice," April 2013, SOURCED). In 2015 Lurah Bilut had over 2,000 residents: 60% Malay, 30% Chinese, 10% Indian and others (The Star, 2015-07-27, SOURCED).
- **Scale:** more than 112,000 settlers resettled 1958 to 1990; Pahang the largest concentration (43,000); no new schemes or settlers after 1990; last scheme Sahabat II, Lahad Datu, Sabah (Wikipedia, SOURCED). By 1990, 114,400 households on about 500,000 ha (The Edge, SOURCED). March 1989: 113,425 settler families, an estimated 680,000 people in 314 projects, two thirds of them second generation; 195 oil palm schemes (71,494 settlers, 63%), 116 rubber (41,374, 36.5%), sugarcane and cocoa the rest (Addnan Din, FELDA Institute of Land Development, Environmental Science Hokkaido Univ. 12(2), 1989, SOURCED, read in full text).
- **Defining feature:** a state-built, state-supervised rural society of standardized holdings, houses and institutions, placed on cleared jungle, all keyed to one tree crop.

### B. What else exists there
- **Land and house [K4]:** 10, 12 or 14 acres (4.0 to 5.7 ha) for the crop; a 0.25-acre residential plot in a planned village with a house built by FELDA; settlers had to live on the scheme (Wikipedia, SOURCED).
- **Selection [K6]:** citizen, married, physically fit, landless or with under 2 acres; age 18 to 40 in the 1985 FAO document (FAO, "Federal Land Development Authority (FELDA)," fao.org/4/ac063e/AC063E10.htm, data to March 1985, SOURCED); 21 to 50 per Wikipedia (SOURCED). The two sources' age ranges differ (likely different periods).
- **Money [K8]:** subsistence during establishment through loans; repayment normally 15 years; from 1 Jan 1985 settlers got shares in the land instead of titles; 1984 monthly income on 4.0 ha oil palm M$1,437.49 vs. 4.9 ha rubber M$725.85; 235 projects on 655,828 ha, 19,000+ staff, 38 palm oil mills with 13 more being built (FAO 1985, SOURCED). Koperasi Permodalan FELDA (KPF), settlers' savings and investment cooperative, founded 1980 (UNVERIFIED snippet, kpf.my).
- **Corporate arm [K8, K9]:** FGV listed 28 June 2012 in a RM9.93 billion IPO, second-largest IPO worldwide that year; controlled 500,000 ha managed by 112,600 settlers (The Edge, SOURCED); FGV now operates 811,140 ha of oil palm (Wikipedia, SOURCED).
- **Institutions on a scheme [K1, P7]:** planned villages with schools, medical centers, places of worship, water and electricity (Wikipedia, SOURCED). A primary school planned for every project, a secondary school for a group of projects, and a kindergarten expected in every settled project (Din 1989, SOURCED). 2010 plan for "model mosques" with kindergartens, clinics and libraries (The Star, 2010-08-22, SOURCED). Scheme committee JKKR (Jawatankuasa Kemajuan dan Keselamatan Rancangan, scheme development and security committee) handles development, welfare and security; FELDA moved in 2024 to restore JKKR in all 317 schemes, where state-appointed village committees (JKKK) already existed; Pahang's chief minister objected to "two heads" (Utusan Malaysia, 2024-01-24, SOURCED). Vice-chairs were formerly elected block by block (UNVERIFIED snippet).
- **Who lives there now [P9, K9]:** 112,635 settlers in 317 schemes; 69% aged 61 to 79; only 4,008 (3.4%) aged 25 to 45 (The Star, 2019-04-11, citing the 2019 white paper, SOURCED).
- **Labor [K7]:** settlers' plots increasingly harvested by foreign (mostly Indonesian) workers; pandemic shortages cost about 80,000 settlers RM264 million a month (UNVERIFIED snippet, 2021). Oil palm harvest rounds: two per month, 15 to 20 days per round (UNVERIFIED snippet). Until Sept 2016 settlers received two payments per harvest cycle; income fell from RM3,000 to 4,000 to RM1,800 to 2,000 a month; RM300 to 400 monthly deductions for replanting loans; at Felda Gelingi 300 of 360 settlers took their land back to self-manage (The Malaysian Insight, 2017-06-14, SOURCED).

### C. Cultural locations

## Pick 4 · Snowy Mountains Scheme and Cooma, Australia

**Claim to test:** "A water-and-power megaproject in hostile mountains built by tens of thousands of workers from dozens of nations with no shared inheritance (K4, K10, K14)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with sharpening.** The numbers are larger than "tens of thousands": over 100,000 people from over 30 countries across 1949 to 1974, about two thirds migrants, peak workforce about 7,300 in 1959. "No shared inheritance" is right in the sense of many origins, but a large share were people displaced by the Second World War who came on two-year directed-labor contracts, and some old enmities persisted (Serbs and Croats). The scheme is both power and water: it diverts about 2,100 GL a year inland to the Murray and Murrumbidgee for irrigation, so it is upstream of pick 5.

### A. Snapshot
- **What it is [K1, K14]:** 16 major dams, 7 original power stations (Guthega 1955 to Tumut 3 1973), 4.073 GW, 225 km of tunnels, pipelines and aqueducts, 1,600 km of roads and tracks; construction began 17 Oct 1949, finished 1974; cost A$820 million; 121 official deaths; National Heritage List 2016; diverts about 2,100 GL/yr to the Murray-Darling Basin (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowy_Mountains_scheme, SOURCED). Chief engineer Sir William Hudson, 1949 to 1967 (SOURCED). Today run by Snowy Hydro Limited, owned by the federal government; Snowy 2.0 pumped storage announced 2017 (SOURCED).
- **Cooma:** on Ngarigo land; gazetted 1849; became the scheme's headquarters in 1949 and is still the operating company's headquarters; population 2,330 (1911), peak 9,103 (1966), 6,715 (2021); 800 m elevation, in a rain shadow, 541.6 mm rain a year, frequent frost (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooma, SOURCED). Railway from Sydney reached Cooma 1889, closed to passengers 1989; Cooma–Snowy Mountains Airport 15 km out (SOURCED).
- **Defining feature:** a single national engineering authority built a network of camps and towns in high, snowy, roadless country and filled them with recruited labor from dozens of countries.

### B. What else exists there
- **Workforce [P9, K14]:** over 100,000 from over 30 countries; about 65% migrants; up to 7,000 on site at once (SBS News, SOURCED; Wikipedia SOURCED). Seventy percent of workers came from Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Britain, Norway, Poland and the former Yugoslavia (SBS, SOURCED). Peak 7,300 in 1959 (UNVERIFIED snippet). "As many as 70 countries" in one review (Dictionary of Sydney blog on McHugh, SOURCED; figures vary by source).
- **How migrants were channeled [K4, K7]:** displaced persons arriving 1947 to 1953 on assisted passage had to work two years in a job chosen by the government (directed-labor contract under agreements with the International Refugee Organization); Bonegilla reception center near Albury–Wodonga, opened 1947, processed more than 300,000 migrants by 1971; families were often split, men sent to remote work while wives and children stayed in hostels (UNVERIFIED, extended-search summary of Wikipedia/NAA/Find & Connect; pages not opened this round).
- **Contractors and records [K14]:** in 1954 the US group Kaiser-Walsh-Perini-Raymond won the 22.5 km Eucumbene-Tumut tunnel and Tumut Pond dam; Thiess Bros set a hard-rock tunneling world record of 165 m in a week in 1963 (UNVERIFIED snippets, Allens/Thiess). Miners were paid bonuses for speed, which raised tunneling risk (University of Wollongong, 2019, on McHugh's research, SOURCED).
- **Camps and towns [K4]:** more than 100 camps and 8 townships (UNVERIFIED snippet, multiculturalaustralia.edu.au). Island Bend camp held 500 to 600 and closed in 1965 (UNVERIFIED snippet). Workers lived in tents or barracks; barrack rooms had a cupboard and heating pipes used to dry clothes, with a drying room and showers; camps were too far from towns to commute (NSW Department of Education, "Life in the SMA camps," SOURCED). One surveyor's assistant lived in a tent for his "first seven years straight" in investigation camps (UOW 2019, SOURCED).
- **Company town [K4, K1]:** Cabramurra, founded 1954 at 1,488 m, built for Tumut 1 and 2 workers, prefabricated houses trucked from Cooma (snippet); about 400 workers and families at first; one-classroom school, pub, store, petrol, library, indoor pool, a single ski lift; whole town moved 500 m and 20 m lower in 1974 into Besser-block houses with steep roofs ($3 million); residence only for Snowy Hydro employees and families; 37 residents in 2016; became drive-in/drive-out from 2018; January 2020 bushfires destroyed many houses, the school and the old ski club; snow 3 to 4 months a year, average 53.2 snow days (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabramurra, SOURCED). Khancoban, the other permanent company town (Wikipedia, SOURCED).

### C. Cultural locations

## Pick 5 · Murrumbidgee Irrigation Area and Griffith, Australia

**Claim to test:** "A planned irrigation town on dry plains that became a food region through migrants of many origins (K1, K12)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with a correction of sequence.** The state planned it (dam, canals, Griffin's town plans) and first settled it largely with returned First World War soldiers; about two thirds of soldier settlers in NSW schemes abandoned their holdings. Italian migrants (first mostly from Veneto, then from Calabria, Sicily and elsewhere) filled the gap and drove horticulture and wine; later Punjabi Sikh, Pacific Islander and other workers followed. The single system was later privatized to an irrigator-owned company (1999). The town also carries a documented organized-crime history (K9).

### A. Snapshot
- **What it is [K1]:** an irrigation district in the Riverina, NSW; planning began after an 1880s royal commission on water; canal works west of Narrandera and Burrinjuck Dam started 1903 to 1906; formally established 1912 when Burrinjuck was commissioned; expanded in the 1970s with the Snowy scheme's water and Blowering Dam; 660,000 ha irrigated (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrumbidgee_Irrigation_Area, SOURCED). A system of weirs, canals and holding ponds fed by upstream dams (SOURCED).
- **Griffith:** established 4 Aug 1916; plan by Walter Burley Griffin (1914), circular streets with radiating avenues (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffith,_New_South_Wales, SOURCED). Population 20,569 (2021). Ancestry: Australian 26.7%, English 25.7%, Italian 22.4%, Irish 7.0%, Indian 6.4%; languages at home: English 65.8%, Italian 5.2%, Gujarati 4.6%, Punjabi 4.4%; Catholic 34.9% (Wikipedia citing ABS 2021, SOURCED). "Approximately 60%" claim Italian heritage (Wikipedia, SOURCED). Sister city Treviso (SOURCED).
- **Defining feature:** a desert-edge plain turned productive by one engineered water system, settled first by plan and then by successive migrant groups who stayed.

### B. What else exists there
- **Water company [K1, K8]:** in 1999 the MIA and districts were separated from NSW government ownership; now Murrumbidgee Irrigation Ltd, an unlisted public company whose permanent shares are held by its irrigator customers; non-profit by constitution; A$500 million in assets, A$40 million annual turnover, 160 employees, servicing over A$2.5 billion in water entitlements; built Roach's Surge Reservoir near Yanco (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrumbidgee_Irrigation, SOURCED). Over 3,200 landholdings, 2,500 customers; offices in Griffith and Leeton (UNVERIFIED snippet).
- **Rice [K1, K8, K14]:** Ricegrowers' Co-operative founded 1950 by Riverina growers to build a mill; now SunRice, headquartered in Leeton; mills at Leeton, Deniliquin and Coleambally; 2,139 employees (April 2024); about 80% of the Australian crop exported in an average year; grain storage over one million tonnes (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SunRice, SOURCED). Leeton's largest employer (UNVERIFIED snippet).
- **Wine and food [K12]:** De Bortoli, Casella, McWilliam's, Warburn and Berton Vineyards among Australia's top 20 wine producers; citrus, rice and vegetables; Baiada poultry described as the Riverina's largest employer (Wikipedia Griffith, SOURCED).
- **Seasonal labor [K4, K7, K9]:** at least about 600 Pacific Australia Labor Mobility (PALM) workers in Griffith; NSW Anti-Slavery Commissioner James Cockayne's report "Be Our Guests" documented overwork, financial exploitation through rent deductions (up to $500 a week for a shared room), overcrowded housing, small-scale labor trafficking and sexual servitude, and recommended a federal inquiry and a "safe multicultural hub" in Griffith (The Area News, 2024-10-22, SOURCED). Griffith and Swan Hill were the first trial sites for Pacific seasonal workers in 2008 (UNVERIFIED snippet, NFF).

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- **Soldier settlement [K9]:** from 1917 irrigable land was reserved for discharged soldiers, many wounded or gassed, who camped at Bagtown and in barracks at Beelbangera and Yenda (UNVERIFIED, search snippet citing Farm Online). ## Pick 6 · Imperial Valley, California

**Claim to test:** "A desert made into a food-export region by one canal, whose single water source is its single point of failure (K1, K9)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and the history proves the failure mode twice.** (1) The first canal, routed through Mexico, broke in 1905 and the whole Colorado River ran into the Salton Sink for about two years, creating the Salton Sea and bankrupting the developer. (2) The replacement, the All-American Canal (1942), is described as the valley's only water source. The claim should be widened: the single source also ties the valley to the Colorado River's interstate allocations, to water transfers sold to coastal cities (2003), and to the Salton Sea, whose shrinking since 2018 exposes dust linked to children's reduced lung function. The labor force is mostly Mexican, much of it commuting daily from Mexicali.

### A. Snapshot
- **Where and what:** a valley in Imperial and Riverside counties, about 50 miles long, almost all below sea level (235 ft at the Salton Sea's edge), between the Colorado River, the Salton Sea and the Mexican border; summer temperatures well over 100°F, record 121°F (28 July 1995); about 2.93 in of rain a year (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Valley, SOURCED).
- **People [P9, K11]:** Imperial County 179,702 (2020): 85% Hispanic or Latino, 3.3% Black, 2.5% Native American, 2.1% Asian; about 65.7% speak Spanish as first language. El Centro 44,322, Calexico 38,633, Brawley 26,416, Imperial 18,631 (Wikipedia, SOURCED).
- **Economy:** agriculture is the largest industry, 48% of employment (Wikipedia, SOURCED). 2023: nearly $2.7 billion on 578,659 irrigated acres; top commodities cattle, alfalfa, leaf lettuce, head lettuce, spinach, onions, broccoli, bermuda grass hay, carrots, alfalfa seed (UNVERIFIED snippets; county PDF returned 404, IV Press 429). 2024: nearly $2.5 billion (down 7.57%); livestock $705.7 million; harvested acres down about 9% (KXO Radio, 2025-11-19, citing Agricultural Commissioner Jolene Dessert, SOURCED).
- **Defining feature:** an irrigated desert below sea level whose existence depends on one diverted river delivered by one canal.

### B. What else exists there
- **The water body [K1, P7]:** Imperial Irrigation District, founded 1911 (taking over from the bankrupt California Development Company), supplies about 500,000 acres with raw Colorado River water; elected five-member board; also California's third-largest public power utility, over 145,000 electricity customers, over 1,100 MW (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Irrigation_District, SOURCED). Under the 2003 Quantification Settlement Agreement IID transfers conserved water to the San Diego County Water Authority, Coachella Valley Water District and Metropolitan Water District (SOURCED).
- **The canal [K1, K9]:** All-American Canal, authorized 1928 with Hoover Dam, completed 1942, 82 miles, up to 26,155 cubic feet per second, built to avoid routing water through Mexico; "the Imperial Valley's only water source"; over 500 people drowned since completion, many of them migrants crossing; about 68,000 acre-feet a year seeped (about 90% into Mexico) until 37 km were lined, completed 2009; IID installed lifesaving buoys at 105 points and bilingual warning signs at 1,414 in 2011 (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-American_Canal, SOURCED). Lining dropped the northern Mexicali Valley water table after 2008 (UNVERIFIED snippet, Springer article).
- **Labor [K7, K4]:** winter vegetable harvest December to March (UNVERIFIED snippet, Arab News/AP 2017); about 90% of US winter vegetables in one 2014 claim (NBC News, SOURCED as the article's claim). Daily cross-border commute from Mexicali: rise about 12:30 a.m., reach the Calexico West port about 1 a.m., wait 3 to 4 hours, peak line at 4:30 a.m. fifteen people wide, two-hour bus to the fields, eight-hour shift from 8 a.m., 9 to 10 hours of commuting a day; about 1,000 in line at first and 8,000 to 10,000 joining; about $300 a week; employers named include Dole, Church Brothers, Tanimura & Antle (NBC News / Type Investigations, 2014-03-25, SOURCED). Many are green-card holders who live in Mexicali because housing is cheaper (UNVERIFIED snippet, AP 2017).

### C. Cultural locations
- **Holtville Carrot Festival [K2]:** Holtville (founded 1903 by W.F. Holt as Holton, incorporated 20 June 1908; 5,605 people in 2020) holds it in late January or early February with a parade and carnival over about ten days (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holtville,_California, SOURCED). Held since 1947 and timed to the carrot harvest (UNVERIFIED snippets; Wikipedia gives no start year). Imperial County has 25% of California's carrot acreage (2022) (UNVERIFIED snippet).
- **Brawley Cattle Call Rodeo [K2, K3]:** started 1957 by Brawley's Chamber of Commerce, first held on the Brawley High School football field (snippet); name chosen by contest (Mrs. Rex Hudson); a week-long run of events with a mixer, chili and rib cook-off, mariachi night, parade and rodeo, drawing visitors from southern California, Mexico, Nevada, Arizona and Texas (KYMA, 2023-11-09, SOURCED). Held each November; estimated 200,000 fans (UNVERIFIED snippet).
- **California Mid-Winter Fair & Fiesta**, Imperial: the county's largest annual event, ten days (UNVERIFIED snippet).
- **Rufino Contreras memorial [K9]:** on 10 Feb 1979, 22 days into a vegetable strike, striker Rufino Contreras, 28, was shot dead by foremen in a Mario Saikhon lettuce field while trying to speak to strikebreakers; memorialized annually (UNVERIFIED, search snippets of UFW and Imperial Valley Press; the UFW page returned 403).

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- **The first canal and the flood [K9]:** California Development Company founded 1896 by Charles Rockwood; engineer George Chaffey joined 1900; the Imperial Canal delivered water from 1901 through Mexican territory; Chaffey's Imperial Land Company promoted settlement; in 1905 the diversion failed and "the whole of the Colorado River poured into the Salton Sink, forming the Salton Sea," for about two years; Southern Pacific won a $1.28 million judgment; the company became insolvent; IID took over in 1911 (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Development_Company, SOURCED). Collapse date 9 Aug 1905 (UNVERIFIED snippet).
- **The Salton Sea now [K9]:** QSA transfers from 2003 reduced inflow; mitigation water (105,000 acre-feet) ran 2003 to 2017 and ended at the end of 2017, after which the shoreline retreated and exposed playa (UNVERIFIED, search snippets of PBS SoCal/Audubon/LAO). A UC Irvine-led study of nearly 500 children (about 1,300 lung-function tests, 2019 to 2022) linked dust from the receding lake to reduced lung function, worst near the shore and in low-income Latino neighborhoods; co-authors include Comite Civico del Valle (UC Irvine News, 2025-10-14, SOURCED).
- **Labor conflict [K9]:** 1928 cantaloupe strike (7 to 10 May) by Mexican pickers, whose union had 2,754 members, won a wage increase (UNVERIFIED snippet of Wikipedia). 1930 lettuce strike, 1 to 23 January, about 5,000 Mexican, Filipino, Chinese, Japanese, Punjabi, African American, Puerto Rican and white workers; demands included 50 cents an hour and an eight-hour day; contractors withheld 25% of wages; no demands met; an April 1930 raid in El Centro made 108 arrests and organizers were imprisoned under the Criminal Syndicalism Act; wages "as low as ten cents an hour" in the 1934 strike (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Valley_lettuce_strike_of_1930, SOURCED).
- **Mixed families [K4, K12]:** Punjabi men who could not bring wives and were barred from marrying white women married Mexican women who worked their fields; first marriages recorded 1916; children mostly raised Catholic and Spanish-speaking, with food drawn from both cuisines (Wikipedia "Punjabi Mexican Americans," SOURCED for law, food and religion; Imperial Valley share of about 250 couples is UNVERIFIED snippet citing Karen Leonard, "Making Ethnic Choices," 1992).

### E. Structural features

- **Border economy [K5, K7]:** the labor supply lives across the border and commutes daily (SOURCED).

- **K11 (very loose):** Spanish is the first language of about two thirds of county residents; English and Spanish signage on the canal (SOURCED).

### Verdicts on the six claims (one line each)
1. **Virgin Lands:** confirmed; the labor came in several streams (Komsomol recruits, specialists, seasonal student and driver brigades) on top of a deported population already in the region; arrivals mostly Ukrainian and Russian; one dedicated research institute (Shortandy) was created to solve the erosion the campaign caused.

3. **FELDA:** confirmed with corrections; settlers were selected landless rural poor, mostly Malay (mixed only in early schemes); land came as a debt and, after 1985, as shares; undividable holdings under the 1960 Act now block the second generation's housing.
4. **Snowy Mountains Scheme and Cooma:** confirmed; over 100,000 workers from over 30 countries, about two thirds migrants, many on two-year directed-labor contracts; 121 deaths; drowned and moved towns; a company town with an employees-only residence rule.
5. **MIA and Griffith:** confirmed with a sequence correction; planned for returned soldiers, two thirds of whom abandoned NSW settlement holdings; Italians, then Punjabi Sikhs and Pacific seasonal workers, made it a food region; the water system is now owned by its irrigators.
6. **Imperial Valley:** confirmed; the single canal failed catastrophically once (1905, creating the Salton Sea) and its successor is described as the valley's only water source; widened to water sales to coastal cities, a shrinking Salton Sea with health effects, and a labor force that commutes daily from Mexico.
