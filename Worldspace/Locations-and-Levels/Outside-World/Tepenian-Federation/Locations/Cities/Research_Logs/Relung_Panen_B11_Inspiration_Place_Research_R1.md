## 1 · Lake Suwa's omiwatari, Japan

**Claim to test:** "A shrine has recorded the lake's ice ridge for centuries, so the lake's freezing is a civic record (K2)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, WITH CORRECTIONS.** (a) It is **two** shrine institutions in sequence, not one: Suwa Taisha's *Tōsha Shinkōki* covers **1443–1681**, and Yatsurugi Shrine's *Miwatari-chō* and *Kojō Miwatari Chūshinroku* run from **1683 to the present**. (b) The record is first a **religious divination** (the ridge is the track of a god crossing; its line is read for the year's harvest and fortunes) and only secondarily a climate record. (c) It **is** civic in a literal sense: in the Edo period the finding was reported **to the shogunate through the domain**, and today it is reported **through Suwa Taisha to the Imperial Household Agency and to the Japan Meteorological Agency**. (d) Since **2019 no ridge has formed**; the 2026 season was the eighth year in a row with none, matching the longest gap in the record (1508–1515). Note: the care note says "Japan nation note"; recorded here as facts only.

### A. Snapshot
- **Lake Suwa**, central Nagano Prefecture, elevation **759 m**; area **13.3 km²**; average depth **4.7 m**, max **7.2 m**; shore **15.9 km**; fed by **31 small rivers**; it is the source of the **Tenryū River**. Shore municipalities: **Suwa** (city), **Okaya** (city), **Shimosuwa** (town). A natural hot spring lies under the lake. [K+ geography] [P3] — SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Suwa (read 2026-10-29).
- **Defining feature:** when the lake freezes fully, day/night expansion and contraction of the ice pushes up pressure ridges (**omiwatari**, "the god's crossing"), **30 cm or more** high. As of 2025 the lake "had failed to freeze over in 18 of the past 25 years." — SOURCED, same page.
- Mechanism as the Yatsurigi Shrine priest describes it: below **−10 °C for 3+ days** the lake freezes fully; later cold spells contract the ice, opening linear cracks that fill and refreeze as ridges. (tenki.jp gives "about −10 °C for about a week".) [K2] — SOURCED, weathernews.jp/news/202502/040205/ (2025-02); tenki.jp/suppl/m_seta/2026/01/16/32752.html (2026-01-16).
- **Population:** Suwa city **48,972** (1 March 2019 est.), area **109.17 km²**; Okaya **48,616** (1 Feb 2019 est.), **85.10 km²**; Okaya fell from **58,443 (1940)** to **47,790 (2020)**. — SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwa,_Nagano; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okaya,_Nagano (read 2026-10-29). Shimosuwa population NOT FOUND (not searched separately).

### The record and the rite (K2 core)
- **Legend:** the ridge is the path of **Takeminakata-no-Kami**, god of the **Upper Shrine (Kamisha)** of Suwa Taisha, crossing to visit **Yasakatome-no-Kami**, goddess of the **Lower Shrine (Shimosha)**. A related myth assigns the perpendicular ridge to a son of the god crossing to meet his father. [K2] [P6] — SOURCED, tenki.jp (2026-01-16); skima-shinshu.com/suwa-omiwatari1/ (read 2026-10-29).
- **Ridge classes:** the first south-to-north crack is the **Ichi-no-miwatari**; a later one in the same direction is the **Ni-no-miwatari**; a crack perpendicular to these is the **Saku-no-miwatari**. Per tenki.jp, all three must be confirmed before official recognition. japan.travel describes the usual pattern as "two from the southeast part of the lake to the north, and one from the southwest to the east." [K2] — SOURCED, tenki.jp; japan.travel/en/sports/snow/snow-travel/lake-suwa-omiwatari/ (read 2026-10-29).
- **Who judges:** the **chief priest of Yatsurugi Shrine** (in Suwa city) and **ujiko (parishioner) representatives**. Observation runs daily from about **5 January (Shōkan, "small cold") to about 3 February (Setsubun)**. In 2026 the priest and staff met at **6 a.m. on 3 February** at **−2 °C**; on **4 February** the priest declared **"Ake-no-umi"** (open water: no crossing this year). The priest measures water and air temperature daily. [K2, K1] [P7] — SOURCED, tenki.jp; greenpeace.org/japan/news/miwatari_observation_2026/ (2026-02); UNVERIFIED for the daily temperature measuring (search snippet citing imidas/Greenpeace).
- **Rites:** on recognition, an on-ice **Haikan-shiki** (viewing ceremony) confirms and purifies the ridges; the position, shape and direction of the cracks are read to divine **the year's harvest and the world's fortunes** (weathernews: "summer weather and harvest"). The finding then goes to Suwa Taisha. — SOURCED, tenki.jp; weathernews.jp; skima-shinshu.com.
- **Reporting chain (the civic part):** at the **Chūshin Hōkoku-shiki** (report ceremony) at **Suwa Taisha Upper Shrine, Honmiya**, on **17 Feb 2018**, about 30 people (the Yatsurugi chief priest, the chief parishioner representative, parishioner representatives, elders and district heads) formally presented a notice giving **date, starting point, ending point, ice thickness and temperature**; the documents then go to the **Imperial Household Agency** and the **Japan Meteorological Agency**. In the Edo period reports went **to the shogunate through the domain** (Takashima domain). [K1, K2] [P7] — SOURCED, shimin.co.jp/archives/2247 (Nagano Nippo / Shimin Shimbun, 2018-02); Edo-period reporting UNVERIFIED (extended-search summary, not read on a page).
- **Yatsurugi Shrine** originally stood on the site of **Takashima Castle** (built **1592**) and was moved when the castle was built; the Suwa lords of Takashima domain revered it as the castle's guardian shrine. Earliest record of the crossing rite claimed as **1397**. — SOURCED (single tourism-media source), skima-shinshu.com; the 1397 date is a single source and should be treated with care.
- **Record statistics:** first 250 years: only **3** years with no freeze; **1955–2004: 12** freeze-free years; **2005–2014: 5**; no freeze in **2015 or 2016**. Ridge years since 2000: **2003, 2004, 2006, 2008, 2012, 2013, 2018**. Timing drifted from early January (15th–17th c.) to late January or "Ake-no-umi" in the later 20th c. Gaps: 1508–1515 (reliability of that stretch questioned by weathernews); **1866–1869** (4 years). The record was used in a *Scientific Reports* study (2016; authors not read on the page) after translating rice-paper entries. — SOURCED, nationalgeographic.com (2016-04); tenki.jp; weathernews.jp.

### B. What else exists there
- **Silk to precision industry:** from **1868 to about 1930** Okaya was one of Japan's largest producers of export silk; a Suwa-style reeling machine dated **1875**. The region moved from silk reeling to wartime defense work to postwar **precision instruments** (watches, cameras). Mills on the lake were converted, reusing buildings, equipment and workforce. The region had a national nickname comparing it to a European watchmaking country (moved to the slang file under the language caution); the precision-industry founders chose it for its low summer humidity. **Seiko Epson** is headquartered in Suwa; Olympus and Kyocera operate in Okaya. [K14, K7] [P8] [SM:competence] — SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okaya,_Nagano; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwa,_Nagano; ginza-nagano.jp and Epson "80 year Journey" UNVERIFIED (snippet only).
- **Natural kanten (agar) — an industry made by the cold:** Suwa is described as the **only** producer of *kaku-kanten* (bar agar) in Japan; it uses hard frost, clear skies, large day/night swings and clean groundwater to freeze-dry agar in fields. Tradition says a merchant from Anayama village (now Chino) brought the method from Tanba around **1830** after seasonal work there; it developed as a **winter off-season side job for farmers**. In **2019–20, 10 producers** worked in Chino; a record warm winter stalled production ("quality concerns arise when products melt mid-process"); the trade reports **labor shortages and no successors**, manual work hard to mechanize. Note a conflict: the trade paper says "nearly 350 years" of tradition, the 1830 origin implies ~200. The prefecture holds an annual specialty products show with a kanten section (**101** entries judged 22 Oct 2021, which noted a very warm second half of the 2020 season); a **Nagano Prefecture Kanten Marine-Products Processing Cooperative** exists. [K7, K12, K14, K1, K9] [P8] — SOURCED, news.nissyoku.co.jp/news/original2020013103 (2020-01-31); pref.nagano.lg.jp r3kanten.pdf (2021); 1830 origin and "only producer" UNVERIFIED (snippet, Chino tourism / prefecture PDFs not opened).
- **Fishing:** the **Suwa Lake Fishery Cooperative** manages wakasagi (pond smelt) fishing, **mid-September to March**, now from heated, anchored **dome boats**; 2020 cost about **¥4,700** (boat + permit). Under-ice seining is said to have **originated at Lake Suwa** and spread to Hachirōgata in the Kansei era (1789–1801). [K1, K12] [R: shared warm shelter] — SOURCED (dome boats), honda.co.jp/fishing/news/news-20200121/ (2020-01-21); under-ice seine origin UNVERIFIED (snippet, Hokkaido research PDF).
- **Transport:** Kami-Suwa Station (JR Chūō Main Line), about 2 h 15 min from Shinjuku by limited express; Chūō Expressway, Route 20. Historically a castle town and post station on the Kōshū Kaidō. [K5] — SOURCED, Suwa Wikipedia; japan.travel.
- **Schools (Suwa city):** 7 public elementary, 4 public middle schools, 2 high schools, 1 combined middle/high. — SOURCED, Suwa Wikipedia.

### C. Cultural locations
- **Suwa Taisha** (four shrine buildings: Upper Shrine Honmiya and Maemiya; Lower Shrine Harumiya and Akimiya). **Yatsurugi Shrine** (Suwa city). **Tenaga Jinja**. — SOURCED, Lake Suwa Wikipedia; Onbashira Wikipedia.
- **Onbashira:** every **6 years** (Tiger and Monkey years); **16** fir trees, **17–19 m**, cut and dragged down mountains (*yamadashi*, spring), riders on logs of up to **12 t** sliding down slopes (*kiotoshi*); erected at the corners of the four shrine buildings (*satobiki*, May). Villages assigned by **lottery** (Upper Shrine) or **fixed assignment** (Lower Shrine) since **1890**; women officially allowed since **1950**; deaths in 1980, 1986, 1992 (two drowned), 2010 (two fell 10 m), 2016. Also nightly ~15-minute summer displays through August and the **All-Japan New Fireworks Competition** in early September. [K3, K8] — SOURCED, suwa-tourism.jp/gosuwa/?p=1458 (read 2026-10-29); organizer NOT FOUND.

### D. How the defining feature shaped things
- The freeze set the **winter calendar**: a daily dawn watch from Shōkan to Setsubun, a judgment by a named office (the Yatsurigi priest with parishioners), a ceremony on the ice, and a report up the chain of state. [K1, K2] [P7]

## 2 · The Elfstedentocht, Friesland

**Claim to test:** "A skating race held only when the ice allows, which a whole province waits for (K2, K3)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, WITH NARROWING.** (a) It is **both a race and a mass tour**: in 1997 about 16,000 tour skaters plus a separate race of about 300. (b) It is held **only** when natural ice is **≥15 cm along the whole ~200 km route** and all **22 district sections** sign off; it then starts within **48 hours**. (c) The waiting is **national, not only provincial**: a 2025 plan expects about **2 million spectators** and **3,500 journalists**. (d) It has been held **15 times** (1909–1997) and **not since 4 January 1997**. (e) As to K3: the original tour is not a festival made on purpose, but it has spawned **made-on-purpose substitutes** (an annual "alternative" race on an Austrian lake since 1989, an annual cycling tour since 1947, a 2019 charity swim).

### A. Snapshot
- **Route:** nearly **200 km**, a loop on frozen canals, rivers and lakes through **11 Frisian towns**: Leeuwarden (start/finish), Sneek, IJlst, Sloten, Stavoren, Hindeloopen, Workum, Bolsward, Harlingen, Franeker, Dokkum. Skaters collect a **stamp in each town and at three secret checkpoints** and must finish **before midnight**. [K2] [P5] — SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elfstedentocht (read 2026-10-29).
- **Organizer:** the **Koninklijke Vereniging De Friesche Elf Steden** (Royal Association of the Eleven Frisian Towns), Leeuwarden; set up after the first race of **1909**; **over 32,000 members**; a **10-person board**, district heads and assistant district heads, many volunteers; general meeting **every year on the second Saturday of December**. Stated aim: "to promote the sport of ice skating, particularly by organising the Elfstedentocht on ice, if possible, annually." [K1] [P7] — SOURCED, elfstedentocht.frl/en/the-association (read 2026-10-29). One full-time staff member — SOURCED, nos.nl/l/2550530 (2025-01-04).
- **Origins:** mentions of skaters visiting all eleven towns in a day **since 1760**; the organized tour conceived by **Pim Mulier** in **1890**; first official tour **2 January 1909**. — SOURCED, Wikipedia; 1909 date also the Schaatsmuseum listing (UNVERIFIED snippet).
- **Province:** Friesland, about **660,000** inhabitants (Jan 2023), **5,753 km²** including water; capital **Leeuwarden** (~123,000). Agricultural (Frisian cattle, Frisian horse), lake and Wadden tourism; **195** of the Netherlands' ~1,200 windmills. Religion (2015): 57.2% none, 28.5% Protestant, 6.6% Catholic. [K+] [P3] — SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friesland (read 2026-10-29; first 100,000 characters only).

### B. What else exists there (the institution's machinery)
- **District heads:** since **2006** the route is cut into **22 sections**; **48 section managers** in all (one head plus one or two assistants each). Tasks: report ice to the board, set up emergency facilities, coordinate with local authorities, ensure a safe ice route, watch the participants, report the lead group's passage, ensure safe passage among the public. They measure ice thickness and arrange stamp posts and walking detours around bad stretches. They rarely meet as a body (last in January 1997, per one spokesperson). [K1, K14] [P7] [SM:competence under pressure] — SOURCED (tasks, 48), elfstedentocht.frl/en/rayonmanagers; 2006 date, ice measuring and the 1997 meeting UNVERIFIED (search snippets).
- **Standing readiness (2025):** **~€400,000 a year** spent keeping ready; "By December 1st, everything must be completely up-to-date"; plan for **30,000 eligible participants (25,000 expected starters)**, transponders and an app; the regional safety authority (**Veiligheidsregio Fryslân**) treats it as a **GRIP-4** event (top coordination level) across police, fire and hospitals. A meteorologist: roughly **two weeks of serious frost** makes 15 cm. [K1, K8, K5] — SOURCED, nos.nl/l/2550530 (2025-01-04).

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- An event that **may happen any winter and usually does not** produced a **permanent organization with nothing to run**: 32,000 members, a yearly meeting, a budget, and a December 1 readiness deadline kept for 28 years. [K1, K8] [P7]
- The ice-dependence produced a **competence community** (district heads, ice masters, village ice clubs) whose skill can only be learned on natural ice, and is now being sent abroad to learn it. [K14, K9]
- Its absence produced **substitutes**: the **Alternatieve Elfstedentocht Weissensee** (Austria) every year since **1989**, organized by **Stichting Wintersporten** (Valkenswaard; membership required, **€30/yr**), **4,500+** skaters and about **8,000** Dutch visitors; a cycling Elfstedentocht yearly since **1947** (skipped 2001, 2020); in **2019** a swimmer covered the route raising over **€6.1 million** for cancer research. [K3] — SOURCED (1989, 1947, 2019 swim), Wikipedia; Weissensee organizer, fee and numbers UNVERIFIED (snippets of weissensee.nl and lawinsider pages, not opened).

## 3 · Aral Sea and Moynaq

**Claim to test:** "What happens to a place when its water is taken for distant farming (K9)."

## 4 · Owens Valley and the Los Angeles Aqueduct

**Claim to test:** "A farming valley whose water was taken by a distant city, with lasting friction (K9)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, AND THE FRICTION IS CURRENT.** Los Angeles bought land and water rights from **1905**, opened the **233-mile** aqueduct in **November 1913**, and by **1933** owned about **85%** of the valley's residential and commercial property and **95%** of its farm and ranch land. Farmers seized and dynamited the aqueduct in the **1920s**; Owens Lake was dry by **1924–1926** (sources differ). Friction continues: lawsuits from **1972**, a **1991** water agreement, re-watering of the lower river in **2006**, a **$2.1–2.5 billion** dust-control program on the dry lake, and (2024) a city utility acting as the **landlord** of most private land in the county, with lease rules that stop businesses from being sold. Narrowing: the water was first taken from **Paiute** irrigators by settlers, before the city took it from the settlers; the Paiute reservations' water rights are **still unrecognized**.

### A. Snapshot
- **Owens Valley**, eastern California: about **75 mi** north–south, floor about **4,300 ft**, between the Sierra Nevada and the White/Inyo Mountains; strong rain shadow. Towns: **Bishop, Big Pine, Independence, Lone Pine**; about **25,000** people; U.S. Route 395 is the spine. The Owens Valley Paiute name *Payahǖǖnadǖ* is glossed "place of flowing water." [K+] [P3] — SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owens_Valley (read 2026-10-29).
- **Water share:** Wikipedia: the valley supplies "one-third of the water for the city." The Guardian (2024): LADWP's holdings provide about **73%** of LA's water (**395,000 acre-feet a year**). Different measures or years; not reconciled. — SOURCED.

### Timeline (the taking)
- **1902** Reclamation Act; federal irrigation project in the valley considered, then dropped as LA's aqueduct plan was approved in **1905**; construction began **1905**. **1913** aqueduct completed, diverting the Owens River about **480 cfs**. **1924** Owens Lake a dry lakebed (Inyo chronology; Wikipedia says completely empty by **1926**). **1924–30** opposition with sporadic violence. **1933** LA owns 85% of town property and 95% of farmland. **1934–41** Mono Basin extension (11-mile tunnel, Mono Craters Tunnel finished **1941**). **1963–70** second aqueduct (~**300 cfs**), finished **June 1970**. **Dec 1972** Inyo County sues under CEQA. **1980** county groundwater ordinance creates the **Inyo County Water Commission and Water Department**. **Oct 1991** Long Term Water Agreement (vegetation goals, Lower Owens River Project). **1997** MOU for re-watering by June 2003. **6 Dec 2006** water returned to the lower Owens River. [K9, K1] — SOURCED, inyowater.org/documents/reports/owens-valley-water-history-chronology (read 2026-10-29); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_water_wars (read 2026-10-29).
- **How the rights were bought:** Wikipedia's summary of historians: the agents used "chicanery, subterfuge ... and a strategy of lies"; buying began **1905** with high offers. **1924**: armed ranchers "seized the Alabama Gates and dynamited part of the system." **August 1927**: the Inyo County Bank collapsed; its owners (the valley's resistance leaders) were convicted on **36** embezzlement counts, ending organized resistance. By **1928** LA owned **90%** of valley water rights. At the 1913 opening the chief engineer said "There it is. Take it." [K9] [SM:man versus society] — SOURCED, California water wars Wikipedia.

### B. What else exists there

Public land is **98%** of the county (snippet). In **2015–16** LADWP restricted lease transfers, citing the **1924 city charter**: a lease may transfer **only once**, then goes to public auction; family transfers only to parents/children, grandparents/grandchildren, spouses; applied retroactively. Since 2015 at least **13%** of county leases reverted to LADWP; **49 of 354** leases/permits taken out of circulation; more leases in month-to-month "holdover" status, evictable on **30 days'** notice. A feed-store owner, a veterinarian of 45 years and a sporting-goods buyer could not sell or transfer; a county supervisor called it potentially "the largest economic impact to the community since LA's original acquiring of Owens valley land." Ranchers faced water cuts and trucked cattle to pasture in Oregon and Nebraska. LADWP declined to comment. Legal obligations to leave water in the valley cost LADWP **$30–40 m a year** in replacement water. [K9, K8, K6] [SM:man versus system] — SOURCED, The Guardian / AfroLA / Mammoth Sheet investigation, 29 May 2024, read via cinemaverde.org repost (first 100,000 of ~123,000 characters).
- **Housing:** the Inyo–LA agreement released **75 acres** split among four towns, with about ten years of delay before auction; another **240 acres** promised with the Manzanar site; neighbor resort growth has created a housing shortage. [K9, K4] — UNVERIFIED (snippets of sierrawave.net and hcn.org; not opened).
- **Owens Lake dust:** the lake was about **100 sq mi**; the exposed bed produced "the highest concentrations anywhere in the U.S. of airborne PM10"; LADWP spent **$2.1 billion** on dust control as of May 2019; shallow flooding covers about **62%** of the control areas; a National Academies panel (Feb 2020) urged methods that use less water. Later figures: **$2.5 billion**, **48.6 sq mi** of controls since 2000, **99.4%** reduction, PM10 from **62,377 t/yr (2000)** to **355 t/yr (2023)**. [K9, K1] [R: maintenance of a damaged system] — SOURCED (2019, 2020), nationalacademies.org news (2020-02-26); later figures UNVERIFIED (snippets; ladwp.com page returned **403**).

## 5 · Saudi desert wheat on fossil water

**Claim to test:** "A state grew its own grain in desert for food security and then pulled back as the aquifer fell (K9)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, WITH A REVERSAL.** (a) From the 1970s the state paid far above world prices for wheat grown under center pivots on non-renewable groundwater; it reached self-sufficiency in the mid-1980s and exported. (b) In **2008** it ordered a cut of **12.5% a year**, ending state purchases of local wheat from **marketing year 2016**, because of aquifer depletion (the crop is **100% irrigated**). (c) In **November 2018** it also banned **green fodder** cultivation. (d) **But wheat came back on a quota basis**: by 2023 the state bought **1.187 million t** from **3,200** registered farmers at a price set above world prices. So the pullback was real but partial; it became a managed, capped program, not an end. Treated as a policy pick: researched as the policy plus two farming places (Wadi al-Dawasir; Al-Jouf / Wadi as-Sirhan).

### A. Snapshot (the policy)
- **Institution:** the **Grain Silos and Flour Mills Organization (GSFMO)**, set up in **1972** to develop the grain industry and hold a **six-month wheat reserve**; it allocated **farmers' production quotas**, set **guaranteed prices**, milled and sold flour, and imported. Renamed **Saudi Grains Organization (SAGO)** Nov 2015, then **General Food Security Authority (GFSA)** Jan 2023. [K1] [P7] — SOURCED (role), USDA GAIN Grain and Feed Annual, Riyadh, 19 Feb 2014 (text extracted from the PDF); 1972 and renamings UNVERIFIED (search snippets of USDA reports).

Subsidies estimated **$15 billion** (1980–94) for wheat that would have cost **under $3 billion** to import; by **1984** the state bought **90%** of the crop. [K8] — SOURCED ($1,000, $15bn, 90%), agsi.org "Saudi Arabia's 60-Year Battle for Food Security" (Marie van den Bosch, 2024-05-13); 1995/2004 and SR 1,750 UNVERIFIED (snippets).

## 6 · Toshka (New Valley) Project, Egypt

**Claim to test:** "A state plan to open a new farming region in empty desert, argued over for decades (K9)."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, WITH TWO CORRECTIONS.** (a) Launched **1997** under Mubarak to pump Nile water from **Lake Nasser** into the Western Desert, reclaim about **540,000–550,000 feddans** in a first phase and, as officials then said, move up to **20%** of Egypt's people into a "new valley." By 2010–2012 only a small fraction was farmed (estimates **1,000** to **~16,500 feddans**, or **21,000 ha**), and it was widely called a failure. Revived under President el-Sisi from about **2014**; by 2024 official figures claim **400,000–500,000 feddans** reclaimed or cultivated. (b) **The desert was not "empty"**: the Toshka and Forkund lands lie in **old Nubian village territory** flooded or vacated in the dam era; Nubians protested in **2016** when Toshka land was offered to investors. (c) The argument is about **water** (Egypt's fixed Nile share), **cost** (estimates from $83 million to $87 billion), **who benefits** (foreign agribusiness, export crops) and **who settles** (few people).

### A. Snapshot
- **Where:** south of Egypt's Western Desert, Aswan Governorate; the pumping station draws from Lake Nasser near Toshka; nearest town **Abu Simbel**, about **230 km** southwest of Aswan (~300 km by road), with an airfield built in the 1970s. [K+] [P3] — SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Valley_Project; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Simbel (read 2026-10-29).
- **Infrastructure:** the **Mubarak Pumping Station** opened **March 2005**, cost about **$436 million**, able to lift about **10%** of Lake Nasser's water (Lake Nasser **5,250 km²**); the **Sheikh Zayed Canal** planned **240–310 km** (sources differ), built about **180 km**, still **60 km** short of Baris Oasis in 2012. Target area in Wikipedia: **2,340 km² (588,000 acres)**. Water need about **5 bcm/yr** against Egypt's **55.5 bcm** share under the **1959** Nile agreement with Sudan (snippet). [K1, K5] — SOURCED, Wikipedia; thenationalnews.com (2012-04-22); egyptindependent.com mega-failure slideshow; water figures UNVERIFIED.
- **Origins:** first proposed alongside Nasser's High Dam plans, abandoned **1964**, restarted **1997**; second phase canceled **2005**; completion deadline extended to **2022**. — SOURCED, egyptindependent.com (undated slideshow).

### B. What else exists there
- **Goals vs. results:** first phase **550,000** feddans; second phase to reach **2 million** feddans by **2017** (Egypt Independent). 2006 reporting: goal to settle **2 million** people by 2017 and create **700,000** jobs; officials said the 540,000 feddans would absorb **50,000** residents (snippet). Results: a researcher's 2010 estimate **~16,500** feddans; government figures 1996–2010 **~1,000**; The National (2012): **21,000 ha** farmed against a goal of over **200,000 ha**, "a few thousand" jobs against **700,000** needed a year nationally, and **no** cities, factories, schools or hospitals built. An official called it "media propaganda for Mubarak to have his legacy." [K9, K8] — SOURCED, egyptindependent.com; thenationalnews.com (2012-04-22); dailynewsegypt.com/?p=60195 (2006-02-17).
- **Cost:** government figure **$83 million** vs. U.S. State Department **$87 billion**; 2006: over **LE 5 billion** in eight years; Al-Monitor: **LE 40 billion** spent originally. [K8] — SOURCED (as each source states; not reconciled).
- **Technical problems:** high **soil salinity**; **underground aquifers** at risk of saltwater mixing; **clay** that dries into hollows where center-pivot wheels stick; heavy **evaporation**. The Toshka Lakes (formed when Lake Nasser overflowed) were shrinking by 2001 and one had dried by 2006 (snippet). [K9, K14] — SOURCED (salinity, clay), Wikipedia; Egypt Independent; lakes UNVERIFIED.
- **Investors:** **Kingdom Agricultural Development Co. (KADCO)**, owned by a Saudi prince, bought **100,000 feddans** in **1998**; after the 2011 revolution it agreed (June 2011) to keep **25,000** feddans (10,000 owned, 15,000 on a three-year usufruct) (snippet); The National: **42,000 ha**, agreed to return **31,500**. **Al Dahra** (Abu Dhabi): **18,000 ha** (2012); in Egypt since **2007**, three projects on **67,000 acres** (Toshka, East Oweinat, El-Salhiya) (snippet). **South Valley Development Company** (state): **9,660 ha** prepared (2012); 6,000 of its 120,000-acre concession developed (2006). 2006 crops: **tomatoes, potatoes, grapes**, with **70%** aimed at Europe for early ripening. [K8, K9] — SOURCED, thenationalnews.com; dailynewsegypt.com (2006); KADCO 1998/2011 and Al Dahra details UNVERIFIED.
- **Work and settlement (2006):** about **15,000** residents in an area uninhabited ten years earlier; a 25-year-old technician earning **LE 500/month** (above Delta wages) on a rotation of **30 days on site, 10 days off**; a ministry official: "Because it's a remote area, the product is expensive." [K7, K4] [R: rotation/rest pattern] — SOURCED, dailynewsegypt.com (2006-02-17). Workers mostly live on site in project housing; some staff commute from Abu Simbel (snippet). **New Toshka City**: official projection **80,000** people, 90 km NW of Abu Simbel, **2,973** feddans (snippet); census count **2,655** (citypopulation.de lists it under the **1996** census; a snippet says 2006 — conflict noted). — SOURCED (2,655), citypopulation.de.
- **Revival under el-Sisi:** half the land to be allotted to graduates at about 1 acre each via the **Tahya Misr (Long Live Egypt) Fund** (Wikipedia). On **25 May 2024** two water-lifting stations opened: **12** sets lifting **6.3 million m³/day** and **10** sets lifting **5 million m³/day** (total **11.3 million m³/day**) up **40 m**, serving **100,000** feddans of the second phase and **40,000–60,000** in a future phase; **11** main pumping stations in all; the **National Company for Land Reclamation and Agriculture** said to have cultivated **500,000** feddans; a Wadi al-Sheeh project with **2,000** feddans wheat and **10,000** varied crops. A Jan 2024 snippet gives **420,000** feddans total, and a target of **600,000** by end-2025. [K1, K8] — SOURCED, egyptian-gazette.com (2024-05-25); Wikipedia; 420k/600k UNVERIFIED (Ahram page **403**).

### K9 · Displacement and protest (Nubians)
