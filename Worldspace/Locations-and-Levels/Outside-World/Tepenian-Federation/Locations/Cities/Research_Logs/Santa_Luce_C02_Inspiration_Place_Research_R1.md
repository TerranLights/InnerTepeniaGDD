## 1 · Winnipeg, Canada

**Claim to test:** a cold, isolated interior city of roughly three-quarters of a million, nationally low-profile, with an arts scene often credited to long winters and a winter festival.
**Verdict on the claim:**
- *Population:* **CONFIRMED.** City 749,607 (2021 Census); CMA 834,678 (2021); 2025 estimate 850,260. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg (read 2026-10-29).
- *Cold:* **CONFIRMED.** January mean about −16.4 °C; record low −47.8 °C; record low wind chill −57.1 °C. Also notably sunny (2,352.9 h/yr, ranked second-sunniest Canadian city by the article). SOURCED (same page).
- *Isolated:* **NARROWED.** The nearest metro of equal or larger size is Minneapolis–Saint Paul, ~619–636 km straight-line, ~733–757 km by road; Regina is ~530–575 km. These figures are from distance calculators and a Wikipedia-derived page whose isolation sentence carries "citation needed." UNVERIFIED (snippets only). It is, however, a transport hub, not a cut-off place (see B).
- *Nationally low-profile:* **NOT TESTED / NOT FOUND** as a measurable claim; no source located that measures this. Left open.
- *"Winter made the arts":* **NARROWED, not confirmed.** The sources I could read treat winter as at most one factor among several. Matador Network (Josh Heller, 2012-11-02) poses winter, affordability, arts funding and the city's dark humor only as *questions* to a roundtable, and records curator Sigrid Dahle's "Gothic Unconscious" theory (the city "haunted by the ghosts of its traumatic social history") and a collaborative, DIY, cooperation-over-competition culture. SOURCED: https://matadornetwork.com/nights/why-is-there-so-much-art-in-winnipeg. A Galleries West piece is reported (snippet) to call the winter explanation a myth that would apply equally to Edmonton, Saskatoon or Regina, and to credit the University of Manitoba School of Fine Arts and networking. ### A. Snapshot
- Capital and largest city of Manitoba, at the confluence of the Red and Assiniboine rivers (The Forks), on the eastern edge of the Canadian Prairies; Lake Winnipeg ~65 km north. Land area 461.78 km² (CMA 5,285.46 km²); elevation 239 m. Incorporated 1873; "Unicity" amalgamation 1972. Has North America's largest extant mature urban elm forest. SOURCED: Wikipedia, Winnipeg (2026-10-29). [P3]
- Defining features as the sources present them: river-confluence site with a 6,000-year history of human use; railway and transport hub; deep winter cold. [K+ confluence/hub]

### B. What else exists there
- **Economy** (SOURCED, Wikipedia): ~450,500 jobs (2019); largest sectors health care and social assistance 14%, retail 11%, manufacturing 8%, public administration 8%. Largest employers: Province of Manitoba, University of Manitoba, City of Winnipeg, Manitoba Hydro, Manitoba Liquor & Lotteries; private: Canada Life, StandardAero, SkipTheDishes. Royal Canadian Mint (Winnipeg plant, 1976) produces all of Canada's circulating coinage. CMA GDP CA$45.0 bn (2020). [P8]
- **Institutions** (SOURCED, Wikipedia): University of Manitoba (1877, Western Canada's first university); University of Winnipeg (charter 1967); Université de Saint-Boniface (French-language); Canadian Mennonite University (1999); Red River College Polytechnic (diplomas, apprenticeships); Booth University College (Salvation Army, 1982). Hospitals: Health Sciences Center (with HSC Children's), St. Boniface General, Grace, Seven Oaks, Victoria General, Concordia, Deer Lodge Center. National Microbiology Laboratory: "one of only a handful of biosafety level 4" labs in the world (Public Health Agency of Canada and Canadian Food Inspection Agency). [P7, K14]
- **How goods and people move** (SOURCED, Wikipedia) [K5]: freight rail by CN, CP, BNSF Manitoba and Central Manitoba Railway; "the only major city between Vancouver and Thunder Bay with direct US freight rail connections"; VIA Rail at Union Station; PTH 75 to Emerson, the busiest Canada–US crossing on the Prairies; Perimeter Highway ring road (1969); CentrePort Canada, ~8,100 ha inland port and Canada's first Foreign Trade Zone; James Armstrong Richardson International Airport (redeveloped 2011, CA$585 m). Winnipeg Transit since 1882.
- **Who lives there** (SOURCED, Wikipedia, 2021 figures) [P9]: Indigenous 12.4% (highest share of any major Canadian city); Métis 6.5% (47,915, highest number and share of any major Canadian city); immigrants 27.3%, top origins the Philippines, India, China; Filipinos 11.3% of residents (highest share of any major Canadian city). Religion: 50.4% Christian, 36.4% none.
- **Downtown and the cold:** downtown buildings are linked by the enclosed **Winnipeg Walkway** (skywalk) system; >72,000 people work downtown; 237 official neighborhoods. SOURCED (Wikipedia). [P3, K+ climate-adapted infrastructure]

### C. Cultural locations (named)
- **Royal Winnipeg Ballet** — Canada's oldest ballet company and longest continuously operating in North America. **Winnipeg Symphony Orchestra**. **Manitoba Opera**. All three at the **Centennial Concert Hall**. **Royal Manitoba Theatre Center** — Canada's oldest English-language regional theater, 250+ performances/yr. **Winnipeg Art Gallery** (1912; oldest public gallery in Western Canada; world's largest public collection of contemporary Inuit art). **Canadian Museum for Human Rights** (opened 2014-09-27; only national museum west of Ottawa). SOURCED (Wikipedia, Winnipeg). [K13, P6]
- **Festival du Voyageur** [K2, K3] — proposed 1967 by Georges Forest; incorporated 1969-12-18; first held 1970 in Provencher Park "in celebration of Manitoba's centennial", honoring the fur-trade voyageur; permanent site Whittier Park (Voyageur Park), Saint-Boniface, since 1978; 10 days each February; attendance 67,380 (2025), peak 102,000 (2016); run by non-profit Festival du Voyageur Inc. (~10 permanent, ~200 temporary staff); funders include the governments of Canada, Manitoba and Winnipeg, FrancoFonds, Musicaction, Winnipeg Arts Council, The Winnipeg Foundation. Activities named: snow sculptures, music, arts and crafts, exhibits. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_du_Voyageur (2026-10-29). Described as Western Canada's largest winter festival (Wikipedia, Winnipeg). [K8]

SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklorama (2026-10-29). Volunteer counts conflict across sources (8,000 to 20,000). [K8]
- **Winnipeg Fringe Theatre Festival** (second-largest alternative theater festival in North America); **Winnipeg Folk Festival**. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Winnipeg). [K13]
- **The Forks** [K10, K2] — National Historic Site (1974) where Nakoda, Cree, Anishinaabe and Dakota used the confluence as a seasonal stop and trade link; fur-trade hub from the 1730s; rail yards and immigration sheds from the late 1800s. Redeveloped from 1987 (Forks Renewal Corporation; since 1994 The Forks North Portage Partnership). Contains **The Forks Market** (in two former railway stables; ~300 artisans), **Johnston Terminal** (1928 freight warehouse), **Manitoba Children's Museum**, **Manitoba Theatre for Young People**, **The Plaza** skate park (2006). >4 m visitors/yr, 200+ events/yr, mostly free. Winter: skating plaza, snow park, outdoor curling bonspiel, and the **Nestaweya River Trail** on the river ice (Guinness longest skating rink 2008; length varies with ice; record lost 2013). SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forks,_Winnipeg (2026-10-29).
- **Warming Huts: An Art and Architecture Competition on Ice** [K3, K13] — first established 2009 by Sputnik Architecture Inc. (Wikipedia; press snippets say architect Peter Hargraves proposed it to The Forks in 2008); every January; three winners chosen by blind jury; installed on the frozen rivers; stated purpose "attracting the public back to the downtown section of Winnipeg, especially during the cold winter months." SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Warming_Huts;_An_Art_and_Architecture_Competition_on_Ice (article flagged for issues). ## 2 · Porto Alegre participatory budgeting (Orçamento Participativo, OP)

**Claim to test:** a city of over a million that ran part of its budget through neighborhood assemblies, putting civic negotiation at neighborhood scale. **Care:** verify how much of the program survives.
**Verdict on the claim:**
- *City of over a million:* **CONFIRMED.** 1,332,845 (2022 census); metro ~4.1 m. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Alegre (2026-10-29).
- *Part of its budget through assemblies:* **CONFIRMED, with a correction of scale.** The base unit was the **region** (16 at the start, 17 since a region was added in 2007), not the single neighborhood; neighborhood assemblies fed regional "Great Assemblies," thematic assemblies, and a citywide council. SOURCED: Goldsmith 2000 (Planners Network); Participedia case 5524 (both read 2026-10-29; URLs below).
- *How much survives:* **CORRECTED from "suspended since 2017."** Wikipedia (Porto Alegre and Participatory budgeting pages) say suspended since 2017 / active until 2017. But: assemblies were halted in 2017 (mayor Nelson Marchezan Jr.) and, per a Sul21 snippet, reactivated in 2018 (UNVERIFIED); then paralyzed through the pandemic; resumed 2022; held 2023; **canceled 2024 because of the floods**; **resumed 2025** with >9,000 participants after 14 of 23 assemblies by 2025-07-29. SOURCED: Jornal do Comércio, Sofia Utz, 2025-07-29, https://www.jornaldocomercio.com/politica/2025/07/1212483-assembleias-do-orcamento-participativo-ja-receberam-mais-de-9-mil-participantes.html. The money attached is now small: R$10 m for 2022 across 17 regions and six thematic groups (figure given in a partisan interview, see below); R$15 m for 2023 and R$20 m for 2024 (Jornal do Comércio correction note, snippet only, UNVERIFIED). Whether this meets the definition of a real participatory budget is disputed: the Atlas Mundial do OP (as quoted by former mayor Raul Pont) concluded the OP was effectively extinguished in Porto Alegre.

### A. Snapshot
- **Porto Alegre:** capital of Rio Grande do Sul, on the east bank of Guaíba Lake where five rivers meet; area 496.827 km²; winters mild to cool, windy and rainy, typically 10–19 °C, recorded −0.7 °C (2012). 2022 census: 73.6% White, 13.4% Pardo, 12.6% Black. Large German (≈50,000 arrived in the state 1824–1914) and Italian (from 1875, mainly Veneto) immigrant descent; the state's Afro-Brazilian population concentrates in the city. SOURCED: Wikipedia, Porto Alegre. [P3, P9]
- **The OP:** first full process 1989 under mayor Olívio Dutra (Workers' Party, PT); its "most meaningful form" ran 1991–2004 (Wikipedia, Porto Alegre). PT governed 1989–2004. SOURCED.

### B. What else exists there (the institution and its setting)
- **How it worked at its height** [K1, K7] (SOURCED: William W. Goldsmith, "Participatory Budgeting in Porto Alegre, Brazil," Planners Network, 2000-03-18, https://www.plannersnetwork.org/2000/03/participatory-budgeting-in-porto-alegre-brazil):
  - The year opened with the government's **Presentation of Accounts** on the previous year's spending.
  - 16 district "**Great Assemblies**" held in public places — union centers, gyms, churches, clubs — where the government reported and presented the year's investment plan.
  - A **nine-month debate**: each district ranked twelve in-district themes (for example pavement, schools, water lines) and citywide cross-cutting works.
  - **Delegates** elected roughly in proportion to attendance; bound by neighborhood votes, reporting duties and recall.
  - Investment split among districts by publicly debated **weights**: population, a poverty index, a shortage measure (for example lack of pavement), and stated priorities.
  - Officials said the OP covered "about half the city budget."
  - Participation: fewer than 1,000 (1990); 16,000+ (1998); about 40,000 (1999).
  - Who came: district assemblies and delegates drawn disproportionately from the poor; thematic groups more professional and middle-class; not limited to PT supporters.
- **Formal structure** (SOURCED: Participedia, https://participedia.net/case/5524, last updated 2020-12-28) [K1, K6]: 17 regional and 6 thematic assemblies; annual cycle of preparatory meetings → regional and thematic assemblies → municipal assembly (cycle start moved January→February in 2012); **one delegate per 10 participants** (as of 2018); the **COP** (Participatory Budget Council) — two councillors and two alternates elected at each assembly, plus two nominees of the Union of Residents' Associations (UAMPA) and four non-voting city council members; it plans, proposes and supervises municipal revenue and spending. A 2003 gender-parity measure for councillor and delegate boards was adopted as a "recommendation." Participation reached 50,000 by 2004. Participedia credits the process with turning down a five-star hotel in favor of a public park and convention hall. (Council size reported as 42 or 44 in other snippets; UNVERIFIED.)
- **Outcomes** (SOURCED, Goldsmith 2000 and Participedia; both trace to a World Bank paper, Bhatnagar et al. 2003): sewer and water connections 75% → 98% of households (1988–1997); schools quadrupled since 1986; health and education share of budget 13% (1985) → nearly 40% (1996); new public housing 1,700 people (1986) vs 27,000 (1989). OP share of total spending 17% (1992) → 21% (1999) (Wikipedia citing Smith et al. 2015). Own-source revenue +269% 1988–2004 (Wikipedia citing Schneider and Baquero 2006). Shortcomings named by the World Bank paper: very poor and young people underrepresented (Wikipedia). [P7, K8]
- **Decline** [K9] (SOURCED as a partisan account: interview with Raul Pont, PT mayor 1997–2001, Brasil de Fato, 2022-03-30, https://www.brasildefato.com.br/2022/03/30/orcamento-participativo-saiu-das-maos-da-comunidade-diz-ex-prefeito-de-porto-alegre-raul-pont/): abandonment began under José Fogaça (2005–2010) and continued under José Fortunati (2010–2017), in stages — total-budget transparency ended while the regional/thematic structure was kept; resources quietly reduced; internal rules bureaucratized and leaders co-opted. In 2017 Marchezan allocated no funds and held no assemblies, without formally abolishing the OP. Pont lists stalled works (about ten preschool projects, one sewage plant in two decades). Participedia (neutral) also records "accusations of more clientelistic relationships" after 2004 and that the system continued because governments lacked backing to dismantle it. The Sul21 snippet says the 2017 suspension was justified by a lack of money even for demands pending from previous years (UNVERIFIED).
- **Current form** (SOURCED, Jornal do Comércio 2025-07-29): residents present regional demands, choose priority themes, elect delegates and councillors who form the regional and thematic forums and the council. Average attendance 1989–2022 was 11,918; record 2015 (20,000+). In 2023 housing was the top priority in five regions; education and paving three each; culture two; sanitation, social assistance and health one each. The secretary says demands renew as works are delivered. Snippets (UNVERIFIED) say 2023 participation rose 52.72% over 2022. [K1]

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- The OP's meeting circuit used existing gathering places (union halls, gyms, churches, clubs) as assembly sites (Goldsmith). The city's reputation for participatory democracy coincides with it hosting the first World Social Forums (both SOURCED as facts; no source read states a causal link, so none is asserted). [K10]
- Spending shifted to sanitation, schools and housing in poor districts (outcomes above).

### E. Structural features
- The OP's weight depended on how much money a mayor placed in it; the same structure persisted with very different budgets (R$-scale "about half the budget" in 1999 by officials' account vs R$10–20 m reserves in 2022–2024). [K8]
- **K6 everyday rules:** inside the OP, rules are written in an internal regulation (Regimento Interno) refined each year by participants (Pont interview), with a 1-per-10 delegate ratio, recall, and a parity recommendation (Participedia). For everyday city life, no curfews, alcohol rules or similar were researched in this round: **none found / not searched**.
- **Friction and loss** [K9]: **2024 floods** — began 2024-04-29; Guaíba Lake reached 5.31 m, above the 1941 record of 4.76 m; most areas of the city flooded; airport, Metro and buses suspended; statewide 181 dead (as of 2024-07-07), at least 580,000 displaced. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Rio_Grande_do_Sul_floods. The 2024 OP assemblies were canceled because of them (Jornal do Comércio).
- **Where else the tradition is practiced today** (SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participatory_budgeting, 2026-10-29): over 11,500 municipal PB processes worldwide (2024), up from ~1,500 (2014); Paris devoted ~€75 m (2023); New York City, 24 council members put $24 m of capital funds to residents (2024); Cambridge, Massachusetts, eleventh process, 1,300+ ideas for $1 m (FY26). [K1]
- **K11 speech:** Portuguese. Languages of German- and Italian-descended communities not checked on a page this round.

## 3 · Baarle-Hertog / Baarle-Nassau

**Claim to test:** a town where two administrations interlock street by street, forcing household-scale coordination.
**Verdict:** **CONFIRMED, and sharpened.** Two municipalities in two countries interlock down to individual buildings; a building's nationality, address and property tax follow its front door; and since 2021 the two councils have sat together as one cross-border legal body whose decisions need a majority **on each side separately**. Sources below.

### A. Snapshot
- **Baarle-Hertog** (Belgium, Antwerp province, Flemish): 3,083 residents (2025-01-01); 7.41 km² in 26 parcels — 22 Belgian exclaves inside the Netherlands plus 4 parcels linked to other Belgian municipalities; the largest section is Zondereigen, on the Belgian mainland. **Baarle-Nassau** (Netherlands, North Brabant): 6,899 (2021); 76.29 km²; villages Baarle-Nassau 5,330, Ulicoten 1,110, Castelré 140; eight Dutch counter-enclaves (N1–N8), six or seven inside Belgian exclave H1. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baarle-Hertog ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baarle-Nassau (2026-10-29). (The combined article's 2026 infobox gives 6,668 and 2,592; figures differ by year/source.) [P3, P5]
- **Origin:** medieval treaties, land swaps and sales between the Lords of Breda (House of Nassau) and the Dukes of Brabant, ratified in the Treaty of Maastricht (1843). Parcel H22 was disputed from the 1830s until 1995 (given to Belgium). SOURCED (Wikipedia; Brussels Times 2019). [K9]
- **Defining feature:** the border runs through roads, gardens, shops and living rooms, marked on the pavement (white crosses, "NL"/"B"; iron pins on all streets); house-number plaques carry a national flag. SOURCED: Wikipedia, Baarle-Nassau; Brussels Times (Marianna Hunt, 2019-06-26, https://www.brusselstimes.com/59439/belgiums-lost-town-of-baarle-hertog); Euronews 2018-03-21.

### B. What else exists there
- **Governance — two of nearly everything** [K1, K4]: each town has its own elected council, mayor and church; two postal services, two bus companies, two sets of national law (Brussels Times). Police: the two police forces share one office on the Parallelweg in Baarle-Hertog (Wikipedia, Baarle) — other sources say "two police forces"; both may be true.
- **Shared services** [K1, K14] (SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baarle, 2026-10-29):
  - **Fire service** merged 2010-01-01 into one body of Dutch and Belgian volunteers, based at C.A. Bodestraat 2, Baarle-Nassau.
  - **Cultural center with combined library**, funded by both councils; the border runs through the building, which has a Dutch entrance (Pastoor de Katerstraat 7) and a Belgian one (no. 5). Staffed by Belgian and Dutch employees.
  - A joint council handles electricity, water, gas, highway maintenance and refuse collection; calls between the two towns are charged at local rates.
  - A single **tourist office** affiliated with both national tourist boards (seat at Singel 1, Baarle-Nassau, per a snippet; Singel 1 is confirmed as the BGTS seat in the statute below).
- **The joint legal body: BGTS Baarle** [K1, K6] — SOURCED, primary: *Statuten BTGS Baarle*, versie 8 juli 2021, Gemeenteblad Baarle-Nassau 2021 nr. 422604 (published 2021-11-24), https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2021-422604.pdf (text extracted and read 2026-10-29, translated by me):
  - Predecessor: the **GOB** (Gemeenschappelijk Overleg Baarle, "Joint Consultation Baarle"). On 2013-06-25 the GOB in plenary set a work order "to arrive at a European Municipality," with a cross-border legal person as an intermediate step; an expert group formed mid-2017 advised on 2019-04-30; the GOB decided in principle on 2019-06-25 to create a **Benelux Grouping for Territorial Cooperation** under the Benelux Treaty of 2014-02-20 (in force 2019-01-01).
  - Members: the municipality of Baarle-Hertog and the municipality of Baarle-Nassau; area = both territories; seat Singel 1, 5111 CC Baarle-Nassau. **Working language: Dutch.** Supervisors: the Dutch Ministry of the Interior; in Belgium the Flemish government (or the provincial governor on its instructions).
  - Purpose: to deliberate and decide on "all matters deemed of common interest," align the two towns' policies, and run joint projects; it can hold property, employ staff, keep its own budget and bank account. Competences can be transferred to it by amending the statutes. The first transfer written in is **waste management** (awareness, collection, transport, processing of all waste streams), with possible substitution to the Belgian waste association IOK Afvalbeheer.
  - Organs: the **Enclaveraad** (Enclave Council) = the full municipal councils of both towns, including both executive boards; only members with a vote in their own council vote. Meets at least twice a year (accounts in the first half, next year's budget in the second); meetings public except for privacy matters or by a two-thirds vote with a majority in each delegation; budget and accounts meetings always public. **Quorum: half plus one of the voting members from each municipality. A decision passes only with an absolute majority in each of the two municipal delegations**, and is then sent in identical wording to both councils for ratification.
  - The **Enclavecollege** (Enclave Board) = the full executive boards of both towns; meets in private; decides "in principle by consensus," otherwise by absolute majority per delegation.
  - **Chair rotates** between the two mayors; the mayor of Baarle-Hertog chairs the first year. Statute amendments need three-quarters of votes cast with three-quarters of each participant's members present.
  - Snippet (UNVERIFIED): cost split changed in 2020 to 30% Baarle-Hertog / 70% Baarle-Nassau, by population.
- **Schools** [P4]: Baarle-Hertog — De Vlinder, De Horizon (primary). Baarle-Nassau — De Uilenpoort, Bernardusschool (Ulicoten) (primary); De La Salle (secondary). SOURCED (Wikipedia).
- **Population shift** [P9]: Baarle-Hertog had 2,988 residents in 2022 and, for the first time, more Dutch than Belgian nationals; Dutch nationals can stand on its election lists; the mayor (Frans De Bont) said the town still feels strongly Belgian; residents called the community "one big group." SOURCED: VRT NWS, 2022-02-09, https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2022/02/09/voor-het-eerst-meer-nederlandse-dan-belgische-inwoners-in-baarle/. Why the Dutch move there: NOT FOUND (not in the article).
- **Daily logistics** [K5]: there are no supermarkets on the Belgian side, so residents buy groceries in the Netherlands; fuel is cheaper on the Belgian side. SOURCED: Euronews, 2018-03-21, https://www.euronews.com/2018/03/21/this-is-the-world-s-most-complex-border-situation.

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- Every shared service (fire, library, waste, utilities) exists because neither town can run its territory without crossing the other's; the 2021 statute institutionalizes a **double-majority** rule so neither side can outvote the other. SOURCED (statute). [K1]
- Border commerce: weekly Sunday shopping because Belgian shops may always open on Sundays; fireworks retail; restaurants that moved diners to the Belgian side when Dutch closing hours were earlier; tourism to the border markings (a "money-spinner" for the tourist office). SOURCED (Wikipedia, Baarle; Brussels Times). [K8]
- **WWI**: Belgium was occupied while the Netherlands was neutral; the Dutch government sealed off the exclaves so they could not be occupied without crossing Dutch territory; they sheltered refugees and a clandestine radio transmitter; the Dutch government built a church and school for stranded Belgian residents. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Baarle-Hertog; Brussels Times). [K9]
- Historic **smuggling**: a resident told Euronews people wore clogs backwards to mislead customs. SOURCED (Euronews). [K9]

### E. Structural features
- **K6 everyday rules — yes, several, all produced by the border:**
  1. **Front door rule**: a building straddling the border has its address in the country containing its front door; one house on Loveren has two numbers (Belgian 2, Dutch 19) with the front door on the line. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Baarle). Property tax follows the front door, and "locals wanting to reduce their bill need simply shift their entranceway"; where a doorway is split, residents "just pick a side." SOURCED (Brussels Times 2019). Enforced by each state's tax and address registries.
  2. **Closing hours, drinking age, fireworks, COVID-19 rules** differ by side: drinking age 16 in Belgium, 18 in the Netherlands (Brussels Times); restaurant tables moved to the Belgian side to stay open later; separate COVID-19 rules in 2020 (Wikipedia; VRT). Enforced by each country's authorities on its own ground.
  3. **Inter-council rules**: double-majority voting, rotating chair, Dutch as working language (statute).
- **Friction** [K9]: the two councils must constantly negotiate sewage, public lighting and road building; once one laid brick and the other asphalt on the same road. The Baarle-Nassau mayor (Marjon De Hoon-Veelenturf) called a single European municipality "brilliant" but still a utopia; the Baarle-Hertog mayor (Leo van Tilburg) called unification "legally impossible at the moment" and said cultural sensitivities would resurface. SOURCED (Euronews 2018).
- **K11 speech:** Dutch on both sides (Flemish/Belgian Dutch in Baarle-Hertog; Netherlands Dutch in Baarle-Nassau); Dutch is the BGTS working language. SOURCED for the working language (statute) and the Flemish status of Baarle-Hertog (Wikipedia). Dialect use not checked.

## 4 · Sark, Channel Islands

**Claim to test:** governance at near-household scale in a tiny self-governing community.
**Verdict:** **CONFIRMED.** 562 residents (2022 census) govern themselves through an 18-member elected assembly (Chief Pleas) with nine committees, on which roughly one resident in thirty sits as a voting member; volunteering and multiple roles are the norm (Sark Government). Its own legislature sets taxes, vehicle rules, dog rules and housing qualification. Sources below. (Arithmetic "one in thirty" is mine: 18 / 562.)

### A. Snapshot
- Island in the south-west English Channel, part of the Bailiwick of Guernsey (a Crown Dependency), ~5.45 km² including Brecqhou; main settlement The Village; ~3 miles long × 1.5 miles wide, ~40 miles of coast. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sark (2026-10-29); Lighting Management Plan (below). [P3]
- **Population:** 562 (census April 2022, published September 2023; first census in 50+ years), up from 493 in 1971; peak 785 in 1841. 11% arrived in 2020, 10% in 2021, 6% in early 2022; more than half moved there in the past decade; fewer than 20% have lived there 40+ years; 28% born in Sark or Guernsey; half born in the British Isles, about one in seven elsewhere in Europe. 365 working; skilled trades 27% and managers/directors 25% the largest groups; higher share of economically active over-65s than many jurisdictions; 71% have private health insurance. SOURCED: Guernsey Press, 2023-09-18, https://guernseypress.com/news/2023/09/18/quarter-of-sarks-population-arrived-in-last-three-years/. Snippet (UNVERIFIED): 290 private dwellings and five hotels. [P9]
- **Defining feature:** a feudal fief (granted 1565 by Elizabeth I to Helier de Carteret on condition he keep it free of pirates and held by at least 40 men; he leased 40 tenements, 40 survive) that converted to an elected legislature in 2008. SOURCED (Wikipedia). [K+ feudal-to-elected governance]

### B. What else exists there
- **Government** [K1, K6]:
  - **Chief Pleas**: 18 directly elected Conseillers (four-year terms, half elected every two years; last election 2024), plus a non-voting Speaker and the non-voting Seigneur (Christopher Beaumont, since 2016). History: founded c. 1579 / before 1597 with the Seigneur, Seneschal and the 40 tenement holders; 12 popularly elected Deputies added 1922; People's Deputies 1957; 2006 vote (25–15) for reform; Privy Council approval 2008-04-09; first democratic election 2008-12-10. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Pleas ; Wikipedia, Sark.
  - **Nine committees**: Agriculture, Environment & Sea Fisheries; Development Control; Douzaine; Education; Harbors, Shipping & Pilotage; Medical & Emergency Services; Policy & Finance; Scrutiny Management; Tourism (incl. Public Health). SOURCED (Chief Pleas page).
  - **The Douzaine** (usually seven members) handles day-to-day administration: waste, roads, other public works, licenses, public property, and appoints key officials including police and law officers. In April 2024, after a string of resignations left the committee with only its chair for several weeks, Chief Pleas voted 13–2 to remove him and seated seven new members. SOURCED: Bailiwick Express, 2024-04-12, https://www.bailiwickexpress.com/news/fiery-debate-sees-sarks-one-man-douzaine-ousted/. [K9]
  - Officers: Seneschal, Prévôt and Greffier chosen by the Seigneur; Treasurer, Constable and Vingtenier elected by Chief Pleas; the census report was timed ahead of the October **Michaelmas meeting** of Chief Pleas (Guernsey Press 2023). Criminal law handled by Guernsey under the Reform (Sark) Law 2008; small police station and jail. SOURCED (Wikipedia; Guernsey Press).
  - Secrecy friction: retiring Speaker Arthur Rolfe (2020-01-29) said Chief Pleas meetings are public but standing committees, "where the constructive discussion takes place," meet behind closed doors, and called the process "archaic." SOURCED: https://www.bailiwickexpress.com/news-ge/chief-pleas-retiring-speaker-slams-island-secrecy/. [K9]
- **Taxes** [K8]: no income, capital gains, inheritance or sales tax; revenue from Property Tax, Personal Capital Tax, Impôt (on alcohol, tobacco, certain fuels), Property Transfer Tax (7.5%; replaced the feudal Treizième in 2006) and a visitor Landing Tax. Returns due 28 February. SOURCED: Sark Government, https://sarkgov.co.uk/living-on-sark (2026-10-29); Wikipedia. Snippet (UNVERIFIED): Landing Tax to rise £1.40 → £1.47 per adult in the 2025 budget, which noted a drop in staying visitors.
- **Services** (SOURCED, Sark Government page) [K5, K1]:
  - No NHS; private insurance strongly encouraged; resident doctor, surgeries Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri 08:30–13:00; emergency evacuation to Guernsey by launch or RNLI lifeboat, charged; dentist April–September only; no optician; a Guernsey vet visits monthly.
  - School: one island-funded school, ages 3/4–13; secondary at Les Beaucamps High School in Guernsey or home-schooled. (Wikipedia gives ages 4–15; the government page is more recent.)
  - **No mains water** (catchment, borehole or well; households treat their own; Fire Service deliveries in dry spells); **no mains sewer** (pits emptied by the island, £18.37/load); no mains gas (bottled); heating oil from two private firms; petrol and diesel from one retailer; weekly rubbish collection at £88.80/person/yr; sub-Post Office five days a week.
  - Volunteer fire and rescue since 1958; Sark Ambulance Service (Wikipedia).
  - Shops on **The Avenue**: The Food Stop (groceries), Gallery Stores (general goods, Post Office, bank counter), Mon Plaisir (groceries); most outlets deliver.
- **Electricity** [K9, K8]: supplied by a private company, Sark Electricity Ltd (SEL), at a regulated rate (54p/kWh on the government page) from a diesel generator (LMP 2010). A Price Control Commissioner exists under a 2016 law; Chief Pleas is pursuing compulsory purchase of SEL. In August 2025 SEL added legal-cost levies, taking the price to 53.6p + 60p = £1.13/kWh (Guernsey's standard tariff 24.77p); the commissioner (Shane Lynch) opened a review; in 2024 he had allowed a rise from 54p to 56p. SOURCED: Guernsey Press, 2025-08-28, https://guernseypress.com/news/2025/08/28/sark-electricity-announces-second-tariff-rise-in-a-week. Snippets (UNVERIFIED): 2019 rise to 85p "the highest known price in the world"; a hotel chose to generate its own power; the commissioner later halved the 2025 price.
- **Work** [K7, K14] (SOURCED, Sark Government): tourism employs the most residents (restaurants, pubs, hotels, guest houses, campsites); resident builders, plumbers, electricians, gas fitters, hairdressers, carers, nurses; other professionals brought in; "Volunteering is common, and many residents hold several roles."
- **Getting there** [K5]: no airport; ferries to St Peter Port, Guernsey (~55 min), summer service to Jersey (Wikipedia). The **Isle of Sark Shipping Company** has been the year-round passenger and cargo lifeline since 1969; cargo vessel *Sark Viking* carries up to 65 t (snippets from sarkshipping.gg, UNVERIFIED; company bio page 404). Maseline harbor is the commercial harbor; a tractor-drawn bus carries passengers up Harbor Hill; labeled luggage is delivered by island carters (snippets, UNVERIFIED). Snippet (UNVERIFIED): in one (undated) month 12,000+ arrived by ferry plus ~2,500 by private boat. The 2010 LMP gives "more than 40,000 visitors" in the previous year and a resident population of about 650.

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- Smallness plus self-government means very few people hold every office: committees combine what elsewhere are ministries; a committee can shrink to one person; many residents hold several roles (sources above). [K1]
- The fief's 40 tenements shaped the legislature until 2008 (tenement holders sat by right). [P7]
- The car ban, absence of street lighting and high electricity cost underpinned the Dark Sky designation and its astronomy tourism (LMP). [K3]
- Seigneurial privileges survived into modern law: the Seigneur once held the sole right to keep pigeons or an unspayed dog; the dog provision was repealed in 2008 (Wikipedia) — yet the current government page still states it is illegal to keep an unspayed bitch on the island for more than 28 days (SOURCED, Sark Government). Both stated as the sources give them.

### E. Structural features
- **K6 everyday rules — yes, many, written into island law or practice:**
  1. **No cars.** Only tractors, agricultural/construction vehicles and mobility scooters can be licensed; importing a motor vehicle needs Road Traffic Committee permission; tractor drivers need a license after a test administered by the **Constable**; **tractors may not carry people**; there are no taxis; cycles (including e-bikes) are taxed annually; horses and carriages for public use licensed annually; dogs and horses licensed annually at the Constable's Office. SOURCED (Sark Government page).
  2. **Tractor curfew**: "all tractors must be off the road network between the hours of 10pm and 6am"; most residents carry torches as presence markers when walking at night. SOURCED: *Sark – Dark Sky Island: Lighting Management Plan*, prepared for La Société Sercquaise by James H. Paterson, Lighting Consultancy and Design Services Ltd, version 02.2010, https://darksky.org/app/uploads/2018/03/Sark_LMP.pdf (text extracted locally and read 2026-10-29).
  3. **Lighting**: no public street lighting at all; the plan's measures are **voluntary** ("encouraged") — limit overspill, fit fully shielded fixtures, switch off or reduce exterior lighting at 22:00 in the island core zone; audit found 582 external luminaires, 436 (75%) compliant. The IDA may withdraw the designation if measured darkness falls. SOURCED (LMP).
  4. **Housing qualification**: homes are "local market" (built since 1976; occupant must be locally qualified — born on Sark, 15+ years' residence, or family of such a person) or "open market" (unrestricted); any new dwelling needs Development Control Committee permission. SOURCED (Sark Government page).
  5. **Franchise**: residents 18+ can join the electoral roll after two years' residence; there is no Sark citizenship. SOURCED (Sark Government page).
- **Friction and loss** [K9]: the Barclay brothers bought neighboring Brecqhou in 1993 and contested Sark's control of it; candidates backed by their interests won few seats in 2008 and 2010 (Wikipedia); the long-running electricity dispute (above); committee secrecy complaints; the 2024 Douzaine collapse.
- **K11 speech:** English in daily use. **Sercquiais**, a Norman dialect, was still spoken by a few older inhabitants in 1998. SOURCED (Wikipedia). No current speaker count found.

## 5 · Whittier, Alaska

**Claim to test:** nearly the whole town lives in one building, making "everyone knows everyone" the organizing fact of civic life in a hard climate.
**Verdict:**
- *Nearly the whole town in one building:* **CONFIRMED.** 272 residents (2020 census); Wikipedia: "nearly all" live in Begich Towers; the rest mostly in a second condominium building, Whittier Manor (snippets give 80–90% in the Towers; UNVERIFIED for the exact share). SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whittier,_Alaska ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begich_Towers (2026-10-29).
- *"Everyone knows everyone":* **NARROWED.** A resident teacher told NPR (2015) that most people know one another, but that some residents value the social life and others prefer seclusion. SOURCED: NPR *All Things Considered*, 2015-01-18 (reporting by Erin Sheehy and Reed Young for *The California Sunday Magazine*), https://www.wnyc.org/story/welcome-to-whittier-alaska-a-community-under-one-roof/. No source read describes it as "the organizing fact of civic life."
- *Hard climate:* **CONFIRMED.** ~197 in (5,000 mm) precipitation/yr; ~268 in (682 cm) snowfall; January mean −2.1 °C; winter winds up to 60 mph (NPR). The hardness is wetness, snow and wind more than cold. SOURCED (Wikipedia; NPR).
- *Access, not climate, is the dominant constraint* (see K6).

### A. Snapshot
- Head of Passage Canal, west side of Prince William Sound, ~93 km south-east of Anchorage; Chugach Census Area (Unorganized Borough); incorporated 1969; total area 19.64 sq mi (12.25 land). Population 220 (2010) → 272 (2020); ~250 year-round (Alaska Beacon 2026). SOURCED (Wikipedia; Alaska Beacon/Alaska Public, below). [P3]
- Origin: WWII U.S. Army Camp Sullivan — a port and railroad, rail spur opened 1943; the port became the entry point for soldiers entering Alaska. 1964 Good Friday earthquake: tsunami to 13 m, 13 killed, >$10 m damage. SOURCED (Wikipedia). [K9]

### B. What else exists there
- **Begich Towers** [K4, K10] — 14 floors, three connected towers, 196 one- to three-bedroom apartments; designed 1953 for the Army Corps of Engineers as the Hodge Building, opened 1957 (with the Buckner Building), used by the Army until the early 1960s; public building after the 1964 tsunami; renamed 1972 for Congressman Nick Begich (lost in a plane crash in the area); since 1974 managed by the **Begich Towers Condominium Association of Apartment Owners Inc.** Contents (Wikipedia): post office, general store, laundromat, small Baptist church, two floors of bed-and-breakfast rooms, conference room; school linked by tunnel with an indoor playground. NPR adds: police station down the hall from the entrance, a small market and a convenience store, a health clinic for minor ailments (not a hospital), church in the basement; halls with bulletin boards and pale yellow concrete-block walls; B&B on the top two floors. 2016 façade and mechanical renovation funded by a $3 m USDA loan; as of 2021 all four elevators needed a major overhaul (hundreds of thousands of dollars). SOURCED (Begich Towers Wikipedia; NPR). [K8, K14]
- **Buckner Building** — 273,600 sq ft former military building (1953), closed for decades because of asbestos and other hazards. ## 6 · Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard

**Claim to test:** a research settlement where several national programs work side by side in one tiny place.
**Verdict:** **CONFIRMED and specified.** Eighteen institutions from eleven countries have a more or less permanent presence, five of them year-round, in a settlement of ~30–35 people in winter and ~115–130 in summer. All share one state-owned company's infrastructure, one mess hall and one town-wide code of conduct. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ny-%C3%85lesund ; Svalbard Museum, https://svalbardmuseum.no/en/nyalesund (both read 2026-10-29).

### A. Snapshot
- Western Spitsbergen, on Brøggerhalvøya at Kongsfjorden; has no formal legal existence as a town; described as the northernmost functional civilian settlement. Population: infobox 117 summer / 35 winter; text 30–35 year-round, 114 summer; Svalbard Museum "about 30" year-round, "over 130" in summer. SOURCED (Wikipedia; Svalbard Museum). [P3]
- **Defining feature:** a former coal company town converted into a shared international research facility, still wholly owned and run by one state company. [K+ company town]

### B. What else exists there
- **The company: Kings Bay AS** [K1, K8] — founded 1916 as Kings Bay Kull Compani (from Ålesund); nationalized 1933; renamed Kings Bay AS 1998, "removing the final reference to coal"; state-owned (Wikipedia, company page: Ministry of Trade and Industry; Ny-Ålesund page: Ministry of Climate and Environment as ultimate owner — sources differ, likely by date). Runs the whole settlement: airport, real estate, power and water, catering, harbor, cruise-ship handling; also administers Bjørnøen AS (owner of Bjørnøya). 32 employees (2007); revenue NOK 36.8 m (2005). SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Bay_(company).
- **Research stations** (SOURCED, Wikipedia) [K4, K14]: Norway — Zeppelin Station (485 m, reached by aerial tramway), Sverdrup station, VLBI geodetic station at Hamnerabben (Norwegian Mapping Authority, Kartverket), Svalbard Rocket Range (world's northernmost sounding-rocket site); Germany and France — AWIPEV (joint); Italy — Amundsen-Nobile Climate Change Tower and Dirigibile Italia station; UK — British Antarctic Survey; China — Arctic Yellow River Station; India — Himadri; Japan — National Institute of Polar Research; South Korea — Dasan; Netherlands — University of Groningen station. The research station as a whole is led from the Norwegian Polar Institute (contact headnpi.nya@npolar.no in the code of conduct).
- **Coordination body: NySMAC** (Ny-Ålesund Science Managers Committee) — established 1994 "to enhance cooperation and coordination among research activities" at the facility; members are all parties with major interests in Ny-Ålesund; four research **flagship programs** launched 2008–2011 with Svalbard Science Forum; strategy plan 2026–2030; 58th meeting February 2023. Snippets only (UNVERIFIED): nyalesundresearch.no NySMAC documents and assw.info listings. [K1]
- **Getting in and out** [K5]: Ny-Ålesund Airport, Hamnerabben — 808 m gravel runway, no terminal or hangar; a Dornier 228 flies two to four times a week to Longyearbyen; **tickets require permission from Kings Bay** (Wikipedia). Harbor with one large ISPS berth, commonly used by cruise ships (Wikipedia). Fresh produce depends on a ship arriving about **every six weeks in summer** (SOURCED: https://nyalesundresearch.no/daily-life-in-ny-alesund/). Mail at least three times a week in summer, twice in winter (SOURCED: Dutch station page, https://arcticstation.nl/station.php?nr=286). All waste is sorted (23 categories per the Dutch station) and shipped to the mainland at the producing institution's expense; dumping is forbidden.
- **Daily life** [K7, K10, K12] (SOURCED: nyalesundresearch.no daily-life page; arcticstation.nl):
  - **Service Building**: reception, the common **mess**, laundry, garbage return, nurse.
  - Meals: three a day in the mess plus evening self-service sandwiches. Low season (Oct–Mar) weekday breakfast 07:30–08:30, lunch 12:00–13:00, dinner 16:30–17:30; high season (Apr–Sep) lunch 12:20–13:00, dinner 16:50–17:30; weekends brunch 10:00–13:00, Saturday coffee 15:00–16:00, Saturday dinner 18:30/18:50–19:30. Late dinners must be requested before 15:00 and kept in a warming cabinet 19:00–22:00; field food lists 48 h ahead to Kings Bay. In summer meals are served in two groups.
  - Mess rules: wash hands, outerwear off, clean clothes and indoor shoes, sort dishes and trash; **on Saturdays people dress up**; own wine or beer allowed at weekends. Shoes off at every building entrance — "a habit from the coal-mining era" (Dutch station).
  - **Kongsfjordbutikken** shop: souvenirs, snacks, toiletries, soft drinks, alcohol; open Mon and Thu 17:00–18:00; alcohol only on showing the boarding pass from the mainland flight.
  - **Velferden** (welfare association): gym, jacuzzi, lends boats and kayaks, runs social gatherings including a pub, manages cabins. Saturday bar at **Mellageret**, 20:00–02:00, volunteer-run; residents are expected to take a turn staffing and cleaning it. Mess lounge with TV, pool table, electronic piano. **Ny-Ålesund Town and Mine Museum**, free, open 24/7.

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- Shared infrastructure owned by one company means one mess, one shop, one welfare association and one set of rules for everyone; national stations keep their own buildings and codes but share the town-wide **Code of Conduct**, which "complements the Codes of Conduct of individual research institutions and employers." SOURCED (CoC, below). [K4]
- The research purpose produces the town's most distinctive rule — the radio-quiet zone — which protects the geodetic VLBI station. [K6]
- Mining-era habits persist (shoes off indoors). [K+]

### E. Structural features
- **K6 everyday rules — yes, extensive and written down:**
  1. **Radio-quiet zone**: a 20 km zone bans devices transmitting at 2–32 GHz, including mobile phones, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, to protect the VLBI station (Wikipedia). The code of conduct: "Turn-off Bluetooth and WiFi on all devices. Their use is prohibited by law and disrupts sensitive measurements performed by Kartverket and other institutions." Also: "Avoid phone use during mealtimes" and be aware of phone use "to protect the community feeling."
  2. **Alcohol**: permitted, but abuse prohibited; must not interfere with work, field safety or emergency readiness; intoxication unacceptable in field settings; colleagues should "intervene early." Purchase only with the mainland flight boarding pass (Svalbard regulation, per the shop page).
  3. **Conduct**: zero tolerance for sexual harassment; prohibitions on racism, xenophobia and exclusion; respect "shared quiet hours and personal space"; mental-health guidance naming "darkness, isolation, limited privacy, and intense work periods."
  4. **Enforcement and reporting**: report to one's home institution's person of trust, the local leader, the station leadership (Norwegian Polar Institute), or the **Kings Bay nurse, who serves as person-of-trust for the community**; outcomes range from mediation and changed living arrangements to formal investigation, "Removal from fieldwork or from Ny-Ålesund," and reporting to employers, funders or authorities.
  - SOURCED: *Ny-Ålesund Research Station – Code of Conduct*, last revised 2026-03-12, https://nyalesundresearch.no/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CoC_NyA_RS-1.pdf (text extracted locally and read 2026-10-29).
  5. **Polar bears**: residents must carry rifles; town doors stay unlocked so anyone can take shelter during an attack (Wikipedia). [K6, K1]
  6. **Access control**: flight tickets require Kings Bay's permission (Wikipedia).
- **Friction and loss** [K9]: mining 1917–c.1929, resumed 1945; accidents in 1948, 1952 and 1953 killed 28; 76 died in the mines 1946–1962; on **1962-11-05** an explosion killed 21 men, 11 never recovered; the mines closed and inhabitants left in 1963; Prime Minister Einar Gerhardsen's government resigned because public opinion held the state-owned company had failed on worker safety. SOURCED (Svalbard Museum). Snippets (UNVERIFIED) give the resignation date as 1963-08-24 after a Storting no-confidence vote (the Kings Bay Affair). The state had invested NOK 21.7 m in safety modernization by 1961; the coal market collapsed in 1957 (Svalbard Museum).
- **Transition**: ESRO satellite telemetry station 1964/1967–1974; research expanded in the 1990s (Wikipedia, both pages; dates differ slightly between pages). [P8]
- **Visitor pressure**: AECO and Kings Bay issued community-specific visitor guidelines because of rising visitor numbers; a cruise ship can enlarge the population "enormously, albeit temporarily" (snippet, UNVERIFIED). [K9]
- **K11 speech:** a multinational community (stations from eleven countries); Norwegian is the host language. The working/common language used between stations is NOT FOUND on a page read.
