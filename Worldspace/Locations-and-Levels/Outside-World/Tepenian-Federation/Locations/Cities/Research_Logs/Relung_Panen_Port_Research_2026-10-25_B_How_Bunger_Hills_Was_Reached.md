# Relung Panen Port Research, Slice B: How the Bunger Hills Has Actually Been Reached

**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Scope:** Real-world access history and methods for the Bunger Hills oasis (Knox Coast, Wilkes Land; about 66°15′S 100°45′E). This covers real infrastructure and logistics only. Nothing here describes the fictional setting. The stations' operators appear only as coordinates and as facts about infrastructure. No culture, identity or founders are drawn from them, and nothing here is a recommendation.
**Method:** Web research in English, Russian and Polish. Primary sources were opened and read where they could be reached: PDFs were downloaded and their text extracted, and the Australian Antarctic Division (AAD) season schedule archive for 1947/48 to 2025/26 was downloaded and parsed in full. Each claim is marked **SOURCED** (read in a primary or secondary source, cited) or **UNVERIFIED** (seen only in a search-engine snippet or a secondary summary that could not be opened, or internally inconsistent). Distances marked **CALCULATED** are great-circle distances that I computed from coordinates given in the cited sources. They are not source statements.

---

## 1. Findings summary

**Overall picture (SOURCED, many sources):** No one has ever been recorded bringing cargo to the Bunger Hills by ship and then hauling it overland from the coast. Every recorded access since 1947 has been **by air**:

- seaplane landing (1947);
- ship-based helicopters, plus one Weasel relay leg (1948);
- ski aircraft from Mirny onto the glacier or lake ice, then helicopter (1956–1966);
- ship to Mirny, then helicopter staged along the coast (1959, 1979);
- ship standing off in the pack or at an ice-shelf-front bay, then ship-based helicopters (1986–2022);
- ski aircraft (Twin Otter, CASA 212, Basler) from Casey, Mirny or Davis landing on Transkripsii Inlet ice, plus C-17 airdrops (1988–2025).

Ships have stood off at the following recorded distances: **50 nm** (1986, held off by pack), **~90 km** (2015), **69 km** (2022, Malygintsev Bay), and roughly **"100 km" by helicopter** (2022, a Polish account).

## 2. Findings by question

### Q1. The three stations: establishment and resupply

#### 1.0 Pre-station access (for context)

- **1947, US Navy Operation Highjump.** A PBM Mariner flying boat under Lt Cdr David E. Bunger landed on open water in the hills in February 1947. Source: Wikipedia "Bunger Hills", citing Byrd, *National Geographic* 92:429–522, 1947. **SOURCED (secondary).** A search snippet attributed to Gore et al. (Antarctic Science, 2025, "Rock cairn … Operation Windmill") says the "lake" was brackish, an arm of the sea, and that the landing was most probably on **Cacapon Inlet or Edisto Channel**. **UNVERIFIED**: the Cambridge PDF returned 403 and was behind Cloudflare, so I could not read it.
- **January 1948, US Navy Operation Windmill.** Source: south-pole.com "Operation Windmill" page (philatelic and history site). **SOURCED (secondary).**
  - The icebreakers USS *Edisto* and *Burton Island* rounded the Shackleton Ice Shelf and turned south into the pack at about 102°E on 8 Jan.
  - "Breaking a way through the ice proved very difficult." On 12 Jan they reached "a pool of open water northwest of Mill Island, 40 miles from Point 10 (later designated Point 5)".
  - Two Weasels set up "a small relay camp and gasoline cache … between the ships and the eventual shore camp".
  - Helicopters ferried men and equipment. The surveyors Merritt and Krause were ashore by the end of 12 Jan. "The small Bell helicopter crashed in a white-out seven miles from the ship." Everyone was evacuated on the morning of 15 Jan.
  - **This is the only recorded surface leg (Weasels over ice) between a ship and the oasis that I found, and it was a relay to a fuel cache, not a full haul.**
- **AAE Western Party (1911–14)** first saw Cape Hordern at the western end but "were unable to reach it due to heavy crevassing." Source: Wikipedia "Bunger Hills" (USGS GNIS text). **SOURCED (secondary).**

#### 1.1 Oazis (USSR), 1956–1958

Main source: Savatyugin & Preobrazhenskaya, *Rossiyskie issledovaniya v Antarktike*, Vol. 1, Gidrometeoizdat, 1999, §3.5, pp. 35–39, as cited on ru.wikipedia (raw text read). Where another source is used, it is named in the bullet.

**Reconnaissance**
- 23 Jan 1956: eight Soviet personnel arrived by **Li-2, An-2 and an Mi-4 helicopter**. They set up a **temporary base 20 km from the oasis** and worked for a week, then returned to Mirny.
- Gvozdetsky (English chapter "The First Soviet Expedition to Antarctica", southpolestation.com PDF) gives the same visit as: "A 13-man team, flown in two airplanes and a helicopter … spent six days … A temporary base was set up on the nearby glacier, and from this take-off and landing strip, the helicopter took the explorers to the center of the oasis." **SOURCED. The two sources disagree on team size (8 vs 13).**
- "Early April 1956": the first expedition cargo was delivered to the oasis area. **SOURCED (Savatyugin via ru.wiki). The method is not stated.**
- 6 Aug 1956: an An-2 landed on Lake Figurnoe, was caught by a hurricane, and returned without choosing a site. The site was chosen on 27 Aug 1956 after several reconnaissance flights, on the criteria of fresh water and landing areas for helicopters and airplanes.

**Construction, September–October 1956**
- Materials, food and "a small tractor" were airlifted to Lake Figurnoe. A temporary base with a KAPSh-1 tent was set up at the aircraft unloading point.
- 19 Sep 1956: a Li-2 brought six people. Cargo began moving to the site on 21 Sep.
- Gvozdetsky: "Tents, building materials, a small tractor, a radio station, scientific equipment, fuel (gas) and provisions were flown to the oasis."
- *Pravda* No. 291 (17 Oct 1956, reprinted on the xyh-polar LiveJournal): "During September–October the transport aircraft of the main base Mirny made **dozens of flights**, delivering a group of builders and the necessary materials from Mirny to Oazis." **SOURCED.**
- Opened 15 Oct 1956. A helicopter pad lay **300 m** from the houses.

**Supply regime**
- Summer: aircraft flew from Mirny to an **ice airfield 15 km from the station**, and helicopters carried the cargo on to the station. Winter: heavy and light aircraft landed directly on Lake Figurnoe ice next to the station. Sources: ru.wiki (Savatyugin 1999); Dubrovin (ivki.ru) agrees ("airfield … 15 km from the station on the glacier surface"). **SOURCED.**
- Station chief G. Pashchenko, *L'vovskaya Pravda*, 10 May 1957 (read in the archived polarpost.ru repost): "Communication Oazis–Mirny is only by aviation and radio. Aircraft from Mirny in summer landed on a glacier aerodrome **20 km** from the station. Delivery of cargo and people to Oazis is possible only by helicopter. With the onset of winter even this possibility disappeared." **SOURCED. Note the 15 km vs 20 km disagreement.**
- Pashchenko, *Vodnyi Transport*, 16 May 1957: "Aircraft and a helicopter delivered several dozen tons of building materials, scientific instruments, gas cylinders, fuel for the power station, food and other gear." **SOURCED.**
- Wintering parties: 2 people (1956), 7 (1957), 8 (1958). The station was mothballed on **17 Nov 1958**. Sources: ru.wiki; Dubrovin. **SOURCED.**

**Ships:** No source places a Soviet ship at the Bunger Hills coast for building or supplying Oazis. Cargo arrived by ship at **Mirny** and was flown east from there. **SOURCED as the stated route; "no ship call at Bunger" is my reading of the absence of any mention: NOT FOUND, not a negative proof.**

#### 1.2 Dobrowolski (Poland). Handover Jan 1959, not founded in 1979

The task brief lists Dobrowolski as "1979". The station is Oazis itself, **handed to the Polish Academy of Sciences on 23 Jan 1959**. 1979 was a re-occupation. **SOURCED** (PPR 1985; dobrowolski.igf.edu.pl).

- **1958/59.** Source: dobrowolski.igf.edu.pl/wyprawy/1958-1959/. **SOURCED.**
  - Left Gdynia 24 Dec 1958 on MS *Mikhail Kalinin* with the 4th Soviet Antarctic Expedition.
  - From Mirny ("approximately 340 km") on Soviet transport: **a Li-2 carried people and cargo to Scott Glacier; the final 20 km was by Mi-4 helicopter.**
  - Cargo about **3 metric tons**. Arrived 21 Jan 1959; took possession 23 Jan; stayed 9 days.
- **1965/66.** Source: dobrowolski.igf.edu.pl/wyprawy/1965-1966/ and the archived arctowski.pl "Z wyprawami radzieckimi" page. **SOURCED.**
  - Flown from Moscow to Australia by Il-18, then by the *Ob* to Mirny.
  - Mirny→oasis by **Li-2** intended to land on the frozen lakes. "During landing, the skis sank under the ice". The aircraft later sank. The party was retrieved after several days by a rescue aircraft.
- **1973/74 attempt: failed before departure.** Source: archived arctowski.pl (S. Rakusa-Suszczewski's notes). **SOURCED.**
  - "The station lies deep in the continent … It is separated from the sea by the **approximately 100-km-wide Shackleton Ice Shelf**. The nearest station is Mirny, but the distance is about 350 km. The basic problem … was transport. The helicopters at our disposal did not meet the required conditions. In the end the trip … did not take place."
- **1978/79.** Main source: Krzemiński & Wiśniewski, "The Polish Expedition to the A. B. Dobrowolski Station on the Antarctic continent in 1978/1979", *Polish Polar Research* 6(3):377–384, 1985 (journals.pan.pl PDF, text extracted). **SOURCED.**
  - **Ship and cargo.** MS *Zawichost* (Polish Ocean Lines; a "laker" of more than 7,000 t displacement, sides reinforced for pack ice) left Gdynia on 19 Nov 1978 and called at Las Palmas and Cape Town. It carried "about 100 t of the expedition equipment, including two Mi-2 helicopters, with the total weight of 20 t, and 40 t of fuel. The other 40 t included … generators, measurement devices, furniture, food." (The OCR text reads "1001"; read as 100 t, which matches the 20 + 40 + 40 breakdown.)
  - **Unloading at Mirny, not Bunger.** The plan was for the icebreaker *Somov* to lead the ship from the edge of the broken ice to the fast-ice barrier. *Somov* was delayed, so *Zawichost* made its own way to Mirny (27 Dec 1978). It moored on 30 Dec "at the wall of the snowdrift … on the sea ice, in the shade of the glacier cliff", after sounding the depth there.
  - **Problems at the unloading berth.** After the helicopters, spare parts and reserve fuel were landed, it had to leave quickly "because the keel continuously struck a submarine rock and the floating ice was pressing on." It re-moored on 6 Jan 1979 for the rest of the cargo and sailed on 8 Jan. **One Mi-2 was damaged during unloading.**
  - **Mirny → oasis (350 km).** Soviet Mi-8s first set up "a fuel station halfway between the 'Mirnyj' Station and Bunger Oasis". On 15 Jan a Mi-8 carried drums to "Shackleton Glacier". The team flew in by Soviet helicopter on 17, 18 and 21 Jan. "All equipment, fuel and most members of the expedition were transported … by Soviet helicopters."
  - **In the oasis.** Transport inside the oasis was by the remaining Polish Mi-2.
  - **Exit, 21 Feb 1979, after heavy snow on 20 Feb.** The men were moved "first, over 130 km from the station to the shelf Shackleton Glacier, where the auxiliary fuel store was; the helicopter transported the men and equipment four times. This operation lasted from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. … The helicopter took off from Oasis in fog and after 20-minute flight landed in a sunny weather on the shelf glacier." At 3 p.m. an **IL-14** from Mirny landed on the Shackleton Glacier and took most of the people and equipment to Mirny.
  - **The pickup ship was damaged.** They left Mirny on 17 Mar 1979 aboard MS *Antoni Garnuszewski*. While reaching Mirny, that ship's "ribs were pressed in and the hull was broken in two spots under the water line."
  - **Internal inconsistency.** "Over 130 km" in a "20-minute flight" implies about 390 km/h, well above any Mi-2 cruise speed. Either the distance or the time is wrong in the source. I flag this and do not resolve it. **UNVERIFIED as to which figure is right.**
  - **Wikipedia's "an overwintering attempt failed … evacuated to Mirny … on March 17" is contradicted by the primary source.** The expedition was a summer program (Nov 1978–May 1979), it left the oasis on 21 Feb, and 17 Mar is the date it boarded at Mirny.
- **1988/89.** Two Polish biologists sailed on RV *Akademik Fëdorov* with the 34th Soviet Antarctic Expedition and arrived at the oasis on 4 Feb 1989 (65 days). They stayed at Soviet **Oazis-2, "established in 1987 approximately 250 meters west"** of Dobrowolski. Source: dobrowolski.igf.edu.pl/wyprawy/1988-1989/. **SOURCED. The final leg is not stated.**
- **2021/22 (4th Geophysical Expedition).** Sources: portalstoczniowy.pl (10 Nov 2021); polsatnews.pl (8 Jan 2022); forumakademickie.pl; titan.uio.no blog. **SOURCED.**
  - Bremerhaven, 10 Nov 2021, to Cape Town; left Cape Town 5 Dec on *Akademik Fëdorov* with the 67th Russian Antarctic Expedition (RAE); Mirny on 29 Dec; left Mirny on 1 Jan "toward the Shackleton ice shelf".
  - **Helicopter "about 100 km" to the oasis**, landing 8 Jan 2022 at 10:10 Polish time "after several days of waiting for good weather". The route was "over tall cliffs and deep crevasses of the Shackleton Ice Shelf".
  - Total sea journey "approximately 15,000 km … approximately 2 months".

#### 1.3 Edgeworth David Base (Australia), 1986

**Before the base**
- 2 Mar 1977: MS *Nella Dan* (voyage V4) made a one-day call at "Bunger Hills". *The Silence Calling* (Bowden 1997, timeline) records this as the "First visit of an Australian field party to the Bunger Hills". Source: AAD schedule 1976/77. **SOURCED.**
- 6–9 Mar 1985: *Nella Dan* V7, stop logged as "Edgeworth David / Shackleton Ice Shelf". This was the reconnaissance: "eight potential sites identified during a short reconnaissance the previous summer". Sources: AAD schedule 1984/85; *Antarctic* Vol. 11 No. 1. **SOURCED.**
- Oct–Nov 1985: MV *Icebird* carried "fuel for Bunger Hill activities" and left Casey on 31 Oct "to establish an emergency fuel depot in support of Bunger Hill operations". "Unable to reach Merrit Island to lay the depot as planned", it left the fuel at **Snyder Rocks**. Source: *Antarctic* Vol. 11 No. 1, p. 13. **SOURCED.**

**Establishment**
- *Nella Dan* (V5) left Hobart on 4 Jan 1986 on "the 2,000 nautical mile journey to south west of Bunger Hills with 22 scientists and support staff". It was at "Edgeworth David" 13–17 Jan 1986. Sources: *Antarctic* Vol. 11 No. 1, p. 14; AAD schedule 1985/86. **SOURCED.**
- *The Silence Calling* (Bowden 1997, ch. "Living Dangerously"): "a field party of 22 … (with three helicopters), to the Bunger Hills, 450 kilometers to the west of Casey Station". **SOURCED.**
- Site description: "on the western side of Bunger Hills in a sheltered bay close to a small freshwater lake and about 8 km west of Dobrowolski". The base is fiberglass "apple" and "melon" huts, "transportable by helicopter". "The first shelter was flown in on 18 January and four the following day. The sixth, a darkroom, was accidently dropped on the Edisto Glacier when a helicopter safety catch malfunctioned." Source: *Antarctic* Vol. 11 No. 1, p. 14. **SOURCED.**

**Retrieval**
- *Nella Dan* (V8) left Hobart on 24 Feb 1986 and was at "Edgeworth David" 4–5 Mar. "Heavy pack ice covering 9/10 of the sea prevented her from approaching closer than **50 nm** and three helicopters transferred the 22 … to the ship". Sources: *Antarctic* Vol. 11 No. 1, pp. 15–16; AAD schedule. **SOURCED.**
- The same article: "ice was extending further out to sea than normal particularly near Australia's new Edgeworth David base", and *Icebird*'s drive gear cracked on 17 Feb, 1,200 nm south of Hobart, on "the last voyage to collect the Bunger Hills party". **SOURCED.**

**Later Australian ship visits**
- *Icebird* V1 at Edgeworth David 30 Nov–2 Dec 1986, after laying the "Snyder Rocks Fuel Depot".
- *Nella Dan* V5, 8–14 Jan 1987: see Q5.
- *Aurora Australis* V3, 10 Dec 1995: "Place summer party". S-76 helicopter flights to and from Bunger Hills are logged 4–8 Dec 1995 and 21–26 Feb 1996.
- *Aurora Australis* V4, 10 Dec 1997: "Fly-in fuel depot".
- *Aurora Australis* V6, 14 Mar 2000: "Conduct helicopter inspection of Apfel Glacier AWS and runway site enroute to Casey, on opportunity basis".

Source for all four: AAD shipping and air schedule archive. **SOURCED.**

Policy context: *The Silence Calling* records that director Bleasel set up EDB as part of a policy of cheap summer bases. Bleasel is quoted: "Unfortunately a great iceberg came and shut that off soon afterwards." **SOURCED. No date or location for the iceberg is given.**

#### 1.4 Oazis-2 / "Oazis Bangera" seasonal base (USSR/Russia), 1987 onward

- Built 1987 about 200–250 m west of Dobrowolski. Sources: dobrowolski.igf.edu.pl 1988/89 page; ru.wiki citing Headland 1997 and Riffenburgh 2007. **SOURCED.**
- A search snippet says "Since 1981 a field camp of the RAE" (AARI seasonal-bases page). **UNVERIFIED**: the fetched page carried only a link list.
- Used through the mid-1990s per en.wikipedia; reactivated as a seasonal base from 2017, when RAE geology moved to the oasis. Sources: en.wikipedia "Bunger Hills"; RPR 3(37) 2019. **SOURCED.**
- Access is by ship-based helicopter from the RAE vessel standing in the Mawson Sea. See Q2.

### Q2. Modern access

#### Australian Antarctic Program (AAP)

**Air route, 2023/24 (Denman Terrestrial Campaign).** Source: AAD "This Week at Casey", 22 Dec 2023. **SOURCED.**
- Almost 39 t of equipment, supplies and food went by C-17 Hobart→Wilkins.
- It was moved 70 km over snow (snow vehicles and track trailers) to Casey.
- It was then loaded into Baslers "for the 240 nautical miles, or 1.5 hour flight, to Bunger Hills".
- Two BK117 helicopters were flown down by C-17 and were based at EDB for the summer. Field sites lay "generally within 50 nautical miles of Edgeworth David".

**Air route, 2022/23 (camp preparation).** Source: AAD Initial Environmental Evaluation (IEE), "Denman Terrestrial Campaign – Operational Support", Sep 2022 (PDF read). **SOURCED.**
- Personnel by A319 Hobart→Wilkins.
- Basler and Twin Otter ski aircraft "deliver personnel, fuel and cargo from Casey station ski landing area (SLA) to the ice adjacent to EDB". The IEE notes these flight paths match "established and pre-existing landing areas, used annually to support Twin Otter flights between Casey and Davis stations."
- Cargo is "pre-placed on the adjacent ice in Transkripsii Inlet", taken by quad bike "to the edge of the tide crack", and over the crack "manually transferred to the field camp using walking tractors."
- C-17 dry-cargo airdrop. Ships (*Aiviq*, *Happy Dynamic*) carry cargo only as far as Casey.

**Recorded distances from Casey.** 440 km (AAD Field sites; IEE), 450 km (AAD news 2023–24; *The Silence Calling*), 455 km (AAD 2016 airdrop news), 430 km (AAD 2010 news), 240 nm (AAD 2023). **SOURCED (the figures vary).**

**Flight times logged in the AAD schedule.** Source: AAD schedules 1999/2000 and 2012/13. **SOURCED.**
- Twin Otter Mirny→Bunger Hills, 26 Dec 1999: "Departed Mirny 0715Z and landed at Bunger Hills at 0906Z" (1 h 51 min).
- Bunger Hills→Davis, 5 Jan 2000: "Departed Bunger Hills 0244Z. Landed davis 0701Z" (4 h 17 min).
- Davis→Bunger Hills, 16 Jan 2000: "arrived BH abt 4 hours later."
- Bunger Hills is also an interchange point: 4 Jan 2013, "This flight was met a Bunger Hills and pax were transferred to Basler for onward to Casey".

**Earlier Australian flights.** Source: AAD schedules. **SOURCED.**
- Twin Otter Casey→Bunger Hills→Mirny→Davis, 8–10 Nov 1988.
- Twin Otter Mirny→Bunger Hills, 1 Dec 1998.
- CASA C-212 Casey→Bunger Hills, 15 Dec 2009, "Taking field support personnel into Bunger Hills to set up camp".
- C-17 airdrop "Dome C, Bunger Hills", 15 Dec 2022.

**Season dates.**
- 2022/23: team at Casey late Nov, deployed within about a week, up to 6 weeks on site; C-17 drop in the 2nd week of Dec. Source: IEE.
- 2023/24: about 8 weeks over summer. Source: AAD news.
- 2024/25: December 2024–February 2025, "approximately eight weeks". Source: SAEF story.

**SOURCED.**

#### Russian Antarctic Expedition (RAE)

**2021/22 (67th RAE).** Source: AARI weekly bulletins, read in Russian for the weeks of 23–30 Dec 2021, 13–20 Jan 2022, 20–27 Jan 2022, 10–17 Feb 2022 and 17–24 Feb 2022. **SOURCED.**
- *Akademik Fëdorov* unloaded at Mirny by ship's helicopters "at a distance of about 23 km from the station" (24 Dec–1 Jan). It left Mirny at 08:00 on 1 Jan "for the Bunger Oasis area" and arrived on 3 Jan at 18:45, "awaiting flying weather for the start of helicopter operations."
- 5–19 Jan: "the ship was in Malygintsev Bay at a distance of 69 km from the area of the Oazis Bangera base, providing … helicopter delivery of personnel and cargo." Base de-mothballed on 7 Jan. Air-landed geology on 10–12 Jan was cancelled for non-flying weather.
- 19 Jan: the ship left early because of changed ice at Mirny. On 20 Jan it was at **65°45′S 100°47′E** heading out "in difficult ice conditions". **CALCULATED:** that point is about 58 km from Oazis and 56 km from EDB.
- 12 Feb: back in Malygintsev Bay. 12–13 Feb: helicopters removed 19 people and 5 containers. 13 Feb: base and all field camps mothballed. 14–16 Feb: geology on the coast by ship helicopter. The ship left on 17 Feb and reached Mirny on 19 Feb.

**2019 (64th RAE).** *Akademik Fëdorov* was in the Bunger Oasis area 23 Jan–16 Feb 2019. Source: RPR 3(37), 2019, p. 12. **SOURCED.**

**2017–2019 ship stations.** RPR 3(37), 2019, p. 17: support for the new seasonal base "requires the ship to stay in the Mawson Sea (specifically, in the area of the bays of Malygintsev or Milovzorov)". **SOURCED.**

**2015.** *Akademik Fëdorov* "took up the most convenient position near the barrier", about **90 km** from the Bunger Oasis field camp, and helicopters were to fly to the camp. Source: glonass-iac.ru bulletin, 13–18 Jan 2015. **SOURCED.**

### Q4. Landing places and the haul

**Where ships have actually stood for the oasis (all recorded transfers onward were by air):**

1. **1948.** "A pool of open water northwest of Mill Island". Helicopters, plus a Weasel relay camp and fuel cache. Source: south-pole.com. **SOURCED.**
2. **1959 and 1979.** The ship went to **Mirny**, about 350 km west. The transfer was by Li-2 or Mi-8 with intermediate stops on the "Scott Glacier" (1959) or the "Shackleton Glacier", meaning the ice shelf (1979, fuel cache). **SOURCED.**
3. **1986.** *Nella Dan*: arrived 13–17 Jan, distance not stated; held off at **50 nm** by pack on 4–5 Mar. **SOURCED.**
4. **2015.** *Akademik Fëdorov*: "near the barrier", about 90 km from the field camp. **SOURCED.**
5. **2019 and 2022.** *Akademik Fëdorov* in **Malygintsev Bay**, 69 km from the base area (2022), or "Malygintsev or Milovzorov" bays (2017–19). These are ice-front bays in the Mawson Sea. **SOURCED.** Their exact coordinates are **NOT FOUND** (see §3).
6. **2022, Polish account.** The helicopter covered "about 100 km" from near the Shackleton shelf. **SOURCED.**

**No ship has been recorded mooring at the ice-shelf front and landing cargo for a surface haul to the oasis.** NOT FOUND in any source read. The one moored unloading in the record, *Zawichost* in 1979, was at Mirny.

**Bottom line.** The 50–85 km range falls inside the spread of sourced figures. However:
- the ice front itself moves (next paragraph);
- the "edge" in the Polish 130 km figure probably refers to a different part of the shelf; and
- the only measured operational distances (ship to base, 2015–2022) are **56–90 km**.

**The front moves.** Thompson et al., *The Cryosphere* 17:157–174, 2023 (PDF read). **SOURCED.**
- The Shackleton Ice Shelf front advanced 18 km over 1962–2022, about 0.3 km/yr in 2015–22.
- The **Scott Glacier** floating front (between Mill Island and Chugunov Island, north of the hills) "has experienced more variability". It was about 10 km further inland in 2009 than in 1962, and lost a section more than 25 km long in April 2022.
- "There is no clear margin between Scott Glacier and Apfel Glacier offshore of the Taylor Islands."

**Terrain on the route (sourced descriptions):**

- **Oasis margin.** "A rocky hill area, to which the front of the ice-sheet is adjacent in the east, surrounded by Apfel and Edisto Glaciers in the south and west, and by the shelf glacier and long-term sea ice in the north". Ice-cored moraines occur at the margin. Source: PPR 1985. **SOURCED.**
- **Eastern oasis.** Relief up to 165 m, height differences up to 100 m, "glacier-deepened valleys with steep slopes", "coarse-block moraine" in the valleys. Source: RPR 3(37) 2019, p. 22. **SOURCED.**
- **Transkripsii Inlet.** An epishelf lake bounded by Edisto Glacier to the north and bedrock to the south. Depth to about 100 m; fresh water to 70 m. "It does not break up." Summer ice about 3 m thick, maximum 5 m. Tide cracks along the shores. Cryoconite pits up to 6 m across. Hills up to 100 m are air obstacles. The base→inlet shore is "5 km over rocks (a GAZ-71 transporter covers this in 3 hours)". An **Il-14 landed on this inlet in the 1960s**. Source: RPR 3(37) 2019, pp. 11–14. **SOURCED.**
- **Apfel Glacier.** An outlet glacier around the south of the oasis feeding Edisto Glacier. Blue ice; cracks under 25 cm wide, mostly water-filled; slopes under 2.5 %; no internal voids found in 9,000 m of ground-penetrating radar (GPR) lines. Source: RPR 3(37) 2019. **SOURCED.**
- **Crevassing.** The 2022 helicopter flew over "tall cliffs and deep crevasses of the Shackleton Ice Shelf" (titan.uio.no). The AAE Western Party was stopped short of Cape Hordern by "heavy crevassing" (Wikipedia/GNIS). **SOURCED.**

### Q6. Airstrip, blue-ice runway or permanent-facility proposals

- **Australia, 1999–2000.** Sources: AAD news "Australia moves closer to Antarctic air link", June 2000; AAD schedule 1999/2000; Skytraders article (Colborne). **SOURCED.**
  - Studies "pointed to … a suitable blue-ice runway site … in the Bunger Hills to provide an alternative landing site" for intercontinental flights, and a "staging point for intracontinental flights".
  - "It is expected that a Hercules C130 aircraft will be used to trial the Bunger Hills blue ice runway this coming summer."
  - The *Aurora Australis* V6 itinerary (14 Mar 2000) includes a "helicopter inspection of Apfel Glacier AWS and runway site".
  - The idea was later superseded. Choosing the long-range A319 "saved the large expense of establishing an alternate aerodrome and facilities at Bunger Hills, 250nm away."
  - **Whether the C-130 trial flew: NOT FOUND.**
- **Russia, 2008 and 2019.** Polyakov, Martyanov & Sukhanova, "Organization of a new RAE airfield in the Bunger Oasis area", *Rossiyskie Polyarnye Issledovaniya* No. 3(37), 2019, pp. 11–15 (PDF read). **SOURCED.**
  - In January 2008, a 53rd RAE helicopter reconnaissance of the whole oasis perimeter found one candidate: SW Transkripsii Inlet, with room for two runways (W–E and N–S).
  - In Feb 2019 (64th RAE), an engineering survey ran 27,300 m of GPR lines plus cores on the inlet ice (2.95–3.20 m thick). It found the ice "quite suitable" for light and medium ski aircraft (An-2, BT-67), and its bearing capacity sufficient even for "heavy aviation on wheeled undercarriage".
  - Zone 1 (smooth, used by Australian Twin Otters) is recommended for small aircraft; Zone 2 for medium aircraft.
  - A reserve strip was surveyed on Apfel Glacier blue ice (azimuth 90°, slopes under 2.5 %), with crevasses to be filled for the BT-67.
  - Bedrock strips were ruled out by "complex relief".
  - Purpose: logistics for the base and a base for the An-2 aerogeophysics lab. **No source found on whether the strip has since been commissioned: NOT FOUND.**
- **Australia, current status.** The AAP Aviation IEE 2020–24 (Appendix 5, read) lists Bunger Hills as a landing site: "depot for flights between Casey and Davis", up to 40 drums, "unprepared ice; facilities (Edgeworth David)". The AAD Intracontinental SLA page lists no formal Bunger SLA. **SOURCED.**
- **Poland, 2018–2022.** A revitalization program for Dobrowolski. A 2018 plan said access would be by Australia's icebreaker *Nuyina*; the 2021/22 trip in fact went by the Russian *Akademik Fëdorov*. The 2018 article mentions "a landing strip located 15 kilometers away". Seasonal visits plus autonomous year-round instruments. Source: dzienniknaukowy.pl 2018 (summary of the article). **SOURCED for the plan. The 15 km strip in a modern article is UNVERIFIED as a current facility; it matches the 1950s ice airfield.**
- **No proposal for a permanent year-round facility or a harbor** at the Bunger Hills was found. **NOT FOUND.**

Matches were found in 1976/77, 1984/85, 1985/86, 1986/87, 1988/89, 1995/96, 1997/98, 1998/99, 1999/2000, 2000/01 (Shackleton marine science only), 2003/04, 2009/10, 2012/13 and 2022/23. **No Bunger or Edgeworth match in any other season**, including 2023/24 and 2024/25, whose schedules do not log the Basler flights to Bunger (the field camp is known from news items).
- **AARI weekly bulletins, 2021/22**, by URL pattern. Pages for 06.01–13.01, 27.01–03.02, 03.02–10.02 and 30.12.2021–06.01.2022 returned 1.6 KB "404" pages [D-s, wrong date windows]. The windows 23.12–30.12.2021, 13.01–20.01, 20.01–27.01, 10.02–17.02 and 17.02–24.02.2022 were read. The AARI news listing URL returned 404.
- **Polish Polar Research 1985 and 1981 PDFs**: text extracted with pdftotext (OCR text layer).
- **RPR No. 3(37) 2019 PDF** (data.aari.ru): text extracted.
- **AAD IEE 2022 PDF; AAP Aviation IEE 2020–24 PDF** (documents.ats.aq): text extracted.
- **NZ *Antarctic* Vol. 11 No. 1 (1986) PDF**: text extracted.
- ***The Silence Calling*** (vdoc.pub HTML): grepped. epdf.pub returned a stub [D-s].
- **Wayback captures via curl**: arctowski.pl p=120 [F]; polarpost.ru t=338 (Pashchenko 1957 articles) [F]; geolmarshrut.ru [F, little on Oazis]. WebFetch is blocked from web.archive.org, so curl was used.
- **AADC gazetteer**, bounding box 65.4–66.0°S, 99.5–102.5°E [F]: Mill Island ("about 38 km north of the Bunger Hills"), Novosilskogo Dome, Shackleton Ice Shelf. Name searches for Malygin, Cacapon, Transkripsii and others found nothing [D-q] (the Australian gazetteer does not carry these Russian or US names in that form).
- **Cambridge Core** (three articles) and **researchers.mq.edu.au PDF**: [D-s] Cloudflare/403, by both WebFetch and curl.

- **dobrowolski.igf.edu.pl**: home, historia, expedition pages [F]. infrastruktura and 2023/01 had no logistics content.

### Self-corrections logged

- The task brief's "Dobrowolski 1979" is a date for re-occupation, not founding (the station became Polish in 1959). Corrected in §1.2.
- Wikipedia's "overwintering attempt failed … evacuated 17 March 1979" is contradicted by the 1985 primary communique. Noted in §1.2.
- *The Silence Calling*'s "January 1986" for the Bleasel/*Somov* besetment conflicts with the AAD schedule and Lex Harris (Jan 1987). Noted in Q5.
- I first read "Malygintsy" as a possible transliteration error. The AARI Russian text reads "бухте Малыгинцев" (Malygintsev Bay) consistently across four bulletins and RPR 2019, so the form is kept.
- Station distances (Casey, Mirny, Davis) are left to the sources' own figures. I did not compute them from recalled station coordinates.
