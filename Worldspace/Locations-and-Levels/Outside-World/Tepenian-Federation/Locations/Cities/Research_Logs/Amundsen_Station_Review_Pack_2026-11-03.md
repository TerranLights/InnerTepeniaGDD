# Amundsen Station: review pack for the town redesign steps (2026-11-03)

**Status: PROPOSALS ONLY. Nothing here is ruled or applied; no existing file was edited.** Prepared for review with the developer. Facts come from the 16 research units and the files named in section 1; no web research was done for this pack. Marks follow the units: SOURCED / UNVERIFIED / DERIVED / NOT FOUND. Anything I computed myself is marked DERIVED with the arithmetic.

**What I read.** The framing file (2026-10-29), the spec, the canon inventory (population, role, hostel, open rulings), tracker sections on Amundsen Station and the pair check (0.7), the composite language-bloc file (2026-11-03), the candidates file (104 rows and 14 facets), and, for each of the 16 units (B1 to B7, C1 to C9) plus the B-round gaps follow-up, the opening, the factual table, the NOT FOUND list and the conflicts list in its FINAL section; for C9 also the verification of the 13 uncertain rows. **I did not read every body line of the roughly 9,000 lines of the 17 research files;** the digests rest on their FINAL tables and lists. I read no Byrd file. The two 2026-10-29 files (real-world research; surface and subsurface town precedents) were not part of the 16 units and are not digested here.

**Developer decisions this pack rests on:** Amundsen Station becomes a proper town (2026-10-31); the population does not change (6,857 Census I, 4,891 Census II; about 1,121 and 913 humans); morale and belonging matter as much as survival (the *Better Call Saul* line, 2026-11-01); the station and Byrd must feel like two different DLCs (0.7); **new 2026-11-03: its people are a composite of all of Tepenia, because they come for periodic stays from everywhere.** Stage order: the CST works out who the people are (food, music, everyday life); holidays and religion come after the CST; language is a fact of the place, set early (Hawaiian first, then the language of whoever holds the majority; with no majority the second slot stays open).

## 1 · Short digests of the 16 units

Each digest: what it studied; the strongest findings (with the unit's marks); what it could not find. Figures are as the unit gives them; conflicts the unit itself flagged are noted.

### B1 · The big Antarctic stations as communities
Studied McMurdo as a town (populations, departments, services), the Pole's winter crew beyond science, social and civic life, food, arts, winter-over lengths, McMurdo's municipal services, and eleven other stations.
- McMurdo: NSF says up to 1,100 in summer and up to 200 in winter, about 2,300 people a year; mostly contractor support staff; about 70% men (AP 2023). SOURCED. Winter counts conflict across sources (126 at the start of winter 2022; 150 to 200; 188 and 280 "winter total"): the page never defines weekly average versus peak versus total.
- The Pole's 2010 facilities crew was 13 of 47 people; the kitchen had 4 to 5; the station has 154 single rooms, a gym, sauna, music room and arts room. SOURCED. Midwinter dinner, the 300 Club, a film festival and the Dome Brewers Association are on record.
- Other stations in the table: Rothera 27 in winter (22 staff, 5 scientists); Concordia 13 (8 and 5); Neumayer III 9; Troll 6 (209 applicants, undated); Halley VI unstaffed in winter since 2017.
- NOT FOUND: McMurdo head counts for the trades, galley and services; a Pole power-plant roster; any McMurdo "lifer" statistic; Pole religious services; couples policies. Several official sites were unreachable (listed in the unit).

### B2 · Cold-town utilities and staffing
Studied who runs water, sewer, power, fire and health in cold towns (Longyearbyen, Ny-Ålesund, Inuvik, Iqaluit, Utqiagvik, Nuuk, Nunavut, Alert, Norilsk) and compared with small US municipalities.
- Longyearbyen: 2,515 at the end of 2025; council about 140 full-time equivalents; fire and ambulance 25 (4 full-time, 21 part-time); hospital 23 staff; the energy plant lost 13 posts in the diesel change. SOURCED.
- Nuuk's utility: 411 employees for 20,600 customers, so about 50 customers per employee (DERIVED).
- Failures with dates: Utqiagvik west-side outage 11 February 2026 (pipes failing under thaw); Nuuk blackout 28 December 2024; Norilsk fuel-tank collapse 29 May 2020, over 20,000 t; Iqaluit trucked service suspended for lack of drivers.
- A US benchmark (municipalities of 5,000 to 9,999, 2023 sample): per 1,000 residents about 2.38 police, 1.12 firefighters, 1.03 highways, 0.76 water. DERIVED.
- NOT FOUND: operator or driver head counts for any named water system; control-room staffing for any microgrid; Longyearbyen energy-plant staff.

### B3 · Technical facility staffing and campus
Studied how big technical facilities house and staff their people: CERN, ITER, accelerators, nuclear plants and their towns, cable and satellite stations, observatories, record vaults, dams and canals.
- CERN (2019): 17,663 members of personnel, of whom staff 2,660, fellows 770, users 13,039. SOURCED (one report gives 12,428 users elsewhere).
- ITER (2024): 1,092 staff plus 218 project associates; about 5,000 regularly on the worksite; Europe 64% of staff. SOURCED.
- Paranal: a 120-room residence; about 100 people live there at a time; 9,450 meals and 4,630 overnight stays a month; a safe-state minimum of 21 people (2020).
- Prypiat on 1 January 1986: 49,400 people, average age 26; 13,414 apartments; 15 kindergartens and primary schools; 27 cafes seating 5,535; evacuated 27 April 1986. SOURCED. Pituffik: 636 dorm rooms; everyone must live on base.
- NOT FOUND: Bilibino plant head count, ALMA total head count, current CERN residence split, several vault staff counts.

### B4 · Remote contract and rotation worker communities
Studied communities of contract and rotation workers: Kwajalein, Ascension, Diego Garcia, Pituffik, Ny-Ålesund, Longyearbyen, Alert, Mount Pleasant, Tristan da Cunha, offshore platforms, Canadian and Australian work camps, Chilean mines.
- Kwajalein about 1,250 to 1,300 (2016–17) with about 900 Ebeye workers commuting daily; Ascension 806 (2016) with no right of abode; Diego Garcia in 2017 about 1,000 US plus nearly 2,500 contractors. SOURCED.
- Work camps: 27,313 people in Fort McMurray project accommodations (2021); Rio Tinto over 19,000 Pilbara rooms; Diavik 1,230 employees (2023); Escondida strike 44 days (2017).
- Longyearbyen: 2,556 (January 2025) with about 22% yearly outflow; 63.6% Norwegian.
- NOT FOUND: Kings Bay staff count, Alert contractor head count, Mount Pleasant current population by status.

### B5 · Timbuktu and record-keeping communities
Studied the Timbuktu manuscript libraries and communities built around keeping a record: Cairo Geniza, Dunhuang, Dead Sea Scrolls, Haeinsa, Mount Athos, Saint Catherine's, national libraries and archives, vaults.
- Timbuktu: custodians are family heirs under a public oath; 21 collections opened to the public after 1996; 32 to 35 family libraries; one estimate of 108 collections (2009); the Mamma Haidara library had 15 employees and about 5,000 visitors a year (2009). SOURCED.
- Dunhuang's protection team grew from 4 (1992) to over 200 (2024, UNVERIFIED). The Geniza fragments are now in about 70 institutions.
- Svalbard seed vault: no permanent staff; about 1.36 million accessions; late-2016 meltwater in the tunnel. NARA's St. Louis center lost about a third of 52 million military files in a 1973 fire.
- NOT FOUND: any formal "guild" of Timbuktu keepers; lending rules; ink and paper sourcing; many staff counts.

### B6 · Multinational facility governance and staffing
Studied how multinational facilities and neutral places are governed and staffed: CERN, ESA, ITER, ISS, Antarctic stations, Svalbard, Pheasant Island, Andorra, Neutral Moresnet, Tangier, International Geneva, internet exchanges, the UN.
- CERN: 25 Member and 11 Associate states, a directorate of five directors plus an information officer, 35 staff delegates; ITER's Council decides by consensus, then weighted vote. SOURCED.
- Concordia: a 2026 PNAS paper on one crew of 12 found subgroups by language and nationality, rising mistrust and falling cohesion. Andorra: two-thirds of residents cannot vote in communal elections.
- Neutral Moresnet: 256 (1815), 2,275 (1858), 4,668 (1914); an advisory council from 1859; no court and no appeal. Tangier's assembly had 27 seats in 1945 (DERIVED).
- UN Secretariat geographic posts: 22 unrepresented, 34 underrepresented, 29 over range (2019).
- NOT FOUND: the CERN Convention clause on "as wide a geographical basis as is possible", an ISS language policy document, terms of reference for Concordia's station leader, how Longyearbyen and Barentsburg settle disputes.

### B7 · Morale and long confinement
Studied how isolated crews are kept sane: stations, carriers, submarines, the ISS, Longyearbyen, Kwajalein, Villa Las Estrellas, Esperanza Base, and the research on the "third quarter" effect.
- McMurdo's ICE FM has about 30 volunteer DJs in winter and 75 in summer; the Pole has 154 rooms, two two-minute showers a week and limited satellite windows (about 5 hours a day, 2021 report); Concordia has a Saturday midnight pasta and a writing club. SOURCED.
- Carriers: the ship's store profits fund morale programs; submarines run patrols of 70 to 77 days or more.
- Villa Las Estrellas: about 80 in winter; hospital with one doctor, one nurse, two beds; school closed 2018 after 33 years (last class five pupils). Esperanza Base: winter 56 to 66, ten families (nine in 2010).
- The "third quarter" dip is supported in some datasets and not in others; Mars-500's abstract says 93% of mission weeks, its body text 8 of 74 weeks (about 11%): the unit leaves the conflict open.
- NOT FOUND: a formal sun-return ritual at Pole, Concordia or Halley; a station mediation protocol; the share of winterers removed for conduct; divorce rates.

### B-round gaps follow-up (closes gaps from B1 to B7)
- McMurdo galley 28 cooks and 35 dining attendants (2007); Pole carpenters 9 in summer, 2 in winter; the Pole's Rodriguez well about 14 gallons a minute. SOURCED.
- Hoover Dam operators: 16 per shift in three shifts at the start, later 7 per shift. Chuquicamata: 24,000 residents in the 1960s, closed August 2007, 3,343 families moved.
- ESA's staff survey (2,751 respondents, 2023): 28% experienced harassment. SIRIUS isolation crews: audio contact fell 89% (DERIVED). Bellingshausen has no jail; a suspect waited in the station church for the first flight (October 2018).
- NOT FOUND: McMurdo trades head counts, the Pole sewage operator's title and roster, Bilibino plant head count.

### C1 · Towns built around a megaproject, a science installation or a spaceport
Studied Boulder City, the Panama Canal Zone, Suez canal towns, Oak Ridge, Los Alamos, Hanford, Tsukuba, Akademgorodok, Kourou, Baikonur and others. The run stopped at a search budget limit and was resumed (afternoon rows marked "PM").
- Boulder City: 3,903 (1950) to 14,885 (2020); dam peak crew 5,251; a mess hall of 6,000 meals a day; gambling banned from the start and still in the charter; liquor legal only from 1969. SOURCED.
- Los Alamos: 1,500 (January 1943); LANL about 10,500 employees, 40% living in town. Oak Ridge: about 75,000 (1945), 7 gates, 17 denominations. Kourou: 659 (1961) to 24,824 (2023). Starbase TX: 6 (2008) to about 500 (2025). Rjukan peak about 12,000 (1920, conflicts with 8,350).
- NOT FOUND: the Suez company's head count; Skolkovo's actual population.

### C2 · Network relay stations, switching points and timekeeping institutions
Studied cable stations (Porthcurno, Bamfield, Valentia, Fanning, Direction Island), Chappe towers, Inca and imperial relay systems, exchange points, key ceremonies, GPS control, time laboratories, deep-space and satellite stations.
- Bamfield 1901: 20 operators; rent-free housing, tennis, cinema, dance hall, a 3,000-book library; closed 1959. Alice Springs repeater: six men.
- Chappe network: 534 towers in 1844, operators over 90% of personnel, working 365 days a year. DE-CIX: over 250 staff of 35+ nationalities. SvalSat: about 40 living locally, over 150 antennas (KSAT ad, July 2026).
- NIST Boulder lost its time signal in December 2025 (error under 5 microseconds). BIPM about 60 to 70 staff.
- NOT FOUND: several wartime and staffing counts (listed in the unit).

### C3 · Long cold roads, junction towns and leave hostels
Studied polar traverses, winter roads, Alaska roadhouses and junction towns, Pamir and Silk Road stops, hospices and caravanserais, seafarers' missions, soldiers' homes, the USO and Vietnam-era R&R.
- South Pole Traverse: 8 to 10 people, about three trips a season. Tibbitt to Contwoyto winter road: about 600 km, up to 10,922 loads (2007), four trucks every 20 minutes.
- Coldfoot 34 residents (2020); Deadhorse 1,310 (2020) plus several thousand transient workers.
- Leave hostels: Mission to Seafarers recorded 39,166 ship visits and 144,352 center visits in 2025; the USO has 250+ locations, about 800 staff and 21,000 volunteers; Vietnam R&R was one 5-day leave per tour, and Sydney took 280,000 to 300,000 visitors (1967–71). SOURCED.
- NOT FOUND: operator counts for several roadhouses; Tash Rabat's operator.

### C4 · Daily life, work and health at altitude and in extreme cold
Studied altitude towns and stations (Pole, Concordia, La Rinconada, Potosí, El Alto, Lhasa, Nagchu, ALMA) and cold-city routines (Yakutsk, Norilsk, Utqiagvik, Longyearbyen, Iqaluit), plus food, cooking and body-weight findings.
- La Rinconada (about 5,000 m): excessive erythrocytosis 44% and chronic mountain sickness 14% among 1,594 people studied. El Alto 885,035 (2024 census); its cable car has 10 lines and carried 78.7 million passengers in 2024.
- Nagchu piped liquid oxygen to more than 15,000 people in 23 institutions. Yakutsk closes schools at −45, −48 or −50 °C by grade; an 8-hour outage at −46 °C is on record.
- Concordia crews lost weight over winter (4.5% ± 6.1%); Rothera crews gained. McMurdo's galley froze 98% of its food and shipped a 13-month supply.
- NOT FOUND: Harbin heating-season dates; several population counts.

### C5 · Maintenance as a way of life
Studied crews that never stop upkeep: the Forth Bridge, Golden Gate, Eiffel Tower, Sydney Harbour Bridge, cathedral workshops, Ise Jingu, the Q'eswachaka bridge, alpine huts, lighthouses, pipelines, automated terminals, the BAM railway.
- Golden Gate: 13 ironworkers, 3 pusher ironworkers, 28 painters, 5 painter laborers and 1 chief painter (50 in all, DERIVED). Eiffel Tower: about 50 painters, about 60 t of paint per campaign.
- Strasbourg's cathedral fund has 33 employees (22 technical, 6 administrative, 3 documentation), daybooks since 1897 and account books since the 14th century. Ise Jingu rebuilds on an 8 to 9 year rite cycle, reusing two thirds of the old timber.
- Alpine clubs record over 50,000 volunteer hours a year. BAM towns: Severomuisk fell from over 12,000 (1984–90) to 992 (2017).
- NOT FOUND: the Forth Bridge's permanent post-2011 team size.

### C6 · Automated workplaces and human-robot practice
Studied automated container terminals, remote mining control rooms, Pilbara fly-in villages, unmanned platforms, factories, warehouses, a robot hotel, care robots.
- Maasvlakte II: "600 people" (union quote) and 45 remote operators trained. Qingdao: 9 remote operators versus 60 to 80 (vendor). Yangshan IV: about 100 versus at least 700.
- Henn-na hotel opened with 30 staff, later 7, with 243 robots, over half removed in 2019. About 10% of 9,000 Japanese care facilities had any care robot (2019); the seal robot Paro was liked, the Hug robot abandoned in days.
- Amazon: one study found severe injuries down 40% and non-severe up 77% (ILR Review).
- NOT FOUND: Fanuc Oshino staffing; several head counts.

### C7 · Faith, ritual and rites of passage in remote working communities
Studied chaplaincies and ritual forms in stations, camps, platforms, bases, islands and shared holy places. (The unit records that no religion is endorsed or transplanted.)
- McMurdo's Chapel of the Snows: three Air National Guard chaplains rotate each summer for 4 to 6 weeks. The Pole has no chaplain found. Longyearbyen's church is open around the clock, with 52 nationalities in the congregation.
- Kwajalein has one chapel shared by Catholics and Protestants (set up and torn down for each service). Schiphol's meditation center opened 5 April 2018 and has 60,000+ visitors a year. Maitri (India) had 21 people in 2021, of whom 11 took part in Janmashtami.
- NOT FOUND: Pole religious services beyond visiting chaplains; Tangyud and other monastery counts beyond those listed.

### C8 · Civic festivals, light and darkness, food, music and the arts
Studied sun-return days and light festivals, ice and snow festivals, mess halls and food cycles, choirs, print studios.
- Longyearbyen's sun returns on 8 March with a noon gathering on the old hospital steps. Tromsø's Soldagen is 21 January: one bakery of 35 staff baked 40,000 sun buns (2016). Rjukan's three 17 m² mirrors light about 600 m². Lerwick's Up Helly Aa: a committee of 17, 46 squads.
- Yellowknife's snow castle: 25 staff and about as many volunteers. Fairbanks volunteers harvest over 4,000,000 lb of ice a year (about 2,000 short tons, DERIVED). Kinngait's print collection runs since 1959.
- Australian station meals per year: Casey 56,000, Davis 47,000, Macquarie 27,000, Mawson 22,000 (2017).
- NOT FOUND: Rjukan mirror maintenance crew; several populations.

## 3 · The population makeup: a composite of all of Tepenia

### 3.1 What the ruling implies
- **Totals do not change.** 6,857 (Census I: 1,121 humans, 5,736 robots) and 4,891 (Census II: 913 humans, 3,978 robots), per `DR-58` ("the official census governs") and the 2026-10-31 scale ruling. The work is about who the residents are.
- **The spec's current table is a computed one** (31 rows: 5 Primary, 17 Significant, 9 Notable), built by the same method as for a resident city (the composition file reduced the naive figure to 0.2% of the humans and 1% of the robots, `Upper_Earth_Immigration_Composition.md` L1157 to L1159, then the shares were de-stacked). It carries two annotations that are not shares-based: Norway "(Amundsen heritage, Saint Roald)" and Chile "(unique Union Glacier route advantage)". Under `DR-32` (namesakes are never a basis for data) the Norway annotation is already questionable (canon inventory C-12); under a nation-wide composite neither annotation has a role.
- **What a composite means in the numbers.** Each nation's share of the station equals its share of all humans counted in the National Origin Totals (Census Section B, Census I humans). That is the only national-origin table in the census file; there is no Census II one (Section C holds city and subnet populations only).
- **The language consequence.** On that composite no language bloc is a majority (English 25.5 to 32.2%, Mandarin 8.2 to 8.5%, Spanish 5.1 to 8.3%, Korean 6.5%, Japanese 6.1%; `Amundsen_Station_Composite_Language_Blocs_2026-11-03.md`), so by the standing rule Hawaiian (later Tepenian) is the civic default and **the second-language slot stays open**. Naming a supporting tier (the 2026-11-03 definition: widely enough understood that business and daily dealings can be done in it) is the developer's call; English is the only bloc above about 10%.

### 3.2 The arithmetic
1. Source: Section B, Census I humans, 83 named nations plus a maritime fleet line (24,942, no nation), total 15,623,523 (DERIVED check: the 83 nations sum to 15,598,581; plus the fleet = 15,623,523).
2. **Share** of each nation = its humans ÷ 15,598,581 (the assigned nations only, so the table sums to 100%; the fleet has no nation). Example: Korea 1,016,930 ÷ 15,598,581 = 6.519%; Sinian Federation 1,011,660 ÷ 15,598,581 = 6.486%.
3. **Counts:** each share times the station's total, rounded by the largest-remainder method so each column sums exactly to its target (Census I humans 1,121; Census I robots 5,736; Census II humans 913; Census II robots 3,978). The spec's own note says robot figures use the same proportions as the human population, and I did the same. For example Korea: 6.519% × 1,121 = 73.1, so 73 humans; × 5,736 = 373.9, so 374 robots.
4. **Tiers** by the composition file's rule ("tier boundaries fall at the largest ratio gaps in the ranking"), with the spec's two refinements (Primary never more than six rows; no row at 4.5% or more is Notable). Rows at 4.5% or more: six (Korea, Sinian Federation, Japan, Australia, CSA, United Kingdom). The ratio gap from the sixth (United Kingdom 5.016%) to the seventh (Indonesia 4.263%) is 1.176, the largest among the first seven gaps, so **Primary = the six**. The next break is the largest gap among rows between 12 and 45 in rank: New England 2.522% to Austro-Bavaria 1.497% (ratio 1.685), so **Significant = ranks 7 to 13** (Indonesia, Germany, Brazil, France, Appalachia, Cascadia, New England) and **Notable = the other 70**. A literal reading (largest gap anywhere below the Primary break) would put the second break at Hawaii 0.062% to Alaska 0.035% (1.78), which divides rows of under one person apiece; I did not use it. This is a judgment call (question 2 below).
5. **Check:** shares sum to 100.000%; humans, robots and both censuses sum exactly to the totals.

### 3.3 Open question that needs the developer: the robots
The spec and the census apply the human proportions to robots. But robots are not immigrants; each has a fabrication-chamber city (every city has chambers, except Dome Fuji), and canon says robots at the station came from across Tepenia on rotation. The composite language file computed a second basis (B2) that weights by each city's robots plus humans; it differs by under 0.1 point for the top blocs. **The robot-side translation of a national origin needs his input** (what "from Korea" means for a robot built in a Tepenian chamber: the chamber city's composition, the founding nation of the person it is modeled on, or the same proportions as the humans). Until he rules, the proposal below applies the humans' proportions to robots, exactly as the spec does now.
