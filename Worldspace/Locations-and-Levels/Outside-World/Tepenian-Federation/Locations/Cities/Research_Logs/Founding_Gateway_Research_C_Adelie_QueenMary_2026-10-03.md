## 2. ROW 1: DUMONT D'URVILLE (Adélie Land)

### 2.1 Site facts (sourced)
- Station at 66°39′46″S 140°00′04″E on Île des Pétrels, elevation 42 m. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumont_d%27Urville_Station. French Wikipedia (Terre Adélie article) gives 66°39′47″S 140°00′10″E, https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terre_Ad%C3%A9lie. The IPEV page gives 66°40′S 140°01′E (https://institut-polaire.fr/fr/antarctique/la-station-dumont-durville/). The three agree within about 1 km.
- Adélie Land lies "between 136°E and 142°E" (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad%C3%A9lie_Land; French Wikipedia says the same), with a shore length of about 350 km. Time zone is stated as UTC+10 for the territory (both Wikipedias). **Solar time arithmetic: 140.0°E / 15 = 9.33 h, so the site's solar offset is UTC+9 as the brief says; the official station clock is UTC+10 per French Wikipedia.** Only mention this if clock time matters.
- Wikipedia reports Adélie Land is "known for its katabatic winds which push snow and sea ice away from the coast," and quotes a 1915 Science article calling it "the stormiest spot on the face of earth."
- French Wikipedia (Terre Adélie): average coastal wind 36 km/h, monthly gusts above 170 km/h, record gust 320 km/h on 16 June 1972. **One source; not cross-checked.** AAD's geography page (https://www.antarctica.gov.au/about-antarctica/geography-and-geology/) separately says katabatic winds "have reached speeds of 327 km/h along coastal slopes."
- Population: about 30 in winter, up to 120 in summer (Wikipedia); 25-35 winter, up to 120 summer (IPEV). Port Martin, an earlier station, was 62 km east.

### 2.2 The claim: longitudes
- Tokyo 35°41′23″N 139°41′32″E = 139.69°E (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo). The site is 140.00°E. Difference 0.31°, about 28 km at 35.7°N. **True.**
- Osaka 34°41′38″N 135°30′8″E (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka) is 4.5° west of the site, about 410 km at that latitude. Japan Standard Time is keyed to 135°E at Akashi (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Standard_Time), so Japan's clock meridian is 5° west of the site's meridian.
- Wikipedia's 140th-meridian-east table (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/140th_meridian_east) lists, north to south: Honshū's Chiba Prefecture (Bōsō Peninsula) and Tokyo Bay at about 35.5°N, then Pacific Ocean from 35°01′N, **Indonesia (island of New Guinea) 2°21′S to 8°14′S**, Arafura Sea, Gulf of Carpentaria, **Australia (Queensland and South Australia) 17°43′S to 37°29′S**, Indian Ocean from 37°29′S ("Australian authorities consider part of Southern Ocean"), Southern Ocean from 60°S, **Antarctica at 66°42′S, "Adélie Land, claimed by France."** This confirms that the meridian that links Tokyo Bay to the site is the same line, and that it ends exactly at Adélie Land. It also shows the line is land for roughly 25 degrees of latitude. Note: the meridian crosses Indonesian New Guinea (Papua), not Papua New Guinea; the PNG border is at 141°E, and Jayapura is at 140°43′E (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayapura).
- Initial great-circle bearing Tokyo to site: 179.9° (computed). **A near-due-south line is geometrically correct.**

### 2.4 Is a "near-due-south run" physically meaningful?
- **No for a ship; yes only as a line on a globe.** The 140°E line is blocked by New Guinea and the Australian continent (above). A hull from Japan must go **east of Australia** (Philippine Sea, Solomon Sea or Coral Sea, Tasman Sea, then south around Tasmania) or **west of Australia** (Indonesian archipelago via Lombok/Sunda, Indian Ocean, around Cape Leeuwin, then southeast).
- My waypoint estimates (NOT a source): Tokyo to the site east of Australia about 11,800 km (6,400 nm); west around Australia about 13,300 km (7,200 nm). The eastern route is only about 4 percent longer than the great circle; the western route is about 17 percent longer. Tokyo to Hobart by the eastern route about 9,170 km (4,950 nm).
- Great-circle distances to Hobart (computed, numbers only): Tokyo 8,771 km (4,736 nm); Osaka 8,709 km; Naha 7,942 km; Ishigaki 7,842 km. Hobart to the site is 2,682 km (1,448 nm). Hobart to Sydney 1,057 km; Tokyo to Sydney 7,827 km; Tokyo to Fremantle 7,936 km (my computation).
- Southern Ocean and Antarctic coast: no source found for a lane-by-lane description of the Tasman/Southern Ocean passage. Generic facts: the "Roaring Forties" blow 40°-50°S, "peak gusts over 25 m/s" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roaring_Forties); Antarctic sea ice peaks near 18 million km² in September and bottoms near 3 million km² in February, and "even during summer months, ships face severely limited access to coastal regions" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_sea_ice).

### 2.5 Japan's actual Antarctic route (CONTEXT (b), not an argument)
- Japan's ships do **not** use a due-south route. The icebreaker Shirase goes Tokyo (Harumi Wharf, since 2022 Tokyo International Cruise Terminal) to **Fremantle, Western Australia** (arrival about 25-27 November), then to Syowa Station (69°00′S 39°35′E); round trip about 5 months; 61st expedition logged 21,398.5 nautical miles. Source: Japanese Wikipedia, https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/しらせ_(砕氷艦・2代). The first-generation Shirase also stopped at Fremantle for a week (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/しらせ_(砕氷艦・初代)). Fuji's maiden voyage 1965-66 called at **Fremantle, Colombo and Cape Town** (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ふじ_(砕氷艦)). Sōya's expeditions 1956-62 called at **Singapore and Cape Town** (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宗谷_(船)). Shirase's new-generation record shows an entry at **Sydney** on 18 March (return leg; year not given in the extract) (2代 page).
- The brief's list of stopover ports is therefore **PARTLY SUPPORTED**: Fremantle, Cape Town, Singapore, Colombo and Sydney are documented; **Hobart/Tasmania as a Japanese stopover was not found in any Japanese ship page read.** English Wikipedia's "Antarctic gateway cities" article assigns Japan to Cape Town (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_gateway_cities), which conflicts with the Japanese pages (Fremantle for Shirase). **Contradiction between sources, unresolved; the ship pages are the more specific.**
- Japanese Wikipedia on Syowa says the straight-line distance from Yokosuka is about 14,000 km and that crews fly to Australia and board there.
- No evidence was found of any Japanese program presence in Adélie Land. (Absence in the fetched pages only; not a proof of absence.)

### 2.6 Nearest populous gateway to Adélie Land
- **Hobart is the nearest city of size** by great circle from the site (computed): Hobart 2,682 km (1,448 nm); Invercargill NZ 2,793 km; Launceston 2,839 km; Dunedin 2,921 km; Melbourne 3,224 km (1,741 nm); Christchurch 3,230 km; Adelaide 3,530 km; Wellington 3,530 km; Sydney 3,720 km; Perth 4,177 km.
- Population: Greater Hobart 255,250 (ABS, 30 June 2025, https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/population/regional-population/latest-release); Tasmania 580.7 thousand (ABS, 31 March 2026). Greater Melbourne 5,435,590 (ABS June 2025). Christchurch urban area 407,800 (June 2025, Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia's Tasmania article: "About 2,500 kilometres (1,300 nautical miles) south of Tasmania island lies the George V Coast of Antarctica" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasmania). My computation from South East Cape, Tasmania to Cape Denison is 2,610 km (1,410 nm), so the Wikipedia round figure is a rough one.
- **CONTEXT (b):** Hobart is the French program's departure port. L'Astrolabe carries supplies from Hobart, "5 round-trips between November and March" (Wikipedia); IPEV says 4-5 rotations Hobart to the station, 1,200 t cargo, 60 persons, station reachable October to March (IPEV page). AAD's 2026-27 schedule (https://www.antarctica.gov.au/antarctic-operations/travel-and-logistics/shipping-and-air-schedules/2627/): **VR1 departs Hobart 4 Dec 2026, arrives Dumont d'Urville 11 Dec (7 days), departs 19 Dec, back in Hobart 24 Dec (5 days); VR2 departs Hobart 30 Dec, arrives 4 Jan (5 days), departs 16 Jan, Hobart 21 Jan (5 days).** French Wikipedia on crossing time: "5 days at best if the sea is free of ice; nearly 35 days in 1976-1977; a failure in January 1949 when the coast was blocked by about fifty kilometers of pack ice." Historical: first expedition left Tasmania 26 Dec 1955 on Norsel, reached Petrel Island 2 Jan 1956 (7 days). Wikipedia says Hobart is "the home port for both Australian and French Antarctic operations."
- Access caveat (context): "Ice and strong katabatic winds often prevent landings, either by boat or by helicopter" (Wikipedia, Dumont d'Urville Station); the station keeps a skiway; a coastal airstrip 10 km away is used October to February (French Wikipedia, "Base antarctique Dumont-d'Urville").

### 2.7 Stronger or equal gateway overlooked?
The claim names Tokyo/Japan, not a gateway. If "gateway" means the **nearest populous coast facing the site across open water**, that is **Tasmania (Hobart)** at 2,682 km, about one-quarter of the Tokyo distance. Hobart lies 7.3° of longitude (about 600 km) east of the site, bearing about 187° from Hobart to the site, i.e., due south within 7 degrees. Mainland Australia's south coast (Melbourne, Adelaide) is 3,200-3,500 km. These facts do not make Japan a wrong pick on a "same meridian" criterion; they show the meridian is the only geometric argument available for Japan, and that it is carried by the map, not the sea.

## 3. ROW 2: DENISON (Cape Denison, Commonwealth Bay, George V Land)

### 3.1 Site facts (sourced)
- Cape Denison 67°0′S 142°40′E (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Denison); Mawson's Huts 67°00′31.6″S 142°39′39.7″E (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawson%27s_Huts). Commonwealth Bay 66°54′S 142°40′E, about 48 km wide at its entrance, between Point Alden and Cape Gray (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Bay).
- George V Land runs between 142°02′E and 153°45′E (Wikipedia). The site is therefore in the **Australian Antarctic Territory**, just east of the French sector (which ends at 142°E; the AAT "excludes French Adélie Land" at 136-142°E per https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Antarctic_Territory). Dumont d'Urville is 123 km west of Cape Denison (computed).
- **Solar time:** 142.66°E / 15 = 9.51 h, so UTC+10 (rounded) as the brief says; it sits near the midpoint between UTC+9 and UTC+10.
- Wind: Mawson called it "the windiest place on Earth." Wikipedia (Commonwealth Bay): winds "regularly exceeding 240 km/h" and "average annual wind speed of 80 km/h." AAE records: gusts 240-290 km/h (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australasian_Antarctic_Expedition); the AAD's own history site quotes the 1913 "puffometer" readings of 150-180 mph gusts and a six-hour afternoon average of 97 mph on 30 July 1913 (https://mawsonshuts.antarctica.gov.au/cape-denison/at-home/windy-windier/). **The "80 km/h mean" and "240 km/h regularly" are Wikipedia-only; the 1913 gusts are primary-sourced via the AAD site.** I did not find a modern meteorological paper in this pass.
- **Access conditions:** B09B, a giant iceberg, grounded in Commonwealth Bay about 2010, locked in fast ice and, in 2011-12 and 2012-13, prevented L'Astrolabe from entering the bay (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Denison). The Adélie penguin colony fell from 160,000 to 10,000 between 2011 and 2016, and the penguins had to commute about 65 km to open water (Commonwealth Bay and Cape Denison pages). The Mertz Glacier Tongue lost about half its length in February 2010 in a collision with B-9B (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mertz_Glacier), and that article states the calving cut bottom-water export by up to 23 percent in the Adélie Land region (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Bottom_Water). **Katabatic winds push sea ice off this coast, forming open-water coastal polynyas** (Adélie Land and Antarctic Bottom Water pages), which is the physical background to the access question: open water near the coast is common but the bay is storm-swept and iceberg-dependent.

### 3.2 The claim: "almost due south of Tasmania, so Hobart is the nearest populous gateway"
- **Longitudes:** Hobart 147°19′30″E = 147.33°E; site 142.66°E. The site is **4.66° west** of Hobart (about 380 km at Hobart's latitude). The great-circle bearing from Hobart to the Huts is 184.4° (computed), i.e., south with a 4-degree westward drift. "Almost due south" is fair; "same meridian" is not true.
- **Distances (computed, great-circle):** Hobart to the Huts 2,698 km (1,457 nm); Hobart to the mouth of Commonwealth Bay 2,686 km (1,450 nm); South East Cape, Tasmania to the Huts 2,610 km (1,410 nm); Hobart to the Mertz Glacier Tongue area 2,742 km.
- **Published figures (conflict, flagged):** Wikipedia (Mawson's Huts): "some 3,000 km (1,900 mi) south of Hobart." The AAD history site: "approximately 4,000 km south of mainland Australia" (https://mawsonshuts.antarctica.gov.au/). Wikipedia (Tasmania): "About 2,500 km (1,300 nm) south of Tasmania island lies the George V Coast." **The three published figures are 2,500, 3,000 and 4,000 km; my great-circle figures are 2,610 (from the island's south tip) and 2,698 (from Hobart). The 3,000 km and 4,000 km figures look like round or route-length numbers rather than great circles, and the 4,000 km figure disagrees with Melbourne (3,250 km computed) as the "mainland" nearest point.** Use 2,700 km (1,460 nm) with the method stated.
- **By ship (CONTEXT (b) and historical):** In 1911-12 Mawson's Aurora left Hobart 2 Dec 1911, reached Macquarie Island 13 Dec, resumed south 24 Dec, entered Commonwealth Bay 8 Jan 1912 (36 days including a Macquarie stop; Wikipedia, AAE page). Modern voyages: Chimu Adventures runs a voyage to Mawson's Huts "every year from Hobart," generally stopping at Macquarie Island and returning via the New Zealand sub-Antarctic islands; Aurora Expeditions' new ship Douglas Mawson (delivered 2025) is planned to include the Huts (Wikipedia, Mawson's Huts). **I could not open an operator itinerary with voyage length for the Huts (Chimu and Aurora pages did not yield it; Aurora's Ross Sea and East Antarctica page lists only 26- and 34-day itineraries without naming the Huts).** AAD supply: Nuyina resupplies Casey, Davis and Mawson plus Macquarie Island, and its 2026-27 schedule lists no call at Commonwealth Bay. Mawson Station, for scale, is a 10-12 day voyage from Hobart (Wikipedia, Mawson Station).
- **By air (the claim's Wilkins/Casey angle):** Hobart to Wilkins Aerodrome, 66°41′27″S 111°31′25″E, is about four hours by Airbus A319 (Wikipedia, Wilkins Aerodrome); AAD says the A319 carries about 400 passengers per summer and the C-17A hauls cargo; Wilkins is "70 km southeast of Casey" (AAD aviation page), 65 km in Wikipedia. Hobart to Wilkins 3,411 km (computed). **Wilkins is 1,347 km (computed) west-northwest of Cape Denison, so that air bridge does not reach Commonwealth Bay. There is no scheduled air access to Cape Denison in any source read.** The Hobart-Casey distance in Wikipedia is 3,443 km.
- **Population:** Greater Hobart 255,250 (ABS, 30 Jun 2025); Tasmania 580.7 thousand (ABS, 31 Mar 2026); Greater Melbourne 5,435,590; Greater Perth 2,452,765; WA 3,096.2 thousand (ABS). Hobart hosts the AAD, CCAMLR, IMAS, CSIRO, the Bureau of Meteorology Antarctic office and "the world's highest concentration of Antarctic and Southern Ocean research scientists" (AAD, https://www.antarctica.gov.au/nuyina/nuyinas-home-port/); Nuyina is berthed about five minutes from the city center with a dedicated Antarctic terminal. Wikipedia lists Hobart as a gateway serving Australia, France and China.

## 4. ROW 3: RELUNG PANEN (Bunger Hills oasis)

### 4.1 Site facts (sourced)
- Bunger Hills: 66°17′S 100°47′E, stretching 65°58′S-66°20′S and 100°20′E-101°28′E, about 450 km² (some sources 750-942 km²), "notably ice free throughout the year" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunger_Hills). Dobrowolski Station 66°16′29″S 100°44′49″E (Wikipedia); Edgeworth David Base 66°15′S 100°36′E, an Australian summer-only base opened 1986 (Wikipedia; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgeworth_David_Base, "up to 8" people). AAD (https://www.antarctica.gov.au/antarctic-operations/stations-and-field-locations/field-sites/): the base is "approximately 440 km west of Casey research station and 85 km inland from the Shackleton Ice Shelf." Wikipedia (Bunger Hills): "surrounded by glaciers," bounded on the north by the Shackleton Ice Shelf "which separates the area from the open sea." Russian Wikipedia: 46 km south of Mill Island.
- **Solar time:** 100.75°E / 15 = 6.72 h, so UTC+7 as the brief says.
- **Which coast?** English Wikipedia: "a coastal range on the Knox Coast in Wilkes Land." Russian Wikipedia: "western part of Wilkes Land... Knox Coast." Polish Wikipedia: "on the border between Queen Mary Coast and Knox Coast" (https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaza_Bungera). The coast boundaries: Queen Mary Land runs to **Cape Hordern at 100°30′E** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_Land); the Knox Coast runs 100°31′E to 109°16′E and is part of Wilkes Land (Wikipedia, Knox Coast and Wilkes Land). The Bunger Hills (100°20′-101°28′E) straddle that 100°30′E line, but the main body and both stations sit east of it. **The label "Queen Mary Land" is therefore weak: sources agree the oasis is on the Knox Coast or on the boundary.** A SCAR gazetteer entry could not be opened (placenames.aq is a search interface and I could not run its search). This is an **open thread**.
- The site's nearest regional features (all from Wikipedia unless noted): Shackleton Ice Shelf at 66°00′S 100°00′E, spanning 95°E-105°E, about 384 km along-shore, about 33,820 km², its western section projecting about 145 km seaward; Denman Glacier 66°45′S 99°30′E, about 110 km long, bedrock 3,500 m below sea level; Mill Island; Mirny (66°33′S 93°00′E, 345 km west by computation, Queen Mary Land); Casey (66°17′S 110°32′E, 437 km east by computation, AAD says about 440 km).

### 4.2 The claim, part by part
**(a) The longitudes.** Bunger Hills 100.75°E (Wikipedia: 100°47′E; Dobrowolski 100°44′49″E). Padang 0°57′S 100°21′11″E (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padang); George Town, Penang 5°24′52″N 100°19′45″E (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Town,_Penang); Kuala Lumpur 3°08′52″N 101°41′43″E (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Lumpur); Singapore 103.82°E and Jakarta 106.85°E are my inputs, not fetched. Computed east-west offsets from the site meridian: **Padang 0.39° (about 44 km) west; Penang 0.42° (46 km) west; Port Klang 0.64° (71 km) east; Kuala Lumpur 0.94° (105 km) east; Singapore 3.07° (342 km) east; Jakarta 6.10° (675 km) east.** So the Padang-Penang pair is exact and KL is very close; Singapore and Jakarta are not "on the site's meridians" in a tight sense.

**(b) The open-water run.** Wikipedia's 100th-meridian-east table (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100th_meridian_east): Strait of Malacca from 6°34′N, **Sumatra 2°43′N to 0°29′S**, Indian Ocean 0°29′S to 2°31′S, **North Pagai (Mentawai) 2°31′S to 2°51′S**, Indian Ocean from 2°51′S, Southern Ocean from 60°S, **Antarctica (Australian Antarctic Territory) from 65°39′S**. The 101st-meridian table (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_meridian_east) is similar: Sumatra to 2°27′S, Indian Ocean to 60°S, Southern Ocean to Antarctica at 65°24′S. **SUPPORTED: from the Sumatran coast south of about 2°51′S the meridian is water for about 62 degrees of latitude with no land until Antarctica.** Padang to the site: 7,264 km (3,922 nm), initial bearing 179.8° (computed). Teluk Bayur (Padang's port, "the largest and busiest harbour on the west coast of Sumatra," Wikipedia) 7,258 km.

**(c) Peninsular Malaysia is not on that open-water run.** Penang (7,972 km, 4,304 nm), Port Klang (7,703 km) and Kuala Lumpur (7,719 km, 4,168 nm) lie on the **Strait of Malacca**, a strait "900 km long, 65 to 250 km wide" between the peninsula and Sumatra (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca). Going south from there, a ship must leave either by the **northern mouth** (about 5.9°N, 95°E) or by the **Singapore Strait and Sunda Strait**. My waypoint estimates (not a source): Penang via the northern mouth about 8,600 km (4,640 nm); Port Klang via the northern mouth about 8,790 km; Port Klang via Singapore and Sunda about 7,880 km (4,250 nm); Singapore via Sunda about 7,550 km; Jakarta via Sunda about 6,830 km (3,690 nm). **So the run is on the meridian for the Sumatran Indian-Ocean coast; for Malaysia's west coast it is a meridian match on the map plus a detour by sea.** The east-facing, Pacific-side Malaysian and Indonesian coasts are not relevant.

**(d) The Antarctic end of the run.** Ice edge: the oasis is behind the Shackleton Ice Shelf; the Wikipedia article on the shelf gives its western part as projecting about 145 km seaward, so a ship reaches an ice-shelf front or sea ice, not a beach. AAD's 2026-27 schedule (https://www.antarctica.gov.au/antarctic-operations/travel-and-logistics/shipping-and-air-schedules/2627/) shows the Nuyina doing "Denman Glacier (4-7 Feb)" and "Kerguelen Plateau (2 Mar)" on its V3 voyage, so Australian ships can reach the Denman/Queen Mary coast in February. **There is no ship route to Bunger Hills in any source read.** Russian Wikipedia says the Soviet Oazis station (opened 1956, closed 17 Nov 1958) was supplied by aircraft from Mirny (about 360 km west) to an ice airfield 15 km from the station and then by helicopter. Polish Wikipedia says the station had "difficult access" and was "used only sporadically." Dobrowolski Station was dormant for lack of funds and "the high costs of air transport" (Wikipedia); Polish scientists returned in January 2022 (Wikipedia). **CONTEXT (b):** access today is Australian, via the Casey-Wilkins air bridge and over-snow or helicopter transport; the AAD page does not say how Edgeworth David Base is reached.
- Roaring Forties and Furious Fifties: Wikipedia (Roaring Forties): westerlies at 40°-50°S, average about 10 m/s, peak gusts over 25 m/s, the Brouwer Route of 1611 used them across the Indian Ocean to Java, and "below 40 degrees south there is no law; below 50 degrees there is no God." A due-south sail from Padang crosses the doldrum/trade belt, the subtropical ridge, the Forties, the Fifties and the sixty-degree belt (**I did not find a source for the Sixties' wind figures beyond the Wikipedia mention of the Screaming Sixties**).
- Antarctic sea ice: maximum about 18 million km² in September, minimum about 3 million in February; Wikipedia says ships face "severely limited" access to coastal regions even in summer.

**(e) Populations on that meridian band.**

Indonesia 288,315,089 (2025 estimate); Indonesia spans 95°E-141°E.
- Peninsular Malaysia: "some 20 million" in the 2020 census, 27.8 million in 2025 per the same Wikipedia demographics page (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Malaysia); Malaysia 34,374,300 (2026 estimate), 32,447,385 (2020 census). Wikipedia's state table gives Selangor 7,363,400, Perak 2,569,600, Penang 1,800,400, Kedah 2,217,500, Kuala Lumpur 2,067,500, Johor 4,186,300, Negeri Sembilan 1,240,100, Malacca 1,047,100 (**the table has no year label in the extract and the Selangor figure exceeds the 2020 census count, so treat as a recent estimate, one source**). ### 4.3 Verdict detail
- "Southeast-Asian maritime pair": **the pairing is geography-valid for the meridian only.** Sumatra (Padang) and Penang are 0.4° from the site meridian; Sumatra faces the Indian Ocean directly; Peninsular Malaysia's west coast faces a strait.
- "Open-water run ... crosses the Indian Ocean on the site's meridians": **SUPPORTED** for a due-south run from the Sumatran coast (water from 2°51′S to the ice edge).
- "Queen Mary Land coast": **CONTRADICTED in label (weak)**; sources place the oasis on the Knox Coast/Wilkes Land edge, with the Queen Mary Coast ending at 100°30′E. The Shackleton Ice Shelf (95°-105°E) and Denman Glacier (99°30′E) are Queen Mary Coast features immediately west.
- **Stronger gateway the claim overlooked:** yes, in distance. **Western Australia (Albany 3,650, Perth/Fremantle about 3,940 km) and Tasmania (Hobart 3,819 km) are about half the distance of Sumatra (7,264 km) and the nearest large populations.** The claim's two-country coast has the larger meridian match and the longer run. This is the developer's call; it is not a ruling here.

## 5. SURPRISING OR UNVERIFIABLE, AND CROSS-CUTTING NOTES

1. **Hobart is closer to Bunger Hills than Perth is** (3,819 vs 3,943 km, computed). Western Australia's coast faces the site only at its southwest corner (Albany 3,650 km, bearing 193°).
2. **The Hobart air bridge does not touch Commonwealth Bay.** Wilkins is 1,347 km away; the "Wilkins/Casey flights" angle in the brief does not apply to Row 2.
3. **Published distances to Cape Denison conflict:** 2,500 km (Tasmania page), about 3,000 km (Mawson's Huts page), 4,000 km (AAD history site, "from mainland Australia"). My computed great-circle is 2,610-2,698 km.
4. **The French sector ends 123 km west of Cape Denison.** The site is in the Australian Antarctic Territory but is reached on the same Hobart supply line as the French station, and French resupply was blocked from Commonwealth Bay in 2011-12 and 2012-13 by iceberg B9B.
5. **Row 1's meridian match is closer than the brief says** (0.31°, about 28 km). The best-known Japanese stopover in the sources is Fremantle, which is 24° west of the site's meridian.
6. **Time-zone nuance:** the site's solar UTC+9 (Row 1) differs from the official UTC+10 stated for Adélie Land.
7. **English Wikipedia "Antarctic gateway cities" lists Japan under Cape Town; Japanese ship pages show Fremantle.** Unresolved.
8. **Bunger Hills' coast label** is inconsistent across sources (Knox Coast in English and Russian; the Queen Mary/Knox border in Polish).
9. **Indonesia is not a party to the Antarctic Treaty.** This is a fact about the real world only and not an argument for or against any founder.
10. **Unverified in this pass:** operator itinerary durations to the Huts; modern wind climatology; sea-ice climatology for the Bunger Hills coast; Indonesian or Malaysian polar activity; the SCAR gazetteer entry for Bunger Hills; true shipping-lane distances (all distances here are great-circle, except my labeled waypoint estimates).
