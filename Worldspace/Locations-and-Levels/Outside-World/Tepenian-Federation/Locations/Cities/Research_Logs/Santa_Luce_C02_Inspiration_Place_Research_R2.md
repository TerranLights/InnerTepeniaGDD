## 1 · Winnipeg, Canada

**Round 1 covered:** population, climate, economy, institutions, transport, demographics, the big arts bodies, Festival du Voyageur, Folklorama, The Forks, Warming Huts, Royal Art Lodge, 1919 strike, winter parking bans, Portage and Main reopening. **Round 2 below chases:** Portage and Main closure reason, artist-run centers, the city's neighborhood-run recreation system, Indigenous and immigrant community institutions, hall culture, and further festivals.

### A. Other settings (neighborhoods, everyday spaces)
- **Neighborhood community centers as the everyday recreation layer** [K1, K10, P4] — "There are 63 independent Community Centers that operate 81 recreation facilities in Winnipeg." They are independent non-profits "run by volunteer boards who manage the facility, hire staff, and provide programs for residents," elected by community members; spaces include ice arenas, fieldhouses, gyms, meeting rooms, commercial kitchens, and, outside, sports fields, courts and **outdoor ice rinks**. City operating grants follow a **Universal Funding Formula** with three factors: heated square footage, population base and **number of outdoor ice rinks**; the grant "does not cover all operating costs," so centers also live on fees, rentals and fundraising. SOURCED: City of Winnipeg, https://www.winnipeg.ca/recreation-leisure/fitness-leisure-centres/community-centres (read 2026-10-29). The umbrella **General Council of Winnipeg Community Centers (GCWCC)** trains boards (governance, finance, facility operations, programming), manages City grants; City Council appoints three community representatives to its board (same page, SOURCED). GCWCC founding in 1971 at the Unicity amalgamation: snippet from a business directory, UNVERIFIED. A legacy City page (snippet, UNVERIFIED) says the City only installs and removes the knock-down outdoor hockey boards, while each center floods and maintains its own ice. [R: volunteer upkeep of shared facility]
- **North End, 1930s, as remembered** [K4, P4] — mostly Ukrainian and Polish households with some German, Italian and a few Anglo families; relief payments, unpaved streets, wooden sidewalks, uninsulated houses, grocery credit; small family bakeries and dairies; a Saturday-evening stroll along **Selkirk Avenue** as a pastime; winter hockey on **neighborhood rinks built by volunteers on city lots**; North End residents fed the On-to-Ottawa trekkers in 1935. SOURCED: Jerry Szach (ed. Nolan Reilly), "Playing in the Shadow of the Ukrainian Labor Temple," *Manitoba History* no. 60, Feb 2009, https://mhs.mb.ca/docs/mb_history/60/playinginshadow.shtml (read 2026-10-29). [K9]
- **West End / Sargent Avenue** as the center of Filipino settlement [K4, P9] — neighborhood shares of roughly 45–47% Filipino around Sargent near Arlington and Wall are repeated across pages that trace to one source. UNVERIFIED (snippets). Community start often dated to 1959 (four nurses settling), and large-scale garment-industry recruitment in the late 1960s–70s (Filipino Journal and CBC snippets, UNVERIFIED; CBC garment-workers page returned 403).
- **Saint-Boniface** — **Center culturel franco-manitobain (CCFM)**, 340 boulevard Provencher, created 1974 (building designed by Étienne Gaboury on the site of a demolished 1898 convent school/1913 Oblate juniorate); mandate to "offer, promote and sponsor cultural and artistic activities in French for the benefit of the entire population of Manitoba." Tenants: Théâtre Cercle Molière, Center du patrimoine, Union nationale métisse Saint-Joseph du Manitoba, a choir, Les Éditions du Blé (publisher), community radio Envol 91.1 FM, the provincial francophone youth council (Conseil jeunesse provincial), a restaurant; also a school, a dance school and an improvisation league; funders include Francofonds; sponsor Caisse Assiniboine (a credit union). SOURCED: https://ccfm.mb.ca/en/le-ccfm/ (2026-10-29). [K10, K13, P6]

### B. Subcultures
- **Indigenous urban institutions** [K1, K4, K9, P9]
  - **Bear Clan Patrol** — begun in the North End in 1992, "when the neighborhood faced poverty, addiction, mental health issues, and homelessness"; widely recognized from 2015 after the killing of First Nations teenager Tina Fontaine. Unarmed volunteers walk main roads and back alleys "watch, listen, and help," offering first aid, de-escalation, sandwiches and water "regardless of weather"; liaison with the Winnipeg Police Service's missing-persons unit; patrols five days a week; 250+ volunteers; chapters in Brandon, Portage la Prairie, Selkirk and Opaskwayak Cree Nation. The director calls the approach "culturally sensitive enforcement." SOURCED: Crown-Indigenous Relations and Northern Affairs Canada, "Peaceful protectors," https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1727206548879 (modified 2025-11-20; read 2026-10-29). Snippets (UNVERIFIED): the 1992 group was formed by workers at the Ma Mawi Wi Chi Itata Center and petered out; James Favel restarted it in 2014–15; volunteer counts quoted variously (900+ by 2018; 1,500+). [SM: competence under pressure] [K14]
  - **Ma Mawi Wi Chi Itata Center** — founded 1984 by Indigenous people, mostly women, after an 18-month-old Indigenous child drowned in an overcrowded non-Indigenous foster home; at the time Indigenous children were 70–80% of children in care in Manitoba (as the author gives it). The name reflects working together to help one another. In 1997, after staff concluded the agency had become a bureaucratic agency with an office on Broadway far from the people it served (paraphrase), a three-year consultation moved it to an asset-based community-development model; offices moved into the neighborhoods. Programs: a Pow Wow Club for youth, Indigenous Cubs and Scouts, an athletic-cultural program, ceremonies and traditional knowledge, support groups for women, men, young fathers, mentoring for young women, subsidized housing; about 200 staff, staff and board Indigenous. SOURCED: Jim Silver, CCPA Manitoba *Fast Facts*, 2009-09-10, https://www.policyalternatives.ca/news-research/fast-facts-nous-travaillons-tous-ensemble-afin-de-nous-entraider-ma-mawi/ (read 2026-10-29). Snippet (UNVERIFIED): today 50+ programs from 17 Winnipeg sites; federal CA$4.2 m (Nov 2024) for an Indigenous-led family care center in Elmwood. [K1]
  - **Manito Ahbee Festival** [K2] — annual, end-of-October weekend in Winnipeg; pow wow, marketplace and food, youth program, a jig-and-fiddle program; the name was "gifted to the festival through ceremony" from a sacred site in the western Whiteshell, an Ojibway phrase meaning "where the Creator sits," and the source of the province's name; values are the seven sacred teachings. SOURCED: https://www.manitoahbee.com/about-us (2026-10-29). Founding year not stated on the page; snippets say the 2025 edition was the 20th (UNVERIFIED); opens with a sacred-fire ceremony at **Oodena Circle**, The Forks (CBC snippet, UNVERIFIED); a youth title honors missing and murdered Indigenous women and girls (snippet, UNVERIFIED).
- **Ukrainian labor-left hall culture (historical)** [K4, K10, K13, K9] — the **Ukrainian Labor Temple**, 591 Pritchard Avenue at McGregor, built 1918–19 largely by volunteers, raided by police in the 1919 General Strike, "Winnipeg's only remaining labor hall from the period"; its basement print shop produced what the author calls the only Ukrainian daily paper ever published in Canada. Weekly cycle (1930s): Saturday stage presentations (dramas, operettas, comedies) "for over twenty years consecutively," Friday movie night, Sunday-morning Junior Section of 100+ children (boys: wood carving, model building, chess; girls: Easter-egg painting, embroidery), Sunday-night concerts with **mandolin orchestras**, choirs and folk dance; Ukrainian-language night school; unemployed men gathered there for company and debate; Jewish neighbors attended the plays; admission by "silver collection" on an honor system. In 1941 the federal government closed all Ukrainian Labor Temples under wartime measures. SOURCED: Szach 2009 (above). Run today by the Association of United Ukrainian Canadians; provincial heritage site 1995, city 1997, national historic site designation announced 2009 (snippets, UNVERIFIED). ### E. Cultural activities and civic life

SOURCED: Sam Thompson, Global News, 2025-06-27, https://globalnews.ca/news/11262494/portage-main-pedestrians-reopens (read 2026-10-29). ### A. Other settings
- **Where the OP meets now** [K1, K10] — the first 2025 ordinary meeting of the **Conselho do Orçamento Participativo (COP)** took place on 2025-02-04 at the **Teatro de Câmara Túlio Piva**, three hours, with the mayor, vice-mayor and secretaries debating with councillors. ## 6 · Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard

**Round 1 covered:** Kings Bay AS, the station list, NySMAC (snippets), access and supply, the mess and its hours, shop, welfare association and Saturday bar, the 2026 Code of Conduct (radio-quiet zone, alcohol, harassment, the nurse as person-of-trust), polar-bear rules, the mining disasters and 1963 government fall. **Round 2 below chases:** NySMAC's own documents and the 2013 Ny-Ålesund Charter (Round 1 open thread), the visitor guidelines, winter and polar-night life, staff rhythms, and the language question.

### A. Other settings
- **A town of stations, by type of occupancy** [K4, K7] — the 2013 Charter's station table distinguishes **permanently manned** stations (Norwegian Polar Institute's Sverdrup Station; the German–French AWIPEV/Koldewey and Rabot; the Norwegian Mapping Authority's Geodetic Observatory; Kings Bay's Marine Laboratory; Zeppelin "open all year") from **seasonal** ones (UK NERC Harland Huset; Japan's Rabben; Netherlands' London 3 and 4; Italy's Dirigibile Italia; Norway's SvalRak; Korea's Dasan; China's Yellow River; India's Himadri; and others). Some stations occupy reused older buildings (for example the "Yellow House," the "Mexico Building," "London" houses). Station history: ESRO satellite telemetry 1964; Tromsø Northern Lights Observatory 1966; Norwegian Polar Institute station 1968; Cambridge summer field base 1972; first regular Norway–Svalbard air link September 1975 (one flight a week); the international facility began in 1991 with four stations. SOURCED: *The Ny-Ålesund Charter*, NySMAC, April 2013 (preface by Nick Cox), https://nyalesundresearch.no/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/N-A-charter-Revision-4.pdf (WebFetch could not read it; text extracted locally with `pdftotext` and read 2026-10-29). Note: Kings Bay's history page gives "Eleven institutions from 10 countries" with permanent stations (SOURCED, https://kingsbay.no/history/), the AECO guideline sheet "100+ institutions from 20+ countries" carrying out projects (below), and Round 1's Wikipedia figure 18 institutions from 11 countries — counts differ by definition and date. [P5]
- **The polar night** [K7, P4] — darkness or faint light until the sun returns around mid-March; at the Italian station a small group wintered 2021–22 keeping instruments running at the Gruvebadet lab (air sampling day and night, year-round) and the Amundsen-Nobile Climate Change Tower, while microbiologists studied winter soil microbes; −20 °C air, −44 °C wind chill; station manager Mauro Mazzola: winter temperature at Ny-Ålesund has risen "by about 3°C per decade" over 25 years. SOURCED: Jacopo Pasotti, *PolarJournal*, 2022-06-11, https://polarjournal.net/arctic-in-winter-does-not-sleep/ (read 2026-10-29). Snippets (UNVERIFIED): polar night without sunrise mid-November to end of January; Kings Bay keeps the harbor area dark for polar-night studies; food stored in large freezers for the winter.
- **Winter population and social pattern** [K10, K4] — at the start of January 2020 about 30 people were in town; the writer's four-person team was the only visiting research group; station staff usually five or fewer, most often two or three; "Everyone eats in the same cafeteria," so residents meet at least three times a day; a pub upstairs from the cafeteria opens every Saturday night; stations host movie nights and science talks; the writer reports "a very strong sense of togetherness" and that the town is not lonely despite half a year of darkness; with no Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or cell service and internet only by cable, people fix meeting places and times in advance and wait for late partners. SOURCED (a visiting researcher's blog): Kirstin Meyer-Kaiser, "Life in a northern town," 2020-01-04, https://kirstinmeyer.blogspot.com/2020/01/life-in-northern-town.html (read 2026-10-29). In winter the station is "home to around 40 persons" (AECO 2024 sheet, below). [R: one shared meal hall]

### B. Subcultures
- **Kings Bay staff (the service population)** [K7, K14] — Kings Bay runs the cafeteria, dorms, marine lab, flights and cargo; its employees are mostly in their 20s or 30s on **two-year contracts, with renewals limited so that staff return to mainland life**; station directors are often early-career researchers who stay one to three years. SOURCED (blog, Meyer-Kaiser 2020, as above). Kings Bay's own page did not state employee numbers (Round 1: 32 in 2007).
- **Station chiefs as the unit of responsibility** — under the 2004 accident and incident plan ("14 stations, one plan"), station chiefs are responsible for their own personnel; **the decision to call the rescue service in Longyearbyen "is the sole responsibility of the station chief,"** who first informs the **Kings Bay watchman**. SOURCED: Charter 2013. [K1, SM: competence under pressure]
- **K11 speech — Round 1 open thread, still open:** Norwegian is the host language; NySMAC's Terms of Reference, the Charter, the AECO sheet and the Code of Conduct (Round 1) are all written in English, which shows English as the written language of the international bodies; Kings Bay's 2012 annual report was mainly Norwegian with English sections (snippet, UNVERIFIED). **No source states the spoken language of the mess or between stations: NOT FOUND.**

### C. Leisure industries
- **Cruise visitors under research priority** [K8, K9, K6] — the AECO/Kings Bay "Research Station Specific Guidelines" (2024 revision): "Research has priority, and other activities must adapt"; peak activity spring and summer; meals all in the common dining hall; supplies, fresh fruit and vegetables arrive by boat **every 6–8 weeks** (Round 1's resident page said about every six weeks in summer); the museum and the Telegraph building are open 24/7 free; the store is open to visitors (shoes off in the hallway); **visitors must stay on roads and boardwalks, switch off Wi-Fi and Bluetooth within 20 km, fly no drones, keep away from the dog yard, enter no buildings without permission, and not leave the settlement without polar-bear protection**; the only public toilet is by the harbor office; parts of the settlement are bird sanctuaries; Svalbard's largest collection of protected buildings. ### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **NySMAC — Round 1 open thread, answered from its own documents** [K1, K6] — established 1994 (first meeting Ny-Ålesund, 27 July 1994; chair Pål Prestrud); founding institutions: Norwegian Polar Institute, Norwegian Mapping Authority, Norwegian Institute for Air Research, Alfred Wegener Institute (Germany), NERC (UK), National Institute of Polar Research (Japan). **Membership:** any scientific institution running long-term programs with (semi-)permanent presence; new members approved by NySMAC; **one representative and one vote per institution**; observers: Kings Bay AS, the Research Council of Norway, Svalbard Science Forum, SIOS; the chairs of the four flagship programs are invited. **Chair and vice-chair** elected for two years, staggered by a year, re-electable once, **from different institutions**; each institution pays its own travel. **Secretariat** hosted permanently by the Norwegian Polar Institute; internal "project information and discussion" forum. **Meetings twice a year** — spring usually at Arctic Science Summit Week, fall hosted by a member (the 2013 list shows meetings rotating among Ny-Ålesund, Potsdam, Cambridge, Kjeller, Ravello, Stockholm, Tokyo, Kunming and others). **Decisions by consensus; institutions that do not respond or attend "are assumed to be neutral ... and are therefore expected to support the consensus decision"** (clause added at the 12th meeting, February 2000). **Annual membership fee** €1,500 until 2022, indexed to Statistics Norway price regulation from 2023; it pays secretariat staff, travel and meetings, and no individual travel. Objectives include avoiding "negative competition between scientific institutions" and promoting "collaboration, mutual understanding and friendship." SOURCED: *NySMAC Terms of Reference*, May 2023, https://nyalesundresearch.no/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/2023-nysmac-tor.pdf (text extracted locally and read 2026-10-29); Charter 2013. A 2026–2030 strategy plan exists (snippet; not opened).
- **Mission Statement** (adopted by NySMAC 1997-08-24): seven points including "Give scientific research and monitoring priority over other local human activities, such as tourism and commercial fishing" and "The success of the station will be judged on its scientific merits and achievements." SOURCED: Charter 2013. [P6, P7]
- **K6 everyday rules (additions to Round 1), from the 2003 safety policy and later agreements** (Charter 2013, SOURCED): a verbal safety brief soon after arrival covering 14 headings (fire, medical care, boating, labs, waste, radio, airport, snow-scooter routes, "Ny-Ålesund rules (no wi-fi, ... radio silence etc)"); **in the village, rifles are carried unloaded with the bolt removed or withdrawn**; anyone carrying a rifle or flare pistol must pass a weapons course (with polar-bear behavior) from Kings Bay, the Norwegian Polar Institute or UNIS, **valid one year**; before going into the field, personnel record departure time, estimated return, route, radio/phone/weapon details on a station book or whiteboard, sign back in on return, and the station chief acts if they are overdue; stations monitor VHF channel 16 and a private channel; crevasse, polar-bear and avalanche information is posted on a whiteboard **in the Kings Bay mess building**. [K14, R]
- **Waste, fishing, gold**: the Charter's appendices also cover waste management, fishery protection in Kongsfjorden and gold prospecting at Svansen (headings read; contents not extracted in detail).
