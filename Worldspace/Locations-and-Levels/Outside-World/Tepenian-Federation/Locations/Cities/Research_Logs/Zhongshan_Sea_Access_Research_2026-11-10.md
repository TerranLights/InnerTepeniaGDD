# Sea access to the Larsemann Hills: real-world research (2026-11-10)

> **Filed 2026-11-10** in answer to the developer's words (`DR-133` item 2): *"Definitely highway, for sure. Whether by sea is also year-round, or seasonal, needs to be researched"*. Made by one fresh research reader on the open web (brief: real-world facts only; no conclusion about any fictional city). The decision on Zhongshan's coastal sea leg is the developer's.


*Fresh research reader, 2026-11-10. Real-world facts only, from web sources. Nothing here says anything about any fictional place. Bracketed numbers point to **Sources**. "Search-result text" means the fact was seen only in a search engine's summary and the page itself was not opened; treat those as single-sourced and unverified.*

## Answer in brief

**What the facts say: today, sea access to the Larsemann Hills is seasonal, and even in season ships rarely reach the shore.**

- **The season.** Every program that supplies the three stations there sends its ships between about **late November and mid-March**. The COMNAP catalog (2017) lists ship visits for Zhongshan in January, February, November and December [13], for Progress in January–March and December [14], and for Bharati in January–February [16]. Recent dates fit that window: Xue Long 2 entered the dense ice on 24 Nov 2025, and the big unloading ended 14 Dec 2025 [21]. The Xue Long's second call at Zhongshan was 14–15 Mar 2026 [25]. Akademik Fedorov worked at Progress 8–21 Feb 2023 [35]. Vasiliy Golovnin was at Bharati from mid-January to early March [40][41].
- **Why the rest of the year is closed.** Landfast ice (sea ice frozen to the coast) forms in early to mid March [4]. It is a continuous cover by about mid-April [2] and grows to roughly **1.2–1.8 m** by late October to late November [1][2][4]. It can reach **60–100 km north of Zhongshan** in the cold season [5]. It breaks up mid-December to late January [4] and stays gone for only a short time: the ice-free spell near Davis is about a month, and at Zhongshan it is about 10–20 days shorter [4].
- **Even in season, ships stop offshore.** The 2014 Larsemann Hills management plan says no anchorages or barge landings are designated "due to the variable sea ice conditions". It says vessels usually anchor about 5 n mi offshore, and that "helicopters are therefore the only reliable means by which persons and supplies can be transported ashore quickly" [12]. Recorded stopping points off Zhongshan run from about 12 km out (2025) to 44 km (2018, "the farthest of any year") [21][20]. Cargo then goes over the fast ice by snow vehicle and sledge, or by helicopter [21][22][12].
- **No midwinter voyage found.** This reader found no recorded ship voyage to the Larsemann Hills, or to anywhere in Prydz Bay, in June–August. The furthest into the off-season that real ships have gone nearby:
  - mid-October 2002: the icebreaker Kapitan Khlebnikov broke the last 5 km of fast ice into Davis [28];
  - late March 2015: Aurora Australis turned back to Davis through sea ice [55];
  - mid-March to late April 2026: an autumn science survey of Prydz Bay by Xue Long 2, with no landing at the stations [26][27].
- **Year-round ice classes exist.** Polar Classes 1–5 are rated for "year-round operation" in progressively thinner ice [42]. Arctic ports and mines with landfast ice do take ships in winter, but only with heavy icebreaker escort or purpose-built ice-class ships; the Voisey's Bay plan in Labrador also set a spring stop [51][53]. No year-round Antarctic sea service exists. This reader found no study of one.
- **No open-water channel in winter.** The nearest recurring polynya (an opening in the sea ice), the Davis Polynya, is centered about 107 km from Zhongshan (straight line computed by this reader), northwest of Davis. Like the others in eastern Prydz Bay, it is wind-driven and opens and closes several times a winter [10]. None of the sources describes it as a winter shipping channel to the coast.

**Does it break out every year?** Mostly, but not completely.

- **The offshore fast ice breaks up every summer** in all the sources that address it [3][4][5].
- **Some ice survives the summer.** "Occasionally, a small portion … may survive through the summer within some small shielded narrow fjords near ZS" [4]. In 2012, ice ringed by islands and grounded small icebergs survived to become multi-year ice [1]. In Nella Fjord, Hao et al. write (translated by this reader) that the summer ice breaks up but hardly melts and freezes together again after March, so it is mostly multi-year ice [7]. The management plan says the fjords and bays "are rarely ice-free", and that sea ice persists among the near-shore islands until late summer [12].
- **No year without a breakout was found.** No source describes a summer when the offshore fast ice failed to break out (see Open threads).
- **The opposite extreme at Davis.** In mid-July 1998, an "unusually extensive midwinter breakout" removed the inner fast ice. It followed a 958 hPa low that lasted 3.5 days [9].

### 1.2 The same coast at Davis (Vestfold Hills), for the regional pattern
- **Davis fast-ice record** [9]:
  - onset early to mid March;
  - long-term mean maximum **1.684 m** (1998 excluded; the record high was 1.98 m in 1993);
  - mean final breakout day of year 347 (mid-December) in the late 1950s, and day 367 (about 2 January) in the 1980s–90s, "mostly between mid December and February";
  - fast-ice season about **295 days**.
- **2009–2018 seasons:** Davis maximum 1.64 ± 0.08 m, reached mid-October to mid-November; ice-free period about 1 month [4].
- **COMNAP (2017):** Davis sea-ice breakup "December" [15]; Progress "January, December" [14]; Bharati "February" [16]. The Zhongshan field is blank [13].

### 1.3 Pack ice in Prydz Bay by season
- **The yearly cycle.** In the melt season (**November to March**) the bay's ice-free area "is usually connected with the open water outside of the bay". The freeze season is **April–October** [10].
- **Near shore.** Close to the Larsemann Hills, "the pack ice is extensive inshore throughout summer" [12].
- **Long-term trend.** Satellite records 1979–2010 show the ice season getting shorter near Davis and between the Amery and West ice shelves (areas tied to coastal polynyas), with strongly positive and negative trends side by side in Prydz Bay [11] *(search-result text)*.
- **What ships have met:**
  - **2008:** Xue Long hit very hard ice 58 km out and at times made 200–300 m a day. It was stopped by an "ice-bridge" of compacted ice 5 m or more thick and hundreds of meters wide, where multi-year fast ice met drifting pack [19].
  - **Nov 2025:** Xue Long 2 broke 68 n mi of ice in 5 days, through large areas of ridges and deep snow. The outer fast-ice zone had more than 10 km of chaotic broken ice [21].
  - **Oct 2002:** Kapitan Khlebnikov met "dense and at times consolidated pack ice, in places over 2 m thick" on the way to Davis, and, at some point on the same voyage, was halted near 65°S by wind-driven ice pressure [28][66] *(the pack details and the halt are search-result text)*.
  - **Late March 2015:** Aurora Australis needed almost two days for the roughly 400 n mi back to Davis "because of sea ice". After the evacuation it was 120 n mi out of Davis with "a further 100 nautical miles of ice" to open ocean [55] *(search-result text; the distances differ between reports)*.
  - **Autumn 2026:** on 27 Mar 2026, force-12 winds with snow over the Davis Polynya; new ice forming as needle, grease and pancake ice [26].
- **Icebergs:**
  - frozen into the coastal sea ice near Progress (Dec 2021) [33];
  - drifting icebergs complicated the approach to Bharati (Feb 2026) [41];
  - an iceberg about 80 km long skirted near Davis (Dec 2006) [31];
  - small grounded icebergs anchor the fast ice and the polynya ice barriers. A giant iceberg group grounding near Barrier Bay closed that polynya in September–October 2008 [10];
  - iceberg D-28 (about 1,636 km²) calved from the Amery Ice Shelf in September 2019 and was watched as a possible threat to shipping channels [59].

### 1.4 Polynyas
Hou & Shi (2021), using 2002–2011 sea-ice concentration data, found **four recurring polynyas in eastern Prydz Bay** [10]:

| Polynya | Location | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Barrier Bay | about 80.5°E 67°S, west of iceberg D15 and the West Ice Shelf | coastal |
| **Davis Polynya** (Massom's "Prydz Bay Polynya") | about 77.5°E 68.5°S, in the Svenner Channel/Dubinin Trough, NW of Davis | coastal |
| Four Ladies Bank | about 77.5°E 67°S | offshore |
| Iceberg D15 | about 81°E 66°S | offshore |

The **MacKenzie Bay Polynya**, at the Amery Ice Shelf front, is "the only polynya in the west" [10].

- **Are they open in winter?** Yes, intermittently. They were studied over **April–October** because they are a freeze-season feature [10].
  - Their areas "peaked more than 5 times and less than 10 times each year".
  - They are wind-driven, and they need at least one stationary ice barrier upwind, usually fast ice anchored by small grounded icebergs.
  - 2003–2011 mean April–October area: Davis Polynya **4,106 km²** (mean daily maximum 14,012 km²); the four together 15,624 km².
  - The fast ice in the Four Ladies Bank polynya "disappeared in late December or early January every year".
- **A polynya is not clear water.** Here a polynya means sea-ice concentration **below 75 %**, and the "core" of each is where that happens on 24 % or more of days [10]. Polynyas are places where new ice forms fast [26].
- **MacKenzie timing.** The MacKenzie Bay Polynya is reported open from late March to late October or early November *(search-result text; source paper not identified)* [67].
- **Nearest to the Larsemann Hills.** No polynya off the Larsemann Hills themselves appears in these sources. The Davis Polynya center is about **107 km** from Zhongshan (straight line computed by this reader from [10] and [13]).

**What the station-area management plan says (2014 text; revised in 2021, revision not read)** [12]:
- "No anchorages or barge landings are designated for the Area due to the variable sea ice conditions."
- "Vessels usually anchor approximately 5 nm offshore, depending on ice conditions."
- Small-boat access to eastern Broknes is "difficult and sometimes impossible" because ice debris, blown in by the prevailing northeasterly winds, lies up to hundreds of meters offshore.
- "Helicopters are therefore the only reliable means by which persons and supplies can be transported ashore quickly."
- "Resupply is generally undertaken once a year in summer" (Zhongshan).

**A national report on the problem.** Hao et al. ## 3 · What year-round access would take

### 3.1 The ice a winter ship would have to cross
- **Fast ice:** a continuous belt reaching 60–100 km north of Zhongshan [5], growing through winter to about 1.2–1.8 m by late spring [1][2][4].
- **Snow** on top: deep snow was reported in 2025 [21]. Xue Long 2's rating assumes 0.2 m of snow [44].
- **Older and deformed ice:** second-year and multi-year patches inshore [1][7]. Ridges and more than 10 km of chaotic broken ice sat in the outer fast-ice zone in 2025 [21]. In 2008 an ice-bridge of compacted ice was 5 m or more thick [19].
- **At Davis:** the fast ice is "notoriously hard to break through" [76] *(search-result text)*.

**Polar Class wording (Transport Canada, verbatim)** [42]. The class wording is intentionally vague, and a ship's Polar Class "may not reflect its actual icebreaking capability" [74] *(search-result text)*.

| Class | Rated operation |
|---|---|
| PC 1 | "Year-round operation in all Polar waters" |
| PC 2 | "Year-round operation in moderate multi-year ice conditions" |
| PC 3 | "Year-round operation in second-year ice, which may include multi-year ice inclusions" |
| PC 4 | "Year-round operation in thick first-year ice, which may include old ice inclusions" |
| PC 5 | "Year-round operation in medium first-year ice, which may include old ice inclusions" |
| PC 6 | "Summer/autumn operation in medium first-year ice, which may include old ice inclusions" |
| PC 7 | "Summer/autumn operation in thin first-year ice, which may include old ice inclusions" |

**Ships now used in the region, set beside the ice figures above** (these are capability figures, not proof of what any ship could do there in winter):

| Xue Long | — | about 1.1–1.2 m [19][69] |

### 3.2 Real winter voyages, and what they show
- **Winter voyages in Antarctica have happened, but in the pack ice, not to a station through fast ice:**
  - **Polarstern, 8 Jun–12 Aug 2013, Weddell Sea:** about −30 °C and polar night with only a few hours of twilight. The press release calls the ship one of the few in the world able to navigate the Antarctic Ocean in winter [46].
  - **Akademik Fedorov, 13 May–17 Jun 1992:** in the ice in the Weddell/Atlantic sector [48].
  - **SIPEX (2007), SIPEX-2 (2012) and ARISE (2003):** late-winter and spring pack-ice voyages off East Antarctica at 115–125°E [71] *(search-result text)*.
- **The earliest Australian station voyages stop short.** In early September 2023, RSV Nuyina broke through sea ice to a point **144 km from Casey** and flew helicopters in; it did not reach the station [54].
- **A comparable East Antarctic coast: Lützow-Holm Bay (Syowa).**
  - The bay stays enclosed by thick ice even in summer. Shirase reaches Syowa by **ramming**, backing off 200–300 m and driving forward at full power [49].
  - The fast ice is 1–2 m thick, and multi-year ice exceeds 3 m [49].
  - Shirase **could not reach Syowa in 2011–12** ("the first time in 18 years"); cargo went about 30 km over the sea ice. It failed again in 2012–13 [50] *(search-result text)*.
  - Big breakouts there come at intervals of 10–20 years [75] *(search-result text)*.
- **Year-round landfast-ice shipping elsewhere (Arctic):**
  - **Yamal LNG (Sabetta):** Arc7 carriers rated to about 2.1 m sail year-round. In winter they use nuclear icebreaker escort on the Kara Sea legs. An Arc4 carrier failed four attempts to reach the terminal in early December, even with escorts [53] *(search-result text)*.
  - **Voisey's Bay, Labrador (environmental assessment panel, late 1990s)** [51]:
    - winter passages through landfast ice in **January–March only**, and only after new ice is at least 20 cm thick;
    - ships of CAC3 class;
    - icebreaker support needed in pack ice but not in the landfast ice;
    - **no shipping in April–May**, to protect seal pupping and the peak of travel on the ice by local people;
    - the ship track is about three beam widths wide and refreezes within hours, but the panel recorded cracks, ice bridges and more dangerous shore ice as effects.
  - The Voisey's Bay ship Umiak I (ICE-15) makes about 3 kn in 1.5 m ice [52]. Its real calendar was not settled: one single-source report has shipping halted until 22 May (2020), another a halt every 6 December [73].

### 3.3 Limits on record
- **Pressure and besetment.** In 1985 the supply ship Mikhail Somov entered the ice near Russkaya (Pacific sector) on 5 Mar. It tried to get out from 12 Mar to 12 Apr, then drifted beset from **12 Apr to 25 Jul (135 days)**. With the icebreaker Vladivostok it reached the ice edge on 12 Aug [47]. Closer to the Larsemann Hills: 200–300 m a day in 2008 [19], and 900 m in 9 hours in 2025 [77] *(search-result text)*.
- **Darkness.** The polar night at Zhongshan is given as "58 polar nights" a year [58]. AARI gives the polar night at Progress as 28 May–16 Jul [17]; the two counts differ. Bharati helicopter transfers were run "during daylight hours" [41]. Arctic practice in polar night: no optical satellite ice images, so ships rely on radar and SAR; some unescorted ships move only by day when helicopters can scout the route [70] *(search-result text)*.
- **Wind and cold:**
  - Katabatic winds blow from the northeast "on most summer days" [12][14].
  - Recorded maximum winds: Progress 129.6 km/h [14]; Davis 206 km/h [15].
  - A 15–17 Jul 2022 cyclone brought minute-mean winds above 30 m/s at Zhongshan and about 36 hours of blowing snow. In coastal East Antarctica, winter blowing snow can occur more than 70 % of the time [57].
  - Cyclones break up the fast ice: Nella Fjord, January 2012 [1]; Davis, July 1998 [9].
  - Winter monthly mean temperatures run −15 to −18 °C [12]; the July mean at Zhongshan is −19.7 °C [13].
- **Ice shelves and icebergs:** see §1.3. Grounded icebergs hold the fast ice in place [10] and complicate the approaches [33][41].
- **Wildlife on the ice.** Weddell seals pup on the local sea ice from October and molt on it from late December to March [12].

### 3.4 Studies of year-round Antarctic shipping
None found, after several queries (search log S30, S48, S69).
- The closest is the US National Academies (2007) on McMurdo. Its seaborne resupply is an annual late-January–early-February sealift behind one heavy icebreaker, which is a single point of failure [56] *(search-result text)*.
- Season-length projections by Polar Class exist for the Arctic (for example a 2013 Climatic Change study of PC3 and PC6 ships) but none was found for the Antarctic coast [72] *(search-result text)*.

## 4 · Coastal routes

### 4.1 East: Larsemann Hills ↔ Vestfold Hills (Davis)
- **Distance:**
  - The Vestfold Hills are 110 km to the northeast of the Larsemann Hills [12].
  - Zhongshan–Davis is **108.9 km (58.8 n mi)** in a straight line, computed by this reader from the COMNAP coordinates [13][15]. This agrees with COMNAP's 109 km from Davis to the "closest emergency facility" [15] and a helicopter database figure of 109.1 km [63].
  - By land, the plateau route from Davis is about 330 km [12].
- **The coast between the two** [12][62][31]:
  - the Dålk Glacier, about 15 km long, on the east side of the Larsemann Hills, draining into Prydz Bay between the Larsemann Hills and Steinnes;
  - glaciers flowing into Amanda Bay, seen from the ship 15 mi north of Zhongshan;
  - the Rauer Islands (about 68°51'S 77°50'E), 60 km northeast of the Larsemann Hills, lying between the Sørsdal Glacier Tongue and Ranvik Bay;
  - the Sørsdal Glacier (28 km long), whose tongue runs into Prydz Bay.
- **Sea passage time.** Summer transits by Aurora Australis:
  - Left the Zhongshan fast-ice edge at 13:50 on 5 Dec 2002 and was parked in fast ice 1 km NW of Davis by 20:00 [29];
  - left Davis at 14:00 on 21 Nov 2003 and was at the Zhongshan fly-off point (18 mi out) the next morning [30];
  - sailed overnight from Davis to 15 mi north of Zhongshan in Dec 2006 [31].
- **Ice on the route:**
  - Both ends were in fast ice in early summer [29].
  - The ship parked off Zhongshan was "totally surrounded by white ice, with no dark water visible" (21 Dec 2006) [31].
  - The Davis Polynya lies offshore of this stretch, in the Svenner Channel/Dubinin Trough [10].
  - No source was found for a winter sea route, or a sea-ice vehicle traverse, along this coast. The management plan gives only the overland route [12].

### 4.2 West and southwest: toward the Amery Ice Shelf
- **The sources disagree on distance.** The management plan puts the Larsemann Hills "approximately halfway between the Vestfold Hills and the Amery Ice Shelf" [12]. The COMNAP Zhongshan entry says "several hundreds of kilometers away from the Amery Ice Shelf, Grove Mountains and Prince Charles Mountains in the southwest" [13]. Not settled; see Open threads.
- **Landmarks:**
  - The Bølingen Islands lie 25 km southwest [12].
  - Sandefjord Ice Bay (about 69°40'S 74°25'E, about 46 km wide) forms the head of Prydz Bay, between the Amery Ice Shelf to the west and the Publications Ice Shelf to the east [60] *(search-result text, Wikipedia-derived)*. It is about **83 km** in a straight line from Zhongshan (computed by this reader).
  - The Amery front was about 255 km long in 2015 and 273.5 km in 2021 (Sentinel-1) [61] *(search-result text)*. Wikipedia's reference point for the shelf is 69°45'S 71°00'E [59].
- **Ice:**
  - The MacKenzie Bay Polynya forms at the western and central Amery front in the freeze season [10][67].
  - Massom et al. report a shortening ice season in the near-coastal zone "between the Amery and West Ice Shelves", a stretch that, on the map, takes in the eastern Prydz Bay coast including the Larsemann Hills (this reader's reading of the geography) [11] *(search-result text)*.
  - Xue Long 2 worked at the Amery front in autumn 2026 [27].
  - No source on regular shipping to the Amery front outside summer was found. Zhongshan supports Amery Ice Shelf research [13], and Chinese and Australian inland traverses have been staged from the Larsemann Hills [12].
