## 1 · #100 Trieste and the bora

**Claim to test:** a port and junction of several cultures where a violent downslope wind pours off the plateau onto the town, so wind (not cold) is the defining hazard, and the city built handrails and habits around it (K6, K1).

**Verdict on the claim:** **Largely confirmed, with corrections.** (1) The bora is a katabatic (downslope) north-to-northeast wind off the Karst plateau behind the city; Trieste is named as one of its three "mouths" (with Rijeka and Senj). (2) It is cold as well as violent (it can push temperatures below freezing and brings ice), so "wind, not cold" is better put as "wind, with cold riding on it." (3) Handrails do exist: fixed handrails (they carry a local name, recorded only in the slang file) survive in the Old Town (Città Vecchia) and on via Tigor; the **ropes** once stretched across exposed corners and squares were temporary, put up when needed and removed afterwards, and have since disappeared. (4) No formal "bora rulebook" was found: the city's responses are ad hoc closures (schools, waterfront roads, public gardens) ordered on the day, plus a body of household adaptations. (5) Since 2004 the wind has its own museum, and it has its own festival (Boramata; founding year not found).

### A. Snapshot
- **Location and size:** northeast Italy, head of the Gulf of Trieste, on a narrow strip between the Adriatic and Slovenia (Slovenia about 8 km east). Municipality area 85.11 km², elevation 2 m. **Population 198,622 (2026 figure as given on Wikipedia);** 17th-largest city in Italy. Capital of the autonomous region of Friuli-Venezia Giulia. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste (read 2026-11-20). [K+ snapshot] [P3]
- **What it is:** a deep-water port and maritime gateway for northern Italy, Germany, Austria and Central Europe; under Habsburg rule 1382-1918; free-port status granted 1719 by Charles VI (lasting to 1791); in the 19th century Austria's most important seaport and the fourth-largest city of Austria-Hungary; annexed to Italy after the First World War; Free Territory of Trieste 1947-1954 (Zone A under Allied military government); returned to Italy 1954. SOURCED: Wikipedia, Trieste (2026-11-20). [P5]
- **Defining feature, the bora:** "a northerly to north-easterly katabatic wind" that blows hardest when a polar high sits over the snow-covered interior plateau behind the Dinaric range while a low lies over the warmer Adriatic; it strengthens at night as air cools; most common in winter; blows in gusts; two kinds are named, the clear-sky "light" bora (*bora chiara*) under anticyclones and the cloudy "dark" bora (*bora scura*) with rain or snow. Gusts in the city "usually above 120 km/h reaching to maxima of near 200 km/h." SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora_(wind) (2026-11-20). The Trieste article gives up to 140 km/h on the port piers and says it can push temperatures below freezing. SOURCED: Wikipedia, Trieste. [K+ hazard] [P3]
- **Records (they conflict by station):** the 171 km/h gust of 1954 is described as the official city record (Istituto Talassografico, before the wind broke the anemometer) and was logged at Largo Pestalozzi / via del Mulino a vento, where a protective barrier was later built. SOURCED for the place and the barrier: https://www.ilnordest.it/vivere-il-nord-est/cultura/bora-trieste-dove-quando-mappa-museo-ykqxckw9 (read 2026-11-20). The station attribution and a 188 km/h gust at the Istituto Nautico in March 2010 are UNVERIFIED (MeteoWeb, search snippet only). A 50.8 m/s (about 183 km/h) gust at the ISMAR anemometer on Molo Fratelli Bandiera, night of 10-11 February 2012, is UNVERIFIED (search snippet; powderguide/thesis). On 11 February 2012 gusts of 168 km/h (average 88 km/h) were reported by the press: SOURCED below.
- **Season:** autumn to spring (ilnordest, SOURCED); the museum's founder suggests November to February for the strongest bora (artistsandclimatechange.com, SOURCED). **Trend:** meteorologist Renato R. Colucci is reported as saying violent bora storms tied to Arctic-continental air incursions have **decreased sharply in recent decades** (no figures given). SOURCED: https://www.ilpiccolo.it/cultura-e-spettacoli/bora-trieste-nuovo-libro-ies-d4jsdtk4 (article dated 2025-12-08). [K9] [P3]

## 2 · #101 Essaouira, Morocco

**Claim to test:** a windswept port that was once the coastal end of caravan routes and is now known for a large annual music festival; constant wind is part of its identity (K13, K3). Care: its wind is a trade wind, not katabatic; festival founding year to check.

**Verdict on the claim:** **Confirmed on all three points, with refinements.** (1) **Caravan port:** the modern city was founded in the 1760s by Sultan Mohammed ben Abdallah as the port for goods that crossed from sub-Saharan Africa via Timbuktu and over the Atlas to Marrakesh; the straight road to Marrakesh explains the site. (2) **Festival:** the Gnaoua (Gnawa) and World Music Festival was **first held in 1998** (its director says she conceived it in 1997), with attendance figures of 400,000-500,000 over four days reported, many concerts free. (3) **Wind:** a strong summer trade-wind (north to northeast) season, roughly April/May to September/October; a national wind farm sits 15 km south of town on a site averaging 9.45 m/s at 40 m. The same trade winds drive the coastal upwelling behind the sardine fishery, and the town's classical-music festival is named for them (Printemps Musical des Alizés). The trade wind's link to the town's identity is plainly real; the "Wind City of Africa" label is described by one guide as partly tourist marketing (UNVERIFIED, snippet).

### A. Snapshot
- **Location and size:** Atlantic port city, Marrakesh-Safi region, Morocco. **Population 82,962 (2024 census).** SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essaouira (read 2026-11-20). [P3]
- **Founding:** modern city built from the 1760s by Sultan Mohammed ben Abdallah (Mohammed III); French engineer Théodore Cornut directed the work for about 12 years; Ahmed el Inglizi (an English convert to Islam) built the harbor entrance. Earlier: a Portuguese fort (Castelo Real) fell to the Regraga in 1510, four years after it was built; under Juba II a purple-dye works processed murex shells on the Îles Purpuraires; a Roman villa excavated on Mogador island. **Claim to test:** roadside displays show live wind speed and gusts and roads close for wind rather than cold; volunteer search-and-rescue teams, funded partly by their own annual sales, are a respected local competence (K14, K1, K8). Care: the rescue association's funding details need checking.

## 6 · #23 Columbia River Gorge and Hood River, Oregon

**Claim to test:** a gap where air pours between interior and coast, producing strong winds that the town turned into a sporting culture (K13). Care: nation note, structure only; seasonal wind direction differs; use the "wind through a gap" structure.

**Verdict on the claim:** **Confirmed, with the seasonal structure made precise.** The Columbia Gorge is "the only near sea-level passage through the Cascade Mountains" of Oregon and Washington, and wind is driven through it by the pressure difference across the range. **Summer:** westerlies (high pressure offshore, a thermal low over the hot interior), strongest in the afternoon; these reliable westerlies made it a premier windsurfing area. **Winter:** easterlies, when cold dense air pools east of the Cascades and drains west through the gap, giving damaging gales at the western end, snow and freezing rain. Hood River, a timber-and-fruit town struggling after the timber decline in the 1980s, drew windsurfers from about the early 1980s (winds of 25 mph or more), grew a cluster of gear designers and shops, then kiteboarding; tech (drone and aerospace firms) followed from the late 1990s. The sport culture brought second homes and short-term rentals that the city now regulates.

### A. Snapshot
- **Hood River:** county seat of Hood River County, Oregon, on the Columbia at the mouth of the Hood River, about 30 miles north of Mount Hood, across from White Salmon, Washington; **population 8,313 (2020 census)**, 3,432 households; 3.40 sq mi; elevation 279 ft; climate Csb with about 30 inches of precipitation; post office 1868, incorporated 1895, county seat from 1908; Hispanic or Latino 23.5% (1,951 people, 2020); median household income USD 65,567 (2016-2020). SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hood_River,_Oregon (read 2026-11-20). [P3] [P9]
- **The gap wind:** gap flow forms when a pressure difference develops across the Cascades parallel to the gap; summer westerlies peak in the afternoon as the interior thermal low deepens; winter easterlies are strongest when a cold air pool forms in the Columbia Basin, with the strongest winds near the gap's western exit (hydraulic effects rather than a Venturi effect in the December 2000 case); over 90% of Portland's freezing rain comes with east-to-southeast winds; snow falls almost exclusively during easterly gap flow. SOURCED: University of Washington page on Columbia Gorge gap flow, https://atmos.uw.edu/~cliff/gorge.html (undated; read 2026-11-20). [K+ wind] [P3]
