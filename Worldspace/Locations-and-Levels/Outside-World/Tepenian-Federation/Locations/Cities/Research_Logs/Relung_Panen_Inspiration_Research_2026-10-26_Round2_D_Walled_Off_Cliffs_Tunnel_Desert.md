### A. Other settings

**Aurland: the old school-district map (how the municipality is cut into living units)**
- Because "the road to school was often long ... and during the winter months it could also be dangerous," Aurland was split into many school districts with ambulant teachers who moved farm to farm in two-week stints (Education Act of 1741). School districts: Vangen, Flåm, Berekvam, Myrdal, Undredal, Dalen, Vassbygdi, Nærøydalen (Dyrdal, Bakka, Skjerpi); plus teaching at high farms (Sinjarheim, Stondalen, Skjerdal, Otternes and others). First schoolhouses: Flåm 1862, Vassbygdi 1866, Undredal 1871, Dalen 1876, Vangen 1879 ... Myrdal 1921. Vangen's 1933 school was nicknamed **"Universitetet" (the University)** because nearly every level of schooling was gathered in it. SOURCED: https://kringom.no/en/aurland/school-districts-and-school-buildings-aurland (Ingvar Vikesland, published 7 Jul 1999, updated 8 Apr 2025).
- Today those old districts still show as separate village associations and halls (see D): Undredal, Vassbygdi, Gudvangen (Ljom), Bakka, Flåmsdalen, Upsetedalen (Myrdal), Rygg and Bakkane, Ytstebøen. SOURCED: Brønnøysund Register Center open API, query `kommunenummer=4641&organisasjonsform=FLI`, run 2026-10-26 (76 registered associations): https://data.brreg.no/enhetsregisteret/api/enheter?kommunenummer=4641&organisasjonsform=FLI

**Aurland: the summer-farm (støl) ladder above Undredal**
- Undredal used a ladder of summer farms: **Melhus** (~140 m, 2 km out; moved up in late May; 12–14 stølshus at most) → **Langhuso** (300 m, 6 km; by 2 July) → high mountain farms (Jonadalen, Nisedalen, Grindafletene) for ~3 weeks → back to Langhuso early September until Michaelmas, 29 September. The 2 July move-up day had "good food ... games and dancing outdoors"; until then maids ran the støl, after that the farmers' wives and children. In its peak 14–15 farmers used Langhuso; typical holding ~50 goats and 4–5 cows. In 2003 three Undredal farmers built a new summer dairy at Melhus; ~250 goats move between Melhus and Langhuso; after "a number of applications and setbacks" they won the right to keep making cheese from raw milk. SOURCED: https://kringom.no/en/aurland/mountain-farm-langhuso-valley-undredalsdalen (Åsmund Ohnstad, 2006, updated 11 Jun 2025).

**Aurland: the high plateau as a working and teaching space**
- The wild-reindeer herd of **Nordfjella Zone 1** (between Hemsedal, Hol, Aurland, Lærdal) was exterminated in 2017–2018 after chronic wasting disease (CWD) was found in 2016; the last animals were killed in March 2018 by the state nature inspectorate. SOURCED: Norsk villreinsenter report (below) for 2017–18; March 2018 and 2016 dates UNVERIFIED (search snippets of vetinst.no and jeger.no).
- The official working group wrote: "We face a long period without reindeer hunting in large parts of Nordfjella. Coming generations lose the chance to hunt reindeer, fewer are recruited into hunting experiences and traditions..." As a response, every 6th grade in the five municipalities gets a free "reindeer day in the mountain": in 2023, 29 pupils from **Aurland, Flåm and Borgund** schools went to **Flotane** (29 Aug 2023); staff from **Aurland Fjellstyre** (the mountain board) taught lasso, bow and arrow, reindeer games, served reindeer kebab cooked on a fire and dried reindeer heart. SOURCED: https://villrein.no/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Kunnskap-om-villrein-til-barn-og-unge-i-Nordfjella-rapport-og-regnskap-2023.pdf (Kamilla Rust, Norsk villreinsenter Sør, 2024; reprints Hallingdølen, 2 Sep 2023).

### B. Subcultures

The generation now growing up has no reindeer to hunt in Zone 1 (see A). SOURCED: Brønnøysund API; villrein.no report.
- **Shooting clubs** (an old rural sport club form): Aurland Skyttarlag (1872), Blåfjell Skyttarlag (1906), Undredal Skyttarlag (1950), Stålring Skyttarlag (2019), Sogn og Fjordane Sportsskyttarklubb (Flåm, 2006). SOURCED: Brønnøysund API (names, dates only).
- **Seasonal tourism workers (Flåm, Geiranger):** mostly foreign, housed by employers (rent levels in A). Geiranger, 2021: a group of 14 work immigrants took a Norwegian A1 course every Thursday 18 Jan–mid-May at Valldal school, set up by **Monja Mjelva of Hotel Union** "now that activity at the hotel is low"; seven of them from Hotel Union, others from Geiranger Fjordservice and Blåtind; most lived in Geiranger, Eidsdal and Norddal; from the Netherlands, Slovenia, Scotland, New Zealand, Bulgaria, Lithuania, Estonia (and a Czech worker quoted). English is the default working language "when guests come from many countries," and it becomes the language with managers and colleagues too; the COVID border closure brought mostly Norwegian guests and pushed staff to learn Norwegian. Quote, Mihhail (in Norway since 2006, speaks Russian, Estonian, English): "You almost have to like nature when you're going to live there." SOURCED: https://www.storfjordnytt.no/2021/samfunn/forebur-seg-til-sesongen-og-tilvaeret-i-noreg (Alfred Nilsen, 18 Mar 2021).
- **Hotel company sport:** "Hotel Union Geiranger Bedriftidrettslag" (company sports club, registered 2021). SOURCED: Brønnøysund API, query `forretningsadresse.postnummer=6216&organisasjonsform=FLI`, run 2026-10-26 (10 associations).

## 2. Cinque Terre, Italy

Round 1 covered population, railway, terraces, monorails, Sciacchetrà, the cooperative, visitor counts, the 2011 flood, Via dell'Amore, Monterosso's festival list, the Manarola nativity, Signorini. Round 2 adds below.

### A. Other settings

- **Hill hamlets above the sea villages:** besides the five villages there are "numerous smaller localities scattered on the hills, the most populous Volastra and Groppo." Riomaggiore comune: 1,426 inhabitants (Istat, 31 Aug 2020), of whom ~350 in Manarola. SOURCED: https://www.lecinqueterre.org/faq/abitanti-cinque-terre.php . Above Vernazza, the San Bernardino and Fornacchi areas (~4.5 km from the village center) are recommended as quieter places to stay. SOURCED: https://gigigriffis.com/ask-a-local-what-should-i-do-see-and-eat-in-vernazza-italy/ (interview with Miriana Rovaron, La Spezia travel agent; date not shown).
- **Riomaggiore's built details:** the **Marina** (harbor) is reached by stairs passing under the railway; the railway's arrival "divided the village in two, making an entire quarter, the Malborghetto, disappear." Before the Rio Maggiore stream was covered, bridges crossed the main street. **"Uservàn"** is the name for the upper back entrance of the tower houses, used to get into the house during a sea storm. **"Pidapünta"** is a little-known panoramic small square at the top of the stairway from the Marina toward the castle. SOURCED: https://www.lecinqueterre.org/10curiositariomaggiore.php . Boats are pulled up dry on the central ramp of the Marina. UNVERIFIED (snippet).
- **Only Monterosso has a real seafront promenade**; Monterosso has the one "almost sandy" beach, the others are pebble. Population: only Monterosso and Riomaggiore exceed 1,300; Vernazza under 800; Manarola ~350; Corniglia under 200. SOURCED: https://www.ilpost.it/2023/04/05/cinque-terre-vogliono-meno-turisti/ (5 Apr 2023); Rovaron interview (beach).
- **Trails:** "until about 50 years ago, the trails were the only roads that locals used to get around (apart from riding in their boats)" (Rovaron, interview above, SOURCED as her statement). The Park maintains ~130 km of trails; most tourism stays on the low trails, so one-way walking was introduced; seven themed upper routes, "Vie delle Cinque Terre," were created to spread walkers. SOURCED: https://www.avvenire.it/attualita/cinque-terre-non-basta-gestire-i-flussi-occorre-salvare-lidentita_109139 (Arianna Salvatori, 2 Jun 2026).
- **Terraces as a contested space:** tourists on the trails strip grapes; grower Heydi Bonanini lost at least 2 quintals on the Volastra–Corniglia path one year and growers now fence vines with high nets "not from insects or animals, but from people." SOURCED: https://www.dissapore.com/notizie/cinque-terre-turisti-rubano-quintali-di-uva-nei-vigneti/ (date not shown; cites Il Secolo XIX). Wild boars attack low vines. SOURCED: Avvenire 2024 (below).
- **Manarola's "Anfiteatro dei Giganti":** terraces once covered "more than a thousand hectares" at their greatest extent; abandonment over the last fifty years raised landslide risk; the Manarola community set up **Fondazione Manarola** to recover the terraces of the valley called the "Amphitheater of the Giants." SOURCED: https://www.fondoambiente.it/luoghi/manarola-i-terreni-terrazzati-dell-anfiteatro-dei-giganti (FAI "Luoghi del Cuore," 2016 campaign). Owners lend land to the foundation on 20-year leases at symbolic rent. UNVERIFIED (snippet).
- **Schools:** Vernazza comune has a state infant school (Vernazza-Capoluogo, Via San Francesco 56) and primary school "G. Guidoni" (Via Brigate Partigiane); lower-secondary schools are in Monterosso ("E. Fermi," Loc. Servano) and Riomaggiore; all belong to the Istituto Comprensivo of **Levanto**; upper secondary is outside (Levanto, La Spezia). SOURCED: https://www.tuttitalia.it/liguria/95-vernazza/31-scuole/ (read 2026-10-26).
- **Health and emergency space:** see E (volunteer ambulance associations, guardia medica).
- **Housing:** Riomaggiore mayor Fabrizia Pecunia (2023): "Residents are decreasing, it becomes impossible to rent a house, we are losing the cultural identity." SOURCED: Il Post 2023. Short-term letting both pushed people out and lets some owners stay; B&B pioneer Sara Barani (opened 1997): "if residents remain owners something is preserved. The real problem is the big real-estate agencies." Many overnight visitors now sleep in La Spezia, where asking rents rose from €8.05/m² (Oct 2021) to €10.30/m² (Oct 2025), ~28%. Riomaggiore (Jan 2025) limited new fry shops, take-aways, laundromats and money-transfer shops; Vernazza is following; Vernazza's attempt to tie business licenses to residence was rejected by the Region. SOURCED: Avvenire 2026.

### B. Subcultures

- **Anchovy fishermen (Monterosso):** anchovies are "the 'Pan du ma', the bread of the sea" of Monterosso people; from 29 June (St Peter's day) small boats go out at night with the **lampara** (lamp) and the **cianciolo** purse seine; fish are headed, gutted, layered with salt in chestnut barrels or terracotta jars, pressed with a ~10 kg weight and cured in the dark 40–60 days. SOURCED: https://www.lecinqueterre.org/cucina/acciughe.php . A cooperative, "Cooperativa Pescatori Cinque Terre Portofino," sells the catch daily except Sunday 7:30–9:30 at its port-road base; skipper Paolo Armento fishes March–October on the boat "La Lupa" with about a dozen crew. UNVERIFIED (snippet, viaggiando-italia.it).
- **Terrace winegrowers, old and returning:** "only the elderly have remained as farmers" (grower Samuele Heydi Bonanini, Possa winery, Riomaggiore). Bonanini returned to his grandparents' land after his parents left; to assemble 3 ha he had to buy **74 separate plots**. SOURCED: https://www.avvenire.it/economia/economia-civile/nelle-cinque-terre-rifugiati-al-lavoro-dalla-vigna-speranza-e-dignita_80864 (Paolo Lambruschi, 17 Sep 2024). - **Refugee terrace workers:** the Caritas "IntegrAzioni" program (protocol 2016; Fondazione Carispezia, Caritas La Spezia-Sarzana-Brugnato, the Park, farm unions; ~90 partners; 100+ courses, 115 people hired) trained refugees in dry-stone walling; Bonanini's **"Sciacchetrà del Migrante"** on the Lavaccio hill above Riomaggiore: 1+ ha cleared (half diocese land, half his), vines restored March 2022, 10 migrants trained, two hired; first 40 bottles from the 2019 harvest, 200 from the second; target 400. Quote, Moussa (Senegal, 32, arrived 2016 by boat from Libya): "Now I live and work in Riomaggiore, I help fix the walls and then work in the vineyard. ## 3. The Amalfi Coast, Italy

Round 1 covered the 13 comuni, SS163 and its 2021 landslide ordinance, lemons in outline, paper mills, colatura, Ravello Festival, the hospital, alternate-plate rules, Sant'Andrea feast dates, the Regatta, Byzantine New Year. Round 2 adds below.

### A. Other settings

- **The lemon terraces as a workplace:** terraces framed by dry-stone walls 3–7 m high, chestnut-pole pergolas, channels and cisterns that store sudden rain for dry months; the farmer "must also be a stonecutter, a carpenter and a hydraulic engineer"; farmers still carry baskets of up to 70 kg along mountain paths hundreds of meters up; growers are nicknamed **"contadini volanti" (flying farmers)** because they climb the pergolas. FAO designated the Amalfi lemon system a **GIAHS** (Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System) in August 2025. Quote, Gino Amatruda (third-generation grower, Valle delle Ferriere): "Whole families lived on lemons. Today it is very hard. Not many young people intend to continue." SOURCED: https://unric.org/it/i-limoni-di-amalfi-e-il-loro-territorio-stratificato-e-resiliente/ (UN Regional Information Center, 4 Nov 2025).
- **Women carriers ("formichelle," little ants):** formerly women did the harvest carrying up to 60 kg of lemons on the shoulder on a straw cushion over the neck, in chestnut baskets weighing 5 kg empty; the technique survives "although women no longer do it"; the Aceto family (lemon growers for 7 generations, since 1865) now uses a **teleferica** (small cableway) and runs a rural museum. SOURCED: https://www.scattidigusto.it/2021/06/02/ho-assaggiato-i-migliori-limoni-di-amalfi-biologici-e-raccolti-a-mano/ (2 Jun 2021).
- **Tramonti (inland, the "green lung"):** a "diffuse village" of 13 hamlets each with its own church (13 churches, 5 chapels incl. a rock-cut chapel of San Michele, a convent); ~700 vines counted over 100 years old, some ~400 years, pre-phylloxera; Tintore grape; Fiascone tomatoes hung under porches; fior di latte from the Caseificio al Valico at the Chiunzi pass; Sal De Riso's pastry lab with 85 staff on three shifts. SOURCED: https://www.quotidiano.net/itinerari/campania/tramonti-pizza-costiera-hozx6uig (Monica Guzzi, 4 May 2026). "More than 4,000 inhabitants": UNVERIFIED (snippet).
- **Cetara (fishing village, ~2,000 people):** Torre Vicereale (16th c.) now a civic museum of local "costaioli" painters; Church of San Pietro (9th c.) with a Latin–Arabic plaque. SOURCED: https://www.travelmar.it/en/visit-cetara (Travelmar ferry company page, read 2026-10-26).
- **Atrani:** resident Linda Orozco (15 years there): "we have lost seventeen percent of our resident population in the past fifteen years. There are only 300 homes in Atrani for the 800 residents ... no new homes can be built. When I moved here there were about six short-term rental apartments ... Now approximately 220 of the 300 homes are short-term rentals," so shops for locals (grocery, clothing, household goods) are replaced by tourist shops. SOURCED (as her statement): https://chandiwyant.substack.com/p/overtourism-problems-and-escaping (Chandi Wyant, 8 Sep 2024).
- **Positano's beach and stairs:** the Washington Post writer was asked to leave Spiaggia Grande "once, because I was not a resident; the second time, because I had not paid 40 euros to enter the private strand." In Positano, high season May–October, ~400 boats a day use a makeshift port; residents compete for ferry seats for school and medical visits. SOURCED (beach): https://www.boston.com/travel/world-news/2024/09/03/influencers-italy-amalfi-coast/ (Washington Post reprint, 3 Sep 2024). Port and ferry figures: UNVERIFIED (search snippet attributed to the same piece; not found in the part I read).
- **The off-season:** the season ends around the first weekend of November ("ponte dei Morti"); nearly all hotels close by then; seafront restaurants and beach clubs close mid-October; hydrofoils and ferries stop end of October and restart early April; the worst stretch is early January to early March, "when many inhabitants of the Coast go on holiday somewhere warm and most businesses close to prepare for the new season"; Amalfi and Vietri are the liveliest year-round, Positano "suffers most from seasonality"; on sunny winter Sundays people from nearby towns drive to the coast. SOURCED: https://www.positano.com/it/e/l-inverno-in-costiera-amalfitana (commercial tour site, undated; read 2026-10-26).

## 4. Big Sur, California

Round 1 covered the highway, closures 2017–2026, the 2017 footpath commute, 1981 land-use plan, workforce commute, schools in outline, fire brigades, health center, Henry Miller Library, Esalen, New Camaldoli, Esselen land return, marathon and River Run. Round 2 adds below.

### A. Other settings

- **Big Sur Village** (the "town" people confuse with the region): a ~1 mile strip of Highway 1 in the Big Sur Valley, 24 mi south of Carmel, with the post office, Big Sur Bakery, Big Sur Lodge (62 rooms), café, bar and grill, gallery, pub, yoga studio, artists' studios, the **Big Sur Grange Hall**, a few apartments, one of the coast's three gas stations, and Big Sur Station (visitor center, Pine Ridge trailhead). Slopes rise from ~135 ft to 3,500+ ft at 25–90°; after the 1972 Molera Fire a mudflow on 18 Nov 1972 buried the post office area in several feet of mud. Until ~1924 a dirt road often impassable in winter; the 30 mi trip from Carmel could take three days by wagon. The post office was "Arbolado" (1889–1910, at Posts) until residents petitioned for "Big Sur" (granted 6 Mar 1915). Coastal population ~1,463 (this article). Public bus (Monterey–Salinas Transit) three times a day Memorial Day–Labor Day, weekends only in winter, interrupted by high winds. SOURCED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Sur_Village,_California (read 2026-10-26).

### A. Other settings

- **Inside Begich Towers ("BTI"):** 14 stories; built by the military in 1956 (as the Hodge Building) for up to 1,000 officers, soldiers and dependents; transferred to civilian owners 1973; called the "wilderness skyscraper" at its dedication. ~100 residents "plus or minus 20" (2016; Whittier pop. 214 in 2015); contains condos, rental units, city offices, police, post office, mini-market, **gym, clinic, pharmacy and church**. Metal cupboards, 1950s counters, bright pink bathroom tile; by 2015 only one of two boilers worked (water, not glycol, in the pipes: "If they quit working, eventually the water would freeze and the pipes would burst," GM Dan Johnson). Board president Karen Dempster (retired school superintendent, 12th-floor three-bedroom condo): "I love the way the military builds things. I just can't stand their color choices." SOURCED: https://adn.com/alaska-life/2016/08/18/begich-towers-home-to-about-half-the-population-of-whittier-gets-a-much-needed-overhaul/ (Mike Dunham, 19 Aug 2016).
- **The basement and the school tunnel:** at lunch, students run "down the subterranean walkway that spills out into BTI's cavernous basement," past storage cages and **a room of rentable freezers ($15/month)** stocked with meat, bread, frozen dinners, "how people survive on days when it seems they may never feel the sun's warmth again"; the school has an indoor playground and a hydroponic garden whose equipment came from a police bust of a pot grower in a BTI condo; kids gather in the BTI lobby at lunch against the rules, a "big bone of contention": some older residents want the building to be more of an adult or retirement facility, others say "if the children go away, so will the energy in the building." Teacher Erika Thompson (ninth-floor condo): "I take the garbage out, I see my students. I do my laundry, I see my students"; she once held reading class in her living room at Thanksgiving. SOURCED: https://edition.cnn.com/interactive/2015/07/us/whittier-alaska-american-story (Moni Basu, CNN "Great American Stories," July 2015).
- **The only other housing:** "a small apartment complex down the road. It's the only other place to live in Whittier" (round 1's Whittier Manor, UNVERIFIED by name). Some, including the mayor then, chose not to live in BTI. SOURCED: CNN 2015.
- **Weather and ground level:** ~198 in of rain a year ("the wettest city in Alaska"), average snowfall 258 in, sun on ~133 days; in summer "there may be 1,000 boats in town," many on trailers on onshore lots; in winter snow covers "the storage, docks, stacked Conex boxes and dilapidated buildings and vehicles that can give Whittier the look of a junkyard at ground level"; "the crowds depart, most of the restaurants and tourist businesses close down." SOURCED: ADN 2016. CNN 2015 gives "22 feet" of snow and 80 mph winds; Governing gives 250 in and 60 mph. SOURCED: CNN 2015; https://www.governing.com/archive/gov-whittier-alaska-elevator-town.html (David Kidd; undated). Figures differ by source.
- **Winter population:** "fewer than 200 winter residents" (Governing); "only about 220 people live in Whittier year-round" (CNN 2015). SOURCED.
- **Summer jobs exceed residents:** at summer peak "500 jobs and 253 residents." UNVERIFIED (search snippet; source not identified).
- **Seafood plant:** Great Pacific Seafoods (in Whittier since the late 1980s) closed after a Chapter 7 bankruptcy; it had brought "more than 100 seasonal workers" who "used hotels and rooms and bought liquor and bought food" (R.C. Collin, owner of the Whittier Harbor Store); tender boats ran fish and ice between dock and fishing boats on its contracts. SOURCED: https://juneauempire.com/news/whittier-feels-effects-of-great-pacific-seafoods-closure (AP/APRN, 13 Jun 2016).
- **New cruise terminal fire (2026):** the new $80 million **Glacier Gateway Terminal** cruise dock caught fire on 14 Sep 2026 (reported ~11 a.m., out ~12:30 a.m.); "no working hydrants" nearby, so Anchorage and Girdwood crews ran tenders in 10-minute loops to a working hydrant (~161,000 gallons); the tunnel was closed for hours for emergency access; Phillips Cruises' 137-ft catamaran *Klondike Express* was badly damaged; mutual aid from Anchorage, Girdwood, Moose Pass, Bear Creek, Cooper Landing. SOURCED: https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/2026/09/15/afd-new-80m-whittier-cruise-dock-had-no-working-hydrants-when-fire-started/ (Zaz Hollander, 15 Sep 2026).

### B. Subcultures

- **Year-round tower residents:** "We're all family here," with "equal parts dysfunction. Everyone takes care of each other, but everyone also knows each other's business." Year-rounders work in commercial fishing, recreation and tourism, or for the state ferry and the railroad. Many residents own T-shirts reading **"POW" — "Prisoner of Whittier."** People who miss the last tunnel crossing sleep in their cars at the entrance. Residents are called **"Whittierites."** A 2000-era book called it "The Strangest Town in Alaska." SOURCED: CNN 2015.
- **Waves of arrivals:** Brenda Tolman (sign painter from California, arrived 1982 with twin boys: "I was terrified when I first came out of that tunnel ... The town looks like it should be in a horror movie"); then "oil barons occupied Begich Towers" and she lettered their boats; after the late-1980s oil-price fall "a whole new crowd" moved in; "more recently, waves of people from American Samoa, Guam, the Philippines and Hawaii have settled here." Tolman, the building's "matriarch," notary public and painter of carved wooden halibut, moved out because of BTI's "forced socialization": "At BTI, anyone can cruise over in their socks and give him [the mayor] their opinion." She keeps two reindeer in a pen across the street. "You gotta be good with being by yourself, or it can get lonely." SOURCED: CNN 2015.
- **Samoan community and church:** of two Sunday services in the BTI basement (near the storage cages), one is the Samoan congregation; Tolman attends for the music. SOURCED: CNN 2015. The church is **Malamalama Fou ("New Light Church")**, Samoan-language Baptist; pastor Su'e Su'e and family came from Samoa; first sermon 10 Nov (2013) with 20+ congregants; weekly Bible study and Samoan feasts on the first Sunday of each month. UNVERIFIED (snippets of Samoa News / CathNews; Samoa News 429; CathNews has an expired certificate and was not opened). Blogger Peter Santenello (26 Feb 2023) names the church and says 35 schoolchildren and "12 educators, most of which are Samoan" (wording ambiguous). SOURCED as his claim: https://petersantenello.com/blog/whittier-alaska-a-one-building-town-with-a-big-heart/ (the same post wrongly calls the 1964 quake the largest ever recorded; that part not used).
- **Cruise crews:** crew members come into BTI for refreshments; a resident (Jeanette) bakes chocolate-chip cookies for them; "Whittier offers them an opportunity to find companionship, spiritual sustenance, and some relaxation." SOURCED (as the blogger's account): Santenello 2023.

- **Volunteer fire and EMS:** the Whittier Fire Department is volunteer with mutual aid; a seasonal paid EMT (May–September) covers cruise-season call volume; EMTs must respond within 5 min on duty, 30 min off duty; someone is always on call at night for an ambulance to get through the tunnel. ## 6. Lüderitz, Namibia

Round 1 covered population, B4 road, railway, port tonnage, mariculture, diamonds, the hydrogen plan, water, schools, informal settlements, the Benguela compound (2009), GIZ planning, Kolmanskop, Shark Island, colonial buildings, the Crayfish Festival (2025) and the Speed Challenge. Round 2 adds below.

### A. Other settings

- **Fish factories and the work floor:** fishing is the town's main employer ("Industries, such as mining and tourism, are secondary"); over 5–10 years companies have moved quotas to Walvis Bay because of cost and remoteness: they "apply for fishing quotas from Lüderitz but once they get the quota, they move the processing of the quota to Walvis Bay." Factory pay (2021): N$14–16/hour at Lüderitz vs N$18–20 at Walvis Bay; unions push housing and transport allowances ("we start at N$3 000 for rent and the best you can get is N$500"). Regional councillor Suzan Ndjaleka: NovaNam went from up to eight vessels to about four, "a 50% reduction in the workforce and even seasonal workers go for months without an income." SOURCED: https://www.namibian.com.na/fishing-on-decline-in-luderitz/ (14 May 2021). NovaNam employs 1,200 temporary workers; accusations of race-based pay: UNVERIFIED (snippet). 2004: at least 300 NovaNam workers suspended after a protest that began when a supervisor was killed by a colleague on the production floor; the union said the firm used supervisors "as a buffer between management and workers." SOURCED: https://www.namibian.com.na/novanam-workers-suspended (5 Jul 2004).
- **2026 lockout:** about 300 employees of **Marco Fishing** (longline hake) locked out for three weeks+; the Nafau ǁKaras coordinator Simon Mattheus: "Should this month end while the lock-out is still in place, nobody will have a cent to take home"; seagoing basic pay N$4,000; union asked N$27/hour for land-based workers plus transport and housing allowances; CEO Steven Rajzman cites hake catches down "more than 45%" over five years and limited access to historical grounds south of Lüderitz; the union had planned to strike in November–December, "the most commercially critical period." SOURCED: https://allafrica.com/stories/202608210060.html (The Namibian via AllAfrica, 21 Aug 2026).
- **Harbor and waterfront:** a separate fishing harbor where "colorful boats are all tied up"; a waterfront "mall" of small shops, souvenir vendors and cafés opening onto a plaza over the anchorage; OK Grocery across the street; within five minutes' walk: a Shell station, another grocery, hardware, gas-bottle refill, laundry, bank. SOURCED: https://justalittlefurther.com/just-a-little-further/africa/first-things-first-luderitz-namibia (sailing couple David and Marcie Lynn, 6 Jul 2015).
- **Segregated geography:** "Here, the white people have their side and we have ours," Nama teenager Virginia April (2016): "Outside of work we don't communicate really, but no one has a problem with one another." Older residents remember apartheid-era segregation, which "still [has] an unmistakable imprint on life here." Shark Island (2016): "a quaint stretch of B&Bs and coffee shops," with a plaque to Cornelius Frederiks near a larger wall naming German pioneers killed in the wars. SOURCED: https://www.csmonitor.com/layout/set/amphtml/World/Africa/2016/0318/What-s-in-a-name-For-this-Namibian-town-it-s-all-about-colonial-history (Ryan Lenora Brown, 18 Mar 2016).
- **Planned worker towns:** the town council plans a settlement for the ~15,000 workers expected for green hydrogen, seeking to add 290,000 ha to its 11,290 ha boundary; new townships at sites "Note 2" and "Note 5," ~25 km and ~50 km east of town, also for a steel project and the Kudu gas power project; "polycentric model," "smart city concept"; Hyphen: "still in the planning phase." SOURCED: https://www.namibian.com.na/luderitz-to-build-worker-settlement-to-accommodate-15-000-workers/ (19 Nov 2024). **Hydrogen status (fills a round-1 gap):** final investment decision pushed to **end of 2027**; promised up to 15,000 construction and 3,000 permanent jobs, 90% for Namibians. SOURCED: https://www.namibian.com.na/hyphen-delay-raises-questions-on-promises-made/ (Shania Lazarus, 6 Oct 2026). Phase 1 resized to 3.75 GW, 175,000 t/yr hydrogen → 1 Mt/yr ammonia: UNVERIFIED (Engineering News snippet; page 403).
- **Inside the restricted desert:** Namdeb staff breathalyze drivers before they enter the former Sperrgebiet; old mining settlements with stone houses and a cemetery where German-inscribed tombstones stand beside "simple wooden crosses"; Bogenfels rock arch (55 m). SOURCED: https://www.namibian.com.na/trip-into-the-diamond-desert (22 Jul 2018).

### B. Subcultures

- **"Buchters":** "as residents of Lüderitz call themselves" — across racial lines the town name gives "an identity, however imperfect, to be a part of." SOURCED: CSM 2016.
- **The name dispute (2013–2016):** President Pohamba announced the town would revert to **!Nami≠nus** (Nama); opponents: Charles Pieters (colored, sells photos to tourists at Kolmanskop: "Our people are dying of hunger and you want to use the money for this?"; "Some people like to joke that Lüderitz is the next ghost town"); a resident of German descent: "It's ugly and no one likes the sound of it"; supporters: Mariana Draghoender (Nama, cook in a waterfront café: "for me it's easier to say !Nami≠nus than Lüderitz"), Jorab /Useb (Namibian Indigenous People's Platform). Ovambo beer-company sales rep Tiser Shivute: "we must also remember that he built our town." Outcome: the town stays Lüderitz; the constituency is !Nami≠nus. "Town of 12,000" (2016). SOURCED: CSM 2016.
- **Nama community:** Nama people speak Khoekhoe (Nama) and many also Afrikaans. UNVERIFIED (snippet). The 1883 purchase: Lüderitz bought the anchorage and 5 miles around it from Captain Josef Frederiks II of Bethanie for £100 in gold and 200 rifles, then a 140 km strip for £500 and 60 rifles. SOURCED: https://www.az.com.na/nachrichten/lderitzbucht-the-small-cove-of-the-south-2019-06-25 (Allgemeine Zeitung, 3 Jun 2019).
- **Ovambo contract workers (history):** 1930s–1980s, "Owambo men generally spent much of their adult lives as contract workers away from home"; recruitment through SWANLA, locally called **"omutete wOkaholo"** ("to queue up for the [identity] disk," for the numbered wrist bracelet); men housed in compounds for "single" men; contract workers were 43% of the black labor force in 1938 and 83% in 1971; the workers' "brotherhood" provided "well-being, social security, and recreational needs." "There had been labor action at the mines in Lüderitz ... almost every year between 1946 and 1959"; from 1949 the Cape Town Food and Canning Workers Union organized in **Lüderitz Bay, "the southernmost fish canning center."** SOURCED: https://www.namibian.com.na/namibias-workers-spearheaded-its-fight-for-independence-part-1/ (Heike Becker, 4 May 2026). The Benguela single-quarters compound (round 1) is in this lineage; the source does not say so explicitly.
- **Fishing workers and unions today:** Nafau (Namibia Food and Allied Workers Union) and NASAWU (Namibia Seamen and Allied Workers Union); see A. Seagoing crews vs land-based factory workers have different pay scales (AllAfrica 2026, SOURCED).
- **German-speaking community and heritage:** "Most of Lüderitz was designed by German architects ... today the town remains heavily German influenced in architecture, cuisine, and culture"; "khaki-clad German tourists who flock here year round." SOURCED: CSM 2016. Karneval, Schützenverein or other German clubs today: NOT FOUND (searched in German).

SOURCED: https://www.noonsite.com/business/luderitz-yacht-club/ (read 2026-10-26); Lynn blog 2015. Club hours 16:00–22:00 weekdays, 12:00–22:00 weekends: UNVERIFIED (snippet).
