### A. Other settings

**The education district: Suseong-gu**
- Suseong-gu is "one of the most prosperous and high-density areas of Daegu" and "the site of the city's most extensive hagwon district," nicknamed the **"Gangnam of Daegu."** Residents include "doctors, professors, judges and other high valued careers."
- 76.54 km², **407,570 residents (September 2024)**, 5,336 per km².
- Landmarks: Suseong Lake, the Sincheon riverside park, Yongjibong, Daegu National Museum.

**SOURCED** — Wikipedia "Suseong District" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suseong_District). Gyeongshin High School in the district produced 12 Seoul National University admits and 106 medical-field admits for 2021: **UNVERIFIED** (search summary of asiae.co.kr).

**The industrial complexes as workplaces**
- **Seongseo Industrial Complex** (Dalseo-gu): **2,847 firms, 47,064 workers (2023 Daegu city statistics), about 16 workers per firm.** It makes textiles, electrical and electronic goods, transport equipment and wood products. **SOURCED** — Pressian, 2024-11-15 (https://pressian.com/pages/articles/2024111522063889695).
- **Daegu Dyeing Industrial Complex** (Bisan-dong, Seo-gu): **127 firms and about 10,000 workers (April 2020)**; 60–80% of output normally exported to the US and Europe; a shared heat-and-power plant and wastewater treatment serve the complex. During the 2020 export collapse it ran at about 50% and one owner with 45 years in dyeing said it was "harder than the IMF era." **SOURCED** — Newsis, 2020-04-06 (https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20200406_0000984338). *This partly closes R1's NOT FOUND on dyeing-complex employment.*
  - **Relocation plan:** the city plans to move the complex to a 330,000 m² "advanced fiber complex" in Gunwi by **2030**. In a survey, **94 of 119 responding firms opposed** the move. Firms now get cheap electricity and steam from the in-complex plant; the proposed small modular reactor for the new site is not due until 2035, and an LNG plant is being considered instead. **SOURCED** — Maeil Shinmun, 2025-03-10 (https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2025031015132860591). The 125-firm count in the same reporting: **SOURCED** (same article).

**The old downtown: Dongseongno**
- Vacancy in mid-to-large commercial buildings in Dongseongno rose from **12.47% (2019)** to **19.8% (Q3 2024)** and **23.3% (Q3 2025)**, against a city average of 17.4%. The **Daegu Department Store** (over half a century old) closed in **July 2021** and has stood empty for nearly five years. **SOURCED** — Gyeongbuk Maeil, 2026-05-04 (https://kbmaeil.com/article/20260504500027). ⚠ That article reports a mayoral candidate's (Ryu Kyu-ha) pledge to buy and convert the store; the pledge is not a fact about the city.
- The city's **"Dongseongno Renaissance"** project (from the second half of 2023) covers commerce, space, culture and transport: a "downtown campus town" pilot in 2024, a shared district brand **"Play It Dongseongno"** with a mascot "Vic D," pop-up stores and a dessert festa. **UNVERIFIED** (search summary of Gyeongbuk Maeil 2025).

**Hyangchon-dong (the wartime arts quarter, now an elderly quarter)**
- Developed as an entertainment district by Japanese residents in the 1920s, it peaked in the 1930s. During the Korean War, writers and artists who fled to Daegu gathered in its tea houses and bars. **SOURCED** — Twig24/Seoul Shinmun travel, 2020-12-03 (https://twig24.com/news/lifestyle/trip/2020/12/03/20201203018001).
- Named wartime venues and what is on their sites now (same source):
  - **Renaissance Music Salon**, a classical listening room opened in 1951 by Park Yong-chan; now a restaurant.
  - **Baekrok Teahouse**, where painter **Lee Jung-seop** drew on Lucky Strike foil in 1955; now a motel.
  - **Cheongpodo Teahouse**, named for Yi Yuk-sa's poem "Green Grapes"; now a parking lot.
  - **Hwawol guesthouse**, the poet **Gu Sang**'s lodging. Gu Sang is said to have paid artists' unpaid drink tabs.
- **Today the alley divides by age:** younger visitors go to hanok cafes and period-costume photo studios, while the elderly gather at a venue for **people aged 60 and over only** (the source renders its name as "Pankorea Adult Text," apparently an adult dance hall, or 성인텍). **SOURCED** for the age split; ⚠ **the venue's exact name and type: UNVERIFIED** (a Korean search for "판코리아 성인텍" returned nothing).
- A 2023 column names further wartime meeting places: the **Daeji beer hall**, **Hyangyang dabang** and **Monami dabang**. **SOURCED** — Maeil Shinmun, 2023-11-09 (https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2023110714592682322).

**Converted industrial spaces for artists**
- **Daegu Art Factory** occupies the former state tobacco monopoly's **Daegu tobacco manufacturing plant** (later KT&G). Next door, **Suchang Youth Mansion** (opened **14 December 2017**) converted KT&G employee housing into exhibition spaces, studios and a cafe. **SOURCED** — Maeil Shinmun, 2018-03-08 (https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2018030819533486916). That the housing was used from 1976, closed in 1996, and was selected for a 2016 culture-ministry regeneration grant: **UNVERIFIED** (search summary).

**The mountains**
- **Palgongsan** was designated South Korea's **23rd national park on 31 December 2023** (provincial park since 13 May 1980). It covers **126.058 km²** across five administrative units in Daegu and Gyeongbuk and holds 5,296 species, 92 cultural resources and two parish-head temples, **Donghwasa (9th parish) and Eunhaesa (10th)**. **SOURCED** — IUCN, March 2024 (https://www.iucn.org/story/202403/palgongsan-national-park-designated-korea-celebrates-4th-anniversary-national-park-day).
- **Apsan** (660.3 m), spanning Nam-gu, Dalseo-gu and Suseong-gu, is described as "a representative hiking site for Daegu citizens" with a cable car and observatory. **UNVERIFIED** (aggregator snippets only).

**Gunwi (the rural county added in 2023)**
- **Hwasan Village** (Samgukyusa-myeon, Gunwi), at about 700 m on Hwasan (828 m), was settled in the 1960s when **180 households, about 1,000 people,** moved there under a government mountain-reclamation program. It fell to **20 households** and has recovered to about **56 households, 120+ people**, with **1,000+ visitors a month**, observatories, a windmill viewpoint and an unfinished 1709 fortress. Infrastructure now includes large car parks, cafes, restaurants and the Hwasan Village Experience Center lodging. **SOURCED** — Maeil Shinmun page (https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2025122401384118235; the URL dates it 2025-12-24, but the fetch returned no headline or date, so the page date is unconfirmed). The telltrip.com article on the same village returned HTTP 403. That incorporation into Daegu in 2023 improved access and drew visitors: **UNVERIFIED** (search summary).

**The new airport (R1 gap)**

Administrative procedures are largely complete; **funding** under the public-private model is the main obstacle. Residents in the relocation zone have lived for years under property restrictions. **SOURCED** — Maeil Shinmun, 2026-10-11 (https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2026100808585164563). ### B. Subcultures

**Migrant workers and foreign residents (R1 gap: the city-wide total)**
- **City-wide foreign residents: 61,554** "by recent city statistics," including **9,897 workers, 8,709 international students and 5,838 marriage migrants**. About **93% of foreign workers are in manufacturing and production**. By district: Dalseo-gu 19,092; Dalseong-gun 13,147; Buk-gu 10,584; Seo-gu 5,417; Dong-gu 4,680; Suseong-gu 3,417; Nam-gu 2,929; Jung-gu 1,363; Gunwi 925. **SOURCED** — Gyeongbuk Maeil, 2026-06-08 (https://www.kbmaeil.com/article/20260607500291). ⚠ The article gives no reference year and does not say whether naturalized citizens and children are included. A search summary gives **45,295 (1.93%) for 2024** with the same worker, student and marriage-migrant figures. That figure is likely the non-citizen subset of the national "foreign resident" count, but this is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Daegu Foreign Worker Support Center:** 8th–9th floors of Jinwang Tower, 863 Dalgubeol-daero, Dasa-eup, Dalseong-gun. Director Kim Deok-hwan. It runs counseling and interpretation, language education, cultural events and legal help, especially on wage disputes, for about 10,000 foreign workers. **SOURCED** — same article.
- **A union of migrant workers:** the Korean Metal Workers' Union **Seongseo Industrial Complex Branch** has about **150 members, about 130 of them migrant workers**.
  - It runs **free clinics** (doctors from Doctors for Humanitarian Practice) and **Korean classes** with volunteer teachers.
  - **Ten "country leaders,"** one per nationality, organize meetings.
  - Vice-chair **Cha Min-da** joined through a 2010 wage-theft case.
  - Documented cases: workers at a lumber firm made to split scrap wood for heating without pay; a firm that started shifts at 4 a.m. against 8 a.m. contracts, recovering about ₩40 million.
  - About 69% of employment-permit migrant workers nationally work in firms of under 30 people.
  - Dalseo-gu had **12,610 foreign residents** "as of April."

  **SOURCED** — Pressian, 2024-11-15.

**Muslim students**
- **Dar-ul-Emaan Kyungpook Islamic Center**, Daehyeon-dong, **Buk-gu**, run by the Kyungpook Muslim Student Association: **80–150 students**, mainly Pakistani, Nigerian and Bangladeshi.
  - They used a house in the alley as a prayer room from 2014. Construction of a mosque was approved in September 2020. Residents' protests began in early 2021 and construction was halted by order in February 2021. In December 2021 a court ruled for the students, and the Supreme Court upheld the ruling.
  - Residents placed **pig heads** in the alley and held **pork barbecues** by the site. The National Human Rights Commission called this "hate speech" (March 2023).
  - The article describes construction as continuing into 2025–26.

  **SOURCED** — Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar-ul-Emaan_Kyungpook_Islamic_Center). ⚠ A search snippet placed it in "Dalseo District"; the article body says Buk-gu. **Completion date: NOT FOUND.**

**LGBTQ community and its opponents**
- **Daegu Queer Culture Festival**, first held in **2009**. The 15th edition (17 June 2023, theme "We, Already") was held at the Banwol intersection on Dongseongno and chaired by Bae Jin-kyo.
  - Mayor **Hong Joon-pyo** sent about **450 public servants** to stop the stage setup. About **1,500 police** protected the event.
  - Christian churches and merchant groups counter-protested from midday to 6 p.m.
  - After a 2019 complaint to the National Human Rights Commission, police cooperation improved.

  **SOURCED** — Korea Times, 2023-06-18 (https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/southkorea/society/20230618/daegus-queer-festival-perseveres-despite-conservative-oppositions-intimidation-tactics). Yonhap's estimate of 500 officials: **UNVERIFIED** (snippet).

**The US military community**
- **USAG Daegu**, headquartered at **Camp Henry, Nam-gu**. **Camp Walker** handles the main life-support functions. **Camp George** has the elementary school and housing. **Camp Carroll** is in Waegwan, Chilgok, outside the city.
  - About **5,000 people in the Daegu enclave** and about 4,200 at Camp Carroll: soldiers, DoD civilians, Korean employees, KATUSAs, contractors and families.
  - Constituted in 2003 as Area IV Support Activity and renamed in 2007.

  **SOURCED** — Wikipedia "United States Army Garrison Daegu" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Garrison_Daegu). English-practice programs with Nam-gu students: **UNVERIFIED** (army.mil snippet). **Land-return or relocation plans: NOT FOUND.**

**A religious group: Shincheonji**
- The Shincheonji Church of Jesus in Daegu (Nam-gu) had about **8,000** in its congregation; authorities tried to trace almost **10,000** members in February 2020. By 23 February 2020, **329 of Korea's 602 confirmed COVID-19 cases** were linked to the church. **UNVERIFIED** (search summaries of Korea Times and wire reports; not opened).

**Political and regional identity**
- Daegu and North Gyeongsang ("TK") are described as a **conservative stronghold**. Every popularly elected mayor since the first election in 1995 (independent Moon Hee-gap) has come from the conservative camp. **UNVERIFIED** (Kyunghyang snippet).
- **Dialect:** the Gyeongsang dialect is described as **the only Korean dialect that keeps lexical tone**. Daegu speech represents its northern variety, as distinct from Busan's. **UNVERIFIED** (language-learning blogs only; an academic source on Daegu tone was not opened).

**Youth and music subculture**
- **Club Heavy**, at 244 Hyeonchung-ro near **Keimyung University**, opened in 1995 and has staged shows since 1996. It has held **over 2,400 performances by about 1,900 acts in 30 years** and is described as "Daegu indie music history."
  - **30th anniversary** (from 18 September 2026): a free exhibition, the compilation album "Daegu Indie Music Chronicle 'Heavy 30'" with 11 artists, two concert days and an archive book.
  - Artists include MINOS, Ssonoros, 1watt, Bindal, Sim Sang-myeong, Inaekkum, Kimkul, Bijero, Sindosi, **Drinking Boys and Girls Choir** and Cinnamon Jam.

  **SOURCED** — Sports Kyunghyang, 2026-09-15 (https://sports.khan.co.kr/en/article/202609152134007).
- Other venues (Korea Times, 2024-05-19, **SOURCED**, https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/southkorea/globalcommunity/20240519/finding-the-scene-3-daegus-top-5-favorite-live-music-venues):
  - **Communes** (Dongseongno, about 70 capacity): open stage on Wednesdays, jazz jam on Thursdays
  - **Led Zeppelin**, by Keimyung University
  - **Livehall Rock King**, near Namsan Station (Line 3)
  - **Sugar Joe's**, an expat bar outside Kyungpook National University

  The scene is "smaller than Seoul and Busan," and venues close often.

- **Bangcheon Market** (R1 gap):
  - The name comes from the riverbank levee (bangcheon) beside the Sincheon stream. It began after Liberation in 1945, when **returnees from Japan and Manchuria** started trading to survive, marking out stalls with rope. It was licensed around 1979 and grew into Chilseong-dong, drawing buyers from Gyeongsan, Gosan and Cheongdo.
  - During the Korean War the 14-year-old **Kim Woo-choong** (later chairman of Daewoo) sold newspapers there.
  - The Sincheon was once clear enough for bathing and fishing.

  **SOURCED** — Maeil Shinmun, 2009-08-27 (https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2009082713130332000). ### E. Cultural activities and civic life

- **2.28 Democratic Movement (28 February 1960):** Daegu **high-school students** protested against the Syngman Rhee government. It is described as South Korea's first democratization movement and the spark for the 3.15 and 4.19 uprisings. It became a **national commemorative day in 2018**; President Moon Jae-in attended the first ceremony run by the veterans ministry, with about 1,200 attendees including surviving student participants. The **2.28 Central Memorial Park** is downtown. **UNVERIFIED** (search summary of Gyeongbuk Maeil and Journalists Association pages; not opened). ⚠ The summary gives the ceremony date as "March 28," which looks wrong for a February 28 commemoration; the date is not confirmed.
- **The 2003 subway fire:** on **18 February 2003** an arsonist set a train on fire at **Jungangno Station**, killing **192**. **SOURCED** (death toll) — search summary of Wikipedia; treat as **UNVERIFIED**.
  - A memorial space opened in the station only in **2015**. It has a floor plaque, an orange wall with the victims' names, glass cases of burned door handles, couplers and an ATM, and a looping video.
  - The **Daegu Safety Theme Park** opened in 2008. The author believes schoolchildren are taken there to learn evacuation.

  **SOURCED** — goughlui.com personal blog, 2020-01-05 (https://goughlui.com/2020/01/05/opinion-daegu-2003-2-18-subway-fire-memorial-aftermath/); ⚠ a visitor's blog, not an official source. **How the anniversary is marked, and any memorial-site dispute: NOT FOUND.**
- **Buddha's Birthday lantern parade:** the **"Hyeonghyeongsaeksaek Dalgubeol Gwandeungnori"** is organized by the **Daegu Buddhist Association** with the city. On 27 April 2025 about **5,000 citizens and Buddhists** walked a **4.5 km route from Duryu Park** through the Duryu, Bangogae, Sinnam and Gyesan five-way intersections to Banwoldang. **SOURCED** — Gyeongbuk Maeil, 2025-04-27 (https://kbmaeil.com/article/20250427500563). Sky-lantern release at Duryu baseball ground in earlier years: **UNVERIFIED**. **Whether sky lanterns are still released: NOT FOUND.**
- **Daegu Colorful Festival**, from 5 May for two days, with a **2 km parade** through downtown (**UNVERIFIED**, hancinema snippet). The **Powerful Daegu Festival**, a citizen-participation parade with 630,000 visitors over two days and an LED night parade (**UNVERIFIED**, snippet). The **Dongseongno Festival** (May): a song contest, fashion show and concerts (**UNVERIFIED**, Wikipedia snippet).
- **Mutual aid in the 2020 outbreak:**
  - Daegu Medical Association chair **Lee Sung-gu** messaged about 5,700 local physicians.
  - About **400 doctors and 3,000 nurses** worked in designated hospitals and screening centers, and over 1,300 nurses volunteered in one day.
  - The **Seomun Market merchants' federation** donated towels, T-shirts, underwear and socks to Dongsan medical centers.
  - **Market landlords cut or waived rents.**

  **SOURCED** — Korea Times, 2020-04-03 (https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/health/20200403/citizens-lend-support-to-combating-covid-19-outbreak-in-daegu).

### B. Subcultures

**Bolivian community and the Urkupiña devotion**
- The **Fiesta de Nuestra Señora Virgen de Urkupiña** began in Córdoba in **1981**, with the mass in barrio **Las Flores** and the fiesta in **Villa El Libertador**, and later moved entirely to Villa El Libertador. The bill describes it as held without interruption since **1985**.
  - It falls on the **weekend nearest 15 August**.
  - Two images, the **"Virgen Grande" and "Virgen Chiquita,"** were brought from Bolivia in **1986** by organizer Rosell Vargas and head the procession on Avenida de Mayo.
  - **Nine days of masses**. On the Friday comes the vigil, when the images are dressed in **aguayos, hat and chuspa**. On the Saturday: the change of **pasantes**, the blessing of arches, the procession, flag-raising, the **blessing of "cargamentos,"** and a parade of dance groups (caporal, tinku, diablada, morenada, salay, potolos, llamerada, saya afroboliviana, kullaguada, toba, pujllay).
  - A band festival closes the parade, with **dozens of food stalls**.
  - Dancers make **three-year vows**.
  - **Organization:** an organizing commission of founders and regular helpers, plus **"pasantes,"** sponsors chosen by "the owner of the Virgin" and previous pasantes. Pasantes are usually people of standing and means who pay for the Virgin's "birthday party." Parties between incoming and outgoing pasantes (**"rodeos" or "rodeadas"**) are held two to three months before. "The organization of the celebration begins when it ends."
  - The Virgin is also seen as a **"giver of work"** ("dadora de trabajo").

  **SOURCED** — bill 4163-D-2025 (above). The bill declares the fiesta part of national intangible heritage. That the Chamber declared it "of interest" in **2017**: **SOURCED** (same document).

**Indigenous: the Comechingón community of Pueblo de La Toma (Alberdi)**
- About **seven descendant families** began meeting in **2007**, prompted by research at the **Instituto de Culturas Aborígenes (ICA)**, a Córdoba institute founded in 1992, and its research center. The **public proclamation of curacas** during the **Semana del Aborigen in April 2008** is treated as the community's founding day. It adopted a **cacicazgo (chiefdom) political model**.
  - Members describe childhood **barter** of home-grown produce across the river bridge.
  - The community remembers **Curaca Rubén Villafañe** as the last to resist police chief Fabriciano Martínez during the expropriations.
  - It pursued legal recognition (personería jurídica).

  **SOURCED** — Lucas Palladino, "Territorio y procesos de comunalización…," V Jornadas de Jóvenes Investigadores, IIGG-UBA, 2009 (https://www.aacademica.org/000-089/183.pdf).

**Football fan subculture: Los Piratas (Club Atlético Belgrano)**
- **Club Atlético Belgrano** was founded **19 March 1905**; its stadium is in **Alberdi**, "a popular barrio near the city center."
- **Los Piratas** were founded, by the "official" version, on **9 July 1968** in the **Bar de Zingarella** (others say a hardware store) on **Arturo Orgaz** street. They are described as "the first barra of Córdoba" and, by many, of the country, because they were the only one that **traveled to other provinces**. 1968 was the year Belgrano first played in the national AFA tournament.
- **Weekly meetings** were held at the **Bar de Luna**, under the stadium's popular stand.
- Until 2010–2011 there were **five factions**, including "Los Piratas Celestes de Alberdi" (claiming direct descent from the 1968 founders) and "La 19 de Marzo."
- Standing is earned through **"aguante"** (endurance and fighting) and **travel**: "you need a track record, to have frozen, to have traveled."
- Roles include a **"referente" of the barra's music**, who organizes the drums and brass and composes chants and **designs and sells barra clothing**.
- Membership runs in families: the third-generation member interviewed avoided anything blue (Talleres' color), even Adidas sportswear.

**SOURCED** — Nicolás Cabrera, "Las resonancias del pasado…," *FuLiA/UFMG* v. 2 n. 1, 2017 (https://periodicos.ufmg.br/index.php/fulia/article/download/13797/10979/37109). Present-day size of "500+," mostly male adolescents and adults: **UNVERIFIED** (search summary of a UNC ethnography). In 2026 the Belgrano barra paid a fireworks fine the club had received: **UNVERIFIED** (442/Perfil headline).

**Youth from the popular barrios and the police**
- **Marcha de la Gorra** ("March of the Cap"), first held on **20 November 2007**, the International Day of the Rights of the Child, and **every year since**. Thousands of mainly young people march **through the city center against police violence**.
  - The name is ironic: the **cap** is a common accessory of working-class youth and a sign by which police stop them.
  - Its first target was the provincial **Código de Faltas (Law 8,431)**. In **April 2016** that law was replaced by the **Código de Convivencia Ciudadana (Law 10,326)**. Both contain offenses, "merodeo" (loitering) under the first and "conducta sospechosa" (suspicious conduct) under the second, that are the ones most used for arbitrary detentions.
  - The march uses banners, colored flares, graffiti, chants, theater, **murgas**, drums, **cuarteto rhythms and rap**, and body painting and costumes.
  - Young people interviewed describe being stopped whenever they leave their barrio. One walks about twenty blocks to the main road, "always full of police."

  **SOURCED** — Macarena Roldán, "Narrativas activistas de jóvenes cordobeses…," *Revista Estudios Avanzados* 32, 2019/2020 (https://www.redalyc.org/journal/4355/435569924004/435569924004.pdf). The organizer was the **"Colectivo de Jóvenes por Nuestros Derechos"**; the march has spread to Río Cuarto, Villa María, San Francisco and others: **UNVERIFIED** (search summary).
