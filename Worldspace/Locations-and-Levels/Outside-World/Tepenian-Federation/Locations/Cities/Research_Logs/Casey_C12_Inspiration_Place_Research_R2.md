### A. Other settings
- **The wholesale and farm market (Terminal Asoagro and its neighbors)** [K4, K5, K7, K12, P4, P9]: on the Pan-American Highway (Route 5) at the city's south exit, bordering the Azapa Valley and the San José river (which runs only in the altiplano rainy months, late January to early March). Run as three associations with three yards: the **Asocapec** yard (oldest; retail of furniture, wiring, building materials, used clothing, pet food, car parts, machinery), the **Asoagro** yard (retail produce, plus goods brought from the Tacna Free Zone; formalized in 1982 after small farmers broke with Asocapec's control of produce), and the **SAN (Sociedad Agrícola del Norte)** cargo yard, the distribution point for trucks to the rest of northern and central Chile. **From 3 a.m. every day pickups of Bolivian Aymara farmers from Azapa and Lluta queue to sell tomatoes, corn and vegetables**, often with wives and relatives; Chilean truckers buy for Tarapacá, Antofagasta and the center; Peruvian and Bolivian trucks bring produce daily. Women are the absolute majority selling in the Asoagro yard. Four forms of work observed: day wage (CLP 10,000-20,000 for up to ten hours), stall rental (CLP 100,000-300,000 a month), 3-6 month contracts (nine hours a day, minimum monthly wage of CLP 320,500, pension and health contributions), and unpaid family labor. Women street vendors buy greens at the SAN and carry them door to door in woven cloths; men load and unload trucks as casual hands; some Bolivian women who started as children selling frozen juice now run import-export firms with fleets, warehouses and cold rooms, buying bananas in Cochabamba and fruit in Ecuador and Colombia; their Chile-born children are described as the next generation. The market has been an "ethnic enclave" since its creation in the 1980s. SOURCED: Casparrino, Guizardi and Valdebenito, "Escenas de un protagonismo: migrantes aimaras bolivianas en el Agro-mercado de Arica (Chile)," *Diario de Campo* (INAH, Mexico) 4th epoch, year 5, no. 12, January-June 2021, pp. 108-133 (fieldwork April, May and September 2019; PDF text extracted and read 2026-11-20), https://revistas.inah.gob.mx/index.php/diariodecampo/article/download/20168/22799/44718. ### E. Cultural activities and civic life
- **Putre patronal fiesta of the Virgen Asunta** [K2, K1, K12, P6, P7]: the town's oldest and most important fiesta, run 13-17 August with the main day 15 August, uniting Catholic and Aymara rites. Organized by **alféreces** (a married couple sponsoring for one year) and **mayordomos** (three-year service; nine of them lead the rites), with the married couple as the core unit (the source explains this through the Aymara idea of complementary duality). Sequence: 14 August, cleaning the church, dressing the images of the Virgin and San Ildefonso, receiving the religious dance companies at the village entrance at 11:00, receiving the brass band at about 13:00, vespers at about 15:00; 15 August, dawn greeting and flag-raising at 07:00 by the alféreces, central mass at 11:00 and procession with four stations, naming of the next alféreces, and a **community lunch given by the alféreces** in the courtyard of their house around the ritual table; a farewell day with libations (ch'alla) of alcohol, coca leaves and red wine at ritual tables beside the church with a ritual specialist; the band visits the nine mayordomos' houses; and on **18 August a "feast of the cooks and other helpers,"** where sponsors host everyone who cooked and served, with food and drink as the only payment (no money). Other Putre fiestas: **Pachallampe** (communal potato sowing in November, which opens the agricultural ritual year), Crosses, Holy Week, San Juan, Candelaria, San José, the Day of the Dead, All Saints and Carnival. SOURCED: Miria Luque Arias, "Fiesta patronal de Putre: forma de compartir en familia," *Atenea* (Universidad de Concepción) no. 521, 2020, pp. 155-169, https://www.redalyc.org/journal/328/32865443010/32865443010.pdf (PDF text extracted and read 2026-11-20). *(Mortuary note: the source mentions a cemetery visit; recorded only as a pointer.)*
