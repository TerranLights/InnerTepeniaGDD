## 63 · Timbuktu (Tombouctou), Mali

**Claim to test:** "A city where desert caravan routes met a river, a threshold between two worlds, with seasonal salt caravans (K5, K7)."
**Verdict:** CONFIRMED in substance, with narrowing. Timbuktu sits a few km north of the Niger River and depends on a river port (Kabara/Koriomé) that is usable only part of the year; the Taoudenni salt trade still runs to it, but by 2019 salt was "increasingly" moved by truck rather than camel, and caravans are much smaller than historically. Since August 2023 the city has lived under a blockade declared by the armed group JNIM, which has reshaped exactly the K5/K7 functions the claim names (see D and E).

### A. Snapshot
- **Location:** Northern Mali, at the southern edge of the Sahara. English Wikipedia gives two distances to the Niger, "20 km" in its lead and "15 km north of the main channel" in its geography section, and does not reconcile them. SOURCED (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu, read 2026-11-21). [K+ geography]
- **Population (urban commune):** 29,732 (census 17 Apr 1998); 54,629 (census 1 Apr 2009); 84,821 (estimate for 1 Jul 2023, "based on corrected 2022 census results"). SOURCED (citypopulation.de, commune 061204). Universalis gives 54,453 for 2009 (UNVERIFIED, snippet only).
- **Quarters:** the commune has 8 quarters: Abaradjou, Badjindé, Bella Farandi, Djingareye Ber, Hammabangou, Kabara, Sankoré, Sareikeina. SOURCED (REACH/IMPACT, *Impacts du blocus sur les marchés de Tombouctou*, Dec 2023, PDF read locally).
- **Climate:** hot desert (BWh); mean annual rain 182.8 mm, mostly June to September; mean daily maxima above 40 °C in April to June; record 49.0 °C. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Timbuktu).
- **Defining feature:** a World Heritage city (inscribed 1988) of three earthen great mosques (Djingareyber, Sankoré, Sidi Yahia) and saints' mausoleums and cemeteries, plus a manuscript culture; also the river-desert transfer point for Taoudenni salt. Inscription year 1988 and the three mosques SOURCED (CRATerre case page; UNESCO search snippets; UNESCO's own page refused, 403). Danger listing 1990 (mosques) and again 2012 (UNVERIFIED, snippets of UNESCO/Wikipedia). One press source (Africanews 2025-10-13) says "1989"; the UNESCO and CRATerre date is 1988.
- **Administrative change:** the northern part of Timbuktu and Goundam cercles (about 323,326 km², about 134,000 people) was separated to form Taoudénit Region, created by law 2012, governor appointed 19 Jan 2016; in 2016 the Taoudénit governor stayed in Timbuktu for security and logistics reasons. UNVERIFIED (Wikipedia and MINUSMA PDF snippets only).

### B. What else exists there
**Economy (K7, K8).** REACH (Dec 2023) names the pillars of the local economy as agriculture, livestock, fishing, trade and crafts. SOURCED. Rice is the main crop, mostly on irrigated perimeters south of town (Daye, Koriomé, Hamadja); Wikipedia SOURCED. A Malian socio-economic paper says rice growing in Timbuktu cercle is run by farmer organizations of Koriomé, Daye and Hamadia on the territory of Alafia commune (UNVERIFIED, snippet). Women's market-garden perimeters exist near town (Tarabangou, 5 km, extended by 11 ha with solar pumping; Tabachabatht, 15 km) (UNVERIFIED, UNICEF and Luxembourg aid snippets; the LuxDev page returned 404).
**Trades (K14).** A local heritage site (exploretimbuktu.com, undated, SOURCED) describes the trades and who traditionally holds them:
- Bakers: women only, traditionally from Songhay noble families, baking flat loaves in beehive earthen ovens, mostly at dawn, some with a second batch about 10 am or an evening batch in certain neighborhoods. [K7, K12]
- Butchers: "powerful guild" with four great families, a chief who distributes work, and a ritual dance. [K14]
- Masons: mostly low-caste *gabibi*; some master builders trace roots to Morocco; a belief that only the original builder or his descendants may work on a house restricts competition. (This page says "no formal guild"; the heritage sources below speak of a *corporation des maçons* that maintains the mosques. Both recorded.) [K14, K6]
- Carpenters: one family makes the traditional doors and grilles; modern carpentry is open to all.
- Smiths: Tuareg artisans (metal, wood, leather; wives do the leatherwork), attached to noble families.
- Fishers are Bozo; fish sellers are Bella; herders of others' animals are of former servile status; porters carry by hand, push-cart or donkey cart at fixed fees; welders and mechanics keep old vehicles running and make stoves from oil drums. [K14, R]
**Social history note (K9, sensitive):** the exploretimbuktu page itself describes several trades in terms of hereditary "castes" and former slave status (Bella, Haratin). A 2008 Temedt/Anti-Slavery International report said several thousand Bella remained in servitude in Gao Region (UNVERIFIED, snippet). Recorded plainly as a fact about the social order; no Timbuktu-specific current figure was found.
**Institutions.**
- Ahmed Baba Institute (IHERI-AB): founded 1973, financed mainly by Kuwait; new 4,600 m² building completed 2009 (about EUR 5.8 million; South African-led project after a 2001 agreement), with air conditioning and automatic fire suppression. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Ahmed Baba Institute). On 28 Jan 2013 departing fighters set fire to it. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Timbuktu).
- Manuscripts: families and institute staff smuggled "tens of thousands" to Bamako in 2012; after digitization most were brought back in August 2025 (ministerial delegation arrived 11 Aug 2025). The returned collection is put at 28,000 manuscripts. Private family libraries still keep manuscripts in wooden chests; SAVAMA-DCI's founding member says families "receive no support" and may be tempted to sell. SOURCED (Africanews 2025-11-19; gouvernement.ml 2025-08-12). Overall evacuation totals differ by source (about 285,000 to 350,000 items; UNVERIFIED).
- University of Timbuktu: Decree no. 2022-0498/PT-RM sets its organization (UNVERIFIED, Journal officiel title only; faculties and enrollment NOT FOUND).
- Hospital: a 2014 MSF note gives the Timbuktu hospital 54 beds (UNVERIFIED, snippet). Current staffing NOT FOUND.
- Markets (K5): four main markets: Yobou Ber (Badjindé), Yobou Tao (Sankoré), Foire Yobou, and the Madougou livestock market (Abaradjou). A market-management mechanism groups the town hall and traders. SOURCED (REACH Dec 2023).
- Airport: 2,110 m paved runway; Sky Mali was the last commercial airline and halted flights on 11 Sep 2023 after shelling. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Timbuktu and Siege of Timbuktu). In December 2025 a festival organizer described being stuck in Bamako "en raison de l'absence de vols." SOURCED (Journal du Mali, issue 555, PDF read locally).
- Power: a thermal plant run by Energie du Mali (EDM). SOURCED (Dakaractu, 26 Jun 2026; see E).
**Languages (K11, loose):** Koyra Chiini (Songhay) is the majority language and local lingua franca; Hassaniya Arabic and Tamasheq were each spoken by about 10% before the 1990–94 rebellion; Bambara is in growing use; Arabic is the language of scholarship and religion. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Timbuktu).

### C. Cultural locations (names, what each is)
- **Djingareyber mosque** (built 1325 per Africanews/CRATerre; the 700th anniversary was marked in 2025), **Sankoré mosque**, **Sidi Yahia mosque** (about 1400; smallest, three aisles and a courtyard). SOURCED (Africanews 2025-10-13; CRATerre). [K2]
- **Sidi Yahia's "secret door"**, the only door facing west, kept shut since anyone remembers and forbidden to touch (SOURCED); the tradition that it opens only at the end of time is UNVERIFIED (UNESCO and press snippets). Torn off by occupying fighters on 2 July 2012; restored by local carpenters April to August 2016 under UNESCO supervision; reinstalled at a ceremony on 19 Sep 2016. SOURCED (JusticeInfo/AFP, 19 Sep 2016). [K6, K9]
- **Mausoleums of saints** (the city's popular epithet for them is recorded in the separate slang file): ten sites attacked 30 Jun–11 Jul 2012, including Sidi Yahia. SOURCED (Trust Fund for Victims page). Two memorials, **Louha** and **Qâloum**, and a permanent mausoleum exhibit at the **municipal museum** came out of the ICC reparations program (2022–2026). SOURCED (TFV).
- **Flame of Peace (Flamme de la Paix)**: monument on the city's northwest outskirts built after the 27 March 1996 burning of about 3,000 weapons surrendered at the end of the 1990s Tuareg rebellion (3,600 in another source). UNVERIFIED (UN press, Notre Dame peace-accords database snippets). In Dec 2025 it served as the open-air night stage for the national Biennale. SOURCED (Africanews 2025-12-25).
- **Salle Ali Farka** (hall), the **stadium**, the **Place de la Mascotte** and the Ahmed Baba Institute were the 2025 Biennale venues. SOURCED (Journal du Mali 555).
- **Bread ovens**: beehive earthen ovens, high concentration in Sankoré. SOURCED (exploretimbuktu). [K12]
- **Radio Buktu**: local station whose music library was destroyed during the 2012 occupation. SOURCED (HRW, 25 Sep 2012).
- **Festivals (K2, K3):**
  - *Maouloud* (Prophet's birthday) week: described by a Malian outlet as all-night readings in illuminated mosques, youths learning to recite the main text, a closing Fatiha at Sankoré and a "Tanaré" ceremony linked to Djingareyber on the square near Sankoré. UNVERIFIED (koulouba.com snippet; the page redirected to an advertising domain and was not followed). Wikipedia says "every January", but the date moves with the lunar calendar; recorded as Wikipedia's wording only.
  - *Annual replastering of Djingareyber*: hundreds of residents of all ages, organized with the traditional masons' guild (spokesman Bilal Mahamane Traoré); held 12 Oct 2025 with the 700th anniversary. SOURCED (Africanews 2025-10-13); the 12 Oct date UNVERIFIED (bamada.net snippet). Banned during the 2012 occupation; first resumed on 15 Aug 2013 (UNVERIFIED, CRATerre snippet). [K1, K2, K14]
  - *Festival au Désert*: 2001 Tin Essako, 2002 Tessalit, 2003–2009 Essakane (about 65 km from Timbuktu), 2010–2012 Timbuktu outskirts, then "in exile" as touring caravans abroad. UNVERIFIED (Wikipedia/Afropop snippets). [K3]
  - *Festival Vivre Ensemble* (Living Together): 5th edition closed 31 Jan 2021 (SOURCED, Sahelien.com 2021-02-02); an edition held 27–29 Dec 2024 drew tourists from about 30 countries (UNVERIFIED, TRT Afrika snippet); an edition was held in December 2023 during the blockade (SOURCED, Wikipedia "Siege of Timbuktu": "Residents held an annual festival in December 2023"). [K3]
  - *Biennale artistique et culturelle 2025*: 18–28 Dec 2025, first time in Timbuktu; 20 delegations, five disciplines; Forum mondial des civilisations 19–20 Dec. SOURCED (Journal du Mali 555; Africanews 2025-12-25). Locals quoted are divided: a historian calls it a spectacle with promised projects (a 7,000-seat stadium, two 40-villa estates) not delivered. SOURCED. [K3, K8, K9]

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- **The earthen mosques require yearly communal labor**, which created a standing institution: masons' families organized under a chief mason, with the imam and mosque committee coordinating; "everyone participates, women, youth, elders" (Regional Council president, Africanews 2025). The ritual doubles as teaching the next generation. [K1, K14, P8, R]
- **Salt from Taoudenni (about 664 km north)** shaped a payment custom: per camel load of four bars, two to the extractor and two to the transporter; a bar sold for about 5,000 FCFA in Timbuktu; a caravan man earned about 100,000 FCFA per round trip. Caravans of 20–100 camels with two to four men; about 50 km and up to 10 hours a day; a stop at Araouane (230 km); camels do not enter the city: loads go to a member's or salt merchant's house on the outskirts. SOURCED (exploretimbuktu Azalai page). At the mine, up to 1,000 self-employed miners in high season, 3–6 bars a day each, first-quality salt 5,000 FCFA, second 1,000–1,250 FCFA, lowest grade for animals; truckers barter rice, millet and clothes for salt; transport "increasingly" by vehicle. SOURCED (Sahelien.com, 15 Jun 2019). Historically a main caravan in early November and a smaller one in late March (UNVERIFIED, Wikipedia "Azalai" snippet). [K5, K7, SM:competence]
- **The river transfer point:** before the blockade, food came mainly by the Goundam road and Kabara; charcoal came by the Kabara road; in the rainy season roads become impassable and supply is by boat docking at Koriomé or Kabara **from July to January, during the flood**; the Kabara–Koriomé route takes about two weeks to deliver goods to town. SOURCED (REACH Dec 2023). Kabara canal (3 km) dredged in 2007 (Wikipedia, SOURCED); a PIARC study says the silted canal opens Kabara only a few weeks a year (UNVERIFIED, snippet, undated). [K5, K7]
- **Manuscript culture produced a family-library institution** (private libraries, SAVAMA-DCI, the state institute) and a 2012 smuggling operation by residents. [K1, K14, SM:competence]
- **Heritage status drew international money and law into the town:** the ICC convicted Ahmad Al Faqi Al Mahdi (2016, nine years) for attacks on ten monuments; reparations EUR 2.7 million (17 Aug 2017); TFV program: individual payments Apr 2019–4 Jun 2024 to 1,691 eligible victims (descendants of the saints and people whose livelihood depended on the buildings), collective measures Jul 2022–31 Mar 2026 for 70,000+ people, including 70 locally led initiatives, mausoleum maintenance training and a maintenance fund. SOURCED (TFV node 536). [K1, K8, K9]

### E. Structural features (industry, economy, arts, work and life)
- **Blockade since 8 Aug 2023 (K5, K9).** Non-state armed groups broadcast a voice message announcing a blockade of the region; checkpoints, road closures, movement restrictions and entry taxes followed; two public transport vehicles to Rharous intercepted (22 Aug), a goods truck burned near the city (27 Aug); river restrictions reported. Survey of 43 traders, 17–22 Nov 2023: 49% had closed (79% of those for more than two months); 98% of closures due to insecurity; sugar, oil, flour, milk and fuel up an estimated 70–100% in three months; goods from Mauritania and Algeria (flour, sugar, tea, pasta) hit hardest; the new supply route ran Bamako–Mopti by truck, Mopti–Koriomé by *pinasse*, then by road. SOURCED (REACH Dec 2023). About 33,000 fled the city and surroundings; about 1,000 to Mauritania. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Siege of Timbuktu).
- **2025–2026:** JNIM attacked a base near the city with a car bomb in June 2025 (14 attackers killed per the army; airport shelled). SOURCED (Wikipedia, Siege). Fuel blockade on Mali from September 2025 (UNVERIFIED snippets, ISS/AFP). From 19 June 2026 the Timbuktu thermal plant was shut for lack of fuel; residents bought black-market fuel from Algeria; EDM said continuous supply would allow one to two hours of power per neighborhood; temperatures above 40 °C. SOURCED (Dakaractu, 26 Jun 2026). Coordinated JNIM and FLA attacks across Mali began 25 Apr 2026 (Kidal taken; defense minister killed in Kati); Timbuktu was not among the cities struck that day. SOURCED (Global Initiative, 8 May 2026) and UNVERIFIED (NPR, AP snippets).
- **River risk:** a *pinasse* from the Bourem Sidi Amar fair to Tonka capsized near Diré on the night of 8 Jan 2026 with about 50 aboard, heavily loaded with rice sacks; at least 33 dead per local press. SOURCED (RFI via AllAfrica, 13 Jan 2026). [K5, K9]
- **Tourism collapse (K8, K9):** more than 45,000 visitors in 2006, 4,000 in 2009, 429 in Jan–Jun 2011 (SOURCED, Rural21, 8 Feb 2012; Wikipedia gives about 6,000 for 2009 and 492 for Jan–Apr 2011, so figures differ by source). A German tourist killed and three kidnapped in Timbuktu in late Nov 2011 (SOURCED, Rural21).
- **Curfew (K6):** Journal du Mali (Dec 2025) says the city had been "sous couvre-feu depuis trois ans." SOURCED. The curfew was widened after the 2 June 2025 attacks (UNVERIFIED, RFI/APA snippets); current hours NOT FOUND. A national order of 3 Jun 2026 bans motorcycles of 125 cc and over outside regional, cercle and arrondissement capitals; governors may go further (UNVERIFIED, RFI/Africanews snippets).
- **K6 rules imposed during the 2012–13 occupation (historical, sensitive):** music banned (radios, live music, ringtones; only Quranic recordings allowed); women beaten for uncovered heads; smoking punished (a blacksmith beaten in his home, 13 Aug 2012); foosball tables ordered indoors; weddings, baptisms and gatherings effectively banned; visits to family graves barred; an "Islamic Police" with a courtroom in a former hotel and judges drawn from regional marabouts; floggings in public. SOURCED (HRW, 25 Sep 2012). The ICC convicted the Islamic Police's de facto chief, Al Hassan, on 26 Jun 2024 and sentenced him to 10 years on 20 Nov 2024 (reduced by 12 months in July 2025); reparations ordered 28 Apr 2026. UNVERIFIED (ICC and press snippets; ICC page refused, 403).
- **K6, standing customs:** the Sidi Yahia door taboo (above); the hereditary-builder restriction (above).
- **Mali's withdrawal from the Rome Statute:** notified to the UN on 24 June 2026 per HRW/press snippets; the TFV page says "30 June 2026." Dates differ; recorded both. Takes effect one year after notification. UNVERIFIED/SOURCED mix as stated.

## 64 · Agadez and the Cure Salée (In-Gall), Niger

**Claim to test:** "A desert gateway town where caravans staged before crossing, with a seasonal gathering nearby (K2, K5)."
**Verdict:** CONFIRMED, with a modern twist. Agadez is the historic staging town of the Aïr; the salt-caravan route to Fachi and Bilma (the *Taghlamt*) ran from around Agadez, and in the 21st century the town became the staging point for motor convoys of migrants northward to Libya and Algeria, a trade criminalized 2016–2023 and legal again since November 2023. The "seasonal gathering nearby" is the Cure Salée at In-Gall, held at the end of the rainy season (September or early October), when herders bring animals to the salt lands. Note: In-Gall's distance and direction from Agadez differ by source (English Wikipedia as read by the fetch tool: about 100 km "east"; Niger's state agency ANP: about 160 km west). Recorded as conflicting; NOT resolved.

### A. Snapshot
- **Agadez:** capital of Agadez Region and of Tchirozérine department, southern edge of the Sahara, beside the Aïr massif. Population (census): 20,643 (1977), 49,424 (1988), 77,060 (2001), 110,497 (2012). SOURCED (Wikipedia, Agadez). Al Jazeera (29 Jul 2025) reports the population "reportedly doubled to 300,000" with migration (SOURCED as a report of a claim; not a census). Climate: hot desert, mean annual rain 111.5 mm mostly July–August, record 49.1 °C. SOURCED (Wikipedia).
- **Defining feature:** the Historic Center of Agadez, World Heritage since 2013 (criteria ii, iii), developed in the 15th–16th centuries when the Sultanate of Aïr settled there; 11 irregular quarters whose street pattern follows old encampment boundaries; Grand Mosque with a 27 m mud-brick minaret (Wikipedia: built 1515, rebuilt 1844). SOURCED (Wikipedia, Historic Center of Agadez; Agadez). About 20,000 people live in the historic center (SOURCED, Le360/AFP 26 Sep 2024).
- **In-Gall:** a department and commune (61,171 km²; commune 47,170 in 2011 per text, 51,903 in 2012 per infobox) whose town has fewer than 500 year-round residents and swells to several thousand during the Cure Salée. Oasis with gardens, fruit trees and date palms; salt evaporation ponds at Teguidda (about 15 km north). Historically a Songhay outpost and a center of the Aïr Sultanate; French fort early 20th century; army fortification during the 1990s insurgency. SOURCED (Wikipedia, In-Gall).

### B. What else exists there
- **Economy (K7, K8):** market town; transport hub for uranium mined around Arlit; known for its camel market and silver and leatherwork. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Agadez). The Arlit mine company SOMAIR (63.4% Orano, 36.6% state SOPAMIN) came under state control in Dec 2024 and was nationalized by ordinance adopted 19 Jun 2025; the mine workers' union SYNTRAMIN backed the decision. UNVERIFIED (World Nuclear News, APA snippets). Artisanal gold rush from early 2014 at Djado, then Tchibarakaten and the Aïr; Djado closed by government April 2017. UNVERIFIED (DIIS, Small Arms Survey, Vice snippets).
- **The migrant transport economy (K5, K7, K9, sensitive):** Law 2015-36 (enforced from 2016) criminalized transporting irregular migrants; hundreds of drivers, brokers and *passeurs* were charged and vehicles seized; the military government (CNSP, in power after the July 2023 coup) repealed it in November 2023 (decree 25 Nov 2023 also canceled convictions). Since then: dozens of overloaded pickups gather at the town gates and follow the army north; some January 2024 convoys had more than 100 vehicles; a passeur said escorts ran every Tuesday; the mayor said 98% of vehicles leaving north now declare themselves (30% before); each migrant pays the municipality 1,000 FCFA; a trip to Libya cost about 300,000 FCFA (Al Jazeera, 12 Apr 2024, SOURCED); Al Jazeera 2025 gives 150,000 FCFA and about four days (SOURCED). Migrants wait in informal shelters (the local term for them is recorded in the separate slang file) in the Doubai neighborhood; hubs are the old bus station and the transporters' union office; houses double as truck-loading garages. SOURCED (Al Jazeera 2024). Under the 2016–2023 regime Euronews (Sep 2018) reported migrant flows down 80%, "thousands" of jobs lost, and camels falling from 500,000 to under 300,000 FCFA in the livestock markets on the outskirts. SOURCED. A 2017 governor's decree reportedly barred large passenger vehicles leaving Agadez unless they carried food (UNVERIFIED, snippet). Alarm Phone Sahara answers emergency calls from the desert. SOURCED (Al Jazeera 2024). [K1, K6, K9, SM:system]

### C. Cultural locations

His sister served as *magajia* (women's representative) with a parallel court. SOURCED (Mixmag, 15 Apr 2022) and UNVERIFIED (NPR, Relix snippets). [K13]

- **The Cure Salée (K2, K3):**

## 65 · Zagora and the "Tombouctou 52 jours" sign, Morocco

**Claim to test:** "A last-town-before-the-desert whose landmark is a sign giving the travel time to a distant destination (K2)." Care note: verify whether the sign still stands and in what form.
**Verdict:** CONFIRMED with a correction on "last town." Zagora is the administrative center of the Drâa valley and the place where the tarred road historically ended; today the road continues 98 km south to M'hamid El Ghizlane, where it ends, so M'hamid, not Zagora, is now the literal last town (SOURCED, Frommer's online guide, undated text). **Sign status:** the **original painted sign was demolished** together with the arches at the town's southern end during road works, and a **replica was painted at the new junction with the road west to Foum Zguid** (SOURCED, Frommer's; undated). English Wikipedia's main text says the original "has been replaced by a mural painting," while its own footnote says a new sign was posted and "the old one still remains"; the article does not resolve this (SOURCED, read 2026-11-21). Wikivoyage places a sign "a few meters from the roundabout where the road splits left to M'Hamid and right to the airport" (SOURCED, condition not stated). A March 2026 piece (Dance with Lions / slowmorocco.com) says the sign "stands," that the national government had announced a plan to replace it under a modernization program, and that copies then appeared at guesthouses and cafés (camel facing different ways, arrows pointing different ways), several owners claiming theirs is the original, one on a small replica kasbah at the last roundabout out of town (SOURCED). A forum claim that the original is in a Rabat museum is UNVERIFIED (Horizons Unlimited snippet; page refused 403). **Best reading of the evidence: a sign reading "Tombouctou 52 jours" with a camel and rider stands at the southern exit near the M'hamid/airport roundabout, but it is a repainted replica, not the original; several private copies also exist. No dated news report of the removal was found.** [K2, K+ landmark, P6]

### A. Snapshot
- **Location:** Drâa river valley, capital of Zagora Province, Drâa-Tafilalet region; altitude 724 m. SOURCED (fr.wikipedia, en.wikipedia).
- **Population:** urban commune 26,174 / 34,851 / 40,067 / 42,294 across four censuses, the last on 1 Sep 2024 (UNVERIFIED, citypopulation snippet; en.wikipedia gives 42,275 for 2024, SOURCED). French Wikipedia gives 52,168 for 2004 (SOURCED, likely a wider unit). Province: 285,545 in 2024 (UNVERIFIED, Wikipedia snippet). - **Climate:** hot desert; about 61 mm rain a year; mean July maximum 44.9 °C; mean January minimum 3.8 °C. SOURCED (en.wikipedia).

SOURCED (Frommer's). ## 108 · Tamanrasset, Algeria

**Claim to test:** "The main town on the trans-Saharan road where trucks and travelers resupply before the long crossing south (K5, K7)."
**Verdict:** CONFIRMED in substance, with narrowing. Tamanrasset is the chief town of the Algerian Ahaggar on the Trans-Sahara Highway (national road N1), about 400 km north of the only official Algeria–Niger crossing (In Guezzam/Assamaka). Commercial truck traffic south is real but regulated and partly informal: a barter regime limited to four southern wilayas, a state-run annual fair (Assihar) with convoys to Niamey, and much informal trade. Since 2019 the wilaya has been split (In Salah and In Guezzam became separate wilayas), and the Mali border has been tense since April 2025. The town is also the main southern collection and expulsion point for sub-Saharan migrants. [K5, K7, K9]

### A. Snapshot
- **Location:** Ahaggar (Hoggar) mountains, southern Algeria, at about 1,320 m; capital of Tamanrasset wilaya; grew from caravan routes linking Kano, Lake Chad, Gao, Agadez and Zinder; Charles de Foucauld built a hermitage there in 1905, followed by a French fort (Fort Laperrine). SOURCED (en.wikipedia, Tamanrasset).
- **Population:** town 72,741 (1998), 92,635 (2008 census) (SOURCED, Wikipedia; citypopulation snippet). Wikipedia gives an uncited 2025 estimate of 140,955 (recorded as uncited). Wilaya 137,175 (1998), 176,637 (2008), before the 2019 split (UNVERIFIED, citypopulation snippet).
- **Administrative change:** In Salah and In Guezzam, delegated wilayas since 2015, became full wilayas (council of ministers 26 Nov 2019; In Guezzam instituted 18 Dec 2019) (UNVERIFIED snippets). The boundary decree (Executive Decree 21-128 of 29 Mar 2021) set off marches in Tamanrasset (Friday and Saturday) and Tazrouk by Ahaggar Tuareg notables who said Ahaggar land, mineral wealth and gas and industrial-salt reserves had been "expropriated" in favor of Djanet and Illizi; they asked for a return to the 1986 commune map; the Interior Ministry said no territorial border had changed. SOURCED (Algérie Eco, 19 Apr 2021). [K9]
- **Defining feature:** the regional capital and supply node of the deep south, gateway for the Ahaggar and the road south. [K5]
- **Languages (K11, loose):** Tamahaq (Tuareg) and Arabic; the city has a large population of Malian and Nigerien origin (SOURCED, Algerie360, 21 Jan 2010). No census language breakdown found.

### B. What else exists there
- **Water (K1, K5):** the In Salah–Tamanrasset transfer: over 700 km of pipeline (750 km along the development corridor), two well fields about 70 km north of In Salah, six pumping stations, a 50,000 m³ terminal reservoir, a 100,000 m³/day desalination (demineralization) plant; initial capacity 50,000 m³/day (needs about 25,000), planned to double by 2030; cost 197 billion dinars; built by Chinese and Algerian–Lebanese–Turkish consortia; commissioned by Water Minister Sellal (article of 15 Mar 2011); about ten settlements of about 10,000 people planned along the route (In Amguel, Tit, Outoul, Arak). SOURCED (Algerie360, 15 Mar 2011). Earlier decades of water crisis (UNVERIFIED snippet).
- **Barter trade (K1, K5, K6):** interministerial *arrêté* of 2 Jul 2020 (JO no. 44, replacing the 1994 text) restricts cross-border barter with Mali and Niger to registered wholesalers resident in Adrar, Illizi, Tamanrasset and Tindouf, with storage and transport; the wali sets an annual list of authorized wholesalers and can withdraw it. **Exports allowed:** dry dates (not Deglet Nour), salt, household metal and plastic goods, blankets, crafts, clothing, soap, olive oil, olives, honey, cleaning and hygiene products. **Imports allowed:** live livestock, henna, green tea, spices, turban and *tari* fabrics, mango, red wood, honey, animal feed, Tuareg clothing, crafts, treated hides, local perfumes, fish. Imports **may not be resold outside the four wilayas**; each import must be matched by exports within 3 months (one 3-month extension); a 10% guarantee if exporting first; a committee chaired by the wali monitors prices. SOURCED (Algérie Eco/APS, 4 Aug 2020). A 2011 World Bank paper estimated more than USD 85 million of Algerian exports to Mali (120 trucks) plus 60 trucks in transit to Niger and Nigeria, with official statistics capturing under 3% (UNVERIFIED snippet). [SM:system]
- **Transport-cost compensation (K1, K5, K8):** suppliers of 33 listed goods to 19 southern wilayas, Tamanrasset included, can be reimbursed land-transport costs (decree of 31 Mar 2024; list by interministerial order of 30 Oct 2025, JO no. 82). Listed goods include flour, semolina, vegetables, eggs, meat, milk, infant flour, coffee, tea, sugar, rice, pasta, cooking oil, animal feed, soap, school supplies, butane, medicines, rebar and other building materials, books. SOURCED (Radio Algérienne/APS, 16 Dec 2025). A 2010 report quotes residents saying state payments per convoy from the north do not show in shop prices, and calls fruit and vegetable prices "surréalistes" (SOURCED, Algerie360 2010).
- **Assihar (K3, K8):** state trade fair named after the city's main market. 35th edition 26 Dec 2021–8 Jan 2022 (more than 200 companies; venues: the 7 ha Assihar market in the center, a 17,500 m² site opposite, a 3 ha goods area 13 km north), organized by Tasdir and ALGEX (SOURCED, Echorouk, 26 Dec 2021); 36th edition 23 Dec 2022–6 Jan 2023 with ministers of Mali and Niger (UNVERIFIED snippet). A convoy of 690 t (960 t in the Arabic headline) left for Niamey timed to an edition (UNVERIFIED snippets). 2024–25 editions NOT FOUND.
- **Gold (K7, K14):** from 2020 young people's micro-enterprises were licensed to mine artisanal gold (57 authorized in Dec 2020 out of more than 170 applications), ore processed at the ENOR Amesmessa plant; over 128 kg produced from June 2021; 93 new sites announced in Tamanrasset, In Guezzam and Tindouf. UNVERIFIED (Algérie Eco, Maghreb Emergent snippets).
- **Health (K9):** a fire at the old Mesbah Baghdad hospital in July 2025 killed three; a new 240-bed hospital opened 10 Aug 2025; 577 specialists posted to the south from 2023 plus 150 in early 2024 on six-year contracts. UNVERIFIED (Radio Algérienne, AllAfrica snippets).
- **University:** annex of the University of Algiers 2004, university center 2005 (Executive Decree 05-301, 16 Aug 2005), university status later (2020 per one profile); over 6,000 students in 2024/25 (UNVERIFIED snippets).
- **Airport:** Tamanrasset (Aguenar–Hadj Bey Akhamok). SOURCED (Routard). Air Algérie flight 6289 crashed at the city in 2003 (SOURCED, Wikipedia).

### C. Cultural locations
- **Assekrem** (about 80 km): Foucauld's stone hermitage (1911) on the plateau, with a refuge; views over the Atakor volcanic peaks; two Little Brothers of Jesus still lived there in 2022 (UNVERIFIED snippet). Foucauld was killed at his Tamanrasset hermitage on 1 Dec 1916 and canonized 15 May 2022. SOURCED (Routard for Assekrem) / UNVERIFIED (Britannica and press snippets for dates).

- **Ahaggar Cultural Park:** 633,887 km², delimited by Executive Decree 87-231; access through 12 control posts; managed by the national office (ONPCA) under the Ministry of Culture, empowered to ban, authorize or regulate pastoral, craft, tourist, film, event and research activity in protection zones and to review development projects; collaborative management agreements with communities. SOURCED (IUCN workshop case study, Feb 2020, PDF read locally). [K1, K6]
- **Imzad (K13, K14):** the single-string fiddle played only by Tuareg women; by the early 1990s only five women mastered it; the association "Sauver l'Imzad" (with Les Amis du Tassili) founded international imzad meetings in 2009 and training classes; **Dar l'Imzad** inaugurated in Tamanrasset in November 2011; an open-air school at Tin Tarabine (about 400 km south) since 2003 trained 40 young women. SOURCED (Algerie360/APS, 7 Mar 2012).
- **Music scene (K13):** the band Imarhan (formed 2006) built the Aboogi studio in Tamanrasset from local materials, with Africa Express funding; the region had no recording studio; built "for the use of the community of Tamanrasset." SOURCED (Loud And Quiet, 26 Apr 2022). Music marks weddings and family events (same source). ### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- **Water and goods must be brought in from the north** (pipeline from In Salah; transport-cost compensation for 33 staple goods), and trade south is fenced by special law (barter limited to residents, resale banned outside four wilayas). [K1, K5, K6]
- **The crossroads role draws migrants:** quarters are described in a 2010 report as Sersouf (north; first settled by migrants, scrap housing, scavenging), Tahaggart across the wadi (a strong share of people of Malian and Nigerien origin; some born locally without papers), and Gataâ El Oued (cattle market, general market, power plant, social housing). Smuggling caches (fuel tanks) and demolition of unpermitted buildings recur. SOURCED (Algerie360, 21 Jan 2010). In 2001 day laborers waited in Gataâ El Oued for construction recruiters at about 250 DA a day; shared rooms about 200 DA a month; most cafés, restaurants and construction firms were owned by Kabyles and Chaouis from the north. SOURCED (Afrik.com/El Watan, 10 Sep 2001). [K4, K7]
- **Military and security presence:** a car-bomb attack on the gendarmerie group headquarters on 3 Mar 2012 wounded 23 (15 gendarmes, 5 civil-protection staff, 3 civilians), claimed by MUJAO (UNVERIFIED, Algerie360 snippets). In 2010 a joint Sahel military committee was based in the city (SOURCED, Wikipedia). [K9]

### E. Structural features
- **Migrant expulsions (K9, sensitive):** migrants arrested across Algeria are often detained in Tamanrasset and trucked to the "zero point" on the Niger border, then walk about 15 km to Assamaka; Alarm Phone Sahara counted 31,404 expelled to Niger in 2024 and up to 2,222 in unofficial convoys 1–21 Apr 2025. Legal basis: Law 08-11 (2008; art. 37 "waiting centers"; five days to appeal) and Law 09-01 (2009; irregular exit a crime, 2–6 months). SOURCED (Global Detention Project, 10 Jul 2025). The army reported about 24,000 turned back at the border zones in 2025 (In Guezzam, Tinzaouatène, Bordj Badji Mokhtar); migrants who reach southern cities work in farming, building or trade (SOURCED, RFI via AllAfrica, 8 Jan 2026). In July 2016, after clashes in several quarters, a census by the association Green-Tea counted 2,724 displaced migrants (Guinea 600, Nigeria 420, Mali 300, Liberia 275 and others) gathered at the southern exit toward In Guezzam near the migrant reception center (SOURCED, Algerie360, 17 Jul 2016).
- **Nuclear-test legacy (K9, sensitive):** France carried out 13 underground tests at In Ekker 1961–1966 (UNVERIFIED, French Senate report snippet). The Béryl test of 1 May 1962 in the Taourirt Tan Afella massif, about 150 km north of Tamanrasset, vented: a cloud to about 2,600 m, contamination measurable up to about 150 km; nine soldiers about 600 mSv; about 240 Kel Tohra nomads estimated up to about 2.5 mSv; no data on the health of the Saharan Tuareg population; the food chain never studied. SOURCED (Wikipedia, Béryl incident). France's 2010 Morin law created compensation; a 2025 bill to widen it (including for Algeria) passed the National Assembly (UNVERIFIED snippets; final adoption NOT FOUND).
- **Tourism rules (K6):** foreign visitors to the Ahaggar must use an agency approved by the authorities, travel by 4x4 on organized circuits, and use guides for almost all activities (SOURCED, Routard, page showing a March 2026 French red-zone warning). Algeria reported 47,000 foreign tourists in the Sahara in the 2024–25 season, attributed to a "regularization visa" (SOURCED, APS, 13 Nov 2025; no Tamanrasset breakdown).
- **Border tension (K9):** on the night of 31 Mar–1 Apr 2025 Algeria shot down a Malian drone near Tinzaouatène (then in Tamanrasset province, per AP); Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger recalled ambassadors; Algeria and Mali closed their airspace to each other; Mali filed an ICJ case. UNVERIFIED (AP, Al Jazeera snippets).
- **Tuareg leadership (K1):** the Amenokal headed the Kel Ahaggar confederation from about 1750; recent press records disputes among notables over marginalization (undated snippets; UNVERIFIED).

## 54 · Mendoza and the Cristo Redentor pass, Argentina (with the Chilean side, Los Libertadores)

**Claim to test:** "The last city before a high mountain crossing that closes in bad weather, with trucks waiting for the pass to reopen (K5, K7)."
**Verdict:** CONFIRMED, with narrowing. The pass (Sistema Integrado Cristo Redentor / Paso Los Libertadores on the Chilean side) closes for snow most winters, and trucks wait in staged holding lots. But the **"last town" is Uspallata** (about 1,800 m, Las Heras department), not Mendoza city: Uspallata holds the cargo control complex and the main truck lots, and Mendoza city is the metropolitan and logistics base further back along National Route 7 (RN7). The winter of 2026 brought a closure of **34 consecutive days** (reopened to trucks 18 Aug 2026), called historic. [K5, K7, K9]
**Dating caution:** several sitioandino.com.ar pages display a "4 de agosto de 2026" date that is the site's display date, not the article date (one such page was in fact signed 4 Jul 2024). Dates below are the article dates where the page gave them.

### A. Snapshot
- **Mendoza province:** 2,043,540 people (2022 census, final). Departments: Guaymallén 321,966; Las Heras 234,401; Maipú 219,402; San Rafael 215,020; Capital (city proper) 127,160 per Fundación Metropolitana; Greater Mendoza 1,056,893 (citypopulation/INDEC) or 1,273,144 (Fundación Metropolitana), depending on definition. UNVERIFIED (Los Andes, citypopulation, Fundación Metropolitana snippets).
- **Uspallata:** 3,437 (2001) to 9,654 (2010), +148%, unplanned growth, weak health and waste services; the largest population and service center before the crossing and site of the first border-control post. SOURCED (Unidiversidad/UNCUYO Institute of Geography study, 27 Nov 2016).
- **The pass system:** three complexes: Los Horcones (Integrated Control Area for people entering Argentina), Los Libertadores (Chile) and the Uspallata cargo complex. SOURCED as a government description (UNVERIFIED snippet of argentina.gob.ar). The international road tunnel opened May 1980, 3,080 m long (UNVERIFIED). The Caracoles rail tunnel (1910) of the Transandine Railway (passenger service closed 1979, freight 1984 after an avalanche on the Chilean side destroyed track) is being widened for vehicles (UNVERIFIED, Memoria Chilena and press snippets). Works began February 2026 and will take years: Caracoles widened first, then the Cristo Redentor tunnel refitted, ending with two lanes each way in separate tunnels (UNVERIFIED, Sitio Andino snippet).
- **Defining feature:** an Andean crossing near 3,200 m that is the main truck route between Argentina (and Mercosur) and Chile's Pacific ports. Traffic estimates: up to 1,500 trucks/day and 400,000 private vehicles/year (UNVERIFIED, Los Andes Jan 2026 snippet); 800–1,000 trucks on high-flow days (UNVERIFIED); about 2,000 trucks gather in Uspallata after one day of closure (SOURCED, Los Andes, 24 Jun 2022, Vialidad coordinator).

### B. What else exists there
- **Wine (K7, K8):** Mendoza has about 142,800–147,379 ha of vines (2024; figures differ between official dossiers) and produced about 10.57 million hl of wine and must in 2024; it accounts for about 56% of Argentina's vineyard workers (about 16,000 registered, about 3,000 unregistered); about 5,000 seasonal migrant harvest workers arrive each season; harvest mechanization advancing in the Uco Valley. UNVERIFIED (Mendoza dossiers, Unidiversidad snippets).
- **Water governance (K1):** the Departamento General de Irrigación; canal-level **Inspecciones de Cauce** whose inspectors are elected every four years by all irrigators in their jurisdiction; turn schedules (*planillas de turno*) issued by the inspections (Law 1920 of 1950); an annual winter **"corta"** (cut-off period) with reduced turns set by the agency; vigilance commissions of at least three irrigators up to date on their fees. UNVERIFIED (mendoza.gov.ar snippets; the one press page read, of 30 Apr 2013, only confirms the Inspecciones de Cauce exist and took on groundwater tasks). The urban profile (sidewalk, *acequia* ditch, tree row, street) and planned urban greenery date from rebuilding after the earthquake of 20 Mar 1861 (UNVERIFIED, academic snippets). [K1, K6, R]
- **Mountain hospital (K5):** Hospital Luis Chrabalowski, Uspallata, at 1,800 m, the only hospital on the Mendoza cordillera; covers about 200 km of four routes and about 15,000 residents; about 3,000 people a day on RN7; 12 beds (16 in emergency); two emergency doctors; three ambulances (one 4x4); 221 truckers treated in 2024; daily health rounds of the ACI customs lot during closures (one closure lasted nearly 17 days); member of the High Mountain Crisis Committee (police, gendarmerie, army, ministries). SOURCED (mendoza.gov.ar, 18 Nov 2024).
- **Road crews (K14, K7):** Vialidad Nacional camps at Uspallata, Punta de Vacas, Puente del Inca and Las Cuevas (UNVERIFIED snippet). A crew of 26 on rotating shifts; after heavy snow work starts at 2–3 a.m. (about −8 °C) to hand the road over by 9 a.m.; traffic then runs to 23:00; salt stops working below about −7/−8 °C, so calcium chloride is used; preventive wetting before snow; Vialidad tells Gendarmería how far the road is clear and Gendarmería decides open/close. SOURCED (Los Andes, 24 Jun 2022). [SM:competence]
- **High-mountain villages (K4, K10):** Puente del Inca had about 17 residents in July 2026 per a resident (UNVERIFIED), 27 long-standing families and a conflict with the provincial government over a relocation plan in 2023 (UNVERIFIED); one multi-grade school with 32 pupils (UNVERIFIED); Las Cuevas keeps about a third of its people in winter and was cut off about 15 days in July 2026 until Vialidad opened a "sanitary breach" (UNVERIFIED, Diario Uno, Los Andes snippets). On 21 Jul 2026, 42 people were evacuated from Puente del Inca, Penitentes and Polvaredas by an 11-vehicle convoy to the Uspallata bus terminal. SOURCED (Mendoza Post).
- **Aconcagua Provincial Park:** season 1 Nov–30 Apr; over 83,000 visitors in 2024–25, over 106,000 in 2025–26 (mostly Horcones valley); permits sold only through registered service companies (Decree 2322 for 2025–26); foreigners need search-and-rescue insurance (2023 note). UNVERIFIED (mendoza.gov.ar, Diario Uno snippets). [K14]

### C. Cultural locations
- **Cristo Redentor de los Andes** statue at the border summit, inaugurated 13 Mar 1904 (more than 3,000 attendees, troops of both nations) after the 1902 May Pacts; proposed by Bishop Marcolino Benavente; sculptor Mateo Alonso (some sources: Manuel). Reached from Las Cuevas by a mountain road. UNVERIFIED (Los Andes, La Nación, Billiken snippets).
- **Fiesta Nacional de la Vendimia (K2, K3):** first official festival 1936 (decree of Governor Guillermo Cano), with a 1913 precedent (UNVERIFIED snippets). Sequence: Blessing of the Fruits (last Sunday of February); **Vía Blanca de las Reinas** (first Friday of March, departmental queens on floats at night; over 200,000 spectators); **Carrusel Vendimial** (Saturday morning, with riders in gaucho dress); **Acto Central** at the Teatro Griego Frank Romero Day (light-and-sound show, over 1,000 performers, honors the Virgen de la Carrodilla, ends with election of the national queen and fireworks). SOURCED (en.wikipedia; page flagged for citations). The Greek theater has hosted it since 1963 (UNVERIFIED). Each department holds its own festival first. [K2, K8]
- **Folk music (K13):** the *tonada* (recognized as provincial intangible heritage), *cueca cuyana* and *canción*; peñas gather families and young people; figures include Hilario Cuadros, Alberto Rodríguez (collector of tonadas), Ismael Moreno, Buenaventura Luna. UNVERIFIED (Diario de Cuyo, UNCUYO thesis snippets).
- **Plaza Independencia, Parque General San Martín, the Alameda:** post-1861 and later civic spaces (UNVERIFIED snippets). [K10]
- **Shop hours (K6, K7):** traditional split hours with a midday siesta closure, especially in summer; the city government has pushed continuous hours ("Comprá a la Siesta" program, siesta band 14:00–16:30, not mandatory); 65–70% of central shops reportedly on continuous hours. UNVERIFIED (El Sol, Sitio Andino, Unidiversidad snippets; dates unclear).

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- **A staged holding system for trucks (K1, K5, K6):** the provincial **protocol for prolonged closures** (in force since 2024; tested in a 17-day closure that year) stages freight along the corridor: in June 2025 the high-mountain points (ACI, YPF station, EG3 Doña Carmen) held 610 trucks at capacity; 60 at Red Mercosur and 204 on the RN40 shoulders at the RN40/RN7 junction; the Eloy Guerrero station (RN7) retained 420 in seven steps toward La Paz; remaining capacity about 800. Coordinated by provincial ministries, Mendoza Police, Gendarmería, the pass coordination and Las Heras municipality. SOURCED (mendoza.gov.ar, 17 Jun 2025). A Horcones protocol lets that complex hold trucks up to its 600-space parking capacity in closures over 72 h (UNVERIFIED snippet).
- **Reopening rules (K6):** in August 2026 the reopening ran trucks only, Argentina→Chile 18–20 Aug (to Guardia Vieja), then Chile→Argentina 21–22 Aug (to Uspallata), 09:00–21:00 Argentine time, with **priority to trucks already documented in the ACI Uspallata lot**; tourist traffic suspended from Uspallata. SOURCED (Canal 26, 17 Aug 2026). About 1,350 trucks crossed on the first day (UNVERIFIED snippet). Winter hours close the system at night (e.g., 23:00 at Uspallata from 31 May 2026; 9–21 Argentina/8–20 Chile on some days); chains are compulsory in winter (UNVERIFIED snippets; mendoza.gov.ar headline "Es obligatorio el uso de cadenas…").
- **Biosecurity closures (K6):** the system was closed from 10:00 on 19 Feb 2026 to 12:00 on 20 Feb 2026 after a suspected *Aedes aegypti* mosquito was found, by the Chilean coordination with Argentine agreement. SOURCED (Diario de Cuyo, 18 Feb 2026). Chile's SAG requires a free, mandatory sworn declaration from every adult entering; fruit, seeds, plants, flowers, honey and natural-material crafts are banned; undeclared risk products are fined (UNVERIFIED, sag.gob.cl snippets).
- **The Chilean terminal:** the **Puerto Terrestre Los Andes** (El Sauce, Los Andes commune; 24.5 ha) is the concessioned site where Chile's Customs, SAG and the regional health authority inspect all road freight through Los Libertadores; commercial building of 50 offices with food, banks and public toilets; warehouse over 1,500 m² and parking for 65 trucks; concession from 2005, extended to 31 Aug 2029. SOURCED (MOP concession leaflet, June 2026, PDF read locally).
- **Waiting life (K7, K10, sensitive):** July 2024: more than 5,000 truckers waiting after more than 20 days; two drivers died (one Brazilian, one Chilean); customs toilets "precarious"; a shower cost 4,000–5,000 pesos; Uspallata vendors expensive; a union official alleged 400 trucks of one association got priority. SOURCED (Sitio Andino, 4 Jul 2024). July 2026: Gendarmería's Uspallata squadron (Escuadrón 27) ran a field kitchen at the integrated complex serving hot meals to drivers in the holding lots. SOURCED (Mendoza Post, 21 Jul 2026). 2016: Paraguayan drivers cooked together, walked 7 km to buy food and drove 20 km for a hot shower; nights −3/−4 °C in Uspallata and −15 °C at the border. SOURCED (Última Hora, 15 Jun 2016). [SM:competence]

## 55 · Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port (Donibane Garazi), France

**Claim to test:** "The last town before a mountain crossing on a pilgrim route, where a pilgrim office gives route and weather advice before people commit (K1, K5)." Care note: nation note, structure only.
**Verdict:** CONFIRMED. The town is the main starting point of the Camino Francés before the Pyrenees crossing to Roncesvalles (Orreaga). A volunteer-run pilgrims' reception office (association Les Amis du Chemin de Saint-Jacques des Pyrénées-Atlantiques, 39 rue de la Citadelle) registers, stamps credentials, and directs walkers; in winter the high route (Route Napoléon over the Lepoeder pass) is closed by the Government of Navarre and walkers must take the valley route via Valcarlos. Weather-advice practice is documented only in secondary sources (see F). [K1, K5, K6]

### A. Snapshot
- **Location and size:** commune of the Pyrénées-Atlantiques at the confluence of the Nive and Laurhibar; area 2.73 km²; altitude 159–320 m; former capital of Basse-Navarre; about 7 km from the Spanish border at Arnéguy. SOURCED (fr.wikipedia).
- **Population:** 1,121 (1793); peak 2,332 (1841); 1,404 (1921); 1,612 (1962); 1,432 (1990); 1,580 (2015); 1,510 (2020); 1,479 (2023); down 6.81% 2017–2023. SOURCED (fr.wikipedia). The municipal bulletin notes that a tourism label brings a "surclassement démographique," setting the commune's legal total population at 3,771 for that purpose. SOURCED (Bulletin municipal no. 3, Jan 2025, PDF read locally).
- **History:** 12th-century bastide; fortified by Sancho VII of Navarre in the early 13th century; market and fair charters 1329; taken by Spain 1512; castle destroyed 1530; Citadelle (Mendiguren) built 1625–27 and expanded under Vauban (1686–1700), now occupied by a school and not open to visitors; the Navarrese estates' last session held in the Mendiri house, 19–22 Sep 1789. SOURCED (fr.wikipedia).
- **Defining feature:** the pilgrim threshold. The reception office recorded **58,451 pilgrims of 121 nationalities in 2024** (+1.90% on 2023) and **54,979 in 2025** (118 nationalities): top nationalities 2025 French 9,689, American 7,993, Spanish 4,384, Korean 4,308, Italian 3,794; Americans 14% in 2024 and 2025; peaks in May and September (over 10,000 passages each in 2024). SOURCED (Bulletin municipal Jan 2025; AFCC/ACIR *Note de conjoncture 2025*, April 2026, PDF read locally). The town received the label "Commune Haltes • Chemins de Compostelle" in June 2024 (SOURCED, bulletin).
- **Languages (K11, loose):** French and Basque (Euskara); the bulletin is bilingual. In Basse-Navarre and Soule, 47.5% (or 49.5%, the two OPLB documents differ) of people 16+ were Basque speakers in 2021 (UNVERIFIED, OPLB snippets). Schools include a Basque-immersion ikastola (Garaziko Ikastola) and a bilingual private school (SOURCED, fr.wikipedia).

### B. What else exists there
- **Pilgrim infrastructure (K1, K5):** the reception office at 39 rue de la Citadelle (Maison Laborde) stamps credentials (SOURCED as fr.wikipedia/Maison Laborde naming; UNVERIFIED for hours 07:30–12:00, 13:30–20:00 from an old guide); the municipal pilgrim refuge (Ospitalia, 55 rue de la Citadelle) belongs to the town, its upkeep is borne by the association, it admits only credential-holding pilgrims who first check in at the office (UNVERIFIED snippets; prices 12–16 € dorm reported); 5,887 credentials sold through the national agency's website in 2025 (SOURCED, AFCC note; this is a national web figure, not the office's). In spring 2024 the office went from 5–6 pilgrims a day in winter to 450 and one day over 500 after Easter; the town lent the former fire station on rue Sainte-Eulalie, where mattresses were laid for unhoused pilgrims. SOURCED (bulletin, account by a volunteer). A 2019 forum-relayed post claims a record 558 in one day with 44 sleeping in the municipal dojo (UNVERIFIED).
- **Refuges on the high route:** Refuge d'Orisson (about 8 km up, 793 m): dormitories of 6, 8 and 8 plus two 2-bed chalet rooms; booking strongly advised; walkers only; credential required. UNVERIFIED (refuges.info and guide snippets).
- **Gendarmerie:** the local brigade patrols the Camino routes from March to October; the association's multilingual volunteers inform walkers year-round. UNVERIFIED (gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr snippet; page refused 403).
- **Transport (K5):** rail line Bayonne–Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port (opened 1889); buses from Bayonne and Saint-Jean-de-Luz; RD 933 to Spain (SOURCED, fr.wikipedia). Private luggage and taxi services to Orisson, Valcarlos and Roncesvalles operate late March to late October (UNVERIFIED snippets).
- **Schools:** four primary (public, public nursery, Garaziko Ikastola, Sainte-Marie bilingual), collèges de la Citadelle (public) and Saint-Jean-de-Mayorga (private), Lycée de Navarre and the private agricultural Lycée Frantses-Enia. SOURCED (fr.wikipedia).
- **Economy (K7, K8):** tourism hub of inland Basque Country; within the Ossau-Iraty cheese appellation area (SOURCED, fr.wikipedia); 270 active establishments in 2023 (UNVERIFIED). The Irouléguy wine cooperative (founded 1952 by nine growers at Saint-Étienne-de-Baïgorry, AOC 1970; appellation about 270 ha; 60% organic in 2021) is the valley's wine institution (UNVERIFIED snippets). The Commission syndicale du Pays de Cize manages about 12,000 ha of undivided mountain commons (summer pastures), with sworn wardens enforcing pasture rules and a grazing contract (*facerie*) with the Aezkoa valley in Navarre (UNVERIFIED snippets). [K1, K7]

### C. Cultural locations
- **Porte Saint-Jacques** (pilgrims' entry from Ostabat), **rue de la Citadelle** (stone houses with dated lintels), **rue d'Espagne** (pilgrims' departure), **Église de l'Assomption** (formerly Notre-Dame-du-Bout-du-Pont; Cavaillé-Coll organ restored 2002–04), **Prison des Évêques** (now a museum, garden linked to the Maison des Évêques/Maison Laborde), former **Hôpital Sainte-Marie** (served "poor, travelers, pilgrims"). SOURCED (fr.wikipedia). The "Routes of Santiago de Compostela in France" were inscribed by UNESCO on 2 Dec 1998 (78 components); whether the Porte Saint-Jacques is a listed component was NOT confirmed (searched).
- **Pelota (K10, K13, K14):** the **Trinquet Garat**, for decades run by the Garat family, a high place of bare-hand pelota; the commune rented it from 1973 (managed by the pelota society Goizeko Izarra), bought it in 1993; Monday games now run by the association ESKU PILOTA. SOURCED (bulletin). Also the fronton and the Jaï-Alaï (used for a Malandain Ballet Biarritz show). SOURCED (bulletin).
- **Markets (K10):** a Monday market (morning, local food) at and around the covered market; the town made it pedestrian and enlarged it for non-food stalls (UNVERIFIED snippets). A potters' market, 2nd edition 4–5 May 2024 (SOURCED, bulletin). The Basque urban cemetery inventoried (441 burials) and restored with volunteers (SOURCED, bulletin; recorded as a fact only).
- **Festivals (K2, K3):** Fête de la Saint-Jean, 21 Jun 2025; town fêtes 14–17 Aug 2025, preceded by **house rounds** (*Tournées des Maisons*) by quarter on Saturdays in August (e.g., 2 Aug: Route Napoléon, Kurutxamendi and Route St-Michel); organized with the youth association Donibane Garaziko Gazteria (SOURCED, bulletin). The 2026 program listed a road race, children's day, pelota finals (chistera and bare hand), Basque dances by Garaztarrak, a *mus* card tournament and a braderie (UNVERIFIED, tourism-office listings). Festival de théâtre "Citadelle en Scène" each August (SOURCED, bulletin).
- **Sport:** FC Garazi (football, 1977), US Nafarroa (rugby, 2003), Stade Pierre-Camou (named 2024). SOURCED (fr.wikipedia).

### D. How the defining feature shaped those things
- **A reception institution for a shared problem:** an association of volunteers runs the office, maintains the municipal refuge and counts pilgrims; the town adds emergency floor space when beds run out; the commune sought a hospitality label (K1, K4, K10). [SM:competence]
- **Winter closure of the high route (K1, K6):** since 2015 Navarre's Directorate General of Interior closes the eastern variant over the Lepoeder pass every year from 1 November to 31 March, at a coordinate near the Bentartea col at the entry to Navarre; walkers must take the western variant via Luzaide/Valcarlos and the Ibañeta pass (about 24–25 km). Reason: many winter rescues; the route climbs from 154 m to 1,432 m. UNVERIFIED (Noticias de Navarra 7 Nov 2025 and alberguescaminosantiago snippets; Noticias de Navarra pages refused, 406). The Burguete fire station carried out 135 rescue or search operations in 2017, 114 in 2018 and 103 in 2019 across its whole area (UNVERIFIED snippet). Forum users report the French side remains legally open and that French police turn walkers back; a fine of about EUR 12,000 is rumored but no official source was found (UNVERIFIED; see F).
- **Hospitality as public order (K6):** dog bans in named public spaces and fines (135 €) for not picking up waste (SOURCED, bulletin) are the only municipal behavior rules found; no rule on pilgrims' conduct, camping or alcohol hours was found.

### E. Structural features
- **Seasonality (K7):** pilgrim peaks in May and September; winter flow of 5–6 a day (bulletin); services such as luggage transport close in winter.
- **Saturation (K9):** a commercial diagnosis cited in snippets says traders and residents sometimes feel "saturation" with pilgrims, and that most traders find pilgrims spend little (UNVERIFIED; the PDF returned 404).
- **Population decline** of the commune alongside the pilgrim boom (SOURCED figures above).
