## 1 · #85 Magadan and the Kolyma Highway · ⚠ PARTIAL (not ticked; stopped at the search-budget limit, see STOP note below)

**Claim to test:** a coastal port that is the starting point of the one long road into a cold, remote interior; ships arrive only when the bay's ice allows, and everything bound inland passes through the port first.

**Verdict so far (provisional):**
- **The port is NOT ice-locked; the claim must be corrected on that point.** Ice is present in Nagaev Bay from late November to mid-June (SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagaev_Bay, read 2026-11-20), but the Port of Magadan operates year-round with icebreaker escort (SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadan, read 2026-11-20). In winter 2022–23 the Rosmorport icebreaker *Moskva* worked the port and its approaches from 25 December 2022 for 131 days, escorting 108 vessels; escort ended 5 May 2023 by the harbor master's order, set from a Rosgidromet long-range forecast and an analysis of Okhotsk Sea ice (SOURCED, en.portnews.ru/news/347035, read 2026-11-20). In January 2026 *Moskva* began escort late because fast ice was delayed by an abnormally warm December (UNVERIFIED, search snippet of www1.ru, 2026-01-07). [K5, K7, K1] [P3]
- **"Everything inland passes through the port" is broadly supported but rests on trade-press figures:** PortNews reports repeat that the port handles about 90% of the Magadan Region's cargo, including the state-organized seasonal "northern delivery" of goods (UNVERIFIED, snippets of en.portnews.ru items 2010, Nov 2024, Mar 2025; no official port statistic found). The oblast must bring in about 50% of its food from outside (SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadan_Oblast, read 2026-11-20). The other entries are Sokol Airport (large aircraft) and Magadan-13 (small aircraft); there is no railway (the Magadan–Palatka line ran 1941–1956) (SOURCED, Magadan article). [K5] [P5]
- **The road and port were built by forced labor in the camp system: confirmed** (details below).

### A. Snapshot
- **Magadan:** city on the northern coast of the Sea of Okhotsk, at the head of Nagaev Bay within Taui Bay, on the isthmus of the Staritsky Peninsula. Founded 1929 in the Ola River valley near the settlement of Nagayevo; town status 14 July 1939. Census population: 27,313 (1939); 62,225 (1959); 151,652 (1989, peak); 99,399 (2002); 95,982 (2010); 90,757 (2021); estimate 89,193 (2025). Subarctic climate (Dfc): coastal averages about −22 °C in January and +12 °C in July; record low −37 °C (20 Dec 1995), record high 27.8 °C (15 Jul 2021); about 586 mm precipitation a year; growing season about 100 days; permafrost and tundra cover most of the region. (All SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadan and /Nagaev_Bay, read 2026-11-20.) [P3]
- **Magadan Oblast:** population 385,340 (1989), 182,726 (2002), 156,996 (2010), 136,085 (2021), estimate 134,202 (2025); the article calls it the federal subject with the highest rate of depopulation in Russia (SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadan_Oblast, read 2026-11-20; the article's own intro gives a conflicting 1989 figure of 542,868, so the 1989 number needs checking against the census). [K9] [P9]
- **R504 Kolyma Highway:** 2,031 km, part of route M56, from Magadan to Nizhny Bestyakh on the Lena opposite Yakutsk, where it meets the Lena Highway. Points along it: Khandyga, Kyubeme, Ust-Nera, Kadykchan, Susuman, the Kolyma River bridge at Debin, Orotukan, and Gerba (where the Anadyr Highway branches toward Chukotka). Federal road status 2008; a maintained all-weather gravel road, paved for 159 km at each end (Yakutsk–Churapcha and nearest Magadan). The Aldan is crossed by a summer ferry at Krest-Khaldzhay (bridge planned for 2029). The older Summer Road via Tomtor is largely unmaintained, with washed-out bridges; the page says it is impassable in summer as of 2026. A lower-traffic shortcut, the Tenkinskaya road, links Magadan and Susuman via Ust-Omchug. (SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R504_Kolyma_Highway, read 2026-11-20.) [K5] [P5]
- **Defining feature:** the city was founded as the sea gate and supply base for the Kolyma goldfields and was the administrative center of Dalstroy, the forced-labor gold-mining and camp system, from 1932 to 1953 (SOURCED, Magadan article).

### B. What else exists there
- **History of the port and the camp system (reported plainly):** Dalstroy was created in November 1931 (UNVERIFIED, snippet citing the Labor and Defense Council resolution). Sevvostlag, the camp that supplied Dalstroy's labor, was created in April 1932; its prisoners began the first stretch of the road in 1932, and prisoner labor on it continued until 1953 (SOURCED, R504 article). Nagaev Bay was the transit point for prisoners arriving by sea, who were then sent to camps in Magadan and the Kolyma; Sevvostlag headquarters were there from 1937 (SOURCED, Nagaev Bay article). Close to 1 million prisoners were sent to Kolyma over about a quarter-century and at least 125,000 died there; prisoner labor mined the gold and built the infrastructure (SOURCED, The Moscow Times, Polly Jones, article dated 10 Dec 2025, read 2026-11-20). Death-toll estimates vary widely: records show 10,251 deaths in the Kolyma prison system in 1938, mostly from disease; a 1950s CIA estimate of 3 million is described as flawed; a review of Martin Bollinger's work cites 130,000; the widely repeated claim that 250,000 to 1,000,000 who died were buried in or beside the road is said to lack documentary or physical confirmation (SOURCED, R504 article). Dalstroy's first director, Eduard Berzin, built the camp infrastructure 1932–1937 and was executed in 1938 (SOURCED, Magadan article). [K9] [P6] [SM:a dangerous or fascinating figure or system]
- **Port:** opened 1932; first mooring berths December 1933; second jetty 1935; renamed Magadan Commercial Sea Port 1977; handled more than 4 million t around 1980; opened to international freight and passengers 1993; 13 berths (3 oil products, 2 containers, 8 other goods), about 32.64 ha; in 1947 the explosives-laden ship *General Vatutin* blew up in the bay, destroying port equipment, cargo and buildings; in 2016 added to the list of ports accepting nuclear and radioactive cargo (SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Magadan, read 2026-11-20). Throughput was 1.65 million t in 2023 (UNVERIFIED, Interfax snippet). Second-largest seaport of Russia's north-east after Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky (SOURCED, Magadan article). [K5] [P8]
- **What comes in:** coal is the clearest bulk example: the Magadan thermal power plant received about 225,000 t of coal during the 2024 navigation, while the inland Arkagala power station takes coal from the Kadykchan open pit year-round (UNVERIFIED, snippet); a 2010 report describes the port working nearly around the clock during the northern delivery to get food and fuel to remote Kolyma districts, with the city boiler house's 250,000 t of Kuzbass coal marking the delivery as complete (UNVERIFIED, snippet, 2010). [K5, K7]
- **Economy:** gold mining and fisheries are the main income; gold output had fallen by 2007 and fishing fell short of quotas (linked to an aging fleet); smaller industries include pasta and sausage plants and a distillery; one of the lowest agricultural self-sufficiency rates in the Russian Far East (SOURCED, Magadan article). Oblast: nearly 2,000 placer gold deposits, 100 gold-ore and 48 silver-ore deposits; fishing (pollock, herring, cod, salmon, crab) is the second sector; a special economic zone was extended to 31 Dec 2025 (SOURCED, Magadan Oblast article). The Natalka gold deposit, about 400 km from Magadan, was discovered in the early 1940s and is worked by a Polyus subsidiary (UNVERIFIED, snippet). Polyus job listings for another deposit advertise 60-shift rotations on a six-days-on, one-off pattern with paid inter-rotation rest (UNVERIFIED, hh.ru snippets; not shown for Natalka itself). [K7, K8] [P8]
- **Workforce and people (2021 census, oblast):** Russians 109,773 (87.7%), Ukrainians 3,380 (2.7%), Evens 2,062 (1.6%), Uzbeks 904 (0.7%), Koryaks 742 (0.5%), Buryats 639 (0.5%), other 7,659, not stated 10,926 (SOURCED, Magadan Oblast article). Indigenous peoples listed: Evens, Koryaks, Yupik, Chukchi, Orochs, Chuvans, Itelmens, traditionally fishing or reindeer herding; their institutional support collapsed after 1987 and many are unemployed (SOURCED, same). The village of Ola, east of Magadan, is officially a place of compact Indigenous habitation; Indigenous residents and descendants of early Russian settlers live by fishing (UNVERIFIED, snippet of the Norwegian Polar Institute ANSIPRA page by W. Dallmann); Ola census population 5,704 (2021) (UNVERIFIED, citypopulation.de snippet). [K4] [P9]
- **Rural emptying along the interior:** Susumansky District's rural population fell from 9,764 (1991) to 116 (2007); Yagodninsky District's from 13,843 (1991) to 445 (2007); "entire villages" emptied (SOURCED, Magadan Oblast article). [K9] [P9]
- **Pay and northern benefits:** Far North wage coefficients and seniority supplements apply (a 1.7 regional coefficient is cited for a Magadan Oblast settlement, with seniority supplements up to 80% of pay; additional northern leave) (UNVERIFIED, snippets of ppt.ru and similar; age of the coefficient rule unclear). Specific rules on subsidized travel for residents NOT FOUND (one search). [K8]
- **Institutions:** North-Eastern State University; the University of Alaska Anchorage offers a scholarship to full-time students from Magadan; newspaper *Magadanskaya Pravda*; regional library named after Pushkin (SOURCED, Magadan article). Sister cities: Anchorage (1991), Tonghua (1992), Zlatitsa (2012), Shuangyashan (2013); Jelgava suspended its agreement in 2022 (SOURCED, same).
- **Religion (oblast, 2012 survey):** Russian Orthodox 29.6%, "spiritual but not religious" 27%, atheist and irreligious 13.2%, Islam and Old Believers about 1% each (SOURCED, Magadan Oblast article).

### C. Cultural locations (named)
- **Mask of Sorrow** (Ernst Neizvestny, opened 1996), on a hill above the city; stone markers below it name many of the Kolyma camps (SOURCED, Moscow Times; marker detail UNVERIFIED, snippet). Annual 30 October ceremony (national Day of Remembrance of Victims of Political Repression) with flowers and the reading of names ("Returning the Names"); in 2025 the mayor and governor did not attend, and teachers and librarians were told orally not to hold or publicize repression events (SOURCED, Moscow Times). [K2, K9] [P6]
- Other monuments: a tribute to the singer Vadim Kozin; a monument to Eduard Berzin (SOURCED, Moscow Times).
- **Regional local-history museum** (state-funded; two sections on Kolyma and Dalstroy, mostly emphasizing industrialization and wartime heroism) (SOURCED, Moscow Times); Regional Museum of Anthropology; a geological museum (SOURCED, Magadan article). Beyond the city: the **Memory of Kolyma** museum in Yagodnoye founded by Ivan Panikarov (several thousand items, sometimes shown in private apartments for lack of government support); a Shalamov museum in Debin closed in 2023 for alleged safety violations; a federally backed open-air memory park in the region with a 2030 target, while a planned Dneprovsky camp museum about 300 km away is reportedly shelved (SOURCED, Moscow Times). [K9, K13]
- **Magadan Theater; Gornyak Cinema; Magadan Hotel** (SOURCED, Magadan article). The city's music-and-drama theater grew out of the camp system: a first theater at a camp administration club in 1938, a troupe of imprisoned and convoy-free actors, musicians and designers; the Magadan Gorky Music and Drama Theater formed 3 Sept 1941 and opened 5 Oct 1941 (UNVERIFIED, snippets of bigenc.ru, cyclowiki and an academic article by I. I. Krylovskaya on gitis.net). [K13] [P8]
- **Churches:** Cathedral of the Life-Giving Trinity (Orthodox, completed 2008); Catholic Church of the Nativity (completed 2002) (SOURCED, Magadan article).
- **Dyukcha ethnic park**, Magadan: venue for both festivals below (SOURCED, runews24 and 360.ru).
