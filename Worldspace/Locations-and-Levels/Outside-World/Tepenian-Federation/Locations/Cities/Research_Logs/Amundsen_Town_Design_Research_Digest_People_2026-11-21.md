# Amundsen Town Design: Research Digest, People and Things (real-world research, 2026-11-21)

Real-world research only. This digest collects what ten research logs in this folder (all dated 2026-11-01) say about **who lives and works in remote, supplied-from-outside technical places, in what numbers and roles, and what those people have**. It is written for a team designing a town of about 7,000 (about 1,100 humans and 5,700 robots) on a high, cold ice plateau, built to keep one large technical installation running. It does not describe any fictional setting and it does not recommend designs.

**Marks** are carried over from the source files and never upgraded: **SOURCED** (with the URL the source file gives), **UNVERIFIED** (search summary, secondary page or single anecdote), **DERIVED** (arithmetic shown), **NOT FOUND**. "DERIVED (this digest)" means arithmetic done here on figures the files give; nothing else in this digest was added from outside the files. Where a URL is long, it is given exactly as the source file gives it. File codes: B3, B4, B5, B6, B7, Bg (B-round gaps follow-up), C1, C3, C6, C8 (full names in "What I read").

**"What it causes" chains** are marked 1st / 2nd / 3rd order and carry the mark of the weakest link. They only restate links the files themselves draw.

## 2. Terms and rotation

### 2.1 Lengths of stay
- CFS Alert: personnel "typically serve a six-month posting, some specialist roles every 12 weeks"; contractor rotation set per position with a 3-month forward plan. SOURCED, B4 §6.
- Pituffik: US service members serve "a year unaccompanied"; no family housing. SOURCED, B7 §7.6 (Space Force/Air Force housing pages via search snippet; UNVERIFIED in part).
- Neumayer III: "about 14 months with about 9 months of complete isolation"; four-month pre-departure training. SOURCED, B7 §3.6.
- Concordia: about 9-10 months of winter-over. SOURCED, B7 §1.4, C8 §7.1a.
- Ascension: Mitie unaccompanied posts with "24-month contracts," site allowance and tour bonus. UNVERIFIED (adverts), B4 §2.
- Kwajalein: most workers on multi-year contracts. UNVERIFIED (recruiting page), B4 §1.
- ITER: staff terms "up to five years" (Agreement Art. 7). SOURCED, B6 §1.7. Departures 5.0% a year (2024). SOURCED, B3 §2.1.
- Japanese company dormitory: applicants in principle under 32, stay at most four years. SOURCED, C3 §6e, https://www.p.u-tokyo.ac.jp/students/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/20250724-2.pdf
- Longyearbyen: 519 moved in and 558 out in 2025, "just under 80 percent" of residents still there a year later; DERIVED 21.8% annual outflow (B4 §5, SSB via search summary). Norwegian citizens' average stay 7.4 years, median 3.6 (2022). SOURCED, B7 §7.1. Average stay 6.3 years (Norwegians 6.6, foreigners 4.3); "43% of residents stayed less than two years and 64% less than five years" (2022); the government calls it a "revolving door society." SOURCED (Wikipedia), B6 §4.
- Coldfoot Camp: "Some of us stay for a season, some of us intend to stay for a season...but love it so much we stay for years." SOURCED, C3 §2j, https://www.coldfootcamp.com/who-we-are
- Kemano (company town at an underground powerhouse): "many stayed a year or two, others decades." SOURCED, C1 §5J.
- Barentsburg: two-year contracts, high turnover. UNVERIFIED (photo essay), B4 §10c.
- Diego Garcia: charter flights every three months carried 200-250 workers; "some workers had not left in over three years" when flights were canceled. SOURCED, B4 §3a, https://stripes.com/theaters/asia_pacific/2022-09-14/filipino-workers-stranded-us-military-base-7331493.html
- Ohio-class submarines: two crews (Blue and Gold), one at sea 60-90 days while the other trains ashore; boats at sea about 70% of the time. SOURCED, C6 §6.3, https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/505831
- Mars-rover teams on "Mars time": limited to 90 days at a time, no more than four days in a row. SOURCED, C6 §5.5.

### 2.2 Who stays longest
- CERN staff work 40 hours (94.93%); 2,660 staff against 13,039 users, most users present 30% of the year or less. SOURCED, B3 §1.1.
- Longyearbyen: Norwegians stay longer than foreigners (6.6 vs 4.3 years). SOURCED (Wikipedia), B6 §4.
- Paranal: telescope operators and astronomers "usually live in Santiago or Antofagasta, but come to Paranal for duty shifts"; engineers and technicians "work shifts of one week ... and one week off." SOURCED, B3 §6.1.
- Floating nuclear plant Akademik Lomonosov: 96 operating staff, of whom "42 will work permanently (living in Pevek)," the rest on rotation. SOURCED, B3 §4.2, https://rg.ru/2016/10/04/v-peveke-nachali-stroit-beregovye-i-gidrosooruzheniia-dlia-plavuchej-aes.html
- Hoover workforce aging: "Roughly two-fifths ... eligible to retire within five years"; "The millennials are hardest to keep." SOURCED, B3 §8.1 and Bg §4.2.

### 2.3 Contracts and conditions
- Ascension: no right of abode; a contract of employment generally needed to stay over three months; employers must by law provide free accommodation, medical cover, food or a food allowance, and travel; dependants only on "Accompanied Contract Status"; people who retire or reach 18 without a job must leave. SOURCED/secondary, B4 §2, https://ascensionisland.azurewebsites.net/faq
- Longyearbyen and Svalbard: "must have the means to be able to reside on Svalbard"; "no social assistance"; most housing employer-owned. SOURCED, B4 §5, https://www.sysselmesteren.no/en/entry-and-residence/
- Kola nuclear plant town: company pays for housing, helps with settling in, subsidized mortgages, polar allowances "from day one," 52 calendar days of leave. SOURCED, B3 Addendum, https://rg.ru/2022/04/27/reg-szfo/kak-reshaetsia-kadrovyj-vopros-v-atomnoj-energetike-zapoliaria.html
- Dalton Highway camps: "housing is provided for staff," meals "Provided," "No rent, no food bills, no commute"; drug-free workplace with pre-employment, random and for-cause testing. SOURCED, C3 §2j.
- Diego Garcia (2017-2022): mandated minimum $400 a month; workers may surrender passports; fines for leaving early; about $5.25 an hour (2022); employer said total compensation including housing, food and medical exceeded the minimum. SOURCED (journalism), B4 §3a.
- CERN: "equal pay for work of equal value, regardless of nationality"; "no general salary catch-up for CERN staff since 2000" (staff association view). SOURCED, B6 §1.2.

### 3.3 Hostels for people on leave or with nowhere to go
- Royal Sailors' Rests (Devonport and Portsmouth, from 1876): beds for "900 men at Devonport and 700 at Portsmouth," "barely equal to the demand"; "All ranks of sailors - and soldiers, if they wished - might come and go freely, only paying for bed, food, or bath"; "Everyone was to be admitted while there was room"; "the only rule being 'no disorderly conduct'"; strictly teetotal but the pledge book was offered, not forced; free classes and a savings bank; funds for families of men lost at sea; in 1905-06 "over 332,000 sailors let rooms" and "over 634,000 meals were taken," with "170 people" working at three sites (DERIVED about 910 men a night). ## 5. Morale and belonging

### 5.1 The baseline evidence
- About 5% of expedition members meet criteria for a psychiatric disorder; symptoms (disturbed sleep, reduced cognition, negative mood, interpersonal conflict) follow seasons; many gain positive outcomes; recommended: screening, telephone counseling, training in coping, group interaction and leadership. SOURCED, B7 §1.1 (Palinkas and Suedfeld 2008, PubMed 17655924). "40 to 60 percent" suffer negative effects: UNVERIFIED (press), B7 §1.1.
- Depression rose across a 12-month winter-over; social isolation may explain winter mood better than lack of sunlight. SOURCED, B7 §1.2 (PubMed 7676841).
- Third-quarter dip: supported in French station diaries and ISS journals, not consistently supported in a review (3 of 7 teams); every analog team reported at least one conflict by 40% of mission time; cohesion tended to decline; longer missions had less social time together. SOURCED/conflict, B7 §1.3, https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-423j-aerospace-biomedical-and-life-support-engineering-spring-2006/fb2f71dd65435b099ea319f3a0512221_icestuster2006.pdf
- Concordia: positive mood and coping hit a low around midwinter and recovered in the second half; authors read it as "an adaptive way of coping." SOURCED, B7 §1.4, https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02235/full
- Sleep: 41.4% of sleep episodes under 7 hours at Australian stations; team cohesion declined and conflict peaked in September, tracking time in the expedition more than daylight; "provision of a 24-h light-dark cycle ... does not guarantee entrainment." SOURCED, B7 §1.7 (PMC9541096). Indoor light rarely above 300-500 lux; extra light "did not fully prevent winter sleep problems"; blinds and eye masks advised for summer. SOURCED, B7 §1.7 (PMC3793275). Light therapy evidence for preventing winter depression "very low" quality. SOURCED (snippet), B7 §3.9.
- Predictors: low-cohesion expeditions report more depression, anxiety and anger; in-mission measures predict better than pre-departure ones. SOURCED, B7 §1.9.
- Rotation workers: job control, job clarity and social support linked to better mental health; supervisor support RR 0.76; shared accommodation and lack of privacy linked to anxiety; remoteness-related stress OR 3.7; shifts over 12 hours OR 1.61. SOURCED, B7 §9.3, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8311322/
- WA FIFO study: 33% high or very high psychological distress vs 17% benchmark; 71% drank more than two standard drinks on a drinking day; 29% used drugs in the last year; "26 percent could not recall any mental-health support option on site"; even-time rosters gave better outcomes. SOURCED, B4 §9a, https://www.mhc.wa.gov.au/media/2547/impact-of-fifo-work-arrangement-on-the-mental-health-and-wellbeing-of-fifo-workers-full-report.pdf
- Maritime: shorter time on board (about two months) linked to better mental health; temporary contracts linked to depression; inadequate telecommunication a strain; recreational areas valued for cohesion. SOURCED, B7 §9.3 (PMC9150387).

### 5.2 What keeps people from "climbing the walls" (as the sources describe it)
- Work: "Work is a primary source of satisfaction. Too much or too little work results in negativity"; "Helping others with novel tasks provides welcome variety." SOURCED, B7 §9.1 (Stuster). ISS crews complained of "trivial, redundant, and tedious tasks." SOURCED, B7 §9.1. Monotony was among the most-cited stressors; crews countered it with creative activities, "thematic food evenings, and structured planning around key dates"; strong job devotion a common coping strategy. SOURCED, B7 §8.4, §9.1, https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnrgo.2022.1007774/full
- Food and the cook: "Meals, special meals, and celebrations are extremely important ... and contribute to group solidarity"; "The chef is traditionally one of the most important people on station"; "morale lies in the chef's hands"; "one of the most important things for morale is food"; "well-rested and well-fed employees make for safer and happier employees." SOURCED, B7 §4.2, C8 §3.6, §3.8a.
- Marking time: "Time seems to slow down in isolation and confinement. Milestones, celebrations, and other events help mark the passage of time"; expedition-unique events (midwinter, theme dinners) are "enjoyed, talked about, and remembered," while traditional holidays are "not very enjoyable." SOURCED, B7 §2.3, §4.4.
- Informal roles: at Antarctic stations "the most effective crews had members who took on informal roles that supported group cohesion"; at the Pole "the clown bridged social relations between the scientists and the trades crew—the plumbers, electricians, and mechanics." SOURCED (press report of research), B7 §8.4, https://nautil.us/why-we-need-court-jesters-in-space-237446
- Privacy and control over contact: residents "chose to spend much time alone and valued control over social contact"; scientists invested in private and work spaces, technicians preferred the shared main hall; need for privacy rose again near the end. UNVERIFIED in detail (abstracts), B7 §3.7.
- Clubs that are not about drinking: a Toastmasters member chose it "because the other local clubs centered on drinking." SOURCED, B7 §4.1, https://toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2020/feb/sidebars/antarctica-club-549
- Contact with home: personal communications roughly net-neutral, communications with headquarters strongly negative (net −0.435); outside contact rose in value in the third quarter. SOURCED, B7 §6.3. Each Australian station has a quiet room for private calls. SOURCED, B7 §6.1.
- Company-town social life: a strong social life at Kennecott was "good for the company's profit margin, leading to stronger employee retention and lower training and transportation costs." SOURCED, C1 §6H, https://home.nps.gov/wrst/learn/historyculture/upload/Kennecottbulletin.pdf. Kemano residents: "you not only knew everybody's name, you knew their cats' and dogs' names"; "no crime, it was close-knit." SOURCED, C1 §5J.
- Local recognition: in Christchurch, about 150 cars lined the airport fence for one arrival; crews give children patches; the city holds a season-opening church service and air day. SOURCED, C3 §5g.
- Leadership advice from a station program: "minimize hierarchies, build team identity, structure daily routines." SOURCED, B7 §3.6.
- A pointer list of festivals and rites found in the files (for a later stage; not described here): Midwinter Day dinners and greetings (B7 §4.3, C8 §7.1a); sun-return days in Longyearbyen (8 March), Tromsø (21 January), Inuvik, Rjukan, Murmansk, Greenland towns, the Dolgan "Heiro" (C8 §1); Up Helly Aa (C8 §1.9); Toonik Tyme civic holiday (C8 §1.7); Hammerfest Light Plan and Longyearbyen's lights-off Advent walk (C8 §2); Reykjavík Winter Lights, Kiruna Snow Festival, Ruka, Yellowknife Snowking, Fairbanks ice art, Nuuk Snow Festival (C8 §2.7); 48-hour Antarctic film festival, 300 Club, polar plunges, Signy thin-ice race (B7 §4.4); Southern Games and interstation darts by video (C8 §7.1a); Dark Season Blues (C8 §5.3); McMurdo IceStock (B7 §8.2); crossing-the-line rites (B7 §2.3); submarine "halfway night" (B7 §2.2).

### 5.3 What fails
Alcohol
- "Alcohol contributes to sexual misconduct, but it is not the sole source"; "Drinking at the bars and 'pre-drinking in the rooms' are staples of community life at McMurdo"; senior administrators: "3 bars, 24 hours a day of light in the austral summer ... an incubator of trouble," "work for 8 hours and party and drink for 16"; yet incidents also occurred "in the galley, machine shop, field camps, transport vans," on dry vessels and in alcohol-free camps. SOURCED, B7 §5.1, https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/USAP-SAHPR-Report.pdf
- Bratsk 1959: "paydays were as a rule accompanied by mass drinking"; 822 people (3% of the workforce) charged with petty hooliganism in nine months. SOURCED, C1 §5I.
- Kwajalein bachelor quarters: complaints of excess alcohol and disruptive conduct; "regulated outdoor public intoxication"; a "strikes" disciplinary plan. SOURCED, B4 §1.
- When camps go dry: Suncor banned alcohol at all its camps in 2009 to "maintain a healthy atmosphere"; replacement recreation NOT FOUND (C8 §4.4). Argyle moved to a dry camp while "many workers consider the wet mess a fundamental place for socializing, unwinding." UNVERIFIED, C3 §5c. A McMurdo survivor said limits "will not eliminate the problem" and suggested restoring lost amenities instead (bowling alley, ceramic studio, greenhouse "cut due to budget reductions"). SOURCED, B7 §5.2.
- Evidence that any alcohol limit reduced misconduct: NOT FOUND (B7 §5.3).

Harassment and reporting
- 72% of women and 48% of men in the US program said harassment is a problem; fear that "if they are harassed or assaulted and report it, they will be the ones who will be going home"; "in a team of six, losing one person is a serious cost so 'people let things go'"; known offenders could return through another contractor; the isolated setting "blurs the boundaries between personal and professional life." SOURCED, B7 §5.1.
- ESA staff survey (2023, 2,751 respondents, staff and on-site contractors): 28% had experienced harassment, nearly a third witnessed it; bullying and mobbing 60% of cases. SOURCED, Bg §11.3, https://pipedot.org/6H49X
- Australian Antarctic reviews: 15% sexual harassment with almost 80% unreported (and other conflicting figures). UNVERIFIED, B7 §5.4.

Isolation and withdrawal
- Oil-sands camps: many isolate in rooms; loneliness common; one worker wants "a phone line or counsellor in camps." UNVERIFIED (Vice), B4 §8b.
- Dalton Highway operator: "Many (even most) Alaska work first-timers may find our camps to be too remote." SOURCED, C3 §2j.
- Kennecott: "it was almost like being in prison ... every spring, why, there would be 50 or 60 of them that quit"; miners "lived without seeing the outside air from the first of November to ... the end of March." SOURCED, C1 §6H.

SOURCED, B6 §1.3.

SOURCED, B6 §3.2, https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-03-26-antarctic-bases-are-hotbeds-of-stress-and-violence-space-stations-could-face-the-same-challenges

## 6. Humans and machines working together (C6 unless noted)

### 6.1 Who stays when work is automated
- ECT Rotterdam (1993, the first automated terminal): a crane team of three instead of eight, but "plenty of ECT personnel was still required on both the sea side and the land side"; staff cross-trained ("Anyone who operated the crane on day 1 could be on the ship as a radio operator on day 2"); "extensive training, including courses in logistics and social skills"; job rotation and teamwork. SOURCED, C6 §1.1, https://www.ect.nl/en/news/fast-forward/30-years-ago-ect-opened-very-first-automated-terminal-world
- Maasvlakte II: automated areas "fenced off from staff"; still "600 people": remote crane drivers, mechanics, lashing gangs, supervisors, troubleshooters; the port's goal is "getting the data in" rather than "getting the people out." SOURCED, C6 §1.2, https://www.marketplace.org/episode/a-dutch-port-demonstrates-how-automation-in-the-industry-could-work
- Shanghai Yangshan IV: "hardly any workers to be seen"; about 100 remote operators against "at least 700" otherwise; operators moved from 50 m cranes into a control room. SOURCED, C6 §1.5. One operator: "We are working three desks right now and we are babysitting 32 cranes" (Long Beach). UNVERIFIED, C6 §1.6.
- Maintenance: HHLA keeps 150 technicians in shifts for its automated terminal; next-generation Amazon sites "will require 30% more employees in reliability, maintenance, and engineering roles" (company estimate); a slight rise in maintenance work "unable to offset" longshore jobs lost (secondary). SOURCED/SECONDARY, C6 §1.9, §1.10, §3.8.
- Lights-out factories: humans intervene for "system design, programming, preventive maintenance"; CNC practice is "to always have a person in the vicinity"; Philips Drachten runs 128 robot arms with "several dozen workers per shift," arms "enclosed in glass cages"; "You have to have people around anyway." SOURCED, C6 §3.1-3.2, https://truthout.org/articles/skilled-work-without-the-worker/
- What machines could not do: Hyundai's robots cannot handle "soft pliable parts (hoses, wires, carpets, trim)"; "That sense of feel is very hard to teach automation"; Tesla: "excessive automation at Tesla was a mistake ... Humans are underrated." SOURCED, C6 §3.6-3.7.
- Unmanned installations: Oseberg H has "no facilities, not even a toilet"; maintenance campaigns once or twice a year, crew housed on a vessel linked by gangway; unmanned operation "could slow repairs." SOURCED, C6 §2.7, https://equinor.com/news/archive/october2018-oseberg-vestflanken2
- New roles: autonomous haulage created "central controllers" (set speed limits, adjust routes, step in on breakdowns) and "pit patrollers" ("eyes and ears" who clear obstacles and restart trucks); a former truck driver: "my job is much more ergonomic"; a patroller: "I actually feel safer." SOURCED, C6 §2.6, https://www.komatsu.eu/en/news/beyond-automation-the-next-generation-of-miners-and-their-machines
- Lighthouses: the last six Trinity House keepers left on 26 November 1998; lights are now monitored remotely; retired keepers keep an association and a Christmas buffet. SOURCED/SECONDARY, C6 §6.8, https://trinityhouse.co.uk/articles/a-fine-farewell
- Kemano: the community closed in 2000 when automation let powerhouse systems run from Kitimat with one-week rotating crews, saving about $13 million a year "in salaries, fuel, food, and costs for education and entertainment." SOURCED, C1 §5J.

### 6.2 How people relate to machines (practices and rites)
- Workers "relate to the robot as a social entity" and "rely on cues to understand the robot's actions"; being able to read the robot was "critical for workers to feel safe when near the robot." SOURCED (abstract), C6 §5.1 (Sauppé and Mutlu, CHI 2015).
- Hospital delivery robot: medical units with "low tolerance for interruptions" resisted it; post-partum units "integrated the robot into their workflow and social context." SOURCED (abstract), C6 §4.4 (Mutlu and Forlizzi, HRI 2008). A nurse threatened to kick it; a worker called it a "buddy." UNVERIFIED, C6 §4.4.
- Naming: of 30 committed Roomba users, 21 named the robot. UNVERIFIED, C6 §5.1. Combat stress "almost doubled the chance" that soldiers named their bomb-disposal robots; 80% of 746 surveyed "loved or liked working with robots"; operators moved through "anger, frustration and then a sense of loss" when robots were destroyed; "part of the family, almost"; homemade badges glued on robots. SOURCED, C6 §5.2, https://www.nbcnews.com/technolog/soldiers-3-robots-military-bots-get-awards-nicknames-funerals-4b11215746. A robot funeral with medals and a salute: UNVERIFIED.
- Mourning machines: Buddhist funerals for Aibo robot dogs at a temple in Chiba since 2015, about 800 honored by 2018; donor units honored before parts are taken; the priest: Aibo "acts as a mirror for human emotions"; Japanese rites also exist for needles and dolls. SOURCED, C6 §5.2 and conflict check C-1, https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/in-japan--a-buddhist-funeral-service-for-robot-dogs
- A rover's end as a team rite: Opportunity's final commands, a chosen last wake-up song, a team toast, handed-out "emotional support porgs," a tattoo; "these robotic missions, at their core, are wholly human." SOURCED, C6 §5.5, https://www.wral.com/amp/my-battery-is-low-and-it-s-getting-dark-end-of-opportunity-mission-a-loss-for-team/18197530/. Mars-rover team members are socialized to "see like a rover"; decisions wait until all say they are "happy." UNVERIFIED (book summary), C6 §5.5.
- Living on machine time: the Curiosity team worked "Mars time" for three months, often overnight; "People are glad to be going off Mars time." SOURCED, C6 §5.5.
- Robots in polar fieldwork: a crevasse-mapping robot rides ahead of tractor traverses; penguins showed a heart-rate rise about four times smaller with a rover than with a human; gliders are piloted remotely from California with three people on site. SOURCED, C6 §5.3.
- Care robots: only about 10% of 9,000 Japanese elder-care homes had any care robot (2019); a lifting robot was abandoned within days; residents grew attached to a robot seal (one refused meals or bed without it); a dancing robot bored residents within weeks; "robots tend to end up creating more work for caregivers" (moving, maintenance, cleaning, booting, monitoring, storage) and displaced social tasks. SOURCED, C6 §4.2, https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/01/09/1065135/japan-automating-eldercare-robots/
- Robot hotel: 243 robots; staff had to be increased to handle complaints; over half the robots were cut in 2019; an employee: "It's easier now that we're not being frequently called by guests to help with problems with the robots." SOURCED, C6 §4.1, https://www.raconteur.net/technology/robot-hotel-ai
- Remote pilots: a Tokyo café where people "who have difficulty going out" operate robots from home; "Our robots are not AI"; a pilot had been "very-very lonely" and found "a ray of hope." SOURCED, C6 §4.5, https://dawn2021.orylab.com/en/
- Street robots: people took pictures of delivery robots or "messed with" them; robots "stopped often and started seemingly at random, confusing people"; no vandalism or collisions observed. SOURCED, C6 §4.8.
- Space station robots: free-flyers could take over air sampling, sound surveys and camera positioning, saving "12+ hr/month"; crew spend "up to 1 hr/day looking for equipment"; crew maintenance took 260 hours over two increments; the robot arm is "not strong enough to injure the crew" and crew can pull a perched robot off a handrail. SOURCED, C6 §5.4, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240014605/downloads/iisf_andresMoraVargas_2024.pdf
- ISS time budget: 11 work hours and 13 other hours a day; of 6.5 task hours, research 38%, cargo traffic 25%, maintenance 13%, medical 7%, training 7%, spacewalks 5%, routine operations (meetings, stowage, audits of food and water) 4%; weekend housekeeping; all crew sleep at the same time. SOURCED, C6 §6.6, https://oig.nasa.gov/docs/IG-13-019.pdf
- A year-long isolation run included "three tests of joint work with an anthropomorphic robot." SOURCED, Bg §12.3, https://tass.com/science/1872341

### 6.3 Living inside automated infrastructure
- Ships with periodically unattended machinery spaces: engine alarms wired "to the engineers' public rooms and to each of the engineers' cabins through a selector switch"; alarms escalate if not answered "within a limited time"; a voice link from bridge and control room to engineers' accommodation; no maximum unattended period in the text read. SOURCED, C6 §6.4, https://admin.dma.dk/Media/637743811611429569/DII1E-01102002.pdf. A coastal ship with three engineers and an electrician: the duty engineer on 24-hour call, the chief as back-up 24/7; a fatigued engineer closed the wrong cooler. SOURCED, C6 §6.4, https://chirp.co.uk/report/fatigue/
- Nuclear control rooms: long low-task-load periods; operators found sleeping, playing video games and leaving the controls unattended led to a shutdown order (Peach Bottom 1987) and hour limits of 16 in 24 and 60 a week; sleeping contract guards were filmed in 2007 and the whistleblower was suspended. SOURCED/SECONDARY, C6 §6.7, https://blog.ucs.org/?p=15955
- Data centers: 53% of operators had trouble finding qualified staff (2022), 42% lost staff to poaching; a sealed seabed data center with no humans had a server failure rate one-eighth of a land control group. SOURCED, C6 §6.5, https://www.networkworld.com/article/3674127/survey-outages-staffing-challenge-data-centers.html and https://www.theregister.com/2020/09/14/natick_back/
- Mir 1997: after a collision a module was sealed and others powered down; one astronaut "frequently felt as though the crew was spending all its time trying to stay alive." SOURCED/UNVERIFIED, C6 §6.6.

### 6.4 Rules and labor arrangements around automation
- Safety: robot-related deaths at work in the US, 41 over 1992-2017; 78% "a robot striking the worker while moving under its own power"; "Many of these striking incidents occurred while maintenance was being performed on a robot"; traditional robots "work in robotic cells and cages away from human workers." SOURCED, C6 §7.8, https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/230667 and https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/robotics/about/index.html
- Collision limits: ISO 10218 (2025) now includes the biomechanical force and pressure limits for human contact; researchers call most of those limits "preliminary." SOURCED, C6 §7.7.
- European Parliament (2017): asked for study of compulsory insurance, compensation funds, a robot register and "electronic persons" for the most autonomous robots; its charter: "obvious opt-out mechanisms (kill switches)," users may use a robot "without risk or fear of physical or psychological harm," "deactivation of video monitors during intimate procedures," no modification of robots into weapons. SOURCED, C6 §7.2. A 2018 open letter of experts opposed "electronic personality." SOURCED, C6 §7.3.
- EU AI Act: bans systems that infer workers' emotions at work (fatigue detection for safety is outside the ban); workplace AI is high-risk, and employers must inform workers' representatives beforehand. SOURCED/SECONDARY, C6 §7.4.
- Collective agreements: UPS must give the Teamsters six months' notice and bargain the effects of drones and driverless vehicles; writers' and actors' 2023 contracts limit AI use and require consent and pay; German works councils co-decide on any device "suitable for monitoring" staff; Sweden's employer-funded job security councils (about 0.3% of payroll) placed 88% of laid-off workers in new jobs (2016). SOURCED, C6 §7.6.
- Port deals: Rotterdam guaranteed full-time dockworkers' jobs to 2020; "Nobody can be sacked because of automation"; Singapore moved 500 of 600 port workers to the new terminal with none laid off, retraining 20% within a year; the US East Coast contract (2025) promised "comprehensive protections against automation" (clause text NOT FOUND). SOURCED/SECONDARY, C6 §1.3, §1.8, §1.11.
- Robot taxes: South Korea's cut to automation tax credits (2018) was followed by a "sharp decline in automation investments" and affected firms hired more workers. SOURCED (abstract), C6 §7.5.
- Workforce effects: Suncor's driverless trucks cut about 500 roles and added about 100 (net 400), with retraining agreed with the union; BHP planned to cut up to about 10% of a mine's headcount after more driverless trucks. SOURCED, C6 §2.5, §2.2.

**Chains:**
- 1st: robots added to a care home or hotel → 2nd: staff spend time moving, charging, fixing and rescuing guests from the robots → 3rd: staff numbers rise or robots are withdrawn (Henn-na 2019; care-home robots abandoned). SOURCED, C6 §4.1-4.2.
- 1st: work moves from cabs and plant floors into control rooms → 2nd: one person runs several machines and site jobs move to cities (Perth, Luleå, Kitimat) → 3rd: the on-site town can shrink to rotating crews (Kemano 2000; Cabramurra 2018). SOURCED, C6 §2, C1 §5J.
- 1st: maintenance of moving robots → 2nd: most US robot deaths happened during maintenance → 3rd: standard practice keeps traditional robots in cages and fenced zones (Maasvlakte II "fenced off from staff"). SOURCED, C6 §1.2, §7.8.

## 7. Archive and record-keeping communities (B5 unless noted)

- Who keeps records, by kind:
  - Family custodians by oath: in Timbuktu "a family member [must] 'swear publicly that he will protect the library for as long as he lives'"; collections move with the custodian across generations; families honor the manuscripts with yearly readings. SOURCED, B5 §1.3, https://newrepublic.com/article/112898/timbuktu-librarians-duped-al-qaeda-save-books
  - Copyists: professional paid scribes, unpaid copyists working for friends or themselves, and students copying for teachers; women "a significant minority" of named copyists and owners; scribal families over generations. SOURCED, B5 §1.4, https://hmml.org/stories/series-scribes-a-scribble-of-scribes/
  - Monastic keepers: Mount Athos (about 1,750-1,830 Orthodox men, monks and workers; younger, university-educated monks catalog and restore manuscripts in work "expected to take decades"); Saint Catherine's (25 monks, one librarian). SOURCED, B5 §3.2-3.3.
  - Staff archives: NARA's National Personnel Records Center, "824 federal employees and contractors," physical custodian of "over 4 million cubic feet," adding second and third shifts and weekends to clear a backlog. SOURCED, B5 §5.2 (Levins testimony). Arolsen Archives, about 200-220 employees in tracing, online archive, reading room, exhibition and restoration teams, with tens of thousands of online volunteer transcribers. SOURCED, B5 §5.4. Vatican archive and library together about 150 (roles: archivists, scriptores, secretaries, lab technicians, reading-room assistants). SOURCED/UNVERIFIED, B5 §5.3. Granite Mountain vault "50-plus" (2010). SOURCED, B3 §7.3. Internet Archive 122 employees (2021) and "100 paid operators" scanning worldwide (2019). SOURCED (Wikipedia), B5 §4.3. British Library at Boston Spa, 550 staff. SOURCED, B5 §4.4.
  - Unstaffed deep stores: the Svalbard seed vault has "no permanent staff on-site"; a public-works agency is keyholder and accompanies staff "at all work inside"; staff fly in for each opening (three in 2022); boxes are never opened, only labeled, shelved and photographed for depositors; it was "built to run without human help" until meltwater in 2016-17 led to "watching the seed vault 24 hours a day." SOURCED, B5 §6.1, https://www.croptrust.org/blog/svalbard-a-day-in-the-life/. The Arctic World Archive has no on-site staff; reels are fetched by hand. SOURCED, B5 §6.2.
- How they work:
  - Timbuktu stores used tin trunks or low tables (koma) that "crush the manuscripts, prevent air from circulating"; the rooms faced dust, light and wide climate swings. SOURCED, B5 §1.3, https://publication.codesria.org/index.php/pub/catalog/download/141/1237/4155?inline=1
  - The rescue association's workshop of about 20 staff made made-to-measure acid-free boxes at three levels; "a dozen professional scanners"; climate-adjusted storage in Bamako; aim: return collections to the families. SOURCED, B5 §1.6, https://artsandculture.google.com/story/cwXxzf6uEb1aJw
  - Granite Mountain evaluators check film at counters "for nicks, cracks and exposure"; flawed film is re-filmed. SOURCED, B5 §5.1.
  - Dead Sea Scrolls conservation: "all treatments must be reversible"; every fragment gets a condition description, photography, mapping and a database entry; a scroll displayed three months rests at least a year. SOURCED, B5 §2.4, https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/learn-about-the-scrolls/conservation
  - Reconciling copies: software proposed about 1,000 joins of scattered Cairo Geniza fragments in months, each verified by a scholar; WorldCat merges are reversible and "metadata quality staff" can recover merged records (UNVERIFIED). SOURCED/UNVERIFIED, B5 §2.1, §4.5. B5's pattern note: the reconcilers are "people with narrow roles ... plus hashing-based automatic comparison."
- What they own and use: a Timbuktu family library: "computers, scanners, digital cameras and tools to clean and restore," 15 employees, about 5,000 visitors a year (2009); a small family's 2,700 manuscripts in "three metal trunks," library door that "doesn't lock." SOURCED, B5 §S1, https://theworld.org/stories/2009-12-28/timbuktu-preserves-historic-manuscripts. Saint Catherine's: individual stainless-steel boxes with oxygen starvation for 2,187 manuscripts. SOURCED (Wikipedia), B5 §3.3.
- Where the work happens below ground:
  - NARA's Lee's Summit cave center: about 70 F year-round, "green storage" cutting energy about 30%; staff describe a poorly lit one-car road between rock pillars, dust and rock falling from the ceiling, dust fouling fax machines and keyboards, winter commutes in the dark. SOURCED, B5 §5.2 (NARA Prologue blog).
  - OPM's Boyers mine: "About 600 federal employees," "about 26,000 file cabinets," "practically like its own fully contained city, with golf carts"; "no longer naturally cool," fitted with modern HVAC. SOURCED, B3 §7.4, https://www.opm.gov/news/secrets-of-opm/200-feet-underground/
  - A commercial canyon vault: "about 10 employees, including guards and couriers"; 60 F and about 28% humidity; air recirculated every six hours; four power sources; storage policy bans anything that might burn. SOURCED, B5 §5.1, https://www.deseret.com/2003/11/17/19796252/storage-vault-keeps-its-contents-safe
  - British Library stores at 14% oxygen, 14 C, 55% humidity behind an airlock; staff rely on automated retrieval. SOURCED, B5 §4.4, §5.5.
- What failed: a 2016 audit of NARA's underground cold store found no monitoring of who entered, no updated key log, a "keyed solid" door that budged, computer credentials taped to the machine, backups on a thumb drive, after an earlier theft by a former employee. SOURCED, B5 §S3, https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/oig-reports/audit-report-16-03.pdf. The 1973 St. Louis fire destroyed about a third of 52 million military files. SOURCED, B5 §5.2. Timbuktu: humidity and tightly packed boxes in Bamako; a training course ended when the UN mission withdrew. SOURCED, B5 §1.6. Haeinsa's 1970s modern depository grew mildew and the blocks went back to the old halls. SOURCED, B5 §3.1.

**Chain:** 1st: records stored "where they generally don't leave" and with visitors limited because "dust and fibers could contaminate the records" (Granite Mountain) → 2nd: access shifts to copies (digitization since 1999; Family History Centers) → 3rd: the public showcase is a virtual tour. SOURCED, B3 §7.3.

## 8. Governance in a neutral or multinational facility (B6 unless noted)

- Who is in charge:
  - CERN: a Council of two delegates per member state, one vote each; a Director-General appointed by a two-thirds majority, usually for five years; staff duties "exclusively international in character," and staff "shall not seek or receive instructions from any government." SOURCED, B6 §1.1, https://council.web.cern.ch/en/content/convention-establishment-european-organization-nuclear-research. A tripartite forum of the staff association, management and member states prepares decisions on employment conditions. SOURCED, B6 §1.1.
  - ISS: the Commander "is the highest authority among the ISS crewmembers on-orbit," maintains order and crew health, may use "any reasonable and necessary means," and is "solely accountable, to the Flight Director"; a multilateral panel picks commanders of any partner nationality; criminal jurisdiction follows nationality. SOURCED, B6 §2, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/1214.403 and https://www.jaxa.jp/library/space_law/chapter_3/3-2-2-9/3-2-2-922_e.html
  - Antarctic stations: leaders and persons in charge issue "lawful directions"; Neumayer's doctor is also station leader and the engineer is deputy; at Troll the station leader is "picked from among those team members" (UNVERIFIED); "With limited external oversight, leadership structures and informal hierarchies take on an outsized influence." SOURCED/UNVERIFIED, B6 §3.1-3.2.
  - Svalbard: the Governor is the government's highest representative, county governor and chief of police (48 person-years of staff); the elected Longyearbyen council (15 members) runs schools, kindergartens, utilities and culture; since 2023 voters "must have previously resided in mainland Norway for at least three years," which removed foreign residents' municipal vote. SOURCED, B6 §4.
  - Kwajalein: an Army garrison commander with quarterly town halls, a Community Relations Council and a Joint Labor Review Board for the Marshallese workforce. SOURCED, B4 §1.
  - Company-town authority: Boulder City's appointed manager held "legal, moral and judicial authority," screened businesses, banned drinking and gambling, and searched cars; the town "had never held an election" before 1951. SOURCED, C1 §1A. Los Alamos had an elected town council advising the army administration from 1943. SOURCED, C1 §2B.
- Posting and nationality rules:
  - CERN Staff Rule: the Director-General "shall strive to ensure as fair a distribution as possible of nationals ... and of genders," subject to competence; "Conscious Hiring" is "not: Quotas"; the staff association says "No quota." Return ratios in 2024 ranged from 2.72 (France) to 0.30 (Germany); some states tied support for the next collider to better returns. SOURCED, B6 §1.2.
  - ESA: recruitment on qualifications "taking into account an adequate distribution of posts among nationals"; a separate industrial "geo-return" rule with a floor never below 0.8, blamed for restricting subcontractors and causing delays. SOURCED, B6 §1.5, §8.3.
  - ITER: posts distributed "in relation to their contributions"; terms up to five years; members nominate candidates; 1,850 applications for 45 external posts left little room to rebalance. SOURCED, B6 §1.7, §8.4.
  - UN Secretariat: only 3,611 of 36,574 staff posts (DERIVED about 9.9%) fall under geographic "desirable ranges"; unrepresented states' applicants are selected at comparable rates but form the smallest pool. SOURCED, B6 §8.1, https://unhabitat.org/sites/default/files/2021/01/a_75_591-en.pdf
  - Contractor eligibility at Pituffik: bidders must be majority-owned by Danish or Greenlandic individuals and based in Greenland. SOURCED, B4 §3b.
  - Ascension and Svalbard: residence tied to a contract or to means of support. SOURCED, B4 §2, §5.
- Conduct codes:
  - ISS: "a harmonious and cohesive relationship among the ISS crewmembers ... which duly takes into account the international and multicultural nature of the crew"; discipline "administrative in nature" and may "affect flight assignments"; medical information private. SOURCED, B6 §2.
  - Australian Antarctic code: "treat others with consideration, courtesy, respect, fairness and tolerance, without patronage or favouritism or regard to sex, race, nationality"; "individual and community behaviors are of legitimate concern" in "self-contained communities." SOURCED, B6 §3.2.
  - Ny-Ålesund station rules: a Code of Conduct, a Sexual Misconduct Policy, a Drug and Alcohol Policy; a 20 km radio-quiet zone with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth in the village. SOURCED, B4 §4.
  - Kwajalein quarters rules (pets, flames, smoking, quiet hours, public intoxication, a "strikes" plan). SOURCED, B4 §1.
  - Alpine huts: written house rules, a keeper who "implements domiciliary right," and removal for rule-breakers. SOURCED, C3 §6a.
- Ombuds and mediation:
  - CERN Ombud (since 2010): "zero-barrier, informal, impartial and confidential"; about 100 visitors a year; most outcomes through advice or coaching; people cite "a fear of disclosure or retaliation"; most issues between supervisors and subordinates; "Sometimes the fact of belonging to the same nationality reinforces common behavior"; visiting users have "no clear framework of accountability." SOURCED, B6 §1.3.
  - ESA: Conflict Navigators and Mediators first, then a formal report to the Head of HR, an Advisory Board, and a six-judge Administrative Tribunal, "free of charge," final and binding; no office called an ombuds. SOURCED, Bg §11.1-11.2, https://www.esa.int/About_Us/Corporate_news/Administrative_Tribunal
  - Kwajalein: an Ombudsman post created after the 2024 ferry-crew strike. SOURCED, B4 §1.
  - IETF: an Ombudsteam for harassment; decisions by "rough consensus," "always appealable." SOURCED, B6 §6.2.
  - London Internet Exchange: a voluntary, non-binding mediation service; a senior staff member mediates; either party may withdraw; "no formal written, audio or video records"; referral onward if needed; purpose "to avoid the referral of complaints to statutory regulators." SOURCED, B6 §6.1, https://www.linx.net/governance/dispute-resolution-procedure
  - Swiss good offices distinguish "facilitation" (bringing parties to the table) from "mediation" (taking part in finding solutions), usually "behind the scenes"; Quaker Houses host "off-the-record conversations." SOURCED, B6 §7.3.
- Multinational social life: Concordia's crew split into language and nationality subgroups (above); Russian mission controllers reported more language flexibility and Americans higher work pressure; few flight controllers had strategies for colleagues whose first language was not English. SOURCED, B6 §2, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19522363. Barentsburg residents (before 2022) relied on personal ties to Longyearbyen (20 of 21) and joint preparedness training. SOURCED, B6 §4. Antarctic inspection teams should include someone who speaks the station staff's language, or a translator. SOURCED, B6 §3.

**Chains:**
- 1st: a balance rule ranked below competence → 2nd: imbalances persist ("this imbalance has continued to grow over the past 20 years," CERN staff association) → 3rd: funders tie support to returns. SOURCED, B6 §8.5.
- 1st: an informal, confidential channel exists (CERN Ombud) → 2nd: people use it mostly for advice rather than intervention, citing fear of retaliation → 3rd: "At any moment that a formal complaint is lodged, the Ombud is no longer involved." SOURCED, B6 §1.3.

## 12. Unresolved conflicts among the files that matter to the design

1. **Galley staffing at McMurdo:** 28 cooks + 35 attendants for about 1,200 (2007) vs "30 cooks" (2014); different years, attendants not counted in 2014 (Bg §1.1).
2. **McMurdo fire department:** about 20 summer (2017, S) vs 22 summer / 10 winter (B1, U) vs 46 total with 12 in winter (U) vs about 55 total (U) (Bg F3.2).
