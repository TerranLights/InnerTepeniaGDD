## 2.1 Hammerfest — the 2007 Light Plan (primary municipal document)
Source: "Veileder for belysning og lysskilt" (guideline for lighting and illuminated signs), municipality of Hammerfest, https://hammerfest.kommune.no/_f/p1/i165e540c-3274-4d80-98fa-0694251a11ec/veileder-for-belysning-og-lysskilt.pdf, read locally by pdftotext. Norwegian; my translation. SOURCED.
- Legal basis: based on "Hammerfest Lysplan" adopted by the municipal council **11 May 2007**, plus the Planning and Building Act (sections 107 and 74 no. 2, as the document cites them); the guideline is to be evaluated regularly.
- Foreword: Hammerfest "was the first town in Europe with electric street light"; "the first town in Norway to make a comprehensive light plan where **3,500 light points are controlled via the internet**"; "dynamic light": lights dimmed up and down "according to natural light conditions, traffic conditions or festivals and New Year's Eve"; project slogan "a city of light" (*en by av lys*); previously lighting meant "to get enough light", now aesthetics and the difference between "right" and "wrong" lighting.
- Stated purposes (list in section 1.4): a pride-presenting light design; **"improve quality of life for residents in the dark season (mørketiden)"**; traffic safety and less vandalism; control to save electricity; profile Hammerfest as an "Energy town"; increase tourism appeal.
- Rules: new areas, larger buildings, streets, shared outdoor spaces and renovations must document lighting (calculations in Relux/Dialux, light sources, positions, color temperature in kelvin, color rendering Ra, technical function, maintenance and operation plan). Private small houses: unshielded sources over 100 lux at 3 m can be required to be shielded; no documentation required. Illuminated signs: not more than 25 cd/m2 for signs meant to be visible up to 1 km, 50 cd/m2 legible to 1 km, 70 cd/m2 legible to 2 km; letters not above 50 cm; no white surfaces except logo and text. Facade lighting must not shine up into the air ("light pollution") and ground-set facade lights must not obstruct **snow clearing**. Special effects (pavement ads, projected laser light, flashing) allowed only for events of short duration such as Christmas lighting and festivals; permanent ones need application. Light poles may be designed so festival banners can be hung.
- Table of target levels (horizontal plane, average): national road 94 and main streets 5-10 lux; side streets in center 3-5 lux; paths 1-3 lux; squares 1-5 lux; bus stop/terminal 5-10 lux; pedestrian crossings 20-40 lux (not dynamic); residential 3-5 lux; parks 1 lux; cemetery 1 lux; landmarks 3-10 cd/m2; facades under 3 cd/m2; shop windows under 35 cd/m2; color temperatures mostly 3000-6000 K (harbor, promenades 2400-7000 K). Fixtures to be "adapted to the climate in Hammerfest"; street lights to use reflector technology "to avoid light pollution"; Ra above 70; lamp life at least 10,000 hours, at least 75 lm/W; "orange sodium gas light may only be used in defined areas".
- The guide includes a caption: "A copy of the lanterns used during the pioneer work in 1891." No cost, staff count or complaint record: NOT FOUND.
- **1891 claim: conflict between sources.** The municipality's own foreword says first town in Europe; Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammerfest) says "the first urban settlement in Northern Europe to get electrical street lights"; two merchants reportedly brought the technology after a Paris fair (Wikipedia). ## 4.1 Svalbard — the monthly quota (primary: the Governor)
- **SOURCED** (Governor of Svalbard, https://www.sysselmesteren.no/en/alcohol/): persons entered in the Population Register for Svalbard (over the legal age) may buy a monthly quota: "up to 2 bottles of spirits (or up to 4 bottles of strong wine)", "up to 0.5 bottle of fortified wine (alternatively one bottle every two months)", "up to 24 cans or half bottles of beer", and "light wine for reasonable consumption"; at Nordpolet AS one must show proof of residence and identification; "Nordpolet is responsible for checking that the monthly quota is met"; students and seasonal workers can apply for a special permit; stays over 30 days need a special permit from the Governor with a quota by length of stay; stays of 24 hours to 30 days can qualify on presentation of a scheduled flight ticket.
