## 1.4 Rjukan, Telemark — Sun Day, Solfest, the sun mirror, the cable car; and Viganella (neighbor)
- **Sun Day and Solfest. SOURCED** (Visit Rjukan https://visitrjukan.com/en/things-to-do/the-sun-mirror/): Sun Day 12 March ("when the sun shines again on Fabrikkbrua in the west of the city"); Solfest "a firm tradition in Rjukan since 1925"; next Solfest Saturday 24 April 2027 (page text). The town is in shade "from mid-October until mid-March" per same page. **Conflict:** other sources say shade for "five" or "six" months (National Geographic: five winter months; ABC: about six months); Gaustaview says "roughly September to March".
- **Mirror design. SOURCED** (Visit Rjukan): three computer-controlled mirrors at 742 m above sea level, each 17 m2; reflected light covers an elliptical area of about 600 m2 on the square; "solar effect 80-100%"; unveiled 2013. **Conflict on lit area:** National Geographic (pre-opening, 2013) said about 200 m2; Visit Rjukan says 600 m2. The 2011 design article (Teknisk Ukeblad, https://www.tu.no/artikler/solspeilet-blir-virkelighet/250158) described one 100 m2 mirror weighing 14 tonnes: the built design differs from the 2011 plan.
- **Mechanism. SOURCED** (TU 2011): steel-tube support column, sun screen, concrete foundation, rotating ring, horizontal and vertical axes; washing system ("the mirror must be kept clean for optimal effect"); must withstand wind and snow; powered by solar panels and simple wind turbines; feasibility study done 2003. Search summaries (UNVERIFIED): re-adjusts every 10 seconds.
- **Cost and funding. SOURCED:** 5 million NOK (Wikipedia Rjukan; ABC: "5 million NOK (about $778,000) at the time"; National Geographic: "$847,000 (5 million Norwegian Krones). The town and several Norwegian companies are funding it." TU 2011: two of the five million were to be sought from private actors). The dollar figures differ because of exchange rates; the NOK figure is stable. Search-summary mention of Hydro as main sponsor: UNVERIFIED.
- **Ownership/operation/staffing. NOT FOUND** (died at sources). Krossobanen page (https://www.krossobanen.no/en/solspeilet) says "Tinn municipality constructed the sun mirror, consisting of three heliostats at 742 meters above sea level" and lists Krossobanen AS as the contact entity, but does not say who owns or maintains it. Search-summary line "Tinn Kommune lanserer Solspeilet i ny teknologisk utgave (november 2023)" (Visit Norway timeline): UNVERIFIED, no cost found. Number of people who maintain it: NOT FOUND.
- **People. SOURCED:** idea proposed 1913 by Oscar Kittelsen and Sam Eyde (Krossobanen page); revived by artist Martin Andersen in 2005; project leader (2011) Øystein Haugan, former culture director; light expert Jonny Nersveen tested the effect (TU 2011). Over a thousand people signed a petition against (TU 2011, population given as about 3,600). Local use, one resident quoted by ABC: "I prefer the real thing but it's a good substitute if you have no other choice" and "It really makes a difference in our cold, dark winters."
- **Rjukan population** 3,012 (2025) (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rjukan).
- **Cable car. PARTLY SOURCED:** Krossobanen 1928 purpose "to give the people of Rjukan access to sunlight in winter" appears only in a search summary (UNVERIFIED; the Krossobanen page read does not state it).
- **Viganella, Piedmont (neighbor, the other sun mirror). SOURCED** (Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viganella): shaded 83 days a year, 11 November to 2 February; mirror of 14 steel sheets, 8 m wide and 5 m high; cost "€100,000, or approximately €540 per resident"; a heliostat that tracks the sun so light "always reflects onto the town square"; a former mayor: "It comes from a desire to let people socialize in winter when the town shuts down due to the cold and the dark." Funders (province, Fondazione Cariplo, municipality) and installation date December 2006 from search summaries only (UNVERIFIED). Residents: 174 in 2011 (table) / 204 in 2010 (infobox).

## 1.9 Lerwick, Shetland — Up Helly Aa (verified from the official press pack)
Source: Up Helly Aa Information Pack 2026 (https://www.uphellyaa.org/files/up-helly-aa-press-pack-jan-2026.pdf), read in full via pdftotext.
- **Date:** "always held on the last Tuesday in January"; the next day a public holiday "for many".
- **Participation. SOURCED:** "participants must have been a resident in Shetland for five years before they can take part in a squad and the procession." Visitors are welcome as onlookers and to halls "providing they can get their hands on much sought-after invites." Gender: "For the first time in 2023, Up Helly Aa guizers were able to manage their squads ... with no gender restrictions"; in 2024 the Jarl was joined by one daughter and three nieces, "the first occasion where there had been female participation in the Jarl Squad in Lerwick."
- **Organization. SOURCED:** voluntary; "a committee of 17 members elected by the guizers, with a new member being elected every year"; "To become Guizer Jarl ... a member must serve 16 years on the committee" (the 2026 pack). **Conflict:** Wikipedia list says 15 years; the 2026 Jarl's own interview says he joined the committee in 2008, served 18 years "with two additional years added due to the postponement ... Covid" and retires after 19 years as Chief Marshal in 2027. Use the pack's 16.
- **Scale. SOURCED:** "1000+ torches" built by volunteers, work starts "at the end of October, on at least four nights a week"; galley and Jarl Squad secret until the morning of the day; 2026: Jarl Squad plus "the other forty six squads" in the evening torch procession (the Jarl's interview says "45 other squads" - the pack contradicts itself, 46 vs 45); torches lit on a firework signal at 19:30; eleven halls; morning parade from 08:15, Jarl Squad visits schools, hospitals, eventide (care) homes and the museum.
- **Funding. SOURCED:** "a non-profit association ... funded by the generous donations from local businesses and the guizers"; committee members collect donations from local businesses with a "collection sheet" in November (Jarl's interview).
- **Junior festival. SOURCED:** Junior Jarl elected by secondary one and two pupils at Lerwick schools; he picks a squad of fifteen from secondary two; the junior procession is for primary seven to secondary two.
- **History. SOURCED:** tar-barrel Yule and New Year festivities from about 1840; named Up Helly Aa and made a January event with guizing and a torchlight procession from about 1870; galley late 1880s; Guizer Jarl 1906; Jarl Squad every year after the First World War; "In the winter of 1931-32 there was an unsuccessful move to cancel the festival because of the dire economic situation"; resumed 1949 after the war. 2026 Jarl Lynden Nicolson works for Scottish Water by day.
- **Chief Marshal** oversees the festival each year and is the Jarl-to-be's predecessor role (the pack: Jarl "taking on his next designated role as Chief Marshal for 2027").
- Note for facts: Up Helly Aa is a late-January fire festival, not a sun-return event in its own account (the pack does not describe it as one); it is placed here as a dark-season communal festival.

# SECTION 2 — Light as civic infrastructure and art

## 2.1 Hammerfest — the 2007 Light Plan (primary municipal document)
Source: "Veileder for belysning og lysskilt" (guideline for lighting and illuminated signs), municipality of Hammerfest, https://hammerfest.kommune.no/_f/p1/i165e540c-3274-4d80-98fa-0694251a11ec/veileder-for-belysning-og-lysskilt.pdf, read locally by pdftotext. Norwegian; my translation. SOURCED.
- Legal basis: based on "Hammerfest Lysplan" adopted by the municipal council **11 May 2007**, plus the Planning and Building Act (sections 107 and 74 no. 2, as the document cites them); the guideline is to be evaluated regularly.
- Foreword: Hammerfest "was the first town in Europe with electric street light"; "the first town in Norway to make a comprehensive light plan where **3,500 light points are controlled via the internet**"; "dynamic light": lights dimmed up and down "according to natural light conditions, traffic conditions or festivals and New Year's Eve"; project slogan "a city of light" (*en by av lys*); previously lighting meant "to get enough light", now aesthetics and the difference between "right" and "wrong" lighting.
- Stated purposes (list in section 1.4): a pride-presenting light design; **"improve quality of life for residents in the dark season (mørketiden)"**; traffic safety and less vandalism; control to save electricity; profile Hammerfest as an "Energy town"; increase tourism appeal.
- Rules: new areas, larger buildings, streets, shared outdoor spaces and renovations must document lighting (calculations in Relux/Dialux, light sources, positions, color temperature in kelvin, color rendering Ra, technical function, maintenance and operation plan). Private small houses: unshielded sources over 100 lux at 3 m can be required to be shielded; no documentation required. Illuminated signs: not more than 25 cd/m2 for signs meant to be visible up to 1 km, 50 cd/m2 legible to 1 km, 70 cd/m2 legible to 2 km; letters not above 50 cm; no white surfaces except logo and text. Facade lighting must not shine up into the air ("light pollution") and ground-set facade lights must not obstruct **snow clearing**. Special effects (pavement ads, projected laser light, flashing) allowed only for events of short duration such as Christmas lighting and festivals; permanent ones need application. Light poles may be designed so festival banners can be hung.
- Table of target levels (horizontal plane, average): national road 94 and main streets 5-10 lux; side streets in center 3-5 lux; paths 1-3 lux; squares 1-5 lux; bus stop/terminal 5-10 lux; pedestrian crossings 20-40 lux (not dynamic); residential 3-5 lux; parks 1 lux; cemetery 1 lux; landmarks 3-10 cd/m2; facades under 3 cd/m2; shop windows under 35 cd/m2; color temperatures mostly 3000-6000 K (harbor, promenades 2400-7000 K). Fixtures to be "adapted to the climate in Hammerfest"; street lights to use reflector technology "to avoid light pollution"; Ra above 70; lamp life at least 10,000 hours, at least 75 lm/W; "orange sodium gas light may only be used in defined areas".
- The guide includes a caption: "A copy of the lanterns used during the pioneer work in 1891." No cost, staff count or complaint record: NOT FOUND.
- **1891 claim: conflict between sources.** The municipality's own foreword says first town in Europe; Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammerfest) says "the first urban settlement in Northern Europe to get electrical street lights"; two merchants reportedly brought the technology after a Paris fair (Wikipedia). ## F.3 Unresolved conflicts
1. Longyearbyen population: about 2,100 (Wikipedia, UNIS page) vs about 2,500 with roughly 30% foreign (SALTO case study).
2. Solfestuka weekday labels: council page prints "Thursday 6 to Saturday 15 March" but 6 March 2026 is a Friday (DERIVED); Polar Journal: Sunday 8 March.
3. Rjukan shade period: mid-October to mid-March (Visit Rjukan) vs five months (National Geographic) vs six (ABC) vs roughly September to March (Gaustaview). Lit area: 200 m2 (National Geographic 2013 pre-opening) vs 600 m2 (Visit Rjukan). Design: 100 m2 single mirror (TU 2011) vs three mirrors of 17 m2 (built).
4. Up Helly Aa committee service: 16 years (2026 pack) vs 15 (Wikipedia list); squads: 46 other squads (route text) vs 45 other squads (Jarl interview) in the same pack.
5. Hammerfest 1891: "first in Europe" (municipal foreword) vs "first in Northern Europe" (Wikipedia).
6. Abu Simbel: 21 vs 22 February; relocation described as "raised 300 m" (NASA page) vs "reassembled 60 m from its original site" (The National).
7. Svalbard alcohol: Vinepair says unlimited wine; the Governor says "light wine for reasonable consumption"; brewing law change dated 2014 (Life in Norway) vs 1920s ban petitioned for about five and a half years, brewery opening August 2015 (Vinepair).
8. Rothera winter population: 22 (CSM) vs 17 (NPR summary). WIFFA origin station: McMurdo and Scott (AAD) vs South Pole (Polar Journal). Tristan ship visits: 8, 9, "around 10".
9. NSF artists program: B1's finding aid says it ended in 2019 and was re-envisioned as Polar STEAM in 2022; NSF page presents AAW as managed within Polar STEAM. B1 AAWP size ("one or two" vs "2 to 4") remains open.
10. Antarctic Sun: Wikipedia says founded 1996 and does not mention the McMurdo News/Sometimez lineage that search summaries give.
11. Vernadsky text: "newcomers, 10 people, were the majority of the 29-person expedition" does not add up (as reported by the fetch tool).
