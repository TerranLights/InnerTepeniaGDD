## 3. Per-site possibilities and notes

### Pergamino (Hurd Peninsula, Livingston Island; 62°39'47"S 60°23'17"W; solar UTC−4)
- **Primary: Ushuaia, 984 km.** Other Fuegian options: Puerto Williams 954 km, Punta Arenas 1,222 km.
- **The Río de la Plata (Montevideo, 3,100 km; Punta del Este 3,103; Buenos Aires 3,123)** is "Atlantic-side" and zone-aligned, but **no source names Montevideo as an Antarctic gateway**, and Wikipedia's gateway-cities article lists only Punta Arenas, Ushuaia, Cape Town, Hobart and Christchurch. A great-circle line from Montevideo passes through or beside the Falkland Islands; real sailing deviates around them.
- Falklands (Stanley, 1,229 km); Argentina's Patagonian Atlantic coast (Río Gallegos 1,335 km; Comodoro Rivadavia 1,921 km).
- **Montevideo is a real logistics node, not a cruise gateway:** Uruguayan Hercules, direct Montevideo to King George about 7 h (first direct run 4 Jan 2021); the older route went via Mar del Plata, Viedma and Comodoro Rivadavia to Punta Arenas, an overnight stop, then 2.5 h across; the Uruguayan ship calls at Punta Arenas and Puerto Williams. No regular Peninsula cruise voyage departs Montevideo (it is a provisioning and crew-change port, a 2025 call for RRS *Sir David Attenborough*, and the end port of one northbound itinerary). Livingston's own logistics ship uses Ushuaia and Punta Arenas (context). Artigas base is on King George Island, 3,012 km from Montevideo (source figure).
- Drake Passage: about 800 km at its narrowest (Cape Horn to Livingston Island; es.wikipedia 808.17 km); the 650 km figure on one Cape Horn page is unsupported (land-to-land about 815–840 km; BAS bathymetry about 850 km).

### Puerto Abrigo (Goudier Island, Port Lockroy, Wiencke Island; 64°49'31"S 63°29'40"W)
- **Primary: Ushuaia, 1,145 km.** Alternatives: Puerto Williams 1,123 km (nearest settlement of any size), Punta Arenas 1,362 km (a city of 132,363), Cape Horn 1,005 km, Diego Ramírez 970 km, Río Grande 1,250 km, Stanley 1,495 km.
- Bearings from the site: Cape Horn 346°, Ushuaia 344°, Puerto Williams 346°, Punta Arenas 339°; the Fuegian coast lies north-northwest across the western half of the Drake Passage. **No source used the phrase "Pacific mouth".** Ushuaia, Puerto Williams and Punta Arenas all sit in the channel system that links both oceans; none "faces" one ocean only.
- Source distance figures: Punta Arenas to the Antarctic coast 1,418.4 km (es.wikipedia); Ushuaia to the Peninsula about 1,100 km (en.wikipedia).
- Strait of Magellan: 570 km long, "several hundred miles shorter than the Drake Passage" route (en.wikipedia).
- Port Lockroy 2024–25: **22,577 tourists from 140 vessels**, 7,892 museum visitors (UK Antarctic Heritage Trust; resolves 18,000 vs 10,000). Approach: the Drake, then the Gerlache Strait and the S-shaped Neumayer Channel; no source gives the direction of traverse. Punta Arenas to Palmer Station (27 km from the site) about 4 days.

### Contrapunto (King George Island; Chilean village at 62°12'02"S 58°57'50"W)
- **Primary: Ushuaia, 983 km.** Alternatives: Puerto Williams 949 km, Río Grande 1,066 km, Punta Arenas 1,226 km, Cape Horn 838 km, Diego Ramírez 841 km, Isla de los Estados 879 km.
- **Air:** Teniente Rodolfo Marsh Martín Aerodrome, gravel runway about 1,290 m, opened 12 Feb 1980; about 50 intercontinental and 150 intra-Antarctic flights per season; Punta Arenas to King George about **2 hours by jet** (DAP; four sources) and **2.5 to 3 hours by Hercules**; the first pass's "about 4 h" has no support. Chilean Navy icebreaker *Almirante Viel*, Punta Arenas to Bahía Fildes, about 60 hours (Jan 2025). IAATO 2024–25: 152 southbound and 166 northbound flights into Teniente Marsh (10,067 air-cruise passengers). Aerovías DAP began Punta Arenas flights 12 Feb 1989; charter, not scheduled.
- **Sea:** the shortest crossing from South America is to the **western** end of the chain (Smith Island 826 km, Livingston 830 km, King George 844 km from Cape Horn); King George is 12 to 20 km farther.
- Villa Las Estrellas: about 80 winter and 150 summer residents (single-source for Wikipedia figures).
- Context only: the island hosts stations of ten countries; Brazil's resupply flies from Base Frei near Punta Arenas.

### Signy (Signy Island, South Orkney Islands; 60°42'30"S 45°35'42"W; solar UTC−3)
- **Primary: Ushuaia, 1,488 km.** Alternatives: Stanley 1,252 km, Puerto Williams 1,444 km, Río Grande 1,526 km, Punta Arenas 1,734 km, Río Gallegos 1,762 km, King Edward Point 896 km.
- **Brazil:** its southernmost coast (Chuí 3,057 km, Rio Grande 3,224 km) reaches Signy across open South Atlantic water about 7° of longitude east of the Falklands, but **Argentina and Uruguay are nearer on every measure**, and no part of Brazil touches the Scotia Sea.
- **South Africa:** Cape Town is 5,376 km (2,903 nmi) due east (bearing 87° from Signy). No source ties a Cape Town service to the South Orkneys. S.A. Agulhas II (home port Cape Town; range 15,000 nmi at 14 knots) supplies SANAE IV and other sites; SANAE IV is "more than 4,000 km from mainland South Africa".
- **Sea conditions:** the South Orkneys are ice-covered from late April to November; the Weddell Sea is "treacherous" with permanent ice fields; strong ice-class vessels are required for remote sites. **RERUN:** fast ice at Signy averages **149 days** (1903–2008), forming about 2 June and breaking out about 28 October (search-summary level, Murphy et al. 2014). Cape Town to the Weddell Sea ice edge: 10 days (Endurance22); to Antarctica 13 days (SANAE).
- **RERUN, supply and access:** **sea only, no runway**, supplied by the British ship (Nov 2025, Dec 2024), formerly the *James Clark Ross* from the Falklands in about 3 days (BAS pages, search-summary level); over 1,300 km from the Falklands. **No Brazilian, South African or Uruguayan operational link to the South Orkneys was found across 6 angles.**
