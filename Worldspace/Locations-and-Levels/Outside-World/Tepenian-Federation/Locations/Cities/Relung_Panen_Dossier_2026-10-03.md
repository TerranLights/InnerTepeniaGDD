## 5 · Geography

- **Position:** ~66°15′S 100°45′E (spec). The real oasis center is also cited as 66°17′S 100°47′E; both round to UTC+7. Knox Coast, Wilkes Land (**corrected 2026-10-03**: the Australian gazetteer's wording; earlier drafts said Queen Mary Coast), between **Mirny (93°01′E)** and **Casey (110°31′E)**; **18 arcmin north of the Antarctic Circle**.
- **Terrain:** ~450 km² of bare rock (to ~942 km² with inlets and lakes); bounding box ~41 km N–S by 51 km E–W; rugged low hills, **many freshwater and saline lakes**, extensive dry valleys, little lying snow.
- ⭐ **Algae Lake (Lake Figurnoye):** 25 km long, up to 137 m deep, the **largest body of fresh water on the continent**; most Bunger lakes are not ice-covered year-round (some are perennially covered; glacier contact decides which).
- **Walled on every side:** the ice sheet rises to the southeast; outlet glaciers close it to the south and west, among them the **Denman Glacier** (66°45′S 99°30′E; 11–16 km wide, descends 110 km, heavily crevassed, **the deepest point on continental Earth beneath it**, an ice-filled canyon 3.5 km below sea level); the **Shackleton Ice Shelf** (fronts the coast 95°E → 105°E, ~384 km along-shore) seals the northern approach, **64–145 km** of shelf between the oasis and open sea.
- **Coastal? "Qualified yes, but shelf-locked":** the sea entered the oasis before ~7.7 ka BP; saline inlets and marine channels lie just north toward the Highjump Archipelago. Literature: *"sea- and land-based approaches are difficult and rarely attempted."* *(Brief §3.5.)*
- **Hidden place:** discovered only because a naval pilot saw it from a seaplane window in 1947 *(real-world discovery fact, flavor only).*
- **Fits entirely on rock.** One of only two Tepenian cities that do (the spec names Davis as the other; `README.md` says Davis and Marambio, see §10). Nothing here is built on ice or on a maintenance treadmill because of its ground.
- **The two facts that define the site, and they pull against each other:** (1) more usable ground than anywhere in the Federation; (2) the hardest place to reach: *"Land to spare and no way to ship — the exact inverse of Denison's problem."* *"It is not remote because it is poor; it is empty because it is sealed."*
- **Notable locations in the spec:** Algae Lake · the Denman Glacier margin · the Casey spur (unnamed) · the Transkriptsii Gulf (a tidal epishelf channel to the sea, if the subglacial port is ever ruled).
- **Walled-off ecosystem (`DRQ-05d`):** open scientific speculation that seals and penguins may be trapped behind the Shackleton shelf as isolated populations. Flagged by the developer as wanted; **unchased**. It would be a research industry, a fragile food resource, and an ethical problem with no clean answer (a genetically unique population that is also edible, next to the one city that is not short of anything).

## 6 · Climate (🔬 annual anchors measured on site; monthly curve constructed)

- **Source:** automatic weather station on White Smoke Lake, Bunger Hills oasis, Jan 1992 – Jul 1993; a two-year Russian series (1956–58) exists but the monthly breakdown is not retrievable. The monthly curve is Mirny's shape × 1.16 continental amplification blended toward Davis's oasis summer, then normalized to the measured mean, and **validated to 3.3%** against the measured 3,987 freezing degree-days (constructed 4,117).
- **Measured:** mean annual **−11.2 °C** (Mirny's READER mean is the same) · mean wind **4.6 m/s** · solar flux 115 W/m² · **71 °C-days above freezing** · winter freezing degree-days 3,987 · summer peak days can exceed +10 °C.
- **Type:** East Antarctic coastal **oasis, shelf-sealed**: a continental temperature range at a coastal latitude. Warm by Antarctic standards (−11.2 °C vs −17.3 °C in the McMurdo Dry Valleys).
- **Wind:** easterly, fierce in events, but the basin sits **out of the katabatic drainage**; calmest coastal site in the Federation with Davis (~5.6 m/s); Mirny is 10–14 m/s, Casey 7–10 m/s.
- **Range:** warmest month (Jan) +0.6 °C, coldest (Aug) −18.7 °C, amplitude 19.3 K (Mirny 14.4 K).
- **Estimated (weakest column):** records ≈ +12 / −39 °C; precipitation **~92 mm** w.e./yr (regime: polar arid oasis; falls 92, lands ~60, ~32 lost to sublimation and wind).
- **Daylight:** **no polar night** (June minimum ~2.7 h); refracted midnight sun in minimal form only (~24 h at the December solstice; 22.4 h geometric).
- ⭐ **The hazard is the SUMMER, not wind and not cold:** meltwater, saturated regolith, drainage, freeze–thaw and slush. *"A city here fights water, not wind — the only large Tepenian site where that is the primary answer."* Wet cold rather than dry cold.
- **Monthly means (°C), Jan → Dec:** +0.6 · −2.1 · −9.4 · −14.9 · −17.3 · −17.2 · −18.1 · −18.7 · −17.7 · −13.7 · −5.7 · −0.0. Daylight (h): 21.0 · 16.8 · 13.2 · 9.4 · 5.7 · 2.7 · 4.1 · 7.8 · 11.6 · 15.3 · 19.4 · 24.
- **Gameplay notes in the spec:** the melt season is a calendar (December–February is a different place from July); wind is not the threat. Hardcore Mode influences are TBD.
- **Still obtainable by hand:** Gregorczuk 1980 (the monthly series) would upgrade the constructed column to measured.

## 7 · Purpose and economy

- ⭐ **The Federation's second agricultural region.** `Division_of_Industry/13` §7, working from human caloric need and knowing nothing of this city, found a **26.0% national food deficit** (need 545,902 vs supply 404,191) and that a second agricultural region at ~765,007 residents with a 35% agricultural sector closes it: *"{{Bunger Hills City}} is not optional."* **Revised later (`13`, after Lazar's reclassification): the nation then shows a 12% surplus, and the city becomes "firmly optional" (desirable for an uncorrelated second supply, not required for survival).** `14`: *"IS being founded — 'I can't think of any argument against doing so.'"*
- **Original rationale (`04` §6.4):** *"the largest ice-free area in East Antarctica has no city on it"*; developer, 2026-09-01: *"There could easily be a city built at Bunger Hills (no idea yet what it could be called)."*
- ⭐ **The water fact "is the whole city."** Every other city pays the full latent heat of fusion to turn ice into water (water and sanitation scores 3.9–10.2% of municipal economies). This one draws on standing liquid fresh water, **the lowest water burden in the nation**, *"a moral anomaly as much as an economic one"*; the open question is what "wasting water" means where water is not scarce. `08` notes it is the one place where the normal developed-world water-use figure could apply.
- **How it grows food:** indoor stacked hydroponics, as everywhere (the criterion is power, footprint and foundation, not soil, sun or mild weather). **Footprint and foundation are in overwhelming surplus.** ⚠ **Power is the open, binding question:** no geothermal, no measured wind resource, off-grid.
- ⏸️ **Proposed, not ruled:** the chain *phosphorus arrives → fertilizer → food → food ships out*; port, farm and processing as one system. **Phosphorus cannot be manufactured**; a national agricultural region is where it physically lands, so *"whoever holds this city holds the food supply of 31.7 million people"*: the most concentrated point of failure in the Federation.
- **Outdoor belt:** the terraformed coastal belt is ruled at 1,500 km²; **~400 km² of it is allocated to the Bunger Hills region** (serving this city, Mirny and Casey), distributed across three climate systems so they fail independently (`12`).
- **Seed vault:** *"The second agricultural region should hold a copy of what it grows."* Deferred until the city exists (`15` §3).
- **Livestock tier:** the agricultural cities (Davis and this one, if founded) are named as a livestock-tier attachment point; specific assignments not made (`11`, `DRQ-10`).
- ⏸️ **Division of Industry three-tier split: unwritten.** `Division_of_Industry/16` excludes it ("deferred by ruling"); `15` List A item 2: developer, 2026-09-02: *"let's hold off on {{Bunger Hills City}}, and we can figure out what the baselines for everything are according to what makes sense with the rest of the country so that it's #1.) internally consistent with the national character and distinct from other cities as its own community with its own unique identity."* **Deferred ON PURPOSE; do not "helpfully" close.** `09` lists only its D factor (1.43, the same as the Halley-subnet cities); every other figure is TBD. **No §15 percentages exist.**
- **Relationship to Davis:** open: sibling breadbaskets, rivals or complements. Davis is the existing 35% agricultural exporter; this city does the same job on 450 km² instead of ~410.
- **Food-price gradient (brief §3.5):** cheapest tier = Davis, this city, Mirny (via Davis, ~680 km) and Casey (via the spur, ~443 km). Developer observation: Mirny, a heavily blue-collar working-class town, eats cheaply and well (*"you don't get paid much at Mirny, but you eat"*), sourced to Davis, not to this city.

- **Why the road is hard:** not mountains but the Shackleton shelf (floating, calving, no permanent road) and the Denman Glacier.

- ⏸️ **Open (`DRQ-05a`):** was the Denman crossing *never attempted* or *attempted and failed*? ("We tried, and the ice won" vs "we never bothered"; a failed attempt leaves an abandoned works site, "a considerable location, and a monument.") Not decided.
