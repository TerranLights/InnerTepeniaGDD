# City renaming — candidate names (research, 2026-10-03)

**Status: SUGGESTIONS, except three names ruled 2026-10-03: Abowasa → Santa Luce (`DR-36`), Sayowa → Temirötkel (`DR-37`), Princess Elisabeth → Utstein / Utsteinen (`DR-38`).** Only Bunger Hills City is still open. No file-level rename sweep has been done yet. A city's official name is reserved to the developer (`05` §3); these are `REQUESTED` items for the developer to accept, change or discard. Placeholders (`{{ }}` / `‡`) stay in force everywhere until a name is ruled.

**Scope set by the developer, 2026-10-03:** Sayowa · Abowasa · Bunger Hills City · Princess Elisabeth. **Not** renamed: Sanay (stays), Lazar (stays; not on today's list). **The Tri-Cities' unified name** needs its own linguistic research first (a pan-Sinitic name) and is a separate task.

**Approach chosen:** "I propose, you pick." Candidates are built from each city's ruled founders' languages, the real place, and the naming patterns already in canon (carry-over, phonetic adaptation, condensed, prefix/suffix, and *replacement in the founders' language* — the way Shirayuki was named).

## ⚠ Research limits — read before treating any meaning as settled
- **Every WebSearch call was refused** (the session's 200-search budget was already spent). All four researchers verified by fetching dictionary and encyclopedia pages directly: Wiktionary, Wikipedia, Bokmålsordboka/Nynorskordboka, Kamus Dewan (prpm.dbp.gov.my), the KBBI text mirror (kbbi.web.id), the Norwegian Polar Institute place-name API.
- **Kazakh words are VERIFIED ONCE** (Wiktionary only). A second dictionary is still owed.
- **Not done for any name:** brand/trademark and game/web-presence collision checks; systematic Hindi, Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Korean meaning checks beyond what Wiktionary lists; native-speaker checks; pronunciation in Tepenian is a developer choice.
- **LAW 0-R says research fully.** A second pass with a fresh search budget should run before any name goes into canon.

## 1. SAYOWA (Mawson subnet) — ✅ **RULED 2026-10-03 (`DR-37`): renamed TEMIRÖTKEL (темірөткел)**, a developer-proposed compound of candidate 5's *temir* and candidate 1's *Otkel*. *(Candidate set kept for the record.)*

**Ruled founders (`DR-30`):** first established by the CIN **or** Kazakhstan (open), then industrialized primarily by Kazakh industrialists. **Open:** which of the two; whether the new name echoes the old sound (all candidates below are clean breaks).

| # | Candidate | Language | Meaning | Fit | Main risks |
|--:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Otkel** (өткел, *ötkel*) | Kazakh | "crossing, passage; ford" (өт- "pass" + -кел) | Names the ~4 km ice crossing and the spur road in the founders' own language. Also a real Kazakh water-crossing toponym (Zhaman-Otkel). | Plain noun, low drama; verified in Wiktionary only |
| 2 | **Brod** (брод) | CIN Slavic (Czech, Slovak, Slovene, Polish, Russian, Bulgarian, Belarusian: "ford"; Serbo-Croatian, Macedonian: "ship") | ford / ship | One word for the crossing *and* the freighters, in different CIN languages. | Looks like **Byrd**; real towns (Slavonski Brod, a heavy-industry river-crossing city); German *Brod* is an old spelling of *Brot* |
| 3 | **Huta** | Polish / Ukrainian (Czech *huť*), from German *Hütte* | ironworks, steelworks | The working word for a metal complex; matches the city's function | **Nowa Huta** is a near-exact real analogue; hundreds of villages named Huta; Swedish *huta* = to scold |
| 4 | **Pristan** (пристан) | Bulgarian / East and South Slavic | harbor, haven, landing place | A landing place for a city whose harbor must be icebroken open | Mild echo of "pristine"; lightly like Princess Elisabeth / Port Lockroy |
| 5 | **Temirzhol** (теміржол) | Kazakh | "iron road" = the ordinary word for **railway** | Iron + road for an iron-and-highways junction | Reads as a railway station; three syllables; real village/station name (unverified) |
| 6 | **Muzjol** (мұз жол) | Kazakh (constructed) | "ice road" | Names the ice crossing | **Compound not attested** in Kazakh; a natural phrase would be *мұз жолы* |
| 7 | **Boran** (боран) | Kazakh | blizzard, storm | The seaward katabatic wind | Weather-themed, not tied to industry or the crossing; Boran cattle, a common Turkish given name |

**Near misses:** Temirtau (a real steel city of ~185,000), Muzart, Bolat (given name), Vrata/Brama, Darbaza (Darvaza gas crater), Rév / Vad / Brasta, Esik / Aral / Tengiz.
**Pairing logic:** Kazakh-founded → Otkel (or Temirzhol/Boran); CIN-founded → Brod (or Huta/Pristan). "Replacement in the founders' language" only works once the first-establisher is chosen.
**Orthography:** Kazakh Latin has changed several times; the 2021 revision table was used. Tepenian spelling drops diacritics anyway.

## 2. ABOWASA (Halley subnet) — ✅ **RULED 2026-10-03 (`DR-36`): renamed SANTA LUCE.** *(Candidate set kept for the record.)*

**Ruled founders (`DR-22`):** Italy and the CIN, jointly. **Developer's own steer (`R-16`):** "along the lines of 'Santa Maria'." Style only — nothing implied about residents' religion.

| # | Candidate | Language | Meaning | Fit | Main risks |
|--:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Santa Luce** | Italian | "holy light" (santa + luce) | Closest to the Santa Maria line; a lit refuge on a dark rock in the ice. Also a real Tuscan comune name (altered Santa Lucia). | "Santa" may read as Santa Claus; same *type* as Santa Maria; tiny real comune of the same name |
| 2 | **Candelora** | Italian | Candlemas ("festival of candles"); Italian proverb that snow on Candelora means winter is over | A hearth-and-lantern name for a winter-built inland refuge | Four syllables, shares a shape with Concordia; surnames and churches only, no place found |
| 3 | **San Bernardo** | Italian | St Bernard of Menthon, patron of mountain-dwellers and alpinists (Pius XI, 1923); founder of the Great St Bernard hospice | A saint of refuges at altitude | **Heavy collision:** San Bernardo, Chile (>300,000) and many towns; the St Bernard dog; feast day PARTLY verified (May 28 or June 15) |
| 4 | **Rocca** | Italian | "fortress, stronghold; rock" | A fortified rock in the ice on bedrock; the pattern of real Italian hill towns | 70+ comuni *contain* Rocca (none is just Rocca); surname, lunar crater |
| 5 | **Scoglio** | Italian | rock rising out of water; also "obstacle" | The exact Italian word for a rock in a sea | Hard for English readers to say (SKOL-yoh) |
| 6 | **Cima** | Italian | summit | A nunatak is a summit out of the ice; short and warm | Cima, California; Nissan Cima; a dated Turkish sense (from Arabic) is vulgar — Arabic not checked |
| 7 | **Skala** | Slavic / Italian / Greek | "rock" (Czech, Slovak, Slovene, Polish, Russian, Bulgarian); "ladder, stairs" (Italian *scala*, Greek) | The one word that carries the joint venture in itself | **Highest risk:** a contemporary Ukrainian military unit is named Skala; La Scala; Zastava Skala car; Polish town Skała |
| 8 | **Ostoja** | Polish | "mainstay, refuge, haven" | The CIN-only option | Does not follow the Italianate steer; villages, a noble clan, a Warsaw housing district |

**Correction to a premise in the brief:** St Lucy's patronage is the blind, eye disease, authors and martyrs. "Light" comes from her name (*lux*) and her December 13 feast, not from a formal patronage. Santa Luce is proposed as a word, not as a patronage claim.
**Near misses:** Santa Lucia / Santa Barbara (far more famous; Barbara's patronage — miners, artillery — fits nothing here), Monteneve / Roccaneve (*neve* is Italian slang for cocaine), Santelmo (Filipino folklore omen; "St. Elmo's Fire"), Stanca ("tired"), Sala, Hrid.
**No natural Italian + CIN fusion** was found; compounds such as "Roccaskala" read as forced.

## 3. BUNGER HILLS CITY (Mirny subnet) — to replace `{{Bunger Hills City}}`

**Ruled founders (`DR-34`, 2026-10-03):** Indonesia and Malaysia, jointly. **The existing candidate list** (Dobrowolski, Oazis, Bunger, Figurnoye, Edgeworth David) predates `DR-34` and holds real station/feature names in Polish, Russian, Danish-American and Australian sources, none in the founders' languages.

| # | Candidate | Meaning (Malay / Indonesian) | Fit | Main risks |
|--:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Telaga** | lake; well; pond (both languages; Old Malay attested 684 AD) | The one lake word identical in both nations; the lake-dotted oasis | Compound *telaga tahi* = toilet; Russian *телега* near-homophone; a Telugu caste homograph (no subcontinental implication, but see the "No Subcontinentals" rule); many small real places |
| 2 | **Teduh** | calmed (wind, waves); sheltered from the sun; peaceful, safe | The oasis as a sheltered hollow against the ice sheet; the one-road refuge | Chinese reading not cleared; "TED-uh" stress |
| 3 | **Lumbung** | raised granary for produce (KBBI); *lumbung pangan* = food reserve | The most direct statement of the city's job (closing the food deficit) | Labeled **Indonesian** in Kamus Dewan (Malaysian: *jelapang*); "-bung" may read comic; Candi Lumbung, documenta concept |
| 4 | **Tasik** | lake; archaic/colloquial "sea" (Malay); only dialectal "lake" in Indonesian | Holds both "lake" and "sea" in one word — suits epishelf lakes with sea water beneath | **Tasikmalaya** (a city of ~770,000, popularly shortened to "Tasik") |
| 5 | **Makmur** | prosperous, abundant, lacking nothing (from Arabic) | Matches the food-surplus role | One sense is "populous" — awkward for the emptiest city; very common in place names |
| 6 | **Tirta** | water (Sanskrit loan); ritual/holy water | Compact "water" name | Indonesian-leaning (no Kamus Dewan entry); Sanskrit loan (flag: "No Subcontinentals"); Tirta Empul, Le Minerale |
| 7 | **Sendang** | spring/pool (labeled Javanese in both dictionaries) | A modest communal water word | A minority-language loan; typhoon name; mild echo of Sanay/Sinheung |

**Near misses:** Harapan (a Malaysian political coalition; duplicates Esperanza), Wahah ("oasis"; reads "wa-ha-ha"), Tunas ("shoot"; plural of *tuna*), Taman (a suburb label), Subur ("fertile"; reads "suburb"), Ranu, Jelapang, Padang.
**Open question:** one shared word (Telaga, Teduh) or a deliberate lean toward one nation's form (Lumbung, Tasik).

## 4. PRINCESS ELISABETH (Halley subnet) — ✅ **RULED 2026-10-03 (`DR-38`): renamed UTSTEIN (English / national form); native form UTSTEINEN.** *(Candidate set kept for the record.)*

**Ruled founders (`DR-22`):** the Scandinavian Trade Union nations, jointly (Norway, Sweden, Finland, Denmark, Iceland, the Faroes). **The old name** is a Belgian princess's, and Belgium no longer exists in the setting; the princess is flavor only (`DR-32`). **The city** is a ruin with a straggling band of survivors, on Hwy 7-ext between Lazar and Sayowa.

| # | Candidate | Language | Meaning | Fit | Main risks |
|--:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Utstein** (Utsteinen) | Norwegian (read naturally in Swedish and Danish) | "the outer stone" — **VERIFIED**, the Norwegian Polar Institute's own gloss of the nunatak's real name | The city takes its own rock's name; neutral, no princess, no station | Utstein Abbey and Church (Stavanger), the "Utstein Style" cardiac-arrest guidelines, a submarine class; not a town |
| 2 | **Varde** | Norwegian / Swedish / Danish | stone cairn marker; beacon fire | What the survivors are to a traveler on the highway | **Varde, Denmark** is a real town (~14,000); Latvian *varde* = frog |
| 3 | **Kuling** | Norwegian / Swedish / Danish | a gale (Beaufort 6–8) | The wind that defines the site | **Kuling** = old postal spelling of Guling (Lushan), China; shape close to Kunlun; names the instrument of destruction |
| 4 | **Vihuri** | Finnish (Uralic) | violent gust, squall (from Old East Slavic *вихърь*) | The only Finnish candidate; a gust with no shelter | Asteroid, an aircraft, a surname; Russian root word is also a missile name (unverified) |
| 5 | **Bylur** | Icelandic | snowstorm; gust | Wind and snow arriving at once | Reads "BY-lur"; **Byrd** shares By-; albums; Indian villages |
| 6 | **Klett** | Norwegian; Icelandic/Faroese *klettur* | rock, crag, cliff | The bare rock the city stands on | Very common surname; German publisher |
| 7 | **Fonn** | Icelandic *fönn*, Faroese, Nynorsk | snowdrift; wind-driven snow; Norse legend: Fǫnn, daughter of Snær | "That which was blown" | Reads as "fond"; the least on-point for a destroyed city |

**Near misses:** Skjold/Sköld ("scold"), Hamar (a Norwegian city of ~33,000), Puuska, Blåst ("Blast"), Tuisku, Myrsky, Orkan, Storm.
**Open questions:** should a ruin carry a name for the wind that destroyed it (Kuling, Vihuri, Bylur), for what remains (Varde, Klett), or neutrally (Utstein, Fonn)? Does "the outer stone" count as place-derived or station-derived? No strong Danish- or Faroese-only word was found.
