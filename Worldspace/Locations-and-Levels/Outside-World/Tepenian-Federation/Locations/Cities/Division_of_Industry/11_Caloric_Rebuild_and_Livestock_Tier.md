# PART 1 — THE CALORIC REBUILD

## The method: three chains that can genuinely disagree

**The fatal defect in the old model was that demand and supply were the same number.** The rate
`53 per 1,000 humans` was computed by summing the canon §15 food sectors and dividing by population — a
**supply** figure inverted into a **demand** rate, then compared back against its own source.

**The fix is to derive each chain from something nobody in this project chose:**

| Chain | Derived from | Independent of |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Demand** | human caloric and macronutrient requirement | every §15 sheet |
| **2 — Supply ceilings** | measured yields per m², per tonne, per animal | the labor model |
| **3 — Labor** | real-world productivity per unit of output | the demand figure |

**They can now contradict each other. That is the entire point.**

## Chain 1 — Demand, from biology

**The anchor is measured, not assumed.** At **Halley Bay**, mean intake over a year was **3,600 kcal/man/day**,
composed of **12.1% protein, 39.8% fat, 48.1% carbohydrate** — and the study's own observation is the important
part: **that intake is what you would expect of a moderately active worker in a temperate climate.**

> ## ⭐ **Shelter removes most of the cold penalty. The calories go to ACTIVITY, not to temperature.**
> **This cuts against inflating Tepenia's requirement — a heated city is not an ice traverse.** *(Expedition
> figures of 4,900–7,400 kcal/day are polar athletes hauling sledges, and are the wrong anchor for a city.)*

**Population-average setting.** A temperate whole-population average runs ~2,350 kcal/day *(children, elderly
and sedentary included)*. Tepenia carries a higher share of physical, industrial and cold-transit work, so:

> ### **2,800 kcal/day, population-average. +19% over temperate, far below the station-worker figure.**

**Composition** follows modern polar guidance *(50–55% carb, 30–35% fat, 15–20% protein)* rather than the
historical fat-heavy ration: **50% carbohydrate · 35% fat · 15% protein.**

| National annual requirement | |
|---|--:|
| **Energy** *(15,623,523 humans — only humans eat)* | **15.97 × 10¹² kcal** |
| Carbohydrate | 1,995,905 t |
| **⭐ FAT** | **620,948 t** |
| Protein | 598,772 t |

## Chain 2 — Supply ceilings

### ⛔ The ocean's ceiling, and the reductio that settles the argument

**Applying the rate CCAMLR actually operates — ~1.0% of standing stock per year — to a circumpolar krill
biomass of ~300 Mt, SPREAD rather than concentrated** *(per `10` Finding 5)*:

| | |
|---|--:|
| Sustainable krill | 3,000,000 t/yr |
| Sustainable finfish | 250,000 t/yr |
| **→ share of national calories** | **15.9%** |
| **→ share of the fat requirement** | **16.5%** |
| → share of the protein requirement | 67.6% |

> ## ⛔⛔ **To supply Tepenia's fat from krill alone would take 20,698,275 t/yr — THIRTY-THREE TIMES the entire
> real Area 48 trigger limit. It is not a stretch; it is arithmetically impossible.**

**⭐ So the ocean is a PROTEIN source, not a calorie source and emphatically not a fat source.** It can cover
two-thirds of the protein and one-sixth of everything else. **That is its ceiling, and no management scheme
moves it.**

### ⭐⭐ THE MARINE EMPLOYMENT FINDING — the largest single result in this pass

**Real fishing fleets are astonishingly productive.** Norway lands ~2.5 Mt with ~11,000 fishers *(227 t each)*;
Iceland ~1.3 Mt with ~4,000 *(325 t each)*. **At 250 t per fisher-year, Tepenia's entire sustainable national
catch needs 13,000 FISHERS.**

**Now compare that against what the canon §15 marine sectors imply:**

| City | §15 marine workers | Would land | vs. the whole sustainable national catch |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| **Cape Adare** | 177,732 | 44.4 Mt | **13.7×** |
| **Janbogo** | 124,985 | 31.2 Mt | **9.6×** |
| **Belgrano** | 98,484 | 24.6 Mt | **7.6×** |
| **Princess Elisabeth** | 79,430 | 19.9 Mt | **6.1×** |
| **Troll** | 65,568 | 16.4 Mt | **5.0×** |
| **⛔ The whole marine pool** | **691,076** | **173 Mt** | **53×** |

> ## ⛔ **The canon marine percentages CANNOT be fishing crews. Read as fishers, they would land fifty-three
> times what the Southern Ocean can sustainably yield.**

**⭐ This RESOLVES three standing problems at once:**

1. **The Cape Adare provider contradiction** *(`04` §3 vs §4, open since the pass began)*. **Cape Adare is not
   a food provider.** Its marine sector is a **port, shipping, and marine-science** sector. The contradiction
   was the model detecting a category error.
2. **The marine-discount question** *(`10` Finding 4 — the single biggest sensitivity lever)*. **The ⅓ and ⅔
   discounts were both far too generous.** The food-capture fraction of a "marine" sector is **single-digit
   percent**, not 33% or 67%.
3. **The Halley-subnet food gap.** It was never closeable by fishing, because **fishing is not a large
   employer anywhere.**

> ### ⭐ **And it reverses the national dependency map. Tepenia is not a fishing nation that also farms. It is
> an AGRICULTURAL nation with a small, highly-capitalized fishery attached.**

### The crop tier — and `08` §4.5's open question answers itself

Using a potato-analogue staple *(the historical polar staple, and the best kcal/m² in controlled environment
agriculture)* at **7,000 kcal/m²/yr**:

| | Growing area |
|---|--:|
| Staple/calorie crop | **1,141 km²-equivalent** |
| Fresh vegetables *(100 kg/person/yr at 40 kg/m²)* | 39 km²-equivalent |

| Stacking | National footprint | Per city |
|---|--:|--:|
| 1 layer | 1,180 km² | 31.0 km² |
| **6 layers** | **197 km²** | **5.2 km²** |
| 12 layers | 98 km² | 2.6 km² |

> ## ⭐ **`08` §4.5 called stacked-vs-spread "the largest determinable uncertainty in the model" and asked for a
> canon decision. The caloric basis answers it: TEPENIA MUST STACK.** **Spread, every city needs 31 km² of
> horizontal growing floor under glass, in Antarctica. That is not a city; it is a province of greenhouses.**

**⚠ And this reframes the crop tier's real constraint.** Stacked growing is lit and heated growing. **The
staple tier is a POWER load before it is a labor pool** — which matches `04` §5's Vostok diagnosis exactly:
*"not 'too few farmers' but 'food here costs power the city does not have.'"* **That diagnosis was never
Vostok-specific. It is the national condition, and only Vostok was poor enough to show it.**

# PART 2 — THE LIVESTOCK TIER

## ⭐ Why it is forced rather than optional

**After the ocean is fished to its ecological ceiling:**

```
fat required   620,948 t
marine supply  102,500 t
------------------------
FAT GAP        518,448 t/yr  must come from somewhere else
```

**Greenhouses do not make fat.** Leafy and fruiting CEA crops are water, fiber and vitamins. **Nothing in the
model as it stood could produce a third of the national calorie requirement**, and the old single-bucket rate
had no way to notice, because it counted *producers* and never asked what they produced.

> ## ⛔ **The old model was, in effect, feeding Tepenia on salad and krill. The developer's objection —
> *"a pretty good way to go insane"* — was a nutritional diagnosis, and it is correct in the arithmetic.**

## The two routes to fat, compared

### ⚠ First, the trap — because the obvious version is fatal

**Livestock fed on human-edible crops DESTROYS calories at roughly 10:1.** A Tepenia that grows staples under
lights to feed animals has built a machine for turning electricity into famine. **Livestock is only defensible
as an UPGRADER: converting biomass humans cannot eat into fat and protein they can.**

### Route A — grow oil crops directly for humans

| | |
|---|--:|
| Growing area | **2,074 km²-equivalent** — **1.8× the entire staple tier** |
| Labor | **2,592,241 FTE** |

### Route B — livestock upgrading non-human-edible biomass

**A lard breed, not a modern lean hybrid.** [The **Mangalitsa** is a real Hungarian heritage breed carrying a
fleece-like coat it grows in autumn and sheds in spring, at **60–70% body fat** against ~50% for an ordinary
pig](https://www.hobbyfarms.com/mangalitsa-pigs-breed/) — bred for cold hardiness, foraging, and lard.

| | |
|---|--:|
| Pigs per year | **5,760,536** — **0.37 per person** *(real-world US ~0.40: in line, not fantastical)* |
| Fat delivered | 518,448 t — closes the gap exactly |
| **Lean meat as a by-product** | **190,098 t = 31.7% of the national protein requirement** |
| Feed required | 1,728,161 t of concentrate |
| Marginal growing area *(see below)* | **117 km²-equivalent** |
| Labor | 1,080 stockworkers + 145,757 feed-crop FTE |

> ## ⭐ **VERDICT: livestock is the area-efficient path to fat, and it is not close.**
> **At the margin — counting only the feed that must be grown deliberately — Route B uses 6% of Route A's area
> and 6% of its labor.** **Even if EVERY kilogram of feed had to be grown from scratch, Route B still needs
> 1,152 km² against Route A's 2,074 — 56%.** **The animal is a better oil press than an oilseed is.**

## ⭐ The feed loop, with the inclusion caps that make it honest

**Feed is the binding constraint — not barn space, not climate, not warmth.**

| Source | Available | **Usable** | Why capped |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| Krill processing residue | 1,050,000 t | **259,224 t** | [fluoride and flavor taint — broiler quotas *"should not exceed 70–80 g per kg feed"*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7295028/); 15% assumed for a bred-tolerant Tepenian line |
| Finfish offal | 100,000 t | 100,000 t | 10% inclusion cap |
| Crop residue, starchy fraction | — | 256,616 t | pig-digestible portion only |
| Recoverable food waste | — | 937,411 t | ~60 kg/person/yr |
| | | **1,553,252 t** | |
| **Requirement** | | **1,728,161 t** | |
| **⛔ SHORTFALL** | | **174,909 t (10.1%)** | closed by a dedicated feed crop |

**⭐ Two findings fall out of the caps, and both are load-bearing:**

1. **The krill residue stream is the reason the tier works, and the fluoride is the reason it doesn't work
   completely.** [Deshelling is the real-world fix — it lowers fluoride and improves growth](https://thefishsite.com/articles/shell-crucial-for-suitability-of-krill-in-fish-feed).
   **And fluoride accumulates in BONE, not muscle — so the animal absorbs the problem and the meat comes out
   clean.** *(This is the honest place for the developer's genetic-modification proposal: not fur, but **feed
   tolerance** — pushing the inclusion cap above what any real breed manages.)*
2. **⭐ Pigs are monogastric and CANNOT digest the fibrous 769,849 t of crop residue.** **That stream needs
   ruminants — goats or sheep.** **So the tier is necessarily at least two species, for a physiological reason,
   not a flavor one.** *(Precedent: [reindeer introduced to South Georgia by Norwegian whalers in 1911 for meat
   thrived to ~7,000 animals at 40–80/km²](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer_in_South_Georgia) — so
   thoroughly that they had to be eradicated 2013–15. **Sub-Antarctic livestock is not speculative.**)*

## ⭐ On genetic modification — the fur is already solved, and the real reason to breed is failure

**The developer proposed engineering pigs for fur and extra fat. The research says the fur needs no
engineering — the Mangalitsa exists** — and it says something more useful about *why* a polar nation would
keep such an animal.

> ## **The barns are heated. You do not breed for cold because the barn is cold. YOU BREED FOR COLD BECAUSE THE
> BARN FAILS.**

**In a country whose central historical catastrophe is the destruction of a continent-wide power grid, livestock
selected for the outage rather than the ordinary day is exactly correct** — and it means **the herds that
survived the Long Night War are precisely the fatty, woolly, cold-tolerant ones.** *(The cautionary precedent
is real: [Byrd's 1933 expedition ran a working dairy on the Ross Ice Shelf](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/first-cows-in-antarctica),
and it half-worked — milk fell to *"a trickle,"* and Klondike was put down after severe frostbite. **An
unheated animal in Antarctica dies of its extremities first.**)*

## Two integrations that come free

- **Barns are heat and CO₂ sources; stacked growing needs both.** Co-siting the animal tier with the crop tier
  makes the barn a **subsystem of the farm** rather than a competing load. Nutrient return closes the loop as
  infrastructure and stays there.
- **⭐ It fixes what the reserve is MADE of** *(`10` Finding 8's unanswered question)*. **Rendered fat and cured
  meat are shelf-stable, calorie-dense, and need no freezer.** The classic polar reserve is salt pork, not meal.
  **A store of lard and cured meat rotates on a scale of years; a krill-meal store rotates on months and is 120
  days of protein with a micronutrient problem.**

## Siting — where the barns go, and it is not where it is warmest

> ### **Livestock goes where the FEED is, because feed is the constraint. Feed is krill processing, crop
> residue, and urban waste — so the barns cluster at processing points, not at the mildest latitudes.**

Which means the tier attaches to **the krill-landing and processing cities**, **the agricultural cities**
*(Davis, and {{Bunger Hills City}} if founded)*, and **the largest population centers** *(Lazar, Esperanza,
Cape Adare)* for the waste stream. **⏸️ Specific city assignments are NOT made here — that is DRQ-10.**

# What this changes, and what it does not

**✅ Changes:** the B1 rate *(53 → 120.7)*, every baseline load, the freedom margins, the national dependency
map's food row, the Cape Adare classification, the marine discounts, and the diagnosis of the Halley subnet.

**✅ Adds:** a livestock tier, a processing-and-preservation industry, and a physical composition for any
future reserve.

**❌ Does NOT change:** population, workforce, the difficulty ladder, the other 21 industries, the selective-
application step, or the two findings that survived validation — **the coalition structure, and the Weddell Sea
as an unclaimed third food center.**

  above. **They are choices about physical quantities, which is the point — the old rate was a choice about
  our own spreadsheet.**
