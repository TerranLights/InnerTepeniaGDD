# 2. ⚠ WHAT THE GAP ACTUALLY MEANS — my framing was wrong, and I should say so plainly

**DRQ-09's real question was:** *does a §15 percentage include the city's own food need, or sit on top of it?*

**I reframed it as:** *what fraction of food work is local handling versus concentrated growing?* — and
recommended 10/90 on that basis.

> ## ⚠ **Those are not the same question, and the reframe smuggled in an assumption: that GROWING IS ALWAYS
> CONCENTRATED. It is not, and `04` §4 already said so — *"Every city grows what it can locally."***

**The gap is the model reporting that assumption back as a 1.55-million-worker hole.** A country cannot ship
90% of its calories across Antarctica; **most cities must grow most of their own food, and that work is
BASELINE — unlisted, because §15 only ever described what was distinctive about a city.**

### ✅ The corrected structure — three parts, not two

| | Where | What names it |
|---|---|---|
| **Handling** *(10%)* | every city | baseline, unlisted |
| **Growing for yourself** *(most of the 90%)* | every city that physically can | **baseline, unlisted — this is what the reframe lost** |
| **Growing for export** *(the surplus)* | the few cities with real advantage | **⭐ this is what "Davis: agriculture 35%" names** |

**The 10/90 split remains correct as a description of the WORK.** It simply does not map onto the
baseline/distinctive boundary the way I framed it. **Davis's 35% is export ON TOP of feeding Davis.**

## ⭐⭐ THREE THINGS THE MODEL ADDS THAT CANON HAS NOT YET CONNECTED

### 1. The food crisis has TWO clocks, not one, and canon only describes one

**Canon treats "food shortage" as a single consequence of grid failure. The caloric model splits it:**

| Tier | What it supplies | **How it fails** | **Recovery** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hydroponics** | carbohydrate, ~50% of calories | **fast** — a crop dies in days | **one growing cycle** |
| **Heated barns** | **fat, ~35% of calories** | **slow but catastrophic** — canon: an outage over a day kills the livestock | **⛔ YEARS. Canon: *"The animals take years to replace."*** |

> ### **So a Concordia blackout has a shortage you recover from and a shortage you do not.** **The half of the
> food supply that cannot be regrown is the animal half — and it is a third of the calories, not a garnish.**
> **That is a sharper stake than "food shortages," and it is already latent in canon; the model only prices it.**

### 2. ⭐ The Amundsen Resonance Effect is a FOOD mechanism, not only a power one

**§11 establishes that the Tower was a 324 GW continent-scale flywheel providing *regulation*, and that its
loss means permanent harmonic instability and *"synchronized blackouts across multiple districts."***

> ## **Indoor growing does not merely need power. The barns need CONTINUOUS power — canon's own threshold is
> about one day.** **So the Tower's destruction does not simply reduce Tepenia's energy; it removes the
> stability that the animal tier depends on to exist.**
>
> ## **The central historical catastrophe of the setting and the thing that kills the livestock are the same
> event, by a mechanism that can be stated precisely.**

### 3. ⚠ An unconnected implication, flagged as a QUESTION rather than asserted

**§10: geothermal wells are *"contaminated with radioactive or chemically unstable byproducts from wartime
weapons."*** **§13: the heating grid is *"physically intertwined with… hydroponic water systems."***

> **Canon has not connected these two. If contaminated geothermal and hydroponic water share infrastructure,
> Concordia has a FOOD-SAFETY problem on top of a food-quantity problem.** **⏸️ NOT asserted — the files do not

## ⭐ And why CONCORDIA is the last city, when better-sited cities died
