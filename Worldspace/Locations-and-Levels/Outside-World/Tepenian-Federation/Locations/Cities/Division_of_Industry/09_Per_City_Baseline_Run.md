# 3. What the table says

**⭐ 1. The two ice-shelf cities top the country, and it is not close.** **Halley and Neumayer at ~50.3%** —
**half of everything they have, spent on continuing to exist.** Nothing else reaches 45%. Cold, wind, altitude
and isolation are all beaten by *the ground not being solid*, which the model was never told to prioritize.

**⭐ 2. The Peninsula is Tepenia's easy country.** Rothera, Casey, Palmer City, Juan Carlos, Sejong and
Esperanza occupy the bottom of the table at **33.8–34.6%.** ⭐ **And this is the same subnet the krill
grounds sit in** — **the cheapest places to live are also where the food is.** That is a real geopolitical
fact the model produced without being asked.

**⭐ 3. The robot-only cities are the cheapest in the nation despite the worst conditions.** Kunlun 28.6%,
Dome Fuji 27.3%, carrying the corpus's highest difficulty multipliers. **No schools, no clinics, no farms, no
children.** Their environment is the hardest and their job is the smallest.

**4. The ordinary-coastal cluster is real, not a bug.** Fourteen cities sit between 35.2% and 35.8%. **They
genuinely are alike** — mid-latitude, coastal, road- or sea-connected, ~49% human. **The earlier flat line was
a defect because it included Neumayer and Denison; this cluster is a finding.**

**5. Concordia lands mid-table at 40.8%** — driven by Dome C's 3,233 m altitude and interior position. ⚠ **The
capital has its own district methodology and this figure should not be deposited there without a deliberate
decision.**

# ⭐⭐ 3.5 — THE FREEDOM MARGIN. A worldbuilding finding, not a calculation.

**Developer observation, 2026-09-01, reading the table:** *"throughout the country, a hazy range of around
roughly, approximately half to three-fourths of the country are able to work in jobs they **want** to."*

**The arithmetic is exact: baseline runs 27.3–50.4%, so the residue runs 49.6–72.7%.**

## Two qualifications that sharpen it rather than undermine it

1. **⚠ It is an UPPER BOUND.** The three robot-keyed industries are excluded. Adding them raises baseline
   everywhere and **most at the robot-heavy cities — exactly where the margin currently looks widest.**
2. **"Not baseline" is not quite "wanted."** The distinctive tier also holds **provider work** — Davis's
   agriculture, Sinheung's fabrication, Sanay's docks. **That labor is mandated too, just nationally rather
   than locally.** A stevedore at Sanay is not there by preference; the Federation needs a port. **The truly
   elective residue is what remains after both.**
   > **And the mirror holds: baseline work is not unwanted work.** Someone can want to be a nurse or a
   > firefighter. **Baseline means *necessary*, never *disliked*.**

## ⭐ The comparison that makes it mean something

**The same sectors in the modern United States run roughly 29% of the workforce.**

| | Baseline | **Cold-tax vs. US** |
|---|--:|--:|
| **Rothera · Casey · Palmer City** | 33.8–34.1% | **+5 points** |
| *the fourteen-city coastal cluster* | ~35.5% | +6 |
| **Denison** | 44.4% | +15 |
| **Halley · Neumayer** | **50.4%** | **+21** |

> ## **Tepenia is not near subsistence. It is a developed society paying a cold-tax — and that tax runs from
> about five points on the Peninsula to twenty-one on the ice shelves.**

## ⭐⭐ Which makes it a FREEDOM GRADIENT DRAWN BY GEOGRAPHY

> ### **A Rotheran has roughly two-thirds of their working life unspoken-for. A Neumayerite has half.**
>
> **Where you are born determines how much of your life belongs to you — and not through wealth, class or
> policy, but through what the ground under the city is doing.**

**This is an inequality axis the setting does not currently name, and it runs OPPOSITE to the familiar one:**

> **⭐ The Peninsula is simultaneously the easiest place to live AND where the food comes from** *(the Scotia
> Sea krill grounds sit in the same subnet as the six lowest-baseline cities)*. **Meanwhile the
> hardest-working cities in the country have the least to show for it.** **That is a resentment engine, built
> into the geography, requiring no villain.**

## Threads this opens — flagged, not settled

- **What does spending half your life on the maintenance of existence do to a people?** Plausibly: pride in
  endurance, suspicion of ease, a settled conviction that the Peninsula is soft. **Halley's own spec already
  calls it "the city that moves"** — a civic identity built on doing the hardest thing.
- **And what does two-thirds unspoken-for produce?** Arts, hospitality, research, the elective trades —
  **which is exactly what the Palmer subnet's existing §15s already show** *(Palmer City entertainment 35%,
  Shirayuki arts 25%)*. **The model independently reproduced a pattern already in canon.**
- **Why would anyone stay at Neumayer?** Because it is home; because the work is unambiguously meaningful;
  because leaving is not simple. **All three are characterization, not economics.**
- **⚠ This should reach the culture work**, which will never open a Division of Industry calculation file.

# 5. ✅ THE WORKFORCE FORMULA — RESOLVED, and it was an error of mine, not of the model

**Developer ruling, 2026-09-01:** *"Robots definitely do NOT work 100% of the time. They require downtime
(most prominently for overnight recharging) just like anybody else. Also, they're fully conscious and
sentient, which means that if they spend all their time working, they're virtually guaranteed to go insane."*

**The trial model's author had refused to endorse the formula** — *"the rule I was given says 100% of robots;
I followed it, but I do not believe it, and it is carrying more of the answer than any rate."* **I inherited
that doubt and was about to halve robot participation**, which would have pushed Casey to ~51% and Neumayer to
~76%.

> ## ⚠ **That would have been wrong. I conflated PARTICIPATION with HOURS.**
>
> **`robots × 1.0 + humans × 0.5` is a HEADCOUNT OF WORKERS, not a count of hours.**
> **Every robot is a worker** — no childhood, no retirement, no dependants.
> **Half of humans are workers** — the rest being children, elderly and non-participants.
> **Both then work a normal shift. Downtime, recharging and leisure are already inside that, equally, for
> both populations.**

**The formula is correct. Every figure in §2 stands unchanged.**

> **⭐ And the finding it protects is §3.5's freedom margin — which the same ruling makes far more important
> than an economic statistic.** Per `Robot_Biology_and_Culture/Robot_Physiology_and_Cultural_Practices.md`:
> **on Upper Earth a robot's leisure was afforded by their loving romantic partners; in Tepenia it is a fact
> of life.** **For the robot half of the population, the freedom margin measures the thing the exile was
> for** — which means the geographic gradient in §3.5 is not an economic curiosity. **It is a political
> inequality in the one currency this nation was founded to secure.**
