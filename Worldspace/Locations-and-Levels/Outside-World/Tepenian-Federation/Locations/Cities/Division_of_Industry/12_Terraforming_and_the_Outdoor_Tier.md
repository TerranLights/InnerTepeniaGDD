# Three Mitigations — Multi-Species Livestock, Feed Basis, and the Terraformed Coastal Belt

> **Raised by the developer, 2026-09-01, on reading `11`.** `[CGRM 2026-09-01 · Path 4 · research]`
>
> **Verdicts up front: #1 STRONG and already half-forced. #2 PARTLY THE TRAP — one half works, one half
> destroys calories. #3 ⭐ THE LARGEST OF THE THREE — but as a ~20% contribution, not a replacement, and its
> real value is not the calories at all.**

---

# 1 — MULTIPLE COLD-SUITED LIVESTOCK ✅ Strong, and `11` already forces it

**`11` reached this from physiology before it was proposed: pigs are monogastric and cannot digest the
769,849 t of fibrous crop residue.** That stream requires ruminants. **The tier was never going to be one
species.**

**What the research adds is that sub-Antarctic livestock has a real track record, and the failure modes are
documented too:**

| Species | Role in the loop | Real precedent |
|---|---|---|
| **Lard pig** *(Mangalitsa-type)* | **fat** + the concentrate/waste stream | [60–70% body fat, fleece-like coat, bred for cold hardiness and lard](https://www.hobbyfarms.com/mangalitsa-pigs-breed/) |
| **Sheep** | fibrous residue → meat, fat, **wool** *(B5 textiles)* | Kerguelen, Falklands — sustained sub-Antarctic flocks |
| **Goats** | the hardiest fiber converter; marginal ground | broad cold-climate record |
| **Reindeer** | **pasture** species — only viable if #3 happens | [introduced to South Georgia 1911, thrived to ~7,000 at 40–80/km²](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer_in_South_Georgia) |
| **Poultry** | eggs — continuous, efficient | ⚠ but eats **concentrate**, competing with pigs |
| **Cattle** | dairy — high feed cost, poor cold performance | [Byrd's 1933 Ross Ice Shelf dairy: milk fell to *"a trickle,"* Klondike put down after severe frostbite](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/first-cows-in-antarctica) |

> ### ⭐ **The species split is not variety for its own sake — it is a FEED-STREAM split.** Each animal exists
> to eat something the others cannot. **Add a species only when there is an unconsumed residue stream to
> justify it; otherwise it competes with the pigs for concentrate and makes the balance worse.**

**⚠ The cattle line is the useful one for the setting.** Dairy in Tepenia should be **expensive, small, and
prestigious** — a thing some cities manage and most do not, because the real attempt half-failed.

# 2 — "HYDROPONICS PLUS KRILL TO FEED THEM" ⚠ Half right, and the other half is the trap

## The half that works: krill — but it is capped, and the cap is already binding

**Krill residue is the single best feed input Tepenia has** *(`11` Part 2)*. But
[broiler quotas *"should not exceed 70–80 g per kg feed"* for fluoride and flavor](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7295028/),
and [deshelling is the real-world mitigation — lower fluoride, better growth](https://thefishsite.com/articles/shell-crucial-for-suitability-of-krill-in-fish-feed).
**Even at a generous 15% inclusion, only 259,224 t of the available 1,050,000 t is usable.** The stream is
**already maxed out in `11`'s model, and the tier is still 10.1% feed-short.**

> **⭐ So "more krill for the animals" is not available. The constraint is not how much krill Tepenia lands —
> it is how much of it an animal can safely eat.** *(This is where the developer's genetic-modification
> proposal has real work to do: **raise the inclusion cap.** Every point of tolerance gained is ~17,000 t of
> feed unlocked.)*

## ⛔ The half that is the trap: hydroponic output as feed

**Feeding livestock on human-edible hydroponic output destroys calories at roughly 10:1** — and Tepenia's
hydroponics is the **most expensive food on Earth** to produce, because it is lit and heated. A stacked farm
growing fodder is a machine for converting electricity into a 90% loss.

> ## ⛔ **The rule that must hold: HYDROPONICS FEEDS PEOPLE. LIVESTOCK EATS WHAT PEOPLE CANNOT.**
> **The moment an animal eats something a human could have eaten, the tier stops paying for itself.**

**⭐ But #3 rescues exactly this.** Pasture and outdoor fodder are **not** human-edible and **not** power-hungry.
**The feed problem is not solved by hydroponics or by more krill; it is solved by ground.**

# 3 — ⭐⭐ TERRAFORMING THE COASTAL REGIONS

## The gap that has to be closed, and it is one number

| | |
|---|--:|
| **Esperanza, January mean** | **1.5 °C** |
| **Reykjavík, July mean** *(≈65°N — where potatoes are a commercial crop)* | **≈12 °C** |
| **The deficit** | **≈10.5 °C** |

> ## ⭐ **The Antarctic Peninsula's problem is NOT latitude. At 63–65°S it sits at Iceland's latitude, and
> Iceland farms.** [Commercial potato production runs to 70°N in northern Norway where permafrost is absent,
> and Icelanders grow potatoes at Akureyri, 65.4°N, on up to 22.5 hours of daylight](https://heritagepotato.ca/northernclimates/iceland/).
> **The problem is that the Southern Ocean gives the Peninsula no Gulf Stream. Terraforming's whole job is
> closing ~10.5 °C.**

## ⭐ And there is native stock to build on — the setting does not have to invent it

**Antarctica already has two native vascular plants, and one of them is a GRASS.**
**[*Deschampsia antarctica* (Antarctic hairgrass) and *Colobanthus quitensis* (pearlwort)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10857404/)**
— and critically, **they are already expanding**: over five decades, a >1 °C rise in mean summer air
temperature and a longer growing season have driven **rapid expansion**, with seedlings colonizing new ground.

> ### **The developer's "genetically-modified grasses" has a real ancestor to modify, growing on the actual
> ground, already spreading on its own. That is a far stronger footing than an imported species.**

**And soil formation is not hypothetical either.** [Seabird-enriched (ornithogenic) Antarctic soils develop
thick organic horizons genuinely enriched in organic carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/237086948_Ornithogenic_Gelisols_Cryosols_from_Maritime_Antarctica_Pedogenesis_Vegetation_and_Carbon_Studies),
supporting mosses, lichens and flowering plants. **Antarctic ground can hold real soil; it just does so in
patches where something delivered the nutrients.** **Terraforming is doing deliberately and at scale what
seabird colonies already do accidentally and locally** — and
[manufactured soil (organic material blended with crushed rock) is established real-world practice, with stable
soil aggregates developing over roughly 7–10 years](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10643389.2021.1989909).

> **⭐ That 7–10 year maturation is a usable in-fiction number: a terraformed field is a decade-long civic
> project, not a season's work. Somebody's parents started it.**

## How the 10.5 °C actually gets closed — and it splits in two, which is the important part

**No planetary engineering is required. All of this is landscape engineering, and all of it is real.**

| Tier | Technique | Gain | **Survives a grid collapse?** |
|---|---|--:|---|
| **PASSIVE** | walled fields, windbreaks and shelter belts; albedo darkening *(crushed basalt, char)*; manufactured soil over an insulating layer; cloches and low tunnels | **+3 – 8 °C** | ✅ **YES** |
| **ACTIVE** | circulated municipal waste heat under the field | **closes the remainder** | ❌ **NO** |

> ## ⭐⭐ **THIS SPLIT IS THE DESIGN. The passive layer is a wall, a windbreak, and dark earth — it cannot be
> switched off. The active layer is the city's waste heat, and it dies when the city's power dies.**

## ⚠ What terraforming CANNOT fix — and this is a hard physical bound

**Polar night. No amount of soil or shelter creates winter light.** The outdoor tier is **inherently
seasonal — one intense summer crop and nothing else** — which is exactly how Iceland, Alaska and northern
Norway work. [The midnight sun's very long photosynthetic periods compensate for sub-optimal temperatures](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11540-025-09854-0),
and Alaska's Matanuska-Susitna Valley sets vegetable records on ~20-hour days.

> ### ⭐ **And the thing that makes seasonality WORK is the one resource Antarctica gives away free: cold
> storage. A single harvest, stored, feeds the year. That is how every northern agriculture in history has
> operated.**

## The numbers — what it is actually worth

### Energy

| Staple tier *(10.37 Mt potato-equivalent/yr)* | kWh/kg | TWh/yr | share of national power |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| **Indoor stacked** | 10.0 | **103.7** | **11.6%** |
| Greenhouse | 4.0 | 41.5 | 4.7% |
| **Outdoor field** | 1.5 | **15.6** | **1.7%** |

> ## ⭐ **Moving the staple tier outdoors saves ~88 TWh/yr — very nearly 10% of national electricity
> consumption — in a country whose CURRENCY IS DENOMINATED IN GUARANTEED GRID CAPACITY.**
> **This is not a farming decision. It is a monetary one.**

### ⛔ Land — and this is the constraint that caps the whole idea

| System | Yield | **Field area needed** |
|---|--:|--:|
| Indoor stacked *(growing area, stackable)* | 7,000 kcal/m²/yr | 1,141 km² |
| **Sheltered terraformed field, good year** | 2,500 | **3,193 km²** |
| **Sheltered terraformed field, poor year** | 1,500 | **5,322 km²** |

**Total ice-free land in all of Antarctica is ~21,000–46,000 km² — 0.18% of the continent — and the fraction
that is coastal, mild enough, and near a city is far smaller.**

| Terraformed | Yield | Share of national calories |
|--:|--:|--:|
| 500 km² | 2,000 kcal/m² | **6.3%** |
| 1,000 km² | 2,000 | **12.5%** |
| **1,500 km²** | 2,000 | **18.8%** |
| 3,000 km² | 2,000 | 37.6% |

> ## ⛔ **VERDICT: terraforming CANNOT replace the indoor tier. There is not enough ice-free coastal ground on
> the continent. A realistic program lands at roughly 10–20% of national calories.**
>
> ## ⭐⭐ **But that is not what it is for. It is the ~15% that DOES NOT NEED THE TOWER — plus the pasture and
> fodder that make multi-species livestock work at all.**

# ⭐ THE SYNTHESIS — the three suggestions are one chain

```
TERRAFORMED COASTAL BELT
   |-- outdoor staples, stored over winter ......... ~10-20% of national calories, near-zero power
   |-- PASTURE and outdoor fodder .................. the feed problem, solved with ground instead of power
          |-- ruminants: sheep, goats, reindeer .... eat what pigs cannot
          |-- pigs stay on krill residue + waste ... the fluoride cap stops binding
                 |-- FAT ......................... the gap the ocean cannot fill
```

**Suggestion #2 fails on its own terms and succeeds through #3.** The feed basis Tepenia needs is **not**
hydroponics *(a 10:1 loss)* and **not** more krill *(capped by fluoride)* — **it is grass, which is exactly
what terraforming produces and what the extra species in #1 exist to eat.**

---

# ⭐⭐ THE CANON IMPLICATION — and it may be the most valuable thing here

**The three food systems have OPPOSITE risk profiles, and the setting's history sorts them:**

| System | Fails when | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Indoor stacked** | **the power fails** | ~10% of national electricity |
| **Outdoor terraformed** | **the summer fails** | land, and a decade of soil-building |
| **Marine capture** | the stock fails | capital; capped at ~16% of calories |

> ## **A prosperous Second Interwar Tepenia, with Amundsen Tower regulating a continent-wide grid, would lean
> INDOOR — because power was abundant and indoor never has a bad year.**
>
> ## ⭐⭐ **Then the Tower fell. And the passive half of the terraformed belt — the walls, the windbreaks, the
> dark earth, the soil somebody's grandparents spent a decade making — is the half that did not switch off.**

**⏸️ This is a candidate answer to a question the project has not formally asked: what is anyone eating in
2822? NOT RULED — flagged.** But it is the first food answer that survives the war on its own physics, and it
makes the terraformed belt **the reason there is still a country**, rather than a farming footnote.

**And it hands the setting a seasonal culture that continuous hydroponics never could:** a harvest, a store,
and a winter spent knowing whether the store will last. **A reserve stops being a program somebody has to
propose and becomes simply what a farming year IS** — which retires the entire national-reserve question from
`10` by making it unnecessary.

# ✅ RULED — DRQ-11, developer ruling, 2026-09-01

> ## **THE TERRAFORMED COASTAL BELT IS CANON, AT 1,500 km².**
> `[CGRM 2026-09-01 · Path 6 · developer ruling]`

| | |
|---|--:|
| **Scale** | **1,500 km²** |
| Share of national calories *(pre-war)* | **18.8%** |
| Outdoor workers | 30,000 |
| Indoor workers freed | 535,714 |
| **Net national labor saved** | **721,295 = 3.0% of the workforce** |
| **National baseline load** | **43.8% → 40.8%** |
| **National freedom margin** | **56.2% → 59.2%** |

**Why 1,500 and not more** *(the developer accepted the recommendation and its reasoning)*:

1. **Returns go flat past it.** 1,500 → 2,000 km² buys **one point** of freedom margin and adds **6.3 points**
   of national calories exposed to weather.
2. **The insurance stays intact.** Indoor remains the majority tier. A 50% crop failure costs 9.4% of national
   calories — survivable with a store. At 3,000 km² the same failure costs **18.8%**, which is a famine.
3. **⭐ And a methodological reason.** 3,172 km² would restore the baseline to *exactly* the 37.5% the project
   had before the caloric rebuild. **A mitigation that precisely cancels an inconvenient finding is a
   mitigation that has been tuned to cancel it.** Food really is more expensive than the old model said;
   1,500 km² recovers most of that cost while leaving some of it visible.

## ✅ SITING — all three regions, and the distribution is the point

**Ruled: the belt is DISTRIBUTED, not concentrated.**

**The land exists.** Antarctica is **~49,500 km² ice-free (0.35%)**, and *"most of these ice-free areas exist at
low altitudes near the coast, especially along the Peninsula"*:

| Region | Ice-free area | Belt allocation | Serves |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| **Antarctic Peninsula + offshore islands** | **8,000 km²** *(14% of national ice-free)* | **~700 km²** | Palmer subnet — Esperanza, Palmer City, Rothera, Juan Carlos, Sejong, Signy, Port Lockroy, Marambio |
| **East Antarctic coastal oases** — Vestfold **400 km²**, Larsemann **~40 km²**, Schirmacher **34 km²** | part of East Antarctica's **30,400 km²** *(55%)* | **~400 km²** | Mirny subnet — Davis, the Tri-Cities; and Lazar |
| **Bunger Hills** *(the largest single oasis)* | **450–942 km²** | **~400 km²** | {{Bunger Hills City}}, Mirny, Casey |

> ### ⭐ **Distributing it is not a convenience — it is the reason 1,500 km² is safe.**
> **A single Peninsula belt fails as one unit: one bad summer, one weather system, one famine.** Three regions
> in **three different climate systems fail independently.** *(This is the concentration-vs-resilience critique
> that `10` Finding 5 raised against the fishery — applied correctly the first time here, rather than
> retrofitted.)*

**⚠ Two things the land figures force, and both are useful:**

- **The McMurdo Dry Valleys are the largest ice-free region in Antarctica (~4,500 km²) and are the WORST
  candidate** — hyper-arid, extremely cold, effectively without liquid water. **Tepenia's biggest patch of bare
  ground is agriculturally useless**, which is a good hard fact sitting next to Scott and Fort McMurdo.
- **⭐⭐ Lazar sits on the Schirmacher Oasis — 34 km².** **The largest city in the Federation (1,287,003 humans)
  stands on thirty-four square kilometers of ice-free ground.** **It cannot feed itself locally and never
  could.** This sharpens `11`'s finding that the Halley subnet's food security is structural and severe.

## ✅ GRASSES — native stock, with imported comparators

**Ruled:** the engineered grasses **descend from native *Deschampsia antarctica*** — real, already on the
ground, and already expanding on its own as summers lengthen.

> **⭐ And the developer added a second half worth keeping:** imported grasses are maintained **as comparative
> genetic stock** — to measure what the native line does differently, and to introgress traits that let the
> engineered line **grow more widely** than *Deschampsia*'s natural range allows.

**This makes the program an ongoing scientific institution rather than a finished piece of infrastructure** —
a seed bank, a comparative-genetics program, and a permanent argument about how far the belt can be pushed.

# Open, and flagged rather than decided

- **⏸️ Passive/active ratio** — the single decision that determines how much of the belt survives the Tower.
  **At 1,500 km², a passive-only belt yields roughly 1,000 kcal/m² instead of 2,000 — ~9.4% of PRE-WAR national
  calories.**
- **⏸️⭐ POST-WAR ADEQUACY — flagged by the developer, 2026-09-01, and genuinely open.**
  *"A rather substantial proportion of the national population emigrated into orbit via Amundsen Tower during
  the latter half of the Second Interwar Period."* **The belt's post-war adequacy is a RATIO, and only its
  numerator is known.** Census I is the peak; Census II is post-orbital-migration; **no Census III exists.**
  **The same 1,500 km² covers 9.4% of pre-war need, ~19% at half the population, ~28% at a third.**
  **⚠ Whether the belt feeds post-war Tepenia is therefore a DEMOGRAPHIC question, not an agricultural one —
  and it cannot be answered until the war's population cost is settled.** *(Ties to `README`'s parked
  war-damage item, which the developer deliberately left low-priority tentative.)*
- **⏸️ Which specific cities host and work which sections of the belt.**
- **⚠ Species additions to the livestock tier must each name an unconsumed residue stream**, per the rule in §1.
- **⚠ DISTRIBUTIONAL CAVEAT.** **Halley and Neumayer sit on ice shelves and have NO ice-free land.** They are
  the two most burdened cities in the country, and **the belt does nothing for them directly.** On `09` §3.5's
  freedom gradient the belt is **regressive** unless the food moves and the labor credit moves with it — which
  lands on **DRQ-09**, still unruled.
