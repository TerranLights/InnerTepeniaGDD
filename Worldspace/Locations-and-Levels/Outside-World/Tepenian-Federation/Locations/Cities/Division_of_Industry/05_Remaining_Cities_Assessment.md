# ⭐⭐⭐ 1. THE LARGEST STRUCTURAL PROBLEM IN THE COUNTRY: the Halley subnet cannot be fed

**This is bigger than anything the pilot run or the provider survey found, and it was invisible until the
providers were listed out.**

| Halley subnet city | Humans |
|---|--:|
| Lazar | 1,287,003 |
| Halley | 709,749 |
| Neumayer | 613,735 |
| Princess Elisabeth | 553,768 |
| Belgrano | 531,925 |
| {{Abowasa}} | 504,237 |
| Troll | 475,721 |
| Sanay | 231,576 |
| **TOTAL** | **4,907,714** |

> ## **4,907,714 humans — 31.4% of the national human population — in a subnet with NO named food provider.**

**And it is far from both established food centers.** Queen Maud Land and the Weddell Sea sit at roughly
30°W–15°E. **Davis and {{Bunger}} are at 78–101°E. The Scotia Sea krill grounds are at 40–60°W.** The Halley
subnet is thousands of kilometres from each, on the opposite side of the continent from the first and across
the Weddell Sea from the second.

**The subnet's four providers are Belgrano (logistics, medicine), Troll (logistics), Sanay (logistics) and
Princess Elisabeth (energy engineering). Every one of them is logistics or engineering. Not one is food.**

## The resolution: ⭐ the Weddell Sea is Tepenia's THIRD food center of gravity, and nobody has claimed it

**The subnet is visibly already fishing** — Belgrano maritime 20%, Halley marine 20%, Lazar 15%, Princess
Elisabeth 15%, Troll 15%, {{Abowasa}} 15%, Sanay port 30%. **The activity is in the corpus. The designation is
not.** So this is a naming and sizing gap, not a viability crisis — **but at 31.4% of the nation's humans it
must be closed before any Halley-subnet city's §15 can be finalized.**

**⚠ Two of those marine entries are NOT dockside food production and must not be counted as such:**
- **Halley's 20% is explicitly indirect** — *"Halley has no docks of its own."*
- **Lazar is not coastal.** The Schirmacher Oasis sits inland of the Queen Maud coast. Its 15% is presumably
  indirect in the same way. **This needs checking before Lazar is treated as a fishing city.**

**Genuine dockside candidates for the Weddell/Queen Maud "fishbucket":** **Belgrano** *(maritime 20%, "one of
the Halley subnet's two coastal receiving points")* · **Sanay** *(port/shipyard 30%, the subnet's maritime
gateway)* · possibly **Neumayer** or **Troll** on the Queen Maud coast. **A coalition designation may fit
better than a single city — the same shape the Scotia Sea question has.**

## ⭐⭐ RESOLVED 2026-09-01 — the subnet feeds itself by COMMUTING, not by two cities carrying eight

**The producer pass found the real problem, and it was not a shortfall.** Belgrano and Sanay *could* feed the
subnet — **but only by spending literally everything:**

| | Spare capacity | Existing export role | **Left after food** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Belgrano | 492,422 | aviation 282,075 | **≈ 0** |
| Sanay | 211,860 | port + trucking 173,941 | **≈ 0** |

> **No arts, no hospitality, no weird industries, no local texture. Their entire character budget consumed by
> baseline, logistics and fish.** **That was the "unreasonable strain" — not a deficit, but two cities left
> with nothing to *be*.**

### ✅ The developer's fix: commuter labor on the maritime food lines

> *"When residents of {{Abowasa}} and Halley 'commute to/from work'… at least some of them, where they're
> going is: **Halley ↔ Belgrano** and **{{Abowasa}} ↔ Sanay** — and to a fair extent, at least a modest
> percentage of them are working the maritime food lines."*

**⭐ The pairings work geographically and on existing highway.** Halley ↔ Belgrano ≈ **330 km**;
{{Abowasa}} ↔ Sanay ≈ **395 km** — both on **Hwy 7**, both squarely in **rotational-residence range** rather
than daily-commute range, matching the {{Abowasa}} pattern already established in `01` §5.1.

**And a modest flow closes the gap several times over:**

| Assumption | Producers added | vs. the 10,035 deficit |
|---|--:|--:|
| **5%** of Halley + {{Abowasa}} workforce commuting, **20%** of them on food lines | **18,796** | **1.9×** |
| **10%** commuting, **30%** on food lines | **56,388** | **5.6×** |

### ⭐ Why this is structurally better than importing from Mawson

1. **It is local** — no 2,000 km haul, and it does not depend on Mawson's East Antarctic krill figures, which
   are **the shakiest numbers in the food table** *(the literature says East Antarctic krill habitat is
   "less well-documented")*.
2. **⭐ It preserves Belgrano's and Sanay's character budgets**, because the food work is done by *imported
   labor.* Their own spare capacity stays free. **That is the actual fix.**
3. **It gives Halley and {{Abowasa}} an economic role beyond being residential** — and makes labor
   externalization a **national pattern** rather than one city's quirk.
4. **⭐ It changes what "provider city" means.** Belgrano and Sanay are not providers because their own people
   fish. **They are the PORTS where a regional labor pool works.** The subnet feeds itself *collectively.*

**⚠ Consequences to carry into the city files:** Halley and {{Abowasa}} both need **labor-externalization
lines** in their §15s *(and {{Abowasa}}'s destination is now known: Sanay)*. **And Halley — canon's "city that
moves" — becomes a city whose people rotate out as well.** That is a great deal of motion for one place, and
worth using.

## ✅ RULED 2026-09-01 — Belgrano and Sanay SHARE the Weddell food production

> `[CGRM 2026-09-01 · Path 6 · developer ruling]`
>
> *"It appears to be perfectly reasonable to have food production (via maritime activity) shared across
> Belgrano and Sanay (as they're both coastal cities)."*

**Both are genuine dockside cities** — Belgrano *(maritime 20%, "one of the Halley subnet's two coastal
receiving points")* and Sanay *(port/shipyard 30%, the subnet's maritime gateway)*. **Neither becomes a
breadbasket-style single source; they carry it jointly.**

**⭐ This is the second coalition-shaped food answer in the corpus**, after the Scotia Sea. **Only the East
Antarctic agricultural core resolves to single named cities (Davis, {{Bunger}}). Both maritime centers
resolve to coalitions** — which is the correct shape, since a fishery is a *sea* worked by several ports
rather than a field owned by one city.

**Still open:** the split between them, their food sectors' sizes, and whether the Weddell coalition takes a
colloquial title the way the Scotia Sea one may take "fishbucket."

**⚠ Not applied to either city's §15 yet** — both are set-aside providers and remain unsubdivided.

> ## ⭐ This overturns a claim I made earlier in this pass.
> **I previously said Denison should carry the corpus's maximum construction burden, on wind.** That is wrong.
> **Denison fights its buildings' surroundings. Halley periodically moves the entire city.** Relocation is not

>
> **`01_Burden_Scoring_Model.md` needs a driver it does not have: FOUNDATION STABILITY** — grounded rock
> *(oasis, discount)* → grounded ice *(baseline)* → **floating shelf *(corpus maximum)*.** It pairs naturally

**Real-world confirmation:** Halley Station has been rebuilt or relocated repeatedly across its six
generations, and Halley VI was towed inland in 2017 ahead of a growing chasm.

# ⭐ 3. Oasis density — Lazar and the Tri-Cities are impossible as stated

| Settlement | Population | Ice-free ground | Density |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| **Lazar** *(Schirmacher Oasis)* | **2,620,319** — the largest city in Tepenia | **~34 km²** | **~77,000/km²** |
| **Tri-Cities** *(Zhongshan + Sinheung + Shirayuki, Larsemann Hills)* | **3,527,096** | **~40 km²** | **~88,000/km²** |

**For scale: Manhattan is ~28,000/km². These figures are three times the densest inhabited places on Earth.**

## ✅ RESOLVED 2026-09-01 — geographic expansion onto ice. **No census adjustment needed.**

**Developer instruction:** try geographic expansion past the ice-free zones first; **only if that proves
impossible or still yields unreasonable densities**, adjust census numbers by reallocating some to
{{Bunger Hills City}}. **⚠ And explicitly — that fallback would be a numbers correction so the figures make
sense against real-world geography, NOT an in-fiction relocation of living citizens.** *(Not needed. Recorded
so the option is not mistaken for a decision, and so nobody later reads a population change as a migration
event.)*

### The canon obstacle, and why it does not actually bind

**`Canon_Gap_Resolution_Method/Resolution_Log.md` CGRM-009 named a project-wide pattern:** the four Specs that
state an extent *(Davis, Lazar, Fort McMurdo, Sinheung)* **all state it as the ice-free terrain the city
occupies**, and Fort McMurdo's treats land area as **capping** population *("island cap")*.

> ## ⭐ But that pattern is a SELECTION EFFECT, not a national rule — and this pass is where it breaks.
>
> **Those four cities are precisely the oasis cities.** Meanwhile **Concordia sits on ~3,200 m of ice at
> Dome C, Vostok on ~3,500 m, and Halley and Neumayer on *floating shelves*.** Ice-free ground is **under 1%
> of the continent.** **If "extent = ice-free terrain" were a national constraint, roughly 31 of 38 cities
> would violate it.**
>
> **Tepenia demonstrably builds on ice. The oasis is where it builds CHEAPLY.**

### The arithmetic — expansion is modest

**Target: Lazar's own canon density (53,058/km²), recorded in CGRM-009 as Tepenia's densest.**

| | Population | Oasis | Needed at 53,058/km² | **Ice-founded expansion** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| **Lazar** | 2,620,319 | 34 km² | **49.4 km²** | **+15.4 km²** — an apron ~1.3 km wide around the rock |
| **Tri-Cities** | 3,527,096 | 40 km² | **66.5 km²** | **+26.5 km²**, ~9 km² per city |

**Both are comfortably achievable and neither is sprawl.** **Geographic expansion resolves the problem
outright.**

> ### ⚠ Discrepancy found, unresolved — check before reusing the benchmark.
> **CGRM-009 records Lazar at 53,058/km² "in the 34 km² Schirmacher Oasis."** But **2,620,319 ÷ 34 =
> 77,068**, not 53,058. The two cannot both be right. **Either Lazar's Spec already states ~49.4 km² (and the
> log's "34 km²" is the oasis figure rather than the city's extent), or the density was computed against a
> different population.** **This is currently the corpus's density benchmark, so it should be reconciled
> before anything else is scored against it.**

### And the expansion is generative rather than merely corrective

**Ice-free ground is where you build cheaply — not the only place you can build.** So these cities **extend
out onto the ice beyond the rock**, and that produces a real internal geography:

> ### **An oasis core — rock-founded, stable, cheap to build on, desirable.**
> ### **An ice periphery — expensive, unstable, requiring the engineering the core does not.**

**That is a class and status gradient written into the ground itself**, and for **Lazar — the largest city in
the country** — it is a major civic structure that nothing currently describes. **The Tri-Cities' "cluster
economy" also stops being about proximity and becomes about a shared 40 km² of rock three cities are
competing for.**

**⚠ Note the inversion against §2:** Lazar and the Tri-Cities have the corpus's *cheapest* foundations at the
center and expensive ones at the edge. **Halley and Neumayer have no cheap ground at all.**

# 4. Denison — the wind, and a sector the model must be able to reproduce

**Cape Denison, Commonwealth Bay is the windiest place on Earth at sea level** — Mawson's *"Home of the
Blizzard,"* mean annual winds around 22 m/s. **Denison's §15 already carries `Structural/wind engineering:
~25%`, an unusually large sector.**

**The pilot's highest wind multiplier was Casey at 1.45, for 7–10 m/s.** Denison belongs near **2.0**.

> **This is the model's best available falsification test after the fat-middle problem.** **If the instrument
> cannot reproduce Denison's existing 25% wind-engineering sector from its drivers, the instrument is
> wrong** — and that is worth discovering on one city rather than thirty-one.
