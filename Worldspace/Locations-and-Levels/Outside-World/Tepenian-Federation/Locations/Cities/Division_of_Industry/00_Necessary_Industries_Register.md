## The derivation frame

**Not "what does a city usually have."** The audit question is what must exist for a person — human **or**
robot — to be **born or manufactured → housed → warmed → watered → fed → clothed → moved → connected → healed
or maintained → taught → employed → governed → protected → and finally disposed of**, in a place where
**nothing can be improvised from the environment and there is no mainland to fall back on.**

**That last clause is the one that does the work**, and it is where Tepenia departs hardest from its real-world
analogs. See §A below.

# ⚠ A. The research finding that inverts a real-world fact — read before using this list

**Real remote cold settlements run *low* healthcare, not high.** Svalbard employs **under 2%** of its workforce
in public health care against mainland Norway's 4%. This is not an oversight: remote settlements **screen who
is allowed to arrive** and **evacuate anyone who becomes a long-term burden**. Longyearbyen has famously
limited provision for dying there. The low number is a *policy*, expressed as an economy.

**Tepenia has no "out." There is no mainland. The valve is closed.**

**So a Tepenian city must internalize the entire care burden its real-world analogs export** — obstetrics,
chronic disease, geriatrics, long-term disability, psychiatric care, palliative care, and the whole apparatus
of dying somewhere on purpose. **Tepenian healthcare should therefore run *above* ordinary national norms,
while its closest real inspirations run below them.**

> **⭐ This is the `Real_World_Basis_Extrapolation_Method` working exactly as intended, and it is worth
> preserving as a worked example.** The real-world fact was *not* the answer. **The real-world fact identified
> the mechanism — remote places export their sick — and Tepenia's own situation then inverted the magnitude.**
> A striking real fact is not automatically a city fact. The mechanism transferred; the number reversed.

**Second, smaller correction from the same research:** the sweep treated healthcare as its headline absence
(29/36). **Administration is probably the corpus's larger real error** (33/36). Nunavut runs **60% public
sector**; Svalbard **~50%** against a 35% mainland baseline. **Remote settlements are administration-heavy**,
because allocation, rationing, permitting, registry, and logistics coordination are what make remote habitation
possible at all. It is not bureaucratic bloat; it is life support with a filing cabinet.

# B. The register — 18 industries in four tiers

**`LINE`** = surfaces as its own §15 entry. **`FOLD`** = genuinely present, but subsumed into a broader entry
rather than itemized.

> **⚠ Why the LINE/FOLD split exists.** Eighteen bullet points in every city's §15 would collapse the format
> and guarantee thirty-six identical-looking lists. **Roughly 6–9 should surface per city — and *which* ones
> surface is itself differentiating.** Cape Adare surfaces marine extraction; Vostok surfaces thermal and
> enclosure. **A city's §15 should be legible at a glance as *that city's*, which a fixed 18-row template can
> never be.**

## Tier A — survival-critical *(failure kills people in hours to days)*

| # | Industry | Default | Why non-optional **here specifically** |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Thermal & power** | `LINE` | Heat is not comfort, it is the survival precondition — Concordia's own canon calls dome-and-corridor heating exactly that. Generation, distribution, and a maintenance load that never stops. **Post-Tower this is the national wound**, and the currency was energy-backed. |
| A2 | **Water & sanitation** | `LINE` | Water is *ice*: the cost is energy to melt, not pressure to pump — a different industry from the one every real city runs. Waste cannot be landfilled in permafrost nor discharged into a frozen sea. |
| A3 | **Enclosure & atmosphere integrity** | `LINE` | Distinct from construction. Seals, pressure, air handling, CO₂, humidity. **A dome breach is not a repair job; it is a mass-casualty event.** |
| A4 | **Construction & structural maintenance** | `LINE` | Every structure fights conditions engineered to destroy it: katabatic wind, drift burial, frost heave, thermal cycling. **Denison's entire civic identity is wind-engineering and it has no construction sector.** |
| A5 | **Emergency services** | `FOLD` | **Fire in a sealed structure is catastrophic** — no venting, toxic smoke, nowhere outdoors to evacuate to. Plus SAR and industrial rescue. Absent corpus-wide. |

## Tier B — sustenance and the maintenance of bodies *(failure kills over weeks to years)*

| # | Industry | Default | Why non-optional **here specifically** |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Food production & distribution** | `LINE` | Closed-loop hydroponics per the `City_Logistics.md` precedent; fishing where coastal. **The distribution half is the part nobody writes down.** |
| B2 | **Human healthcare** | `LINE` | See §A. Must carry what real analogs export. |
| B3 | **Robot maintenance, repair & parts** | `LINE` | The exact structural counterpart for **~51% of the national population** (16,403,077 robots vs. 15,623,523 humans). Frames, actuators, sensor calibration, processor service. **A civic utility, not a personal habit.** |
| B4 | **Coolant & siligel supply** | `FOLD` | Canon-specific. A *consumable* survival input, meaning continuous production — same criticality as water, for half the population. |
| B5 | **Textiles & survival gear** | `FOLD` | Cold-weather clothing is life-support equipment. Manufacture, fitting, repair, and a replacement cycle driven by how hard the climate chews it. |

## Tier C — continuity across generations *(failure kills the city over decades)*

| # | Industry | Default | Why non-optional **here specifically** |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Education & training** | `LINE` | General schooling where there are children — **plus the trade pipeline that replaces every Tier A/B specialist.** In an isolated city you cannot hire in a replacement welder; you must have *made* one. |
| C2 | **Childcare & eldercare** | `FOLD` | Shaped, not removed, by normalized artificial wombs and a male-skewed exile demographic. |
| C3 | **Administration & records** | `LINE` | See §A — probably the corpus's largest real gap. Allocation, rationing, permitting, registry, logistics coordination. |
| C4 | **Materials recovery & recycling** | `LINE` | **In a closed continental economy the scrap stream is a resource stream.** Absent corpus-wide, large, and unusually generative culturally. |
| C5 | **Mortuary & decommissioning** | `FOLD` | **You cannot bury in permafrost.** What happens to human remains — and, the better question, **what happens to a robot's body?** For a majority-robot civilization this is a real institution nobody has built. |

### ⭐ Tier C additions — 2026-09-01, found by cross-check against the BLS Standard Occupational Classification

> **The original eighteen were derived from a life-cycle audit — *what keeps a body alive*. Cross-checking
> against the SOC's 23 major occupational groups exposed four industries that keep a SOCIETY running, which
> that framing structurally could not see.** *(`to-be-integrated/BLS_-_Standard_Occupational_Classification_
> Manual_2018.pdf`.)* **Every one is already implied by standing canon.**

| # | Industry | Default | SOC group | Why it is non-optional **in Tepenia specifically** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C6** | **Legal & justice** | `LINE` | **23-0000 Legal** | **Canon establishes a three-tier criminal justice system** — exile → supervised labor → Aquarius subject. **Courts, adjudication, enforcement and legal support are all implied and none are staffed.** A society with a sentencing ladder has a judiciary |
| **C7** | **Community & social services** | `FOLD` | **21-0000 Community & Social Service** | Counselors, social workers, **and religious workers.** Canon has **five robot religions**, a published Polydimensional Animism with rituals and doctrines, and an institute training **robot counselors.** All currently unemployed |
| **C8** | **Finance, currency & allocation** | `LINE` | **13-0000 Business & Financial Operations** | Canon has an **energy-backed national currency**, post-war **regional currencies plus a cross-subnet trade standard**, a **black market**, and a rationed/metered economy. ⭐ **In a rationed economy, allocation IS the financial system** — this is larger here than in a market society, not smaller |
| **D4** | **Computing & data systems** | `FOLD` | **15-0000 Computer & Mathematical** | **Arcanet, Leyline, bridge units and jack-in are all canon.** Nobody currently runs, maintains or writes for any of them |

**And one expansion rather than an addition:**

| # | Was | **Now** | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A5** | Emergency services *(fire, SAR)* | **Emergency & PROTECTIVE services** | **SOC 33-0000 Protective Service** bundles fire and rescue with **policing and enforcement.** The register had the first and not the second — **but a three-tier justice system presumes somebody makes arrests.** |

> **⚠ Deliberately NOT added: 55-0000 Military Specific.** Tepenia fought the Long Night War, so a military
> existed — **but whether it is a standing peacetime civic industry during the Second Interwar is a canon
> question, not a modeling one.** Routed rather than assumed.

**Register total: 18 → 22 industries.**

### ⭐⭐ The REVERSE check — what Tepenia needs that the SOC cannot classify

**Run the comparison the other way and the real world's complete taxonomy of human work has exactly four holes
when applied to Tepenia. All four are the same hole.**

| Tepenian role | Nearest SOC categories | Why neither fits |
|---|---|---|
| **B3 Robot maintenance** | **49-0000** *(Installation, Maintenance & Repair — machines)* vs. **29/31-0000** *(Healthcare — persons)* | ⭐ **It falls between two categories that never overlap on Earth, because Earth has no machine persons.** Servicing a robot is simultaneously both and neither |
| **Robot semantic / emotional care** *(Sinheung Institute)* | 21-0000 counselors · 29-0000 practitioners | **No equivalent exists.** A clinician for a machine mind is unclassifiable in a taxonomy built for one kind of mind |
| **Cradle chamber operation** | 51-0000 *(Production)* | **Production is for goods.** This produces **people** |
| **Robot decommissioning & ossuary work** | 39-4000 *(Funeral Service Workers)* | Funeral service is defined around human remains. **Metal bones that never decay are a different profession** |

> ## **The four things the SOC cannot classify are precisely the four things that make Tepenia Tepenia.**
> **Every one of them exists because robots are persons.** A taxonomy of 867 real occupations, built by a
> society of one kind of person, has holes in exactly the shape of the second kind.
>
> **This is worth more than a completeness check — it is a diagnostic of what the setting's actual novelty
> is.** Not the cold, not the isolation, not the domes. **The unclassifiable work.**

# C. The nineteenth slot — reserved, and not on the list

**Every city additionally reserves a slot for its LAW G industry** — the one that sounds insane until context
makes it inevitable. **It is deliberately not numbered here**, because it is the opposite kind of thing: not
what the city must have, but what only this city could have.

**Procedure, six moves, honesty gate, quota and anti-tic calibration:
`Canon_Gap_Resolution_Method/05_Bulk_Mode_for_Repeated_Shape_Gaps.md` §LAW G.**

**In-corpus model:** Cape Adare's guano extraction. **Reserve the slot in all 36; let intensity vary** — if
every city is strange, none is.

> ### Band — developer ruling, 2026-09-01: **0.5% – 8% of total economy.**
> **The 0.5% floor is a characterization, not a rounding error** — a bedroom city like Scott, partly inhabited
> by people who work in Fort McMurdo and don't live where they work, is *where nothing happens*, and that is
> the point. **The 8% ceiling** prevents the strange industry from becoming the thing the city *is*, which
> would re-create the object-colonization failure this whole effort started from.

### First candidate on the board — "sky water" *(2026-09-01)*

**Frost / rime harvesting**, from a developer feasibility question. **Researched and reclassified**: it fails
completely as a Tier A water supply *(plateau air is ~430× drier than temperate air and already saturated —
there is no dew point left to chill it to)*, **but the passive form is free**, because radiative cooling drives
surfaces below the frost point without refrigeration and **these cities already pay crews to scrape accreted
ice off their own structures.** Byproduct→product and liability→asset at once.

**Full physics, figures, and the unchased third-order thread** *(unmetered roof frost as an off-registry
parallel economy)*: `../Research_Logs/Division_of_Industry_Research_Log.md` Session 2. **Not yet assigned to
any city.**

# D. Level-2 questions that are the developer's, not this system's

**Per LAW D, these resolve nationally and each closes ~30+ cells at once. Per LAW A, that is exactly why none
of them may be settled here.** Groundwork prepared; routing to `Developer_Ruling_Queue.md`.

1. **Food.** Does `City_Logistics.md`'s Concordia hydroponics precedent generalize to all 36? *(If yes: one
   ruling closes 33 cells and dissolves the never-canon "Davis feeds 30 cities" dependency, leaving Davis as
   the only **export** agriculture.)*
2. **Robot maintenance.** Is it a public utility, a private trade, or a self-service norm? **This is a
   civilizational-character question, not an economic one** — it decides whether a robot in Tepenia is
   maintained, maintains itself, or is *someone's customer*.
3. **Death and decommissioning.** What does Tepenia do with human remains where burial is impossible — and
   what does it do with a robot's body? **Almost certainly the most culturally generative single ruling on
   this list.**
4. **Healthcare magnitude.** Confirm §A's inversion: Tepenian cities carry the full care burden because there
   is no evacuation destination. *(Binds all 36 and sets the whole B2 row.)*
5. **Materials recovery.** Is recycling a civic obligation, an industry, or an informal economy? *(Interacts
   with `Parallel_Economies_Survey.md`.)*

# E. What this register does **not** do

- **It does not size anything.** Presence only. Sizing is `01_Burden_Scoring_Model.md`.
- **It does not assert that all 18 apply everywhere.** Necessity is modulated by population composition — a
  0-human city has near-zero B2 and maximal B3. The model handles that.
- **It has not been validated against a single city.** Nothing here has survived contact with real city data.
  **The four-city pilot is the test, and its declared falsification condition applies to this list too:** if
  the pilot's §15s come out looking alike, the fault may be this register rather than the scoring model.
