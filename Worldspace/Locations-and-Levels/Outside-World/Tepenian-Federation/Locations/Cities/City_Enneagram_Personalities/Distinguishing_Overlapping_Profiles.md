# Distinguishing Overlapping Profiles

The three-axis Enneagram framework only has 27 possible combinations, and Tepenia has 36 profiled locations (35 cities + Amundsen Station) — so overlap was inevitable. Only **Dome Fuji** (Instinctive/Withdrawn/Positive Outlook) and **Abowasa** (Thinking/Compliant/Positive Outlook) turned out unique. Every other city lands in one of 11 shared groups below, ranging from 2 to 6 cities deep. This file exists specifically to answer: if two cities score identically, what actually tells them apart?

The short answer, true across every group below: the three axes describe *how* a city relates to conflict, other cities, and its own inner life in the abstract — they say nothing about *what* a city is actually about. Two cities can withdraw for entirely different reasons, or assert themselves through entirely different means. That's where the real distinguishing detail lives.

---

## Group 1 — Instinctive / Withdrawn / Competency (6 cities)

**Byrd, Sanay, Denison, Davis, Mirny, Zhongshan**

The largest overlap in the whole set — six cities that are all self-sufficient, quietly capable, and physically grounded rather than analytical or image-driven. What separates them is *why* each one withdraws and *what* its competence is actually applied to:

- **Byrd** withdraws out of near-total circumstance — the most isolated surviving city in Tepenia, its aviation route permanently broken, its whole identity organized around enduring that isolation at the largest scale (a staggering underground city beneath a sparse surface).
- **Sanay** withdraws by choice, holding the subnet's *actual* critical infrastructure (the real Arcanet nexus) while letting Halley keep the naming credit. Its competence is about quietly carrying real power without needing recognition for it.
- **Denison** withdraws architecturally — a single, fully interlinked structure sealed against the most extreme wind-engineering conditions in Tepenia. Its competence is a specific, singular engineering feat, not a general disposition.
- **Davis** withdraws into informal, self-directed culture-building — smuggled books, a quiet arts scene — layered on top of a genuinely industrial mining/fabrication economy. Its competence is about balancing hard labor with modest, self-made enrichment.
- **Mirny** withdraws behind a literal physical windbreak, a concentric ring of buildings shielding industrial yards inside. Its competence is rough, industrial, and unapologetically plain — pride that never needed to be earned through anything but showing up.
- **Zhongshan** withdraws into self-sufficiency through its own Prydz Bay fishing economy. Its competence is the subtlest of the six — "chaos that is actually structure," an order that doesn't even announce itself as order.

**The distinguishing axis:** scale and object. Byrd = isolation at maximum scale. Sanay = power without credit. Denison = a singular engineering feat. Davis = industry plus quiet self-culture. Mirny = industrial shelter. Zhongshan = the quietest, most invisible order of all six.

## Group 2 — Thinking / Compliant / Competency (5 cities)

**Amundsen Station, Halley, Esperanza, Juan Carlos, Sejong**

All five are dutiful, analytical, and capable — service-oriented cities whose value comes from a function performed on behalf of others. The *kind* of duty differs sharply:

- **Amundsen Station** serves as neutral political mediation ground for the whole Federation, alongside literal technical maintenance of the Arcanet relay and Amundsen Tower — the only city on this list whose duty is explicitly cross-subnet and political-adjacent.
- **Halley** traded real technical power for naming honor — its own residents built Sanay's nexus by hand, a duty performed specifically *for* someone else's infrastructure.
- **Esperanza** is a logistics partner, splitting the Weddell Sea shipping corridor with Marambio — its duty is coordination within an equal partnership, not subordination to another city.
- **Juan Carlos** is an information keeper — Tepenia's first bureaucratic archive, tracking immigration and watching for former Upper Earth officials. Its duty is about security and institutional memory, not physical labor or logistics.
- **Sejong** facilitates border/customs processing, adjacent to the Machu Picchu Authority — a duty about smoothing other people's transit through the system.

**The distinguishing axis:** what kind of "on behalf of others" each city practices — political mediation (Amundsen), physical labor traded for credit (Halley), equal logistics partnership (Esperanza), information security (Juan Carlos), and administrative facilitation (Sejong).

## Group 3 — Instinctive / Assertive / Reactive (2 cities)

**Troll, Soyuz**

Both are forceful, contested, industrially significant cities — but the *direction* of the pressure is opposite:

- **Troll**'s intensity comes from outside pressure — its airfield is genuinely fought over, flagged as DLC 5's working central-conflict anchor. Troll is a city under real external siege over what it controls.
- **Soyuz**'s intensity comes from within — an unusually sharp national pride the developer explicitly flagged as an outlier against Tepenia's quieter post-national norm. Nobody is fighting Soyuz for anything; its reactivity is self-generated identity, not external contest.

**The distinguishing axis:** besieged (Troll) vs. self-asserting (Soyuz).

## Group 4 — Feeling / Compliant / Positive Outlook (4 cities)

**Cape Adare, Zukelli, Mawson, Bharati**

All four are warm, community-first, and optimistic — but each channels that warmth through a different primary medium:

- **Cape Adare** channels it through folk music and rural small-town intimacy — acoustic instruments, a slow pace, penguins as pets, a "big city, small-town feel."
- **Zukelli** channels it through dense, genre-diverse urban music and a tangled, lived-in cityscape — "a place with a soul," a warmth proven precisely because it was willing to shelter AWOL defectors at real cost.
- **Mawson** channels it through romantic hospitality specifically — Tepenia's honeymoon destination, warmth built around relationship and celebration rather than art or community texture.
- **Bharati** channels it through visual art, fashion, and civic pride — sculptures and murals as the actual built environment, an academic draw built on culture and soul rather than ranking.

**The distinguishing axis:** the medium of warmth — folk music/rural intimacy (Cape Adare), urban music/soulful risk-taking (Zukelli), romance/hospitality (Mawson), visual art/civic pride (Bharati).

## Group 5 — Thinking / Withdrawn / Competency (3 cities)

**Neumayer, Princess Elisabeth, Signy**

All three are technical, self-contained, and quietly capable, but the *specialty* differs:

- **Neumayer** specializes in modular, zero-emission engineering precision, positioned off Hwy 7's main line.
- **Princess Elisabeth** specializes in total off-grid energy self-sufficiency (wind/solar), its very subnet placement settled through careful traverse-corridor geographic analysis.
- **Signy** has no particular specialty at all — its withdrawal is just modest scale and simple isolation, the least distinguished of the three by design (and the thinnest on established lore).

**The distinguishing axis:** engineering precision (Neumayer) vs. energy self-sufficiency (Princess Elisabeth) vs. plain, unspecialized smallness (Signy).

## Group 6 — Instinctive / Compliant / Competency (2 cities)

**Lazar, Sayowa**

Both are physically functional, service-oriented cities, but at very different scales and roles:

- **Lazar** is a genuine megacity that absorbed and evolved through waves of immigration until even its own name changed — a highway *origin* point (Hwy 7's eastern terminus, the Belgrano Highway Extension's start).
- **Sayowa** is a dedicated industrial fabrication and dispatch city — a highway *service* node, moving other cities' goods through the network rather than originating routes of its own.

**The distinguishing axis:** origin point at megacity scale (Lazar) vs. mid-sized service/dispatch node (Sayowa).

## Group 7 — Feeling / Compliant / Reactive (2 cities)

**Belgrano, Janbogo**

Both carry real emotional weight tied to painful history, but the weight comes from different places:

- **Belgrano**'s reactivity is internal — a genuine identity split between its living Second Interwar culture and its post-war decline, the "Boneyard Times" loyalty faction mourning what the city used to be.
- **Janbogo**'s reactivity is externally imposed — deliberately left standing after Zukelli's destruction specifically to witness the strike and carry its deterrent message forward, a trauma inflicted from outside rather than a decline felt from within.

**The distinguishing axis:** internal decline/nostalgia (Belgrano) vs. externally imposed witness-trauma (Janbogo).

## Group 8 — Feeling / Assertive / Positive Outlook (3 cities)

**Dumont d'Urville, Casey, Palmer City**

All three are vibrant and culturally assertive, but at increasing scale:

- **Dumont d'Urville** is dense, small-scale intensity — a "New Orleans at 1/20th scale," live music packed into nearly every downtown eatery on a small island footprint.
- **Casey** is defined by a single iconic landmark — Splinters, Tepenia's largest and most famous bar, famous along an entire coastline rather than citywide density.
- **Palmer City** operates at national scale — Tepenia's actual cultural capital, an entertainment economy with all 43 master-list nations represented, carrying the highest possible historical stakes (the Long Night War's own origin).

**The distinguishing axis:** scale of cultural reach — dense-and-local (Dumont d'Urville), singular-and-regional (Casey), national-and-historic (Palmer City).

## Group 9 — Instinctive / Assertive / Competency (3 cities)

**Fort McMurdo, Marambio, Rothera**

All three are real logistics/economic powerhouses, but the *form* of their power differs:

- **Fort McMurdo**'s power is soft — presence and decision-making gravity, "de facto capital" status that's operational, not political, felt rather than physically moved.
- **Marambio**'s power is dual and physical — a genuine shipyard *and* one of only eight confirmed airports, moving actual goods and people at real volume.
- **Rothera**'s power is singular and technical — specifically its confirmed wheeled-aircraft-capable airport, one rare capability rather than a dual role.

**The distinguishing axis:** soft decision-making gravity (Fort McMurdo) vs. dual physical throughput (Marambio) vs. singular technical specialty (Rothera).

## Group 10 — Feeling / Withdrawn / Positive Outlook (2 cities)

**Scott, Port Lockroy**

Both are quiet, warm, and contented at small scale, but oriented toward different points in time:

- **Scott** is present-tense domestic warmth — "a genuinely decent, quiet place to raise a family," explicitly the calm counterpart to Fort McMurdo's loud energy next door.
- **Port Lockroy** is past-tense heritage warmth — a preserved historic site functioning as a memory-keeper, its fondness aimed backward at what the place once was rather than at ongoing family life.

**The distinguishing axis:** present-day domestic life (Scott) vs. historical/heritage memory-keeping (Port Lockroy).

## Group 11 — Thinking / Withdrawn / Positive Outlook (2 cities)

**Kunlun, Vostok**

Both are isolated, joyful research communities living the exact same "not a job, it's life" ethos — the closest match of any pair in this whole set, and already directly compared to each other in their own individual files. The distinguishing detail is built directly into their subject matter:

- **Kunlun** looks outward and upward — astronomy and communications, a towering antenna reaching toward the sky and toward orbit.
- **Vostok** looks inward and downward — genetics research built around Lake Vostok's ancient, subglacial biology, a mystery buried rather than broadcast.

**The distinguishing axis:** sky-facing (Kunlun) vs. ground-facing (Vostok) — the one true case where the Enneagram triple genuinely undersells just how similar two cities are, and the real distinction is almost entirely about direction of gaze rather than temperament.
