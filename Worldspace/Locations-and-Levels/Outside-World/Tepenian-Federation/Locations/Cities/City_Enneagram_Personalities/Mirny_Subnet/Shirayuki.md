## Major Theme: Feeling (Heart)

Shirayuki is the one Larsemann Hills city unmistakably centered on Feeling — image, identity, and
emotional/aesthetic connection — where its two neighbors both read as Gut-centered. Its defining visual fact is
art *as* civic identity: "sculptures, murals, statues, and other constructed pieces populate every district,"
turning the city into "an outdoor gallery as much as a functioning one," not incidental decoration but "the
clearest physical expression of the city's whole creative identity." Its fashion identity runs the same way —
Alternative/Alt-Rock/Alt-Grunge/Alt-Pop, genuinely homemade rather than bought polished off a rack, personal
self-expression over status or function.

Even the city's academic draw runs on Feeling-center logic rather than Thinking-center credentialism: all three
Larsemann Hills cities have genuinely strong schools, but Shirayuki's own flagship university — now confirmed
2026-07-30 as a large institution oriented around highly technical sub-fields of art and design — draws
students from across Tepenia "not primarily for academic ranking, but for the culture and soul of the place
itself." Students use it as a deliberate stepping stone into the city's actual creative life (the Ashiato
fashion scene, the Glacier-pop music scene) rather than treating the degree as the end in itself — a city whose
appeal is fundamentally emotional and aesthetic, not a Thinking-center appeal to competence or Gut-center appeal
to raw output the way Sinheung's university (materials, fabrication, production) is.

## Hornevian Group: Compliant

Shirayuki's founding story is itself a Compliant story in the purest sense: the city exists because the
International Court of Diplomacy at Jeju-do diplomatically allocated the unoccupied Bharati site to Japan,
specifically to prevent an uncontested third Chinese regional claim — the city was built, from its very
origin, to serve the broader Federation's needs. That's the textbook Compliant orientation (move toward others,
fulfill an obligation), the direct opposite of Zhongshan's self-sufficient Withdrawal or Sinheung's
outward-pushing Assertiveness. The city's own thematic identity names the arc explicitly: *"A Place Decided For
You, Made Into a Place You'd Choose."*

That same Compliant orientation carries into the present day. Cleanliness here isn't defensive or anxious — the
city's spec sheet frames it as "a matter of civic pride," collectively maintained care for shared space and for
whoever visits it. And Shirayuki is explicitly, repeatedly described as a place where "it's genuinely easy to
make friends" — a welcoming, low-friction orientation toward others sitting at the heart of Compliant energy.
Its social contract states this directly: the city's whole identity "depends on supporting varied personalities
and life paths... rather than one dominant civic type" — accommodation as design principle, not just default
courtesy.

## Harmonic Group: Positive Outlook

Shirayuki is, by a wide margin, the warmest of the three cities, and its Harmonic group reflects that directly.
The massive amateur art and music culture — confirmed as least as large a part of the city's identity as its
institutional side — exists "purely because people enjoy making things on their own time," not out of
competitive necessity or emotional reactivity. That same impulse produces the city's homemade Alt-Rock/
Alt-Grunge/Alt-Pop fashion sensibility (Section 11 of its own spec sheet notes it's the identical underlying
instinct, just expressed on the body instead of the canvas or the stage).

Even Shirayuki's relationship to its own imposed origin gets processed through this register: rather than
resenting a home it didn't choose, the city's founding tension is framed as needing "generations to feel
genuinely lived-in rather than simply assigned" — a slow, patient reframing toward belonging, not grievance.
Its counterculture is described as "modest" specifically because "a city this genuinely varied in the
personalities and pursuits it supports doesn't have the same need for organized counterculture that more
singularly-defined cities might" — contentment running deep enough that friction has little to attach to. Where
Sinheung would meet a challenge to its standing with visible intensity (Reactive) and Zhongshan would just
quietly absorb it (Competency), Shirayuki's entire civic temperament is built to meet circumstance — even an
imposed, undramatic founding — by finding what's genuinely good in it.

cities (Cape Adare, Zukelli, Mawson, Esperanza) — see `Distinguishing_Overlapping_Profiles.md`'s Group 4. The
distinguishing axis: each channels its warmth through a different primary medium. Shirayuki's is visual
art, fashion, and civic pride — "sculptures and murals as the actual built environment," an academic and
cultural draw built on soul rather than ranking, distinct from Cape Adare's folk-music rural intimacy,
Zukelli's dense urban music scene, Mawson's romantic hospitality, or Esperanza's founding compact of
guardianship.
