# Halley

*(See `City_Enneagram_Personalities/README.md` for the framework this applies.)*

## Major Theme: Thinking (Head)

Halley's defining fact is genuine, unresolved existential risk — it sits on a moving ice shelf, at real risk of eventually calving into the sea, and has been flagged as a candidate for a future permanent-loss path where the city is lost for good. Its established infrastructure choices are a direct, planned response to that risk: no fixed shipping docks and no airstrip, a deliberate design decision, since permanent structures would simply be carried away by the shelf's own motion. That's Head-center reasoning in its purest form — organizing the whole city around anticipating and adapting to a threat, not merely reacting to or enduring it.

## Hornevian Group: Compliant

Halley gives its name to the entire subnet, yet is explicitly *not* the subnet's technical nexus — that distinction, and the real strategic weight that comes with it, belongs to Sanay. Halley earned the naming honor by sending its own residents to do the direct, hands-on labor of physically building that nexus at Sanay, a genuine act of service performed for the good of the whole subnet rather than for Halley's own advantage. That's Compliant energy: contributing labor and effort toward a collective goal, and accepting a namesake role in place of actual control.

## Harmonic Group: Competency

Halley's no-docks/no-airstrip policy isn't dramatized or treated as a hardship to be endured — it's a genuinely smart, foresighted engineering response to a real physical constraint, handled with the same quiet capability found in the city's overland-only resupply arrangement via Belgrano or Sanay. There's no sign of anxious hand-wringing about the ice shelf's risk, nor forced optimism about it; Halley has simply built its infrastructure logic around the problem and gotten on with things.
