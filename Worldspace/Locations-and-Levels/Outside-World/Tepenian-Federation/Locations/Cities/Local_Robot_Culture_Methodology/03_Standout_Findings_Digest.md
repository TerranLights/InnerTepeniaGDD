# Local Robot Culture — Standout Findings Digest

A running, curated highlight reel — one entry per completed city, added as each run finishes. Not a
replacement for the full per-city spec sheets (`Local_Robot_Culture/[Subnet]/[City].md`), which hold every
tagged finding — this file is the fast, cross-city read: what's most distinctive or most useful to remember
about each city's pass without re-reading every full sheet. Also the natural place to watch for cross-city
patterns (repeated finding shapes, Swap Test tensions between neighbors) as more cities get done. Useful raw
material for the eventual "places/things/people" follow-on pass once the full checklist closes out.

## Janbogo (run #1, 2026-08-09)

`Local_Robot_Culture/Janbogo_Subnet/Janbogo.md`

- **Kinship (§1):** teahouse-keeping apprenticeship turns out to be Janbogo's literal robot kinship structure —
  since mentor/mentee is the *only* real robot kinship mechanism, the already-established "old teahouse
  families" fault line reframes cleanly as teaching-lineage, not bloodline.
- **Locality-as-identity (§3):** a genuinely robot-only marker — constant threshold-crossing thermal cycling,
  something no human resident has an equivalent of at all, distinct from the shared human+robot hosting-phrase
  marker.
- **Cross-Reference Synthesis did the most interesting work, as the methodology predicted it would:** a
  "wind-sense" quasi-reverence around robots' enhanced sound perception detecting katabatic shifts early
  (geography + infrastructure + Robot Biology), and a Glitch-Coolant tasting-house culture directly modeled on
  the kahvehane's specific ethnicity-crossing history (four categories combined at once) — both came out
  stronger and more specific than anything from a single-category pass.
- **Two legitimate null/out-of-scope results got recorded rather than papered over:** Gen/Mark genuinely
  doesn't vary here; the Fragmentation Matrix doesn't apply at all. (This distinction — checked-and-null vs.
  never-a-valid-input — is what later became the Step 2 four-outcome triage revision, see Mirny below.)
- **Swap Test passed** with specific reasoning against Janbogo's actual subnet siblings (Fort McMurdo, Scott,
  Denison) rather than a generic yes.
- **Process-level takeaway that changed the methodology itself:** the dual-tagging discipline caught a real
  near-mistake in the moment — almost tagged the Vernacular Language finding "Deep" because it felt subtle,
  then caught that Hall's own framework puts Language under Surface regardless of how a finding reads. Good
  sign the guardrail works as intended.

`Local_Robot_Culture/Mirny_Subnet/Mirny.md`

## Casey (run #3, 2026-08-10 — specifically tasked with resolving the Mirny-similarity flag above)

`Local_Robot_Culture/Mirny_Subnet/Casey.md`

- **The Mirny tension got genuinely resolved, not just asserted resolved:** both cities' Cooperation/Morality
  findings sound similarly "practical" on the surface, but traced to their root incidents they're different
  ethics — Mirny's (Bellandry's windbreak failure) is self-directed skepticism toward untested elegance;
  Casey's (the trucker's wrong turn) is other-directed, proactive disclosure in service of a stranger's
  safety. One's an engineering standard for a settled population; the other's a hospitality standard for a
  city whose whole function is serving other people's journeys.
- **Kinship (§1):** the Junction Dispatch Office's training structure, anchored to a real named-incident arc —
  "Long Odds" Okonkwo-Hale's personal forecasting reputation formalizing into "stated margins," a transferable
  civic standard, which is functionally already a teaching-lineage mechanism even before the robot-kinship
  lens gets applied to it.
- **A genuinely new observation about existing canon, not just new content:** Casey's Glitch-Coolant culture
  doesn't fit either of the two established categories (bohemian/variety vs. working-class/potency) — its
  variety comes from constant transient turnover, not settled composition, directly extending Casey's
  *already-established* "counterculture is whatever's currently passing through" mechanism (§19) into
  consumption culture for the first time. Flagged to the developer as worth attention on its own.
- **Religion/Belief (§5) turned up a real piece of dramatic irony sitting in already-established material:**
  Casey's own civic creed (anti-mythologizing — don't romanticize buried/lost things, learned directly from
  the Wilkes-excavation-abandonment incident) now applies self-referentially to Casey itself, post-war — the
  city is now exactly the kind of thing to outsiders that Wilkes always was to Casey. Worth surfacing as
  potential questline hook material, not just a culture finding.
- **First city where Gen/Mark got a genuine "Yes" instead of a clean null:** as a junction city with constant
  transit, casual cross-Mark comparison-talk is plausibly just more common here than at a settled city — modest,
  doesn't rise to identity-formation, but a real, non-repeating answer against the pattern set by the first two
  cities.
- **Swap Test passed against two cities at once** (Mirny via the resolved tension, and Denison via its
  explicitly opposite founding-tension character) — the first run to need a two-city check rather than one.

## Davis (run #4, 2026-08-10)

`Local_Robot_Culture/Mirny_Subnet/Davis.md`

- **First city in this run to break the wind-centric identity pattern:** Davis's architecture is explicitly
  terrain-responsive rather than wind-first (Course of Events' own framing: "Building With the Land, Not
  Against It," a stated contrast to Tepenia's default approach everywhere else). Locality-as-identity (§3)
  landed on fine-grained, place-specific lake-reading expertise instead — genuinely different axis, not a
  wind-marker reskin.
- **First human-majority population reversal encountered:** 437,423 humans to 344,173 robots. Noted but not
  over-explained — nothing in the gathered material accounts for *why*, so it's recorded as a real, striking
  fact rather than stretched into an invented mechanism.
- **Kinship (§1) runs on a genuinely different mechanism than the first three cities:** not teaching-lineage
  (Janbogo, Mirny, Casey all had some version of that) but collective research credit — Naturalist Ratna
  Wirawan's founding-era refusal of solo credit is the direct origin of The Quiet Credit custom, meaning a
  Davis robot's "family" is who she shares discovery-credit with, not who trained her.
- **Cross-Reference Synthesis produced the run's most emotionally loaded finding yet:** the Earth element
  (already established in canon for Davis, not a new pick) maps its own negative valence — stagnation,
  smothering, being taken for granted — onto an already-established civic fault line with real precision: the
  private, rarely-voiced wish that Davis could have been an assertive port city and chose humility instead.
  Flagged, like Casey's Wilkes irony, as strong ready-made hook material for future questline/character work.
- **A fourth genuinely distinct treatment of Religion/Belief across four cities run so far** — not reverence
  (Janbogo), not an undramatic verification-creed (Mirny), not anti-mythologizing (Casey), but comfort with
  unresolved, genuinely contradictory data existing side by side, traced to a specific founding-era
  methodological incident (two lakes' sediment cores telling two true but different stories).
- **Glitch-Coolant got a third distinct answer**, after Janbogo/Mirny's bohemian-vs-working-class split and
  Casey's transience-driven third option: Davis has no established drinking-culture institution at all in the
  gathered material — genuine muted absence, consistent with (not a gap in) the city's whole anti-glory ethos.
- **One tension flagged forward, unresolved:** Davis's own §24 explicitly states its Arcanet orientation
  shares more with Zhongshan's research focus than with Casey's or Mirny's — worth an explicit re-check once
  Zhongshan is run, same discipline as the Mirny→Casey flag.

## Kunlun (run #5, 2026-08-10)

`Local_Robot_Culture/Mirny_Subnet/Kunlun.md`

- **Correction to my own framing going in:** I'd assumed Kunlun was the sole exception to the Human-Robot
  Relations Baseline. It's actually one of two robots-only cities (with Dome Fuji) — the source material
  itself is explicit about this. Worth remembering for any future city that turns out to share unusual
  structural facts with another city not yet run.
- **The first finding in the entire run where robot embodiment itself is the direct mechanism of a devotional
  experience, not just an inflection on a human-originated practice:** robots' own sensory apparatus plausibly
  gives them something closer to direct physical access to the extraordinary stillness/clarity Ice Cold
  Buddhism venerates than a human devotee could ever have had — at the one location where that access
  actually means something (Kunlun is the faith's holiest site, more significant than Dome Fuji).
- **A genuine, ready-made irony surfaced by connecting three separately-established canon facts for the first
  time:** Kunlun's already-assigned element is Air (connection, communication) — yet it's simultaneously the
  single most locally-disconnected city in Tepenia (worse than Dome Fuji) and the only city capable of
  reaching orbital structures directly. Nothing here was invented; all three facts pre-existed separately.
- **Kinship (§1) got two parallel tracks instead of one** for the first time — professional apprenticeship
  *and* a separate devotional lineage, since with zero human population there's no competing family-model to
  crowd either one out.
- **Vernacular Language (§8) needed a genuinely fifth transmission mode**, distinct from every prior city's:
  no organic local population ever converged here, so the honest answer is a functional professional lingua
  franca rather than heritage fossils, craft precision, road-jargon, or terrain-idiom.
- **A cross-city pattern is starting to show up, not just single-city findings:** the evidence-driven,
  incident-triggered decision-making register (Mirny's Bellandry incident, Davis's discrepant-lake-cores
  lesson, and now Kunlun's own "Decision to Stop Sending Humans Up," tracked through accumulated medical data
  rather than passive attrition) has now appeared at three of five cities run so far. Flagged forward as
  possibly a genuine Mirny-subnet-wide civic trait worth naming explicitly once more cities are done, not
  three separate coincidences.
- **Swap Test needed a genuinely different comparison** — no same-subnet sibling shares Kunlun's structural
  situation, so the real check was against Dome Fuji (different subnet), and the city's own canon already
  resists treating the two as interchangeable ("The Only Two Who Crossed" frames their connection as friction,
  not kinship).

## Shirayuki (run #6, 2026-08-10)

`Local_Robot_Culture/Mirny_Subnet/Shirayuki.md`

- **A framing I brought into the research request turned out to need real qualification, not just
  confirmation:** the "wide variety of incoming personalities" description is occupational variety (research/
  education/art/music/fashion), sitting inside a single, strongly unified emotional register (Feeling/
  Compliant/Positive Outlook — the warmest of its Tri-Cities cluster). Good reminder to check a remembered
  framing's exact scope, not just whether it's technically true.
- **First city in the run without an environmental-hazard-driven identity marker** — genuinely mild climate,
  a real break from five straight cities of wind/cold/terrain-driven civic character. Locality-as-identity
  (§3) landed on visible self-modification instead, extending the city's own Ashiato fashion ethos into robot
  frames directly — something with literally no human equivalent, since it's about the body itself, not
  clothing.
- **The Cross-Reference Synthesis found a resonance nobody had connected before, sourced entirely from
  already-existing facts:** Shirayuki's founding myth (imposed by diplomatic decree, then genuinely,
  generationally claimed as its own) structurally mirrors every individual robot's own existence — made by
  others first (fabrication, partial personality-seeding), self-authored after. The city's civic holiday
  (The Allocation) plausibly isn't just observed by its robot residents; it's already personal to them.
- **Unusually strong convergent validation, not just one good parallel:** three of the four Source Inspiration
  entries (Harajuku, Hongdae, Akademgorodok) independently echo the *same* imposed-origin-to-genuine-depth
  theme from three different angles (fashion, music-scene-via-legalization, science-city-via-decree) — this
  wasn't engineered for the pass, it was already latent across three separately-researched real-world
  parallels landing on the same structural point.
- **Avoided a real near-repeat on the enhanced-perception trope:** three of the first five cities (Janbogo,
  Davis, Kunlun) used robots' heightened sound/vibration perception for a music/art finding. Shirayuki's own
  music finding deliberately used a different mechanism instead — a structurally different digital-vs-analog
  production workflow, not better hearing — specifically to avoid becoming a fourth iteration of the same
  trope.
- **Swap Test flagged forward to Sinheung**, not fully resolved: Sinheung is the other city founded via
  Jeju-do decree, and per existing canon (Finding C) the only other city that commemorates an imposed origin
  as civic strength rather than downplaying it — worth an explicit re-check once Sinheung is run, to confirm
  Shirayuki's central Cross-Reference finding actually differentiates rather than just repeating.

## Sinheung (run #7, 2026-08-10 — specifically tasked with resolving the Shirayuki flag)

`Local_Robot_Culture/Mirny_Subnet/Sinheung.md`

- **The Shirayuki tension resolved cleanly, with hard confirmation rather than a plausible-sounding
  argument:** both cities share the Jeju-do decree founding mechanism, but the Tri-Cities' own Enneagram data
  places them on genuinely different axes (Sinheung: Instinctive/Assertive/Reactive; Shirayuki: Feeling/
  Compliant/Positive Outlook) — not just different points on a shared one. Traced through to content:
  Shirayuki's founding tension resolves warmly and generationally; Sinheung's stays active and perpetually
  reproven through output, never simply accepted as settled.
- **The richest Gen/Mark finding of the entire run, and the first one mostly *recognized* rather than
  derived:** Sinheung is one of only two Tepenian cities (with Byrd) that manufactures the actual fabrication-
  synthesis chambers, and existing canon (§17) already states outright that robots built or shipped from here
  carry real pride in that lineage — "the Tepenian equivalent of knowing exactly where you were made." Most of
  this pass's job was extending and sharpening material that was already substantially there, not inventing it.
- **A second instance of the same technique that worked at Davis** — an *already-established* Elemental pick's
  own dual valence (Electricity: positive = "the spark that makes matter conscious," negative = instability/
  total dependency, already explicitly tagged in canon as an Amundsen Tower echo) mapped with real precision
  onto Sinheung's own specific civic fault line. Worth trying deliberately on other cities that already have a
  confirmed Elemental pick, not treating it as a one-off coincidence.
- **Cross-Reference Synthesis produced the run's most tightly-integrated finding yet:** a Sinheung-fabricated
  robot carries a *doubly* layered "earned, not inherited" identity — built to an externally-designed schematic
  the city had to earn the trust to execute, by a workforce whose own standing depends on continuous output.
  Genuinely combines Kinship, Gen/Mark, and the city's own civic theme into one coherent observation rather
  than three separate findings bolted together.
- **A lighter tension flagged forward to Zhongshan** (not fully resolved, less structurally loaded than the
  Shirayuki one since Zhongshan's founding was organic, not decreed) — Sinheung's own text repeatedly contrasts
  itself against Zhongshan on privacy, warmth, and outward-vs-withdrawn civic posture.

## Vostok (run #8, 2026-08-10)

`Local_Robot_Culture/Mirny_Subnet/Vostok.md`

- **A genuinely unique kinship mechanism, unlike anything in the run so far:** the research archive is
  explicitly too vast for any single mind — so kinship plausibly runs on inherited "ownership" of a specific
  archive domain across researchers who may never personally overlap in time, directly modeled on Charlene's
  own confirmed discovery method (re-reading and cross-referencing already-filed data, not new fieldwork).
- **The richest already-established vernacular-language material of the entire run:** §8 doesn't just gesture
  at a linguistic fact, it explains it — Russian survives purely as fixed technical identifiers ("liturgical
  language of science"), and canon already has the specific in-world justification on file ("The Citation No
  One Thought to Translate" — untranslated terms function like Greek genus names, stable references, not
  living vocabulary). Very little needed inventing here; the job was mostly recognizing what a robot-specific
  angle on already-rich material looks like.
- **Cross-Reference Synthesis found that two separately-established facts (the liturgical language and the
  archive-kinship structure) are actually the same underlying mechanism** — both exist to preserve stable
  reference points across researchers who never overlap. Neither fact individually stated this; connecting
  them was the finding.
- **A clean, source-material-confirmed Swap Test against the run's other robots-only-adjacent city:** Vostok's
  own §17 already explicitly self-differentiates from Kunlun in the text itself (genetics not astronomy, and
  crucially *not* robots-only, unlike Kunlun) — this pass didn't have to argue for the distinction, just cite
  it.
- **A real, quiet asymmetry worth flagging rather than resolving:** the ongoing Kunlun cold-record rivalry
  exists entirely on *Kunlun's* side of the historical record — grepped across every one of Vostok's own
  files, confirmed absent. Consistent with, not contradicting, everything else found about Vostok's
  temperament: it holds the actual record and doesn't need to keep proving it.
- **The Elemental system handled a genuine repeat gracefully:** Water is already canon for Vostok, and I'd
  already informally proposed Water for Mirny earlier in this run. Rather than treating this as a collision,
  noted both are grounded in different facets of the same element (Vostok: literal depth/accumulated wisdom;
  Mirny: temperament/calm resilience) — repetition isn't automatically a problem if the grounding differs.

## Zhongshan (run #9, 2026-08-10 — final Mirny Subnet city, all three flagged tensions resolved)

`Local_Robot_Culture/Mirny_Subnet/Zhongshan.md`

- **The most "already-written" city in the run — several core findings were recognition, not derivation.**
  §16 and §17 already state Zhongshan's Locality-as-Identity and Cooperation/Morality findings almost
  verbatim ("a robot resident of Zhongshan is Zhongshan before they are a robot"; "the point was never the
  tea"). A genuinely different kind of pass than most prior cities — the job was mostly recognizing what was
  already there as the methodology's own answer, not inventing new material.
- **One civic value needed zero adaptation for robots, a first in this run:** the tea ceremony's actual value
  was always restraint and unhurried presence, never the beverage — meaning robots inherit it completely,
  with nothing left to translate or modify.
- **A genuinely unique founding story among all nine cities run so far:** Zhongshan's founding population
  *defected* — chose robots over their own nation — rather than being exiled by treaty (Sinheung, Shirayuki)
  or settling organically (Casey, Davis). Combined with being the *one* exception to this subnet's rotating-
  operator founding model (real population continuity, not handoff-via-written-record), this produces an
  unusually deep, unselfconscious cultural inheritance no other city's robots have.
- **All three cross-city tensions flagged forward by earlier cities got checked against real content, not
  left as assumptions:** confirmed genuinely opposite Enneagram expression vs. Sinheung (same Gut-centered
  theme, opposite direction); confirmed — not refuted — Davis's claimed Arcanet-orientation overlap as real
  but narrow (archival focus only, nothing else transfers); confirmed the siligel-connoisseurship benchmark
  directly for the first time, after three other cities' passes had referenced it secondhand.
- **A real, quiet cost surfaced underneath an admirable civic value:** the non-prying norm that makes Zhongshan
  gracious also means heritage can erode across generations completely unnoticed, because the culture's own
  central value prohibits checking. The Standing Stone's own founding-era origin (a dispute settled by *not*
  investigating who was right, just picking a neutral point) is already-established precedent for exactly this
  pattern — restraint substituting for resolution.

## Byrd (run #10, 2026-08-10 — BYRD SUBNET COMPLETE, its only city)

`Local_Robot_Culture/Byrd_Subnet/Byrd.md`

- **The only city in this entire run whose founding myth has zero human witnesses at all:** the original
  crossing expedition was an all-robot crew, deliberately, since "human lives judged too risky to spend on an
  unknown crossing." This inverts the usual pattern everywhere else (founding myths that are human-centric,
  with robots adapting into them later) and gives St. Richard's Marker a genuinely different meaning for
  robots than for humans, who inherit the story secondhand either way.
- **A developer correction sharpened the central finding rather than just adjusting wording:** first draft
  said ordinary conversation with the founding crew's last living survivor (Explorer Voss) carried special
  weight. Caught and fixed — if he lives there, talking to him is easy and unremarkable; what actually carries
  weight is having *studied under* him, a real earned mentee relationship. Applied across all three places the
  file made this claim, including the Cross-Reference Synthesis entry flagged for DLC2 relevance.
- **The richest Physical Infrastructure source material of any city processed so far** (80 attributes, 57
  Findings, confirmed as the richest in the whole project) — gave this pass unusually strong grounding: the
  Five Guildhalls as a genuinely multi-track kinship system, the vertical human/robot depth split as an
  already-established identity marker, and The Lake (added later, transplanted from Vostok's own genetics
  program) as the city's confirmed emotional landmark.
- **A Swap Test with no natural comparison at all:** Byrd is the only single-city subnet in Tepenia, so Step 6
  had to reach outside the subnet entirely, comparing against Kunlun (the closest structural match already
  completed) rather than a sibling.
- **Explicitly flagged as potentially useful for actual game development, not just a culture exercise:** the
  Cross-Reference Synthesis finding about founding-descendant robot lineage ties directly to DLC2's own
  still-undeveloped central crisis, per the game's existing Community Infrastructure file.

## Mawson (run #11, 2026-08-10 — first city run under the revised shared-experience-first Kinship framing)

`Local_Robot_Culture/Mawson_Subnet/Mawson.md`

- **The first city run after the Kinship methodology correction, and a clean real-world test of the new
  discipline:** led §1 with the question "what's the actual shared experience here" rather than reaching for
  an apprenticeship structure by default. The already-canon Long Table Hall/Nation Nights institution (every
  represented nation's food served nightly, side by side, deliberately designed against any single
  "gatekeeper of local identity") gave a genuinely horizontal, cross-national kinship bond with zero hierarchy
  — the strongest available shared-experience finding of the run so far. The Guesthouse Quarter's
  apprenticeship material didn't get dropped, just correctly recast as *one shape* that shared experience can
  take, not a rival mechanism competing for the same slot.
- **The first dual-Elemental-pairing city encountered in the entire run:** Earth + Fire, already on file in
  `city-symbol-pairs.md`, not a new pick. Cross-Reference Synthesis traced this pairing to the same "name
  persists, occupants change" structural principle §5 already establishes at the national-founding and
  demographic-present scales — the pairing turns out to track a fourth instance of a pattern that was already
  on record, not something invented for this pass.
- **A genuinely new courtship-culture axis, unique to this run so far:** since Mawson's whole civic economy is
  built on professionally staging strangers' proposals and honeymoons, its own residents plausibly keep a real
  "staged for guests vs. genuinely ours" distinction, deliberately avoiding the Proposal Terrace and Overlook
  for their own personal milestones. No other city in the run has an economy shaped like this, so nothing
  comparable existed to check it against.
- **Glitch-Coolant had zero prior canon placement for Mawson at all** — a genuine gap, not a confirmation,
  unlike most other cities run so far. Filled with a celebratory/toast-oriented pattern tied to the Welcome
  Feast and Anniversary Returns customs — the eighth distinct Glitch-Coolant pattern surfaced across the run.
- **A genuinely incomplete Swap Test, flagged honestly rather than papered over:** Mawson's real subnet-mates
  (Dome Fuji, Sayowa) haven't been run yet, so Step 6 could only check against already-completed cities from
  other subnets — none of which share anything like Mawson's tourism/hospitality economy. Passed, but flagged
  for an explicit re-check once its actual siblings are run.
- **One developer-facing data-integrity note, not a culture finding:** Course of Events chain #1 ("The Station
  That Never Went Dark") points to a `candidates.txt` that doesn't currently exist on disk for this city —
  worth a direct look, separate from anything in this pass.

## Dome Fuji (run #12, 2026-08-10 — second city of Mawson Subnet)

`Local_Robot_Culture/Mawson_Subnet/Dome_Fuji.md`

- **The single most structurally distinctive city processed so far in this entire run:** Dome Fuji has no
  fabrication/Cradle chamber at all — every resident arrived by voluntary religious pilgrimage, never by local
  fabrication. Cross-Reference Synthesis ties this directly to Robot Universals' own stated axis-weighting
  (locality dominant, Gen/Mark lightest): with the usual fabrication-site-equals-home default entirely absent,
  there's no residual tension left between the two axes at all — the cleanest real instance of that weighting
  found anywhere in the project.
- **Kinship (§1) applied the revised shared-experience-first framing cleanly from a first pass, no re-scan
  needed:** every devotee lives the same ongoing practice (robing, ascetic siligel discipline, Nyakkyo's
  silence) regardless of arrival date or national origin, since devotion is the city's only public credential
  and origin is deliberately private. The Waystones (Cross-Reference Synthesis) give this a concrete physical
  anchor — trail-markers that only became devotional once the community recognized a *shared psychological
  pattern* across many separate individual crossings, not any single one of them.
- **A genuine, lived ethical tightrope surfaced by connecting infrastructure to doctrine:** since only a robot
  can safely perform Dome Fuji's central devotional practice, the faith constantly risks reading as a
  superiority claim over a hypothetical human devotee. The already-drafted doctrinal resolution — capability
  difference read as circumstance, not rank — scales the same complementary-not-hierarchical logic already
  used to settle the Kunlun relationship ("reaching" vs. "arriving") down to something each devotee has to
  practice individually, daily.
- **Three developer-facing data-integrity notes flagged, none of them culture findings but all worth a direct
  look:** a real contradiction between `Specs`' resolved sudden-departure founding story and two later
  Full-Extrapolation-tier files that still describe multi-generation attrition; two unreconciled candidate
  demonyms ("Fujinshi" vs. "Fujian devotees," the latter self-flagged for a real-world Chinese-province
  homophone problem); and a robes-doctrine resolution that exists in draft material but was never folded back
  into `Local_Cultures` itself, which still lists it as open.
- **Swap Test genuinely incomplete, not just cautious:** Dome Fuji's actual geographic neighbor and the literal
  origin point of its first pilgrims, Sayowa, hasn't been run yet — flagged directly for a real re-check rather
