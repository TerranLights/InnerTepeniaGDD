# 1 · 🔬 THE PHYSICAL RULES — why Antarctica has almost no harbors

***Read this before assigning a port to anything.*** **Four constraints decide the whole map.**

### 1.1 There is essentially no permanent harbor infrastructure

**The largest station on the continent ran for fifty years on an ICE PIER** — seawater pumped into a contained
area and frozen in layers, cabled back to shore. **Design life 3–7 years; after 2009, one year.** It has since
been replaced by a floating barge pier, and elsewhere a **65-ton floating Modular Causeway System** is
assembled on a ship's deck and floated into place. **Icebreakers cut a channel through fast ice so vessels can
reach the ice edge at all.**

> ## ⭐⭐ SO AN ANTARCTIC PORT IS NOT A PLACE. **It is a recurring act of construction** —
> **built partly from the sea it serves, and expected to fail.**

### 1.2 ⛔ You cannot carve your way to bedrock

**The obvious fix — excavate the ice margin so water reaches rock — fails on two counts:**

| Problem | Why |
|---|---|
| **The rock is too deep** | **Antarctic bedrock is isostatically depressed by the ice's own weight** *(the Byrd Subglacial Basin lies **2,538 m below sea level**; without its ice, Antarctica may be the lowest-lying continent)*, **and glacial erosion OVERDEEPENS** — valleys cut *"hundreds of meters below the lowest continuous surface line."* ***Carving to bedrock at a margin yields a flooded trough, not a quay: the rock you wanted is now hundreds of meters underwater.*** **You would have built a fjord** |
| **Or the rock is too high** | **Where bedrock stands ABOVE sea level — a nunatak — water cannot climb to it.** *See Sanay, §5* |

> ### ⭐ THE RESULT, STATED ONCE
> ***The carve is redundant where it is possible, and useless where it is needed.*** **Bedrock is shallow
> enough to be useful exactly where it already breaks the surface** — **nunataks, oases, islands** — *which
> are the places that already have a shoreline.*

### 1.3 What you carve does not stay carved

- **Ice flows back.** *Halley's shelf moves **400–700 m/yr** — a cut channel closes.*
- **Open water re-freezes** as fast ice, annually.

***So a carved harbor is not a structure. It is a channel that must be re-cut, forever*** — **which returns
you to §1.1's recurring cost, with a larger number.**

### 1.4 ⭐⭐ Unless the heat is not yours to pay for

**Two natural exemptions exist, and Tepenia has both:**

| Exemption | What it is |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **GEOTHERMAL** | **volcanic heat holds water open at no running cost.** ***See `Geothermal_Heating.md`*** |
| ⭐⭐ **POLYNYA** | **wind holds water open at no running cost.** ***See §4 — Janbogo*** |

# 2 · ⚠ THE PORT TYPOLOGY — FOUR TIERS *(proposal)*

**Every Tepenian port belongs to exactly one tier, and the tier decides what it costs and what can kill it.**

| Tier | Held open by | Running cost | Killed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **GEOLOGICAL** | **rock — arms, shelter, a sheltered anchorage** | **none** | *nothing short of geology* |
| ⭐⭐ **NATURAL-THERMAL / WIND** | **volcanic heat, or katabatic scour** | **none** | **the source stopping** *(a calving ice tongue; a dormant vent)* |
| ⭐⭐ **ICEPORT** *(added 2026-09-05)* | **a natural bay in the ICE FRONT itself** — not rock, not heat, not wind | **none** | ⚠ **the shelf it is cut into, moving** |
| **CONSTRUCTED** | **labor — ice piers, re-cut channels, seasonal offload** | ⛔ **paid every year, forever** | **a year nobody pays it** |

> ### ⭐⭐ THE FOURTH TIER — **ICEPORT.** *Missed on the first pass; the typology had only three.*
> 🔬 ***"Atka Iceport… an iceport about 16 km long and wide, marking a more-or-less permanent indentation in
> the front of the Ekström Ice Shelf."***
>
> **An iceport is a harbor made of the ice sheet's own geometry** — **a bay in the shelf front, sheltered,
> deep, and free.** ⭐ **It needs no rock, no volcano, no wind and no labor.**
>
> ⚠ **But it sits in moving ice** *(that shelf carries Neumayer seaward at ~157 m/yr)*, ***so an iceport
> MIGRATES, and on a long enough timescale it calves away entirely.*** **It is the only tier that is
> free today and temporary by construction** — *a harbor with an expiry date nobody alive will see.*

> ## ⚠⚠ THE CONSEQUENCE THIS TYPOLOGY EXISTS FOR — **PROPOSAL, needs a ruling**
> **If any Tepenian port was held open by GRID energy rather than by geology, geothermal heat, or wind, then
> when Amundsen Tower fell in the Long Night War, that port froze shut.**
>
> ***Not destroyed. Closed.*** **No battle damage, no attack — the energy stopped and the sea took the harbor
> back.** ⭐ **This would split the post-war map cleanly: cities whose ports were made of ROCK, HEAT or WIND
> kept them; cities whose ports were made of ELECTRICITY did not.**
>
> ⛔ **Not adopted. It requires a developer ruling on whether any grid-heated harbor ever existed** — *and if
> the answer is "none," the mechanism simply does not fire, which is also a clean outcome.*

# 4 · ⭐⭐ JANBOGO — a functional year-round maritime port

> ## ✅ DEVELOPER RULING, 2026-09-05
> ***"the city of Janbogo also has a functional year-round maritime port (which needs to be covered from the
> wind somehow through some sort of 'semi-dome' structural engineering)."***

## 🔬 Why this is the best-supported year-round port site on the continent

**Terra Nova Bay holds a POLYNYA — a pocket of open water that persists through most of the winter while the
rest of the Antarctic coast is frozen.** It is **one of the few stretches of Antarctic coast often ice-free in
winter.** ***Two things make it:***

| Mechanism | Effect |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Extreme katabatic winds** draining off the Transantarctic Mountains | **drive the sea ice eastward, scouring the bay clear** |
| ⭐⭐ **The Drygalski Ice Tongue** | **blocks ice from drifting back in** — the dominant drift is northward, and the tongue is in the way |

> ## ⭐⭐⭐ SO JANBOGO'S PORT IS NOT AN ENGINEERING ACHIEVEMENT. **IT IS A SITE.**
> ***The wind that makes the place brutal is the same wind that keeps the water open.***

## ⛔⛔ THE SEMI-DOME'S BINDING CONSTRAINT — **it cannot enclose the bay**

**If the structure blocks katabatic flow across the water, it kills the polynya and the bay freezes.**
***The wind IS the port.***

**So the semi-dome shelters the WORKING SURFACE — quays, cranes, cargo, people — and leaves the water open to
be scoured.** ⭐ **Backed against the land, open to seaward. More a grandstand over the docks than a roof over
the harbor.**

> ### ⭐ And that gives the place a permanent sensory fact
> **The wind is always present at the dome's mouth.** ***The port is never quiet, and the shelter has a loud
> edge you cross to reach the ships.*** *A worker's day is divided by that threshold.*

## ⭐ The harbor's second arm is made of ice

**Mawson's Horseshoe Harbor has two arms of rock** *(§5)*. **Janbogo's has one — and it is the Drygalski Ice
Tongue.**

> ⚠⚠ **AN ICE TONGUE CAN CALVE.** ***Janbogo's port depends on a floating structure the city does not
> control and cannot repair.*** **That rhymes with Halley's dependence on Belgrano and Sanay — except
> geological instead of political**, *and unlike Halley's arrangement there is no second supplier to fall back
> on.*

## How the two systems divide the labor

| System | Solves |
|---|---|
| **The semi-dome** | ⭐ **WIND** |
| **The geothermal loop** *(`Geothermal_Heating.md`)* | ⭐ **ICE** — *heated quays and loading aprons do not ice up* |

***Neither alone makes the port work.***

## ✅ ZUKELLI SHARES THIS PORT — developer ruling, 2026-09-05

> ***"Janbogo and Zukelli both do share a port. Them being 8 km apart, there's no reason for them not to."***

⭐⭐ **So the Terra Nova Bay pair shares THREE pieces of infrastructure: one airport, one heat source, and one
port.** *(`Airports.md` — "Zukelli/Janbogo Airport… the two cities are ~8km apart and share one airport
between them"; `Geothermal_Heating.md` — Mount Melbourne.)*

> ### ⭐ THAT IS THE MOST TIGHTLY COUPLED CITY PAIR IN TEPENIA
> **Nothing else in the corpus shares three separate infrastructure systems.** ***Air, heat and sea — every
> way in or out of that bay, and the warmth of both cities, are held in common.***
> ⚠ **And the coupling is asymmetric in consequence: Zukelli was DESTROYED in the war and Janbogo was
> "damaged; partially operational."** *Whatever happened to the shared infrastructure happened to it once, and
> the two cities did not survive it equally.*

## ⛔ 5.2 Canon NO-PORT — Halley, and the reasoning is worth keeping

> ✅ **`Specs/Halley.md`:** *"Halley itself has no shipping docks and no airstrip — a deliberate design
> choice, not an oversight: a city whose whole architecture exists because the ground itself moves at
> 400-700m/year would gain nothing from fixed maritime or aviation infrastructure that the ice would simply
> carry away."*

**All resupply arrives overland** — ships reach **Belgrano or Sanay**, whichever passage is open, and goods are
trucked in via the connector to **Hwy 7**.

> ### ⭐⭐ THE APPARENT CONTRADICTION, AND WHY THERE ISN'T ONE
> **Belgrano's ground moves too — so how does it hold a port when Halley cannot?** ***Because §1.1 says a
> port here is not a structure but a recurring act.*** **Halley's own founding argument was never "a harbor is
> impossible." It was that the RECURRING COST was not worth paying** — *its `Course_of_Events` records the
> losing side proposing exactly "a small, periodically-rebuilt dock, accepted as a recurring cost."*
>
> ⭐ **Belgrano and Sanay pay that cost. Halley declined to.** ***Two cities on the same moving coast made
> opposite calls on the same question, and both were right for themselves.*** **No retcon required.**
>
> ⚠ **And it left Halley uniquely un-exposed:** *a city with no harbor cannot have one switched off.*

## ⭐ 5.3 Mawson — the only natural rock harbor

⭐⭐ **This is the single most valuable piece of maritime geography in Tepenia, it belongs to a chartered city,

## ✅ 5.4 SANAY — **RESOLVED 2026-09-05. The city is inland; the port is at the coast.**

> ## ✅ DEVELOPER RULING, verbatim
> ***"the city is inland (where it's able to be built in a location that's over bedrock), and the port is
> geographically separate, situated at the coast, but jurisdictionally belongs to Sanay."***

⭐ **Sanay is a port city the way an inland rail hub is a freight city: the goods move through it, and the
water is somewhere else.** **The city stands inland because that is where bedrock is** *(Vesleskarvet nunatak —
the same stability that brought the subnet's Arcanet nexus here)*. **Its port is a separate coastal facility
under Sanay's jurisdiction, and the freight moves overland between them.**

⛔ **Nothing is carved, dredged or channeled inland** — *the nunatak stands well above sea level and the
intervening ground is ice sheet. See §1.2.*

**This is what the truckyards, warehouses, clifftop trucking and shipping depots and the import-export
business district are FOR** — and why dock crews rotate against freighter schedules rather than a clock:
***a shift at the port means travelling out to the coast for an offload and convoying back.***

> ### ⭐⭐ AND IT MAKES THE HALLEY SUBNET COHERE
> **Halley trucks its freight overland from Belgrano or Sanay. Sanay trucks its own freight overland from its
> own coastal port.** ***Nobody in this subnet has a dock at their door.*** **Halley's "no harbor by design"
> stops being an eccentricity and becomes the subnet's normal condition taken to its logical end.**

⭐ **One geological feature, two opposite consequences: the bedrock that makes Sanay stable is the same fact
that puts its harbor somewhere else.**

✅ **Applied to `Specs/Sanay.md` § Geographic Basis, 2026-09-05.**

| Fix | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Carve a channel to Sanay** | ⛔ **DEAD** — 160 km of ice sheet *and* 850 m of climb. **Seawater does not go uphill** |
| **Thermal basin at Sanay** | ⛔ **DEAD** — heat does not lift water either |
| ⭐ **The port is Sanay's, but is not AT Sanay** | ✅ **The only physically coherent option** |

> ✅ **ADOPTED — see §5.4 above.** *The third option was ruled by the developer on 2026-09-05 and the

| City | Port | 🔬 What is actually there | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **SIGNY** | **Borge Bay — Factory Cove** | **A large, irregularly-shaped bay dominating the east side of the island, with Factory Cove inside it.** ⭐ **A shore whaling station operated at Factory Cove until 1925–26** — *an industrial harbor, not merely an anchorage.* **There is a jetty with slipway, the preferred landing point.** *The site was chosen partly for nearby freshwater lakes* | ⭐ **GEOLOGICAL** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE FINDING — **these harbors are older than Antarctic science**

***All three are sealer- and whaler-era anchorages, and so is Port Lockroy*** *(§5.6, "a working sealing and
whaling harbor")*. **Factory Cove is named for a whaling factory. Livingston's coves were worked by sealers in
the 1820s.**

> ## SO THE PENINSULA INVERTS THE RULE THAT GOVERNS EVERYWHERE ELSE IN TEPENIA.
> **Everywhere else, the harbor exists because a settlement needed one.** ⭐ **Here, the settlement exists
> because the harbor was already there** — **found, used and named by commercial hunters two centuries before
> anyone came to do science, let alone to live.**
>
> ***These are the oldest working sea infrastructure on the continent, and Tepenia inherited them rather than
> building them.*** ⚠ **That is a real distinction between the Peninsula cities and every other subnet**, and
> it is available to any pass on those cities.

⚠ **Juan Carlos and Sejong are ALSO geothermal** *(`Geothermal_Heating.md`)*. **So they hold rock harbors AND
volcanic heat — two of the three cost-free tiers at once.** ***Signy has neither: its harbor is rock and
nothing else, which is exactly why its isolation is load-bearing.***
