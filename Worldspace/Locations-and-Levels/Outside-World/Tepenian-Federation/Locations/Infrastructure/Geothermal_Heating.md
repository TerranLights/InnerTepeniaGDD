# 2 · 🔬 THE MECHANISM

**Standard practice, and the details matter for what a city looks like:**

- ⭐ **OPEN LOOP: warm water is abstracted from one well, run through the heating network, and RE-INJECTED
  through a second borehole** at a rate that preserves pressure in the aquifer. ***So a geothermal city has a
  paired borehole FIELD, not a single tap*** — and the used water goes back down, not away.
- **Heat pumps and exchangers circulate water between buildings through a shared district loop.**
- **The heat is municipal and shared by construction.** *There is no per-household version of this.*

## ⭐⭐ 2.1 The application that matters most here is not building heat

> **🔬 The standout municipal use of a geothermal district loop is SNOW-MELT INFRASTRUCTURE** — *hot water
> circulated through the loop and a heat exchanger to keep sidewalks and bridge decks clear.*

***In Antarctica that is not a convenience. It is structural.*** **A geothermal city's streets, stairs, quays
and loading aprons do not ice up, and nobody has to clear them.**

⭐ **So the six cities differ from the other 31 in a way visible from the first step off a vehicle:**
**the ground is bare.** *No packed snow, no ice underfoot, no clearing crews, no grit.* **In a country where
every other city fights its own pavement, six cities simply do not have that fight.**

# 5 · ⚠ THE COST — the warmest cities sit on the thing that could kill them

***Geothermal heat is not free. It is paid for by proximity to active volcanism.***

| Source | 🔬 Condition |
|---|---|
| **Mount Erebus** | **continuously active, with a persistent lava lake**, fumaroles, warm soils, and a **400 m system of geothermal ice caves** |

# 6 · ⚠ OPEN — for developer ruling

1. ## ⚠ IS THE HEAT ALSO **POWER**, OR ONLY HEAT? — *developer: "I have no idea"*

   ### 🔬 The research answer: **yes, physically — and the threshold is the whole question**
   **Geothermal electricity is real and standard, in three forms with different temperature requirements:**

   | Technology | Needs | Note |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Dry steam** | **≥150 °C steam** | *"a relatively rare natural occurrence"* |
   | **Flash steam** | **>182 °C fluid** | **the most common type in operation** |
   | ⭐ **Binary cycle** | **<175 °C** — heat transferred to a secondary fluid with a low boiling point | ***the one that makes lower-temperature sites viable at all*** |

   ⭐ **So "can they generate power" is not a physics question — it is a reservoir-temperature question**, and
   binary-cycle plants exist precisely to work sites that are too cool for the other two.

   ### ⚠ BUT THE WORLDBUILDING ARGUES FOR RESTRAINT, AND HERE IS WHY
   > **Look at what the six cities' war statuses already say:**
   >
   > | Status | Cities |
   > |---|---|
   > | **Destroyed** | **Juan Carlos · Sejong · Zukelli** |
   > | ⭐ **Damaged; partially operational** | **Scott · Fort McMurdo · Janbogo** |
   >
   > ⭐⭐ ***"Partially operational" is exactly what a city that kept its HEAT looks like.*** **A damaged
   > Antarctic city with no heat is not partially operational — it is evacuated.** **So the geothermal ruling
   > already explains an existing canon status, and it does so on HEAT ALONE.**

   **Three options, and the middle one is the recommendation:**

   | Option | Consequence |
   |---|---|
   | **A — heat only** | ✅ **Already sufficient.** *Explains "partially operational"; costs nothing elsewhere* |
   | ⭐ **B — heat + LIMITED local power** | **Critical systems only, not a city's full draw.** *Keeps the Tower's fall devastating while explaining why three cities did not simply die* |
   | **C — heat + full generation** | ⚠ **Makes six cities post-war islands of working infrastructure** — ***which competes with the Tower's destruction as the defining post-war fact*** |

   ⛔ **Half of them were destroyed anyway, so no option makes them invulnerable.** *The question is only how
   much of the post-war world still has lights.*
