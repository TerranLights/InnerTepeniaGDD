## 0. Headline (read this first)

1. **The two pictures disagree because they measure different things, and both are right on their own terms.** The earlier agents' "A$1.1-1.7bn" figures are single-country, mostly 2016-2021, goods-or-narrow-definition numbers for Chile, Argentina and Brazil. The "about $20bn" figure is Australia's two-way goods and services trade with the whole of Latin America including Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean, services (mostly education), and, in 2025, a gold-import surge. [I]
2. **I can reproduce "almost $20bn" from Australian Government data.** ABS goods (A$13.4bn) plus ABS services (A$5.9bn) for Latin America and the Caribbean in financial year 2024-25 gives **A$19.4bn**. Calendar 2025 gives **A$22.6bn**. [F data, I arithmetic; sections 2 and 3.]
3. **The Grok figures are not invented; they are Australian Government talking points.** "Around 300 Australian companies", "more than $35 billion" of investment and "almost $20 billion a year" of two-way trade appear in speeches by Australia's Assistant Minister for Foreign Affairs (3 Sep 2025 text opened [F]; 16 Jun 2026 text seen only in search summaries [F-snippet]). The $35bn matches the ABS investment-position stock for 2025 (A$35.5bn) to within 1%. [I]
4. **But the Grok framing ("investment concentrated in mining") is NOT supported by the ABS composition.** About 53% of the A$35.5bn is portfolio securities (mostly equity and bond holdings by funds, not mines), and at least A$7.9bn is reported foreign direct investment. Mining is the visible, strategic part (BHP Escondida, Rio Tinto lithium, South32 Sierra Gorda), not the bulk by value. [I on ABS data]
5. **Scale in context is still small.** Latin America is 0.7% of Australia's goods exports (A$3.5bn of A$523.6bn) and 2.8% of goods imports (A$13.0bn of A$460.4bn), about 1.7% of total goods and services trade, in calendar 2025. [F data, I arithmetic.] The earlier verdict "trade is the weakest structural reason" survives in relative terms; it was wrong only in absolute terms (it understated the total by roughly an order of magnitude because it counted three countries).
6. **Most of the headline total is contingent, not structural**: roughly A$3.6bn of CY2025 goods is gold imports (Peru, Suriname), A$1.1bn is coal to Brazil, about A$6.3bn is Mexico (manufactured imports), and about A$3bn of services is education. The structurally durable cores are (a) mining know-how and capital between Australia and the Andean copper/lithium belt, (b) education and people flows, (c) Australian pension-fund capital in Latin American securities. See section 8.

### 2b. My single reconciled figure

> **Two-way trade in goods and services between Australia and Latin America and the Caribbean, financial year 2024-25: A$19.4bn** (goods A$13.4bn, services A$5.9bn). **Calendar 2025: A$22.6bn.** Definitions: ABS 5368.0 merchandise by country (original, current prices) plus ABS supplementary-information services by country, sum of exports and imports; Mexico and the Caribbean included; ABS "America, nes" and "Central America and Caribbean" residuals included. [I; inputs F]

Is $20bn plausible under ANY definition? **Yes, and it is the natural definition.** Narrower cuts for context (all A$, my arithmetic [I]):

| Cut | FY2024-25 | CY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Whole region, goods + services (the "$20bn") | 19.4bn | 22.6bn |
| Same, excluding gold | 18.2bn | 19.0bn |
| South America only, goods + services (America-nes assumed to be South America) | 11.5bn | 14.0bn |
| South America only, excluding gold | 10.3bn | 10.5bn |
| South America goods only, excluding gold | 5.9bn | 6.2bn |
| Mexico, goods + services | 5.9bn | 6.3bn |
| Goods only, whole region | 13.4bn | 16.5bn |

So for **South America alone** the honest number is about **A$10-11bn a year of goods and services excluding the gold surge, of which about A$4.3bn is services (mostly education)** and about A$6bn is goods. The A$20bn headline needs Mexico (A$6bn) and the Caribbean and Central America (A$2bn) added. If the question is "how much does Australia trade with South America", say about A$10bn (about 0.8% of Australia's trade), not A$20bn and not A$1.4bn.

### 2c. What the headline hides (composition, CY2025)

- Gold imports from Peru (A$2.44bn) and Suriname (A$1.13bn) = A$3.57bn, or 22% of regional goods trade. In calendar 2024 I found no gold import above A$1m from either country (Peru's whole 2024 goods imports were A$162m; Suriname's were about nil), and FY2024-25 was A$1.2bn. [F: ABS API series, MERCH_IMP SITC 97.] The route is not documented. The Perth Mint refines the large majority of Australian gold plus Asia-Pacific-sourced gold, so I infer these are doré or bullion flows to Australian refiners, but I did not find a source naming the importer. [I; U for the importer]
- Mexico imports (A$5.1bn): vehicles, computers, telecom equipment, instruments, ores. Mexico is Australia's largest Latin American trading partner. [F data] The goods look like US-supply-chain and FTA-driven manufacturing (Mexico exports 78% to the United States per the DFAT fact sheet), not a bilateral Australia-Mexico complementarity. [I]
- Coal and coke to Brazil: A$1.07bn (coking coal for Brazilian steel). [F: ABS API SITC 32; DFAT Brazil 2024 lists coal A$1,328m.] Price-cycle exposure. [I]
- Education-related travel (Australian service exports): Colombia A$1,220m, Brazil A$902m (2024), Chile A$236m, Argentina A$126m, Peru A$118m, Ecuador A$117m. [F: DFAT fact sheets.] This is about A$2.7bn from six countries, which is the bulk of the A$2.9bn "America, nes" credit line plus Chile and Peru. [I]
- Alumina and ores to Argentina (A$296-455m); barley, cereals and meat to Mexico, Peru and Ecuador; specialized mining machinery to Chile and Ecuador (A$54m and A$33m in CY2025 goods). METS shows up more in services and local subsidiaries than in goods exports. [F data, I reading]

## 7. Other facts that bear on the "interest" question

- **People flows (services).** Latin America is "now the largest source region for students coming to study in Australia" outside Asia; about 300,000 Latin America-born people live in Australia, a number that "has doubled in the last ten years". [F: Assistant Minister, 3 Sep 2025.] Students in 2025 (Department of Education via a search summary [F-snippet]): Colombia 25,813, Brazil 20,567, Chile 5,223, Mexico 3,979. DFAT's own sheets: Colombia 26,018 (3.1% of Australia's international students), Chile 5,228, Peru 2,640 (2024), Ecuador 2,468, Bolivia 132, Brazil 23,963 (2024), Argentina 3,342 (2023). [F]
- **Direct air link.** LATAM flies Santiago-Sydney daily and Qantas four times a week (2026); the DFAT Chile country brief said these were then "South America's only direct flights to Australia". [F-snippet for 2026 frequency; F for the DFAT statement.] Chile is the hub for Australian companies seeking to cover South America (DFAT Chile brief [F]).
- **Shipping is thin and indirect.** Cargo Chile-Australia is transshipped (San Antonio and Callao to Manzanillo, or via Panama; Maersk San Antonio-Melbourne about 30 days). The lack of direct service is described as a major impediment to trade. [F-snippet; date unknown.] So geography (Pacific latitude match) has not produced a direct bulk trade lane. [I]
- **Queensland**: goods exports to Latin America A$1.36bn in 2025 (mostly coal [I]) per Trade and Investment Queensland (https://tiq.qld.gov.au/export/market-profiles/latin-america) [F]. That is 39% of Australia's total goods exports to the region (A$3.5bn), so one state carries the export side. [I]
- **Counter-seasonality is not a bilateral Australia-South America link.** Australia and Chile are both southern-hemisphere fruit and wine exporters; ABS shows fruit and vegetables as Australia's main import from Chile (A$130m CY2025) but this is small and the two economies mostly compete in northern markets. [I on F data]

## 9. Implication for the Antarctic setting [I, brief]

Within the real-world evidence, Australia does have a continuing but modest interest in South America: about A$10bn a year of goods and services with South America (A$20bn with Mexico and the Caribbean), A$27bn of investment stock in South America, a few hundred companies, a mining-and-METS relationship centered on Chile and Peru, a large education flow, and a direct air link. It is **not** an interdependence on the scale of Australia's Asian links (about 1.7% of its trade). If the fiction needs a reason for Australia and South America to keep a communication channel through a settled Antarctica, the best-evidenced reasons here are **(1) capital and firms in mining and lithium, (2) education and migrant communities, and (3) the Pacific air and cable routes**, not bulk goods trade. Do not use "A$20bn" as a bulk-trade figure without saying that about A$6bn of it is Mexico, about A$3bn is education, and about A$3.6bn (in 2025) is gold. 800 years of extrapolation is outside this slice.
