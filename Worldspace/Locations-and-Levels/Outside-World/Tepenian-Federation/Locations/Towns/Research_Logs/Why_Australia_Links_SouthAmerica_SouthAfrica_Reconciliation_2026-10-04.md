## 1. The answer, as evidence

**Yes, Australia has real reasons to want to communicate with both. They are not mainly goods trade, and they differ by partner.**

- **South America:** real but modest economic ties (capital and firms, education, mining services) plus structural ones (Pacific geometry, the Treaty and gateway system, cable redundancy). **Direct economic interdependence is small relative to Australia's Asian links** (Latin America is about 1.7% of Australia's goods and services trade).
- **South Africa:** a better-evidenced institutional relationship than the first pass found (a 1998 rescue arrangement, adjacent rescue regions, upstream weather, the SKA data flow, a 3-hour business-hours overlap, direct Perth flights) and a **flat, small** trade relationship in which **capital flows mostly toward Australia, not away from it**.
- **Neither:** no source shows trade, mining operations or finance generating a need for a *dedicated* Australia–South America or Australia–South Africa link. Mining remote-operations centers are domestic (Perth for the Pilbara, Santiago for Chilean mines). The only quantified bilateral data flow is the SKA, and it is routed via London and Singapore today.

## 3. Revised ranking of reasons

Same test as before (`Fact vs. Driver`: would it plausibly persist for centuries, and what does it cause?). **Changes from the first-pass ranking are marked.**

### Australia and South America
1. **Antarctic institutions and gateways** (Treaty Secretariat in Buenos Aires, CCAMLR in Hobart, five gateway cities). Durable. **Caveat (new):** by rescue-region limits Australia borders only South Africa and New Zealand, so the Australia-South America coupling runs *through* the ring, not across a shared edge.
2. **Great-circle and Pacific geometry** (Sydney-Santiago stays near 62°S; Perth-Buenos Aires crosses East Antarctica). Unchanged, pure physics.
3. **Capital and firms in Andean mining and lithium** (A$27.4bn South America stock; mining-and-METS ties centered on Chile and Peru; two-way research items such as Codelco and the Western Australia School of Mines, Nov 2025). **Moved UP from "weakest"**: structural in motive (long-lived copper and lithium demand, shared geology), contingent in each deal.
4. **Cable redundancy** (Humboldt: private Google-backed build, about 2027-28; 110-160 ms saving; avoids the Singapore and Red Sea chokepoints). **New and contingent**: a company's build, not a structural fact; the underlying chokepoint exposure is structural.
5. **Counter-seasonal agriculture and mining know-how.** Real, but mostly Australia and South America compete for the same Asian buyers on parallel lanes (Agent C: Australia 55.8% of seaborne iron ore vs Brazil 22.2%).
6. **Goods and services trade, A$10-11bn with South America.** **Still the weakest structural reason**: about 1.7% of Australian trade, goods flat A$7-9bn from 2008 to 2020, a large education and gold component. The earlier verdict "trade is the weakest" **survives in relative terms and was wrong only in absolute terms** (it understated the regional total by about a factor of ten).

**Comms-specific cost:** business-hours overlap between Australia and South America is **0 hours** (Perth vs Santiago is 12 hours apart), so the traffic that exists is asynchronous or automated.

### Australia and South Africa
1. **Rescue arrangement and adjacency.** **Moved UP.** A direct arrangement was signed **23 September 1998** (ICAO paper, 2008); the two rescue regions share an edge. The earlier "no formal agreement" finding holds only at Antarctic-program level.
2. **Weather upstream.** **New.** At the published upper-air wave speed Cape to Perth is about 3 days (agent's arithmetic): South African observations are real forecasting input for Australia.
3. **SKA** (Perth and Karoo). The only **quantified** bilateral data flow ("significant traffic between AU & ZA"), routed via London and Singapore today because no direct Indian Ocean link exists. This is the one concrete comms-demand argument, and **Umoja (planned 2028) is the first cable that would serve it**.
4. **Great-circle geometry.** Perth-Cape Town 8,698 km, open ocean, never near Antarctica; ordinary Indian Ocean circuit.
5. **Corporate chain, not market:** Western Australian alumina (A$914m, 38% of exports) to South32's Hillside smelter; wheat A$314m (13%). **Capital runs mostly to Australia** (South African investment in Australia A$10.1bn vs Australian A$5.5bn in South Africa, 2024; DFAT calls Australia's stake "legacy investment"; Gold Fields now takes 44% of attributable output from Australia).
6. **Business-hours overlap.** Perth-Johannesburg 6 hours apart, no daylight saving, **3 shared hours**; direct Qantas Perth-Johannesburg relaunched December 2025.
7. **Trade:** flat, small (28th as an Australian export destination, 31st as an import source; Australia is 0.9% of South Africa's exports).
