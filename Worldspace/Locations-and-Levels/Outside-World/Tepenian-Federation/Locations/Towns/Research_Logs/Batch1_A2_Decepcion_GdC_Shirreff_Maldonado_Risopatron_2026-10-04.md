# Research report: five South Shetland summer stations (Decepcion, Gabriel de Castilla, Cape Shirreff / Guillermo Mann, Maldonado, Risopatron)

All sources were accessed 2026-10-04. No project files were edited or written. Scratch downloads are in the session scratchpad only.

Conventions:
- [S#] is a source key; the table is at the end.
- "Operator nation" is context only.
- "(summary only)" means the fact was seen only in a search-result summary, not in a document I opened. Treat it as unverified.
- "(inference)" means my own arithmetic or reading, not a stated fact.
- All distances marked "(computed)" are haversine distances from COMNAP Nov 2024 coordinates [S1].

## Headline findings

1. **The inventory's "Shirreff Base (Chile)" coordinate is the Chilean "Dr. Guillermo Mann" camp.**
   - COMNAP [S1] lists the Chilean camp at -62.47015, -60.771178 as a "Refuge", Seasonal, Open, established 1991. That is 34 m from the inventory value (computed).
   - "Shirreff Base" is the name Wikipedia gives the adjacent US NOAA camp, renamed "Holt Watters Field Camp" in 2023 [S30, S18].
   - The two camps are about 50 m apart and share one site inside ASPA 149 [S16, S17].
2. **Decepcion and Gabriel de Castilla (GdC) are one site.**
   - They are 1.26 km apart (computed) and form a single "Facilities Zone" inside ASMA 4 [S5, S6].
   - The inventory label "GdC at Pendulum Cove" is wrong. Pendulum Cove is about 6 km north-east and is the ruin of the Chilean Aguirre Cerda station [S5, S21 (Pendulum Cove gazetteer record), S30].
3. **Verdicts, short form:**
   - Cape Shirreff: rock-qualifies.
   - Risopatron: rock-qualifies.
   - Maldonado: rock-qualifies, with caveats.
   - Decepcion and GdC: ice test passes, but they sit on pyroclastic ground in a restless caldera. GdC also has a documented, accelerating erosion problem. Verdict unclear, leaning fail for a permanent settlement.

## 1. DECEPCION STATION (Argentina; operator is context)

**Verified coordinates**
- COMNAP CSV 2024: -62.97676, -60.700695 (62°58.6056'S 60°42.0417'W), elevation 7 m MSL [S1].
- COMNAP 2017 catalogue: 62°58'36.3"S 60°42'02.5"W, altitude 7 m. This is the same value in DMS [S2 p.14].
- ASMA 4 plan text: 62°58'20"S 60°41'40"W, "southern shore of Fumarole Bay" [S5, S6]. That is 0.6 km from COMNAP (computed). It looks like an approximate value.
- Wikipedia (lead only): 62.975284, -60.697771, 0.22 km from COMNAP [S30].
- The inventory value (-62.9768, -60.7007) matches COMNAP. Bay name: Fumarole Bay = "Bahía Primero de Mayo" in Argentine usage [S21, S2].
- Verdict on coordinates: confirmed within about 0.2 km. Elevation 7 m rests on COMNAP only.

**Surface and stability. Verdict: unclear (ice test passes; ground and volcano risk dominates). Confidence: medium on verdict, high on facts.**
- Bedrock or ice:
  - COMNAP: built on "Ice-free ground", discontinuous permafrost, features "Volcanic caldera, Terrestrial geothermal" [S2 p.14].
  - Whole-island background: about 57% of Deception Island is permanent glaciers (often ash-covered). The east coast and part of the south coast are glacier cliffs [S5, S6, S34 (low-reliability Argentine provincial newsletter)].
  - The stations are on the SW shore of Port Foster, outside the glaciated zones. I found no source reporting ice moving toward or through the station.
  - Ground type is pyroclastic ash, scoria and lapilli beach and plain material. For the GdC area the Spanish Army describes a plain at the foot of a slope of "volcanic material" with lapilli-sized beach material [S12]. The same coast runs from Decepcion to Punta Colatinas [S12]. I found no source stating depth to bedrock.
- Volcanic hazard:
  - Deception is an active basaltic volcano, classed a "restless caldera with a significant volcanic risk" [S5, S6].
  - 20th-century eruptions: about 1906–10 and 1967–1970 [S5, S6].
  - 1967: the Argentine station was evacuated several days before the 4 Dec 1967 eruption [S15]. The Chilean Aguirre Cerda station was destroyed [S15, S30]. The year-round Argentine occupation ended then; it has been summer-only since December 1967 [S2 p.14].
  - 21 Feb 1969 eruption: the Chilean and British bases were destroyed. A thin ash coat fell on the Argentine station, which was "unharmed" [S15, Antarctic Journal of the US, May–June 1969 p.87]. The British Base B and the Hektor whaling station were partly destroyed and buried by a lahar [S5].
  - Unrest episodes close to these stations:
    - 1991–92: up to 900 earthquakes, "probably a small intrusion located in Fumarole Bay" [S6].
    - 1999: activity "between Fumarole Bay and Whalers Bay" [S6].
    - 2014–15: alert raised to YELLOW on 17 Feb 2015, back to green after 20 Feb, and Gabriel de Castilla (GdC) closed 24 Feb 2015 [S6].
  - Deformation (2019 plan): horizontal NE motion about 2 cm/yr and subsidence about 6 mm/yr. The Caliente Hill geothermal station records 80–100 °C at 10–40 cm depth in summer [S6]. The 2005 plan said the Port Foster floor was rising about 30 cm/yr [S5].
  - Safe-distance guidance (2019 plan): areas 7–10 km from the eruption centre "could be relatively safe". Inner-coast areas are hazardous from ashfall, pyroclastic surges within about 2 km of a vent, tsunami and tidal surges [S5, S6]. Escape routes out of the caldera take 2–4+ hours on foot and ground vehicles cannot carry people out [S5].
- Current alert level:
  - GREEN all of 2023/24 [S7].
  - GREEN on 29 Dec 2025 (opening), 22 Feb, 27 Feb and 1 Mar 2026 [S10].
  - Last observed GREEN on 1 Mar 2026; the stations are closed in the austral winter, so no later reading exists.
- Monitoring network [S7, S9, S10]:
  - Spain's IGN runs 7 seismic stations, 6 GNSS, a thermometry profile at Caliente Hill, a camera, and Starlink and Hughes satellite links to Madrid.
  - Argentina's SEGEMAR/OAVV runs 3 seismic and 2 GNSS stations installed March 2023.
  - A 2023 SEGEMAR–IGN agreement shares real-time data.
  - Two SEGEMAR stations lost power in a late-Dec 2023 storm (solar panels destroyed by wind) and were repaired in Jan 2024 [S7].
- Erosion and flood: the 2024 shoreline study reports erosion of 0.3–2 m/yr on Port Foster cliffs outside areas affected by the last eruption. South-facing coasts erode and east/west-facing ones accrete [S14]. The Spanish Army posters say the recession affects the whole SW Port Foster coast "from the beach in front of the Argentine base Decepcion to Punta Colatinas" [S12].
- Relocation history: none after 1967 found. A "Chilean refuge" and "Irizar" CALM monitoring sites exist nearby for permafrost [S35 (summary only)].

**Status and years**
- Opened 25 Jan 1948 as a naval detachment [S2 p.14, S30]. One Spanish-authored paper says "operational since 1947" [S9].
- Summer-only since December 1967 [S2 p.14].
- COMNAP: Seasonal, Open, operational period October–March [S1, S2].
- Actual 2023/24 operation: 19 Jan – 23 Mar [S7].
- 2025/26: the Argentine base closed on the afternoon of 26 Feb 2026 [S10]. Opening date not found.
- Nobody winters. The 2017 catalogue and Wikipedia list "3" winter persons. That conflicts with the 2025/26 closure record and the COMNAP "Seasonal" classification [S2, S10, S30].
- Rebuilds: none found.

**Capacity**
- COMNAP 2017: 30 beds; max 36 at once; summer staff 18; 1,030 m² under roof; 337 m² logistics; 16 m² lab (volcanology); medical 6 m² [S2 p.14]. COMNAP CSV peak population: 36 [S1].
- Single figures are from COMNAP and unconfirmed by Argentine primary documents.

**Infrastructure**
- Power: fossil fuel, 220 V, 24 h/day [S2]. The station has its own generator; in Feb 2026 a VHF repeater on Cerro Cruz was powered from it [S10]. CSV: "Fossil Fuel" only [S1].
- Water: a small unnamed lake west of Crater Lake, inside the Facilities Zone, is the freshwater source for both stations [S6, S10].
- Fuel: both stations use secondary containment; drummed fuel stored inside [S5, S6].
- Communications: satellite phone, VHF [S2].
- Workshops: general repairs, mechanical, wood [S2].
- Boats: two Zodiacs with outboards [S2]. No land vehicles listed.
- Helipad: COMNAP 2017 says "Helipad: No" [S2]. The ASMA 4 plan says helicopters generally land at "the helipad at Decepcion Station" [S6]. See contradictions.
- No airstrip, no pier ("ship landing facilities: None") [S2]. Ship visits per year: 6 [S2].

**Access**
- Ship into Port Foster through Neptune's Bellows (about 500 m wide, shallow, with Ravn Rock mid-channel) [S5, S6].
- The Argentine icebreaker ARA Almirante Irizar resupplies "Base Decepcion" with Sea King helicopters (2022/23 campaign) [S33, summary-level].
- Spanish BIO Hespérides runs the Ushuaia → Livingston (Juan Carlos I) → Deception route for GdC [S10].
- Nearest other station: GdC at 1.26 km.

**Other facilities within 25 km**
- GdC, 1.26 km (computed), seasonal, open [S1].
- Whalers Bay, about 7 km (computed): ruins of Hektor whaling station (operated 1912–1931) and British Base B (1944–1969); HSM 71 [S5, S21, S6]. Whalers Bay has not been reoccupied since the 1969 lahar [S5].
- Pendulum Cove, about 6.9 km (computed): ruins of the Chilean Aguirre Cerda station (opened 12 Feb 1955, destroyed 1967/1969) [S5, S30]. HSM 76. A derelict wooden refuge hut stands about 1 km SW of it [S5].
- Nearest other operating station: Spanish Juan Carlos I on Livingston Island, 38 km (computed).

**Stated purpose**
- Argentine program: "mainly geology and volcanology"; meteorological observations from the start; seismograph and ionospheric station added in 1951; 1993 volcanological observatory [S2 p.14, S34].
- Spanish/Argentine joint ASMA purpose: long-term seismological and biological datasets at both stations [S5, S6].

**Protected-area status** (ASMA 4: Measure 3 (2005); revised Measures 10 (2012) and 10 (2019); next review started 2024) [S7]
- Whole island plus Port Foster = ASMA 4 (98.5 km²) [S7].
- The two stations, the beach, and a freshwater lake form the "Facilities Zone" [S6].
- Adjacent: ASPA 140 sites B (Crater Lake), C (unnamed hill, S end of Fumarole Bay) and D (Fumarole Bay); ASPA 145 (Port Foster marine; revised Measure 10 (2023)) [S5, S6, S17].
- Restrictions on station activity: EIA required for new permanent buildings (2005 text) and for quarrying rock; no handling of waste or fuel in the station water catchment; small boats only inside Port Foster; waste removed from the Treaty area each season; wastes segregated; VHF Channel 16 monitored [S5, S6].
- Non-scientific vehicles and camping are prohibited; the Whalers Bay–Baily Head walk is prohibited [S8].

**Other notes**
- Climate: mean annual temperature -3 °C; mean wind 22.3 km/h; dominant SW; annual precipitation 407 mm; snow-free January–February [S2]. Island-wide: -2.9 °C at sea level; about 500 mm rainfall equivalent; extremes +11 to -28 °C [S5, S6].
- Tourism: about 45,000 passenger landings in 2023/24, mostly at Whalers Bay and Telefon Bay [S7]. At least 60 vessels entered Port Foster between 1 Jan and 25 Mar 2024.
- Fuel-spill risk in the enclosed caldera is under discussion by Spain and Argentina [S7].

## 2. GABRIEL DE CASTILLA STATION (Spain; operator is context)

**Verified coordinates**
- COMNAP CSV 2024: -62.97719656, -60.67574545 (62°58.6318'S 60°40.5447'W), 15 m [S1].
- ASMA plan: 62°58'40"S 60°40'30"W, "approximately 1 km to the south-east" of Decepcion [S5, S6]. That is 75 m from COMNAP (computed).
- SCAR gazetteer: Spanish record -62.97725, -60.672528; the USA record -62.983333, -60.683333, 15 m (derived from COMNAP 2006) [S21].
- Spanish 2017 catalogue prints 62°58'40"S 60°00'30"W. The longitude is a typo for 60°40'30" [S3].
- Wikipedia (lead only): 62.976951, -60.675305 [S30].
- The inventory value (-62.9772, -60.6757) matches COMNAP. The inventory's "Pendulum Cove" label is wrong: Pendulum Cove is 6.0 km away (computed).

**Surface and stability. Verdict: unclear, leaning fail for a permanent site. Confidence: high on the erosion facts.**
- COMNAP: ice-free ground, discontinuous permafrost, 15 m [S3]. Features include "Ice cap or glacier" (island-wide).
- Spanish Army and UPM/INTA geotechnical work [S12]:
  - The base sits on a plain at the foot of a slope of volcanic material.
  - The cliff in front, 2.5–3.5 m high, is receding, "especially after storms", from wave action, permafrost loss and fluvial runoff on the active layer.
  - Recession since 2009 is described as manifest. A 164-transect analysis of 2001–2013 imagery covers 1,650 m of coast.
  - Inland, slow periglacial creep and mudflows of water-saturated material "can reach parts of the Base causing serious deterioration" (gullying).
  - A possible hillside landslide has a mapped head scarp; its activity is unknown and must be confirmed inactive "to ensure the longevity of the Base".
  - Electrical resistivity profiles: permafrost is discontinuous near the sea, more uniform on the slope, at less than 1 m depth.
- UPM 2024 abstract: erosion acceleration since 2010 with rates up to 2.5 m/yr (images 1956–2023); a retaining wall has been built to protect the base [S13].
- No source gives distance to bedrock.
- Volcanic hazard: same as Decepcion. GdC itself was closed 24 Feb 2015 during unrest [S6].
- An active-layer study at Crater Lake reports thaw depth falling from about 36 cm (2006) to 23 cm (2014) (summary only) [S35].

**Status and years**
- Built late 1989, inaugurated 1990 [S31, S3]. First set up as a refuge; "designated formally as a station" in 1998 [S3 p.128].
- Seasonal, normally November–March [S3, S31].
- 2023/24: open 1 Jan – 24 Mar [S7].
- 2025/26: opened 29 Dec 2025 (volcano alert green) and closed 20 Mar 2026, a few days early because of forecast bad weather [S10, S11].
- Nobody winters; the station is closed over winter [S10].

**Capacity**
- COMNAP 2017: 36 beds; 792 m² under roof; labs 142 m²; staff 13 + scientists 20 peak; max 36 [S3]. CSV peak population 36 [S1].
- EU-POLARIN says accommodation for 28 [S32].
- Observed: 20 people (13 military, 7 scientists) at opening and 33 (23 military, 10 scientists) on 27 Feb and 1 Mar 2026 [S10].

**Infrastructure** [S3, S10]
- Buildings: living module (kitchen, bakery), seven 4-bed rooms, a scientist module with two offices and two labs, plus containers for boats, nautical gear, wet lab, infirmary, freezer, workshop, logistics stores, an incinerator and three igloos.
- Power: fossil fuel plus renewables, 220 V, 24 h/day [S3, S1].
- Water: from the lake called "Zapatilla" [S10]; a small lake west of Crater Lake [S6].
- Medical: 15 m² facility, one medically trained person [S3].
- Comms: VHF, satellite phone, internet and Starlink [S3, S7, S10].
- Boats and vehicles: five ribbon boats, one ATV, one tracked ATV, two quads, two telehandlers [S3].
- Landing: a wet-dock ("Ship landing facilities: Wetdock") [S3]. No airstrip.
- Long-term monitoring: seismic, meteorological, permafrost, geodesy [S3].

**Access**
- Ship (BIO Hespérides) and helicopter [S3, S10]. Reaching Deception means entering Port Foster via Neptune's Bellows.
- Nearest station: Decepcion, 1.26 km.

**Other facilities within 25 km**
- Same as Decepcion: Decepcion at 1.26 km; Whalers Bay about 5.8 km; Pendulum Cove about 6 km (all computed).

**Stated purpose**
- Logistic support to scientific research and Army research in transmissions, environment, health, clothing and camping equipment [S31].
- Support for Spanish science in Deception: volcanology, seismology, biology, permafrost, pollution [S3].

**Protected-area status**
- Same Facilities Zone and ASMA 4 as Decepcion (see above).

**Other notes**
- Wind: max 130 km/h; mean 24 km/h; mean annual temperature -0.7 °C; precipitation "23.2 mm" (an obvious error; see contradictions) [S3].
- Spanish IGN took over the volcanic monitoring in September 2020 [S9].

## 3. CAPE SHIRREFF: DR. GUILLERMO MANN (Chile) and the adjacent US camp ("Shirreff Base" / Holt Watters Field Camp)

**Verified coordinates**
- COMNAP CSV: Dr. Guillermo Mann -62.47015, -60.771178 (62°28.209'S 60°46.2707'W), 15 m, "Refuge" [S1].
- ASPA 149 plan: camp at the base of Condor Hill, 62°28.249'S 60°46.283'W [S16, S17]. That is 75 m from COMNAP (computed).
- COMNAP 2017 catalogue: Mann at 62°27'S 60°47'W. That is the ASPA reference point at the cape, 2.3 km away; it is not the camp [S2 p.30].
- Wikipedia (lead only): US camp 62°28'12"S 60°46'17"W [S30].
- SCAR gazetteer record "Cape Shirreff Field Station, Livingston Island" has a clearly wrong longitude of -58.4667; the true longitude is about -60.77 [S21].
- Inventory value (-62.4704, -60.7708): 34 m from the Chilean camp (computed).
- Island and peninsula check: Cape Shirreff on the north coast of Livingston Island, between Barclay Bay and Hero Bay [S16]. Wikipedia places the camps on the "Ioannes Paulus II Peninsula" (lead only).

**Surface and stability. Verdict: rock-qualifies. Confidence: medium-high.**
- Geology: Cape Shirreff is porphyritic basaltic lavas and minor volcanic breccias about 450 m thick, K–Ar dated Late Cretaceous (minimum 90.2 ± 5.6 Ma) [S16, S17].
- Landform:
  - An ice-free peninsula of about 3.1 km², with the Livingston Island permanent ice cap to the south [S16, S17].
  - Mostly a raised marine platform (46–53 m), with lower platforms at 7–9 m and 12–15 m [S16].
  - The camp is on the east coast at 15 m (COMNAP) at the base of Condor Hill [S1, S16].
  - "The bedrock is largely covered by weathered rock and glacial deposits"; soils are "fine, highly porous, ash and scoria" [S16].
- Ice: the ice-cap margin is south of the peninsula and moving away. I estimate it lies roughly 1–1.5 km from the camp from the plan's dimensions (inference; not stated).
  - Ice-cap retreat regionally: Livingston glacier cover fell from 832 km² (1957) to 679 km² (2020), about 2.4 km²/yr [S29]. Fossils are being exposed in retreating moraines [S16].
  - No ice velocity data was found for this site.
- Damage and relocation:
  - The US camp was rebuilt (see below) because 26 years of weather meant scientists spent up to 30% of their time on repairs. This is deterioration, not ground failure. [S18, summary]
  - Chilean wind-generator tower is "defunct" in the 2023 plan (it was "wind-powered generator" in 2016) [S16, S17].
  - 2023/24: "no detectable damage to any essential infrastructure" after winter, with 4–14 ft of drifted snow around the main camp [S18].
  - 2024/25: minor overwinter damage (solar system, door handles, propane leaks) [S18].
  - No landslide, erosion or flood problem reported in the sources.
- Volcanic: Deception Island is 56 km away (computed). Ash fall possible; no direct hazard assessment found.

**Status and years**
- Chilean camp: opened November 1991; fibreglass igloo installed 1990/91 [S2 p.30, S16]. Chile's studies there began 1965; more intensive from 1982 [S16].
- US camp: established 1996/97; rebuilt as the 2,000 ft² Holt Watters Field Camp (renamed Jan 2023), three buildings plus a remote bird blind, completed in the 2023/24 season [S18, S30].
- Seasonal, summer-only; buildings stay in place year-round [S16, S17]. COMNAP: Seasonal, Open for Mann [S1]; Wikipedia says "Closed" (conflict; not resolved). In 2023/24 the NOAA team reported all three camps (Cape Shirreff, Holt Watters, Chilean) inspected and opened, and the Chilean camp used as extra housing and storage [S18].
- US season: 2024/25 began 11 Nov 2024 (San Diego), ship from Punta Arenas 15 Nov [S18]. No winter occupation.

**Capacity**
- Chilean camp: COMNAP 2017 about 8 (2 staff + 6 scientists), 8 beds, 250 m² under roof (column order inferred) [S2 p.30]; CSV peak 6 [S1]; INACH 2009: maximum 6 [S20].
- US camp: 7 people in 2024/25 [S18]; 8 NOAA staff plus a 13-person construction crew in 2023/24 [S18].

**Infrastructure**
- Chilean camp: house, igloo, lab module, store, outhouse, quad bike; HF/VHF and satellite phone [S16, S20, S2].
- US camp: berthing, galley, pinniped and seabird labs, an emergency shelter/bird blind, communications tower, ATV shed [S18, S16]. Power is solar with propane heating and water collection [S18].
- Weatherhaven polar tent for extra accommodation [S17].
- Helicopter landing sites A and B at Condor Hill / Ancho Pass [S16].
- Fuel and food: the plan says not to store them in the area unless essential [S16].
- No pier. In 2023/24, 178 full Zodiac loads were landed from the M/V Betanzos [S18].

**Access**
- Ship to anchorage (two identified), then small boats to Módulo Beach; helicopter discouraged 1 Nov – 31 Mar; vehicles only on the coastal strip [S16, S17].
- US route: M/V Betanzos from Punta Arenas [S18]. Chile: INACH (the INACH ship Karpuj was seen at Deception in Feb 2026 [S10]).
- Sea states 1–4 m [S16].

**Other facilities within 25 km**
- None in the COMNAP CSV [S1].
- Nearest: Spanish International Field Camp Byers (a summer camp of two huts) at about 27 km (computed); Juan Carlos I at 29 km (computed). The ASPA plan places the ASPA 126 Byers Peninsula 20 km to the SW [S16].

**Stated purpose**
- Long-term fur seal and penguin monitoring; part of the CCAMLR Ecosystem Monitoring Program (CEMP site no. 2) [S16, S2].
- Chile: fur seals, archaeology, weather, geology, glaciology [S2].

**Protected-area status**
- The camps lie inside ASPA 149 (Cape Shirreff and San Telmo Island), originally SPA 11 (1966). The current plan is Measure 12 (2023), replacing Measure 16 (2022) [S17].
- HSM 59 (San Telmo cairn) is inside the area [S16].
- Restrictions: permit required to enter; main camp facilities limited to within 200 m of the existing camps; camping only within 200 m of the camps plus limited named sites; helicopter route and landing restrictions; vehicles limited to the coastal strip; no fuel or food storage in the area except for essential purposes [S16, S17].

**Other notes**
- Climate (summers 2005/06–2009/10): mean 1.84 °C, max 19.9 °C, min -8.1 °C; mean wind 5.36 m/s, max 20.1 m/s; winter mean (Jun–Aug 2007) -6.7 °C, min -20.6 °C [S16]. NOAA 2024/25 recorded gusts to 62 mph [S18].
- Chilean camp COMNAP climate: wind 12.05 km/h, dominant W, no permafrost [S2 p.30] (precipitation field unreadable).
- Largest Antarctic fur seal colony in the Peninsula region; the camp must not interfere with seal movement [S16].

## 4. PEDRO VICENTE MALDONADO STATION (Ecuador; operator is context)

**Verified coordinates**
- COMNAP CSV 2024: -62.44932928, -59.74096859 (62°26.9598'S 59°44.4581'W), 10 m [S1].
- COMNAP 2017 catalogue: 62°26'96.0"S 59°44'45.8"W. The "96" seconds is invalid; the value is 0.26 km from the CSV if read as 57.6" [S4].
- SCAR gazetteer: -59.742222, -62.449167, 10 m (a COMNAP 2006 derived record) [S21].
- Wikipedia (lead only): 62°26'57"S 59°44'29"W [S30].
- The inventory value (-62.4493, -59.7410) matches the CSV.
- Location detail: Punta Fort Williams (also called Spark Point in one paper [S23]), north coast of Greenwich Island, Guayaquil Bay [S21, S22]. Note a different "Fort William" exists on Robert Island, 8.7 km away (computed).
- Independent elevation: the station stands on a pebble-ridge platform at 10–11 m ("Plataforma Maldonado") [S22].

**Surface and stability. Verdict: rock-qualifies, with caveats. Confidence: medium.**
- Ground [S22, S23, S24]:
  - The station stands on the "Maldonado Platform", a sedimentary platform of wide pebble beach ridges at 10–11 m, a tombolo-like feature, with volcanic bedrock outcropping between the ridges.
  - Bedrock at Fort Williams Point is Late Cretaceous–Paleocene andesites and basalts.
  - A small depression where snow and water remain all summer; the Culebra River (about 2–3 m³/s) flows across the platform.
  - Eight raised ridges reach 13.5 m above sea level.
- 2018 ESPOL geology thesis (undergraduate; lower authority) [S24]:
  - Sediments and ice: sediments are about 80% and ice/snow about 20% (summer) of the point.
  - The station is described as on flat ground, north of a cirque and at the end of a small U-valley.
  - A 2014 photo shows a glacier "a few metres from the station". The thesis concludes the glacier's advance and retreat is toward the station and could cause base erosion or lateral sliding westward; it recommends annual DGPS checks or relocation.
  - Landslide/avalanche hazard "minimal" (flat, no tall slopes); the thesis rates seismic and flood hazards low, volcanic ash fall high.
  - The thesis misstates Deception's distance as about 150 km; the true distance is about 76 km (computed).
- INAE director, 2009: the base was "surrounded by snow and ice" in 2006; by 2009 only rocky ground. Glaciers near the station had retreated 150–200 m in ten years and many had disappeared [S25].
- Larger glaciers: Quito Glacier (marine-terminating) is 2–3 km west [S21, computed 2.2 km to the gazetteer point]. Greenwich Island glacier area fell 14.4% (1957–2023) [S29]; four glaciers on the island's north lost 20.4% (1956–2019) (summary only).
- No ice-velocity data found for any local glacier.
- Station-specific mention: COMNAP lists permafrost "Continuous" and long-term monitoring "seasonal data of glaciological parameters, since 2010" [S4]. An Ecuadorian project estimates mass balance "in a region of the Quito glacier" [S28].

**Status and years**
- Inaugurated 2 March 1990 per COMNAP/Wikipedia [S1, S30]; March 1989 per the INAE director in 2009 [S25]; "35 years" in 2026 implies 1991 [S26]. Not resolved.
- Seasonal: October–March [S4]; summer only. 2009 expedition ran 8 Jan – 22 Feb with 13 scientists [S25].
- The XXIX Ecuadorian expedition (2025–26) worked from the station in the austral summer [S26].
- Ecuador's own text says it "will become a year-round facility within the next five years" (undated; the claim has not materialized) [S27].
- Capacity increased from 22 to 32 in 2012 [S4].

**Capacity**
- COMNAP 2017: 34 beds; max 34; 22 staff + 10 scientists peak (original design 22; 32 since 2012) [S4]. CSV peak 34 [S1].

**Infrastructure** [S4, S28, S30]
- 908 m² under roof; 200 m² labs; 15-person conference room; 500 m² logistics; medical 22 m².

## 5. LUIS RISOPATRON BASE (Chile; operator is context)

**Verified coordinates**
- COMNAP CSV: -62.378533, -59.700724 (62°22.712'S 59°42.0434'W), 15 m [S1].
- Wikipedia: 62°22'42.4"S 59°42'02.5"W (0.01 km from CSV) [S30].
- COMNAP 2017 catalogue prints 62°22'17"S 59°42'53"W, 1.07 km from the CSV [S2 p.36]. INACH 2009 prints 62°22'S 59°39'W, 2.9 km away [S20]. ASPA 112 plan: "62°24'S 59°30'W", about 10 km away (a peninsula centre, not the station) [S19].
- Elevation: 15 m (COMNAP) vs 40 m (ASPA 112 plan) [S1, S19]. Unresolved.
- The inventory value (-62.3785, -59.7007) matches the CSV. Location: isthmus joining Coppermine Peninsula to Robert Island, between Carlota Cove (N) and Coppermine Cove (S) [S19, S30].
- SCAR gazetteer has no Risopatron station record; only Coppermine Cove/Peninsula and Fort William (Robert Island) [S21].

**Surface and stability. Verdict: rock-qualifies. Confidence: medium-high on ground, low on current building state.**
- ASPA 112 plan: the station "stands 40 m above sea level, on solid rock surface, located 150 m from the coastal line", about 100 m west of the protected area [S19].
- Geology: Late Cretaceous basaltic lavas and olivine basalt; the peninsula is a perched strandflat (raised marine surface); the isthmus is a 10 m terrace of marine gravel [S19, S29-adjacent summary].
- Ice: much higher ground on the peninsula is permanently ice-covered [S19]. The Robert Island ice cap lost 17.35% of its area from 1957 to 2023 [S29]. Nothing reports ice moving toward the site. COMNAP: permafrost "None", "Ice-free ground" [S2].
- No damage, relocation or erosion report found. INACH 2009 called the module "in deteriorated condition" [S20].

**Status and years**
- Naval refuge inaugurated 20 March 1949 ("Refugio Naval Coppermine"); opened as a small base 1954; renamed Base Luis Risopatrón 24 Sept 1991 [S30 (lead only); consistent with S2: "refuge 1949, small base 1954"].
- Seasonal (October–March) [S2]. COMNAP CSV: Seasonal, Open, established "1949" [S1]. 2025/26 dates not found.
- Wikipedia says "recently remodeled" (summary only; not verified).

**Capacity**
- 5 (ASPA 112 plan, INACH 2009) [S19, S20]; 6 beds and peak 6 (COMNAP) [S1, S2].

**Infrastructure**
- ASPA 112: 5 modules for accommodation, labs and storage [S19]. INACH 2009: one habitation module and one lounge/dining container [S20].
- COMNAP 2017: 60 m² under roof; 15 m² lab (microbiology); 25 m² logistics; power fossil fuel 220 V for 10 h/day; Zodiac boats; no land vehicles; satellite phone and VHF [S2 p.36].
- Water and fuel storage: not found. COMNAP lists fuel-spill response capability as "No" (reading inferred from field order).

**Access**
- Sea only, landing at the Carlota Cove or Coppermine Cove beaches in front of the station. Air access only by helicopter, in emergencies, landing east of the isthmus outside the ASPA [S19]. No ship landing facilities [S2].

**Other facilities within 25 km**
- Maldonado (Ecuador), 8.1 km; Arturo Prat, 11.3 km [S1, computed]; Prat 11.3 km agrees with Wikipedia (lead only).
- Nelson Island ASPA 133 about 30 km NW [S19].

**Stated purpose**
- Geology, geophysics, glaciology, lakes and terrestrial biology; best suited to geology and terrestrial biology [S2, S20].

**Protected-area status**
- Station contiguous to ASPA 112 (Coppermine Peninsula), designated SPA 16 in 1970; plan revised by Measure 4 (2012). Values: the large moss carpet (about 1.5 ha), giant petrel colonies and other biota. Restrictions: no vehicles; access by permit; sea access only at the beaches in front of the station [S19]. A later revision may exist (not checked).

**Other notes**
- Climate (COMNAP 2017): mean annual -2.3 °C; February 1.6 °C; July -6.7 °C; annual precipitation 511 mm; mean wind 42.1 km/h, max 92.6 km/h; dominant NW [S2 p.36].
- Distance to hospital about 1,000 km [S2].

## Contradictions between sources

1. **GdC "at Pendulum Cove" (inventory) vs actual.** ASMA plans put GdC about 1 km SE of Decepcion at Fumarole Bay [S5, S6]; Pendulum Cove is about 6 km NE and holds the Chilean ruin (HSM 76) [S5, S21].
2. **"Shirreff Base (Chile)."** Shirreff Base is the US camp [S30]. The Chilean camp is Dr. Guillermo Mann. COMNAP lists Mann as Open; Wikipedia says "Closed" [S1, S30]. COMNAP 2017 coordinates for Mann are the cape, 2.3 km off the camp [S2].
3. **Decepcion opening year:** 1948 [S2, S1, S30] vs "since 1947" [S9].
4. **GdC years:** built 1989 [S12, S30]; inaugurated 1990 [S31]; formally a station 1998 [S3].
5. **Maldonado opening:** March 1989 [S25]; 2 March 1990 [S1, S30]; "35 years" in 2026 [S26].
6. **Maldonado capacity:** design 22, now 32 [S4]; COMNAP max 34 [S1, S4].
7. **Risopatron capacity 5 vs 6; elevation 15 vs 40 m; coordinates 1.07 km apart between catalogue versions** [S1, S2, S19, S20].
8. **Decepcion helipad:** "No" [S2] vs helipad at the station [S6].
9. **Decepcion winter population of 3** [S2, S30] vs seasonal operation and winter closure [S1, S10].
10. **GdC capacity:** 36 beds [S3], 28 [S32], 33 observed peak in 2026 [S10].
11. **GdC annual precipitation "23.2 mm"** [S3] vs about 500 mm island-wide [S5]. The 23.2 value is almost certainly an error. GdC mean annual -0.7 °C [S3] vs -2.9 °C island sea-level mean [S5]; Decepcion -3 °C [S2].
12. **Caldera deformation:** the 2005 plan says the Port Foster floor rises about 30 cm/yr; the 2019 plan gives 2 cm/yr horizontal and 6 mm/yr subsidence [S5, S6]. Different measures and periods.
13. **Maldonado glacier distance:** "a few metres" in a 2014 photo per the 2018 thesis [S24] vs retreat of 150–200 m and "only rocky ground" per the INAE director in 2009 [S25]. The thesis also misstates distance to Deception (150 km vs about 76 km computed).
14. **Cape Shirreff Chilean wind generator:** working in 2010-era text [S2, S16] vs "defunct" in 2023 [S17].
15. **SCAR gazetteer longitude error** for the Cape Shirreff Field Station (-58.47) [S21].
16. **Maldonado nearest emergency facility "12 km"** [S4] vs Arturo Prat 5.1 km (computed) / "three miles" [S27].
