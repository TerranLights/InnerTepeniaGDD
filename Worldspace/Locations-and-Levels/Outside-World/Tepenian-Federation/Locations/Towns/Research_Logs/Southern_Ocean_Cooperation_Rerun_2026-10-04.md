## 0. Headline findings (read this first)

1. **The Antarctic coast is tiled by five nations in a closed ring, and each nation's direct SAR neighbors are only the two adjacent ones.** By the IHO/IMO/WMO navigational and meteorological warning areas (NAVAREA/METAREA), the five tiles are South Africa 20 deg W to 80 deg E, Australia 80 deg E to 160 deg E, New Zealand 160 deg E to 120 deg W, Chile 120 deg W to 67 deg 16 min W, Argentina 67 deg 16 min W to 20 deg W. [F, IHO WWNWS-13 self-assessments, 2021]. Search-and-rescue regions (SRRs) follow similar but not identical lines (section 5). **Australia's neighbors in the ring are South Africa (west) and New Zealand (east). Australia does not border Chile or Argentina.** This is the most useful new structural fact: it reframes "Australia to South America" as a chain through New Zealand (or over the pole), and "Australia to South Africa" as a direct adjacency.
2. **A direct Australia to South Africa search-and-rescue arrangement exists: signed 23 September 1998 and registered with ICAO and IMO.** South Africa also has signed SAR agreements with Argentina (8 September 2006) and with France for La Reunion (31 May 2001). [F, ICAO Special AFI RAN Meeting information paper SP AFI/08-IP/5, Durban, October 2008.] The earlier South Africa log said "no formal bilateral agreement found"; that was true of the Antarctic programs (AAD and SANAP) but not of the rescue centers.
3. **No bilateral Antarctic-program agreement exists between Australia and Chile, Argentina or South Africa in anything I could open.** The Australian Antarctic Division's (AAD) formal agreements named in its own 2012 magazine are with China, Russia and France. [F, antarctica.gov.au magazine issue 23, December 2012.] South Africa's own 2004 list of SANAP collaborators (Germany, UK, USA, Poland, New Zealand, Malaysia, Norway, Sweden; logistics help to India, Finland, Russia, Japan, France) does not name Australia. [F, SANAP letter to the Australian parliamentary committee, 14 May 2004.] What does exist is thin and specific: an Australia-Chile whale-research agreement with a scientist exchange via Hobart (27 June 2008), and INACH reporting "five projects" with Australia in 2016.
4. **Real operational contact between Australia and South Africa is documented but episodic**: the 2003 Viarsa 1 pursuit; the 1998 SAR arrangement; a 2008 Indian Ocean medevac; and the shared role of French La Reunion as a back-up rescue center "around the SAR coordination boundary between Australia and South Africa". France is the hinge between the two on sub-Antarctic enforcement (France-Australia treaty 2003; France-South Africa agreement 2016).
5. **Weather physically moves west to east around the ring (Cape to Perth about 3 days; Perth to Hobart about 1 day; Hobart to Punta Arenas about 4 to 5 days; Punta Arenas to Cape Town about 3 days at the upper-troposphere wave-packet group speed).** [I, with an F input for the speed; section 8.] That gives each nation a concrete upstream neighbor whose observations it needs: South Africa for Australia, Australia and New Zealand for Chile and Argentina, South America for South Africa.
6. **The observing networks are organized around a US hub and a Hobart office, not around bilateral ties.** The SOOS International Project Office is in Hobart. Argo floats deployed from South African ships are all supplied by other countries (WHOI, UK Met Office, University of Washington); South Africa procures none. Argentina's navy deployed three US-supplied floats from its icebreaker. [F, details in section 1.]
7. **Merino claim killed as stated; miners claim not confirmed as a mass link** (section 6).

## 1. Southern Ocean observing cooperation

### 1.1 SOOS (Southern Ocean Observing System)

- The SOOS International Project Office is in Hobart, hosted by a consortium (University of Tasmania IMAS, CSIRO, Tasmanian State Government); SOOS is governed under SCAR and SCOR. [F-snippet, search summary of soos.aq and IMAS pages, 2020-2022.] The 2019 annual report confirms the Hobart hosting for 2020 to 2022 and thanks the Australian Research Council's Antarctic Gateway Partnership for the previous five years of funding. [F, https://www.soos.aq/images/soos/about_us/Annual%20Reports/SOOSAnnualReport-2019.pdf]
- The first SOOS Symposium (more than 250 people from 25 nations) was held in Hobart. [F-snippet.]
- 2019 Scientific Steering Committee (SSC) by country: Sweden, Australia (Constable, co-chair), USA (2), France, New Zealand, China, Turkey, Canada, **Argentina (Irene Schloss, biology)**, UK, **South Africa (Sarah Fawcett, biogeochemistry)**. **No Chilean SSC member in 2019**, though Chile (J. Arata, S. Buchan, F. Castro) appears in subgroup lists. Regional Working Groups (RWGs): five regional groups plus a Southern Ocean Regional Panel; 2019 workshops in Incheon (South Korea), with the Amundsen-Bellingshausen RWG workshop (6 countries) and a Dronning Maud Land RWG. [F, SOOS Annual Report 2019.]
- SOOS in 2019 advised on rescue of historic Argentine oceanographic data and advised Australian AUV (underwater vehicle) groups on data management. [F, same report.]
- **[I]** SOOS is a Hobart-centered coordination office with an Argentine and a South African seat at the steering table. It is not a tie among the three nations; it is a tie of each to Hobart.

### 1.2 Argo floats (who deploys, from which ports)

Floats for 2022 take-over cruises were confirmed from the UK Met Office, WHOI and University of Washington. The takeover transect is Cape Town to Marion and back. An infrastructure fund to buy floats was approved but "overall budgets have been cut". [F, https://argo.ucsd.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/361/2022/03/SAfrica_NatRep_AST23.pdf, 2022]
- **WHOI deployment plan, December 2024**: floats "loaded in Cape Town, South Africa on the R/V S.A. Agulhas II" (4 S2A floats); "to be loaded in Cape Town ... for the Marion Island cruise" (6 core floats); "loaded in Cape Town on the R/V Pelagia" (6); "to be loaded in Montevideo, Uruguay on the R/V Hesperides" (5 core, 6 BGC); and **three WHOI floats "shipped to the Argentine Naval Hydrography Service"** (deployed at about 61.6 S 38.4 W, 59.7 S 49.9 W and 65.7 S 70.1 W; "encouraged to deploy floats in the regions of most need"). [F, https://argo.whoi.edu/argo/deployment/DeploymentPlans_Dec2024.pdf] The same document has no Chilean or Australian loading port in the Southern Ocean; its Wellington loading is on a New Zealand vessel. **[I]** The US program uses Cape Town and Montevideo as float ports; Hobart and Punta Arenas are absent from this one plan.
- **Argentina**: its navy deployed three Argo floats from the icebreaker Almirante Irizar near the Antarctic shelf break in a collaboration with WHOI supported by NOAA. [F-snippet, search summary.]
- **Australia**: Argo Australia deployed 55 floats between April 2024 and April 2025 (44 core, 7 BGC, 4 under-ice floats on the Antarctic shelf), from RV Investigator, Aurora Australis [sic, as listed in the summary], RV Kaharoa II, RV Whale Song, RV Sonne, RSV Nuyina, RV Umitaka Maru, MV L'Astrolabe, RV Tangaroa, HMNZS Aotearoa and the Japanese icebreaker Shirase. [F, same report.]
- **[I]** Float deployment is cross-national but is brokered through float-supplying programs (US, UK, Euro-Argo, Australia), not through any Australia-Chile-South Africa arrangement. I found no Argo national report from Chile in the searches. [U]

[F-snippet, IMOS pages.] No Chilean or South African partner appeared. [U beyond the snippets.]

### 1.5 WMO structure for Antarctica

- **Antarctica is not assigned to RA III (South America), RA V (South-West Pacific) or RA I (Africa).** WMO's polar expert panel (PHORS) "operates under the general terms of reference of regional associations with regard to the WMO activities in Antarctica, as in Annex II to the WMO General Regulations." [F, WMO circular 00934/2025/I/ONM/PHORS, 24 February 2025.]
- **EC-78 Resolution 28 (2024)** endorsed "a dialogue of WMO Members that are also party to the Antarctic Treaty System" for Antarctica and the Southern Ocean south of 60 deg S, "where Members with the ability to operate are the main actors." The 2025 survey letter went to 60 countries including Argentina, Australia, Chile and South Africa. [F, same circular.]
- WMO runs the WMO Antarctic Activities (WMOAA) program and the Antarctic Observing Network (AntON). [F, old.wmo.int Antarctica Purpose page, thin summary.] The 2014 AntON figures (106 surface stations, 15 upper-air stations, 6 with two soundings a day) are already in the earlier ring log.
- **[I]** The weather side of Antarctica is explicitly a "Members who can operate" club. That matches the five-ring structure, because the five rescue states are also the five that run the staging ports.

### 1.6 Radiosonde sharing and observing campaigns (YOPP-SH)

- During the Year of Polar Prediction Southern Hemisphere Special Observing Period (2018-19), **2,244 additional radiosondes were launched from 24 land stations and 5 ships; 17 nations contributed**; extra soundings roughly doubled routine launches (average 24 extra per day); most were sent to the WMO Global Telecommunications System; an open archive is hosted by the British Antarctic Survey; the Antarctic Mesoscale Prediction System monitored receipt at NCEP. Routine launchers listed include Mawson, Davis, Casey, Macquarie Island, Novolazarevskaya, Neumayer, Syowa, Halley, Rothera, Concordia, McMurdo, South Pole. [F, Bromwich et al. BAMS, October 2020, https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10232070]
- The same paper credits **the Australian RV Aurora Australis** with extra Southern Ocean radiosondes (October to December 2017, a Tasmania cyclone case); **South Africa's SA Agulhas II** deployed surface velocity buoys in the Weddell Sea in 2019 (five active in the period); and a **University of Magallanes (Punta Arenas) project (DACAPO-PESO)** measured water vapor at Punta Arenas during an atmospheric river that also hit King George Island on 4-7 December 2018. Authors list affiliations in Hobart (Bureau of Meteorology, UTAS), Punta Arenas (UMAG) and Santiago. [F, same paper.] Chile's INACH is thanked for logistics.
- Southern Hemisphere forecast skill lags the Northern; extra radiosondes gave the greatest improvement for deep cyclones near the Antarctic coast. [F-snippet; F in the paper.] The earlier ring log already has this.
- **South African sub-Antarctic upper-air**: Marion Island (46 S, 37 E) radiosonde balloons since 1948 and a weather station run by the South African Weather Service; Gough Island (40 S, 9 W) station since 1956 with hourly observations and upper-air ascents, 2,600 km from Cape Town. [F-snippet, SANAP and SAWS pages.] **[I]** Gough, Marion and Macquarie (Australia) are three upper-air points in the 40 to 55 S westerly belt; plus the French Crozet, Kerguelen and Amsterdam stations between them. I found no source that says Bureau of Meteorology models ingest Marion or Gough data specially. [U]

### 1.7 Ozone and baseline air

- The SHADOZ ozonesonde network is tropical and subtropical (Ascension, Nairobi, Irene in South Africa, Reunion, Java, Fiji, Tahiti, Samoa, Galapagos, Natal). It does not include the Southern Ocean ring sites. [F-snippet.] The relevant networks are NDACC (Lauder in New Zealand is its main Southern Hemisphere mid-latitude site) and WMO Global Atmosphere Watch (GAW). [F-snippet.]
- **Cape Grim (Tasmania)** is one of the three premier GAW baseline stations, sampling "true background" air arriving across the Southern Ocean; **Cape Point (Western Cape)** is a GAW global station run by the South African Weather Service. [F-snippet, CSIRO and SAWS pages.] **[I]** Two baseline stations that sample the same westerly belt at opposite ends of the Indian Ocean; no inter-station agreement found. [U]
- I found no Chilean or Argentine ozone-station roles in the searches. [U]

### 1.8 Voluntary Observing Ships

- WMO VOS ships are recruited by Port Meteorological Officers of all flags; the VOS chair encouraged recruiting ships that work in Antarctic waters or fitting automatic weather stations. [F-snippet, ocean-ops.org VOS pages.] I found no port-specific PMO list for Hobart, Cape Town, Punta Arenas or Ushuaia. [U]

## 4. Sea-ice information services in the southern ring

- **Operators in the Southern Hemisphere**: "Argentina, Australia, Chile, China, Denmark, Germany, Norway, Russia and the USA" are listed as services with Southern Ocean ice-chart capacity; Argentina's Naval Hydrographic Service (SHN) covers its METAREA VI (Antarctic Peninsula, Bellingshausen Sea, Weddell Sea and southwestern Atlantic) for research vessels, tourist cruises and fishing. [F, Frontiers in Marine Science 2022, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.971894/full (summarizer)]
- **Circumpolar charts**: "ice charts around Antarctica have been produced jointly by the U.S., Russia, and Norway with Argentina and Chile contributing complementary regional charts." Argentina's SHN began daily ice charts for METAREA VI in 2015, **Chile began regular ice charts three years later (2018)**; Argentina began iceberg-density charts in 2022; **Australia signed the International Ice Charting Working Group (IICWG) Charter in 2023**. [F, Frontiers in Marine Science 2025 history of the IICWG, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2025.1563900/full (summarizer)]
- IICWG 23rd meeting, Buenos Aires, 26-30 September 2022 (hosted by the Argentine SHN, 19 countries): "Argentina and Chile have both implemented routine public ice information programs ... **the Australian Bureau of Meteorology is developing technical and human resources to increase its ice information capacity**." [F, https://comnap.aq/s/IICWG-XXIII_Executive_Summary_Report-received-12-October-2022.pdf, 2022]
- **SOLOKI (Southern Ocean Limit of Known Ice)**: an IICWG task team led by Jan Lieser and Andrew Fleming (both associated with Australian Antarctic work; presented at IICWG-22, September 2021) works on automated iceberg detection from satellite images and iceberg-trajectory modeling "as a maritime safety initiative in response to an increasing number of icebergs drifting northward into shipping lanes." The loss of Sentinel-1B (December 2021) cut Southern Ocean observing capacity; Argentina's SAOCOM L-band data partially mitigated it. [F, IICWG-XXIII summary; F-snippet for the SOLOKI presentation title and date.] A search summary adds that container ships avoiding Panama could reach high southern latitudes on Australia/New Zealand to Cape Horn passages with "no special measures currently in place". [F-snippet.]
- **2006 snapshot** (WMO-No. 574, 3rd ed.): Argentina's SMARA gives plain-text weekly sea-ice bulletins and a sea-ice edge limit "00 to 131 deg W" and iceberg positions in NAVAREA VI, uses US National Ice Center (NIC) analyses as source data, and has "no sea-ice charts ... regularly prepared"; Australia's Antarctic Meteorological Center at Casey provided on request, in the austral summer, sea-ice concentration analyses for "any Antarctic coastal area between around 180 E and 20 E"; New Zealand produced none; **Chile and South Africa are absent**. NIC produced weekly Arctic/Antarctic ice maps. [F, https://nsidc.org/sites/default/files/wmo_574.pdf, 2006]
- **Australian seasonal sea-ice forecasting**: Bureau of Meteorology ACCESS-S1/S2 (S2 introduced October 2021, using Bureau-generated initial conditions instead of UK Met Office ones); forecasting for Nuyina operations is provided remotely from Australia. [F-snippet, TC 2026 paper and AAD pages.]
- Argentina's SHN is on the 2006 WMO/IOC sea-ice list and in the IICWG; no Chile-Argentina-Australia joint product exists. **[I]** The shared product all of them use is the US/Russia/Norway circumpolar chart plus their own regional ones; the one genuinely shared Southern Ocean ice-safety project (SOLOKI) is IICWG-wide, led from Australia. I found no South African ice service. [U]
- Note on the SIPN South project (seasonal prediction) and the WMO Expert Team on Sea Ice: the searches returned only generic descriptions; no membership list. [U]

## 5. Antarctic SAR and navigational-area boundaries by longitude

### 5.1 The two instruments and why they differ

COMNAP's 2008 information paper says the Antarctic is covered by **5 maritime and 5 aeronautical SRRs**, with the maritime ones extending "all the way south to the Antarctic coast" and the aeronautical ones "all the way south to the geographic pole"; limits "are usually similar, but not necessarily identical"; and "NAVAREAS, SRRs and RCCs are not connected to sovereignty." RCCs "from west to east": MRCC Cape Town and ARCC Johannesburg (South Africa), RCC Australia (Canberra), RCC New Zealand (Wellington), MRCC and ARCC Punta Arenas (Chile), ARCC Comodoro Rivadavia and MRCC Ushuaia (Argentina). The paper's Appendix A map showing the boundaries is an image and has no coordinates. [F, ATCM XXXI IP 099 (COMNAP), 2008; also ATCM XXXII WP 047, 2009.] South Pole facilities "are considered included in New Zealand SRR". [F, SAR Workshop I report, 2008.]

## 6. Merinos in Patagonia, Falklands wool, and miners' migrations

### 6.1 The "first Merinos in Patagonia in 1926 died of the cold" claim: **killed as stated**

- The claim appears in one FarmingUK piece dated 12 January 2009 ("Australia, sheep from the Yorkshire Dales"): "In 1926 the first Merino sheep were imported to Patagonia in the far south of Argentina ... Unfortunately in the Antarctic winter they all died of the cold." The page gives no source. [F, https://www.farminguk.com/news/australia-sheep-from-the-yorkshire-dales-_9982.html] A second Spanish-language lead (Estancia La Adela in Chubut buying Merino rams from Australia, first in 1927) is a different, plausible, undated-source item. [F-snippet.]
- The Argentine sheep textbook chapter (produccion-animal.com.ar, INTA-linked authors cited) says the Merino "is intimately tied to the progress of Patagonia": Merino blood reached the north of Patagonia with Welsh colonists from 1865 (a Creole-Merino cross from the pampas); the "Argentine Merino" came from French (Rambouillet), German (Electoral) and Austrian (Negrete) strains and "adapted very well to Patagonia until the 1950s" but its heavily wrinkled body and covered face led to its **replacement by the Australian Merino**; the Corriedale (from New Zealand) was introduced to the south in 1905; the Falklands and southern Chile supplied Romney, Cheviot and Leicester flocks in the south from 1878. So Merinos were in Patagonia long before 1926 and did not "all die of cold". [F, https://produccion-animal.com.ar/produccion_ovina/produccion_ovina/000-ganado_lanar_en_argentina_libro/05-captitulo_1.pdf, undated Argentine textbook chapter]
- **What is true and Australian:** Australian Peppin-type and Medium and Strong Merinos displaced the old Argentine Merino from the 1950s; a FAO-INTA project began in 1968 with Australian geneticist Helen Newton Turner crossing Australian Merino rams on Corriedale ewes; Poll Merino rams were imported from Australia in 1984 (3,000 ewes inseminated at Estancia Condor, Santa Cruz); Booroola semen in 1986; Peppin rams from Estancia Leleque (Chubut) were used in the project. The text warns that the Peppin type "lacks the build and resistance to withstand the cold desert" while the Strong Merino suits it. [F, same chapter.]
- **Modern flows**: Australian Merino embryos and semen exported to Chile and Argentina (over 40,000 semen straws a year, as one exporter says); an Australian ram sold for A$34,000 for a Chubut insemination program covering about 20,000 ewes; a South American "Multi Purpose Merino" network across Chile, Argentina and Uruguay with more than 800,000 sheep. [F-snippet.]
- **Uruguay** was the first South American importer of Australian Merinos (1956). [F, from the earlier log.]
- **Falklands**: 500 Polwarth ewes and 25 Polwarth rams from Tasmanian studs were introduced in the early 1990s; after 2010 the flock moved to Merino imports from Australian studs, "Merino genetics, semen and embryos imported annually"; Falklands wool and genetics moved "barged or ferried up to Uruguay for testing" and sold through English brokers. [F, Farm Weekly, https://www.farmweekly.com.au/story/8716879/falkland-islands-sheep-boosted-by-australian-merino-genetics (summarizer)] The Polwarth, bred in western Victoria from about 1880, has been exported to South America (where it is called "Ideal"); breeders there have imported rams since the early 1900s. [F-snippet, Polwarth centenary publication (1980).]
- **Magallanes (Chile)**: sheep farming began with Falklands flocks (Fenton Station introduced "Falklands sheep to the south of Chile in 1884"); settlers came "mainly from England, Scotland or Australasia"; the only Australasian manager named in the Warwick history is the New Zealander Alexander Allan Cameron, general manager of Sociedad Explotadora de Tierra del Fuego 1905 to 1915. I found **no Tasmanian or Australian settlers or engineers** named in Patagonia in the searches. [F, MercoPress 2015; Warwick Global History blog.] [U] for Australian-born individuals.
- **Shearing**: Argentina's PROLANA program teaches Tally-Hi shearing and in 2017 looked to New Zealand, Australia, South Africa and the UK for the "New Pattern" method; a South African shearing instructor was due in Chubut for 2017/18. [F-snippet.]
- **[I] Reading:** a real, documented, ongoing Australian-to-Patagonia genetics flow exists (from the 1950s and 1960s, strengthened by FAO-INTA in 1968), plus Falklands imports from Tasmania. It is economic and agronomic. It does not require, and does not provide, a communications link; it does give Hobart and Tasmanian stud farms a reason to know Punta Arenas, Rio Gallegos and Port Stanley.

## 8. Weather conveyor along the ring (calculation)

Input [F]: in the Southern Hemisphere, baroclinic wave packets propagate east at about the upper-troposphere Rossby group velocity, drawn in the source as **27.5 m/s**, with downstream suppression of activity following at about **11 m/s** (the lower-to-mid-tropospheric flow). [F, Boljka, Thompson and Li, J. Climate 2021, Fig. 1 and text, https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10256986] A search summary adds that Rossby wave packet activity is nearly continuous downwind of South Africa in austral summer and that some packets last over 30 days and circle the hemisphere up to three times. [F-snippet.]

Arithmetic [I]: eastward along a parallel (R = 6371.0088 km) from city longitudes (Punta Arenas 70.9 W, Cape Town 18.42 E, Perth 115.86 E, Hobart 147.33 E):

| Leg (eastward) | Arc | Distance at 45 S | Time at 27.5 m/s | Time at 11 m/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Punta Arenas to Cape Town | 89.3 deg | 7,023 km | 3.0 days | 7.4 days |
| Cape Town to Perth | 97.4 deg | 7,661 km | 3.2 days | 8.1 days |
| Perth to Hobart | 31.5 deg | 2,474 km | 1.0 day | 2.6 days |
| Hobart to Punta Arenas (via the Pacific) | 141.8 deg | 11,147 km | 4.7 days | 11.7 days |
| Cape Town to Hobart | 128.9 deg | 10,136 km | 4.3 days | 10.7 days |
| Perth to Punta Arenas | 173.2 deg | 13,621 km | 5.7 days | 14.3 days |

(At 50 S the distances are about 9 percent shorter.) One full lap around 45 S is about 28,300 km, about 12 days at 27.5 m/s.

**[I] Reading:** the upstream-downstream order around the ring is South America, South Africa, Australia, New Zealand, South America. Weather that reaches Perth has passed Cape Town about 3 days earlier; weather that reaches Punta Arenas passed Hobart 4 to 5 days earlier. Forecasts for Australia gain from South African and sub-Antarctic upstream observations at lead times of roughly 3 days; for Chile and Argentina, from Australian and New Zealand observations at roughly 4 to 5 days. That is a physical reason for observation exchange along the ring order, stronger for the adjacent pair South Africa to Australia than for an Australia-to-South America data link. I found no document in which a national weather service says this about its own upstream neighbor. [U]

## 11. What this changes in the earlier logs' ranking

**Confirms**
- Ring of five (not a triangle) and the top ranking of SAR/medevac/gateway logistics: confirmed and sharpened. The five tiles tile 360 degrees of coast with no gap; each rescue center has bilateral machinery with its two neighbors.
- Direct bilateral Antarctic-program ties between the three nations are weak: confirmed (AAD's MOUs are China, Russia, France; SANAP's 2004 list omits Australia; INACH's Asia-Pacific partners are Japan, Korea, China).
- Gateway Cities 2009 statement lapsed: confirmed, with the correction that it lasted 18 months (not 2.5 years) per the project's own authors.
- Aurora Australis donation to Argentina never happened: confirms the earlier "no confirmed outcome" and resolves it.
- Weather-data scarcity as a durable reason: confirmed, and YOPP-SH adds numbers (2,244 extra radiosondes, 17 nations, 24 stations plus 5 ships).

**Overturns or corrects**
- **Chile's "Antarctic sector 53 to 90 W" listed as an SAR boundary (ring log): wrong.** That is the claim sector. Chile's SRR reaches 131 W (Chile's 2025 slide); Argentina's reaches 10 W; the two together cover 10 W to 131 W (their patrol area).
- **"No formal bilateral agreement" between Australia and South Africa (South Africa log): partly overturned.** A signed, registered 1998 SAR arrangement exists (rescue-center level); the program-level gap stands.
- **"Australia to South America" as a direct SAR/institutional link (South America log, "nearest hub across the Pacific"): weakened.** Australia's Antarctic SAR neighbors are South Africa and New Zealand; the Chile side runs through New Zealand (NZ-Chile arrangement 2018 and SAR convenio, Joint Statement 2024). The great-circle geometry argument (over the pole) is unaffected; the institutional chain is Australia, New Zealand, Chile.
- **Sea-ice forecasting "no evidence that Australia, Chile and South Africa jointly produce" (ring log): refined.** Nobody among the three produces a joint product; Argentina (2015) and Chile (2018) issue regional charts; Australia is only now building capacity (IICWG charter 2023) but leads the SOLOKI iceberg-limit task team; the circumpolar chart is US/Russia/Norway.
- **WMO "polar panel not a normal regional association" (ring log, [U]): resolved.** Antarctica falls under PHORS "under the general terms of reference of regional associations"; EC-78 Resolution 28 (2024) created a dialogue among Members with Antarctic activity.

**Adds**
- Adjacency chain (South Africa, Australia, New Zealand, Chile, Argentina) and the NAVAREA/METAREA table with exact limits.
- The weather conveyor timing (Cape to Perth about 3 days; Hobart to Punta Arenas about 4 to 5 days).
- France as hinge (La Reunion SAR arrangements with both; France-Australia 2003 and France-South Africa 2016 enforcement agreements).
- South Africa as float-deployment platform with no float procurement; US WHOI as the float broker; Argentine navy deploying US floats; SOOS office in Hobart with Argentine and South African steering seats.
- Specific small Australia-Chile items (2008 whale cooperation and scientist exchange; INACH "five projects" 2016) and Australia-South Africa items (Macquarie-to-Marion eradication expertise; Viarsa; 1998 SAR).
- Merino: the 1926 anecdote is unsupported; the real Australian influence on Patagonian Merinos begins in the 1950s to 1960s and the Falklands received Tasmanian Polwarths in the 1990s. Wool/genetics is an economic tie to Tasmania, not a communications argument.

**Ranking effect (suggested, [I])**
- Raise: SAR adjacency and bilateral rescue arrangements (now documented pair by pair), and the weather-conveyor physical reason (new).
- Hold: gateway logistics, great-circle geometry, SKA.
- Lower: any claim of a direct institutional Australia-Chile or Australia-Argentina Antarctic tie (none found); Western Australia to Witwatersrand migration (unconfirmed); Merino heritage as a driver (economic only, and the 1926 anecdote is unsupported).
- Net design reading: for Australia the **natural direct partners are South Africa (west, 3 days upstream by weather, the adjacent rescue region, a signed 1998 SAR arrangement) and New Zealand (east)**; links to Chile and Argentina are best modeled as running through New Zealand and the Ross sector or over the pole, with the Peninsula-Punta Arenas-Ushuaia pair as the far end.
