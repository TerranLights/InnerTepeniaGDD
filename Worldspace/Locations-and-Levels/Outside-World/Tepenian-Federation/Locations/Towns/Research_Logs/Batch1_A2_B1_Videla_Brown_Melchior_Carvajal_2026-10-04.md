# Research report: four Antarctic Peninsula stations (Paradise Harbour x2, Melchior Islands, Adelaide Island)

All sources were accessed 2026-10-04 (today), so "(S#)" means "that source, accessed 2026-10-04". Source URLs are in the table at the end.

Reliability tags used below:
- **[V]** = I read the primary document text myself (PDF, CSV or API).
- **[F]** = obtained through the WebFetch summarizer, so wording is not guaranteed verbatim.
- **[U]** = seen only in a web-search summary, or only on Wikipedia or a blog. Treat as unverified.

The operating nation or program is **context only** throughout.

## Headline findings

1. **Three of the four stations are not closed in any real sense.**
   - COMNAP's "Temporarily Closed" flag for Brown and Melchior (Nov 2024 CSV) is an off-season program flag. Both are opened and staffed every Argentine summer. Brown was staffed from early Jan to mid-Mar 2026 with 11 people. Melchior was opened for 2025/26 (S9, S10, S12).
   - Gabriel González Videla (GGV) is "Open" in COMNAP and runs every summer.
   - **Only Carvajal has a real closure story.** Its runway became unusable, it was inactive for about a decade (dates disputed), and a reopening was announced for Jan 2026. I found no confirmation that the reopening actually happened.
2. **Carvajal is not on Leonie Island and is not "near Rothera" in a local sense.** It is Base T / Adelaide on the south tip of Adelaide Island. It sits 39.6 km from Rothera (computed from COMNAP coordinates). Its official name is Teniente Luis Carvajal Villarroel. It is not Prof. Julio Escudero, which is a different Chilean station on King George Island (S2). I could not verify any Leonie Island location (no SCAR gazetteer entry).
3. **The Waterboat Point historic site is HSM 56, not HSM 54.** HSM 54 is the Byrd bust at McMurdo (S4 [V]). There is also HSM 30, a 1950 shelter near GGV, and HSM 29, the Primero de Mayo lighthouse on Lambda Island next to Melchior.
4. **Brown and GGV are about 8.0 km apart**, not "a mile" (see Contradictions).
5. **Surface verdicts** (details per station):
   - GGV: rock-qualifies.
   - Brown: rock-qualifies.
   - Melchior: unclear.
   - Carvajal: station pad rock-qualifies, but its ice runway fails (see below).

## 1. Gabriel González Videla Base (Chile; context only)

**Verified coordinates**
- COMNAP 2024: -64.823862, -62.857499; elevation 6 m MSL (S1 [V]).
- COMNAP 2017: 64°49'25"S 62°51'26"W, altitude 6 m (S2 [V]). This differs from the 2024 value by about 31 m.
- Wikipedia: 64°49'26"S 62°51'25"W, 6 m (S37 [U], lead only).
- SCAR gazetteer, Waterboat Point: -64.8167, -62.85 (a point feature, S3 [V]).
- The 2012 inspection report writes "64.49'S 62.52'W", a typo for minutes (S5 [V]).
- Verdict: all agree within about 1 km. Elevation 6 m is consistent across sources.

**Surface and stability: rock-qualifies (confidence medium-high)**
- The station is on "a small rocky island a few acres in extent and connected to the continent by a small tidal causeway 50 metres in length covered at high tide". It has 7 buildings, mostly on higher ground (S5 [V]).
- SCAR gazetteer: Waterboat Point is "a small island joined to the mainland by a spit at low tide" (S3 [V]).
- COMNAP 2017: "Ice-free ground"; the facility-area features list includes Glacier, Crevasses, Moraines, Permanent snowpatches and Rock, so there is glacier ice in the area but not under the station (S2 [V]).
- Regional bedrock is Cretaceous volcanics and Andean plutons over Permo-Triassic Trinity Peninsula Group metasediments (Birkenmajer, S36 [F]).
- Ice: the station staff photograph the Torre Glacier annually to monitor its retreat (S5 [V]). I found no reported velocity.
- No reports found of relocation, burial, or damage by ice, erosion, landslide or fire.
- Hazards: the island is snow-covered in early season (helipad snow-covered on 11 Dec 2012; 2023 crew had to dig out entrances, S31 [F]). It is a low-lying tidal island at about 6 m. Calving in Paradise Harbour produces swell (Antarctic pilot text quoted by S37 [U]).

**Status and years (including the closure story)**
- Established January 1951 (SCAR, S3 [V]); Wikipedia says 12 March 1951 [U]; COMNAP says 1951.
- Occupied 1951-58. SCAR notes "Since 1964 the Chilean station has been only seasonally occupied." COMNAP 2017 says it was reopened in the early 1980s (S2, S3 [V]).
- Current: summer-only. The 2012 inspection says "Dec-March". COMNAP 2017 says "December-April". A FACh summary says Nov-Mar [U].
- COMNAP 2024 status is **Open**. There is no closure.
- The 2012 inspection found it "run jointly by the Chilean Air Force (providing the base commander) and the Chilean Navy". The Navy's tasks are maritime-traffic monitoring for the MRCC and first-response pollution (S5 [V]).
- Wikipedia calls it "inactive". That is stale or wrong (S37 [U]).

**Capacity**
- 2012: 13 present (9 Air Force + 4 Navy); "maximum capacity was 20" (S5 [V]).
- COMNAP 2017: 15 beds; 11 staff + 4 scientists = 15 (S2 [V]). COMNAP 2024 peak: 15 (S1 [V]).
- 2023/24: 14 people, 9 FACh + 5 Navy (S31 [F]).
- Winter: 0.

**Infrastructure** (mostly from the 2012 inspection, S5 [V])
- 7 buildings: main accommodation with a small lookout tower, generator building, fuel store about 30 m away, old boat shed used as the oil-spill store, and a former laboratory now a museum with a souvenir shop.
- 2 x 75 kVA generators plus 2 x 16 kVA emergency generators. One main generator was being repaired and a 65 kVA replacement was due.
- Fuel: 200 L drums of Antarctic gas oil (about 2.5 t), 10 JP1 drums for helicopters, propane for the kitchen.
- Water: ship-supplied reverse-osmosis water, tanks of 25 t, 7 t and 5 t (about 5 m3 per week), seawater for sanitation.
- Sewage: 3-tank biological treatment plant.
- Helipad (snow-covered in Dec). No land vehicles. 2 Mark V zodiacs.
- Comms: VHF channel 16, HF 3100 kHz link to Fildes, DSC, AIS, 2 Iridium phones, 256 kb internet.
- Gaps: no automatic fire or smoke detection; no defibrillator; a very small clinic with a nurse. Recommendations were to install fire alarms and review small-boat safety.
- COMNAP 2017 lists 595 m2 under roof and "Fossil fuel, Renewable" power. The 2012 report says "no alternative energy sources" (S2 [V]). The station has workshops (mechanical, metal, wood).
- A station jetty is used for landings (S8 [V]).

**Access**
- Chilean Navy ships from Punta Arenas (Lautaro, Aquiles and Viel in 2012). Helicopters from Eduardo Frei on King George Island; Frei is the casualty-evacuation hub (S5 [V]).
- COMNAP 2017: 30 ship visits per year; helipad yes; no airstrip.
- Distance to hospital 1,430 km (Punta Arenas).
- Nearest emergency facility in Antarctica 65 km (S2 [V]).
- Neighbors by computed COMNAP coordinates: Brown 8.0 km, Yelcho 34.8 km, Palmer 56.9 km, Vernadsky 80.7 km (S1 [V], my calculation).

**Other facilities within 25 km**
- Brown Station (Argentina), 8.0 km, seasonal, staffed each summer.
- Refugio Conscripto Ortiz (Argentine Navy, opened 29 Jan 1956 at Punta Beatriz, 200 m from Brown) (S43 [F], S2 [V]).
- Historic sites at the station: HSM 56 (Waterboat Point hut remains) and HSM 30 (1950 shelter honoring G. González Videla) (S4 [V]).
- No other COMNAP facility within 25 km (S1 [V]).

**Stated purpose** (context only)
- 2012: summer maritime-traffic and pollution-response base, subsidiary to Fildes Maritime Station. Science is only a basic met station plus penguin counts and Torre Glacier photos for INACH. The inspectors noted a "shift in focus away from science and towards tourism and... historic interest" (S5 [V]).
- COMNAP: environmental science, geology, glaciology, marine biology.
- 2023 crew: also houses the Port Authority of Paradise Bay (S31 [F]).

**Protected areas and visitors**
- HSM 56 (Rec. XVI-11, proposing Parties Chile/UK, both managing) and HSM 30 (Rec. VII-9, Chile) (S4 [V]).
- I found no ASPA or ASMA at or near the site. No ATCM visitor site guideline exists for Waterboat Point, Brown or Melchior; none are on the ATS list (S35 [V]).
- Station-run visitor control (2012): about 2,000 visitors in 2011/12, groups of 40 or fewer, sensitive penguin areas roped off. A museum and shop are open (S5 [V]).
- Advance notice must be given before visiting (S8 [V]).
- IAATO recorded 35 visits in 2006/07 (S34 [V]).
- Annex V Art. 8(4): listed HSMs may not be damaged, removed or destroyed (S47 [U]).

**Other notes**
- COMNAP 2017 climate: mean annual temperature -6.7 C, July -12 C, Feb -1.9 C, precipitation 915 mm, max wind 70 km/h (NW), snow-free January-March, sea ice breakup December (S2 [V]).
- The site is in the middle of a large gentoo colony. Penguins nest at the entrance of the boat shed (S5 [V]).

**Revival realism**
- Nothing to revive. It is operating each summer, with 13-15 people and 2,000 or more tourists per season. Its limits are early-season snow burial, no fire detection (2012), a small tidal island of a few acres, and the heritage-museum mission.

## 2. Brown Station (Almirante Brown, Argentina; context only)

**Verified coordinates**
- COMNAP 2024: -64.89537, -62.870452; elevation 22 m (S1 [V]).
- COMNAP 2017: 64°53'43.3"S 62°52'13.6"W, 22 m (S2 [V]); about 1 m from the 2024 value.
- Wikipedia: 64°53'44"S 62°52'15"W [U].
- Cancillería and Armada pages: 64°52'S 62°54'W / 64°53'S 62°53'W (rounded) (S15 [F]).
- SCAR: it sits on Coughtrey Peninsula (-64.9, -62.867), "small hook-shaped peninsula at the N side of the entrance to Skontorp Cove" (S3 [V]).
- COMNAP 2017 location text: "Proa Head, Sanavirón/Coughtrey Peninsula" (S2 [V]).

**Surface and stability: rock-qualifies (confidence medium-high)**
- Oceanites (2011): a "point of land with steep sea-cliffs at least 100 meters high on one side... and the sheer face of a tide-water glacier on the other side, to the E". Gentoo nests are "on the bedrock below the ruins of the main, derelict station building". It is on the mainland, with a 30-50 m slope behind the station (S8 [V]).
- COMNAP 2017: "Ice-free ground", features "Bay Islands, Rock", permafrost discontinuous (S2 [V]).
- Marambio.aq: "península Sanavirón, un macizo rocoso con un morro de casi 70 metros" (S16 [F]).
- Regional geology is Cretaceous Andean intrusives over Trinity Peninsula Group (S36 [F]).
- Ice: a tidewater glacier is adjacent. No velocity or retreat figures were found for it. Calving swell is a known harbour hazard (S37 [U]).
- Damage history is fire, not ice. A fire in July 1951 was rebuilt in Jan 1952 (S3 [V]). A fire on 12 Apr 1984 destroyed the main house (see below).
- No report found of relocation, burial or landslide.
- Caveat: the Oceanites description is of the burnt old station (ruins), with the current modules adjacent. COMNAP says the facilities are the Ortiz Hut plus the old main facilities, "linked by a narrow beach" (S2 [V]). Space ashore is limited.

**Status and years (including the closure story)**
- 6 Apr 1951: Naval Detachment Almirante Brown, meteorological observatory (S2, S3 [V]).
- Jul 1951 fire, rebuilt Jan 1952. 29 Jan 1956: Ortiz naval refuge opened 200 m away. Closed Dec 1959 / 1959-60. Transferred to the Argentine Antarctic Institute (IAA) 29 Nov 1964. Reopened 17 Feb 1965 as a permanent science station (S9, S16, S43).
- **12 Apr 1984 fire** destroyed the main building; the emergency house, aquarium, tide gauge and main power plant survived (S43 [F], S42 [F]).
- The fire was set by the station's doctor, who did not want to winter (en-Wikipedia and Spanish accounts, S37 [U]). The crew was evacuated by USS Hero. Destination is disputed: Palmer (S43 [F]) or Ushuaia (S42 [F]).
- Rebuilding:
  - Sporadic summer operation from 1988-89.
  - 1995-96: two modular units (laboratory and residence).
  - 1999-2000: new main house for 8 people (4 bedrooms, kitchen, 2 baths).
  - 2010-11: dock and maintenance work by a 12-person team over 90 days, plus an automatic weather station (S42 [F], S43 [F], S16 [F]).
- It is officially "temporary" since 1984 (S15 [F]).
- **It is not closed.** Recent operation: 2022-23 open 53 days with 11 people (S12 [F]); 2024/25 opened at the Jan 2025 reopening (S11 [F]); 2025/26 early Jan to about 15-16 Mar 2026 with 11 people (S9 [V]).
- On 11 Dec 2012 it was "still unoccupied" (S5 [V]), i.e. it opens in mid-season.
- The COMNAP "Temporarily Closed" flag is a snapshot taken while the station was not yet staffed that season.

**Capacity**
- COMNAP 2017: max 12 (8 staff + 4 scientists); 178 m2 under roof; 41 m2 labs; 53 m2 logistics; "Number of beds 0" [sic] (S2 [V]).
- COMNAP 2024: peak 12 (S1 [V]). Actual crews: 11 (2022-23 and 2026).
- Winter: 0.

**Infrastructure**
- Main house, residence and laboratory modules, power plant, Ortiz refuge, jetty.
- Power: 220 V, 24 h, fossil fuel. Comms: satellite phone and VHF. 2 zodiacs. Medical: none (a first-aid-trained person only).
- Weather station (2010-11). Basic general repair capability. No helipad, no airstrip (S2 [V], S16 [F]).
- Fuel storage details: "two folding 30,000 L tanks" appears only in en-Wikipedia [U].
- 2010: a casualty at Brown could not be treated because there was no doctor or nurse. GGV's nurse treated the patient and the casualty was shipped to Frei (S5 [V]).

**Access**
- By sea only. Argentine icebreaker ARA Almirante Irízar closes the base with its Beach Group and EDPV landing craft (S9 [V]).
- COMNAP 2017: jetty; 100 ship visits per year (mostly tourist ships); distance to hospital 1,162 km (Ushuaia); closest emergency facility in Antarctica 306.7 km (S2 [V]).
- In practice GGV, 8 km away, is the nearest help.

**Other facilities within 25 km**
- GGV (Chile), 8.0 km.
- Ortiz refuge, 200 m.
- Historic sites HSM 30 and HSM 56 are at GGV (about 8 km away, S4 [V]).
- No other COMNAP facility within 25 km. Yelcho is 33.7 km, Palmer 57.5 km, Vernadsky 75.8 km (S1 [V], my calculation).

**Stated purpose** (context only)
- COMNAP 2017: multidisciplinary coastal-environment research program in Paradise Bay (oceanography, chemical, physical and biological data) (S2 [V]).
- 2026: research on mammals, birds and fish, plus the IWC SORP cetacean project (S9 [V]). Science projects in 2025-26 were penguins, flying birds and ichthyology (S45 [F]).
- Argentine officials stress its visibility to the cruise ships and yachts that pass it daily (S45 [F]).

**Protected areas and visitors**
- No ASPA, ASMA or HSM at Brown itself. The nearest HSMs are at GGV. There is no formal ATCM visitor site guideline for it (S35 [V]).
- Heavy tourism: IAATO landings at "Brown Station" were 90 (2019-20), 40 (2021-22), 61 (2022-23), 65 (2023-24) and 57 (2024-25) (S33 [V]). The same site had 97 in 2006-07 (S34 [V]).
- The only possible shore landing is at the station itself, with limited walking space and snow-masked drums and artifacts (S8 [V]).
- Tourists can land when the base is not staffed (S38 [U], a first-hand blog).

**Other notes**
- COMNAP 2017 climate: mean annual temperature -2.4 C, July -6.9 C; snow-free January-February; mean wind 22.3 km/h from the W; sea-ice breakup October.
- Blue-eyed shag cliff colonies are south of the station (S8 [V]).

**Revival realism**
- Not needed; it already runs every summer on a 3-month, 11-person pattern.
- Evidence that year-round use is not planned: it has been "temporary" since 1984. COMNAP 2017 mentions only "renovation of installations... to strengthen logistic and scientific capabilities". I found no announced year-round plan or budget.
- The en-Wikipedia claim of a "plan to renovate completely for potential year-round use" is unverified [U].

## 3. Melchior Station (Argentina; context only)

**Verified coordinates**
- COMNAP 2024: -64.325705, -62.976329; elevation 4 m (S1 [V]).
- COMNAP 2017: 64°19'54.2"S 62°58'58.0"W, 4 m (S2 [V]). **This differs from the 2024 value by about 740 m.**
- Wikipedia: 64°19'33"S 62°58'36"W, 4 m [U]. This is about 22 m from COMNAP 2024.
- Cancillería and Armada: 64°20'S 62°59'W, with a typo "62°65'W" on the Armada page (S15 [F]). IPIEC gives "64°19'5''" (typo).
- SCAR: the station is on Gamma Island (Argentine name Isla Observatorio) "at the NE end of the island, S of Gallows Point", opened 31 Jan 1947. Gallows Point is -64.3333, -62.9833. COMNAP 2017 is 181 m from Gallows Point; COMNAP 2024 is 913 m from it (S3 [V], my calculation).
- Verdict: the station location is good to about 1 km only. Use the SCAR description of the NE end of Gamma Island, south of Gallows Point.
- Elevation: 4 m (COMNAP, Wikipedia) versus "8 m above sea level" (Casa Rosada 2018 and Marambio, S14 [F], S16 [F]).

**Surface and stability: unclear (confidence low)**
- For rock:
  - Marambio and Asociación Polar: "rocky terrain"; the 1947 construction required dynamiting rock. The site is about 8 m asl (S16 [F], S46 [F]).
  - COMNAP 2017 facility-area features: "Bird colonies, Coast, Rock" (S2 [V]).
  - A search summary says "sobre roca a 8 metros" [U].
- For ice:
  - COMNAP 2017 "Type of surface facility built on: Ice-sheet, Moraine"; permafrost "Continuous" (S2 [V]).
  - Oceanites calls the group "many low, ice-covered islands" with "a paucity of wildlife" (S8 [V]).
  - Spanish Wikipedia citing Pierrou (1970): Isla Observatorio is 2 km long, maximum 140 m, "cubierta completamente por una gruesa capa de hielo", with coasts "formadas por altos acantilados de hielo". The English page says 1.6 km long with a 140 m high point (S37 [U]).
- Regional geology: the Anvers-Melchior Islands block of volcanics plus granite, diorite and tonalite plutons (search summary [U]).
- No ice-velocity or bedrock-depth data found.
- The 1947 base was a 27 x 7.5 m prefab (double-insulated) (S16 [F]). It has stood for about 80 years with no reported relocation or ice damage found. That is mild evidence of a stable pad, but the station is at sea level beside an ice-capped island.
- Hazards: the max recorded wind was 222 km/h (NW) per COMNAP 2017; sea-ice breakup October; snow-free only in January; precipitation 1,308.7 mm (S2 [V]).

**Status and years (including the closure story)**
- Established 31 Mar 1947 by Argentine sources (or 31 Jan 1947 per SCAR, S3 [V]); built in 47 days. In 1952 it was the main Antarctic weather forecasting source; an astronomical station was added in 1955; an IGY tide gauge in 1957-58 (S16 [F], S2 [V]).
- Closed as a permanent base 30 Nov 1961. Since then it has been seasonal. It was reopened in 1968-69 for naval hydrographic and marine-biology work (S16 [F]).
- Recent operation: 2017/18 reopened 7 Jan 2018 for 54 days with 11 people, ship Canal Beagle (S13 [F], S14 [F]); 2022-23 open **22 days with 7 people** (S12 [F]); 2024/25 listed among bases for hydrographic and buoy work (S44 [F]); 2025/26 opened with Hydrography Department staff installing AIS equipment (S10 [F]).
- It is not closed. The COMNAP "Temporarily Closed" flag is an off-season flag. The Nov 2024 CSV also shows Cámara and Primavera as "Open" even though Cámara had been closed for two years until Jan 2025 (S11 [F]). That shows the flag is not a consistent indicator.

**Capacity**
- COMNAP 2017: 15 beds, max 15; 12 staff + 0 scientists; 336 m2 under roof; 48 m2 logistics (S2 [V]).
- COMNAP 2024 peak: 15 (S1 [V]).
- Casa Rosada 2018: five structures. Asociación Polar: 4 buildings (2008) "maximum 36 people" (S46 [F]), which conflicts with 15.
- Real crews: 7 to 11. Winter: 0.

**Infrastructure**
- Laboratory, lounge, kitchen, food store, workshop (Cancillería text as reproduced by IPIEC, S17 [V]).
- 6 m2 infirmary with a paramedic. Pier or jetty. 2 zodiacs. 220 V, 24 h, fossil fuel. Comms: satellite phone and VHF. Waste, hazardous-waste and fuel-spill capability are all listed "Yes" (S2 [V]).
- No helipad, no airstrip.
- Historic items: the 1942 Primero de Mayo lighthouse on Lambda Island (still lit, HSM 29).

**Access**
- By ship only: ARA Almirante Irízar, the aviso ARA Puerto Argentino, or ARA Canal Beagle (S10, S13).
- COMNAP 2017: 5 ship visits per year (Dec-Feb); distance to hospital 1,103 km (S2 [V]).
- Nearest others: GGV 55.7 km, Brown 63.5 km, Yelcho 67.7 km, Palmer 71.6 km (S1 [V], my calculation).

**Other facilities within 25 km**
- None in COMNAP (S1 [V]). The Lambda Island lighthouse (HSM 29) is in the same island group (S4 [V]).

**Stated purpose** (context only)
- COMNAP 2017: terrestrial biology, mainly botany. History: "mainly for hydrographic surveys" (S2 [V]).
- 2025/26: AIS installation. 2024/25: hydrographic, oceanographic and buoy-maintenance work (S10 [F], S44 [F]).
- IPIEC (a provincial statistics institute reproducing Cancillería text): climate-change research (S17 [V]).

**Protected areas and visitors**
- HSM 29 (lighthouse, Rec. VII-9, 1972 listing; at 64°18'S 62°59'W) (S4 [V]). I found no ASPA or ASMA. It is not on the ATS visitor-guideline list (S35 [V]).
- The site is "dangerous, difficult, or impossible to access; appropriately visited only by zodiac cruising" (S8 [V]). A cruise report says only "a former Argentinian research station... abandoned many years ago" (S48 [F]); that is a tourist's impression made while it was closed for the season.

**Other notes**
- Climate: COMNAP 2017 gives mean annual temperature -2.9 C, July -9.5 C, February 1.5 C. Marambio gives -3.6 C annual, absolute minimum -36.6 C (Aug 1958).

**Revival realism**
- It is already operated each summer by the Joint Antarctic Command. Crew size and days open vary (7 people for 22 days in 2022-23, 11 for 54 days in 2018). Permanent revival would need a decision on the surface-type question first.

## 4. Carvajal Station (Chile; formerly BAS Base T / Adelaide)

**Verified coordinates**
- COMNAP 2024: -67.761322, -68.914815; elevation 92 m MSL (S1 [V]).
- COMNAP 2017: 67°45'37.7"S 68°54'53.4"W, altitude 4 m (S2 [V]). It is 94 m from the 2024 value.
- Wikipedia: 67°45'40"S 68°54'52"W, 4 m [U].
- INACH: 67°45'40.8"S 68°54'53.3"W (S30 [F]).
- SCAR: "Adelaide" -67.7667, -68.9167, at the S point of Adelaide Island (S3 [V]).
- Rivera et al. 2005 give Fuchs Ice Piedmont at 67°45'09"S 68°55'04"W, about 1 km north of the station (S7 [F]).
- Elevation: 4 m (COMNAP 2017, Wikipedia) versus **92 m (COMNAP 2024)**; unresolved. The ice-runway area spans sea level to 325 m (S7 [F]), so 92 m may refer to something else.

**Surface and stability**

Two separate verdicts:
- **Station pad: rock-qualifies, confidence medium.**
- **Ice runway: ice-fails.** This is the reason for the closure.

Station pad evidence:
- Rivera et al. 2005 (Annals of Glaciology 41:57-62): "On the southern edge of Adelaide Island one of these ice-free areas is occupied by the Chilean Teniente Carvajal station", with "very few rock outcrops" along the Fuchs Ice Piedmont margin. "The slope steepens only at the contact between the ice and the rock outcrop near Teniente Carvajal station." The southern edge "where Carvajal station is located, was much more stable" (S7 [F], read via the Cambridge core-reader; the PDF was blocked).
- COMNAP 2017: "Ice-free ground", altitude 4 m; features include Rock, Glacier, Crevasses (S2 [V]).
- ASPA 117 plan: the neighboring Avian Island, 1.2 km away, has bedrock of Late Cretaceous volcaniclastic sandstones (Mount Liotard Formation) in a down-faulted block "at the southwestern end of Adelaide Island" (S6 [V]). This is adjacent-site bedrock, not a borehole at the station.
- Abinantarctica blog (unverified identity, 2012): the base is on "an isolated rocky bluff with a steep, icy ramp giving access to the main ice-shelf" (S39 [F]).

Ice and runway evidence (S7 [F]):
- The runway is on a local ice divide of the Fuchs Ice Piedmont, separating flow to the SW and S.
- Near-runway velocity is "smaller than the errors"; maximum 9 +/- 0.5 cm/day at both margins of the divide.
- The glacier front is grounded or nearly so. 1976-2001 area loss averaged 1.1 km2 per year.
- Snow conditions "progressively deteriorated" through increasingly early summer melt, with crevasses appearing earlier each season.
- Conclusion: skiway deterioration is real. They recommend searching for crevasse-free runways at higher elevations.
- Chilean reporting: a 2002 geological study by the Valdivia scientific center found cracks in the landing zone (S20, S23 [F]).
- Wikipedia (lead only): "the skiway and 'ramp' to the station from the plateau have all become so unstable" (S37 [U]).

Other points:
- No report found of the station itself being moved, buried or threatened. BAS abandoned the site in 1977 because of the skiway, not the buildings.
- Hazard: exposure to ocean swell makes ship resupply "dangerous" (blog [U]). COMNAP 2017 lists "Ship landing facilities: None" (S2 [V]).
- Max wind 174 km/h, dominant NE (S2 [V]).

**Status and years (including the closure story)**
- BAS Base T / Adelaide was established 3 Feb 1961 on Adelaide Island's southwest tip, chosen over Rothera Point for "a better skiway for aircraft and less sea ice". Continuous occupation to 1 Mar 1977. The skiway deteriorated and operations moved to Rothera in 1976-77 (S37 [U], S3 [V] on dates).
- Transferred to Chile 14 Aug 1984. SCAR: Chile's station "Teniente Carvajal was established here in January 1985". COMNAP: renamed in May 1985 (S2, S3 [V]). The ASPA plan says summer-only since 1982 (S6 [V]).
- FACh ran it in austral summers on Twin Otter ski aircraft (October start). Chilean sources disagree on when it stopped:
  - FACh announcements and news: ceased "2014-2015 season" due to cracks in the landing zone (S18, S19, S21, S22 [F]).
  - glaciologia.cl: abandoned in 2004 for cracks in the runway (S24 [F]).
  - La Tercera and Radio Polar: closed 2014; the 2002 study identified the cracks (S20, S23 [F]).
  - Wikipedia: "Chilean Navy visits almost every summer" [U].
- 2017: INACH built two new labs, a dry lab and a wet lab, named after Eduardo García. Materials were unloaded from the icebreaker Viel in one operation (S29 [F]). So ship access can work.
- Feb 2025: FACh inspection concluded the base could reopen at full capacity. Dec 2025: team to arrive for recovery work. Jan 2026: activation scheduled (S18-S22 [F]).
- **I found no source confirming that the Jan 2026 activation actually occurred.** The latest items I saw (a 24 Sep 2026 Pauta article about Chilean Antarctic airstrips, S28 [F]) say nothing about Carvajal. COMNAP's "Temporarily Closed" reflects Nov 2024, before the reopening announcement.

**Capacity**
- COMNAP 2017: 46 beds, max 46 (12 staff + 34 scientists), 770 m2 under roof, 96 m2 labs, 360 m2 logistics, conference room for 30, power "Fossil fuel, Renewable" (S2 [V]). COMNAP 2024: peak 46 (S1 [V]).
- FACh 2025: 12 people (10 uniformed + 2 civilians) (S18, S19 [F]).
- ASPA 117 plan: "generally accommodated up to 10 personnel" (S6 [V]).
- La Tercera and Radio Polar: about 200 m2 with kitchen, dormitories, storage, common area, shelter and INACH lab (S20, S23 [F]).
- Winter: 0.

**Infrastructure**
- Buildings from the BAS era: Stephenson House, Rymill House, Hampton House, and a plastic accommodation block from 1967 (Wikipedia [U]).
- The 2012 blog describes deteriorated buildings: "doors no longer close properly, the roofs leak", with furnishings left behind (S39 [F], low reliability). Winter visit.
- COMNAP 2017: loader, quad bike, skidoos, zodiacs; helipad yes; HF, VHF, phone and internet per INACH (S30 [F]); 2 ship visits per year (Jan-Mar, Oct-Dec).
- INACH also maintains a sensor-network station here (S25 [V]).

**Access**
- Ship: icebreaker Viel and others. COMNAP lists "Ship landing facilities: None", so cargo comes ashore by boat and landing craft in swell-exposed conditions. 2017 labs were delivered this way (S2 [V], S29 [F]).
- Air: ski-equipped Twin Otters on the glacier runway (now unusable); helipad.
- Distance from Punta Arenas 1,698 km (COMNAP). New Chilean sources cite 2,002 km or 7 days of direct navigation (S26 [F]).
- Nearest others (computed from COMNAP 2024): Rothera 39.6 km, Dirck Gerritsz Laboratory 39.7 km, Turkish camp 70.9 km, San Martín 86.1 km (S1 [V]).
- Winter training trips from Rothera visit it (Wikipedia [U]).

**Other facilities within 25 km**
- Avian Island, ASPA 117, 400 m south of Adelaide Island, 1.2 km from the station (computed 1.6 km). It has two abandoned refuges: Chilean Comodoro Guesalaga (1962/63) and Argentine "Paso de los Andes" (1957) (S6 [V], S3 [V]). Also a Chilean navigation beacon (1998) and the old UK iron-frame beacon.
- No other COMNAP facility within 25 km (S1 [V]).

**Stated purpose** (context only)
- FACh: "hacer soberanía dentro del Círculo Polar Antártico... reactivar todos los servicios" including logistic support, search and rescue, and life support for INACH (S18-S21 [F]).
- INACH: geology, geomorphology, geophysics, glaciology, paleoecology, pollution, atmospheric science, terrestrial and marine biology (S2 [V]).

**Protected areas and visitors**
- ASPA 117 Avian Island (Measure 2 (2018)). Entry by permit only; aircraft "should avoid landing within the Area throughout the year"; helicopter landings only by permit for essential purposes; 100 m marine buffer; about 35,000 pairs of Adelie penguins (S6 [V]).
- A copy of the plan must be kept at Carvajal station. The station itself lies outside the ASPA.
- No HSM found at Carvajal. No tourist traffic found.

**Other notes**
- COMNAP 2017 climate: mean annual temperature -9.8 C, July -17.8 C, February -3 C, precipitation 621 mm, snow-free January-February, sea-ice breakup December (S2 [V]).
- Nearby: the largest Adelie colony on the western Peninsula (Avian Island). Elephant, leopard, Weddell and fur seals; scarce vegetation (lichen and algae).

**Revival realism**
- The reopening is announced as a 12-person FACh operation reconditioning a roughly 200 m2 facility. The announcement gives no plan for restoring the ice runway. I could not confirm that it began.
- A new US$80 million, 3,400 m2 INACH science base (68 summer and 25 winter people, 7 labs) was announced in 2023 with a 2026 target (S26 [F]).
- That has slipped. By June 2025 INACH said Carvajal should become a "Base Científica Conjunta" for all four state operators, with funding still to be sought (S25 [V]). A 13 Sep 2026 article says the Carvajal and Yelcho replacements will follow the Escudero rebuild, for which construction is due between 2028-29 and 2033 (S27 [F]).

## Contradictions between sources

1. **Brown to GGV distance.** The 2012 inspection says "a mile away across the bay" (S5). Spanish Wikipedia says about 10 km. Computed from COMNAP coordinates it is 8.0 km.
2. **Waterboat Point's HSM number.** HSM 56 (ATS list, S4 [V]), not HSM 54 as in the brief. HSM 54 is the Byrd monument at McMurdo.
3. **Melchior HSM 29 location.** ATS says Lambda Island. Some Argentine and Wikipedia text places the base on Gamma Island (Isla Observatorio). Both are right: the station is on Gamma Island and the lighthouse is on Lambda Island.
4. **Melchior coordinates.** COMNAP 2017 and 2024 differ by about 740 m, and 2024 is about 910 m from Gallows Point where SCAR places the station.
5. **Melchior surface.** COMNAP says ice-sheet and moraine; Argentine sources say rock at 8 m; Pierrou-derived text says the island is ice-capped with ice-cliff coasts.
6. **Melchior elevation.** 4 m versus 8 m. **Melchior capacity**: 15 versus "maximum 36".
7. **Carvajal closure date.** 2004 (glaciologia.cl), 2014 (La Tercera, Radio Polar), 2014-15 (FACh). Also "summer-only since 1982" (ASPA plan) versus transfer on 14 Aug 1984 and station established Jan 1985 (SCAR).
8. **Carvajal elevation.** 4 m versus 92 m. **Carvajal size**: 770 m2 / 46 beds versus about 200 m2.
9. **Brown's 1984 evacuation.** To Palmer (Spanish Wikipedia) versus Ushuaia (Minuto Fueguino).
10. **Brown after 1984.** en-Wikipedia and Worldabandoned say "abandoned in 2000, sporadically operated since 2007". Argentine sources say sporadic from 1988-89, with new modules in 1995-96 and 1999-2000. The Argentine sources are more detailed and consistent.
11. **GGV power.** COMNAP 2017 lists renewable energy; the 2012 inspection found none.
12. **Gaps in the brief.**
    - Vernadsky is 75.8-80.7 km (not 30-60 km) from GGV and Brown by COMNAP coordinates; Palmer is 57-72 km and Yelcho 34-68 km.
    - Carvajal is not on Leonie Island.
13. **A search-summary error corrected.** A search result said Brown had a crew of "17 people, 7 scientists and 10 military" in 2024/25; the 17-person crew is for **Primavera**, not Brown. Brown's crews were 11 (S9). Another search result's "97 visits / 35 visits" for Brown and Waterboat Point is from 2006-07, not recent. Another said the US 2006 inspection included Brown; it did not.
