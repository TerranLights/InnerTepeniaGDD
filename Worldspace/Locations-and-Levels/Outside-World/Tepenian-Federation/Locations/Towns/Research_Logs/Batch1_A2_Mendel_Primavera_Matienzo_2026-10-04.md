# Research report: Mendel, Primavera, Matienzo (and the "Belgrano II Skiway" row)

All sources accessed 2026-10-04 unless a different date is shown. Operating nation/program is context only. Source keys [S#] are defined in the table at the end. Items marked "UNVERIFIED" were seen only in a search summary or a secondary page.

## Headline findings

1. **Mendel: rock-qualifies (moderate-high confidence).** The station stands on an ice-free raised marine terrace of sandy sediment over continuous permafrost, 100 m from the shore at 9-10 m. The nearest glacier or ice cap is Davies Dome, about 12 km away, and it is not moving toward the site. Bedrock below the terrace was not verified. The adjacent Cretaceous rock is weakly lithified muddy sandstone ("Whisky Bay Formation").
2. **Primavera: rock-qualifies (high confidence).** It sits on a rocky promontory of a steep granitic massif. Tidewater glaciers lie about 4-5 km away at the head of Cierva Cove. No relocation, damage or burial reports were found.
3. **Matienzo: split verdict.** The base stands on Larsen Nunatak, a volcanic rock outcrop (rock-qualifies for the base footprint, moderate confidence). Everything that moves people and cargo depends on ice: the ski-way is on glacier or ice-shelf-remnant ice, and the nunatak sits in a thinning remnant ice shelf that its own authors expect to disappear. Matienzo also **did not operate in the 2025/26 season**.
4. **"Belgrano II Skiway" row: a coordinate error, not a second airstrip.** Wikipedia's "List of airports in Antarctica" gives Belgrano II Skiway (SAYB) and Matienzo Skiway (SAWZ) identical coordinates, 64.975659 S, 60.071501 W. The Belgrano II row's location text reads "Bertrab Nunatak", which is at about 77.88 S, 34.63 W, over 1,600 km away. The inventory row is a mislocated duplicate. The Matienzo airstrip is SAWZ.

## STATION 1: Johann Gregor Mendel Czech Antarctic Station (Ulu Peninsula, James Ross Island)

### Verified coordinates
- COMNAP Nov 2024 CSV: -63.800625, -57.882592; elevation 10 m MSL. This equals the inventory value. [S1]
- GTN-P borehole record: -63.8012963, -57.88289738; 10 m. This is 0.08 km from the CSV point. [S14]
- COMNAP 2017 catalogue: "63°48'03.8''S 57°52'95.6''W" (printed seconds of 95.6 are impossible, so this is a typo). Altitude 10 m. [S3]
- Wikipedia (lead only): 63°48'02"S 57°52'56"W, 9 m. [S5]
- Argentina-Chile inspection report: 63°48'16.32"S 57°53'8.82"W, which is about 0.4 km from the CSV point. [S8]
- Prošek et al. 2013: "100 m from a sea shore at the altitude of 9 m a.s.l." [S6]
- Verdict: coordinates agree within about 0.5 km. Elevation is 9-10 m.

### Surface and stability (verdict: ROCK-QUALIFIES, confidence moderate-high)
- **Ground at the site.**
  - The building sits on "a marine terrace formed by compacted and fine-grained sandy substrate" ([S6], with the 2004 draft CEE cited as source).
  - The 2005 inspectors called it "a raised beach terrace, firm, well drained and level." [S9]
  - Hrbáček et al. describe a "sandy Holocene marine terrace" on the Mendel permafrost grid, abutting muddy sand of the Cretaceous Whisky Bay Formation. The lithological boundary lies within a 23 m ERT transect near the station. [S7; A-ERT abstract in search summary, UNVERIFIED]
  - Foundations are a grate of oak railway sleepers (minimum 40 cm high) laid in shallow grooves, not piles into bedrock. [S6]
  - Depth to bedrock under the terrace: NOT FOUND.
- **Permafrost.**
  - COMNAP lists it as "Continuous." [S3]
  - Active layer is 0.5-1.0 m [S3], about 55-60 cm at the station terrace in late January [search summary, UNVERIFIED]. Hrbáček et al. report the thickest active layer in the study, 89 cm, on the Berry Hill slopes, not at the station. [S7]
  - Mean annual air temperature is -6.9 °C (2011-2017). Mean annual ground temperature is -5.5 °C at 5 cm and -5.6 °C at 50 cm. [S7]
  - Permafrost thickness estimates for James Ross Island lowlands of about 6 to 67 m come from a search summary only. UNVERIFIED. The primary Fukuda 1992 and Borzotta and Trombotto 2004 papers were not retrieved.
  - Thaw water is judged not to endanger foundations because the sand is well sorted and absorbs it, and the area is in a precipitation shadow. [S6]
- **Ice nearby.**
  - Ulu Peninsula is more than 300 km² of deglaciated ground. Deglaciation on the north coast began 12.9 ± 1.2 ka ago. Only small glaciers remain on plateaus and valley heads. [S7]
  - Davies Dome is at -63.8833, -58.05 [S11], 12.3 km from the station (my calculation). It is about 6.5 km² with a 700 m wide outlet into Whisky Bay (search summary, UNVERIFIED). It is on a different coast and is retreating.
  - Four monitored glaciers within 15 km move on the order of 2-3 m/yr. [S15, from an article by a surveying author, treated as secondary]
- **Prince Gustav Channel.**
  - The Prince Gustav Ice Shelf had "disappeared by December 2002." [S11, SCAR gazetteer]
  - The channel is still ice-choked in some years. In January 2026 the team could not reach the island because "everything is still frozen," and expected a wait of about two weeks. [S17]
  - The site is therefore dependent on sea-ice conditions for ship access, not for ground stability.
- **Relocation, damage, burial, flooding, erosion, landslide.** None found in any source (see Search log). The 2017 inspection found the buildings "in very good condition" after 10 years, with the station "modern, reduced in size and in very good general maintenance." [S8]
- **Periglacial caution.** Stachoň et al. 2014 note that "variable intensity of periglacial processes" (freeze-thaw, mass movement) limits conventional geodetic point stabilization on Ulu Peninsula. [search summary of the CPR article, UNVERIFIED] A local reference point 4003 "was indeed stable." [S15]
- **Reasoning.** The site is ice-free and not on or near moving ice. The fail test is "ice moving fast enough to force relocation," and nothing in the record suggests that. The weak point against "ideally bedrock below" is the sandy terrace over permafrost.

### Status and years
- COMNAP: Seasonal, Open, established 2006 [S1]. Operational period December-March [S3]. Program page says January to mid-March [S4].
- Built in two seasons, 2004-05 and 2005-06. [S3] Construction ended 4 March 2006. [S10]
- Inaugurated 27 February 2007 [S8]. Wikipedia says 22 February 2007 [S5]. A search summary of radio.cz also gives 27 February 2007 (UNVERIFIED).
- Summer expeditions have run every year since 2006. [S3, S8] The 20th expedition ran January-March 2026. [S18]
- No wintering. COMNAP lists winter staff and scientists as blank. [S3]
- No rebuild, move or closure found.
- 2026 season: 26 researchers and technical staff in total. Five weeks were spent at Mendel and two weeks at the Nelson Island camp. [S18]

### Capacity
- COMNAP: 20 beds; peak 20 (4 staff + 16 scientists); conference room 40. [S1, S3]
- Inspection 2017: 17 present, "maximum capacity is twenty-nine." [S8]
- Main building: 12 bedrooms of 1 or 2 beds. [S6]
- The 2005 design target was 15 people. [S9]
- Contradiction on maximum capacity: 20 vs 29 (see below).

### Infrastructure
- **Buildings.** Main building 26.5 × 11.5 m, one story, K-Kontrol OSB/polystyrene sandwich panels. Two laboratories (33 m²), radio room, kitchen, cooled food store, 9 m² medical room. [S6, S3] Plus nine 20-foot containers [S6]. The 2017 inspection counted ten converted containers on a 400 m² footprint. [S8]
- **Power.**
  - Eight 1.5 kW wind turbines, solar thermal panels, and diesel generators (two Gesan R12 at 12 kW and one R30 at 30 kW, per [S6]). The inspection found two diesel sets of 22 and 25 kVA. [S8]
  - Renewables supply about 75% of consumption per [S8]; Wikipedia says 60-90% (secondary).
  - Ni-Cd battery bank at 48 V; about 10 kWh for 24 h if all else fails. [S6]
  - The 2013 paper describes photovoltaic additions; the inspection saw photovoltaic panels. [S6, S8]
- **Water.**
  - Source is a neighboring stream fed by snow patches and thawing ground. It runs most of the summer but may freeze in late February to early March. [S6]
  - Sedimentation tanks, then two 300 L tanks indoors. [S6]
  - The 2017 inspection saw a 9 m³ container of 10 × 250 L tanks and a water treatment plant, with consumption of about 30 L per person per day. [S8]
- **Fuel.** One fuel container. At the 2017 inspection it held 25 drums of diesel and 5 of gasoline (200 L drums). The station uses 6-12 drums per season. The unloading slope is 15-20° over about 150 m with no spill-containment gear for the transfer. [S8]
- **Waste.** Marine incinerator OG120SW (sludge oil 29 L/h). Effluent is pumped to the sea untreated (under 30 people). [S6, S8]
- **Communications.** Iridium, HF/VHF, Inmarsat internet. [S8] COMNAP also lists e-mail and internet. [S3]
- **Vehicles.**
  - 2017: two ATVs, one ScotTrac2000 light truck, three Zodiacs. [S8]
  - COMNAP: four Zodiacs, two 4WD ATVs, one 6WD ATV. [S3]
- **Runway and helipad.** No airstrip. COMNAP says "Helipad: Yes" [S3]. The inspection says no designated helipad, with a natural gravel area used as a temporary helicopter platform. [S8]
- **Landing and pier.** No pier. Cargo comes in by landing craft and Zodiac over the beach. [S6, S9]
- **Medical.** Basic, 9 m², 3 trained staff. Closest emergency facility in Antarctica is 80 km. [S3]

### Access
- **Ship, cargo and fuel.**
  - Once a year, early in the season. In 2017 the Chilean Navy ship AP-46 Almirante Viel resupplied on 9 January from Punta Arenas. [S8]
  - Construction cargo in 2005-06 also came by Chilean icebreaker Almirante Viel via Hamburg and Punta Arenas. [S6]
  - In 2026 the team flew by Hercules from Punta Arenas to King George Island, then took the Chilean OPV "Marinero Fuentealba." [S17]
  - Czech program: "Without our own icebreaker or helicopter, we must rely on transport provided by national programmes of other countries." [S18]
- **Air.**
  - Two scheduled helicopter movements per season by the Argentine Air Force from Marambio. [S8] COMNAP lists 2 flight visits per year, January to March, and 1 ship visit. [S3]
  - Older route: Hercules from Río Gallegos to Marambio (about 4 h), then helicopter about 80 km. The Marambio airstrip is leveled mud that must be frozen. Waits of a week or ten days occur. [S16, 2010]
- Marambio is the evacuation base, with Frei (Chile) as the other. [S8]
- Distances (great-circle, from COMNAP coordinates):

| To | Distance |
|---|---|

### Other facilities within about 25 km
- **Refugio San Carlos** (Argentine Army refuge hut, established 4 October 1959 on San Carlos Point): about 7.6 km. Current status not found. [S11] Wikipedia calls it "Argentine refuge established at San Carlos Point in 1959." [S39]
- **Giant Garden** (named moss area near the station): 2.4 km. [S11]
- **Johnson Mesa / Berry Hill automatic weather and ground-temperature sites** (Czech program): within a few kilometers (not stations). [S13, S7]
- **Argentine scientific refuges** on "Vega Island and Cerro Nevado island" were announced in 2023 as planned, with no timeline. [S38] Vega Island's center is about 20-25 km away. I could not verify that they were built or where they are. UNVERIFIED.
- No other station within 25 km. The nearest are O'Higgins/GARS (53 km), Esperanza (62.7 km), Ruperto Elichiribehety (62.5 km), and Marambio (78.5 km). [S1]

### Stated purpose
- Multidisciplinary research on "ice-free terrestrial ecosystems and geosystems"; long-term climatology, glaciology, permafrost, geology, biology. [S4, S3]
- Operated by Masaryk University (Czech Antarctic Research Programme), which is the only Czech government base in Antarctica. Operator is context only.

### Protected-area status
- **No ASPA, ASMA or HSM near the site.**
  - The ATS Protected Areas Database (182 records) lists no ASPA, ASMA or HSM within about 50 km. The nearest is O'Higgins Historic Site (HSM 37) at 53.8 km, then ASPA 148 Mount Flora at 60.5 km. [S12]
  - The 2017 inspection states "there are no such zones near Ross Island." [S8]
- Station EIA: a CEE was reviewed by the CEP at its seventh meeting in Cape Town in 2004 (ATCM XXVII IP 3) [S9]. The 2017 inspection recommended that the final EIA be added to the exchange system. [S8] The full CEE text was not retrieved.

### Other notes
- **Climate, station vicinity.**
  - Mean annual air temperature -6.8 °C [S3] to -6.9 °C [S7]; February mean -0.1 °C, July mean -14.1 °C [S3].
  - 2008-09 record: mean -4.6 °C, minimum -35.5 °C (9 July 2008), maximum 12.1 °C (25 January 2008). [S13]
  - Precipitation 200-500 mm/yr as snow; flat-ground snow depth 30-50 cm. [S7]
  - COMNAP gives dominant wind W, maximum 126 km/h, and a mean annual of "6 km/h". That mean looks implausibly low for this exposure; treat it as doubtful.
- **Hazards.** A disembarkation slope of 15-20°. Reliance on Marambio for medical evacuation; the inspectors called this "delicate" because of response times. [S8]
- **Distance to bedrock.** Not found for the station terrace.

## STATION 2: Primavera Base (Cape Primavera / Cierva Point, Danco Coast)

### Verified coordinates
- COMNAP CSV: -64.155854, -60.954256; 50 m. This equals the inventory. [S1]
- COMNAP 2017 catalogue: 64°9'35.1"S 60°57'25.5"W (-64.1597, -60.9571; 0.45 km from the CSV point). Altitude 50 m. [S2]
- Wikipedia (lead only): -64.155766, -60.955183; 50 m. [S33]
- IAATO manual, jetty: 64°9'19.53"S 60°57'11.69"W. [S30]
- ASPA 134 plan: "64°09'S; 60°58'W." [S29]
- Fundación Marambio: 64º09'S 60º58'W, 50 m. [S20]
- Verdict: all within about 0.5 km. Elevation is 50 m in every source.

### Surface and stability (verdict: ROCK-QUALIFIES, confidence high)
- **Rock.**
  - Fundación Marambio: the base was installed "en un promontorio rocoso" (on a rocky promontory); "La zona es abrupta, formada por un gran macizo granítico." [S20]
  - COMNAP: "steep, formed by a large granite massif"; "Type of surface facility built on: Ice-free ground." [S2]
  - IAATO: the south side of Cierva Cove is "steep granitic ice free hills." [S30]
  - The ASPA plan cites "different lithologies and a pronounced system of fractures." [S29]
  - The Argentine "granite" label comes from program descriptions, not a geological survey. Specific lithology (granodiorite/tonalite) was not found in any geological source.
- **Ice nearby.**
  - Cierva Point's south-facing slopes are glaciated; north- and west-facing slopes are ice-free. [S39, Wikipedia IBA, secondary]
  - Cierva Cove is about 2.5 nautical miles deep. It ends in two large tidewater glaciers (Breguet and Gregory) at its eastern end, about 4-5 km from the station. [S30; Breguet/Sikorsky glacier names: S39]
  - Primavera sits on the western promontory, away from the calving fronts. The entrance is exposed to large swell from the west. [S30]
- **Permafrost.**
  - A 2018 master's dissertation (TTOP model of Cierva Point, 0.65 km², nine monitoring sites 2012-2018) reports permafrost tables at 0.4, 1 and 5 m depth. [search summary only, UNVERIFIED; I could not open the document]
  - COMNAP's permafrost field for Primavera was left blank. [S2]
  - Terrain and permafrost at the station itself were not separately reported.
- **Slope hazard.** The ASPA plan describes the coastal rocky wall as having, in places, "unstable" scree substrate crossed by canyons. [S29] This refers to the protected area, not the station footprint.
- **Reports of relocation, damage, burial or threat.** None found. Primavera has stayed on the same spot since the Capitán Cobbett naval refuge (23 January 1954). [S2, S20]

### Status and years
- Capitán Cobbett naval refuge founded 23 January 1954 at the same location. [S20, S2]
- Base inaugurated 3 March 1977 (COMNAP, Fundación Marambio) [S2, S20]. Other Argentine government pages say 8 March 1977. [search summaries of argentina.gob.ar; Wikipedia]
- Permanent 1977-1982. Fundación Marambio says it was deactivated "by a resolution of the SCAR" in 1982 (claim not verified elsewhere); since then summer-only. [S20, S33]
- COMNAP: Seasonal, Open. Operational period November-March. [S1, S2] Argentine government pages say the temporary bases run December-March. [defonline summary, UNVERIFIED]
- **2025/26: reactivated.**
  - Listed among the temporary bases opened by the Joint Antarctic Command with the icebreaker ARA "Almirante Irízar" (campaign began 28 November 2025). [S27]
  - Operational during the 2025-26 summer campaign, with personnel carried by the Irízar. [S26]
  - Also reactivated in 2024/25, staffed through March. [S28]
- No wintering since 1982.

### Capacity
- COMNAP: 18 beds; peak 18 (12 staff + 6 scientists); no winter staff. [S1, S2]
- Fundación Marambio "usual complement": 6 scientists, 4 foreign scientists, 8 Army personnel. [S20]
- Wikipedia: 18 summer, 0 winter. [S33]

### Infrastructure
- **Buildings.** The ASPA plan says eight buildings interconnected by walkways to protect vegetation, plus a designated helicopter landing area. [S29] Wikipedia counts 11 structures (secondary).
- **Layout.**
  - Fundación Marambio lists: main house, dining room, power plant ("usina"), laboratory, carpentry shop, infirmary, vehicle park, miscellaneous stores, a cold-storage chamber, radio. [S20]
  - Original 1977 build: two small houses, a store and a basic sanitary block; later a cold chamber, two refuges, power plant and radio. [S20]
- **Power.** Fossil fuel, 220 V, 24 h/day. [S2]
- **Water and fuel storage.** NOT FOUND.
- **Communications.** Satellite phone and VHF. [S2]
- **Workshops.** General repairs and wood workshop. [S2]
- **Vehicles.** Two Zodiac boats with outboards. [S2]
- **Helipad.** COMNAP "Helipad: Yes" [S2]; Wikipedia's airports list gives "Primavera Heliport," concrete surface, at -64.156597, -60.953444. [S31]
- **Pier.** A jetty at 64°9'19.53"S 60°57'11.69"W (IAATO). [S30] The ASPA plan excludes "the beach area utilized for access to the base." [S29]
- **Medical.** Basic; 2 trained staff in summer. [S2]

### Access
- COMNAP: access by sea only, five ship visits per year in January, February, March and December. [S2] Ship cargo and crew.
- 2024/25 and 2025/26: by the icebreaker ARA Almirante Irízar and landing craft (e.g., the "General San Martín"). [S27, S28]
- Landing at the station without DNA permission is not allowed (IAATO "marine only" site). [S30]
- Distances: Matienzo 100.5 km; Melchior 99.5 km; Almirante Brown 123 km; Gabriel González Videla (Chile) 117.6 km (all great-circle from COMNAP coordinates). [S1]

### Other facilities within about 25 km
- **ASPA 134 (Cierva Point and offshore islands).** The ATS database lists it at -64.1667, -61.0167, 3.3 km from the station point. The station and its beach access are excluded from the ASPA. [S12, S29]
- **Extreme Ice Survey camera** photographing Breguet Glacier, at 64°9'49.93"S 60°53'50.38"W (inside ASPA 134). [S30]
- **Bombay Island, Mikkelsen Harbour:** an Argentine refuge hut and beacon, 29.6 km (outside 25 km). [S11]
- No other station within 25 km.

### Stated purpose
- Logistics and maintenance of the base and refuges; support for science; search and rescue; health, communications and weather support to expeditions. [S20]
- Programs: ecological impact, limnology, bird biology, mosses and lichens. [S20] Climate-change effects on pinnipeds, bird monitoring, Deschampsia phytogeography, Cierva Point wetlands. [S2]
- Operated by the Instituto Antártico Argentino under the DNA/Army (context only).

### Protected-area status
- **ASPA 134, Cierva Point and offshore islands.**
  - Designated as SSSI 15 in 1985 (Rec XIII-8), became ASPA 134 in 2002, current plan Measure 5 (2013). Proponent: Argentina. [S29, S12]
  - It covers the ice-free area between Cierva Cove and Santucci Cove plus Apéndice, José Hernández, Moss and Penguin islands. [S29]
  - Entry is by permit only, for science that cannot be done elsewhere, on foot from the base. Movement is limited to unvegetated areas, and pedestrian paths are marked. [S29]
  - Station staff must be instructed in the plan. The plan also asks for studies of the effects of "the neighboring station." [S29]
- The base also described itself as "within the SEIC (SSSI)." [S20]
- No ASMA nearby. [S12]

### Other notes
- **Climate.**
  - Fundación Marambio: maximum 13 °C and minimum -20 °C; NW winds averaging 45 km/h. [S20]
  - Quintana 1992/93 summer record at Cierva Point: mean monthly 1.8 to 2.2 °C, mean wind 7.9 km/h. [search summary, UNVERIFIED]
  - No long-term mean annual temperature found.
- **Biodiversity.** Ten breeding bird species; extensive moss turf (about 80 cm peat); invasive Poa pratensis. [S29]
- **Hazards.** Calving ice and large swell in the bay. [S30]

## STATION 3: Matienzo Base (Larsen Nunatak, Seal Nunataks) and the "Belgrano II Skiway" question

### Verified coordinates
- COMNAP CSV: -64.975865, -60.070948; 32 m. This equals the inventory. [S1]
- COMNAP 2017 catalogue: 64°58'55.2"S 60°04'25.7"W (-64.9820, -60.0738; 0.70 km from the CSV point; note the CSV's minutes value 58.5519 suggests the printed seconds are off). Altitude 32 m. [S2]
- Wikipedia: 64°58'32"S 60°04'17"W = -64.975659, -60.071501; 32 m. [S33]
- SCAR gazetteer: Larsen Nunatak -64.9667, -60.0667 (USA) and -64.9667, -60.0833 (GBR); "Teniente Matienzo" (Russian) -64.9667, -60.05. [S11]
- Fundación Marambio and Argentine government pages: 64º59'S 60º07'W or 64°58'S 60°08'W, and 64°58'S 60°04'W (another page). One DNA page gives "62°59'S 60°43'W," which is clearly wrong. [S21, S24, cancilleria page seen in the log]
- Verdict: the base is at about -64.976, -60.071 within about 1 km across sources.

### Belgrano II Skiway: what the inventory row is
- The inventory row is Wikipedia's "Belgrano II Skiway," ICAO SAYB, with coordinate 64.975659 S, 60.071501 W (wikitext, "Bertrab Nunatak"). [S31]
- Wikipedia's Matienzo Skiway row (ICAO SAWZ, local code BTM) carries the same coordinates and a 4,920 ft (1,500 m) "Sea Ice" runway. [S31]
- Bertrab Nunatak is at 77.883 S, 34.633 W near Vahsel Bay. [S11] Belgrano II station is at -77.873889, -34.627778 in the COMNAP CSV. [S1]
- The airport databases put SAYB at 77°52'27"S 34°37'34"W, 250 m. [S32, airportguide] So the 64.9757 S row is a copy-paste coordinate error. It is 1,662 km from Belgrano II.
- **Conclusion:** The row at Matienzo's coordinates is not a second airstrip. The real Belgrano II skiway is near 77.87 S, 34.63 W. A real, separate Matienzo airstrip (SAWZ) exists.
- **Is the "skiway" the Matienzo airstrip, and what is its status?**
  - COMNAP: Number of airstrips 1, longest runway 1,500 m, width 40 m, 4 flight visits/year (January-March, October-December). Access Air, Sea; ship landing facilities: None. [S2] (The 1,500 m figure may be ski-landing area length; the catalogue wording is ambiguous.)
  - Fundación Marambio: "un sector para anevizaje 1.500 metros al S de la base" (a snow-landing sector 1,500 m south of the base). A DHC-6 Twin Otter periodically moves people and cargo. [S21]
  - Wikipedia's Matienzo page: 1,500 m airstrip, glacier-based, snow-covered, 2 km from the main base, usable by all ski-mounting aircraft. [S33]
  - Other coordinate sources: OurAirports and LFS put SAWZ at -64.9770, -60.1090, about 1.8 km WSW of the base. [S32]
  - 2018 reactivation: the Twin Otter landed on the adjacent glacier "requiring a 1200 m traverse" to the installations, with cargo carried by hand. [S22]
  - So it is a ski-way on glacier or ice-shelf-remnant ice. The "sea ice" surface in Wikipedia's list conflicts with the primary descriptions (glacier). Status: seasonal, ski-equipped fixed-wing only, no developed runway; the base did not operate in 2025/26 (below).

### Surface and stability (verdict: base ROCK-QUALIFIES (moderate confidence); skiway and approach ICE-FAIL; overall UNCLEAR for settlement access)
- **Nunatak.**
  - Larsen Nunatak is one of the "Foca" (Seal) Nunataks, volcanic, on the Larsen ice barrier. Dimensions are about 1,800 m long and "about 300 m high" per COMNAP and Fundación Marambio [S2, S21]; "exceeding 200 m" per the Air Force history [S25]; "rising to 140 m above Larsen Ice Shelf" per the UK gazetteer [S11]. See contradictions.
  - COMNAP: "Type of surface facility built on: Ice-free ground." The nunatak features include bluff, crevasse, ice cap or glacier, ice shelf, nunatak. "The station area is devoid of vegetation." "Glaciers around the nunatak and snowdrifts." [S2]
  - The base occupies a discontinuous 300 m strip at the nunatak's eastern end. [argentina.gob.ar, S24 summary]
  - The group is volcanic: basalt with olivine, clinopyroxene and plagioclase (with andesite and basanite), K-Ar ages from 4 Ma to recent, with "pillow lavas about 1.5 Ma," and the youngest dated nunataks (Donald and Gray) under 200 ka. [S35, Wikipedia, secondary] Pliocene-Recent outcrops are "remnants of several monogenetic volcanoes formed by eruption of vesiculating basaltic magma into shallow water, in an englacial environment" (search summary, UNVERIFIED), so the rock may be volcaniclastic as well as lava.
- **Ice around it.**
  - The nunatak sits in the "Seal Nunataks ice shelf" (SNIS), a remnant of about 743 km² between the former Larsen A (collapsed January 1995) and Larsen B (collapsed 2002), floating with grounded parts. [S34]
  - Flow is about 25 m/yr or less because it is pinned by nunataks. Thinning is 1.9-2.7 m/yr (mean 2.32 m/yr). The authors conclude the SNIS "will not persist for many more years" (2016; data 2001-13). [S34]
  - In 2013 the remnant was losing margin ice and "may be losing contact with some nunataks." [search summary of S34 sources, UNVERIFIED]
  - The 2002 Larsen B collapse took 35 days (31 January-7 March 2002). [search summary, UNVERIFIED]
  - The base was "originally embedded in Larsen A Ice Shelf, which completely disintegrated in 1995," per an Argentine government page. [S24] This conflicts with the SNIS description (see contradictions).
  - Post-2016 status of the SNIS: NOT FOUND.
- **Permafrost.** COMNAP: "Discontinuous." [S2]
- **Sea ice.** Sea ice break-up December; snow-free period January-February. [S2]
- **Relocation, damage, burial reports.** None for the nunatak itself. Operational degradation is a separate matter: the 2026 Argentine draft resolution describes Matienzo and Melchior as not operated "for years" (an opposition legislator's text citing media and military sources, so treat as political and unverified). [S36]
- **Reasoning.** The base footprint is on volcanic rock that does not flow, so the "ice not moving at a speed that would force a move" test is met for the buildings. Access (skiway on glacier or remnant shelf, helicopters from Marambio 183 km away, no ship landing) and the surrounding ice do not meet it, and the ice is in documented decline.

### Status and years
- Inaugurated 15 March 1961 as the joint Army-Air Force "Base Teniente Matienzo" on the former San Antonio refuge. Led by Capt. Ignacio Carro; 240 tonnes hauled by tracked vehicles from Esperanza in mid-winter. [S21, S24]
- Air Force-only from 1964 (renamed in 1965 or 1966; sources differ). [S21, S25, S33]
- Deactivated as a permanent base in the 1971/72 or 1972/73 summer. [S21, S24, S25] Wikipedia gives an extended sequence (closed 1972-73, reopened 8 September 1974, closed again 1984-85, seasonal since 1985; secondary). [S33]
- Reopened seasonally: since 2016 "during all summers" per an argentina.gob.ar summary (UNVERIFIED); 2017/18 reactivation ran 15 January-23 February 2018 with 10 people. [S22, S23]
- **Last confirmed operation: summer 2018. Not operated in 2025/26.**
  - The 2025/26 campaign reactivated Brown, Cámara, Decepción, Primavera and Melchior; Matienzo is absent from the opening lists. [S27]
  - The Navy's Gaceta Marinera (27 March 2026) says Matienzo "did not operate this summer." [S26]
  - Operations in 2019-2024: NOT FOUND (dead end). COMNAP's CSV still lists "Seasonal, Open." [S1]
- No wintering since the 1970s. Summer-only now.

### Capacity
- COMNAP: 12 beds; peak 12 (10 staff + 2 scientists in summer). [S1, S2]
- 10-15 temporary residents; the 2018 crew was 10. [S22, S24]

### Infrastructure
- Accommodation, storage, power-plant building, fuel platform and heliport. [S2]
- Power: fossil fuel, 380 V, 24 h. [S2] Two Antarctic gas-oil generators (Wikipedia, secondary). 2018 work included generator maintenance, 12 V lighting expansion, first successful photovoltaic panel tests, and reactivation of radio station LU1ZAB. [S22]
- Area under roof 1,000 m²; laboratory 700 m² (glaciology). Medical 6 m². [S2]
- Communications: satellite phone, VHF. Vehicles: two snow-cats; no boats. [S2]
- Water source and water system: NOT FOUND.
- Pier: none ("Ship landing facilities: None"). [S2]
- Helipad: yes. Runway: see above.
- Museum: Antarctic Museum restored 2018. [S24]

### Access
- By air only in practice. DHC-6 Twin Otter from Marambio (Air Force Antarctic Flight Squadron "Águila") lands on adjacent glacier, plus Bell 212 and Mil Mi-171E helicopters. [S2, S22, S23]
- Distances: Marambio 183 km (COMNAP; my calculation 183.5 km; Argentine pages say 186 km); Primavera 100.5 km; Esperanza 230 km. [S1, S2, S24]
- 2018 team traversed 1,200 m from the landing glacier to the base by hand. [S22]

### Other facilities within about 25 km
- **None.** The nearest COMNAP facility is Primavera at 100.5 km. [S1] The nearest ATS protected area is ASPA 134 at 100.7 km. [S12]

### Stated purpose
- Meteorological and aurora observations, aerial photographic surveys, geology and geophysics, satellite geodesy, oceanography; the "Larsen project" on retreat and disintegration of the Larsen barrier; starting point for the Larsen/Jason Peninsula. [S2, S21]
- Operated by the Argentine Air Force (under the Joint Antarctic Command since the DNA-to-COCOANTAR transfer; context only). [S26, S36]

### Protected-area status
- None. No ASPA, ASMA or HSM within 100 km. [S12]

### Other notes
- **Climate.**
  - COMNAP: mean annual temperature -5 °C, mean annual wind 18 km/h, dominant wind NW, precipitation snow and rain. [S2]
  - Fundación Marambio (1960s records): mean annual temperature -11.6 °C; extremes 13.1 °C (16 March 1965) and -44.4 °C (5 August 1964). [S21]
  - The two figures conflict (see contradictions).
- **Hazards.** Reaching the base is possible only in good flying weather; the 2008-09 and 2018 seasons were run with small crews. The ice-shelf remnant is thinning and may lose contact with nunataks (see above). [S34]
- **Elevation of the airstrip.** OurAirports gives the SAWZ field at 23 m. [S32]

## Contradictions between sources

**Mendel**
- Season: December-March (COMNAP 2017 catalogue) [S3] vs January to mid-March (program page) [S4].
- Maximum capacity: 20 (COMNAP, CARP) [S3, S4] vs 29 (2017 inspection, from the station chief) [S8].
- Opening date: 22 February 2007 (Wikipedia) [S5] vs 27 February 2007 (inspection report) [S8]; COMNAP uses 2006 as the establishment year (construction finished 4 March 2006) [S1, S10].
- Building count: nine containers (COMNAP, 2013 paper) [S3, S6] vs ten (2017 inspection) [S8]; the 2005 plan was "twelve small single-storey buildings." [S9]
- Renewable share of energy: 75% (inspection) [S8] vs 60-90% (Wikipedia).
- Diesel sets: 12 and 30 kW (2013 paper) [S6] vs 22 and 25 kVA (2017 inspection) [S8].
- Vehicles: COMNAP lists four Zodiacs and three ATVs [S3]; the inspection lists three Zodiacs, two ATVs and one ScotTrac [S8].
- Helipad: COMNAP "Yes" [S3] vs inspection "no designated helipad," natural gravel area [S8].
- 2005 inspection report coordinates for the site read "62°22'S 58°31'W," which is wrong by about 1.4° of latitude; the site is at 63.80 S. [S9]
- Station height above sea: 8 m, 9 m, 10 m across sources. [S3, S6, S14]

**Primavera**
- Opening: 3 March 1977 (COMNAP, Fundación Marambio) vs 8 March 1977 (other Argentine pages) vs "8 March 1977 officially opened."
- Building count: eight buildings (ASPA plan, 2013) [S29] vs 11 structures (Wikipedia).
- Gazetteer lists "Primavera, cabo" at -64.3, -61.07 (ARG) [S11], about 15 km from the station; the Russian gazetteer entry (-64.15, -60.95) matches the station.
- COMNAP says "Dundee Coast" for the location; the real coast is Danco Coast (an error in the catalogue).

**Matienzo**
- Dimensions of Larsen Nunatak: 300 m high (COMNAP, Fundación Marambio) vs "exceeding 200 m" (Air Force) vs 140 m (UK gazetteer) vs highest point of the Seal Nunataks being Murdoch Nunatak at 368 m (Wikipedia) [S2, S21, S25, S11, S35]. A search summary also claimed Larsen Nunatak at 368 m; the fetched Wikipedia text says Murdoch.
- Airstrip surface: "Sea Ice" (Wikipedia airports list) vs glacier or snow on a glacier (Fundación Marambio, 2018 report, Wikipedia Matienzo page) [S31, S21, S22, S33].
- Airstrip distance from base: 1,500 m south [S21] vs 2 km [S33] vs 1.8 km WSW by OurAirports and LFS coordinates [S32].
- Which Larsen ice shelf the base sat in: "Larsen A, which disintegrated in 1995" (argentina.gob.ar) [S24] vs located in a remnant between Larsen A and B in the Seal Nunataks (Shuman et al.) [S34]. The latter is the more specific scientific source.
- Mean annual temperature: -5 °C (COMNAP) [S2] vs -11.6 °C (1960s record) [S21]. The 6-degree gap probably reflects either different periods or a catalogue error; unresolved.
- Reopening: "from 2016 during all summers" (argentina.gob.ar summary) vs a 2026 legislator's text saying Matienzo has not been operated for years [S36] vs Gaceta Marinera saying it did not operate in 2025/26 [S26]. The three are compatible only if operations lapsed between 2018 and 2025; no operations record for 2019-2024 was found.
- Closure as permanent base: 1971/72, 1972/73 or October 1972 (various pages); Wikipedia adds a reopening (1974) and a second closure (1985) not found in the primary pages.
- Marambio distance: 183 km [S2] vs 186 km [S24]; my calculation is 183.5 km.
- Belgrano II Skiway coordinates: see above (copy error).
