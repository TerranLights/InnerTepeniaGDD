## Verdict: QinLing qualifies (high confidence)
Rock/very shallow bedrock under a thin moraine veneer on an island that pins the neighboring Nansen Ice Shelf. Real hazards are katabatic wind and frost heave, not ice motion.

## WAIS Divide Camp elevation (inventory's 5,895 m is wrong)
- **The inventory's 5,895 is the FOOT figure entered as meters** (5,895 ft = 1,796.8 m, the camp/airfield figure on Wikipedia's infobox, uncited). 5,895 m is physically impossible (Vinson Massif is 4,892 m).
- Verified: deep-drill site (79°28.058'S 112°05.189'W) **1,766 m** (J. Glaciol. WAIS Divide core paper; The Cryosphere 2021). WAIS Divide Media Guide 2010/11: surface 1,759 m, **ice thickness 3,465 m**. UW AWS list: 1,833 m (79.468°S 112.086°W). Camp/skiway about 1,797 m (Wikipedia only; no primary source found). COMNAP: seasonal, est. 2005, peak 40, coordinates -79.4833, -112.0833 (rounded), elevation blank. The spread 1,759 to 1,833 m is probably different benchmarks/datums (unverified).
- **Core reached 3,405 m, about 50 m above the bed.** Mean annual surface temperature -31 ± 1 °C; accumulation about 22 cm ice-equivalent/yr.
- **Ice-flow velocity at the core site: 3 m/yr** (The Cryosphere 2021 citing Conway and Rasmussen 2009; not found in the extracted text of the 2009 paper, so unverified at the primary). The drill site is deliberately about 24 km downslope of the divide; the divide migrates toward the Ross Sea at about 10 m/yr and thins about 0.08 m/yr; position has stayed within about 5 km through the Holocene.
- **Not a candidate:** an ice-sheet camp, listed here only to correct the inventory.

## Contradictions (unresolved)
1. CEE nominal site 74°55'S 163°42'E is about 2 km north of the real site (moved in the 2018 draft). Wikipedia 70 m off COMNAP.
2. Neighbor distances: 2014 draft 27/35/36 km (Mario Zucchelli/Gondwana/Jang Bogo); Australian inspection 29 and 38; Final CEE 29/37/38; computed from COMNAP 29.0/36.4/37.4. The ASPA plan's 27/35/36 are from the ASPA.
3. Boulder Clay runway: Italian CEE says 16 km from the Chinese site; computed about 22.8 km; the Final CEE's own overland route survey is 38.6 km.
4. ASPA 161: about 23 to 24 km (Chinese CEEs) vs 16 km (ASPA 178 plan); coordinates differ (164°10'E vs 164°01'E).
5. Building area: 5,528 m2 (2014) / about 5,500 (2018) / 5,250 (Final CEE) / 5,244 (as built).
6. Capacity: design 80/30; COMNAP peak 120; first winter 43 (or 32 construction staff plus others); 2026 winter 18. Not reconciled.
7. Power: Final CEE design (100 kW wind + 100 kW solar + 65 kW micro-gas turbine + 30 kW hydrogen + 3 x 300 kW diesel); 2014 draft (50 kW solar + 300 kW wind); news 230 kW wind + solar (60% renewable); Wikipedia 100 + 130 kW; one think-tank "10 turbines, 26 panels".
8. Winds: 12.0 m/s mean, 43.5 m/s max (July 2003) in the CEEs; 14.2 / 45 m/s (Feb 1985) in the ASPA plan.
9. Which glacier is west: Nansen Ice Sheet (BirdLife, Wikipedia, Australian inspection, Final CEE) vs "two glaciers divided from the Priestley Glacier" (2014 draft).
10. HSM 14: ATS says Rec. VII-9 with Measure 5 (1995), 74°54'S 163°43'E; Wikipedia 1972 and a wrong 77°31'S 169°22'E; the cave itself is destroyed by ablation (ATCM XLIII).
11. Runway: an Indian think-tank says a fixed-wing runway; the Final CEE has none; ASOC 2018 lists "the ice runway" in the CEE contents. Unresolved because the 2018 draft was not retrieved.
12. Timeline: foundation ceremony 7 Feb 2018 but the Jan 2020 inspection found construction "yet to begin"; main construction 16 Dec 2023; planned 4-summer build (to 2024/25) vs actual about 52 to 60 days; early-2017 planned opening not met. **2024 winter: unresolved (2025 is called the first).**
13. WAIS Divide: elevation 1,759 / 1,766 / 1,797 / 1,833 m; coordinates 79.46°S 112.08°W (USAP 2008/09), 79.468 / 112.086 (project), 79°29.0'S 112°5.0'W (USAP 2024/25), -79.4833 / -112.0833 (COMNAP).
