# Research report: Leningradskaya, Russkaya, Parodi / Union Glacier

All sources accessed 2026-10-04 unless noted. Source keys [S#] are in the table at the end. "OP" means the operating nation or program, which is context only. "Unverified" means I saw the fact only in a search-engine summary and could not open the page.

## Headline findings

1. **Leningradskaya is rock-qualifies, high confidence.**
   - It sits on a small bedrock nunatak, not on the ice sheet.
   - It is mothballed (March 1991) and carried as a "seasonal field base". It was only briefly de-conserved in summer 2007-08, and the last visit I could document was January 2020.
   - COMNAP's 2024 "Open / Seasonal / peak 10" is nominal.
2. **Russkaya is rock-qualifies, high confidence.**
   - It sits on a bedrock nunatak about 4 km by 1 km at Cape Burks.
   - It is also mothballed and a seasonal field base. The year-round reactivation promised for 2021 had not happened as of the 2025 AARI releases. AARI surveyed building sites in Feb-Mar 2025.
3. **Parodi is the trap in this inventory.**
   - The inventory coordinates (-80.3056, -81.3442) are the Chilean Air Force Parodi / INACH Huneeus base at Patriot Hills, not Union Glacier.
   - Union Glacier is a different place, about 67 km away in a straight line (computed from the coordinates) and 95 km by traverse. Parodi was dismantled in 2013 and its domes were moved there.
   - Both the old Parodi site and the current Union Glacier sites are on moving ice and fail the project test.
   - Union Glacier has 1,450-1,540 m of ice, a bed near -858 m, and surface speed of about 21 m/a at the camp.
   - Parodi's own site was on a thin snowfield over blue ice, with ice of several hundred metres and flow of roughly 5-25 m/a depending on position.

# STATION 1: LENINGRADSKAYA (Russia, OP = context)

**Verified coordinates**
- COMNAP Nov 2024: -69.501496, 159.391148 (69°30.0898'S, 159°23.4689'E), elevation 300 m MSL, record #70 [S1].
- AARI geographical review: 69°29'45"S 159°22'48"E, met platform 304 m. The same page gives the station-area nunatak elevation as 294.5 m and the nunatak summit as 330.5 m [S3].
- COMNAP 2017 catalogue: 69°30'00"S 159°23'00"E, altitude 300 m [S2].
- Rosgidromet: 69°30'S 159°24'E [S5].
- Wikipedia (lead only): 69°30'05"S 159°23'30"E [S33].
- All of these agree to within about 1 km. Confidence is high.
- The lead name "Cape Belousov" is wrong. Belousov Point is the ice-covered south tip of Anderson Peninsula, near Suvorov Glacier at the head of Rennick Bay [Wikipedia search result, lead only]. AARI places the station on the "Leningradskiy" nunatak, in the "Halladay group" (Russian "группа Халладей"; I did not verify the English name) [S3].
- AARI says in one sentence that the nunatak is east of Tomilin Glacier and 2 km from shore. Elsewhere on the same page it puts Tomilin outlet glacier NW of the station [S3]. The coast is 2.7-3 km away.

**Surface and stability: VERDICT rock-qualifies, high confidence**
- AARI [S3] and COMNAP 2017 [S2] agree on the following.
  - The station is on the western part of the Leningradskiy nunatak, 600 m from its top.
  - The nunatak is a rock ridge about 1 km east-west by 100-150 m wide.
  - It stands 100-230 m above the surrounding glacier surface.
  - The rock is alternating leucocratic granite and grey biotite gneiss, with layers 20-40 m thick.
  - About two-thirds of the nunatak is snow-covered.
  - COMNAP 2017 lists the surface the facility is built on as "Ice-free ground" and the location as "ice-free area at the nunatak top".
- The station's own ground does not flow. The surrounding ice does. The south slope drops to a snowfield and a glacier spur. A 150-180 m cliff lies to the north. Tomilin outlet glacier ends in a 15-20 m barrier [S3].
- I did not find an ice-velocity figure for the glaciers around the nunatak. This is a dead end at the sources.
- Reasoning: a nunatak is exposed bedrock by definition. AARI and COMNAP both describe it as rock, so the "bedrock below" test is met.
- Weak points: the usable ground is tiny. The station territory is only 200-250 m by at most 50 m [S2]. AARI describes persistent storms.

**Status and years**
- The 15th Soviet Antarctic Expedition (SAE) chose the site in Jan 1970 (a PDKO hut was put up). The station was built during the 16th SAE and officially opened 25 Feb 1971 [S3][S5].
- It was mothballed 31 March 1991 and moved to the "seasonal field base" category [S5]. Ru.wikipedia's phrase is "законсервирована (фактически — закрытая)" [S33].
- It was temporarily de-conserved in the 2007-08 summer by the 53rd RAE. Helicopters from Akademik Fedorov were used for the first visits in 17 years. Automatic meteorological and geodetic stations were installed [S16][S33].
  - Someone had broken into nearly every door in the interim, and the buildings were iced to the ceiling. The 53rd RAE could enter only two rooms [ru.wikipedia, S33].
- Last documented visit: January 2020, during the 65th RAE [S5]. Automatic weather and geodetic stations were installed early in the 2000s [S5].
- Current status in AARI and RIA texts: "seasonal field base", listed alongside Molodezhnaya, Druzhnaya-4, Bunger Oasis and Russkaya. This appears in AARI releases for the 68th, 69th, 70th and 71st expeditions (Nov 2022 through Nov 2025) [S6-S10]. Those lines are boilerplate. They do not describe a specific visit.
- I did not find any documented visit after January 2020. Not found at the sources.

**Capacity**
- COMNAP 2017 [S2]: 800 m² under roof, 10 beds, peak summer staff 10, winter 0, medical room 20 m². "The base facilities are currently mothballed."
- COMNAP 2024: peak population 10 [S1].
- Original station: a 148 m² service-and-living building, a diesel plant, two magnetic pavilions, an insulated warehouse, a met site and a geodetic point [S5].

**Infrastructure**
- Power is diesel (COMNAP "Fossil fuel", 220 V) [S1][S2].
- Heat came from diesel cooling-system waste heat. The diesel building held a machine hall, workshops and a bath/laundry [S5].
- COMNAP lists a helipad ("Yes"), no airstrip, and no ship landing facilities. It lists satellite phone as the only communications [S2].
- Automatic met and geodetic stations are in operation [S2][S5].
- COMNAP also lists a staffed medical facility and fuel-spill response. These are paper capabilities for a mothballed site.

**Access**
- By ship only, January-March (COMNAP: 1 ship visit per year, Dec-Mar) [S2].
  - Ice-class vessels are required. There is no natural pier. Fast ice often blocks approach all summer [S5].
  - Cargo goes ashore by Mi-8 helicopter from the ship [S5][S16].
  - Ob drifted involuntarily in 1973 and Mikhail Somov in 1977 [S5].
- Air access: COMNAP lists 0 airstrips. AARI's site survey mentions only a possible ski-plane strip, which was never built [S3].
- Original logistics: from Mirny by radio link. I did not find a current air route.

**Other facilities within 25 km**
- None documented. Not found in COMNAP, AARI or the Wikipedia leads. This is a dead end at the sources, not proof of absence.

**Stated purpose**
- Meteorology, actinometry, aerology, geomagnetism, ionosphere, satellite geodesy, sea-ice information, and medical-biological research. The site was chosen for the Pacific sector and for closeness to the magnetic pole [S5].
- Today: a seasonal base with automatic geodetic and met stations [S2][S5].

**Other notes**
- Climate [S3]:
  - Mean annual temperature -14.2 °C. Absolute maximum +5.1 °C (1985), absolute minimum -40.0 °C (1983).
  - Mean wind 8.4 m/s, maximum gust 78 m/s (and 52 m/s gusts blew away a tent and crates in 17th SAE).
  - Precipitation 59.6 mm per year. Polar night is 53 days (late May to mid-July). Polar day is 70 days.
  - Wildlife is scant: skuas, petrels and a few Wilson's storm petrels, with penguins and seals only on the fast ice.
  - COMNAP's 2017 figures (-14.2 °C, 59.6 mm) match AARI.
- Protected areas: none found. I searched for ASPAs near Oates Coast and found none. The ATS database itself was not queried (dead end at the tool).
- Hazards: storms, whiteouts, fast ice that blocks ships, breaking sea ice, a barrier edge 2.7-3 km away, and moisture and ice damage in buildings.

**Revival realism**
- Evidence only. The station was mothballed in 1991 and de-conserved only for one summer (2007-08), at which point it was iced to the ceiling. The 2020 Rosgidromet text mentions only automatic stations and the January 2020 visit. COMNAP 2017 says "mothballed". The AARI 2025 plans list year-round conversion only for Russkaya, not Leningradskaya [S6][S7]. No plan, funding line or schedule for Leningradskaya was found. The site is a nunatak of about 1 km by 100-150 m.

# STATION 2: RUSSKAYA (Russia, OP = context)

**Verified coordinates**
- COMNAP 2024: -74.765726, -136.800072 (74°45.9436'S, 136°48.0043'W), elevation 126 m MSL, opened 1980, record #78 [S1].
- COMNAP 2017: 74°45'00"S 136°40'00"W, altitude 126 m [S2].
- AARI: three variants on one page (74°46'S 136°52'W, 74°46'S 136°50'W, 74°46'S 136°51'W). It gives 124 m for the station and 125 m for the met platform [S4].
- Wikipedia (en): 74°45'59"S 136°48'10"W, 126 m [S33].
- Russian sources give 134 m [S12][ru.wikipedia, S17].
- The coordinate spread is under 4 km (COMNAP 2017 is rounded). The elevation spread is 124-134 m. All agree on Cape Burks.

**Surface and stability: VERDICT rock-qualifies, high confidence**
- AARI: "Станция построена на выходе коренных пород, сложенных биотит-рогообманковыми гнейсами и представляющими собой нунатак" [S4].
  - The nunatak is about 4 km long (NNE-SSW) and 1 km wide, divided by ravines into northern, central and southern parts.
  - The station is on terraces of the central part, a levelled plateau with up to 26 m of relief.
  - The site is covered with colluvium and weathered debris. Frozen ground starts at about 20 cm depth.
  - There are four lakes up to 2 m deep that freeze to the bottom in winter.
  - Mountain tops in the area are almost snow-free. Small glaciers with icefalls run between the hills. Western slopes have glaciers and eastern slopes are clean.
  - AARI notes some shrinking of glaciation near Cape Burks, tied to wind erosion.
- COMNAP 2017 [S2]: built on "Ice-free ground". The coast is a snow-ice barrier 2-40 m high, and hills of 125-145 m stand along the coastline.
- A secondary reading (search summary only, unverified): a 2018 *Geochemistry International* paper on the Cape Burks gabbroids describes a "small, even hilly rocky surface about 1 × 4 km (relative heights 50-100 m)". The size matches the AARI nunatak [S19].
- Ice velocity near the station: not found. Hull Glacier velocity was a dead end at the sources [Hull Glacier/Hull Bay Wikipedia leads]. The station is on bedrock, so the test is met on bedrock alone.
- Weak points: extreme wind, a coastal barrier (2-40 m) with snow-avalanche risk for ships [S4], and permafrost (not a movement issue).

**Status and years**
- Soviet expeditions: 17th SAE (1972) coastal survey. 18th SAE (1973) started building at Cape Burks (generator block only) and abandoned it after storms. 24th SAE (1979) attempt abandoned. 25th SAE (Feb 1980): *Gizhiga* unloaded by helicopter 132 t of cargo, 87 t of fuel and 13 t of food, and the station opened 9 March 1980 [S4].
- 1984-85: two aluminum-panel buildings on metal foundations were added [S6][S33].
- 1990: conserved on 12 March 1990, after the loss of cargo from *Mikhail Somov* in a storm and budget problems [S4][S11][ru.wikipedia S17].
- Visits during conservation: Feb 2008 (53rd RAE, damage assessed, automatic weather and geodetic stations installed), 2010, 2014 [S13 iz.ru], and possibly 2016 [ru.wikipedia, S17]. The visits listed are inconsistent between sources.
- Reactivation plans:
  - 2019: Roscosmos-driven, with a GLONASS ground station. About 300 million rubles (about $4.6 million) was reported to be budgeted [TASS summary, unverified].
  - Dec 2020 (RGO): "if all goes to plan, next year polar explorers will remain there for winter", 10-13 people [S12].
  - 24 Feb 2022 weekly: the astro-pavilion was dismantled, 22 km of ground-penetrating radar was run over a planned landing pad, and engineering-geological surveys were done for the new winter complex [S14].
  - 25 Mar 2025 (AARI): the 70th RAE did the technical studies. Sites were identified for a new winter complex, science pavilions and a landing area for long-range aircraft [S6].
  - AARI also says "Русская" needs complete reconstruction, including new engineering and living infrastructure and science labs [S6]. No year-round date is given in this release.
  - 70th RAE launch text (Nov 2024): work continues on "подготовка инфраструктуры к функционированию в круглогодичном цикле" [S7].
- Current official status: seasonal field base, with five year-round stations named as Novolazarevskaya, Bellingshausen, Mirny, Progress and Vostok [S6][S7][S8][S9][S10].
- The 67th RAE (Feb 2022) found 18 previously unstudied lakes with surface areas up to 8,500 m². Lakes above 85 m elevation are freshwater and those below are saline [S15].
- The 2021 year-round start did not occur. I did not find a source explaining why (not found at the query: the COVID-delay explanation I tried could not be confirmed).

**Capacity**
- COMNAP 2017: 800 m² under roof, 10 beds, peak staff 10, winter 0, medical room 25 m², "majority of base facilities are mothballed" [S2].
- Planned winter staff: 10-13 (RGO Dec 2020) [S12]; "about 15 from 2021" (ru.wikipedia, unverified). No capacity for the planned new complex found.

**Infrastructure**
- Diesel power plant, 220 V [S2].
- Original wooden panel (PDKO) buildings plus two aluminum-panel buildings from 1984-85 [S6].
- Automatic met and geodetic stations were installed in 2008. They are solar-powered with a wind generator rated to 140 m/s (ru.wikipedia, S17).
- The 2006-08 inspection found the diesel hall buried in snow, windows blown out and rooms full of ice [S12]. Reported later issues: mold in two main buildings and a collapsed medical-building roof [S12][S13].
- COMNAP lists no helipad, no airstrip and no ship landing facilities. Only a satellite phone is listed [S2].

**Access**
- Ship only, in a very short window. AARI says February and March are the only months when ice and weather allow ships to approach [S6]. RGO and others say about three weeks per year [S11][S12][S13].
- Cargo and people go ashore by helicopter from the ship (Mi-8). COMNAP 2017 lists "Helicopter, Ship" and one ship visit per year (Dec-Mar) [S2].
- Air access today: none documented. A "landing area for long-range aircraft" is only at the planning stage [S6]. Recent visits used *Akademik Treshnikov* (Feb 2022) and the 70th RAE ship [S14][S6].

**Other facilities within 25 km**
- None. AARI states Russkaya is the only station on a coastal stretch of more than 1,500 km (the Rosgidromet 2020 piece says 3,000 km between the Peninsula and McMurdo) [S6][S4].

**Stated purpose**
- Original: a station in a huge unobserved area, closing the data gap after Little America (closed 1958) and Byrd (closed 1972) [S4]. Original programs: hydrometeorology, geophysics, astronomy, medical-physiological work.
- Planned: GLONASS and spacecraft tracking for Roscosmos [S12][S13]. Also space weather, geophysics and hydrology [S12].
- Current: seasonal studies (lakes, microbiology, mercury samples) and AMS/geodetic stations [S15][S6].

**Other notes**
- Climate (AARI long-term means) [S4]:
  - Mean annual temperature -12.4 °C. Extremes -46.4 °C (1985) and +7.4 °C (1983).
  - Mean wind 12.9 m/s. Maximum mean wind 61 m/s. Maximum gust 77 m/s.
  - 264 days a year over 15 m/s and 136 days over 30 m/s.
  - Polar night 5 May to 9 August. Polar day 2 Nov to 10 Feb.
  - 16 days of continuous 50-60 m/s storm during the 25th SAE.
- Fauna: Adelie penguin colonies of 120-140 birds, Weddell seals, skuas [S4].
- Protected areas: none found (see Search log).
- Hazards: hurricane winds (windows blown out, 1,700 kg fuel drums torn loose in April 1988), whiteouts, a coastal barrier up to 40 m, permafrost, and a ship window of only a few weeks.

**Revival realism**
- Evidence only. Year-round reopening was announced for 2021 (RGO Dec 2020, Interfax 2019, a TASS-reported budget line). As of March 2025, AARI was still at "technical studies", with sites identified and project documentation yet to be developed [S6]. AARI still lists it as a seasonal field base in Nov 2025 [S8][S9]. The 70th RAE program (Nov 2024) says work continues on preparing infrastructure for a year-round cycle [S7]. The window is 3-4 weeks per year for ships, and the old buildings are largely mothballed. The project has an institutional driver (GLONASS, Roscosmos) but no confirmed construction year or capacity. Of the three stations, Russkaya is the only one with an active, documented revival program.

# STATION 3: ARTURO PARODI ALISTER BASE (Chile, OP = context) and UNION GLACIER

**Identity map (the key finding)**

| Place | Where | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Parodi (EPTAP), with the INACH Huneeus section | Patriot Hills, Horseshoe Valley, Heritage Range, about 80.30°S 81.34°W | Dismantled late 2013 |
| ALE Patriot Hills camp, ANI camp | Patriot Hills BIA, about 1 km from Parodi | Closed as the main camp when ALE moved to Union Glacier (2007-10) |
| EPCCGU (Chilean joint station, Parodi's domes reused) | Union Glacier, about 79.77°S 82.9°W | Open seasonally (Nov-Jan) |
| ALE Union Glacier Camp (private) | Union Glacier, 79°46'S 82°52'W, 700 m | Open seasonally and dismantled each season |
| Union Glacier blue-ice runway SCGC | Union Glacier gate area | Open seasonally |

The inventory coordinates match Patriot Hills (Parodi / Huneeus), not Union Glacier. The Parodi station and the Union Glacier camp are different places. EPCCGU is a separate Chilean station from ALE's private camp, though they share the glacier and ALE provides logistics, flights and accommodation for Chilean personnel [S26].

**Verified coordinates**
- Parodi (the inventory's -80.3056, -81.3442):
  - Wikidata Q19754242 has three coordinate claims: -80.31667/-81.3, -80.31833/-81.30778 and -80.30556/-81.34417 (the last sourced to English Wikipedia). It also has two elevations: 983 m (unsourced) and 880 m (sourced to English Wikipedia, oldid 848982569) [S34].
  - English Wikipedia now says 80°18'15"S 81°23'13"W (-80.3042, -81.3869), 880 m [S33].
  - Spanish Wikipedia says 80°18'S 81°22'W (-80.300, -81.367) [S33].
  - The ARQZE architects' project page says 80°19'S 81°18'W, altitude 855 m [S32].
  - The Huneeus section at -80.30194, -81.34417 (Spanish Wikipedia, secondary) [S33].
  - Patriot Hills Base Camp: -80.299971, -81.344236, 880 m, runway SCPZ [S33].
  - Patriot Hills literature position: 80°18'S 81°22'W [S21].
  - The 983 m figure has no source. Use 855-880 m, which the architects, Wikipedia and a sourced Wikidata statement support.
- EPCCGU, five different positions in sources:
  - COMNAP Nov 2024 (type "Camp", Seasonal, Open): -79.766667, -82.916667 (79°46'S 82°55'W), 680 m MSL [S1].
  - INACH: 79°45'S 82°30'W [S29].
  - Wikipedia (en, EPCCGU): 79°46'05"S 83°15'42"W (-79.768, -83.2617) [S33].
  - ALE camp: 79°46'S 82°52'W, 700 m [S25]. Wikipedia en gives the camp as 79°46'05"S 83°15'42"W (identical to the EPCCGU article), 700 m [S33].
  - Glaciology paper: Union Glacier 79°46'S 83°24'W [S20].
  - Runway: 79°46'40"S 83°19'15"W, 750 m [S33].
- Inconsistency: in the Rivera glaciology paper, the "summer base camp" is visible near stake B11 (-79.757, -82.803). The runway is at the gate (B15-B18, -79.76 to -79.80 lat, -83.28 to -83.37 lon). That puts the camp about 10 km east of the runway [S20]. The USM architecture note says EPCCGU was re-established in a crevasse-free zone about 9 km south of the new blue-ice runway [S27]. Treat the exact positions as uncertain to several km.
- No COMNAP 2017 or Nov 2024 listing exists for Parodi (it was gone by 2024). Union Glacier does not appear in the 2017 catalogue [S2].

**Surface and stability: VERDICT ice-fails (both the old Parodi site and Union Glacier), medium-high confidence**

*Old Parodi site (Patriot Hills):*
- The architects wrote that the station was placed in a "zone of superficial equilibrium": a snowfield 800 m north of the blue ice, with about 2 m of hardened snow resting on blue ice. They say this avoids the sinking and burial seen at other stations [S32].
- The same page gives 700 m average ice thickness for the area and a BIA displacement of about 8 m/yr (citing Casassa 1995) [S32]. That is the architects' secondary claim. I did not verify the 700 m.
- Measured values from the literature:
  - Radar showed about 400 m of ice at the centre of the BIA [S21]. FM-CW radar showed 0 m at the mountain foot rising to about 600 m at 2.5 km distance [S23].
  - The Horseshoe Valley transect: 13 m/a near the margins and 25 m/a at the centre; ice thickness up to 1,520 m [S21].
  - Around Patriot Hills the velocities were smaller, increasing to about 20 m/a at the valley centre [S21]. About 5 m/a at stake P9 (1995) [S22, abstract only].
  - Stake B1 (-80.211, -81.230, about 13 km north of Parodi): 12.7 m/a [S20].
  - The BIA is about 8 km by 2 km north of the nunatak [S22]. The ice there is near equilibrium (+0.43 ± 0.42 m/a, not significant) [S21].
- Bedrock: Patriot Hills itself is an exposed rock massif, but the station was built about 1 km from the camp and 800 m north of the blue ice, on snow over ice, not on the nunatak.
- Net: the old site was on ice with a few hundred metres under it, flowing roughly 5-13 m/a (stake data ranging 0.1-34.6 m/a along the Patriot Hills-Union Glacier track). It fails "ideally bedrock below" and the structure was in fact movable. It was moved.

*Union Glacier (EPCCGU and ALE camp):*
- Rivera et al. 2014 [S20] (full text read):
  - Mean ice thickness 1,450 m (maximum 1,540 m "close to the base camp"). Bedrock minimum -858 m. A shallow pinning point at -190 m sits between this deep area and the grounding line [S20].
  - Stake B11 (-79.757, -82.803, near the camp): 20.5 m/a. B12 (-79.760, -82.931): 20.9 m/a. B13: 22.9 m/a. B10 (-79.717, -82.643): 34.6 m/a. B9/Bstat 33 m/a, 38 km upstream of the grounding line at Constellation Inlet [S20].
  - Union gate (the runway BIA): mean 20 m/a, ranging 11-24 m/a. Mean 22.6 m/a over 21 stakes in 2008 [S24].
  - Glacier near equilibrium (-0.012 m/a elevation change) [S20].
  - 22 stakes on the BIA gave an emergence velocity of -0.07 m/a.
- Verdict: the site is on 1.4-1.5 km of ice moving about 20 m/a toward the Ronne Ice Shelf. Over a decade it moves 200 m. It fails the test. The Chilean programme itself treats the station as non-permanent: modules on skis, and domes that must be dug out each season [S30].

**Status and years**
- Parodi station: sources disagree on its start (1995/1996/1999). Chile began INACH campaigns at Patriot Hills in 1995 [S21]. The Spanish Wikipedia gives inauguration 7 Dec 1999 [S33]. ALE says 1996 [S26]. Operated November-December, every two years, with 25 people [S33].
- It was dismantled in the 2013 season. A team of 32 men and 1 woman from the Army, Navy and Air Force worked 21 days, removed over 120 tonnes of ice and snow by shovel, and left for Union Glacier on 8 Dec 2013 [S28].
- EPCCGU was inaugurated 4 Jan 2014 by President Piñera [S26][S29].
- ALE moved to Union Glacier in 2007/08 for wind alignment (Il-76 operations) [S20]. The runway was certified by Chile's DGAC in Dec 2008 [S33]. ALE moved its primary operations in Nov 2010 [S33]. Patriot Hills was kept as a backup runway [S33].
- Current: EPCCGU is "plenamente operativa" in the 2025-26 campaign [S30]. Union Glacier Camp operates Nov-Jan and is dismantled each season [S25].

**Capacity**
- Old Parodi: initial capacity 24 persons per the architects, six 12 m² Igloo Cabin modules for 4 people each. A theoretical maximum of 100 persons was designed for an extended 30-module version [S32]. Wikipedia: 25 people, 40 maximum life support [S33]. The INACH section held 7 researchers (Spanish Wikipedia).
- EPCCGU: INACH lists 8 researchers (summer, about one month), with motorized snow vehicles and Twin Otter transport [S29]. A 2012 inspection report said the station could hold a maximum of 50 scientists (2011/12 season), with about 20 expected for 2012/13 [Antarctic Treaty Inspection report 2012, scratchpad file chi2012.pdf, ATS PDF; the inspection record describes Chile generally, and I read the passage out of context, so treat as moderately reliable].
- A modernization using Sustérmica modules was reported in Dec 2025 [S30]. An unverified search summary of Zona Militar says the new design allows "about 60 researchers" and a "minimum winter staff of 12 to 15" [S36, blocked]. I could not confirm the winter figure. No source I could open says anyone winters at Union Glacier.
- ALE Union Glacier Camp: up to 70 guests in dual-occupancy tents [S25]. Wikipedia en says up to 160 people (a conflict, see below) [S33]. The runway carries a 60-seat B757 [S25].

**Infrastructure**
- Old Parodi: a 50 m first-phase tunnel (steel arcs with a PVC membrane, 2 m radius), plugged into six fibreglass Igloo Cabin modules. It was extensible by 6-arc units. The architects claim it survived 150 km/h katabatic winds that on 1 January destroyed half of the ANI camp [S32].
- EPCCGU: salvaged Parodi tunnel, "Sastrugi" room and connecting sleeves (membranes replaced), re-erected in a crevasse-free zone [S27]. A new habitation module and a sanitary system were added from Nov 2015 [S27]. In 2025: five Sustérmica modules (bathroom, showers, three dining halls) on skis about 80 cm above the surface, with mixed generator and photovoltaic supply [S30]. Much of the summer settlement is still tents plus two domes that must be dug out each season [S30]. The next three phases (kitchen, infirmary, dormitories, storage) will be done by the Joint Staff (EMCO) [S30].
- ALE camp: clam tents, dining tent, kitchen, showers, medical, meteorology, mechanical bays; solar power; Iridium phones [S25][S33]. ALE does not offer fueling at the runway [S25].
- Runway: blue ice, 3,000 m by 50 m, heading 18/36, IATA UGL / ICAO SCGC [S33]. ALE's own pages describe the intercontinental flight as 4 1/4 hours from Punta Arenas, about weekly Nov-Jan, with ski aircraft (Twin Otters, a Basler BT-67) for onward flights [S25].

**Access**
- Air only. Intercontinental flights from Punta Arenas (PUQ) to SCGC, about 4.25 hours [S25]. Chilean Air Force C-130 and Twin Otters also operate [S31]. Overland: Patriot Hills to Union Glacier is 95 km by traverse (El Pinguino, Dec 2013 [S28]). Wikipedia (en) says 70 km and the USM architecture note says 150 km [S33][S27]. That is a three-way conflict. My straight-line calculation from the coordinates gives about 67 km.

**Other facilities within 25 km**
- For Union Glacier: the ALE camp and the runway are within about 10 km of each other [S20]. Nothing else documented within 25 km. Mount Vinson base camp is a flight away (not within 25 km).
- For Patriot Hills: the ALE Patriot Hills camp (about 1 km), formerly the main camp, now a backup runway [S33]. The Casassa work notes "CB97" base camp and the ANI camp near the BIA [S21].

**Stated purpose**
- Old Parodi: logistics, navigation and communications support for the blue-ice runway, plus glaciology, meteorology, geology and cartography (Wikipedia/ALE) [S32][S26].
- EPCCGU: a joint Army/Navy/Air Force and INACH station for support of Chilean and international science; glaciology, meteorology, microbiology [S29][S33]. In 2025-26 the Chilean Air Force Twin Otters supported work at Monte Vinson and Schanz Glacier, including permanent climate stations [S31].
- ALE: a commercial logistics hub and tourism camp [S25].

**Other notes**
- Climate: Patriot Hills record low -35.8 °C (architects) and mean Jan-Feb about -20 °C [S32]. The Chilean Air Force and INACH describe an annual mean of about -20 °C at Union Glacier [S30]. The architects recorded a katabatic gust of 150 km/h on 29 Dec 1999 [S32].
- Protected areas: none found within about 25 km of Union Glacier or Patriot Hills. I did not query the ATS APA database directly.
- Hazards: katabatic winds (over 200 km/h loads cited in design) [S27], crevasses at the runway and the Gifford Peaks passage (1-5 m wide, snow bridges 1.5-3 m) [S20], burial by drifting snow (stations "quedar sepultada" each season [S29]).
- Wikipedia (en) says the Union Glacier base is 1,080 km from the pole. Chile says 1,129 km and ALE says 1,138 km [S33][S30][S25].

**Revival realism**
- Evidence only. The old Parodi site was deliberately dismantled in 2013 and moved to Union Glacier, so there is nothing to revive at Patriot Hills except a backup blue-ice runway. At Union Glacier, EPCCGU has operated for 12 seasons and is being upgraded (five new modules in Dec 2025, three more phases planned). It remains a summer, tent-and-dome settlement dug out each season, and INACH lists 8 researchers on a one-month campaign. No source I could open shows a wintering plan. The Zona Militar summary mentions one, but I could not verify it. The ALE camp is dismantled each season and operates Nov-Jan.

# CONTRADICTIONS BETWEEN SOURCES

1. **Leningradskaya status.** En-Wikipedia search summaries say "closed in 2008" and "reopened around 2008". AARI, Rosgidromet and COMNAP say it was mothballed in 1991, briefly de-conserved in 2007-08, last visited January 2020, and is a seasonal field base. COMNAP 2024 "Open, Seasonal, peak 10" is a nominal listing.
2. **Leningradskaya location.** The lead said "Cape Belousov". Belousov Point is near Suvorov Glacier at Rennick Bay. AARI says a nunatak by Tomilin Glacier. AARI itself puts the nunatak both east of Tomilin Glacier and with Tomilin Glacier to its NW.
3. **Leningradskaya elevation.** 300 m (COMNAP), 304 m (met platform), 294.5 m (station area), 330.5 m (nunatak summit) [S1][S3].
4. **Russkaya coast.** Ruppert Coast (Wikipedia, lead) versus Hobbs Coast (AARI, COMNAP 2017, Wikipedia on Cape Burks) [S4][S2][S18].
5. **Russkaya rock type.** AARI: biotite-hornblende gneiss. A 2018 petrology paper (search summary, unverified): gabbroids, about 100 Ma. The sizes match, so they describe the same outcrop [S4][S19].
6. **Russkaya elevation.** 124 m, 125 m, 126 m, 134 m [S4][S1][S12].
7. **Russkaya precipitation.** COMNAP 2017 says 1,977.2 mm. En-Wikipedia (search summary) says about 166 mm. I could not resolve this. The COMNAP value looks like a data error but I could not prove it [S2].
8. **Russkaya visit years.** 2008, 2010, 2014 [S13] versus 2010, 2014, 2016 (ru.wikipedia) [S17].
9. **Russkaya reactivation.** Planned for 2021 year-round [S12][S13]. AARI in Mar 2025 was still doing site studies [S6].
10. **Parodi start date.** 1995 (Chilean campaigns at Patriot Hills), 1996 (ALE), 1999 (Spanish Wikipedia) [S21][S26][S33].
11. **Parodi position and elevation.** Four coordinate sets in the sources (see above). Elevations 855 m (architects), 880 m (Wikipedia), 983 m (unsourced Wikidata) [S32][S33][S34].
12. **Union Glacier and EPCCGU positions.** COMNAP -79.7667/-82.9167, INACH 79°45'S 82°30'W, Wikipedia 83°15'42"W, ALE 82°52'W [S1][S29][S33][S25]. These differ by up to about 7 km east-west.
13. **Parodi to Union Glacier distance.** 70 km (Wikipedia), 95 km traverse (El Pinguino 2013), 150 km (USM) [S33][S28][S27]. My straight-line figure is about 67 km.
14. **ALE move date.** 2007/08 (Rivera) versus Nov 2010 (Wikipedia) [S20][S33]. Likely both true: first runway use in 2007/08, runway certified Dec 2008, main operations moved Nov 2010.
15. **Union Glacier capacity.** ALE: 70 guests [S25]. Wikipedia en: up to 160 [S33]. INACH: 8 researchers for the Chilean station [S29].
16. **Union Glacier camp elevation.** 700 m (ALE), 680 m (COMNAP), 750 m (runway) [S25][S1][S33].
17. **Distance to the pole.** 1,080 km, 1,129 km, 1,138 km [S33][S30][S25].
18. **Patriot Hills ice thickness.** About 400 m (radar at the BIA centre), about 600 m at 2.5 km, 700 m (architects), 850 m (the same architects, "perforate 850 m of ice") [S21][S23][S32]. They are different positions and sources, but not reconciled.
