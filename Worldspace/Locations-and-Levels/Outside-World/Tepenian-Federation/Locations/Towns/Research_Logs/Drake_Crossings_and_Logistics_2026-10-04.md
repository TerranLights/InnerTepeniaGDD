## 1. REAL CROSSINGS TODAY

**1.1 Route geometry (as logged by ships)**
- Ships leave Ushuaia in the late afternoon and go east down the Beagle Channel with a pilot. A Chilean pilot left the Sylvia Earle by tender at the channel's east end on 10 Nov 2023 [S45]. The Beagle's east exit is at Isla Nueva and Isla Picton, and Isla Hornos lies to the southwest [S45].
- From there the route is south-southeast across the Drake to the South Shetlands, then the Bransfield or Gerlache approaches [S45, S50].
- Punta Arenas ships go through the Chilean channels to Puerto Williams (about 2 days), then cross [PL; S44]. Returning Gould voyages passed Le Maire Strait, so they came out on the Atlantic side [S50].

## 2. SEASONS AND WEATHER WINDOWS

**2.1 Season shape**
- IAATO: 98% of tourism voyages run in the Peninsula region during the seven-month season, October to April. They depart from Ushuaia or, to a lesser extent, other ports [S1].
- Ushuaia Antarctic voyages by month, 2024-25 [S19]:

| Oct | Nov | Dec | Jan | Feb | Mar | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 78 | 98 | 97 | 94 | 44 | 422 |

  The season usually starts mid to late October and ends late March or early April [S19].
- Argentina's 2025-26 campaign ran from 28 Nov, when the Irizar sailed from Buenos Aires, to 31 Mar [S25]. Chile's navy campaign ran from late October to late March [S36].
- ALE's Union Glacier flights run about weekly, November to January [S51].

**2.2 Off-season crossings**
- R2R lists Gould cruises departing in every calendar month, 1997–2024. Departures by month, January to December: 11, 26, 28, 25, 19, 19, 17, 10, 23, 39, 28, 24 (n=269) [S42, calc]. The NSF subcommittee reports 285 operating days per year over 2009–2018 [S41].
- Examples of winter-season Gould work:
  - **LMG0707**, a hazardous-waste shuttle, left Punta Arenas on 7 Jun 2007 and reached Palmer on 11 Jun.
  - **LMG2003**, a Palmer turnover, ran 3–21 Jun 2020 [S42].
- The Sir David Attenborough called at Palmer for about 24 h on 30–31 May 2025 [S41].
- Winter air support is limited: "limited air/aviation capabilities... in the winter months" [S15].
- In July 2013 two Hercules spent about 30 days at Río Gallegos for Marambio. They completed 10 flights and moved about 450 drums at 40 per flight. They had to leave before 10:30 to return in daylight [S27].
- In July 2017 an Orcadas medevac was delayed for days: the ice runway fractured, Marambio closed, and a Hercules airdropped medicine from Río Gallegos (1,800 km, 4 h) [S27].

**2.3 Departure decisions and holds (documented cases only)**
- The Sylvia Earle left a day late in Jan 2024 after "extremely high winds". The port closed for a few hours, and one ship broke a line and dropped an emergency anchor while trying to dock [S45].
- In Nov 2023 the Sylvia Earle turned back, sheltered in the Beagle, returned to port, and left a second time 6 days after embarkation [S45].
- The Almirante Maximiano waited 31 h [S44].
- A 5 Jan 2026 storm broke the World Voyager's moorings at Ushuaia. Winds were 30 kn sustained with gusts near 40 kn. It struck the pier and the Viking Octantis. Damage was minor [S23].
- No systematic delay or cancellation statistics were found for ships. See dead ends.

## 6. HISTORICAL CONTEXT (short)

- **Cape Horn under sail.** Voyages of 6 to 8 months were recorded [W]. The fastest westward rounding by a commercial sailing ship, Priwall in 1938, took 5 d 14 h. The Panama Canal (Aug 1914) greatly reduced use of the Horn [W].
- **Belgica, 1898.** Left Staten Island on 13 Jan 1898. The South Shetlands were sighted on 19–20 Jan and the Palmer Archipelago on 23 Jan, so 6–10 days [S48].
- **Uruguay rescue, 1903.** Left Buenos Aires on 8 Oct, called at Ushuaia, reached Paulet Island and Snow Hill about 8 Nov, and left Antarctica on 12 Nov [S32].
- **Shackleton, 1916.** "Boat journey across 800 miles of stormy sub-Antarctic ocean"; "the next sixteen days"; landed in King Haakon Bay on 10 May [S48].
- Steam and diesel brought the crossing to about 2 days for expedition ships. The Charcot expeditions were not verified (dead end).

## 7. SAFETY, COSTS AND CONSTRAINTS

- **SAR bodies:** MRCC Ushuaia, MRCC Punta Arenas, ARCC Punta Arenas, MRSC Antártica (Fildes Bay), MRSC Williams, and MRCC Chile (Valparaíso) [S16]. Argentina and Chile run Peninsula-area SAR.
- **SAR assets:** "very limited and spread-out"; the timeliest response is often a "ship of opportunity" [S15]. Gateway cities need hospital and hyperbaric capacity [S15].
- **Medevac examples:**
  - 3 Oct 2026: Chilean Air Force C-130, request 21:00 on 2 Oct, patient moved from Frei to Punta Arenas in under 24 h [S36].
  - 29 Nov 2022: Viking Polaris was sailing to Ushuaia for a Zodiac-injury medevac when the wave struck [S47].
  - A peer-reviewed study of 26 voyages found 4 evacuations among 2,366 passengers, and 1 death [S46].
- **Polar Code:** in force 1 Jan 2017, south of 60°S, as a mandatory SOLAS and MARPOL instrument [S17, S7]. Category A means at least medium first-year ice, B thin first-year ice, C open water. The officer training rule applies from 1 Jul 2018 [S17]. IAATO notes the main continuing challenge is bridge-officer certification [S7].
- **Heavy fuel oil:** banned in the Antarctic from 1 Aug 2011 [S17].
- **Landing limits:** the 500-passenger landing limit and the 1-guide-per-20-passengers ratio come from Measure 15 (2009), as cited by Infuetur [S19].
- **Yachts:** most commercial yachts regularly operating in these waters have been knocked down and several rolled 360°. A typical season sees 20–30 yachts [S12]. 44 yachts were sighted or reported in 2023-24 [S10]. There is "no rescue service" and insurance is required [S12].
- **Biosecurity:** IAATO enhanced procedures during avian influenza require full decontamination between regions [S8]. Ships do biosecurity inside the Beagle before the Drake [S45].
- **Costs, context only:** the "fly" option adds roughly US$4,000–7,000 per person [S50]. Fares of US$6,000–50,000 per person [U]. Port berthing tariffs were reduced recently at Ushuaia (effective date 1 May 2026 [U]), per EPAustral [S33].
- **Fuel scale reference:** Australia's program has shipping at 40–45% of fuel use. This is Australian and not transferable [U].
- A fiber cable across the Drake was found feasible by a Chilean study: 1,600 km, about US$370 million [U].

## 8. WHAT THE EVIDENCE IMPLIES FOR A PALMER-SUBNET SUPPLY ROUTE (evidence only, no lore)

**Supported by evidence**
- **Transit.** Ushuaia to Palmer City is 612 nmi GC, or about 653 nmi via the Beagle exit (calc). That is 44–54 h at 12–15 kn and 76 h at 8.6 kn. Punta Arenas to Palmer takes 4–5 days on a utility ship (Gould).
- **Holds.** Logged weather holds before departure ran from 1 day to 6 days, plus 31 h anchored at Puerto Williams.
- **Frequency precedent.** Ushuaia handled 98 Antarctic voyages in its peak month (about 3.2 a day, calc) on a pier rated for 6 ships a day.
- **Vessel class.** Ice-strengthened ships of 70–130 m at 12–15 kn, with stabilizers or roll tanks and cargo in the 900–2,100 m³ range. Fuel needs tanker-class hulls (Patagonia 10,000 m³).
- **Windows.** Volume runs October to April. Winter sailings are demonstrated (Gould in every month; Sir David Attenborough at Palmer on 30–31 May 2025) but require ice-strengthened hulls. Air support is weakest in winter.
- **Pier depth.** The Palmer analog pier was 18 ft deep and was being rebuilt to 34 ft.
- **Improvement levers with evidence.**
  - Larger hulls: the Sir David Attenborough at 129 m holds 13 kn.
  - Better forecasts: operators already decide on forecasts by captain and expedition leader.
  - Alternative staging: Puerto Williams works as a weather-wait anchorage. Petrel sits near sea level with no Marambio-style cloud banks.
  - Runway repair and extension at Marsh, Petrel and Rothera.
- **Main risks documented.**
  - Single-gateway dependence at Ushuaia: worn piles, federal intervention, labor conflict, a wind breakaway.
  - Marsh fog, with a computed upper bound near 79% flyable days.
  - Hercules fleet availability: Dec 2017 groundings and the July 2013 failure.
  - Breaking-wave window damage (2022).
  - Scarce SAR assets.
  - Funding decisions: NSF ended both ships.

**Not supported by evidence (invention if used)**
- Any "share of crossings" that is calm versus rough.
- The fraction of vessels carrying stabilizers.
- "89–90%" as a statistic (Infuetur's claim; IAATO publishes no per-port counts).
- Exact sailed distances (only GC and a waypoint estimate here).
- Any cadence, pier capacity or national route statistics for 2564 onward.

**Notes against the project's `Ports.md` (§3b).** Its "about 2 days to the South Shetlands" and the 984/1,133/991 km figures agree with this research (calc). The "89 to 90%" figure is Ushuaia's or Infuetur's claim, not an IAATO statistic. Ports.md also says Punta Arenas is the only air gateway to King George Island, which is consistent. But Ushuaia's pier is under federal intervention and worn (2026), which Ports.md does not mention.

## CONTRADICTIONS BETWEEN SOURCES

1. **Marambio runway:** 1,208 m by 40 m (Jan 2024 airfield chief) versus a 1,600 m by 40 m second runway test-flown in July 2015 [S27]. Which is operational now is unresolved.
2. **Sir David Attenborough cargo:** about 900 m³ scientific [S38] versus 2,100 m³ dry cargo [S39].
3. **Irizar speed:** 16.5 kn max (Wikipedia, as built) versus 17.2 kn (search summary of Argentine pages, post-refit).
4. **Patagonia fuel:** 10,000 m³ (gob.ar) versus 9,600 t (defensa.com) [S26].
5. **DPP versus Infuetur Ushuaia counts:** the DPP's 2023 season row shows 555 calls and 164,557 passengers against Infuetur's 602 voyages and 173,211 for 2023-24. DPP 2024 shows 510 calls and 144,172 against Infuetur's 565 and 157,082. The 2022 rows agree within 2%. Definitions differ (positioning voyages) [S18, S19].
6. **IAATO internal:** older papers give different values for the same seasons (e.g. 2019-20: 408 versus 414 voyages). The 579 voyage total does not match the appendix sum [S1, S5, S6].
7. **Ushuaia pier width:** 28 m (national agency) versus 29 m (search summary) [S20].
8. **Lake/Shake shares:** 70/30, "fewer than one in four" and "one in ten" calm. None sourced [S50].
9. **Drake width:** 800 km, 850 km (BAS GEOMAP) and a "650 km" figure that no point pair reproduces [PL].
10. **Dash-7 flight time:** 5 h Stanley to Rothera (BAS) versus 4–7 h in various secondary summaries [S38; U].
11. **Gould berths:** 26, 27, 28 and 37 across sources [S41, W].
