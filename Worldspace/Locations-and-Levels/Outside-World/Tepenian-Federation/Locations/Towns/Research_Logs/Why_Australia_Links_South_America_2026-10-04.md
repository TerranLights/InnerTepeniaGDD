## 1. Antarctic and Southern Ocean cooperation

**Institutional fabric (the strongest "real" link)**
- **The Antarctic Treaty Secretariat is in Buenos Aires** (operating since 1 Sept 2004). Australia proposed Hobart, the US proposed Washington, and Argentina won; consensus came at ATCM XXIV (2001). [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_of_the_Antarctic_Treaty
- **CCAMLR is headquartered in Hobart** (in force 1982). Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Ecuador and Uruguay are full members (Peru is acceding). [F] https://www.ccamlr.org/en/organisation/members and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_for_the_Conservation_of_Antarctic_Marine_Living_Resources
- Six South American states are Antarctic Treaty Consultative Parties: Argentina, Chile, Brazil, Uruguay, Peru, Ecuador. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Treaty_System
- **Five gateway cities** are recognized: Hobart, Christchurch, Cape Town, Punta Arenas, Ushuaia. A 2009 statement of intent among them exists [S: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/15540/antarctica-agreement-signed].
- Hobart's own page says more than 20 countries use Punta Arenas. [F] https://www.hobartcity.com.au/Council/Hobarts-international-relationships/Antarctica
- A 2016-19 Australian Research Council project (Western Sydney University) linked Hobart, Christchurch and Punta Arenas. Partners included Hobart City Council, the Chilean Antarctic Institute (INACH) and the University of Magallanes. [F] https://www.westernsydney.edu.au/ics/projects/past-projects/antarctic-cities-and-the-global-commons-rethinking-the-gateways
- **Search and rescue:** five states (Argentina, Australia, Chile, New Zealand, South Africa) share Antarctic SAR responsibility. Australia's own SAR region is about 53 million km² (a tenth of the Earth's surface). [F] https://www.amsa.gov.au/safety-navigation/search-and-rescue/australias-search-and-rescue-region
- COMNAP's SAR workshops rotate through these states: Chile 2008 and 2016, Argentina 2009, NZ 2018, Hobart 2023. The 2023 report notes that even from the Peninsula (the closest region) a vessel takes about 3 days to reach the coast in good weather. [F] COMNAP SAR Workshop 5 report (PDF saved locally, text read): https://www.comnap.aq/s/SAR-Workshop-5-2023-Final-Report.pdf
- Chile and Argentina run a Combined Antarctic Naval Patrol (PANC) since 1998, covering 10°W to 131°W south of 60°S. [S] https://dialogo-americas.com/?p=5251
- **Treaty-making:** a 1990 DFAT document is titled "Australian Objective Gains Support in Chile" (the Antarctic mining ban / CRAMRA fight at a Treaty meeting in Chile). [F, metadata only] https://heritage.utas.edu.au/index.php/australia-department-of-foreign-affairs-and-trade-antarctica-australian-objective-gains-support-in-chile
- The Madrid Protocol followed in 1991 and took effect in 1998. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_on_Environmental_Protection_to_the_Antarctic_Treaty
- DFAT's current country brief says Australia and Chile "cooperate multilaterally as Parties to the Antarctic Treaty" and are "likeminded" on Antarctica; Australia says the same of Argentina. [S] https://www.dfat.gov.au/geo/chile/chile-country-brief and https://www.dfat.gov.au/geo/argentina/argentina-country-brief
- The 22 Nov 2025 joint statement (80th anniversary) lists the Antarctic Treaty System among shared forums. [F] https://pennywong.com.au/media-hub/media-statements/80th-anniversary-of-diplomatic-relations-between-australia-and-chile/

**What I could NOT find: a bilateral Australia-Chile or Australia-Argentina Antarctic operational agreement.**
- By contrast, New Zealand and Chile signed an Antarctic Cooperation Arrangement on 19 Nov 2018 with an explicit Christchurch-Punta Arenas gateway clause. [F] https://www.treaties.mfat.govt.nz/search/arrangement/734/130
- INACH's director's interview lists Japan, Korea and China as partners, with no Australian agreement. [F, undated] https://www.bcn.cl/observatorio/asiapacifico/noticias/instituto-antartico-chileno-asia-pacifico-convenio-cooperacion
- The Wikipedia page on INACH lists no Australian agreement either.
- Australian intercontinental logistics are Hobart-centric. A parliamentary submission says the Australian Antarctic Division found it unfeasible to link its aircraft to other nations' intercontinental systems. [S] https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/ncet/antarctic/subs/sub37.pdf
- I also found no joint Australia-Chile flights or logistics.
- A 2020 MercoPress claim that Australia's icebreaker Aurora Australis would be donated to Argentina has no confirmed outcome. [F, unconfirmed] https://en.mercopress.com/2020/10/12/icebreaker-donation-for-argentina-australia-s-aurora-australis/comments

**Claims map** (a reason the two ends of the continent do not conflict)
- Seven claimants. Argentina, Chile and the UK overlap between 25°W and 80°W. Marie Byrd Land (90°W-150°W, about 1.6 million km²) is unclaimed. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_claims_in_Antarctica
- The UK, France, Australia, NZ and Norway recognize one another's claims; Argentina and Chile are in the overlap dispute. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_claims_in_Antarctica
- Australia's claim does not overlap with the South American ones. [S]
- From my background knowledge, not fetched: Australia 45°E-160°E and NZ Ross Dependency 160°E-150°W.

## 2. Trade and resources

**Agreements**
- The Australia-Chile FTA entered into force 6 March 2009, Australia's first FTA with a Latin American country. [F] https://www.austrade.gov.au/en/how-we-can-help-you/australian-exporters/free-trade-agreements/chile
- CPTPP: Peru joined 2021 and Chile 2023, alongside Australia and Mexico. [S] https://www.austrade.gov.au/en/how-we-can-help-you/australian-exporters/free-trade-agreements/trans-pacific-partnership
- Peru-Australia FTA took effect 11 Feb 2020. [S] https://www.dfat.gov.au/trade/agreements/in-force/pafta/Pages/peru-australia-fta
- Australia is an observer in the Pacific Alliance. [S] https://www.internationalaffairs.org.au/?p=23840
- No Mercosur FTA exists (only proposals). [S] https://www.internationalaffairs.org.au/australianoutlook/time-for-australia-to-pay-attention-to-mercosur/
- The Cairns Group, founded in Cairns in 1986, includes Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Uruguay, Peru, Paraguay and Colombia, with Australia as a driver. [S] https://www.britannica.com/topic/Cairns-Group

**Scale is modest, and is the main counterweight**
- Chile two-way trade is about A$1.39bn (2024, DFAT-adjusted figures). [S] https://www.dfat.gov.au/sites/default/files/chle-cef.pdf
- Chile two-way trade was A$1.4bn in 2016. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Chile_relations
- Chile exports to Australia were US$235m in Jan-Aug 2026, mostly gold. [F, secondary] https://www.riotimesonline.com/chile-australia-strategic-partnership-2026/
- Australia-Argentina trade was A$1.076bn (2016), with more than 50 Australian companies in Argentina. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Australia_relations
- Australia-Brazil trade was US$1.7bn (2021). Brazil is Australia's largest Latin American export market and about half of Australia's direct investment in the region. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Brazil_relations
- The Rio Times piece says nothing was signed at the Sept 2026 Chile-Australia "strategic partnership" meeting.

**Mining**
- Australia and Chile are both lithium producers, so they are competitors as much as partners. USGS 2025 data: Australia 92kt, China 62kt, Chile 56kt; Chile holds 9.2 Mt of the 37 Mt world reserves. [S] https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-lithium.pdf
- Cross-ownership: Chilean SQM holds 50% of Wesfarmers' Mt Holland (WA) lithium JV, with a Kwinana refinery. [S] https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/wesfarmers-makes-546-mln-bid-for-lithium-spodumene-producer-kidman-resources/
- Rio Tinto and ENAMI have a Chilean lithium JV. [S] https://latinlawyer.com/article/enami-and-rio-tinto-ink-chilean-lithium-jv
- BHP's Escondida copper mine is tied to supply contracts with about 80 Australian firms. [S] https://mining.com.au/australias-mets-companies-turn-to-chile/
- About 60 Australian mining-equipment and services (METS) firms export to Chile. [S] https://www.austrade.gov.au/en/news-and-analysis/news/australian-mets-companies-build-momentum-in-latin-america
- [I] Mining services and desert/high-altitude mining know-how are the most transferable skills. Antarctic construction and logistics could plausibly reuse them, but I found no source saying so.

**Agriculture**
- Counter-seasonal produce is real for Chile and Australia: both are southern fruit exporters, and Chilean grapes and cherries face phytosanitary and seasonal-overlap friction at Australian entry. [S] https://www.freshfruitportal.com/2011/07/08/australia-to-make-crimson-grape-shift/
- Wine is competition (Tri-Nations Chile-Australia challenge), not cooperation. [S] https://the-buyer.net/wine/wines-chile-australia-tri-nations
- Uruguay became the first South American importer of Australian merinos in 1956. Eucalypts underpin about 80% of Uruguayan forestry. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Uruguay_relations
- Southern Patagonian sheep farming grew from Falkland Islands settlers (late 1800s). [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagonian_sheep_farming_boom

**Shipping**
- Chile's Valparaiso to Sydney is a trans-Pacific route that avoids the Panama and Cape Horn detours.
- Historic wool-clipper route: Australia/NZ eastbound along about 40°S, round Cape Horn, then north. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clipper_route

## 3. Shared geography

- **Open Southern Ocean.** The Roaring Forties are interrupted only by Tasmania, New Zealand and southern South America. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roaring_Forties
- **Drake Passage.** About 800 km wide, with waves over 12 m. It is the choke point for the Antarctic Circumpolar Current. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_Passage
- **Gondwana.** The monito del monte (Chile) is more closely related to Australian marsupials than to other American ones. Estimated divergence is about 46 Ma, with Eocene fossils on the Antarctic Peninsula. This is evidence of a land connection through Antarctica. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monito_del_monte
- **Astronomy**
  - Australia has had a 10-year ESO strategic partnership (announced 9 May 2017), giving access to Chile's La Silla Paranal Observatory. [F] https://eso.org/public/announcements/ann17023/
  - The Giant Magellan Telescope at Las Campanas (Chile) has a seven-country consortium: Australia, Brazil, Chile, Israel, South Korea, Taiwan, USA. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Magellan_Telescope
  - SKA-Low is in Western Australia; SKA-Mid is in South Africa. [S] https://research.csiro.au/ska/ska-global
  - Chilean astronomers visited Canberra, Aug 2026. [F] https://www.chile.gob.cl/australia/noticias/astronomos-chilenos-visitaron-canberra-para-fortalecer-la-cooperacion
- **Southern Cross on flags.** Australia, NZ, Brazil, PNG and Samoa, plus the Magallanes region of Chile. [S] https://www.worldatlas.com/articles/flags-that-feature-the-southern-cross.html
- **Aboriginal and Mapuche sky traditions.** I found no direct evidence of shared constellations. Some Indigenous cultures on three continents (including the Moqoit of Argentina) share dark-cloud figures in the Milky Way. [S] https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16649
- **Southern fisheries.** CCAMLR manages krill and toothfish fisheries. Toothfish fisheries are in Areas 48, 58, 88 and attract IUU (illegal) fishing. [S] https://www.antarctica.gov.au/science/conservation-and-management-research/southern-ocean-fisheries/
- **Seasons.** Southern seasons align, so the Antarctic summer window (roughly Nov to Mar) is shared. PANC runs 15 Nov to 15 Mar. [I, supported by S]

## 4. Historical and cultural links

- **Early trade (a durable "Pacific route" story).** Chilean wheat and flour went to New South Wales. About 262 sailings from Australasia to South America in 1831-48, including 171 Sydney-Valparaiso voyages. Australia imported wheat in 21 of 30 years 1830-60. [F] https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-latin-american-studies/article/before-the-gold-fleets-trade-and-relations-between-chile-and-australia-18301848/3C298BEF004107DD92A758A353FE4DE9
- Chile's wheat boom for the Australian gold rush lasted 1850-57. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_wheat_cycle
- Chilean miners brought the "Chilean mill" to Australian goldfields. After the 1973 coup, 21,029 Chileans sought sanctuary in Australia. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_Australians
- **2021 census, born in:** Chile 29,856; Brazil 46,720; Colombia 35,033; Argentina 17,977; Peru 11,531; Uruguay 9,205. [S, ABS QuickStats; Uruguay F] https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/quickstats/2021/8204_AUS
- Brazil was the fifth-largest source of international students in Australia in 2020. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Brazil_relations
- Direct flights: Sydney-Santiago (LATAM, about 14 h) and Melbourne-Santiago. [S] https://www.airdatanews.com/latam-will-resume-direct-flights-between-santiago-de-chile-and-sydney-in-october/
- **Falklands/Malvinas.** Australia sided with the UK in 1982 and imposed sanctions on Argentina. Relations normalized later. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Australia_relations. This is a political variable, not a durable one.
- **Rugby.** Argentina joined the Rugby Championship in 2012 (the Puma Trophy is Argentina vs Australia). [S] https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2012/8/17/argentina-join-rugbys-tri-nations-elite

## 5. Communications history and infrastructure

**Cables**
- Southern Cross (2000) and Southern Cross NEXT (service 7 July 2022) connect Sydney, Auckland, Fiji, Hawaii and the US West Coast. They do not touch South America. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cross_Cable
- **Humboldt:** about 14,800 km from Valparaiso/Santo Domingo, Chile, via French Polynesia, to Sydney. Google and Chile's Desarrollo Pais through the Humboldt Connect joint venture. Billed as the first cable between South America and Asia-Pacific. [F] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/infrastructure/announcing-humboldt-the-first-cable-route-between-south-america-and-asia-pacific
- Humboldt branch units reach Juan Fernandez and Easter Island. [S] https://www.carey.cl/en/desarrollo-pais-y-google-se-asocian-para-desarrollar-proyecto-de-cable-submarino-humboldt-que-conectara-chile-y-australia/
- 2026 status: approved; installation Q4 2026; service 2028. A second Santo Domingo-Panama City cable (about 6,500 km) is reported by one source. [F, single source] https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-technology/optical-fixed-networks/20454-humboldt-cable-gets-go-ahead-from-chile.html
- The Australian Communications Minister (Michelle Rowland) welcomed it as "connecting Australia and Latin America for the first time." [F] Google blog above.
- The 22 Nov 2025 joint statement says Australia and Chile "form a bridge connecting the Pacific with Latin America." [F, Wong statement above]
- **The 2022 plan** had a roughly 2,000 km Antarctic stub to Scott Base (Ross Sea, not the Peninsula), plus Juan Fernandez, Easter Island and NZ branches. [F] https://www.theregister.com/2022/08/01/humboldt_cable_rfp/. The 2024-26 Google version contains no Antarctic spur in the sources I read.
- **Antarctic cables:**
  - Chile (Subtel): Drake Passage study with two options. About 1,600 km, US$370m: Punta Arenas and Puerto Williams with 3 Chilean bases. About 4,100 km, US$620m: 9 Antarctic landings linking Chile, Argentina, Brazil, US and UK bases. Feasibility "confirmed" in 2026, but Chile will not fund it alone and expects about 8 years of approvals. [F] https://maritime-executive.com/article/antarctica-submarine-cable-project-confirmed-feasible and https://www.marineinsight.com/4100-km-fibre-cable-across-drake-passage-could-end-antarcticas-hard-drive-data-runs/
  - Chile-Antarctica cable financed by the Multilateral Cooperation Center for Development Finance (MCDF): US$1.68m for a feasibility study from a Chilean landing station. [F] https://www.themcdf.org/en/what-we-do/projects/2023/Antarctic-Submarine-Cable.html
  - NSF McMurdo study (Dec 2023): Invercargill (NZ) to McMurdo is over 4,900 km; Sydney via Macquarie Island is about 6,424 km; NZ recommended. Branches to Macquarie Island and Terra Nova Bay. [F] https://polarjournal.net/hello-antarctica-subsea-cable-to-mcmurdo-gains-momentum/ and https://www.nsf.gov/geo/updates/connecting-last-continent-new-desktop-study-antarcticas
  - Australia: Bureau of Meteorology proposal (Feb 2021) to cable Tasmania to Mawson, Davis, Casey and Macquarie Island. Preliminary only. [F] https://www.anareclub.org/antarctic-fibre-link/
  - No cable currently reaches Antarctica.

**Satellites and ground stations**
- GEO satellites cannot cover latitudes beyond about ±81°. [S] https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/NSF-Public-Release-DTS-Final.pdf
- SSC runs Punta Arenas (opened 2012, 52.93°S). SSC says it combines with SSC's Western Australia Space Center for "unmatched coverage opportunities" for polar-orbit missions. [F] https://sscspace.com/services/satellite-ground-stations/our-stations/punta-arenas-station/
- KSAT and AWS also operate at Punta Arenas. [S] https://www.ksat.no/news/news-archive/2019/NASA-selects-KSAT-Punta-Arenas-station-for-KA-band-support/
- Starlink: Antarctica has no gateway station, so users reach ground stations in South America, Australia and NZ via laser inter-satellite links. Terminals have been routed via a Sydney ground station, and one source mentions Starlink and AWS in Punta Arenas. [S] https://gizmodo.com/spacex-starlink-internet-connectivity-antarctica-1850018202 and https://dplnews.com/amazon-web-service-compra-terreno-en-punta-arenas-para-instalar-satelites-y-sera-vecino-de-starlink/
- ESA deep-space antennas include New Norcia (Western Australia) and Malargue (Argentina). NASA's DSN has Canberra. [S] https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Operations/ESA_inaugurates_deep_space_antenna_in_Australia
- **Not verified:** Dongara as a Chinese-leased SSC site (Wikipedia/news mention); I did not verify the Tidbinbilla specifics.

**Radio**
- 22 May 1924: Argentine amateur CB8 (Buenos Aires) worked NZ 2AC (Gisborne) for over 2 hours. This is described as the first two-way contact between the two continents. [F] https://w2pa.net/HRH/?p=2926
- Australian shortwave pioneer Charles Maclurcan (2CM) moved to shortwave 21 Feb 1924. [S]
- Early Macquarie Island relay (1912) for Mawson's expedition. [S] https://www.antarctica.gov.au/news/stations/davis/2024/16-february/
- In 1972, IPS 6 m beacons at Casey (VK0GR) and Mawson (VK0MA) showed Antarctica-Australia could be bridged on 6 m. [S]
- HF over Antarctica is hard: multi-hop propagation is rare because ice is highly absorptive, except near the Transantarctic Mountains at dawn and dusk. [F] https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/fac_pubs/155
- HF paths from the south pass through the dark polar cap and favor only low frequencies in polar night. [F] https://www.n4gn.com/sr/propsa.html
- I found no documented Australia-Chile or Australia-Argentina long-path contact and no first direct VK-LU/CE contact.

**Shortest paths to Atlantic-side South America cross Antarctica [C]**
- The Sydney-Santiago great circle dips to about 62°S (near 140°W).
- Sydney-Buenos Aires reaches about 69°S (near 134°W).
- Melbourne-Montevideo reaches about 76°S.
- Perth-Buenos Aires reaches about 86°S, within roughly 4.4° of the South Pole.
- [I] Over millennia, any shortest-signal path from the Australian sector to the Atlantic side of South America runs over or near the continent. A settled Antarctica makes this the natural relay.

**The two ends face different gateways**
- Casey is about 3,400 km from Hobart but only about 2,200 km from McMurdo (the Ross Sea). Casey is about 5,700 km from the Peninsula's Frei station, which is 1,000-1,200 km from its gateways.
- [I] The Australian sector is more naturally linked to the Ross Sea (and New Zealand) than to the Peninsula.

**Jurisdictions on a Casey to Ross to Marie Byrd Land to Peninsula chain** (by the claim map above)
- Australian sector, then NZ's Ross Dependency, then unclaimed Marie Byrd Land (90-150°W), then Chilean claim at 90°W, with overlaps with Argentina and the UK east of 80°W.
- [I] New Zealand and unclaimed territory sit in the middle of any Australia-Peninsula relay. An Australia-Chile link would also have to deal with the Chile-Argentina-UK overlap on the Peninsula.

**Relay options**
- **Pacific cable (real, planned):** Sydney to French Polynesia to Valparaiso, about 14,800 km, not touching Antarctica. Operational 2028. [I] The real cable is about 30% longer than the 11,350 km great circle because of Tahiti and seabed constraints.
- **Southern Ocean cable (Hobart to Punta Arenas, about 8,700 km):** no proposal found.
- **Ross hub (partly proposed):**
  - Tasmania-Casey, a short Casey-McMurdo coastal leg, and an NSF McMurdo-NZ/Sydney cable all appear in proposals.
  - A McMurdo to Peninsula to Punta Arenas leg would join the Chilean Drake Passage project (which targets Peninsula bases, not McMurdo).
  - Scientists have imagined McMurdo as a data hub for Ross Sea sites, and satellite links from the South Pole to McMurdo are easier than from the South Pole to NZ or Australia. [S] https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/space-has-better-internet-than-antarctica-but-that-might-change
- **Ice-sheet terrestrial link:** no precedent for overland fiber anywhere on the ice in the sources I found. Not verified.
- **LEO relay (real now):** polar-orbit satellites with laser inter-satellite links and ground stations in Sydney and Punta Arenas.
- **HF:** unreliable across the polar cap, per section 5.

## 7. Bottom line: reasons ranked by durability

**A. Physical or geometric (millennia)**
1. Shortest Australia-Atlantic South America paths cross Antarctica [C, I]. Sydney-Santiago is 11,350 km, with no land between.
2. A shared open Southern Ocean and westerlies, with Tasmania, NZ and Patagonia the only landmasses in the band. The Drake Passage chokepoint. [F]
3. The Antarctic Peninsula faces South America (about 1,000 km from Ushuaia) and the Australian sector faces Tasmania. That is the structural reason there are two gateway systems. [C, F]
4. Southern sky and astronomy sites (Atacama, Murchison, South Africa) and the Southern Hemisphere sky. Australia already has ESO access and a GMT stake in Chile. [F]
5. High-southern-latitude polar-orbit ground stations. Punta Arenas plus Western Australia is explicitly a coverage pair. [F]

**B. Institutional (centuries if the Antarctic Treaty System persists)**
6. The Treaty Secretariat in Buenos Aires and CCAMLR in Hobart. Six South American Consultative Parties. Five SAR states with rotating workshops. Gateway cities. [F]
7. Cairns Group alignment on agriculture. [S]

**C. Economic-structural (contingent on commodities)**
8. Both are lithium and copper producers (competitors), with cross-ownership (SQM-Wesfarmers) and Australian mining services in Chile. [S]
9. FTA and CPTPP. Counter-seasonal produce. Wool genetics. [F/S]
10. Humboldt itself makes Sydney the Asia-Pacific interconnect for Chile. [F]

**D. Contingent or political (decades)**
11. Diaspora numbers, student flows, rugby, Southern Cross symbolism, Falklands politics. [F/S]

**What this means for the fiction [I]:** the most defensible durable story is not "Australia trades heavily with South America" (trade is about A$1.4bn per country, small). It is that the Pacific cable plus the Antarctic Treaty/CCAMLR/SAR system plus great-circle geometry make Australia and South America each other's nearest Southern Hemisphere hub across the Pacific. A settled Antarctica would turn the over-ice shortcut into a real corridor, with NZ's Ross Dependency and unclaimed Marie Byrd Land in the middle.

## 8. Dead ends, discrepancies and what I could not verify

- **No bilateral Antarctic agreement or joint flights** found between Australia and Chile or Argentina. Evidence is weak and the sources I checked were silent. NZ-Chile (2018) is the only real analogue.
- **No first Australia-South America radio contact** found. The 1924 record is Argentina-NZ.
- **Humboldt discrepancies:**
  - Timeline moved from 2027 to service in 2028.
  - Cost: US$450-650m (2022); up to US$650m with Google up to US$550m (2025, [S] https://www.carey.cl/en/...); the "US$11.5 million" in one 2026 article looks like an error.
