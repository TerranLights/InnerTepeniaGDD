# Research report: is there a durable real-world basis for an Australia / South America / South Africa "Southern Hemisphere triangle" around Antarctica?

Method: web search plus page and PDF fetches, with great-circle calculations run locally. Tags: **[F]** = fact stated by a source I opened. **[F-snippet]** = reported only in a search-result summary and not confirmed on the page. **[I]** = my inference. **[U]** = could not verify. No project files were edited.

Two constraints affected coverage. The shared web-search budget ran out (1000 of 1000) midway, so sea-ice services and some SAR details were finished by direct page fetches only. Several sites were blocked or timed out (see "Dead ends" at the end).

## Headline finding

The real-world structure is a ring, not a triangle. Five countries hold legal Search and Rescue (SAR) responsibility around Antarctica: Argentina, Australia, Chile, New Zealand and South Africa. Five gateway cities carry the logistics. New Zealand and Christchurch are the fourth and fifth corners.

- The strongest, longest-lasting couplings are geographic: SAR duty, medical evacuation (medevac) to a gateway hospital, Southern Hemisphere weather data scarcity, the ozone/Southern Annular Mode (SAM) climate link, and the shape of the great circles.
- The weakest couplings are direct trade among the three and the political groupings (G20, Cairns Group, IBSA). Direct trade is small. I did not find any membership group that unites all three.

## 1. Antarctic Treaty System (ATS) and the gateway cities

**Treaty institutions sit in the ring.** [F]
- The Antarctic Treaty Secretariat is in Buenos Aires. The ATS has 58 parties, 29 of them consultative. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Treaty_System
- The Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources (CCAMLR) Secretariat is at 181 Macquarie St, Hobart, with about 25 staff and 27 members. https://www.ccamlr.org/en/news/2022/international-antarctic-marine-experts-meet-hobart
- The Council of Managers of National Antarctic Programs (COMNAP) Secretariat has been in Christchurch since 2009. It was previously in Hobart (1997–2009) and Washington (1988–97). https://comnap.aq/air-operations

**What each gateway serves.** [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_gateway_cities (Wikipedia, so treat as secondary)

| Gateway | Programs served |
|---|---|
| Punta Arenas | More than 20 programs, more than any other gateway. Wikipedia describes it as the hub for West Antarctica supply (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_Arenas). |
| Ushuaia | Argentina only. Wikipedia says it takes about 90% of tourists, a figure not confirmed elsewhere. |
| Cape Town | South Africa, Russia, Germany, Belgium, Norway, Japan, with flights to Queen Maud Land (below). |
| Hobart | Australia, France, China. It has the highest concentration of Antarctic scientists. |
| Christchurch | New Zealand, United States, Italy, South Korea. |

- On 25 September 2009 delegates from the five cities signed a "statement of intent" to work together on access and protection. https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/15540/antarctica-agreement-signed It lapsed after about 2.5 years. A 2017–2020 "Antarctic Cities" project followed. [F]
- Tourism was about 124,000 cruise visitors a year by 2025, mostly to the Peninsula from South America. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Antarctica [F]
- Port Stanley has a British Antarctic Survey office and a weekly air link to Punta Arenas via Mount Pleasant, begun in 1993. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley,_Falkland_Islands [F]
- Montevideo and Buenos Aires: Uruguay runs Artigas Base on King George Island. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguayan_Antarctic_Institute I could not verify a Montevideo role as a logistics hub for other programs. [U]

**Coordination actually done between programs.**
- **Flights.** The Dronning Maud Land Air Network (DROMLAN) is a coordinated flight service from Cape Town for eleven countries. Members are Belgium, Finland, Germany, India, Japan, the Netherlands, Norway, Russia, South Africa, Sweden and the UK. Flights run up to 9 hours by Hercules or about 5.5 hours by Il-76 and serve about 12 stations. In 2000/01 Australia took part in the evaluation flights but is not a member. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DROMLAN [F]
- **Flight information.** COMNAP publishes the Antarctic Flight Information Manual (e-AFIM) twice a year. COMNAP's Asset Tracking System (CATS) and its Antarctic Telecommunications Operators Manual (ATOM, restricted) are shared with the five rescue centres. https://comnap.aq/air-operations and https://www.comnap.aq/s/SAR-Workshop-5-2023-Final-Report.pdf [F]
- **Peninsula patrol.** Argentina and Chile run a Combined Antarctic Naval Patrol (PANC) each austral summer. The 2023 workshop gives the dates as 15 November to 15 March, and the 2019 report as 30 November to 30 March (the two sources differ). They also share Marsh Aerodrome and Cospas-Sarsat alerting. https://www.comnap.aq/s/SAR-Workshop-IV-2019-Final-Report.pdf [F]
- **Medevac examples.**
  - June 2016 South Pole winter medevac: a Canadian Twin Otter (Kenn Borek Air) staged through Rothera, a British base, then went to Punta Arenas. https://www.wgbh.org/news/2016-06-22/two-patients-rescued-from-south-pole-in-dramatic-winter-evacuation [F]
  - Weather forecasts for that medevac came from a US Navy lab, with other nations assisting. Reported in a search-result summary of a US Antarctic Program page that I could not open. [F-snippet]
  - December 2013: the MV Akademik Shokalskiy rescue, coordinated by Australia's Maritime Safety Authority (AMSA) rescue centre. Ships came from Australia, China, France and the US, and a Chinese helicopter moved the 52 passengers. https://www.antarctica.gov.au/magazine/issue-32-june-2017/operations/emergency-response-in-antarctica/ [F]
  - A joint US-Australia air evacuation from Davis on 5 November 2008 and the 2023 Nuyina trip from Hobart to Casey were reported in search-result summaries only. [F-snippet]
- **Lessons from the SAR workshops.** All from the 2019 and 2023 COMNAP SAR Workshop reports:
  - The 2023 report says any evacuation of a critically injured person goes ultimately to a gateway where hospital capability is full. Gateway hospital capacity "requires continued investment."
  - It also says it is "a long-standing principle" that all programs assist each other, and that medevacs need heavy inter-agency work using National Antarctic Program assets.
  - Even the shortest Antarctic rescue distances are never under 1,000–1,200 km. Five to six sailing days is possible for some coasts.
  - Several events begin in one rescue centre's area and cross into another's, so lead-agency protocols matter.
  - Reports of the 2008 and 2009 workshops show a recommendation that the five countries consider a single regional SAR agreement. In 2009 the status was "in progress." I did not find evidence one exists. [U]

## 2. SAR regions, the Southern Ocean and distress traffic

**Who is responsible.** [F]
- Five rescue-centre operators (RCCs: Argentina, Australia, Chile, New Zealand, South Africa) share SAR coordination over the Antarctic. Argentina, Chile and South Africa each have a maritime and an aeronautical rescue centre. Australia and New Zealand each have one joint centre. https://documents.ats.aq/ATCM32/wp/Atcm32_wp047_e.doc
- Regional split in the 2019 workshop:
  - Peninsula: Argentina and Chile.
  - Dronning Maud Land / East Antarctica: South Africa and Australia.
  - Ross Sea: New Zealand.
  https://www.comnap.aq/s/SAR-Workshop-IV-2019-Final-Report.pdf The 2023 report says the East Antarctic region is covered by "two neighbouring RCCs that do not work under a joint patrol," so they must exchange plans before each season. https://www.comnap.aq/s/SAR-Workshop-5-2023-Final-Report.pdf
- Australia's region is about 53 million km², roughly one tenth of the Earth's surface. It borders the regions of 10 other countries, including South Africa and New Zealand. https://www.amsa.gov.au/safety-navigation/search-and-rescue/australias-search-and-rescue-region
- Chile's maritime rescue region is described as the "5th most extensive in the world." https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/About/Events/Documents/2025%20Polar%20Maritime%20Seminar%20-%20PP%20presentations/Session%204%20-%20Pablo%20Carrasco.pdf
- The Antarctic share is "a small proportion" of each country's total area. Most Antarctic SAR assets are not based in Antarctica, with the Peninsula the exception.
- Longitude boundaries: Chile's Antarctic sector is 53°W–90°W [F-snippet]. South Africa's Antarctic coast area is 20°W–80°E, per a snippet of an International Hydrographic Organization (IHO) national report I could not open. [F-snippet / U]
- The adjacency of South Africa with Argentina in the Atlantic sector is not confirmed. [U]

**Distress comms.** [F]
- GMDSS Sea Area A4 is the polar region beyond Inmarsat coverage (about 70–76° latitude). Ships there need HF digital selective calling (DSC) and NBDP. Iridium has been a recognized GMDSS satellite service since 2020 and removes the A4 category for Iridium-equipped ships. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Maritime_Distress_and_Safety_System
- Cospas-Sarsat's low-orbit satellites cover the poles, and its medium-orbit system (MEOSAR) now has "good coverage across Antarctic." The Chile and Argentina Mission Control Centres appear in the COMNAP contact list. https://www.comnap.aq/s/SAR-Workshop-5-2023-Final-Report.pdf
- Weather areas (METAREAs): Brazil V, Argentina VI, South Africa VII, Australia X, New Zealand XIV, Chile XV. [F-snippet] https://wmo.int/metarea-14

**Ship traffic between the three continents.** The honest picture is that most traffic passes the Cape rather than moving between the three.
- About 130 ships a day round the Cape of Good Hope, per an industry tracker cited by the Cape Chamber. The figure is unconfirmed by South Africa's maritime safety authority (SAMSA). Traffic roughly doubled during Red Sea/Hormuz diversions, and almost none of it stops at South African ports. https://capechamber.co.za/latest-news/how-many-vessels-cape-sea-route-will-it-benefit-sa-inc and https://issafrica.org/iss-today/cape-rerouting-exposes-south-africa-s-maritime-blind-spots
- Cape Horn: no regular commercial cargo route. The Panama Canal removed it, though cruise ships round the Horn. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_Passage (via search result)
- I found no usable AIS count for ships south of 40°S or for Australia-Africa-South America cargo. [U]

## 3. Weather, ocean, ozone and sea ice

- **Data scarcity.** [F] Southern Hemisphere extratropical forecast skill lags the north, most strongly at the poles. The 2018/19 Year of Polar Prediction special observing period added about 2,200 radiosondes, roughly doubling the routine program. They gave the greatest improvement for deep cyclones near the Antarctic coast. A 2022 winter period involved 24 stations run by 14 nations. https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10232070
  - The 2018/19 period also examined an atmospheric river that hit southern South America and the Peninsula. [F-snippet]
- **Antarctic Observing Network (AntON).** [F] WMO paper (March 2014): 106 surface synoptic stations, 84 of them automatic. Only 15 upper-air stations, and only 6 flew two soundings a day. Cited problems: ageing automatic stations, Argos and Iridium charges causing data loss, and declining winter performance. https://old.wmo.int/extranet/pages/prog/www/OSY/Meetings/ICT-IOS8/documents/ICT-IOS8-Doc-4(7)-Antarctic.pdf This is a real, durable reason for pooled stations and relays.
- **Antarctic Meteorological Research and Data Center (AMRDC).** [F] It is a University of Wisconsin-Madison / Madison College project that hosts Antarctic weather data, automatic weather station data from 1980, and satellite composites. It is American, not part of the triangle. https://amrdc.ssec.wisc.edu/about
- **WMO structure.** [F-snippet] Antarctic observing is overseen by the WMO polar expert panel, not a normal regional association. I did not confirm how the regional associations map onto Antarctica. [U]
- **Ozone and SAM.** [F] Ozone-depleted air spreads over southern South America, southern Australia, New Zealand and South Africa. Punta Arenas has documented UV-related skin-cancer increases. The ozone layer should return to 1980 levels around 2066 over Antarctica. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone_depletion Ozone depletion has pushed the Southern Annular Mode into an extreme positive phase, which affects Australian rainfall. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Annular_Mode (the page only covers Australia; effects on Chile, Argentina and South Africa were not in the source). [U]
- **Sea-ice forecasting.** [F] Argentina's Servicio de Hidrografía Naval was on the WMO/IOC sea-ice expert-team list (2006). https://old.wmo.int/extranet/pages/prog/amp/mmop/documents/Jcomm-Groups/spa_si.pdf
  - That list is dominated by northern services. Southern products appear to come mostly from US, Norwegian, Russian and Chinese sources, plus the international seasonal-prediction project SIPN South. https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10352832 [U on who produces operational Antarctic ice charts]
  - I found no evidence that Australia, Chile and South Africa jointly produce sea-ice forecasts. [U]
- **Space weather / HF forecasting.** [F] South Africa's SANSA hosts Africa's only ISES Regional Warning Centre. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Space_Agency Australia's Bureau of Meteorology also produces HF ionosphere charts. https://www.sws.bom.gov.au/Educational/1/3/2

## 4. Southern Hemisphere trade and shipping geometry

- **Great Capes / clipper route.** [F] Cape of Good Hope, Cape Leeuwin and Cape Horn mark the traditional route. Ships ran west to east in the Roaring Forties, mostly at 40–45°S, carrying wool, grain and gold. The route died with steam, Suez and Panama. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clipper_route and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_capes
  - The last cargo grain race was 1949: Passat and Pamir from Port Victoria, South Australia, round the Horn. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passat_(ship)
  - **[I]** That route was Europe-centered. It linked Europe to Australia, not Africa to South America, so it is a poor basis for a modern triangle.
- **Modern Australia-South Africa trade.** [F-snippet, Australia's trade department (DFAT) fact sheet] 2024 goods and services: exports about A$2.29 bn (aluminium ores A$978 m, wheat A$190 m, coal A$153 m) and imports about A$2.69 bn (gold, vehicles). https://www.dfat.gov.au/sites/default/files/safr-cef.pdf (I could not open this directly.)
- **Australia-Chile and Australia-Argentina.** Both are very small.
  - Australia-Chile 2016: about A$1.4 bn. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Chile_relations
  - DFAT 2024 items for Chile and Argentina are in the tens to hundreds of millions of A$ each. [F-snippet]
- **South Africa and Mercosur.** The Southern African Customs Union (SACU)–Mercosur preferential trade agreement entered into force on 1 April 2016. South African exports to Mercosur were only about R193 million in 2020. [F-snippet] https://www.namc.co.za/south-africas-way-ahead-shall-we-samba/
- **Iron ore.** Capesize ships carry iron ore from Brazil to China and from Western Australia to China. [F-snippet] **[I]** Neither route connects the three continents to each other. Brazil-China goes via the Cape and the Sunda/Lombok straits.
- **Counter-seasonal harvests.** [F] Southern Hemisphere wheat and fruit reach northern markets in the northern off-season. Chile cites this as a "main comparative advantage." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Chile Australian wheat harvest runs October to February. [F-snippet]
- **Humboldt Cable.** [F] A 14,800 km Valparaíso–Sydney subsea cable via French Polynesia, owned 50/50 by Google and Chile's Desarrollo País, with service expected in 2027. It does not go through Antarctica. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Cable
- **Waypoints in a settled-Antarctica world.** **[I]** Cape Town, Fremantle/Perth, Hobart, Christchurch, Punta Arenas, Ushuaia and Port Stanley are the natural calling points, because they are the existing gateways. Their roles as bunkering, repair and medevac stops are inference, not documented trade flows.

## 5. Science, radio astronomy and groupings

- **Square Kilometre Array.** [F] Dual-site: SKA-Low in Murchison, Western Australia, and SKA-Mid in the Karoo, South Africa. Construction began in December 2022 and the headquarters is at Jodrell Bank in the UK. Australia and South Africa are founding members. First science is expected 2027–2032. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_Kilometre_Array **[I]** This is the single strongest long-lived Australia-South Africa tie.
- **Chile.** ALMA and ESO's Paranal are in Chile. Australia has a 10-year ESO strategic partnership and takes part in the Giant Magellan Telescope. https://labonline.com.au/content/lab-business/news/australia-enters-strategic-partnership-with-eso-1161260370 The ALMA article shows no Australian or South African role. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacama_Large_Millimeter_Array
- **Groupings.**
  - G20: Australia, Argentina, Brazil and South Africa are members. Chile is not. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20
  - Cairns Group: Australia, Argentina, Brazil, Chile and South Africa are all members. It was founded in 1986. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairns_Group
  - IBSA (India, Brazil, South Africa) and BRICS: no Australia, no Chile. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBSA_Dialogue_Forum
  - Indian Ocean Rim Association: Australia and South Africa are founding members. https://www.dfat.gov.au/international-relations/regional-architecture/indian-ocean/indian-ocean-rim-association
  - South Atlantic Peace and Cooperation Zone (ZOPACAS): Argentina, Brazil, Uruguay and South Africa, but not Australia or Chile. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Peace_and_Cooperation_Zone
  - **[I]** The Cairns Group is the only formal body spanning the three. It is a trade-bargaining group, not an operational one.
  - Argentina's own Antarctic cooperation list names Chile, Brazil, Uruguay, Peru, Colombia, Germany, the US and others, but not Australia or South Africa. https://cancilleria.gob.ar/en/international-cooperation-antarctica-context-south-atlantic

## 6. HF radio and great-circle geometry

**Documented HF facts.**
- **Polar paths are disturbed.**
  - HF links near the poles suffer blackouts from sudden ionospheric disturbances and polar-cap absorption events lasting days.
  - A two-week HF sounding between Christchurch and McMurdo in 1992 was done in a "trans-auroral environment." [F-snippet]
  - Australia's Antarctic HF history records "total blackouts" and heavy interference. HF stayed in use for field parties, ships and aircraft. https://www.antarctica.gov.au/magazine/issue-2-spring-2001/feature/australia-continues-as-telecommunications-innovator/ [F]
- **Super-long HF paths.**
  - A Ukrainian team recorded Moscow and Ottawa time signals at Vernadsky Station on the Antarctic Peninsula since 2010. They saw direct and reverse great-circle paths and also paths off the great circle, via the solar terminator and auroral ovals. https://www.ursi.org/proceedings/procAP19/papers2019/URSIAPRASC2019ExtendedAbstractZalizovskiLongProp.pdf [F]
- **Cape Town to Australia.**
  - A hobbyist at Fish Hoek near Cape Town (Gary Deacon), aiming his antenna at 120 degrees (toward Perth), heard Western Australian medium-wave AM stations on a mostly all-water Indian Ocean path. Reception from beyond about 140 degrees was hard, which he blamed on polar absorption (southeastern Australia and New Zealand). https://dxing.info/dxpeditions/fishhoek_2006_03.php [F]
  - That is a medium-wave DX log, not a documented HF two-way contact. I found no documented Perth-Cape Town or Australia-South America HF two-way contact record. [U]
- **Radio-quiet zones.** [F] Murchison's zone has a 70 km inner zone, a 150 km outer zone and coordination zones to 260 km. It protects about 70 MHz to 25.25 GHz. https://www.atnf.csiro.au/facilities/askap-radio-telescope/arqzwa/ The Karoo's protection starts at about 100 MHz, because Australia was awarded the low-frequency array. https://www.icasa.org.za/uploads/files/SKA_200313.pdf **[I]** Both zones lie above HF (3–30 MHz), so they do not directly restrict HF links. HF band restrictions for radio astronomy come from separate International Telecommunication Union allocations that I did not look up. [U]
- **Karoo law.** The Astronomy Geographic Advantage Act's ground-based protection has a gap for satellite emissions entering the zone. https://techcentral.co.za/the-hole-in-the-law-above-the-karoo/285369/ [F]

**How a polar relay would sit.** **[I]**
- The shortest Australia–South America links (Perth to Santiago, Buenos Aires or Punta Arenas) pass directly over East Antarctica and near the Pole. An Antarctic relay sits on the great circle itself, not off to the side.
- An Antarctic relay does not lie on the Perth–Cape Town or Cape Town–South America great circles, which stay at 41–56°S. Those links need only Southern Ocean or island relays.
- Eastern Australia–South America (Sydney, Melbourne, Hobart) runs along the Antarctic coast at 62–75°S, so a coastal Antarctic relay (Casey, Rothera, a Ross Sea site) would be on-route.

## Bottom line: reasons ranked by durability

1. **Legal and physical division of Antarctic SAR among five rescue centres.** Top rank. It rests on geography, ocean area and distance, and has been stable since before the 1990s. Medevac always ends at a gateway hospital. The SAR workshops treat this as a permanent requirement.
2. **Gateway city logistics.** Very high. Distance, ice and port geography do not change. The Antarctic Treaty Secretariat, CCAMLR and COMNAP are physically located in Buenos Aires, Hobart and Christchurch.
3. **The great-circle geometry.** High. The Australia–South America link crosses or runs along the Antarctic coast, while the Cape Town links stay ocean-bound. This is a purely physical constraint.
4. **Southern Hemisphere weather-data scarcity and the ozone/SAM link.** High while the Southern Ocean stays data-poor, since Antarctic stations are the only land observation there. The ozone/SAM link affects all three (documented for Australia and for Punta Arenas).
5. **HF ionospheric physics and space-weather forecasting.** Medium-high. Polar absorption makes Antarctic HF unreliable, which creates demand for relays and forecasting. SANSA and Australia's space-weather service already supply forecasts.
6. **SKA and ESO/GMT.** Medium. Multi-decade instruments with a legal footprint (Karoo and Murchison quiet zones), but Chile's ALMA has no Australian or South African role.
7. **Counter-seasonal agriculture and the Cairns Group.** Low-medium. Real, but Cairns is a trade lobby and counter-seasonality connects the south to the north, not the three to each other.
8. **Direct trade, shipping and BRICS/G20/IBSA politics.** Lowest. Direct trade is small (Australia–South Africa about A$5 bn, Australia–Chile about A$1.4 bn in 2016) and the political groupings are mutable and incomplete.

## What a worldbuilder should take from this

- Model the ring, not a triangle. Five SAR operators (Argentina, Australia, Chile, New Zealand, South Africa) and five gateways is the documented structure. A Christchurch/Ross Sea node is justified.
- The reason these nodes talk is emergency response and logistics, not commerce. A settled Antarctica makes this stronger, since every settlement still needs a gateway hospital, a rescue centre and a flight corridor.
- The trans-Antarctic link is real only for Australia–South America. Put polar relay or "meta-node" capacity on the Casey–South Pole–Weddell/Peninsula corridor, with Concordia and the Pole as near-route sites. Perth–Cape Town is an Indian Ocean link.
- A sensible HF split: Indian Ocean (Perth–Cape Town) and Atlantic (Cape Town–Buenos Aires/Santiago) circuits are ordinary Southern Ocean HF. Perth/Hobart–South America needs polar paths or relays, with blackouts and flutter fading as a standing drawback.
- An Australian-dominated radio culture is plausible from the existing structure. Australia's rescue region is the largest on Earth, Hobart hosts CCAMLR, and Australia has long Antarctic HF experience. I found no source stating Australian dominance of Southern Hemisphere HF culture. [U]
- Do not make ongoing bulk trade among the three the glue. If you want trade, use Cape/Leeuwin/Horn waypoints and refuel/repair stops, flagged as invented.
- Radio-quiet zones do not conflict with HF. The two astronomy zones protect roughly 70–100 MHz upward.
- The 5–6 days of ship transit and the 1,000+ km minimum distances are good scale anchors for response times.
