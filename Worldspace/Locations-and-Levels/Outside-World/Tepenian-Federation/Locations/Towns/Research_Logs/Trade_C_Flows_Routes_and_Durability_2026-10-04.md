# Australia and South America / South Africa: commodity flows, shipping geometry, and which links are structural (research report)

Question: does Australia have an interest in, or benefit from, communicating with South America and/or South Africa? This slice tests the commodity, shipping and durability side. Background files read first and not repeated: `Why_Australia_Links_South_America_2026-10-04.md`, `Why_Australia_Links_South_Africa_2026-10-04.md`, `Southern_Hemisphere_Ring_Australia_SouthAmerica_SouthAfrica_2026-10-04.md`.

## 0. Bottom line in eight sentences

1. On goods, Australia and its South American and South African peers mostly **compete for the same Asian buyers on parallel lanes that never cross each other**: iron ore (Australia 55.8% of seaborne vs Brazil 22.2%), lithium, wheat (Australia vs Argentina), beef, wine, seeds, cherries. [F] (sources in section 1)
2. The real two-way ties are **corporate and technical, not national**: BHP and Rio Tinto run the world's largest copper mine in Chile; a South African gold company draws 44% of its output from Australia, 16% from Chile and 7% from Peru; SQM (Chile) co-owns a Western Australian lithium mine; Albemarle and Tianqi sit on both sides. [F]
3. The sharp "competitors as much as partners" claim is **true for lithium, false for copper**. Lithium: Australia 31.7% and Chile 19.3% of 2025 world mine output, and the same owners straddle both. Copper: Chile mines 7.3 times Australia's output, so there is no rivalry to speak of. [F][C]
4. The shipping geometry is a **real but unused asset**: Valparaiso to Sydney is about 6,400 nautical miles direct across the Pacific, yet the liner service routes the cargo through Panama (30 to 33 days) because carriers run hub networks. A major Australian embassy page itself names the lack of direct shipping as a barrier. [C][F]
5. Antarctica adds **institutions, not operations**: the Treaty Secretariat sits in Buenos Aires, CCAMLR in Hobart, five gateway cities, shared search-and-rescue duties, and enforcement against illegal toothfish fishing that has already put Australian and South African ships on one chase. No bilateral Australia-Chile or Australia-Argentina Antarctic operational agreement was found. [F]
6. The South Africa link is **heavier on goods than the South America link** in one chain: Western Australian alumina feeds a South African smelter, and Australia is South Africa's largest wheat supplier (28% of 2024/25 imports by one source). Both chains are contingent on specific companies and prices. [F-snippet]
7. One three-continent pattern has repeated for 230 years and is the best "recurring" evidence: **sheep genetics move among Cape, Australia and South America** (Cape merinos to New South Wales 1797; Australian-bred Peppin merino into South Africa's Dohne breed; Dohne embryos back to Australia 1998; Australian genetics to South America since 2002 because South American sanitary rules block South African stock). [F][F-snippet]
8. Ranked by durability, the **structural** links are geology, ocean geometry and the Southern Ocean regime; the **contingent** ones are every price-, company-, FTA- or cable-based link. Section 6 gives the ranking with reasons.

### 1.2 Copper: not rivals; partners through two companies [F]

- Chile mined 5,300 kt in 2025e against Australia's 730 kt (ratio 7.3; refined 1,700 vs 460 kt, ratio 3.7). Peru 2,700 kt. [F, USGS]
- Escondida, the world's largest copper mine (capacity about 1.4 Mt/yr; 1,277,500 t in 2024), is owned BHP 57.5%, Rio Tinto 30%, JECO 10%, JECO 2 2.5% [F, Wikipedia Escondida, WebFetch summary]. It has been Chile's top mine every year since 1996 and was estimated to generate 2.5% of Chile's GDP in 2019 [F, same page].
- BHP FY2025: group copper 2,017 kt; Copper South Australia 316 kt; Spence (Chile) record 268 kt; Antamina (Peru) 119 kt; Escondida record in 17 years [F-snippet] https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/811809/000119312525183071/d35528d6k.htm and https://minersnews.net/bhp-reports-historic-record-in-copper-production-escondida-was-key/. **By subtraction, roughly 84% of BHP's copper in FY2025 came from outside Australia, nearly all in Chile and Peru** [C; the remaining 1,314 kt after the three named non-Escondida assets is mostly Escondida]. BHP is Melbourne-headquartered [U, not re-verified]; Rio Tinto is a London and Melbourne dual-listed group [U].
- Rio Tinto's 2025 equity copper: 883 kt, of which 348 kt in concentrate and 56 kt refined at Escondida [F-snippet] https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2778240-rio-tinto-raises-global-copper-output-in-2025.
- Australian METS exposure: BHP's Escondida contracts to about 80 Australian firms [S, from the prior report]. The Austrade piece I opened says Chile makes 24% of world copper and the Antofagasta region 54% of Chile's copper and 100% of its lithium [F] https://www.austrade.gov.au/en/news-and-analysis/news/australian-mets-companies-build-momentum-in-latin-america.

**Verdict on the user's claim "lithium and copper make Australia and Chile competitors as much as partners":** lithium, yes, and the partners are the same companies; copper, no. At 3.2% of world copper, Australia is not a rival to Chile (23%); the relationship is capital and services moving from Australia to Chile. [C][I]

### 1.5 Fertilizer: a null result that matters [F]

- DAP/MAP about 1 Mt (85% import share), about 30% from Saudi Arabia in 2025; replacement sources in the shock were Bahrain, Morocco, the US and Trinidad and Tobago [F-snippet] (same search result set). ### 1.6 Wheat: Argentina competes, South Africa complements, and a historical reversal [F]

- 2025/26 forecast, USDA ERS March 2026: global wheat exports 221.6 Mt; **Argentina up 8.6 Mt to a record about 19.5 Mt (record crop 27.8 Mt); Australia up 5.7 Mt to about 27 Mt**; Ukraine the only major exporter down [F, WHS-26c https://ers.usda.gov/sites/default/files/_laserfiche/outlooks/113938/WHS-26c.pdf ; absolute figures 19.5 and 27 Mt from the search summary of the same series, F-snippet]. Large Argentine purchases are key to the Vietnam and Bangladesh forecasts, the same buyer set Australia sells to. **Compete.**
- Chile imports about 1.45 Mt of wheat in 2024-25, mainly Canada and the US with Argentina third after a 2022-23 drought cut Argentine shipments about 53% [F-snippet] https://millingmea.com/chiles-wheat-production-continues-to-decline-usda/ and the cropgpt/OEC results. Australia is not a main supplier; Chile belongs to Argentina's neighborhood.
- South Africa, 2024/25 wheat imports (SAGIS, summary): Australia 443,863 t (28%), Russia 397,557 t (25%), Lithuania 194,149 t (12%), Canada 135,876 t (9%), US 107,884 t (7%); total about 2 Mt [F-snippet; the SAGIS PDF itself is a chart I could not read as text] https://www.grainsa.co.za/upload/report_files/20250911_SAGIS---Koring-Week-in--exports-in--uitvoere-2024---2025.pdf. **Complement**, but Russia is at 25% and swaps place with Australia by price, so it is contingent.
- The reverse flow in the 19th century: Chilean wheat and flour went to Australia; more than 403,000 bushels in 1840-42; Australia imported grain in 21 of 30 years 1830-60; the trade died with Australian agricultural development, the gold rush and steam navigation [F] https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-latin-american-studies/article/before-the-gold-fleets-trade-and-relations-between-chile-and-australia-18301848/3C298BEF004107DD92A758A353FE4DE9 . **Strongest single proof that a food-flow link between these places is contingent on development stage, since it vanished in one generation.**

### 1.8 Genetics: a three-continent triangle that keeps recurring [F / F-snippet]

- 1797: merinos bought at the Cape reached New South Wales (26 sheep via Waterhouse and Kent); the Cape flock itself came from a Spanish-origin flock the Dutch house of Orange moved in 1789 [F, from the South Africa prior report].
- 1956: Uruguay became the first South American importer of Australian merinos [F, from the South America prior report].
- 1939: the Dohne Merino breeding program began in South Africa's Eastern Cape from a Peppin-type merino (an Australian-origin strain) crossed with the German Mutton Merino; breed society formed 1966 [F-snippet] https://dohne.com.au/?p=2554 and search summary on dohne.com.au.
- 1998: Dohne reached Australia as frozen embryos into Western Australia; Australian breed society 2000 [F-snippet].
- From about 2002: Australian Dohne and merino genetics (embryos, semen, live animals) go to Chile, Argentina and Uruguay; the stated reason is that **Uruguay, Argentina and Chile are not allowed to import genetics from South Africa for sanitary reasons**, which made Australia the regional source [F-snippet] https://dohne.com.au/australian-dohne-merino-sheep-genetics-excelling-in-south-america/ (403 on direct fetch). An Australian embassy page confirms embryos and semen exported to Chile and Argentina and a MRM joint-venture network in South America of over 800,000 sheep, 15 multiplying studs, 90 breeders in Chile, Argentina and Uruguay; Chile's flock 2.5 million with a goal to double [F] https://chile.embassy.gov.au/sclecastellano/Hola3art5E.html (undated).
- **Reading:** the triangle runs on a biosecurity rule (South American bans on South African stock), so Australia is the broker. That is contingent on the rule and on sheep farming itself, but the repeating pattern (1797, 1956, 1998, 2002) across 230 years is evidence of a standing logic: temperate southern pastoral regions swap breeding stock among themselves because the northern pools are in the wrong season and climate. [I]

## 2. Shipping geometry and times

### 2.1 Method and caveat

Distances below are my computation with the open-source `searoute` Python package (a marine-network shortest-path router built on a Eurostat-style network), run in a sandbox virtualenv [C]. Days assume 14 knots (bulk) and 20 knots (fast container), continuous steaming, no port time or weather.

### 2.4 Panama Canal vs Cape Horn vs the Cape (context)

- Panama's 2023-24 drought cut transits from 31 booking slots/day to 25 (6 Nov 2023) to 22 (Dec 2023/Jan 2024) to 18/day from 1 Feb 2024, with draft cut from 50 ft to 44 ft [F-snippet] https://www.waterwaysjournal.net/2023/11/15/panama-canal-restrictions-extended-deepened/ ; https://maritime-executive.com/article/panama-canal-plans-to-cut-transits-by-40-due-to-ongoing-drought . Every Oceania-Atlantic America and Chile-Oceania liner connection that uses Panama is exposed to this; a direct Pacific lane would not be.
- Cape Horn and the Strait of Magellan: crossing the Drake Passage east of Isla de los Estados is about 765 nm vs 390 nm through the Strait, per a Chilean maritime authority page [F-snippet] https://www.directemar.cl/directemar/route-description .
- Cape of Good Hope: rerouting around the Cape adds about 10 days (about 3,000 nm) for Asia-Europe voyages when the Red Sea is closed; a June 2026 edit of the Cape Route page flags the ongoing Red Sea and Hormuz disruption (from the South Africa prior report).
- **Sailing-era comparison (context only):** Australian wool clippers returned east round Cape Horn along the Southern Ocean westerlies, between 40S and 60S; Sydney to Plymouth about 100 days for a good run, Cutty Sark 84 days, Thermopylae 77 days, Lightning 65 days Melbourne to Liverpool in 1854-55; the route stayed commercially viable into the 1930s with the last documented voyage Moshulu in 1938 [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clipper_route . The line Australia, Cape Horn, Atlantic is the oldest recorded regular **Australia-South America contact**; it was a transit corridor, not a trade, because ships stopped at the Atlantic side only for the wind.

### 3.1 What exists (institutions, no operations) [F]

### 3.2 Fisheries enforcement: the one real operational overlap [F / F-snippet]

- **Viarsa 1 (2003):** Australia's Southern Supporter chased an illegal toothfish vessel from Heard Island for 3,900 nm over 21 days; the South African icebreaker SA Agulhas and the salvage tug John Ross with armed South African officers joined; boarding about 2,000 nm south-west of Cape Town on 28 August 2003; the vessel went to Fremantle (from the South Africa prior report). **Cape Town and Fremantle acted as the two ends of one Southern Ocean enforcement theater.**
- **Operation Icefish (2014-15):** Sea Shepherd's Bob Barker and Sam Simon (departed Australia and New Zealand, December 2014) chased the Thunder, a CCAMLR black-listed vessel with an Interpol Purple Notice issued after a joint effort by New Zealand, Australia and Norway; a 110-day pursuit; "six countries" in the success [F-snippet] https://www.seashepherd.org.nz/latest-news/sea_shepherd_intercepts_toothfish_poachers/ ; https://colto.org/?p=2938 . Chile was not named in the sources I read.
- **Trade side (CCAMLR CDS 2023):** France landed the most toothfish (4,685 t) and **Chile exported the most (3,857 t)**; the US took the most imports, then China; about 98% of imports went to Contracting or cooperating parties [F] https://meetings.ccamlr.org/en/node/32048 . So **Chile and Australia sell the same fish into the same US and Chinese markets and enforce the same catch-documentation scheme.** Compete in the market; cooperate in enforcement. [I]
- Krill: 498,350 t caught in 2023-24 (record) by mostly Norwegian and Chinese trawlers; 2024-25 catch reached the trigger in Area 48 and the fishery closed 1 August 2026 for the first time [F-snippet] https://news.mongabay.com/short-article/2025/08/krill-fishery-in-antarctica-shut-down-after-record-catch-triggers-unprecedented-early-closure/ . Australia and Chile are not significant krill flags in what I read, so krill logistics do not connect them. [I]

## 6. Structural vs contingent: classification and ranking

Definitions. **STRUCTURAL**: would plausibly persist for centuries because it rests on geography, seasons, ocean geometry, geology or the permanent consequences of the Southern Ocean. **CONTINGENT**: rests on a commodity cycle, a company, a treaty, a regime, a price or a technology. **RECURRING**: contingent in each instance but the pattern keeps coming back.

### 6.2 What the ranking says about the user's question

- **Australia's interest in communicating with South America and South Africa is real but it is not primarily goods trade.** The goods link is mostly rivalry on parallel lanes. [I]
- The durable interest comes from: (a) a **shared sea** that no one owns and everyone crosses; (b) **mineral provinces that keep needing the same skills**; (c) **a shared adversary and regime** in the form of illegal fishing and the Treaty system; (d) **a shared market position** against Northern Hemisphere buyers.
- The contingent interest is **corporate**: a few firms hold both sides of lithium, copper, gold and aluminum, and they will carry Australian-Chilean, Australian-Peruvian and Australian-South African information as long as they exist.
- **South Africa has the more mutual commodity structure** (alumina, wheat, gold capital, manganese company, SKA) while **South America has the more mutual technical structure** (copper, block caving, Chilean-Australian education). [I]
