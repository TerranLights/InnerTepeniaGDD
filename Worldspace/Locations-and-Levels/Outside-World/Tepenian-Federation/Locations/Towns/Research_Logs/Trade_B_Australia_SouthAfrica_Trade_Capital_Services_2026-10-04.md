# Australia - South Africa: trade, investment, mining capital, services (Trade B)

Question carried from the project: does Australia have an interest or benefit in communicating (directly or indirectly) with South Africa? This slice covers **trade, investment, mining capital, services, shipping/air/data routes** and ranks each link **STRUCTURAL vs CONTINGENT** (the "Fact vs. Driver" test).

**Tag key.** [F] fact from a source I opened (URL + year) · [F-archive] opened through a Wayback copy because the live site blocked or timed out · [F-snippet] seen only in a search-result summary, not opened · [CALC] my arithmetic, method stated · [I] my inference · [U] unverified.

**Money units.** A$ = Australian dollars (DFAT/ABS series). US$ = SA-side or UN Comtrade/WITS series. Do not add A$ and US$ columns. Calendar year (CY) vs financial year (FY, July-June) is marked.

## 0. Headline findings (read this first)

1. **The trade is small and flat in real terms; it is not what links the two places.** DFAT-adjusted ABS data: two-way goods and services **A$5.16 bn in CY2025** (exports A$2.41 bn, imports A$2.75 bn), **A$4.98 bn in CY2024**; South Africa ranks **28th as an Australian export destination and 31st as an import source (2025)**; Australia is **0.9% of South Africa's exports (rank 21) and 1.2% of its imports (rank 20)**. The 2008 peak was A$5.1 bn nominal; the whole 2010-2021 range was A$2.7-4.0 bn. [F] (sources in section 2)
2. **One corporate chain is half of what Australia ships.** Alumina (aluminum ores & concentrates) was **A$914 m = 38%** of A$2.41 bn exports in 2025; wheat **A$314 m = 13%**. All of the alumina leaves Western Australia (WA's own profile shows A$914 m alumina to South Africa and A$726 m to Mozambique, 2025). It feeds South32's Hillside smelter (Richards Bay) and Mozal (Mozambique). [F] That chain is being sold to Alcoa (announced July 2026). [F-archive]
3. **Capital runs mostly the other way.** South African investment in Australia (**A$10.1 bn stock, 2024; A$14 bn, 2025**) is roughly **double** Australian investment in South Africa (**A$5.5 bn, 2024; ~A$6 bn, 2025**). DFAT's own Annex (Dec 2025) calls Australian investment in South Africa "**legacy investment**." Gold Fields now gets **44% of attributable production from Australia and 12% from South Africa** (FY2025). [F]
4. **The "ASX companies are the largest investor in South Africa's mining sector" line (DFAT 2020 fact sheet) is not reconcilable with ABS investment stock and I found no primary source for it.** It is best read as a count-of-listed-companies/projects statement from the 2013-2015 era, not a value ranking. Reconciliation in section 4. [F for the numbers; I for the reading]
5. **"Gateway to southern Africa" is weakly supported by trade data and moderately supported by institutions.** SADC neighbors (ex-South Africa) were only **A$1.24 bn of two-way trade in 2021** against A$3.14 bn for South Africa. Institutionally, Australia's Pretoria mission covers South Africa, Angola, Botswana, eSwatini, Lesotho, Mozambique and Namibia, and Johannesburg is the only African city with direct air service to Australia. Australian miners operate in Botswana, Namibia, Tanzania and Zambia directly from Perth, not through South Africa. [F / I]
6. **The strongest non-commercial structural links** are (a) the 6-hour, no-daylight-saving time offset (3 hours of shared 08:00-17:00 business time), (b) the only direct Australia-Africa air route (Perth-Johannesburg, relaunched Dec 2025), (c) the Square Kilometer Array's twin processing centers, whose own network design **expects "significant traffic between AU & ZA" but routes it via Singapore and London because no direct Indian Ocean research link exists yet**. [F]
7. **The Grok summary ("smaller market but a resources peer and a gateway to southern Africa") is two-thirds right.** Smaller market: yes. Resources peer: yes, but mostly competitors for the same buyers, with capital and skills moving between them. Gateway: institutional, not trade-flow. Section 8.

- Shares [CALC]: alumina 38% and wheat 13% of exports; gold 20%, vehicles 26% (goods + passenger), travel 16% of imports.
- **Structure, 2023 (WITS, US$m) [F]:** Australia's exports to South Africa: intermediate goods 514 of 1,101 (47%), chemicals 490, fuels 180 (coal), capital goods 177, vegetable products 125. Imports from South Africa: manufactures 955 of 1,117 (85%), of which transport equipment 551 and capital goods 472. So the pattern is **raw/intermediate out, finished vehicles in.**
- **SA-side view, 2024 (Wesgro/Quantec) [F]:** South Africa's imports from Australia: artificial corundum (alumina) US$656 m (54%), wheat US$129 m, coal US$127 m, barley US$13 m. Exports to Australia: goods-transport vehicles US$310 m (29%), passenger cars US$144 m, ore-processing machinery US$40 m.
- **2018-2020 pattern for comparison [F]:** exports led by aluminum ores (A$920 m FY2018-19; A$576 m FY2019-20; A$304 m CY2020), coal (A$266 m; A$201 m; A$146 m). Coal has since dropped out of the DFAT top-six list (A$153 m in 2024 per the earlier agents' snippet; absent in 2025). Gold appears in imports only from 2024-25 [F; the Perth Mint link is [U], section 9].
- Agriculture, 2024 [F, DFAT-Sub20]: Australian agricultural, fisheries and forestry exports to South Africa A$301 m (grains A$211.8 m, meat A$27 m); imports from South Africa A$155.9 m. Seed for sowing exports to South Africa A$7.17 m since 2021 (Australian Seed Federation, Sub 7) [F].
- **Wheat [F, SAGIS 11 Sep 2025]:** Australia supplied **443,863 t = 28%** of South Africa's 2024/25 wheat imports (Russia 25%, Lithuania 12%, Canada 9%). South Africa's own wheat exports that season went mostly to neighbors: Botswana 50%, Zambia 20%, Zimbabwe 16% (SAGIS chart). **[I]** Australian wheat into South Africa is partly a regional supply for the neighbors.

## 4. Reconciliation: "ASX-listed companies are the largest investor in South Africa's mining sector"

**The claim.** HC-Sep20 (Sept 2020, DFAT-origin) states: "ASX listed companies are the largest investor in South Africa's mining sector (followed by Canada, UK)." The same sheet states total Australian investment in South Africa was A$5.9 bn (2019), FDI A$0.9 bn, and Australia was the 7th-largest foreign investor in 2016. Earlier agents repeated it. [F]

**What the numbers say.**
- Australian-recorded investment in South Africa: A$4.6-7.3 bn (2013-2024), A$5.5 bn in 2024; FDI only A$0.6-1.3 bn. All sectors. [F]
- South African capital in Australia is larger and rising: A$10.1 bn (2024), A$14 bn (2025). [F]
- DFAT's Dec 2025 submission: "**much Australian investment in South Africa is legacy investment**, with chronic power and logistics failures, crime and corruption impeding growth." It lists a handful of operators. [F]

**How the two can both be true (my reading).**
1. **Different yardsticks [I].** The 2020 sentence compares *ASX-listed companies as a group* against foreign investors in the *mining sector only*; the ABS stock is by *Australian resident entity and immediate counterparty country* across all sectors. ASX listing is not Australian ownership (global holders), and an Australian subsidiary may hold South African assets through a third-country holding company, which the ABS would record under that country.
2. **Count vs. value [I].** The era that fits the wording is 2013-2015, when ASX led all exchanges in African-asset *company counts* (218 vs 192 TSX, 54 JSE) and 20% of ASX-held African mines were platinum, 27% coal (Aquarius, Coal of Africa, etc.). Counts of companies and projects do not translate to dollars invested.
3. **Original source not found [U].** I could not locate the table behind "followed by Canada, UK." Searches returned only the ASX 2013 company-count brochure and the same sentence repeated.
4. **Direction has since reversed in the biggest deals [F].** 2022-2026: Harmony (Eva, CSA), Gold Fields (Gold Road), Sibanye (Century), Pan African (Tennant Creek, ASX listing) and Exxaro (stake in Jupiter/Tshipi) are South African firms buying into Australian or ASX-listed assets. Australian-side moves in South Africa in the same window: West Wits' new mine (Dec 2025), Rio's Zulti South (US$350 m), and the Alcoa sale of the South32 assets (an exit of an Australian firm from Hillside, to a US firm).

**Verdict for fiction/worldbuilding.** Say "Australian-listed miners are a major *presence* in South African mining" (fact for the named operators), not "the largest investor." The accurate, defensible pair is: Australia is a long-standing but now mostly legacy and shrinking investor in South Africa; South Africa is a rising and larger investor in Australia.

### 6c. Time zones and business-hours overlap [F tzdata; CALC]

### 6d. Data: the Square Kilometer Array and the missing direct link
- **SKA-Mid (Karoo, South Africa)** to its processing center in **Cape Town**: 737 km of fiber, **20 Tbit/s** long haul; SKA-Low (Murchison, WA) to **Perth**: 820 km, **9 Tbit/s** (older figures 8 Tbit/s). Karoo-Cape Town dark fiber (Liquid Telecom) is operational; further links under contract (SKA-NW-2025) [F]. The Australian link was built by AARNet [F]. Each telescope produces ~300 PB a year of data products; AA4 design rates into regional centers ~100 Gbit/s each for the UK, South Africa and Australia [F, SKA-NW-2024].
- **The design routes data between the two countries the long way.** "Five flows on the submarine cable from Perth to Singapore... then join the general purpose routed IP academic network" and "Five flows on the submarine cable from Cape Town to London"; "Different submarine cables used to reach India and Australia." Telescope-to-regional-center and center-to-center transfers run on peered VRFs over the shared academic network. The same deck notes **"Expect significant traffic between AU & ZA"** (AA*, ~30 Gbit/s into the UK/ZA/AU centers) [F, SKA-NW-2024].
- SANReN (7 May 2025) lists its international capacity as WACS 110 Gbps, SAT-3 20, SACS 100, EASSy 20, SEACOM 110, Equiano (not activated) = **360 Gbps total**; **"Future possibility: Umoja cable: capacity on portion from South Africa to Australia - dependent on business case, capacity requirements, funding"** [F, SANReN-2025]. Umoja (Google, Liquid): terrestrial route Kenya to South Africa built; subsea Indian Ocean leg to Perth "underway," no confirmed date [F-snippet].
- **[I] / [CALC]:** a direct Perth-Cape Town fiber would be ~8,700 km great circle, say 10,500-11,300 km of cable at 1.2-1.3x, a round trip near 105-115 ms at 5 microseconds per km. Routing via London and Singapore is several times longer (distances for the cable chain not sourced). Right now the two largest radio-astronomy facilities in the Southern Hemisphere do not have their own shared path, which is a **structural gap that a settled-Antarctica scenario could fill.**

### Fact vs. Driver, the three-why chains on the largest numbers
1. **Fact:** South Africa is Australia's largest African partner (39% of A$12.7 bn). **Why:** it is the only large industrial African economy with Australian-listed assets and the only direct air link. **Why that:** mining capital and corporate integration (C1-C3), not consumer demand. **So:** the relationship is *company-shaped*: if 3-4 corporations change course, the numbers move. It is not a state-to-state or market-pull link.
2. **Fact:** alumina is 38% of exports. **Why:** one integrated owner supplies its own smelter. **Why:** Hillside has no domestic alumina source [I]. **So:** it forces a regular Indian Ocean bulk line and a Bunbury-Richards Bay pairing, the *most durable physical flow* even if the owner changes, but its cargo value depends on Hillside staying open.
3. **Fact:** SA investment in Australia is twice Australia's in SA. **Why:** South African miners and retailers hedge home risk by buying Australian assets (DFAT: "capital exports reflect a desire to diversify and reduce exposure to the local market"). **So:** Australia gains a stake-holder in South African political and energy conditions that did not exist 10 years ago. The *reverse* motivation (Australian capital seeking South Africa) is weaker.
