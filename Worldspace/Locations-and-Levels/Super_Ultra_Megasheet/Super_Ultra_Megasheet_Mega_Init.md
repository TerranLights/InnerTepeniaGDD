## Who Lives Here, and Why

Tepenia's population is not one people with one migration story — it's the accumulated residue of at least six distinct demographic mechanisms, no two regions relying on the same one. Mirny subnet alone documents five (organic immigration, deliberate curated draw, demographic-redistribution accident, doubled dual founding, diplomatic engineering); Mawson subnet adds a sixth register (total founding-population succession, at Dome Fuji, unconnected to any of Mirny's five); Palmer subnet's own two largest cities were explicitly non-national at founding; and Concordia's own population was never planned at all, simply the accumulated overflow of every more exposed coastal city's own refugees, funneled inland by two and a half centuries of attrition.

Running underneath all of it, independently rediscovered by research passes that never cited each other, is the one true constant: robots, not human institutions or bloodlines, are what actually persists. Palmer subnet found this three separate times within a single subnet's own research. Amundsen Station found it in its own ~84%-robot rotating staff, potentially still walking around today, centuries later, in ordinary cities across all six subnets. Halley subnet found it in Neumayer's own unclaimed three-century engineering legacy. This is Tepenia's real answer to "who lives here" — not a nation of humans with robot companions, but a civilization whose actual continuity has always run through the participants who don't age out of remembering it.

## What It Feels Like

Moving through the whole of Tepenia, region by region, means moving through six genuinely different answers to the same two questions — what survived, and why — with no answer transferable to the next region over. Palmer subnet is the clearest single record of what the war actually cost: proof of what thrived, proof of what was lost, three different flavors of what survival meant. Halley subnet is quiet competence carrying weight nobody notices. Janbogo/Ross subnet is survival read as a verdict — every city's fate a legible answer to whether it was worth more alive than gone. Mirny subnet is function outliving the need for credit, at a scale that reaches, without exaggeration, into the player's own body. Mawson subnet is barely a region unto itself at all — three cities more connected to their neighbors than to each other. And at the center of all of it, Concordia is thirteen sincere, incompatible theories of what a real city even is, sharing one physical space because there's nowhere else left to have the argument.

Underneath every one of those six textures sits the same deeper fact: this is a country that lost an inter-subnet relay so essential that every surface region except its own home subnet still feels the loss today, and that built its entire founding document on a coercion nobody with power over the story has ever fully reckoned with.

## Personality (A Whole-Nation Read)

Tepenia doesn't reduce to one collective personality any more than Concordia's own thirteen districts do, or any of the five subnets' own cities did — but a genuine whole-nation pattern emerges once every region's own read sits side by side. Halley subnet's "Competency turned inward." Janbogo/Ross subnet's "Survival read as a verdict." Mirny subnet's "Function outliving the need for credit." Mawson subnet's admission that it has no single unifying trait at all, only a web of partial overlaps. Palmer subnet's own contrast between what thrived and what was lost. Concordia's thirteen-way argument over method in service of one shared, sincere goal.

Read together, these aren't six unrelated moods — they're six regional expressions of the same underlying national trait: **competence that never quite gets the recognition its own scale would justify, held by a population whose actual memory-keepers are rarely the ones anyone thinks to credit.** Tepenia is a nation that works, region by region, harder and more consequentially than its own residents generally realize — a fact independently rediscovered by nearly every research pass this whole project has produced, at every scale it's been run at.

## Real-World Inspirations, Researched

### Primary: Constantinople / Byzantium, "the last city" (already researched for Concordia specifically)

Applies now at whole-nation scale, not just to Concordia — a civilization's entire remaining presence concentrated into one surviving hub, not by that hub's own superiority, but because circumstance allowed it, specifically, to keep standing when everywhere else fell. Every other region in this document exists, in some sense, in the light of what Concordia now has to be for all of them.

### Secondary: The Antarctic Peninsula's own real, historically contested geography (already researched for Palmer subnet specifically)

Grounds the whole-game finding that reachability was always a double-edged trait — the same closeness to the rest of the world that let Palmer subnet flourish first is exactly what made it the war's hardest-hit region, a dynamic that plays out in miniature at every other region's own edge, wherever a highway or Arcanet cable crosses a subnet boundary.

### Secondary: Real multinational research clusters (Larsemann Hills, Queen Maud Land) — already researched at subnet scale for Mirny and Halley specifically

Together they ground this document's own Pattern 5 (founding-population displacement, never the same way twice) — real Antarctica's own genuine density of overlapping national presences is the structural seed every one of Tepenia's own varied demographic-formation stories grows out of.

### Supporting: Sarajevo under and after the siege; Chicago's immigrant neighborhood mosaic (already researched for Concordia specifically)

Both apply at whole-nation scale as much as at Concordia's own district scale — a civilization whose diversity and resilience were forged specifically by hardship rather than despite it, and whose regional identities (subnet culture surfacing inside Concordia's own districts, per Gemini's Janbogo Nexus) persist for generations after the original displacement that produced them.
