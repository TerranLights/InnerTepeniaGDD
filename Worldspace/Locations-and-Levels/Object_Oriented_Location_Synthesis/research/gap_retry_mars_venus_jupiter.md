## 4. CONFLICTS

Each item names the earlier file's value, the new value, a likely reason, and which I now trust.

1. **Mars surface GCR dose equivalent at solar maximum.** The 2009 design study (ADD Table 3-7) gives 10 to 15 mSv per 40 days and 120 to 175 mSv per 545 days at solar maximum (0.22 to 0.38 mSv/day, blood-forming organ, atmosphere only). The Curiosity RAD measurement (MVT 3.3, Hassler 2014) is 0.64 +/- 0.12 mSv/day near solar maximum at Gale (-4.4 km), and SIM24 (2024) says about 0.65 mSv/day. SLABA's 2013 calculation gives 155 to 183 mSv/yr at solar maximum (0.43 to 0.50 mSv/day) for the Viking 1 site. Likely reasons: the 2009 values come from pre-RAD transport models (and a different quantity, blood-forming-organ dose equivalent); solar cycle 24 was weak, so "solar maximum" in 2012 was not as low in GCR as an idealized maximum; the Gale elevation gives a thicker column (21 g/cm^2) than the 16 g/cm^2 model. **Trust the RAD measurement** (0.64 mSv/day) for design; the older numbers are a lower bound.
2. **Unshielded annual surface dose.** SN91 (1991 code): 119 mSv/yr (11.9 rem) blood-forming organ at 0 km; Hassler 2014: 232 mSv/yr (RAD-anchored); SLABA (MEREM Monte Carlo): 156 to 274 mSv/yr. Different eras, targets and solar conditions (the solar phase for SN91's number was not identified in the text I read). **Trust the RAD-anchored value** (232 mSv/yr) and note that the models span a factor of 2.
3. **Global dust storm frequency.** Zurek and Martin (1993), in MVT 3.2: about one in three Mars years. SL19 list: seven Mars years with a confirmed event in about 33 Mars years since 1956 (0.21 per Mars year), and three events in about 13 Mars years since 1994 (0.23). The 1993 figure came from a short record before 2001. **Trust 0.2 per Mars year (roughly one in four to five)**, with the caveat that the pre-1971 record is incomplete and that two storms (Mars Years 27 and 29) started like global storms but were not confirmed.
4. **Mars top-of-atmosphere irradiance.** 586.2 W/m^2 (MVT 3.2, NSSDC) versus 590 W/m^2 (AF90, solar constant 1371 W/m^2). The 0.6% difference is the solar-constant value used in 1990. **Trust 586.2**; scale AF90's irradiance table by 0.9936.
5. **Lava tube width versus volume.** MVT Conflicts 9 could not reconcile "1 to 3 orders of magnitude more voluminous" with "100 times wider." SAURO's tables resolve it: measured pit widths on Mars are 40 to 400 m against "a few tens of meters" on Earth (about 10 times, not 100 times), while the average collapse volume is up to 80 times Earth's, and the estimated intact-tube cross-sections are 20 to 70 m (overcrusted type) or 100 to 450 m (deep-inflation type). **Trust SAURO's tables; "100 times wider" is not supported.**
6. **Mars-orbit dose units.** MARIE's absorbed dose in silicon is 0.21 mGy/day (ICRC03), the same as RAD's surface absorbed dose of 0.210 mGy/day (MVT 3.3), but the dose equivalent is a different matter: the orbit dose equivalent is "about 2.5 times" the ISS value (VAZ07) and the surface value is "similar to ISS" (SIM24: 0.65 mSv/day against ISS 0.6). No journal value of the orbital dose equivalent in mSv/day was reached. Do not compare mGy to mSv. **Trust the ratio statements** for orbit, with the unit warning.
7. **Venera 13 survival time.** 157 minutes (MVT 3.7, HAVOC proofs paper, NASA Langley) versus 127 minutes (NASA Glenn papers NTRS 20140017762 and the many secondary pages) versus "two hours" (NASA Glenn NTRS 20120016535 and the NASA outreach page). Possible reason for 157: counting transmission after landing and descent together, or an error; unresolved. **Trust 127 minutes** (two NASA documents plus secondary agreement), at medium confidence, because no Soviet primary was reached.
8. **Altitude of the Venus 1-bar level.** Landis 2011: pressure of one atmosphere "at slightly over 54 km"; Landis 2003 (MVT 3.8): 0 to 50 C near the 1-bar level just above 50 km. Radio occultation (BAN26): 910 mbar at about 50 km; HAVOC: 1.05 atm and 75 C at 50 km; the secondary table: 1.08 bar and 348 K at 50 km, so 1 bar near 50.6 km at about 343 K (derived). The 54 km figure is 4 km high by every other source, and the scale height near 50 to 55 km (7.1 km, derived from 325 K, molar mass 43.45 g/mol and gravity 8.73 m/s^2) makes 4 km worth a factor of 1.8 in pressure. Possible reasons: reading of a figure; altitude reference; latitude and time variation (VeRa shows a 30 K equator-to-pole contrast at 1 bar). **Trust about 50 km, 340 to 350 K.** The 20 C level near 56 km, which Landis gives and the table reproduces, is at about 0.5 bar.
9. **GCR absorbed dose at the top of the Venus habitable band (62 km).** Dartnell et al. (as cited by Herbst): 9.5e-4 mGy/h for Venusian air; HERBST20 Table 2: 2.49e-3 mGy/h for the same target. A factor of 2.6. The cause is not stated (solar minimum versus other conditions; spectrum). **Use the HERBST20 value (higher) for design.** Water-sphere and microbial-cell targets are 3.2 and 2.2 times the air value at 62 km.
10. **Landis (2011) energy arithmetic.** 5e20 kg x 50 MJ/kg = 2.5e28 J; 2.5e28 J / 1e12 W = 7.9e8 years, but the paper says "850 million years" (7% higher). A rounding or a slightly larger atmosphere mass. No effect on the conclusion.
11. **Europa surface dose rate.** BJS 3B: "5.4 Sv per day" (ESA wording via a summary) and "2.3 Mrad per day" (rejected); a search summary this session: "6 to 7 rad/s" (about 5 kGy/day). None of these is in the SDT report. The SDT's own numbers are mission-integrated: about 1.7 Mrad(Si) behind 100 mil Al, and a processing dose of about 60 Grad at 10 cm over about 1e7 years (about 60 Gy/yr). **Trust only the SDT numbers.** The two surface-rate claims differ by three orders of magnitude and cannot both be right.
12. **Enceladus plume rate.** Waite et al. 2017 (Cassini INMS paper) uses about 200 kg/s; BJS 3C had the same value from a summary. VILL23 (JWST, 9 November 2022 observation): 300 kg/s with a 10% uncertainty on the molecule rate. Different epochs and methods; VILL23 says the rate is similar to the Cassini-era value. **Use 200 to 300 kg/s.**
13. **Attribution of Io's heat flow.** BJS 3B and Open threads attributed "heat flow 1 to 3 W/m^2, about 100 TW" to Park et al. 2024. The Park et al. paper measures the tidal Love number and Q and does not give a heat flow. The heat flow comes from Veeder et al. (cited in DAV23): total about 106 TW. **Re-attribute; values agree** (2.5 W/m^2 derived).
14. **Bullock and Grinspoon end state.** The Wikipedia article on Venus terraforming says their model has an end state of 43 bar and 400 K. I read the whole paper (BG96) and found a "3 bar regime," not 43 bar, and no 400 K figure in the text. **Rejected the 43 bar/400 K claim.**
15. **Enceladus ocean thickness.** Thomas et al. 2016: an ocean 26 to 31 km thick under a 21 to 26 km crust (their interior model matched to the moment of inertia 0.335 MR^2). Derived from a 190 km core (Hemingway et al. 2018 as cited by Shao and Nimmo) and a 14 to 26 km shell: 36 to 48 km. The two use different core sizes and shell estimates; BJS 3C's "about 45 km" falls in the second range. **Report the range 26 to 48 km and name the model.**
16. **Perchlorate arithmetic basis.** The reference dose applies to ingestion; the EPA gave no inhalation value. A habitat dust problem is partly inhalation. The 9.8 mg of dust per day is therefore an ingestion yardstick, not an inhalation limit.

## 5. DERIVED FIGURES

All arithmetic with units; sources for the inputs are in Section 3.

1. **Mars surface irradiance (3.2).** Global irradiance on a horizontal surface at zenith, albedo 0.1: G = G0 x f / (1 - albedo), with G0 = 590 W/m^2 and f from Table I(a) of AF90. Example: tau = 2.0: f = 0.578, G = 590 x 0.578 / 0.9 = 379 W/m^2. Check at tau = 0.1: 590 x 0.883 / 0.9 = 579 W/m^2 (98% of G0). Beam: Gb = 590 x exp(-tau): tau = 2: 590 x 0.1353 = 80 W/m^2. Diffuse = 379 - 80 = 299 W/m^2. On the 586.2 W/m^2 basis multiply by 586.2/590 = 0.9936 (so 379 becomes 377).
2. **Perchlorate (3.4).** Reference dose x body mass: 0.0007 mg/kg/day x 70 kg = 0.049 mg/day = 49 micrograms/day. Dust equivalent at 0.5 wt% perchlorate anion: 0.049 mg / 0.005 = 9.8 mg dust/day. At 0.4 wt%: 12.25 mg; at 0.6 wt%: 8.17 mg; at 1 wt%: 4.9 mg. No-effect level: 0.007 mg/kg/day x 70 kg = 0.49 mg/day.
3. **Dust-storm frequency (3.6).** Mars year = 686.98 d / 365.25 d = 1.8809 Earth years. 1956 to 2018: 62 / 1.8809 = 33.0 Mars years; 7 years with an event: 7 / 33.0 = 0.21 per Mars year. 1994 to mid-2018: 24.5 / 1.8809 = 13.0 Mars years; 3 events: 3 / 13.0 = 0.23.
4. **Launch-window spacing (3.7).** 31 Oct 2026 to 2 Dec 2028 = 763 d; to 23 Feb 2031 = 813 d; to 28 Apr 2033 = 795 d; to 21 Apr 2035 (Type 1) = 723 d. Mean synodic period 779.935 d (BURKE).
5. **MARIE units (3.8).** 1 rad = 0.01 Gy, so 21.36 mrad/day = 0.2136 mGy/day. July 2002 monthly mean 168.68 mrad/day = 1.687 mGy/day, 8.0 times the quiet-month mean of 21.18 mrad/day. The 13-month mean including events: 40.12 mrad/day.
6. **Design-study daily rates (3.8).** 25 mSv / 40 d = 0.625 mSv/day; 30 / 40 = 0.75; 335 / 545 = 0.615; 405 / 545 = 0.743; solar maximum: 10 / 40 = 0.25; 15 / 40 = 0.375; 120 / 545 = 0.220; 175 / 545 = 0.321. Transit: 1,030 / 621 = 1.66 mSv/day; 1,240 / 621 = 2.00; 720 / 360 = 2.00; 475 / 621 = 0.76; 275 / 360 = 0.76. SN91: 11.9 rem/yr = 119 mSv/yr = 0.326 mSv/day; flare 9.9 rem = 99 mSv, 6.3 rem = 63 mSv.
7. **Venus pressure and temperature (3.10).** For 1 bar (1.000e5 Pa) between 50 km (1.08e5 Pa, 348 K) and 55 km (5.38e4 Pa, 300 K): fraction = ln(1.08e5 / 1.000e5) / ln(1.08e5 / 5.38e4) = 0.0770 / 0.6969 = 0.1105; altitude = 50 + 0.1105 x 5 = 50.55 km; T = 348 - 0.1105 x 48 = 342.7 K. For 293.15 K between 55 km (300 K) and 60 km (263 K): fraction = (300 - 293.15) / (300 - 263) = 0.185; altitude = 55.9 km; p = 5.38e4 x (2.39e4 / 5.38e4)^0.185 = 4.63e4 Pa (0.46 bar); linear in p gives 0.48 bar. Scale height H = R T / (M g) = 8.314 x 325 / (0.04345 x 8.73) = 7.1 km.
8. **Venus cosmic-ray dose in annual terms (3.12).** mGy/h x 8,766 h/yr: 51 km water sphere 9.82e-5 x 8,766 = 0.86 mGy/yr; 62 km 8.02e-3 x 8,766 = 70.3 mGy/yr; Earth ground 5e-4 x 8,766 = 4.4 mGy/yr.
9. **Venus conjunction (3.13).** 20 days / 583.92 days = 3.4% of a synodic period; 6 Aug 2011 to 16 Mar 2013 = 588 days.
10. **Landis (3.14).** 2.5e28 J / (1e12 W x 3.156e7 s/yr) = 7.92e8 yr.
11. **Europa (3.16).** 100 eV / (16 x 1.66054e-27 kg) = 100 x 1.602177e-19 J / 2.6569e-26 kg = 6.03e8 J/kg = 6.03e8 Gy = 60.3 Grad (1 Grad = 1e7 Gy). Over 1e7 yr: 60.3 Gy/yr = 0.165 Gy/day. 1.7 Mrad = 1.7e6 x 0.01 Gy = 17 kGy. 150 krad = 1.5 kGy. Interpolation of the Grid2 table for 150 krad: ln(1.62e5 / 1.5e5) / ln(1.62e5 / 1.25e5) = 0.0770 / 0.2592 = 0.297; thickness = 700 + 0.297 x 100 = 729.7 mil = 18.5 mm = 5.0 g/cm^2.
12. **Cooper flux to power (3.17).** 8e10 keV cm^-2 s^-1 x 1.602e-16 J/keV = 1.28e-5 J cm^-2 s^-1 = 0.128 W/m^2; 2e8 keV cm^-2 s^-1 = 3.2e-4 W/m^2.

## 6. CONSTRAINTS FOR ANY INHABITANT (physics and engineering only)

- **Mars temperatures vary mostly with latitude, season and dust.** Polar-night ground and air sit on the CO2 frost point (about 143 to 150 K, 3.1); mid-latitude winter is 146 to 191 K; low latitudes swing about 90 to 100 K on the ground and about 60 K in the air each sol; a global dust storm cuts the equatorial ground swing from about 102 K to about 49 K (3.1). A surface structure needs thermal design for the swing at its own latitude, not a planetary mean.
- **Polar night lasts about 75 degrees of Ls at 70 degrees latitude and about 135 degrees at 80 degrees** (3.1, Table IV). Sunlight is zero there; solar power needs storage or another source.
- **Sunlight on the Mars surface is 0.4 to 0.98 of the top-of-atmosphere flux for optical depths 0.1 to 4 at the zenith** (3.2): 579 W/m^2 at tau 0.1 down to 238 W/m^2 at tau 4; the daily horizontal insolation at 22 N fell to 935 Wh/m^2/day in the 1977 storm and exceeded 3,000 Wh/m^2/day for 200 degrees of Ls in the clear season. At high opacity most of the light is diffuse (3.2), so sun-tracking gives little benefit then.
- **Global dust storms occur in about one Mars year in four to five, starting at Ls 185 to 300** (3.6). Surface power must plan for storms lasting weeks (MVT 3.2), at a rate of about one per decade (0.2 per Mars year is about 0.11 per Earth year).
- **Lava-tube roofs and widths are unmeasured on Mars; the candidate passages are 20 to 450 m wide with heights of tens of meters** (3.3). Any design must treat intact tubes as hypotheses to be surveyed before commitment. Skylights show depths of 15 to 65 m to the collapse floor.
- **Terrain gives little measured radiation shielding.** A butte that blocked 19% of the sky reduced the dose by about 5% (3.3). Mass overhead is what shields, and the albedo radiation is about 19% of the flat-terrain dose.
- **Perchlorate: 0.4 to 0.6 wt% of the soil; a 70 kg adult's chronic ingestion reference dose is carried by about 10 mg of dust per day** (3.4, 5.2). Dust control and no-ingestion design are needed; the inhalation hazard is undefined by the EPA.
- **Shallow ice is real but is at depth that increases toward the equator:** 1 to 2 m under the surface at mid-latitude scarps, with ice more than 100 m thick; hydrogen equivalent to 20 to 100% water by mass poleward of 50 degrees; below 45 degrees only 2 to 10% (3.5). A settlement far from mid-latitudes has no near-surface ice.
- **Earth-Mars departures recur every 723 to 813 days, with minimum-energy C3 of 7.8 to 14.8 km^2/s^2 and transit times of 178 to 412 days** (3.7); an inhabitant dependent on resupply waits two years or more between low-energy opportunities.
- **Radiation: on the Mars surface the GCR dose equivalent is about 0.64 mSv/day (233 mSv/yr); in Mars orbit about 2.5 times the ISS rate; a solar particle event adds 25 to 40 mSv on the surface and about 107 mSv in free space with a 20 cm water shelter** (3.8). Neither mass of regolith nor aluminum nulls GCR: aluminum on the surface raises the dose equivalent by 63% at 100 g/cm^2 while polyethylene reduces it more than twofold (3.8); the first 15 to 20 cm of regolith gives most of what regolith can give, for both solar events and GCR, in the 1991 model (3.8, SN91).
- **No human or large-animal data exist for months at 0.38 g** (3.9). Mouse data at 0.33 g show the soleus muscle's cross-sectional area preserved but the fiber type shifting, with 0.67 g needed for full function. Any claim of "Mars gravity is enough" is an extrapolation from rodents.
- **Venus's habitable band is set by two numbers:** about 1 bar and 343 to 348 K near 50 km, and 20 C with about 0.5 bar near 56 km (3.10). A habitat at 56 km sits in 20 C air at about 0.5 bar, so 1 atm inside means a pressure difference of about 0.5 bar across the envelope, or an interior held near 0.5 bar; a habitat at 50 km has 1 bar outside but air at 70 to 75 C that must be cooled. No altitude offers both Earth temperature and Earth pressure.
- **Venus cloud acid is 75 to 92% H2SO4 with modeled mass loading of up to 15 mg/m^3 at the cloud base** (3.15). Hull and air-handling materials need acid compatibility (MVT 3.8 tests).
- **Venus cosmic-ray dose in the 51 to 62 km band is small to moderate:** 0.86 mGy/yr at 51 km to 70 mGy/yr at 62 km for a torso-size water target, with a quality factor near 10 at the top of the clouds (3.12). Solar particle events add up to about 0.09 Gy in the worst case modeled for the zone's top (3.12).
- **Venus solar conjunction removes useful contact for about 20 to 24 days every 584 days** and leaves almost no contact for about a week (3.13). A habitat there must run autonomously on that schedule.
- **Terraforming Venus is out of reach in practice:** 5e20 kg of CO2, 2.5e28 J, at least 2,000 giant impacts, or a mountain of crust pulverized to over 1 km depth (3.14).
- **Europa's surface is a dose-driven environment:** a lander with a vault still receives about 150 krad(Si) over its mission, against about 1.7 Mrad(Si) behind only 2.54 mm of aluminum (3.16). Dose falls with shielding as in the Grid2 table (3.18): 5.5 g/cm^2 of aluminum cuts the Clipper-tour dose by a factor of about 21 relative to 0.69 g/cm^2, and the dose is still falling slowly at the last tabulated thickness (20.6 g/cm^2, 1.3e4 rad(Si)).
- **Callisto and Ganymede's equator have energy fluxes about 270 to 400 times below Europa's, and Ganymede's polar caps about 16 times below** (3.17), but a primary dose in human units is not available.
- **A robot's electronics in the Jovian system is a total-dose problem first:** 50 krad at the outside of each unit was the JUICE tolerance (3.18); typical electronics in the Lander study are rated to 300 krad (3.16). A human-shaped machine with a conventional semiconductor body needs the same shielding mass per unit dose as any other electronics of that tolerance.
- **Enceladus vents a plume of 200 to 300 kg/s of mostly water (96 to 99% by volume) containing 0.4 to 1.4% molecular hydrogen** (3.19). Below a 14 to 26 km ice shell lies an ocean 26 to 48 km thick, depending on the interior model (3.19).
- **Io's heat flow is about 2.5 W/m^2 (106 TW)** and it carries intense volcanism, with no shallow global magma ocean (3.19).

## 7. CANDIDATE HANDWAVES

Flagged only; the developer decides. I accept none.

- **"Intact, large, safe Martian lava tubes."** No tube has been seen from inside, no roof thickness is measured, and SHARAD has not detected one (3.3). A tube-based settlement needs the tubes to exist at the assumed width, to have a roof that stays up under unmeasured stresses, and to be reachable. This is an assumption, not a result.
- **"A 1 atm, 20 C floating habitat on Venus."** The two numbers sit at different altitudes (3.10): 1 bar at about 50 km is 70 C, and 20 C at about 56 km is about 0.5 bar. A habitat at either must use a pressure difference or a temperature difference. Landis's own lift tonnages for large envelopes (MVT 3.9, Conflicts 16) are unverified.
- **"Terraforming Venus or Mars in a plausible time."** Venus: 2.5e28 J (LANDIS11). Mars: not enough CO2 (MVT 3.11, Jakosky and Edwards). A fictional terraformed planet needs an energy source or an inventory that physics does not provide.
- **"Rapid unshielded travel to Jupiter's moons."** The dose numbers (3.16, 3.18) require mass shielding in grams per square centimeter that scales with how long the stay is. Stays near Europa's orbit are limited by dose; Callisto's orbit is outside the main belts (BJS).
- **"Mars gravity as good as Earth's for a lifetime."** No data at all (3.9).
- **"A robot body is immune to radiation."** The brief's rule: a robot body is a human-shaped machine with mass and electronics. Electronics fail in total-dose terms (50 to 300 krad(Si) design values) well below the lander's unshielded dose of 1.7 Mrad(Si).
