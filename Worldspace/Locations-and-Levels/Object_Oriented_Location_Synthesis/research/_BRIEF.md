# Research brief (shared by all research agents). Final at dispatch; never amended.

**Project.** A fiction universe whose people live in Antarctica and, later, in orbit and on other bodies of the Solar
System. This research gathers **real physics and engineering data** for a library of location "classes". It is **not**
worldbuilding. Do not invent anything. Do not write about societies, cultures, nations or characters.

**Developer's rule (LAW 0-R, binding):** *"Research FULLY. I don't expect you to deliver fast results. What I want is for
you to do it RIGHT. Doing web research is for ensuring realism and accuracy, and that's only possible with real,
accurate input data."* A recalled number is a guess in a confident tone, and a wrong number is worse than a missing one.
**No figure may come from memory.** Every number in your file comes from a source you opened this session.

**Physics fidelity rule.** Everything must satisfy known physics. The only waved item is a robot's nanotech gel brain
(consciousness and sentience). A robot's *body* is a human-shaped machine with mass, heat, charge and joints. It cannot
fly by flapping its arms. Where a finding bears on a human-shaped machine, state the constraint in general terms.

## Your output

Write one Markdown file to the path in your task. **Create it FIRST with a heading and `## SEARCH LOG`, and append as
you go** (an interruption must leave something usable). American English. Sections, in this order:

1. **Scope**: the bodies, orbits or structures you cover, in two or three lines.
2. **SEARCH LOG**: every search string, verbatim, and every page opened, with the date. For each, mark **useful**, **thin**,
   or **dead**, and whether a dead end died **at the query** or **at the sources**. A log with eleven searches, two dead
   ends and four honest "not used" rows beats one with four clean hits.
3. **Findings**: one table per sub-topic. Columns: *Quantity* · *Value and units* · *Source (publisher, title, URL,
   date)* · *Primary or secondary* · *Cross-check (second source, or "none found")* · *Confidence* (high, medium or
   low, and why). **Primary** means a space agency, a peer-reviewed paper, a handbook or a dataset. Popular-science
   pages and videos are **secondary**: use them for leads, and confirm every number against a primary source.
4. **Conflicts**: where two sources disagree, show both and say why they might differ (definition, epoch, model).
5. **Derived figures** (only if needed): show the arithmetic with units and state the basis.
6. **Constraints for any inhabitant** (what a settlement here must cope with, whoever lives there): bulleted, each tied to a
   finding above. No culture, no stereotype, no names of peoples.
7. **Candidate handwaves**: anything that would require physics to be bent for a plausible design. Flag it plainly. Do not
   accept it. The developer decides.
8. **Open threads**: what you could not find, what you would search next, and sources you found but did not read.
9. **Rejected sources**: and why.

## How to search

- **Several angles per quantity.** A quantity found once is one source. Query it from a second direction. Go back to topics
  you logged as covered.
- **Prefer primary:** NASA (NTRS, mission pages, fact sheets), ESA, JAXA, peer-reviewed journals, the AIAA, NASA
  technical reports, and textbook-grade handbooks. Preprints are acceptable if labeled.
- **Dates matter.** Planetary figures are stable. Radiation, debris, mission-plan and engineering-concept figures are not.
  Record each source's date.
- **If a tool you need (`WebSearch`, `WebFetch`) is not loaded, load it with `ToolSearch` first.** Use the standard search
  mode first and the extended mode when results are thin or the fact is hard to find.
- Do not use graphify or any repository search. Do not read or edit any other file in this repository.
  Everything you need is on the web.

## Done means

All of your topic's checklist is covered, or each gap is listed under Open threads with the reason. Your final chat reply:
at most 120 words (where the file is, its line count, number of searches, number of dead ends, anything you could not
open).
