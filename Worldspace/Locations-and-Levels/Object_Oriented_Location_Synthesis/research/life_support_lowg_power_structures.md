# Life support, low-gravity biology, power and heat rejection, hull structures, shielding, rotation dynamics, in-situ construction: physics and engineering research

Research dates: 2026-10-10 and 2026-10-11 (the system date rolled over during the session; most entries are 2026-10-10). Brief: `_BRIEF.md` (LAW 0-R; no figure from memory). Status: complete; every checklist item A-G is covered or listed in section 8.

## 1. Scope

The engineering every sealed, inhabited structure must satisfy, whoever lives there: closed-loop life support for humans (air, water, food, waste, trace contaminants, thermal), partial-gravity and radiation biology, power and heat rejection at scale, pressure-vessel and spin stress in hulls and materials, meteoroid shielding, rotation dynamics, and regolith-based construction. Residents are humans (who breathe and eat) and human-shaped robots (who do not). The sibling files `leo_and_orbital_habitats.md` and `mars_venus_and_transfer.md` (their sections 3 and 8) were read for overlap; figures already established there are cited by file name and not re-derived unless a new angle was needed.

Tooling note. `WebSearch` returns a model-written summary plus links; any number in a summary is a lead only and was never copied unless I then read it in an opened page. `WebFetch` returns a small-model digest; numbers that reached me only that way are marked "digest". Most pages and PDFs were fetched with `curl` and read as raw text (`pdftotext -layout` for PDFs, tag-stripped text for HTML): marked "raw". Date of every entry: 2026-10-10 (system date), unless the cell says otherwise.

### 5C. Hull stress and shell mass

Thin-walled vessel, internal pressure P, radius r, wall thickness t (statics): cylinder hoop stress sigma_hoop = P r / t; cylinder axial stress P r / (2 t); sphere sigma = P r / (2 t). Derivation of hoop: cut a ring of unit length along a diameter; pressure force on the half = P (2 r)(1); two wall sections each carry sigma t (1); equilibrium gives 2 sigma t = 2 P r.

Spinning cylinder with its contents resting on the inner surface: the shell carries centrifugal load per unit area m g (m = areal mass of shell plus contents, at the rim acceleration g), so the hoop line tension is N = (P + m g) r. With m = rho t + m_p (shell plus payload) and allowable stress sigma_a: sigma_a t = (P + m_p g) r + rho t g r, hence

  t = r (P + m_p g) / (sigma_a - rho g r),   shell areal mass = rho t.

- No payload and no pressure: t is unbounded as sigma_a approaches rho g r, giving the largest radius at which a shell can support only itself at 1 g: R_max = sigma_a / (rho g) (rim speed sqrt(sigma_a / rho), the same as the sibling file's V_max). Design allowable sigma_a = min(Ftu/2.0, Fty/1.65) (NASA-STD-5001B): 2219-T81 196 MPa (yield governs): R_max 7.0 km, rim speed 262 m/s. 7075-T6 269 MPa: 9.8 km, 310 m/s. Ti-6Al-4V 462 MPa: 10.6 km, 323 m/s. AISI 4340 896 MPa: 11.7 km, 338 m/s. IM7/8552 unidirectional (taking Ftu/2 = 1,362 MPa, along fibers only): 88 km, 931 m/s. IM7 bare fiber (2,835 MPa): 162 km, 1,262 m/s. These are ceilings with nothing but the shell's own weight; they rise only with specific strength, not with size.
- Pressure only (no spin), P = 101.3 kPa: shell areal mass per meter of radius = rho P / sigma_a: 2219 1.47 kg/m2 per m; 7075 1.05; Ti 0.97; 4340 0.885; IM7 UD 0.117. Mass per enclosed volume of a long cylinder, m/V = 2 rho P / sigma_a: 2.94 (2219), 2.11 (7075), 1.94 (Ti), 1.77 (4340), 0.23 kg/m3 (IM7 UD); of a sphere, m/V = 1.5 rho P / sigma_a: 2.21, 1.58, 1.46, 1.33, 0.18. These are independent of size.

### 5D. MMOD hit counts and shield mass

- Poisson: N = F A t; probability of no penetration PNP = exp(-N). Christiansen's example: F = 7 x 10^-6 + 3 x 10^-6 = 1.0 x 10^-5 per m2 per year (particles of 0.9 cm or larger at 400 km), A = 100 m2, t = 10 years: N = 0.01, PNP = 0.990 (matches the report's 0.99).
- Same flux, other areas (LEO only; assumes a shield that stops everything smaller than 0.9 cm): 1,000 m2 for 10 years: N = 0.1, PNP 0.905. 2,500 m2 (the ISS array area, for scale) for 10 years: N = 0.25, PNP 0.78, one hit per 40 years. 5,000 m2 for 10 years: N = 0.5, PNP 0.61. For a 2.1 x 10^6 m2 torus surface (SP-413, sibling) at the LEO flux: 21 hits per year by 0.9 cm particles, which is why such a structure is not placed at 400 km without a much thicker shield and why free-space (L5) flux values must be used for it.
- Whipple example mass: 0.127 cm x 2.713 g/cm3 (Al6061, MIL-HDBK-5J: 0.098 lb/in3) = 0.345 g/cm2; 0.32 cm x 2.851 g/cm3 (Al2219) = 0.912 g/cm2; total 1.257 g/cm2 = 12.6 kg/m2. TransHab MMOD protection: 3,208 kg / 250.9 m2 = 12.8 kg/m2. Radiation shielding 4,500 to 10,000 kg/m2 is 357 to 794 times 12.6 kg/m2. A 15 g/cm2 shield on TransHab: 34,599 / 10,663 = 3.2 times the whole module's mass.

### 5E. Rotation, spin-up and gravity gradient (inputs: SP-413 torus numbers in the sibling file)

- Torus M = 150 kt (structure only, SP-413 Table 4-1), R = 830 m, 1 rpm (omega = 0.10472 rad/s, a = omega^2 R = 9.10 m/s2 = 0.928 g, rim speed 86.9 m/s). Ring moment of inertia I_z = M R^2 = 1.5 x 10^8 x 830^2 = 1.03 x 10^14 kg m2. Angular momentum L = I omega = 1.08 x 10^13 kg m2/s. Rotational energy 0.5 I omega^2 = 5.67 x 10^11 J = 157 MWh. (The real rotating mass is larger: atmosphere 44 kt and interior mass add to it; this is a lower bound.)
- Spin-up in one year (3.156 x 10^7 s) at constant torque: tau = L / t = 3.4 x 10^5 N m, equal to a tangential force of 413 N at the rim.
- Spin-up with expelled reaction mass at the rim: the final tangential speed 86.9 m/s needs, by the rocket equation, m_prop = M (1 - exp(-86.9 / v_e)): v_e = 2,943 m/s (Isp 300 s) gives 4,370 t; 4,413 m/s (Isp 450 s) 2,930 t; 29,420 m/s (Isp 3,000 s) 443 t. This is the reason SP-413's idea of using the counter-rotating shield (already in orbit) as the reaction mass is attractive (sibling).
- Gravity gradient in a 400 km circular orbit: orbit rate omega_o = sqrt(GM / a^3), a = 6,371 + 400 = 6,771 km, GM = 3.98600435507 x 10^14 m3/s2: omega_o = 1.1332 x 10^-3 rad/s (period 92.4 min). Torque on a body with principal inertias differing by Delta_I, axis offset theta from vertical: tau = 3 omega_o^2 Delta_I sin(theta) cos(theta), maximum 1.5 omega_o^2 Delta_I at 45 degrees. For a thin ring Delta_I = I_z - I_x = M R^2 / 2 = 5.17 x 10^13 kg m2: tau_max = 1.5 x (1.1332 x 10^-3)^2 x 5.17 x 10^13 = 1.0 x 10^8 N m. Spin angular momentum L = 1.08 x 10^13 kg m2/s turns this into a precession rate of about tau / L = 9.2 x 10^-6 rad/s (a precession period of about 7.9 days at the maximum): the spinning ring behaves as a gyroscope; the cost is the periodic momentum exchange or propellant to hold orientation (for example toward the Sun), not a spin-down.
- Docking a 100 t vehicle at the rim of that torus: to match the rim it must be given 0.5 m v^2 = 3.8 x 10^8 J (106 kWh) and angular momentum m R v = 7.2 x 10^9 kg m2/s, which is 0.067% of L; rotating hub (SP-413: 130 m diameter, radius 65 m) tangential speed omega r = 6.8 m/s. The momentum is negligible; matching spin rate or using a despun, bearing-mounted hub is the issue. The shield's relative rim speed at 0.07 rpm is 87 + 6 = about 93 m/s (SP-413 states 100 m/s).

### 5F. Regolith processing

- Microwave sintering: 1.8 kWh/kg = 6.5 MJ/kg; 2.2-2.9 kWh/kg = 7.9-10.4 MJ/kg. A 2.3 kg block at 1.8 kWh/kg: 4.1 kWh. For a 5 cm paving tile at 1,900 kg/m3 (95 kg/m2): 209-276 kWh/m2 of tile at 2.2-2.9 kWh/kg. Sintering a whole 150 kg/m2 (15 g/cm2) shield layer at 2.2 kWh/kg would take 330 kWh/m2, which is why a loose-regolith shield, needing no sintering, is the default in the studies read.
- Oxygen per energy: 1.45 g/kWh = 690 kWh per kg O2; 10.77-15.79 g/kWh = 63-93 kWh per kg O2 (1000 / (g per kWh)).
- MRE plant: 311.34 kW x 8,766 h/yr = 2.73 x 10^6 kWh/yr over 25,000 + 23,900 = 48,900 kg of product = 55.8 kWh per kg of product (109 kWh per kg of metal if oxygen is counted as free). Hardware 6,776 kg / 48,900 kg per year = 0.139 kg per kg per year (the paper: 0.14).
- Shielding cover mass: 15 g/cm2 = 150 kg/m2 on 250.9 m2 = 37.6 t (BVAD: 34,599 kg for the shell volume of 329.4 m3). 50 cm of regolith at 1.5 g/cm3 = 75 g/cm2 = 750 kg/m2.
- Line tension in a pressurized printed shell: N = P r = 101.3 kPa x 5 m = 506 kN per meter of wall (0.5 MPa in a wall 1 m thick); the same result for a larger vault scales with r.


## 6. Constraints for any inhabitant

Each item is something a sealed, inhabited structure must cope with, whoever lives there. Tags point to findings in section 3 or derived figures in section 5. Residents are humans (who breathe, eat and drink) and human-shaped machines (which do not).

**Air, water, food**

- A human needs about 0.82 kg of oxygen and emits about 1.04 kg of CO2, drinks and eats-in-water about 2.5 kg and eats 1.5 kg of food (as shipped) per day, and gives off about 139 W of heat (A1). Those figures scale linearly: 1,000 humans need about 0.8 t of oxygen and 2.5 t of drinking water per day (5A). A population of machines that do not breathe adds heat and electricity load but no oxygen load.
- Cabin air must hold an oxygen partial pressure near 21 kPa; CO2 above about 0.53 kPa (4 mmHg) already degrades people, and the effect of long exposure at 0.5% in microgravity is "unknown but thought to be adverse" (A2). Lower total pressure needs a higher oxygen fraction, which raises fire risk and restricts materials; the 34%-oxygen, 57 kPa case is a stated compromise (A2).
- A fully closed loop is hard in practice: the ISS recovers 98% of its water with a demonstration brine processor (93-94% without) and only 50% of oxygen from CO2, and its recovery hardware has repeatedly failed and been repaired (A3). Even at 98%, a loop that circulates 11-29 kg of water per person per day (bases with showers and laundry) loses 80-215 kg per person per year (5A).
- Food from plants takes space and power: about 30 m2 of crops per person under lamps for 73% of the food mass (Lunar Palace 365), about 40-50 m2 in NASA reviews, 250 m2 of daylight under glass (Biosphere 2) (A4). Electric power for artificial light is 2.6-16 kW per person by NASA's own range, 15 kW at the best measured wheat yield (A4, 5A). Oxygen alone takes 20-25 m2 of crops per person (A4). Biosphere 2 lost oxygen from 20.9% to about 14% in 16 months because of soil respiration and concrete absorbing CO2 (A4); a closed volume is a small buffer.
- Crops, waste processing, exercise and maintenance take crew time: 13.1 crew-hours per m2 of crops per year, about 2-2.5 h per person per day of exercise, 3 h/day meals on the ISS schedule (A4, A5, B3).
- Dust and trace gases accumulate in sealed air: methane 329 mg, ammonia 50 mg, hydrogen 42 mg per person per day plus equipment off-gassing (A2); regolith dust is 10-20% below 20 micrometers, sticks electrostatically, wore suit bearings out in three days and fouled filters and beds (G1).

**Biology under altered gravity and radiation**

- No mammal is known to have completed gestation in orbit; the longest rat exposures covered about half of a 22-day pregnancy, mouse embryos developed to blastocysts in microgravity (23.6% against 61.2% on the ground), and no human data exist (B1). Whether children can develop normally in 0.1-0.4 g, in microgravity or in a spinning structure has never been tested (B1, B2, sibling Richter et al.).
- Without gravity, people lose bone (2-9% over 4-6 months) and muscle (13% of calf volume over six months with exercise); about 2 h/day of exercise slows but does not stop it (B3, sibling). Rodents held at 1 g on a centrifuge did not lose bone or muscle in 35 days; at 1/6 g they kept muscle mass but not muscle fiber type (B2). Thirty minutes a day of 1 g centrifugation only partly protected bed-rested humans (B2). 70% of long-flight crew show eye changes (SANS) and 18% concerning ones (B3).
- Radiation exposure in free space over a 9-month gestation would be about 130 mGy inside a spacecraft shell at Mars-cruise rates, above the 100 mGy at which terrestrial data begin to show effects on the embryo (5A, B4); no study of chronic heavy-ion fetal exposure exists. A thin inflatable shell lets in about three times the trapped-radiation dose rate of a loaded ISS module (D3).
- Radiation shielding sized for settlement dose limits needs 4.5-10 t/m2, 360-800 times the mass of a micrometeoroid shield (E, 5D); for crew mission limits 15-75 g/cm2 of regolith is used (G4). The mass is cheap in situ and expensive to move from Earth.

**Power and heat**

- Every watt used inside a sealed structure has to leave as infrared. In vacuum there is no convection or conduction to a surrounding medium; a surface at 300 K emits only about 390 W/m2 per face at emissivity 0.85 (5B, C4). An ISS-like 12.5 kW per person needs about 16-32 m2 of radiator per person at room temperature (5B). Hot radiators (385 K) shrink the area by a factor of about 2.7 but need a hotter working fluid (5B).
- Solar arrays on the ISS deliver about 64 W/m2 of array area, or 15.6 m2 per kW; ideal 29.5% cells give 401 W/m2 (C3, 5B). A habitat at 5-15 kW per person therefore needs on the order of 12-230 m2 of collector per person depending on how good the array is, and storage for the dark period (about 35 of every 92 minutes in LEO; 14.5 days on the Moon) (C3).
- Nuclear plants of 1-40 kWe have been designed at 145-400 kg per kWe and need radiators of about 3.3 m2 per kWe at 385 K; none has been built for an orbital habitat (C2).

**Structure and shielding**

- A pressurized shell must carry hoop tension P r / t; for a given material that makes shell mass per enclosed volume independent of size (about 2-3 kg/m3 ideal for aluminum at 1 atm), but real modules are 10-30 times heavier (D2, 5C). Habitable modules need a factor 2.0 on ultimate and 1.65 on yield (D1).
- Spin gravity adds a load on the shell proportional to radius; with the shell's own weight alone the largest 1 g radius is sigma_a/(rho g): 7-12 km for metals, 88 km for fiber composite along the fibers; with payload and pressure the practical radius is much smaller and shell mass grows with radius (5C).
- Inflatable shells are about 2-7 times lighter per volume than rigid modules (9-32 versus 43-67 kg/m3) but creep under constant load is "one of the most significant factors" in life; a Vectran test module burst after 49 minutes at 9.9 times operating pressure (D2). BEAM served beyond a two-year demonstration and is certified against debris through 2028 (D2).
- A thin MMOD shield stops particles up to about 1 cm; larger ones are rare but not negligible over large areas and long times: about one 0.9 cm-or-larger hit per 1,000 years per 100 m2 at 400 km (E, 5D). Large structures scale hit counts with area.
- A spinning structure must spin about its axis of maximum inertia, otherwise internal damping makes it tumble (F). In LEO gravity-gradient torque on a 100,000-metric-ton-class spinning ring is about 10^8 N m (5E). Docking requires a non-spinning, bearing-mounted or otherwise matched port.
- A built structure's mass is dominated by what the radiation and pressure require, not by the people: ISS carries 418 kg of station per m3 of pressurized volume (5C); SP-413's 10 million metric tons of shield for 10,000 residents is 1,000 t per person (sibling).

**In-situ construction**

- Sintered or printed regolith has been shown to 5-22 MPa in small pieces with 2-10 MJ of energy per kg; a pressure shell of it needs an inner pressure vessel (G2).
- Extracting oxygen and metals from regolith takes 60-700 kWh per kg of oxygen in demonstrations and about 56 kWh per kg of product in a system study; those energies, not the mass of ore, set the power plant size (G3, 5F).


## 7. Candidate handwaves

Flagged only. None is accepted here; the developer decides. Each is a place where a plausible-looking design would need physics or engineering that no source in this file supports.

1. **Closed ecology at a few square meters of crops per person.** The theoretical minimum is 3-5 m2 per person but only at 1,000 micromole per m2 per second, perfect light absorption and 3.6 kW of electricity per person (A4); the best measured wheat yield in the same work needed 30 m2 and 15 kW per person; Lunar Palace needed 30 m2 for 73% of the food mass; Biosphere 2 needed 250 m2. A design with a small garden feeding everyone, no lamps and no imports is not supported.
2. **Loops closed to 100% for years.** The ISS recovers 98% of water (with a demonstration unit that has needed repeated repair) and only 50% of oxygen from CO2; Lunar Palace reached 98.2% material closure for 370 days with food and solid wastes only partly closed (67% of solids). A settlement that never resupplies any element, with no leakage (BVAD design leakage 0.05-0.14% of the atmosphere per day), is not demonstrated.
3. **Children born, gestated and raised in low gravity or in spin.** Never tested in any mammal for a full gestation, never in a human (B1, B2). Normal development is an assumption.
4. **Partial gravity as a cure-all.** The only orbital partial-g evidence is 35-day mouse studies at 1/6 g and 1 g (B2); 1/6 g kept muscle mass but not fiber type. For humans there is Apollo (sibling) and bed-rest analogs. A settlement at 0.1-0.4 g that needs no exercise is not supported ("seems to be insufficient ... in the long-term", sibling Richter).
5. **Spin radii of kilometers with ordinary shell materials.** The shell of a 3.2 km, 1 g cylinder with 4.5 t/m2 of contents needs about 5.6 t/m2 of 4340-class steel (5C); a 16 km cylinder with O'Neill's 8 t/m2 and 300,000 psi steel passes the stress check (5C) but requires the rotating shell to turn on near-frictionless bearings against a fixed structure, which no source here shows (sibling; O'Neill's own assumption). Fiber composite along the fibers allows a much larger radius, but a pressure vessel is stressed in two directions and composite pressure shells of that size, with 64 MPa transverse strength (D1), are not demonstrated.
6. **Free electrical power per person at O'Neill's 120 kW.** The collector area at ISS array performance (64 W/m2) is about 1,900 m2 per person, plus the same heat to radiate; real habitats read here use 5-15 kW per person (C1). This is not a physics violation, but it is a very large infrastructure assumption.
7. **Printed or sintered regolith as the pressure shell.** Measured strengths are 5-22 MPa for small pieces (G2); a pressurized shell needs a continuous membrane in tension; the ESA design uses an inflatable inside the printed shell. A pressurized printed dome without a liner is not shown.
8. **Regolith shielding that is cheap.** In-situ mass avoids launch, but moving, placing and holding hundreds to thousands of kilograms per square meter requires machines, power and time (G4, 5F); no source gives a placement rate or energy for loose-fill shielding.
9. **Dust-free interiors.** Apollo dust disabled suit bearings in three days and entered the cabin; layered defenses reduce but are not shown to eliminate it (G1).
10. **A radiator-free or low-temperature heat sink.** Not a handwave but a limit: a 300 K surface radiates at most 459 W/m2 per face (5B). Any design that moves the heat of tens of kilowatts per person without large radiators, or hot radiator fluid, breaks the Stefan-Boltzmann law.
11. **Zero-reaction-mass spin-up or despin.** Angular momentum must go somewhere: reaction mass, a counter-rotating mass (SP-413's shield), an electromagnetic or tether exchange with another body, or the planet's magnetic field. There is no source for a free spin-up (5E).
