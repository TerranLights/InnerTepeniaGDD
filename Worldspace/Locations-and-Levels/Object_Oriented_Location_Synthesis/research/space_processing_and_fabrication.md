## Scope

Processing of lunar regolith, metal-rich and carbonaceous asteroid material, volatiles, and hydrogen and helium into
construction materials; fabrication of structures in orbit; fabrication of human-shaped machines (structure,
actuators, batteries, wiring, sensors, computing) from space materials; electronics manufacture in space; plant power
and mass budgets; mass budgets of large orbital structures.

**Plain statement of the bottleneck (from the rows above).** The structure (aluminum, iron, steel, titanium), wiring (aluminum), glass and
ceramic parts, and solar cells of a human-shaped machine can in principle be made from lunar or asteroid bulk materials,
with demonstrations only at gram-to-kilogram laboratory scale. The parts that cannot be supplied from bulk space
material at any demonstrated scale are: (1) **integrated circuits**: no lithography, no clean room and no
semiconductor-grade wafer has been made in space; the tools are of order 100 t each; (2) **permanent-magnet rare
earths**: about a thousand metric tons of asteroid material or about a tonne of the richest lunar rock per robot, with no
demonstrated separation chemistry in space; (3) **lithium**: of order a hundred metric tons of carbonaceous asteroid
material per 2 kWh battery. Cobalt, nickel and copper are not the bottleneck where meteoritic metal is available.

## Conflicts

- **C1. Carbothermal g O per kWh: 0.35 vs 1.45 (2010 test) and 0.28-0.51 vs 10.77-15.79 (CaRD).** [CaRD] reports energy *arriving at
  the primary mirror* (0.35 g/kWh in 2010; 0.28-0.51 g/kWh in the 2025 sea-level tests). [AZ24] reports energy *delivered to the
  reactor* (1.45 g/kWh for 2010; 13.42 ambient and 11.53, 15.79, 10.77 g/kWh in vacuum for CaRD, the vacuum runs heated by laser).
  The difference is the energy basis (incident sunlight through about 33-60% optical efficiency, and weather) plus the heating
  source. Neither includes the CO-to-O2 step, which [CaRD] adds to reach 7.44-7.65 g O2 per kWh of sunlight on the Moon.
- **C2. Ilmenite residue per tonne of oxygen.** [LEW90]: 4.5 t Fe and 5.7 t rutile per t O. Stoichiometry of FeTiO3 -> Fe + TiO2 + O
  gives 3.49 t Fe and 4.99 t TiO2 per t O (D3). Lewis's numbers are about 29% and 14% high; possibly per tonne of O2 from water
  electrolysis with losses, or an arithmetic slip; the report does not show the basis. Use stoichiometry.
- **C3. Lunar solar-cell output per area.** Freundlich and Ignatiev: under 50 g of dopant for "about 1 km2 of solar cells (2-4 MW)".
  Their own 3-6% efficiency and the 2-4 W per 25.6 cm cell imply about 41-82 MW per km2 in full sun at 1 AU (D6). The "2-4 MW" is
  low by about 20 times unless it refers to about 0.05 km2, or to night-averaged and degraded output; the report does not say.
- **C4. Wake Shield Facility diameter and first flight label.** NASA SP-4225 STS-60 page: "2-foot-diameter"; UH SVEC: "12-foot";
  UH CAM: "4 m". 12 ft = 3.66 m, so SVEC and CAM agree within rounding and the SP-4225 "2-foot" is very likely a dropped digit.
  UH SVEC labels the February 1994 flight "STS-69"; SP-4225 places WSF-1 on STS-60 (3-11 Feb 1994).
- **C5. CP-2255 growth claim.** The text says output could reach about one million tons per year "in just 10 years"; its own
  Table 5.2 reaches 1.24 Mt/yr at 10 *working* years = 20 calendar years, and only for the "fast-exponential" case (simple doubling
  gives 51,200 t/yr at 20 calendar years). The first year is set-up with no replication.
- **C6. SPS satellite mass.** The same 1978 report gives 34 and 51 million kg (GaAlAs and silicon options, with 25% contingency) in
  its mass statement and 37.2 x 10^6 kg as "representative" in the transport section, noting the mass "is subject to continual
  updating". Different stages of the same design.
- **C7. Humanoid NdFeB mass.** CRU: about 2-3 kg; blog and investor summaries (not used): 2-4.5 kg and about 3.5 kg for one model.
  Differences in joint count (fingers with or without magnet motors) and motor sizing; none of these sources shows a teardown.

## Constraints for any inhabitant

- **Every kilogram of oxygen or metal made locally costs tens to about a hundred kilowatt-hours** (24 kWh/kg LOX by ilmenite reduction;
  about 34 kWh(th)/kg O2 carbothermal; about 104-135 kWh/kg by the one large MRE run; about 31 kWh/kg of aluminum at SP-413 scale;
  D1-D5, D11). A settlement's industry is first a power-and-radiator problem: the SP-413 refinery rejects 127 MW of process heat
  through 102,400 m2 of radiators.
- **Processing plants pay back their own mass in months to a year at best, on paper** (0.14 for MRE and for the SP-413 aluminum
  refinery, 0.31 for the bootstrap set, 1.0 for the 1980 replicator), but the only metal fabrication flown so far runs at about
  28 kg of plant per kg/yr (D7). The gap between design and demonstration is two orders of magnitude.
- **Most of a habitat's mass is unprocessed shielding** (98% of the SP-413 torus, D13); local industry's largest job is moving and
  packing raw regolith, not refining.
- **Lunar oxygen yields are a few percent of the regolith moved** (2.3 wt% in the MRE run; 1.9-2.2 wt% by H2 reduction of the
  richest mare soil), so oxygen production means tens of metric tons of soil handled per tonne of product.
- **Electronics, rare-earth magnets and lithium cannot be made locally at any demonstrated scale.** Each human-shaped machine
  embodies magnet rare earths equivalent to about a thousand metric tons of carbonaceous asteroid material, or at least half a tonne of
  the richest lunar rock, and battery lithium equivalent to about a hundred metric tons of asteroid material (D8, D9). Any settlement
  that builds such machines depends on an outside supply of chips, magnets and lithium (or on recycling) until a chip fab and
  rare-earth separation exist locally; the studies place that 4-10% "vitamin" import at the heart of every self-supply plan.
- **A chip fab is itself a very large, clean, dust-free import**: one lithography tool alone is over 150 t (Intel 2024), and the
  bootstrap model puts lithography in the fifth machine generation after dust-free buildings are built.
- **Human-shaped machines built locally would be heavier and cruder at first** (Gen 2.0 hardware 2.5 times heavier, "mongrel
  alloys"; aluminum wiring with basalt-fiber insulation in the 1980 design, copper being scarce on the Moon).
- **Hydrogen and helium provide energy, reaction mass, cooling, lift and reducing agent only**; they cannot become structure or
  electronics (section 7). A gas-giant-sourced supply relieves the hydrogen scarcity that limits lunar closure but adds no solids.
- **Welding and printing in vacuum change the process**: no convective cooling, metal vapor plating onto nearby surfaces, spatter
  becoming orbital debris, sealed inert-gas boxes for printing, and electron-beam x-rays near operators.

## Candidate handwaves

Flagged, not accepted; the developer decides.

1. **Making integrated circuits in space at useful scale before the setting provides a full fab chain.** No lithography, clean room,
   or wafer process has been demonstrated in space (section 4); the only modeled path (MET13) needs five machine generations and
   Earth-made chips to build the first lithography tools. A design in which local factories turn out robot brains' supporting
   electronics early would bend the demonstrated engineering, though not physics.
2. **Locally sourced rare-earth magnets in quantity.** The physics allows it, but the processing mass (about 1,000 t of CI material
   or about a tonne of the richest lunar rock per robot at 100% recovery, D8) and the absence of any space separation chemistry
   make large-scale local magnet supply an extrapolation far beyond any study read. Magnet-free motors (induction, switched
   reluctance) are a physics-compliant alternative not researched here (Open threads).
3. **Self-replicating factories reaching millions of metric tons per year within a decade.** CP-2255's own table needs 20 calendar years and the
   fast-exponential assumption (C5); the 1980 masses are "crude estimates"; no replicator of any size has been built. Treat the
   growth curves as upper bounds of a thought experiment.
4. **Plant payback ratios of about 0.1-0.3 as achieved fact.** They are design-study numbers; the flown equivalent is about 28 (D7).
