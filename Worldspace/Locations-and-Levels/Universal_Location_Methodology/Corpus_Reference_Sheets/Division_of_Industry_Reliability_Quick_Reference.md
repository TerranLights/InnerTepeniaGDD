## 2a · ⭐⭐⭐ **PRECISELY WHY AN EXPORT FIGURE FAILS** — *corrected 2026-09-16; the first draft of this sheet overstated it*

> ⚠ **`README` says *"`DRQ-09` still blocks every export figure."* That sentence is `README`'s, and it is
> looser than the evidence.** **`DRQ-09` is the DENOMINATOR question, and `16` Register item 11 settles the
> denominator for pass purposes** *(§3)*. ⇒ ***The denominator is not what stops you.***

**`export = sector − the city's own food need`. The first term is solid. The second has no settled value:**

| # | Basis for the own-need term | Effect |
|--:|---|---|
| **1** | **The old B1 rate `53`** | ⛔ **WITHDRAWN — no independent derivation** |
| **2** | **The rebuilt rate `120.7`** *(`DRQ-08` ✅ answered)* | ⭐ **2.28× larger — it moves the need term by more than a factor of two** |
| **3** | ⭐⭐ **Finding 2: baseline ALREADY staffs the city's own food need, so subtracting it again DOUBLE-BOOKS** | ⇒ **the subtraction may be zero** |

> ## ⇒ ***The spread across these three is roughly 2× on the export term for a large producer. That — not
> `DRQ-09` — is why an export headcount is not writable.***

**And *"feeds N people"* fails for two FURTHER reasons that no ruling will fix:**
- ⛔ **It is normally computed from the WHOLE-WORKFORCE sector figure** — the convention `16` explicitly does
  not use *(§3)*.
- ⛔ **Finding 3's units error:** demand is difficulty-weighted, supply is a raw headcount. **A low-`D` producer
  feeds ~15.1 people; a high-`D` producer feeds ~7.5.** **A single national people-per-producer ratio is not a
  real quantity.**

> ### ⛔ THE HONEST STATEMENT, from `10`'s own closing:
> ***"Until both are ruled, the honest statement is: the national food balance is not determined to better than
> ±15%, and the coalition structure should be reported without the 0.61%."***

# 4 · ✅ WHAT SURVIVED — the six findings that do NOT depend on the disputed arithmetic

*All three independent checkers agreed on these.*

1. ⭐⭐ **THE COALITION FINDING.** **The country is fed by a COALITION of farms and fisheries, not by any single
   city.** **It follows from the SHAPE of the canon §15 sheets, not from the contested rate.**
2. ⭐ **THE WEDDELL SEA IS AN UNCLAIMED THIRD FOOD CENTER**, binding the 31% of the national population in the
   Halley subnet.
3. **The population and workforce layer is solid** — census sums reconcile exactly, zero mismatches on 38 cities.
4. **The difficulty ladder is well-founded** — MCAA additive factors, the `1/(1−L)` conversion, the 60% cap,
   independent convergence with the Nunavut cost ladder.
5. **The selective-application step *(`08` §7.1)* is the model's best idea** — a factor applied to everything
   cancels out of composition, so applying it only to physically-touched industries makes difficulty
   **differentiate** rather than merely scale.
6. ⭐ **The strain metric's denominator is right** — `extra workers / non-food distinctive economy`, so strain
   reads as ***"how much of a city's identity you must delete to eat."*** **That framing is worth keeping even
   though the numbers around it were wrong.**
