# Review Panel — Full Text Quick Reference (Step 8 / Gate 10)

> ⭐ **Tier U — corpus-wide, identical for every city.** Feeds every city's Step 8. Extracted in full from
> `Disciplines/00f_Review_Panel.md` (832 lines), read in full 2026-09-15. **Nothing below is city-specific — it
> is the same panel roster and procedure for all 37 cities.** Only the actual casting and objections raised for
> a given city are Tier C — that's Step 8's own future content, not this sheet.

**Placement:** after Gates 0–9, before the standout is finalized — the panel frequently changes what the
standout should be. **What it is:** a review mechanism, not a generation mechanism — produces additions
(a category nobody covered), inconsistencies (two findings that can't both be true from where this person
stands), and doubts (coherent but doesn't survive contact with how this person actually lives). Reduces
single-lens bias; does not make review independent — the same author writes both the location and the panel's
objections. **⚠ The panel does not check plausibility, though it looks as though it does** — every position
asks whether the location serves *them*, from inside the fiction, already accepting its premises; a panelist
cannot object that a location is implausible, because the panelist only exists if it is. Plausibility is
Gate 11's job, run separately; a clean panel is not evidence for it.

## Panel A — the Flat Archetypes (standing panel, run all six, every time)

Weiland's Flat Archetypes are the resting states between arcs — someone who has completed a passage and lives
in its settled form. Each entry: what it **notices**, what it's **blind to**, its **standing question**.

1. **The Child** — no power, must live inside whatever adults built. Notices: whether children appear at all;
   what's forbidden and why; what's boring or frightening; what a child absorbs without being taught; anywhere
   to play. Blind to: governance, economics, history, consequence. *Asks: what am I not allowed to do here, and
   what happens if I do it anyway?*
2. **The Lover** — oriented toward one other person; wants a life with them, here. Notices: whether intimacy is
   structurally possible; privacy, courtship, where two can be alone; what the place does to a pair. Blind to:
   the collective, the institution, anyone outside the pair. *Asks: could I build a life with someone here, and
   where would we actually go?*
3. **The Parent** — responsible for dependents who cannot yet fend for themselves. Notices: provision and
   safety; who holds the children during work/crisis/ritual; schooling; whether the place is survivable for
   someone who can't give it full capacity. Blind to: high politics, prestige, anything at governance scale.
   *Asks: where are the children right now, and who has them?*
4. **The Ruler** — responsible for the whole rather than for anyone in particular. Notices: whether institutions
   could actually function; legitimacy and succession; what the place costs and who pays; resource flow
   (imports as well as exports); unadministrable rules. Blind to: individual suffering, private life, meaning.
   *Asks: who administers that, who funds it, and what happens when they stop?*
5. **The Elder** — past active contribution; holds the memory; dependent in a different way now. Notices: what
   the place does with people it can no longer use; continuity and historical drift; claims about "always" that
   don't survive someone who was there; dignity in decline. Blind to: urgency, novelty, the newcomer's
   experience. *Asks: was that actually always true — and what am I now, if I can't do that any more?*
6. **The Mentor** — transmits skill and knowledge to people who don't have it yet. Notices: whether knowledge
   actually passes; apprenticeship, qualification, the unwritten rules nobody explains; who is left unskilled.
   Blind to: anything outside transmission; the ends the skill serves. *Asks: how does someone learn this, and
   what becomes of the ones who can't?*

## Panel B — the Life Arcs (pull in when the location touches a passage)

People mid-transformation. A location that makes one of the six impossible has a real, usually unnoticed hole.
Run selectively; record the silences — a silence is itself a finding.

- **The Maiden** — leaving the parental home; individuation. *Can a young person separate here, or is there
  nowhere to go?*
- **The Hero** — proving oneself; the first real test. *What counts as proving yourself here, and who decides?*
- **The Queen** — taking up power over a domain. *How does someone come to hold responsibility, and who is
  excluded from ever doing so?*
- **The King** — sacrificing personal power for the realm. *What does this place ask its powerful to give up,
  and does anyone actually do it?*
- **The Crone** — relinquishing worldly power for inner authority. *Is there a dignified way to stop here?*
- **The Mage** — service beyond the self. *Is there anything here worth a life, and how would someone give one?*

## Panel C — the Shadows (the adversarial reviewers)

Twelve compromised forms of the six Life Arcs, a passive and an aggressive shadow each. Two standing questions
carry most of the value without needing all twelve:
- **The passive shadow** — *where can someone hide here? What can a person get away with by simply never
  acting, and how long before anyone notices?*
- **The aggressive shadow** — *what can be taken here by someone willing to be seen taking it? Who holds power
  that nobody is checking?*

| Life Arc | Passive shadow | Aggressive shadow |
|---|---|---|
| Maiden | Damsel | Vixen |
| Hero | Coward | Bully |
| Queen | Snow Queen | Sorceress |
| King | Puppet | Tyrant |
| Crone | Hermit | Witch |
| Mage | Miser | Sorcerer |

## Panel D — the four faculties and their bipolar shadows (Moore & Gillette)

Orthogonal to Panel A: Panel A is *who is looking*; Panel D is *what is being examined* — faculties a whole
thing needs to function. Used here as diagnostic axes for a place (order, action, knowledge, connection), never
as claims about gender. ⚠ **Terminology collision:** "King" and "Lover" each name both a Life Arc/Flat
Archetype (Panels A/B) and a faculty (Panel D) — always name the system: "the King *arc*" vs. "the King
*faculty*."

| Faculty | In fullness | Active shadow | Passive shadow |
|---|---|---|---|
| King | order, blessing, generativity, stewardship of the whole | The Tyrant | The Weakling |
| Warrior | disciplined action, boundary, transpersonal devotion | The Sadist | The Masochist |
| Magician | knowledge, technique, insight, containment | The Manipulator | The Denying "Innocent" One |
| Lover | connection, aliveness, sensuality, vision | The Addict | The Impotent Lover |

**The four standing questions:**
- **The King** — *does this place bless anyone?* Who is seen and valued here, by what mechanism, and who is
  never noticed at all? A place with no blessing mechanism has a real hole even when everything else works.
- **The Warrior** — *can this place act, and can it stop?* What can this place decide and execute; what will it
  refuse; where does its detachment sit? (Detachment risks the demon of cruelty.)
- **The Magician** — *who knows, and how does knowing pass?* Where is the specialist knowledge, who holds it,
  how does anyone else get it?
- **The Lover** — *is this place alive, and could anyone love it?* Where is the beauty, the pleasure, the joy —
  would a resident say they loved living here? **This is the question the rest of the gate set never asks, and
  the most likely single omission in a place built from structure outward.**

**The eight shadow questions:**
- **Tyrant** — what does this place attack because it is new?
- **Weakling** — where does this place abdicate; where does authority exist on paper with nobody exercising it?
- **Sadist** — where is hardness admired past the point of function?
- **Masochist** — where does this place absorb harm and call it virtue?
- **Manipulator** — who withholds what people need to know for their own well-being?
- **Denying "Innocent" One** — what does this place refuse to know that it already knows?
- **Addict** — what is this place lost in, pursuing intensity past function?
- **Impotent Lover** — what has gone dead here? (Ask of any place that reads as competent and joyless.)

## Panel E — the immature precursors (the developmental axis)

What each faculty looks like before it matures: the Divine Child becomes the King, the Precocious Child becomes
the Magician, the Oedipal Child becomes the Lover, and the Hero becomes the Warrior. Each has its own bipolar
shadow.

| Faculty | Immature precursor | Active shadow | Passive shadow |
|---|---|---|---|
| King | The Divine Child | The High Chair Tyrant | The Weakling Prince |
| Magician | The Precocious Child | The Know-It-All Trickster | The Dummy |
| Lover | The Oedipal Child | The Mama's Boy | The Dreamer |
| Warrior | The Hero | The Grandstander Bully | The Coward |

**Two mechanics:** (1) **The poles switch, and the switch is the tell** — a place that oscillates between
grandiosity and helplessness is not inconsistent, it is one condition at two settings. (2) **The immature layer
is never demolished, only built over** — a mature place still contains its founding-era immaturity, and that
layer is still generating; write a developed place as having built over its origins, not outgrown them.

**The review questions:**
- **The Divine Child (pre-King)** — does this place think it is owed, by right of what it is rather than what
  it does?
- **The Precocious Child (pre-Magician)** — is knowledge here used to understand, or to rank? Who punctures
  inflation, and is anyone allowed to?
- **The Oedipal Child (pre-Lover)** — what is this place still bound to; does it measure everything against
  something idealized and unattainable?
- **The Hero (pre-Warrior)** — **the most useful of the four.** What limitation does this place refuse to
  acknowledge, and what will call it to account? The test for actual maturity: knowing one's limitations, and
  getting the help needed.

## §5 — Composition: the four-corner check on the panel itself

Before accepting a run, confirm the objections came from genuinely different directions. If three positions all
raise versions of the same complaint, that's one objection wearing three hats — the panel has collapsed to a
single lens. Convergence from genuinely opposed corners (e.g. the Elder and the Child, who share nothing) is
the strongest signal available that a gap is real.

## §6 — Casting

The archetype is the position; the casting is the costume — cast each as somebody concrete for the location, so
a concrete person notices concrete things.

| Archetype | Cast as (example) | The question, in costume |
|---|---|---|
| Child | a resident's kid, too young to work | what am I not allowed to touch? |
| Lover | a courting pair with nowhere private | where do we go? |
| Parent | a worker on rotation with a dependent | who has my child during a crisis? |
| Ruler | a quartermaster, administrator, or shift boss | where does it come from, and who settles up? |
| Elder | a worker past their peak, or a long-lived robot | was that always true? |
| Mentor | a trades examiner or an old hand | how does someone learn this? |

**Two standing standpoints, kept across every location** (cast as any archetype — the value is the position):
- **The Neighbor From Across the Line** — a resident of the place this one is set against. Biased by design,
  and the bias is the instrument.
- **The Passer-Through** — crosses in a single day and never returns. The discoverability check — if they see
  the shadow, it's been written as the surface; if they see nothing, the place has no legible face. Both are
  failures, opposite ones.

## §7 — Rules

- **Rule 0 — a negative result is a result**, not a discard. Three informative kinds: a shadow question
  returning "not guilty" (exonerates the location on a specific charge); a Life Arc returning "yes, trivially"
  (characterizes as sharply as a silence); a plain silence.
- **Rule 1 — a position with nothing to say must say nothing.** Manufacturing an objection to fill a slot is a
  failure mode, exactly like padding Gate 1. A position declining to speak because another already covered the
  ground is correct behavior, not a gap.
- **Rule 2 — reviewers need not be sympathetic, fair, or correct.** The Neighbor is biased, the shadows are
  hostile, the Elder will insist on things that aren't true. Their objections are data about how the place is
  experienced, not verdicts on whether it is right.
- **Rule 3 — a position is not guaranteed to get what it wants**, and forcing the place to provide it is the
  single most damaging thing this panel can do. Before accepting any objection: *would satisfying this make the
  location more like the others? If yes, it is almost certainly `unmet`* (or `declined` — see the dispositions
  below). A want the location characteristically would not and should not satisfy is a finding about the
  location, written as characterization, not as a gap to close.

## §8 — How to run it

1. Cast the six Flat Archetypes, then read the finished file once per standing question (not six full re-reads).
2. Add the Passer-Through and the Neighbor, always.
3. Run the Lover's question every time — *is this place alive, and could anyone love it?*
4. Pull in Life Arcs, Faculties and Shadows where the material touches them; record their silences.
5. Apply the four-corner check (§5) before accepting the run.
6. Record only real objections, in the position's own voice and uncensored, then decide each explicitly (see
   the six dispositions below).
7. Panel-derived material becomes a normal Finding, tagged for provenance — or strengthens an existing one,
   which is an equally good outcome.

**Output block**, appended after the QA block:
```
## Review Panel (Phase_Instructions/00f_Review_Panel.md)

**Run YYYY-MM-DD.** Flat Archetypes cast as: Child = …, Lover = …, …
- **The Parent** (cast as …) — <objection> · *inconsistency* → accepted, Finding NN
- **The Child** (cast as …) — <objection> · *addition* → **unmet** — the location would not provide this; recorded as characterization in Finding NN
- **The Lover** — no comment.
- **The Passer-Through** — <what they saw> → PASS / fails on <which mode>
**Life Arcs consulted:** … **Silences recorded:** …
**Refereed conflicts:** <position> vs <position> → settlement …
**Four-corner check:** <did the objections come from different directions?>
**Panel-derived findings:** NN, NN.
**Standing objections not resolved:** …
```

## The six dispositions, defined in full

- **accepted** — write it.
- **noted** — real but deferred; say why.
- **rejected** — say why; a position being wrong is a normal and useful result.
- **refereed** — when two panelists conflict and both are right, do not choose: state what each is right about,
  where each overreaches, and write the finding as the settlement.
- **unmet** — the location knowingly protects this and would refuse to surrender it. Diagnostic about the
  place's self-knowledge; a low count means the location's problems are absences it does not know it has.
- **declined** — the pass refuses the objection on anti-homogenization grounds, but the location has no
  awareness with which to protect anything: the want is real, satisfying it would make the place more like its
  siblings, and nobody here would defend the refusal because nobody has noticed there's something to defend.
  Write it as characterization exactly as Rule 3 requires — *the location has no concept of X, and here is what
  that does to a life* — but never count it as self-knowledge; collapsing `unmet` and `declined` destroys the
  signal this split exists to provide.

## §9 — Known limits

- Not independent review — better-directed attention, not a second opinion.
- The roster transfers across settings and media unchanged; only the casting changes.
- Six mandatory positions is the practical ceiling for attentive use — the Arcs and Shadows are a bench, not a
  checklist; running all twenty-four on every location produces shallower passes, not broader coverage.
- `unmet` should be common; a location that satisfies every position has no character. Expect several `unmet`
  results, clustering on the positions the location is worst equipped for — exactly where its identity is
  sharpest.
- Positions drift toward the author's sympathies over repeated use — the Neighbor and the shadows are most
  prone to softening, since both are written to be unwelcome; check periodically that they still bite.
