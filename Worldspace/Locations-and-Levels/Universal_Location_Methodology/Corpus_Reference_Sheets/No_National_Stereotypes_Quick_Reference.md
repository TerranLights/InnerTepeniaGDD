## The rule itself

A city's real-world **station-builder** nation is a GPS coordinate only. It locates the city on the map. It
must never be used as a reason, cause, or explanation for anything else — not present-day culture, not a
faction's internal conflict, not a character's temperament, not a creative-inspiration pick, not a war strike
target. **Self-check:** if a sentence's meaning would change were the station-builder nation swapped for a
different one, the sentence violates the rule. **Who founded each city is a separate question, answered only by
the Founding Register, on geography and access (`DR-19`).**

**Why it's necessary, not a style preference:** the Falkland Treaty was signed 21 June 2564. We are, today,
closer to Shakespeare's birth (1564) than to that date. Expecting a city's present culture to resemble its
station-builder's real-world stereotypes is exactly that implausible. **Sharper still:** the founding
generation itself had zero living connection to the station-builder — real stations mostly date to 1957–58;
even the oldest (Orcadas, 1904) is ~600–660 years short of 2564. The station's real national origin was
already ancient history on day one of Tepenia's own existence.

**The mechanism across the gap:** most stations were continuously maintained by a rotating succession of
different national operators, handed off repeatedly across the First Interwar (2083–2564) — **which nations
held it, in what order, is explicitly irrelevant and should never be specified.** No institutional knowledge
or tradition survives that chain; but records, research, results, equipment and techniques CAN cross the gap
(`DR-25`, `DR-26`) — exiles can learn from and carry on that material without being "taught by a living
institution." *(Two deliberate exceptions, not defaults: Byrd fell out of the chain and was abandoned; Zhongshan
stayed under continuous Chinese/Sinian habitation, given the Sinian Federation's immediate geographic and
time-zone access (`DR-21`) — reach for an exception only where a city already has its own separate, established
reason.)*

## ⛔⛔⛔ THE AMBIGUITY THAT CAUSED REAL OVER-APPLICATION — read before applying the rule

**THIS RULE GOVERNS THE STATION'S BUILDER. IT DOES NOT GOVERN THE EXILE POPULATION'S ORIGIN.** Two different
questions. Where they name the same nation, that comes from geography and access, never from the station.

| | Station-builder nation | Exile-population origin |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | who physically built the station, centuries before the Treaty | who actually settled the city in 2564 and lives there |
| Established by | real-world history | the Founding Register, on geography and access (`DR-19`); the census gives the composition |
| Status | ⛔⛔ GPS coordinate only | ✅ ADMISSIBLE, once composition is established |

> **The law exists so a city is characterized by who lives there, not by whose station it occupies. It is a
> rule about PROVENANCE, not a rule against ethnicity.** A pass that refuses all ethnic material is
> misreading it, and will produce placeless cities.

## The sequencing rule

**BEFORE composition is established:** may not infer who lives somewhere from national character or
stereotype — the heart of the prohibition, untouched. **AFTER composition is established:** origin-ethnicities
and ethnic-cultures may be taken into consideration — the populations are canon; what they carry is then in
scope.

## The divergence principle — composition names the STOCK; time and place produce the CULTURE

Not "build culture from nothing," and not "transplant the source culture intact." Take the origin culture as
starting stock, then apply local divergence: **time · separation · local environmental setting · local
struggles and hardships · local goals · local sensibilities and habits.** This is an ANTI-CONVERGENCE engine —
two cities founded from the same stock must diverge, because the operator's inputs differ. *(Worked case:
Argentina founded Marambio, Esperanza and Belgrano — same stock, same elapsed time, different local conditions.
If a pass produces similarly-shaped outcomes for all three, at least one is wrong.)*

## How to apply — the operational checklist

1. The station-builder nation may appear as a bare fact about the inherited infrastructure ("the station was
   built by X", `DR-24`), and a station's name or a historical namesake may stand as a naming fact only (`DR-28`) — never
   as the *reason* something else is true. If no figure or fact is immediately apparent, don't force one. *(corrected 2026-11-18: the naming fact is `DR-28`'s, not `DR-24`'s, which covers only the bare infrastructure fact; source L160.)*
2. Build culture from: the exile community's own ORIGIN STOCK (once composition is established) + local
   circumstances (isolation, density, terrain, founding-era pressures) + the divergence operator. Never from
   whoever built the station.
3. Creative-inspiration picks are judged entirely on structural/thematic parallel — never mention shared or
   non-shared nationality with the station-builder nation, in either direction (even a defensive "no overlap" note is
   itself the violation).
4. Plot events (why a strike hit one city, why a faction formed where it did) are built from the city's own
   established facts — population, geography, infrastructure, civic role, timeline — never from its station-builder
   nation.

## Added 2026-11-18 (appended, so no earlier line moves; `00_RUNBOOK.md` Step 7 L2944 cites item 3 at L76–L78)

**From the source, not carried before** (`No_National_Stereotypes.md` L21, L23, L148–L156, L170):
- **A TRADITION cannot cross the gap: it passes person to person, and no one carried it across.** Records,
  research, results, equipment and techniques can (`DR-25`, `DR-26`); learning from a centuries-old written record
  is categorically different from being personally instructed by surviving personnel (L21).
- **Operational skill in a founding-era story** comes from **skilled exiles among the 2564 founding population**
  (people with real institutional backgrounds of their own era, never tied to the station's program), or is
  **rediscovered from the station's records, research and equipment** — never from station personnel bridging a
  600-plus-year gap (L23, L170).
- **The six recorded violations** (L148–L156): (1) explaining a city's culture or a faction's conflict by
  station-builder-nation stereotype; (2) a bare "Being [Nationality]." or "[Nationality] Tepenian city" identity
  claim in a summary line; (3) picking, rejecting or even neutrally mentioning whether an inspiration pick shares a
  nationality with the station-builder, even a defensive "no overlap" note; (4) framing a practice (an institution,
  cuisine, music, art) as inherited from the station-builder nation rather than developed by the exile community
  (an echo of a real tradition is only coincidence, never the explanation; a practice from the exile community's
  OWN origin stock is admissible once composition is established, with the divergence operator applied);
  (5) using the station-builder nation to explain a strategic, military or narrative decision; (6) writing a
  founding history as if the station's original personnel, command structure or institutional continuity
  survived the gap to the 2564 exiles.

**Rulings since the 2026-09-15 read** (`DEVELOPER_RULINGS_LOG.md` L2034, `DR-140` items 2 and 3, every city):

- **No national music form:** music, robots' included, is regional, by the cultural regions now called subnets,
  and is derived after the DCI.
