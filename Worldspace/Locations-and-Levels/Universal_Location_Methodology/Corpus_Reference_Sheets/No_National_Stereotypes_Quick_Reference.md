# Tier U Reference — No National Stereotypes (GPS Law)

> **Corpus-wide binding rule, identical for every city, every project.** Source:
> `Reference/No_National_Stereotypes.md` (172 lines), read in full 2026-09-15.

## The rule itself

A city's real-world **station-builder** nation is a GPS coordinate only. It locates the city on the map. It
must never be used as a reason, cause, or explanation for anything else — not present-day culture, not a
faction's internal conflict, not a character's temperament, not a creative-inspiration pick, not a war strike
target. **Self-check:** if a sentence's meaning would change were the founding nation swapped for a different
one, the sentence violates the rule.

**Why it's necessary, not a style preference:** the Falkland Treaty was signed 21 June 2564. We are, today,
closer to Shakespeare's birth (1564) than to that date. Expecting a city's present culture to resemble its
station-builder's real-world stereotypes is exactly that implausible. **Sharper still:** the founding
generation itself had zero living connection to the station-builder — real stations mostly date to 1957–58;
even the oldest (Orcadas, 1904) is ~600–660 years short of 2564. The station's real national origin was
already ancient history on day one of Tepenia's own existence.

**The mechanism across the gap:** most stations were continuously maintained by a rotating succession of
different national operators, handed off repeatedly across the First Interwar (2083–2564) — **which nations
held it, in what order, is explicitly irrelevant and should never be specified.** No institutional knowledge
survives that chain; but preserved journals, logs, and orientation manuals are artifacts, not living
institutions, and CAN cross the gap — exiles can learn from a centuries-old written record without that being
"taught by a living institution." *(Two deliberate exceptions, not defaults: Byrd fell out of the chain and was
abandoned; Zhongshan's Jeju-do allocation gives it continuous single-population habitation instead — reach for
an exception only where a city already has its own separate, established reason.)*

## ⛔⛔⛔ THE AMBIGUITY THAT CAUSED REAL OVER-APPLICATION — read before applying the rule

**THIS RULE GOVERNS THE STATION'S BUILDER. IT DOES NOT GOVERN THE EXILE POPULATION'S ORIGIN.** Two different
nations, and for most cities they differ.

| | Station-builder nation | Exile-population origin |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | who physically built the station, centuries before the Treaty | who actually settled the city in 2564 and lives there |
| Established by | real-world history | canon: the census, tier tables, per-city founding rulings |
| Status | ⛔⛔ GPS coordinate only | ✅ ADMISSIBLE, once composition is established |

> **The law exists so a city is characterized by who lives there, not by whose station it occupies. It is a
> rule about PROVENANCE, not a rule against ethnicity.** A pass that refuses all ethnic material is
> misreading it, and will produce placeless cities.

## The sequencing rule

**BEFORE composition is established:** may not infer who lives somewhere from national character or
stereotype — the heart of the prohibition, untouched. **AFTER composition is established:** origin-ethnicities
and ethnic-cultures may be taken into consideration — the populations are canon; what they carry is then in
scope.

## The divergence principle — composition names the STOCK; time and place produce the CULTURE

Not "build culture from nothing," and not "transplant the source culture intact." Take the origin culture as
starting stock, then apply local divergence: **time · separation · local environmental setting · local
struggles and hardships · local goals · local sensibilities and habits.** This is an ANTI-CONVERGENCE engine —
two cities founded from the same stock must diverge, because the operator's inputs differ. *(Worked case:
Shirayuki, Japan 36.27%, and Sayowa, Japan diluted to 2.71% — same stock, same elapsed time, radically
different local conditions. If a pass produces similarly-shaped outcomes for both, at least one is wrong.)*

## How to apply — the operational checklist

1. The founding nation may appear as a bare fact (tier tables, "the station was founded by X," a naming
   choice) — never as the *reason* something else is true.
2. Build culture from: the exile community's own ORIGIN STOCK (once composition is established) + local
   circumstances (isolation, density, terrain, founding-era pressures) + the divergence operator. Never from
   whoever built the station.
3. Creative-inspiration picks are judged entirely on structural/thematic parallel — never mention shared or
   non-shared nationality with the founding nation, in either direction (even a defensive "no overlap" note is
   itself the violation).
4. Plot events (why a strike hit one city, why a faction formed where it did) are built from the city's own
   established facts — population, geography, infrastructure, civic role, timeline — never from its founding
   nation.
