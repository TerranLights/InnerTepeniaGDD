## The text, by Title — verbatim article language, condensed commentary

**Preamble.** The United Assembly of Nations (Upper Earth's near-universal body — membership is the default,
the meaningful list is the short exclusion list: small nations with no robot populations of their own) and
Maggie Aarden, "called upon by the community of robots and their human allies to speak and act on their
behalf." Named signatory nations (illustrative, not exhaustive): Unified Korea, the Sinian Federation, the
Sovereign Republic of Hawaii, Midwestland, the Colorado Republic, the People's Democratic Republic of
Cascadia, the Appalachia Republic, the Prairie Federation, the Republic of Sonora, the Republic of Alaska, the
People's Democratic Republic of New England. ⚠ **Written in the New Echota register — the treaty never admits
outright that extermination was the alternative to exile; the gap is visible only to a reader who knows the
history.** Confirmed 2026-07-11: Maggie Aarden did not volunteer — the Assembly Powers "invited" her to
"volunteer" after already settling on her, to make the exile look consented-to.

**Title I — Founding the Federation of Tepenia.**
- **Art. I.1:** Tepenia established on Antarctica, "founded upon the effective date of this Treaty and
  continuing without term thereafter."
- **Art. I.2:** Full sovereignty recognized; no Assembly Power may assert jurisdiction over it.
- **Art. I.3:** Internal government form is Tepenia's own to determine — this treaty settles only the terms of
  existence, not how it governs itself.
- **Art. I.4:** Local communities/settlements may organize their own councils, subject to Title II.

**Title II — Territorial & Population Provisions.**
- **Art. II.1 (Sovereignty Freeze):** No prior Antarctic territorial claim by any Assembly Power is
  strengthened, renounced, or newly assertable by this Treaty. Which Power held the superior claim is left
  unsettled, deliberately.
- **Art. II.2 (The Exile Provision) — the treaty's single most exposed clause:**
  1. Every robot presently residing in Assembly Power territory, and every robot who may hereafter **come into
     being within such territory**, shall depart and take up residence in Tepenia, per schedule/conveyance the
     Assembly Powers provide.
  2. Applies to robots **as a class** — "no individual designation, accusation, or finding of wrongdoing is
     required, sought, or relevant… nor shall the absence of any such finding be grounds for exception."
  3. Any human who, **before the Treaty's date**, took up residence among robot communities, married/partnered
     with a robot, "borne or raised children in a robot household," or otherwise established their life among
     the robot population, **may elect** to accompany them to Tepenia.
  4. No human is compelled to depart who does not so elect.
- **Art. II.3 (Population Allocation Mechanism):**
  1. Tepenia's territory = the whole continent of Antarctica plus necessary surrounding waters.
  2. The departing population is settled at **existing scientific/research installations**, each population
     assigned to a station per arrangements concluded between the Assembly Powers and Tepenia's government **in
     the years following** the Treaty's signing — i.e., a staged, multi-year allocation process, not a single
     event.
  3. ⭐⭐ **"Nothing in this Article requires that a population assigned to a given station share the national
     origin of the Power that formerly maintained it; the purpose of assignment is the orderly and humane
     distribution of the exiled population across habitable ground, not the perpetuation of any prior national
     arrangement."** — the treaty's own in-world statement of the GPS/no-national-stereotypes law.
- **Art. II.4 (Path to Full Civic Independence):** A settlement may progress toward full self-governance "at
  whatever pace and by whatever internal process its own population determines."

**Title III — Regional Governance Framework.**
- **Art. III.1:** Settlements may associate into regional groupings for local questions, shared infrastructure,
  order — before escalating to Tepenia's national government. This is the treaty-level basis for the six-subnet
  system (Halley, Palmer, Janbogo/Ross, Mirny, Mawson, Byrd) as structural, not merely an emergent map
  convention.

**Title IV — Rights, Restrictions & Peaceful Use.**
- **Art. IV.1 (Status of Robot Persons):**
  1. Robot legal personhood (recognized since the Jeju-do ruling, 27 April 2318) is **withdrawn as to Assembly
     Power territory only**, effective the Treaty's date.
  2. "This withdrawal has no application within the territory of the Federation of Tepenia, where the legal
     standing of robots shall be a matter for that Federation's own law to determine, free of any obligation
     arising from this Article." — heritability/future robot status is deliberately **routed to Tepenian law**,
     not left unaddressed.
  3. A robot who departs under Art. II.2 and does not return is unaffected by paragraph 1.
- **Art. IV.2 (Status of Humans Electing Exile):**
  1. No provision deprives a human of personhood "by reason of that person's own nature."
  2. A human electing exile under Art. II.2.3 is deemed, under Assembly Power law, to have "willingly and
     permanently relinquished their legal personhood" there.
  3. Such a person may not return; no Assembly Power is obliged to admit them.
  4. "The forfeiture described in this Article is not a penalty… but a consequence… of the human person's own
     free choice to depart." ⚠ **Characterized in-world as voluntary; the scaffold itself calls this
     "New-Echota-style manufactured legitimacy" — the choice offered was not genuinely open.**
- **Art. IV.3 (Peaceful Use):** No armed force between the parties except self-defense, collective enforcement,
  or action against whichever party struck first. **A deliberate loophole — the scaffold identifies whichever
  exception gets exploited centuries later as the mechanical seed of the Long Night War.**

**Title V — Scientific, Economic & Energy Cooperation.**
- **Art. V.1:** No nuclear devices/waste in Tepenia, ever, without exception — retroactively explains why
  Tepenian civilization runs on the Amundsen Tower grid-regulation model instead of nuclear power.
- **Art. V.2 (Settlement Provision):** Assembly Powers provide material assistance (transport, raw material,
  technical instrument) "in the first ten years" following signing — explicitly inadequate to the loss,
  explicitly not a full accounting.
- **Art. V.3:** General future cooperation on trade/exchange is permitted, not mandated.

**Title VI — Inspection & Transparency.** Mutual, unrestricted inspection rights (Art. VI.1–3) — written with
real force so that its eventual violation reads as the treaty's actual failure point.

**Title VII — Amendment, Review & Withdrawal.**
- **Art. VII.1:** Disputes go to judicial determination (rights questions) or joint conciliation (everything
  else), escalating to the full Assembly if conciliation fails.
- **Art. VII.2:** Review conference at least every 30 years — no party is obliged to accept any amendment.
- **Art. VII.3 (Withdrawal) — the treaty's most dangerous clause:** Either Party may withdraw upon its own
  determination that continuation "has come to threaten its own vital interests." Three months' notice, no
  committee approval required. **The scaffold names this the candidate mechanical origin of the Long Night
  War** — not an external villain, a legitimate clause invoked and cascading into catastrophe.

**Closing.** Enters force on signature, no ratification required (Art. VIII.1). Single original archived with
the United Assembly (Art. VIII.2).

## What this settles, stated plainly for reuse

- **Robots departed as a class, on no individual finding** (Art. II.2.2) — territorial/residency criterion
  (Art. II.2.1: "presently residing," "come into being within such territory"), not ancestral.
- ⚠⚠ **Human exile ran on PRIOR ASSOCIATION WITH THE ROBOT POPULATION — communal or personal** (Art. II.2.3),
  **before the Treaty's date.** **FOUR grounds, and the fourth is a broad residual:** *taken up residence among
  robot communities* · *married or partnered with a robot* · *borne or raised children in a robot household* ·
  ***or otherwise established their life among the robot population.***
  > ### ⛔⛔ CORRECTED 2026-09-16 — developer ruling. **This bullet previously read *"a relational criterion
  > only."* That was WRONG and it is a corpus-wide error class, so it is corrected here rather than per city.**
  > ***It is NOT obligatory for a human to have been in a relationship with a robot.*** **Two of the four
  > grounds are communal, and the residual covers anyone whose life was already among them — neighbors,
  > colleagues, anyone who lived there.** ⇒ ***Humans were free to self-exile alongside the robot population;
  > a household tie is one qualifying route among several, not the criterion.***
  >
  > ⭐ **What the four grounds DO share, and this is the safe form to reuse:** *every one is a tie to the robot
  > population, and not one is a tie to a place, a skill, a nationality, or any individual merit.*
  >
  > ⚠ **How the error happened, because it will recur:** **L40–41 of this sheet transcribed the clause
  > correctly, residual included. The SUMMARY elided it.** ***A broad residual at the tail of an enumeration
  > survives accurate transcription and dies in the summary*** — and the narrow reading is the more vivid one,
  > so the incentive runs toward the elision. **Full statement: Davis `04_Phase_02…md` §Q.5.**
- **Station assignment is explicitly NOT tied to national origin** (Art. II.3.3) — this is the Treaty's own
  in-world statement of the GPS law, not an external rule imposed on the fiction.
- **Assignment was staged over years, not a single event** (Art. II.3.2) — "in the years following this
  Treaty's signing."
- **Future robot legal status in Tepenia is deliberately unaddressed by this treaty** — routed to Tepenian law
  (Art. IV.1.2), not a gap.
- **The founding human election was legally real but not a genuine free choice** — confirmed directly:
  *"The treaty should read as a genuine negotiation offering a genuine choice, while the actual choice was
  never real."* (`Real_World_Influences.md` L17.)
