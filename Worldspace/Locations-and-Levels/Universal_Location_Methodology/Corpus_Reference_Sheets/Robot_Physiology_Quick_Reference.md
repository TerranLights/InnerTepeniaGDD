## The ordinary condition — read this first, always

**No respiratory system; operate in vacuum, including outer hulls, without protective equipment.** Appearance:
human-looking, *Ex Machina*-style — synthetic/cybernetic interiors under realistic skin at hands/face/ears.
**Clothing is ordinary, near-universal, functional AND fashionable** — *"a robot may wear a heavy winter coat
to protect from the cold... she just needs to be prepared for the weather, just like humans do."* Winter
gloves too, same logic. **Insulation vs. dexterity trades off in gloves exactly as it does for a human hand** —
a real, gradient constraint on outdoor work, never a gate.

⛔⛔ **THE GOVERNING RULE:** write a robot as *dressed or not dressed* for a condition, never as *gated by her
body*. Exposure is a preparation problem, the same category it is for a human — not a species-level limit.

## What cold actually costs — `Robot_Cold_Physiology.md`, in full

**Altitude costs a robot nothing. Cold costs a great deal, on completely different axes than it costs a
human.**

- **Recharging is the primary cost.** Charging below freezing causes lithium plating — **permanent** capacity
  loss. Safe cold charging: 5–10% of capacity rate, **10–20× slower**. Three-way trade-off: warm cells first
  (spends reserve) · charge cold slow (10–20× time, exposed) · charge cold fast (permanent damage, emergency
  only).
- **The death spiral:** below some charge threshold, a robot no longer has enough energy to warm itself enough
  to charge safely — **cannot recover alone.** This is why warmed charging shelters are infrastructure, not
  hospitality; why "properly equipped" has a concrete meaning; why a stranded robot is genuinely stranded.
- **Two separate penalties, same resource:** capacity reduction (reversible — warm up and it returns; −20–30%
  near freezing, down to 50–60% at −20 °C) vs. plating damage (permanent, cumulative, one bad charging
  decision). Plus **output sag** — peak deliverable power falls under cold even with charge remaining, a third,
  independent effect.
- **Mechanical effects:** embrittlement below the ductile-to-brittle transition (a dent becomes a crack);
  lubricant failure below ~−40 °C (seizure becomes a real failure mode); elastomer glass transition (coolant
  leaks).
- ⭐⭐ **THE INVERSION: a robot is safer MOVING than resting.** Working generates the heat that keeps cells and
  joints in range. Stopping is the dangerous act — the exact opposite of human cold survival (shelter, still
  yourself, conserve). A cold-adapted robot practice is staged movement, no long outdoor halts, warmth budgeted
  as motion rather than hoarded as stillness.
- ⚠ **Open, not invented:** siligel's behavior at extreme cold (freezing/glass-transition point; whether
  cognition degrades before the body); numeric depletion rates (a balance question); whether plating damage is
  repairable; coolant behavior in deep cold (robots normally shed heat — in extreme cold that inverts).

## Consumption — the robot equivalents

- **Siligel (food):** maintains/repairs internal systems. **Energy replenishment is the FIRST function; repair
  is second** (ruling 2026-09-04). Composition TBD, not yet canon.
- **Coolant (drink):** thermal regulation, genuine physiological purpose — "a robot drinking coolant after hard
  work is the functional and cultural equivalent of a human drinking water after exertion." **Robot coffee:** a
  specialty formulation with distinct internal effects, confirms robots consume for pleasure, not only
  maintenance. **Glitch-coolant (alcohol):** electrolytic-compound formulation producing a "glitching" effect;
  a genuine Tepenia-wide institution that varies by city on a variety/potency axis — bohemian/cosmopolitan
  cities (variety, milder) vs. working-class settings (fewer choices, stronger effect), with a quality-tier
  variant possible within either pole.
- **Smoking:** confirmed canon, genuine (not performed) but mechanically different — robot-specific vapor
  products interacting with internal thermal/sensory systems, producing real internal state changes.
  ⭐⭐ **The hands-and-fire theory:** smoking unifies two distinctly human things — the hand (manipulator) and
  fire (harnessed energy). For robots the parallel holds with fire moving inward: the robot's own internal
  energy processing *is* the fire when vapor is drawn through thermal-management systems. **The experience is
  deliberately left ambiguous** — varies by individual, not resolved, mirrors the game's central
  robot-consciousness question.

## Downtime, leisure, and the freedom gradient

**Robots do NOT work 100% of the time.** Physical: require downtime, most prominently overnight recharging —
**a shared night**, robots and humans stopping together for different reasons. Psychological: fully conscious
and sentient, so constant work risks insanity — **leisure is a condition of remaining sane, not a perk.**
⭐⭐⭐ **The leisure history:** on Upper Earth, a robot's leisure existed only because a romantic partner
granted it — contingent, personal, revocable. **In Tepenia it is a fact of life, universal, owed to nobody —
the material proof that leaving worked.**

**Connects directly to `09_Per_City_Baseline_Run.md` §3.5's Freedom Margin finding:** half to three-quarters of
a Tepenian working life is not spoken for by survival, varying by city — **a freedom gradient drawn by
geography.** For the robot half of the population specifically, that gradient measures the thing the exile was
for.

## Human-robot relations — the baseline

**"Egalitarian, skewed in favor of robots."** Relations are overwhelmingly positive everywhere; where robots
hold an edge, it is not adversarial on either side. **Exception — human-forbidden locations:** Kunlun and Dome
Fuji, purely a safety measure (extreme altitude + cold would kill a human; robots are unaffected) — not
anti-human sentiment.

## Sexuality baseline (Concordia-established, corpus default)

Robots bisexual by default; human women bisexual, human men heterosexual (fixed-gender). Robots treat sex as
"the highest available expression of trust, faith, and commitment" — not reproduction-oriented, since robots
aren't produced sexually. Genuine internal pleasure-receptor network, not purely symbolic. Courtship customs
vary by city/individual; the underlying value (trust) doesn't.

## Robot creation, identity, and culture — the parts relevant beyond one city

- ⭐⭐⭐ **Ch. 13 — Identity is seated in city-locality, not origin, Build, or Gen/Mark.** Read in full
  2026-09-15. **The Guadalajara Analogy:** four humans of wildly different builds, all from the same city,
  are unambiguously "four Guadalajarans" — nobody reads their body types as separate peoples. The same logic
  applies to robots directly: city/culture of origin carries far more identity weight than Gen/Mark, and body
  type (Build's physical result) carries essentially none. **Build still matters, but as circumstance, not
  body type** — which local community had the interest that produced a robot, which traces back to the same
  city she actually lives in, so it doesn't function as a separate axis. A robot built early in the Second
  Interwar era and one built late are **not carrying the same culture**, even in the same city — the
  transmission channel is the community, not the fabrication.
- **Ch. 14 — Gen/Mark: real, structurally independent of city, but never a hierarchy.** Read in full
  2026-09-15. **Mark** = the fabrication-chamber hardware/design generation itself (currently Mark IV,
  designed at Neumayer) — a fact about the chamber, not the robot. **Gen** = the robot's own generational
  identity, tracking which Mark's chamber built her. Gen/Mark's independence from city comes from the
  chamber-SHIPPING network, not from robots being built centrally and relocated — nearly every city has its
  own local chamber (Dome Fuji is the sole exception, deliberately chamberless), but the same Mark generation
  ships to and installs in many cities at once, so Gen tells you almost nothing about which city a robot is
  from. **Despite that real independence, Gen/Mark still carries markedly less felt cultural weight than
  city/locality.** Different Marks carry real cognitive/physical differences (material-level efficiency
  varies by design spec) — **a difference in kind, never in rank**, tied to whatever pressures the nation
  faced when that Mark was designed, and it bonds robots together around a shared historical moment rather
  than dividing them.
- **Three separate things set at fabrication:** Personality (psychological seed, only ever *partially* specced
  by law — Law 1's concrete engineering expression), Build (physical/circumstantial, statistically tracks
  local economic character — hard-labor locales skew "thicker," agility-demanding locales skew "slender"),
  Language (tracks the dominant local culture and the community that built her).
- **Kinship runs on shared experience, not fabrication lineage** — "maker/made" doesn't hold; clan/tribe forms
  on geography and shared ideas/experience (robots lack the genetic basis humans have for it).
- **Manufacturing sites (the Cradle):** currently active — Sinheung, Byrd. Historical — Mountain Pass (ended
  with Amundsen Tower's destruction), Denison (lost when the city was destroyed). Chambers are durable, shipped
  nationwide — a robot can be "born" in any city with an installed chamber, manufacturing site or not. **Dome
  Fuji has no chamber at all**, by design (voluntary residence only).
