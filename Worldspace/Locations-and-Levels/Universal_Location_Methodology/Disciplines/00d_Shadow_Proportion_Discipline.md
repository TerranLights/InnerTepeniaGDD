# Cross-Phase Discipline — The Shadow Is Not the District

> **⚠ Read `00_RUNBOOK.md` first.** It is the mandatory entry point for all district and location
> culture work (binding per the project `CLAUDE.md`), and it opens with **LAW 0 — depth over speed**,
> which governs everything in this file.

**Added 2026-08-29, after a real error caught in a district's second extrapolation pass.** Binding for every phase,
every district, and for any location methodology derived from this one. Read alongside
`00b_General_Population_Discipline.md`.

---

## The rule

> **A district operates on its own sincere conception of what "doing good" means and what a proper community
> looks like — and that conception largely works. The Shadow is what that sincere pursuit produces without
> intending it and without noticing it.**

The Shadow is a **byproduct**, never the operating principle.

## Three tests a shadow finding must pass

1. **Is it unintended?** Nobody chose it, and nobody would defend it if it were named plainly. If the district
   is knowingly doing the bad thing, that is not a shadow — that is a policy, and it belongs in the district's
   politics rather than its shadow.
2. **Is it unnoticed in-world?** Residents do not experience their district as its shadow. If they did, it
   would be a known grievance with a reform faction attached, not a shadow. The people inside see reasonable
   institutions behaving reasonably.
3. **Is it discoverable rather than announced?** The player should not perceive it on arrival. It surfaces only
   through genuine investigation — in specific contexts, situations, and only if they actually go looking.
   A district that reads as sinister on first contact has had its shadow written as its surface.

## What this means in practice

**Proportion.** The overwhelming majority of what a district is, day to day, is people doing what they believe
is right, within a culture that mostly delivers on its promises. The Sanctuary genuinely shelters people. The
Yards genuinely builds things that last. Write that as the reality, because it *is* the reality.

**Register.** A shadow finding should read as *"and this follows from that, though nobody meant it to,"* never
as *"here is what the district is really like underneath."* There is no "really like underneath." The surface
is true. The shadow is also true. Both at once.

**No villains.** This is the project's standing non-malice discipline applied to culture. If a shadow requires
someone to be cynical, self-interested, or knowingly cruel for it to work, it has been written wrong. Rewrite
it so it works with everyone acting in good faith.

**Do not let extraction weighting leak into application.** The `Zodiac_Personality_Substrate/` files
deliberately give heavy weight to §3 Shadow, because the *extraction* brief prioritised capturing what sources
actually said about failure modes — those sections are dense on purpose. **That weighting is an artifact of the
extraction, not a statement about how much of a district is shadow.** Applying a substrate file proportionally
to its own section lengths will produce a district that is 40% pathology, which is wrong.

## ⚠ Do not scale an individual's behavior into a civic sanction

**Added 2026-08-29, from the Frostlands, after a developer correction.**

The substrate files describe **one person at a time** — that is what the source books are, and several of the
files say so outright in their own §15. **A line about how this temperament treats a friend is not a statement
about how the district treats a resident**, and promoting one into the other produces institutions no
population would tolerate.

**The instance:** the substrate says this temperament *"exiles people who keep losing the same argument"* —
which in the source is a person ending a friendship with someone who will not move on. The pass turned it into
a district that **socially excludes a whole faction for being tedious.** The developer's objection was
immediate and correct: *"it may be annoying, but it's not something that they would drive people into
essentially near-guaranteed death over."*

**Two rules come out of it.**

1. **Ask what population share the source claim describes** — the same question `00b` asks about Fashion, now
   applied to shadow material. An individual's impatience scales to **a smaller room and a shorter hearing**,
   not to a sanction.
2. **A sanction must be proportionate to the district's physical conditions, and those conditions must be
   checked.** In the Frostlands, *outside is lethal* — so any exclusion-shaped penalty is a death sentence in
   practice, and a culture whose deepest reflex is that nobody gets left out in the cold **will not impose one
   over tedium.** Before writing any exclusion, ask what it physically costs the excluded person **in that
   district**, and whether the district would actually pay that price for that offense.

**And the corrected version was better.** Replacing exclusion with *"they are invited, agreed with, and nothing
follows"* produced a stronger finding — **a failure mode with no opponent in it**, which is exactly what `00d`
asks for and what the exile version could never have been, since exile requires somebody to do the excluding.

## Check canon for an existing no-villain mechanism before deriving one

**Added 2026-08-29.** Canon sometimes already contains the mechanism, fully formed, and deriving a
second one wastes the work and risks contradicting it.

> ### The shape to look for in existing canon
> ***A surveillance-like capability that NOBODY BUILT*** — **several independent strands of an ordinary,
> long-standing record-keeping culture converging into one.** **No author, no decision, no malice.**
> **A complete no-villain shadow, and such things are frequently already written down before any methodology
> exists to name them.** *(Worked instance archived — `../Test_Runs/Worked_Examples_Archive/`.)*

**So search the district's Canon Reference for its existing shadow material first.** Where canon already
supplies the mechanism, the pass's job is to explain **why that district was the one it happened to** — which is
what the capability reading is for — not to invent a parallel. *(Same rule as for countercultures in
`07_Phase_7` Step C and religions in `02_Phase_2` §8: **check before deriving.**)*

## The model to copy

**A worked standard exists and is archived.** *(The district instance lives in that methodology's own
`Deep_Dives/`, and remains its property.)* **Its own summary states the target exactly:**

> *"The injustice is invisible from the inside, not just unaddressed. Each layer's apparent reasonableness
> reinforces the next layer's: the ceiling looks like an ordinary unfunded infrastructure problem; the
> scheduling looks like ordinary bureaucratic drift; the patronage looks like ordinary human loyalty to people
> you know."*

Every component is individually defensible. No one is doing wrong. The harm is real anyway, and it is only
visible from outside, or from a very long way in. **That is what a shadow should look like.**

**The recurring shape, worth naming: *visible virtue, hidden cost*.** *(An archived finding of exactly this shape predates this note.)*

## The failure mode this exists to prevent

Written plainly, since it has now happened once:

**A second extrapolation pass once rendered its location as one whose every institutional incentive rewards
the opposite of its stated purpose, whose residents game its central virtue for standing, and whose kindest
practice is its purest pathology.** **Each individual observation was defensible and sourced.**
***Together they made the shadow into the location's operating system***, which is not what the place
is and not how it should read. The pass was corrected rather than discarded — the findings survive, reframed as
consequences that emerge at the edges of a system that ordinarily works.

**The tell:** if a reader finishing a district's file would describe the place as *sinister*, *a trap*, or
*secretly awful*, the proportion is wrong — regardless of how well-sourced each individual finding is.
