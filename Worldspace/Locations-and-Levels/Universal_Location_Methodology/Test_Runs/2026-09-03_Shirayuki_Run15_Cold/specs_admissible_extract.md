No Indians or people from the South Asian subcontinent (including Pakistan, Bangladesh, or related nations) ever came to Antarctica or settled in Tepenia. This is established world canon. Therefore:

- The Bharati Station infrastructure was present but **completely unoccupied** when the exile era began in 2564
- Whatever city exists at this location was founded entirely by a **non-Indian, non-South-Asian** exile population
- The city's name, culture, and identity have **nothing to do with India or South Asia**
- The real-world station name "Shirayuki" (Sanskrit: goddess of learning, also a name for India) **does not carry forward** into Tepenia in any form — the city's proper name is Japanese: **Shirayuki (白雪)**, chosen 2026-07-08

See also: `Lazar.md` (Halley subnet) for the same situation with the other Indian Antarctic station — there, the gap was resolved by coalescing with the adjacent, continuously-operated Russian Novolazarevskaya station. Here, a different mechanism was used instead — see Founding, below.

Shirayuki's genuine real-world neighbors are Chinese (Zhongshan, immediately adjacent) and Russian (Sinheung/Progress Station, same subnet) — neither Japanese. Rather than force an organic real-station-adjacency explanation the way Lazar's resolution used, this city's founding was resolved through **the International Court of Diplomacy at Jeju-do** — an important clarification: **this is an Upper Earth institution, not a Tepenian one.** It is the body through which Japan, Korea (Unified Korea), and the Sinian Federation (China) coordinated as peers *before* the War of Upper Earth and the Falkland Treaty, not something operating within or governing Tepenia after the exile.

The reasoning: the allocation decision was made on the Upper Earth side, as part of (or adjacent to) the same broader diplomatic process that produced the Falkland Treaty and the pre-exile arrangements for which real-world Antarctic infrastructure which national exile populations would eventually settle. At the time, Korea already held claim to multiple Antarctic footholds that would become Janbogo and Sejong, and China's presence was already extensive and immediately adjacent at Zhongshan. Rather than let China's proximity default into a third claim, the Jeju-do court — acting in its Upper Earth capacity, before any of this was Tepenian soil — allocated the unoccupied Shirayuki site to Japan instead, a genuine pre-exile diplomatic act rather than anything decided by Tepenia itself. Japanese exiles then settled the site once the Falkland Treaty exile era began, inheriting a claim that had already been diplomatically settled rather than working one out from scratch. This makes the city's founding **institutionally engineered by Upper Earth diplomacy rather than organic** — one of only two Tepenian cities founded this way, not the only one *(corrected 2026-07-13 — Sinheung shares this identical Jeju-do mechanism, allocated to Korea; this specific instance survived the 2026-07-13 fix to the Significance line above, missed because it's a second occurrence in the same file)* — and gives Tepenia's Japanese exile community a second city (alongside Sayowa) with a genuinely different founding mechanism: Sayowa inherited its own real station directly; this city exists because Upper Earth's diplomats decided, before the exile ever happened, that it should.
