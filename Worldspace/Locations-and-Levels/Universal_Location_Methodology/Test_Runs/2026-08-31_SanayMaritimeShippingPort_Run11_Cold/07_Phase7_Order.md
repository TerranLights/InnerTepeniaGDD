## 7a — Work and economy

**Division of labor, at Band-1-equivalent named-role scale** (per `01` §2.3, per this run's own §6
self-correction): dockside handling crews (Shipyard Complex), Trade Yard business/protocol staff (formal,
bounded-contact register per Phase 3 C), and coordination roles bridging the vertical tiers (Phase 3 F) between
dockside operations and clifftop administration.

**The non-thematic export, per `Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md` technique #9.** What the port sends outward
that has nothing to do with its headline function: **repurposed dunnage and cargo-packing material** — the
ordinary byproduct of any freight-handling operation of this scale, worked into simple household goods (per
the same logic as Cancer's own worked example: a cast object with no connection to the district's headline
function, traded citywide, entirely unsolemn) and traded around Sanay through channels unrelated to the port's
own formal Trade Yard business. **This proves the port's economy is a real, physically-generating one rather
than a purely allegorical "it moves freight" abstraction** — freight-handling at this scale genuinely produces
waste material, and waste material genuinely gets used by somebody.

**Checked against `00b`'s object-colonization rule**: this is offered as one additional, small, non-thematic
thread alongside the headline function (already fully covered in Phases 1-5), not as a replacement for
describing the port's general economic breadth — the headline function itself is the port's own dominant
economic answer, correctly, and this finding is explicitly scoped as a minor addition on top of it.

## 7b — Governance and decision

**What is unadministrable, not who holds power.** Phase 5a established that Sanay and Belgrano share the
subnet's receiving-port role on a reactive, weather-dependent basis — "whichever passage is open at the time"
— with no admissible evidence of any formal allocation mechanism deciding in advance which port serves which
shipment. **This is a genuine governance gap, not a policy**: nobody appears to have written a rule for the
case where BOTH approaches are simultaneously blocked, or where a freighter needs to divert mid-voyage from
one port to the other. Whether this gap has ever actually been tested is REQUESTED; its mere existence,
unaddressed, is consistent with Phase 1's own convergent Grudging Tolerance pattern — a costly edge case
nobody has formally planned for because the ordinary case has never forced the question.

**Prohibitions**, distinct from the capability frame's Grudging Tolerance per `02` §3.3: admissible canon
states no explicit safety-refusal rule, but Phase 1's G2 finding (marginal-weather crossing attempts tolerated
but genuinely risky) makes SOME operational safety threshold plausible — a captain or dock authority
empowered to refuse a docking attempt under stated conditions. **REQUESTED, not invented**: the specific
threshold and who holds the authority to invoke it are not established in admissible canon, though the
underlying physical risk (Phase 1 G2) makes some such rule characteristically likely to exist somewhere in this
location's actual practice.

## 7c — Knowledge and transmission

**This is where Phase 1's G4 finding (no living institutional knowledge, only documentation, reconstructed
peer-to-peer) and Phase 4's C (the personal-struggle expression of the same gap) land as a governance
question.** How someone learns this place's work: entirely informal, on-the-job, peer-to-peer transmission —
there is no admissible evidence of any formal apprenticeship institution, because none exists (unlike
Halley's own hands-on construction relationship to the Arcanet nexus, which involves a different population
entirely and lies outside this pass's scope). **What becomes of someone who cannot learn it** is REQUESTED —
admissible canon offers no answer — but given Phase 1's own established absence of a formal exclusion culture
(nothing here is refused at the point of contact), the characteristically consistent guess (offered as a
candidate, not asserted) is reassignment toward lower-stakes, less specialized work within the same operation
rather than expulsion from it. **Flagged explicitly as a candidate awaiting confirmation, not a finding.**

## 7d — Private life, minorities, and counterculture

**Checked canon for an existing counterculture first**: none found in admissible material. The DEMOTED
`Course_of_Events/` chains (per `00_Frame_and_PreFlight.md` §3) may contain relevant unratified material but
were not opened, per this run's own quarantine.

**Derived from the specific pressure**, per `Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md` technique #11: the port demands
total schedule flexibility around externally-determined freighter timing (Phase 4's shift-and-tide rhythm).
**The counterculture: workers who structure their lives around a fixed, protected block of time and refuse to
surrender it to the rotation clock**, even at real cost to their own standing within the port's own informal
reliability economy — accepting worse shift assignments, slower advancement through the Membership-by-
Persistence mechanism (Phase 5d), or simply being known as harder to schedule around, rather than give up
whatever the protected time is for (a family obligation, a commitment elsewhere in the excluded residential
district, anything the schedule cannot be trusted to accommodate). **Kept sympathetic, not criminal**: these
are people the demand does not fit, not people breaking a rule — there is no admissible evidence any formal
rule requires total flexibility, only that the port's own operating rhythm assumes it by default, which is a
different and more insidious kind of pressure exactly matching this technique's own architecture.

**Sanction proportionality, checked against this location's own physical conditions.** Because nothing in
admissible canon gives the port any authority over a worker's residency or presence in Sanay itself (that
authority, if it exists at all, belongs to city-level governance, out of this pass's scope), any actual
consequence for refusing the rotation clock's demands is necessarily WORK-SCOPED, not residency-scoped — worse
assignments, slower informal advancement, social friction among peers who share the burden the refuser does
not. **This is a genuinely proportionate reading, not an escalated one**: the port cannot banish anyone into
Sanay's lethal climate (Phase 1 G2) over a scheduling refusal, and nothing in this pass's own findings supports
inventing a harsher sanction than the location's own actual authority would permit.

**Is the contradiction between groups, or inside each person?** Given Phase 2's finding that the working
population is largely self-selected INTO port labor (accepting the schedule as a known trade-off) rather than
purely assigned, the tension plausibly sits **inside individual workers** as often as between two named
factions — someone who chose this work while also having a life the schedule does not accommodate experiences
the demand as a personal, ongoing negotiation rather than joining an organized counterculture group. **Both
readings are recorded**: a genuine minority who have organized around protecting their time (a population), and
a broader, unorganized set of individuals for whom the tension is simply a private, unresolved cost of the job
they chose. Neither cancels the other; per `03` Phase 7d's own instruction, this is worth having as a finding
in its own right.
