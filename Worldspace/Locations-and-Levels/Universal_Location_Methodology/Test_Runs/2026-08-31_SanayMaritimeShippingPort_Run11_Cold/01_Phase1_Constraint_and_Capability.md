# Phase 1 — Constraint & Capability (the spine)

**Asks:** What can this place do without trying, what can it not do at all, what must it keep paying, and what
does it permit but punish?

**Generators run: G2 (physical/environmental), G4 (founding condition), G5 (network position) — selected for
independence per `02` §5.1.** G1 excluded (compromised, see `00_Frame_and_PreFlight.md` §4). G7 (real-world
inspiration) is used here as research feeding the profile, per Step 1F below, not as a fourth independent
spine generator (three is sufficient; see `00_Frame_and_PreFlight.md` §1 for the reasoning).

**Input set this profile is built from, stated per `02` §4.0's binding rule:** admissible physical/founding/
network-position facts from `Specs/Sanay.md`, `Sanay_Physical_Infrastructure_Attributes.md` items 1-12,
`City_Relationship_Database.md`'s Sanay entries, and G7 research (see the research log) — **NOT** any of the
withheld culture-pass conclusions listed in `00_Frame_and_PreFlight.md` §3. This is a fully-quarantined input
set; a later Gate 6 comparison against the withheld material should be read with that in mind.

## A. Three profiles, run separately before comparison

### G2 — Physical & environmental constraint

| Quadrant | Finding |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | A bedrock-adjacent foundation position (Vesleskarvet nunatak) gives the port a structural stability no ice-shelf-founded facility in the subnet could offer — the same fact `Sanay_Physical_Infrastructure_Attributes.md` item 1 states for the city generally, inherited here for the port's own dockside works specifically. The port's position "inland from the coast... accessible but not immediately adjacent" (Geographic Basis) plausibly gives it partial shelter from the worst of direct open-Weddell-Sea exposure relative to a fully exposed coastal facility, while still remaining tidewater-accessible — a real, if modest, protective asymmetry. |
| **DEFICIT** | No stated ice-class fleet or dedicated ice-breaking capability anywhere in admissible canon. Real-world Antarctic coastal resupply (per this session's own G7 research — SANAP's SA Agulhas II) depends on ice-class vessels and a constrained seasonal window (summer only, roughly Dec/Jan-Feb/Mar); a shorter sea-ice season measurably narrows real Antarctic resupply windows, and Weddell Sea storm activity is specifically implicated in unpredictable ice conditions. Sanay's admissible canon states freighter traffic "at seemingly all hours" with no stated seasonal qualifier — taken literally as year-round, this is physically implausible for an unmodified Weddell-Sea-side port. **Resolved via the both-are-true test at §B below**, not treated as a canon error. |
| **STANDING COST** | Continuous physical maintenance of dockside works against a genuinely hostile climate — katabatic wind exposure (regionally established for Queen Maud Land, and real-world Antarctic coastal geography shows katabatic winds characteristically ACCELERATE toward the coast, stronger at tidewater than inland — a physical-geography inference, not a specific canon figure, since the admissible climate table is stated for the nunatak position, not confirmed for the dockside specifically; flagged REQUESTED). This is a cost paid regardless of freighter traffic volume — the infrastructure has to survive the climate whether or not a ship is in port that week. |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | Attempting a crossing or a docking within a narrow, physically marginal weather/ice window is never forbidden — nobody at the port turns away a freighter for arriving during a risky window — but it is punished: a badly-timed attempt genuinely risks the vessel, the cargo, or the dock infrastructure itself. Nobody polices this; the climate does. |

### G4 — Founding condition

| Quadrant | Finding |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | The exile community inherited a genuinely functional, physically intact resupply-capable site — SANAE-era station infrastructure, continuously maintained through a rotating chain of national operators across the First Interwar Period, handed over already built rather than needing to be constructed from nothing. This is a real, structural founding advantage over any city that had to build its own port infrastructure from an unequipped site. |
| **DEFICIT** | No living institutional knowledge of HOW to actually run the facility survived the handoff chain — per `Specs/Sanay.md`'s own admissible Founding section, "no living scientific mission survived that chain of handoffs... preserved journals, logs, and orientation manuals gave the exiles a real documentary starting point. Learning from a written record isn't the same as being taught by a living institution." The exile community had to develop "their own institutional culture and purpose for the site independently" — meaning the port's entire operating culture, protocol, and practice was reconstructed from documentation alone, with nobody left alive to ask when the documentation was silent or wrong. |
| **STANDING COST** | The burden of maintaining and transmitting institutional knowledge about how to run the port falls entirely and permanently on Sanay's own population, with no external institutional backing to draw on — a sharp contrast with the Halley Arcanet nexus's arrangement, where Halley's own residents did the hands-on construction labor and Sanay merely hosts the result. Nobody built this specific competency for Sanay; nobody helps sustain it. Every generation since founding has had to re-teach it internally, from Sanay's own accumulated practice, not from any inherited mentor institution. |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | Treating the original SANAE-era documentary record (journals, logs, orientation manuals) as authoritative guidance is never forbidden — it is, in fact, the ONLY documented starting point the exile community had, so falling back on it is the obvious, permitted default. But it is punished whenever circumstances have diverged from what a decades-old manual anticipated: the manual cannot answer a question its authors never faced, and treating it as though it can is a standing, unpoliced risk. |

### G5 — Network position

| Quadrant | Finding |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | One of only two receiving ports (with Belgrano) for the Halley subnet's entire South African Upper Earth freighter trade — genuine structural leverage: the subnet's overland connection to Upper Earth resupply runs through this port or it does not happen at all for anything routed this way. It additionally hosts the onward Hwy 7 connector distributing goods to Halley (which "has no docks or airstrip of its own") and further to Troll for aviation-based distribution — a genuine junction role, not merely a terminus. |

| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | Accepting more freighter traffic than the Hwy 7 onward-trucking chain can smoothly absorb is never refused at the dock — nobody turns away an arriving freighter because the trucks are backed up — but it is punished downstream: yard congestion, delayed onward delivery to Halley and Troll, and a growing gap between what the port can receive and what its own logistics chain can actually clear in the same rhythm. |

## B. Comparison table and resolution

| Quadrant | G2 | G4 | G5 | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STRENGTH | Bedrock shelter, partial coastal protection | Inherited, already-functional infrastructure | Dual-port hub + Hwy 7 junction role | **Silent-populated by all three — three genuinely distinct, non-overlapping strengths, none restating another.** Not "agreement" in the literal same-finding sense; each stands on its own generator's authority, and none conflicts with any other. |
| DEFICIT | No ice-class capability / seasonal-window uncertainty | No living institutional knowledge, documentation-only | No own airfield, Troll-dependent for air distribution | Same pattern — three distinct deficits, no direct conflict between them. |
| STANDING COST | Continuous infrastructure maintenance against climate | Continuous, externally-unsupported institutional-knowledge burden | Continuous onward-trucking obligation, nothing stays | **The richest quadrant, and see the synthesis below — a real generator-conflict-shaped tension worth resolving explicitly, not merely three parallel entries.** |
| GRUDGING TOLERANCE | Marginal-weather crossing attempts tolerated, risk-punished | Reliance on outdated documentation tolerated, gap-punished | Over-acceptance of traffic vs. trucking capacity tolerated, backlog-punished | Same structural shape across all three: **something is never refused at the point of contact, and the cost lands downstream/later rather than at the point of decision.** This convergence in SHAPE (not content) across all three generators is itself worth naming — see §C. |

### The both-are-true synthesis on STANDING COST — the richest site in this profile

**Apparent tension.** G4's Standing Cost emphasizes total institutional self-reliance and isolation — nobody
built this competency for Sanay, nobody helps sustain it, the burden is Sanay's own population's alone. G5's
Standing Cost emphasizes continuous outward service to others — nothing stays, everything gets trucked onward
to Halley and Troll, who depend on this exact facility for something they cannot do for themselves. On the
surface these read as opposite orientations: G4 says the port is turned inward on its own survival; G5 says
the port exists entirely to serve outward.

**What single property produces both, per `02` §5.3?** The port does everything required to keep itself and
its dependents running — its own institutional continuity AND the subnet's onward logistics — with **no
external institution's help on either front.** Nobody taught Sanay how to run the port (G4); nobody but Sanay
and Belgrano can receive the subnet's Upper Earth trade at all (G5) — Halley structurally cannot, having no
docks of its own. **The port does everything, alone, for everyone**, on both axes simultaneously: it received
no help learning to do its job, and it gives no rest from doing it for others. This is not two contradictory
findings; it is one disposition (total operational self-sufficiency under a permanent service obligation)
read from two independent generators, and it survives the both-are-true test cleanly — G4 is about the
COMPETENCE axis (how the knowledge got here), G5 is about the SERVICE axis (what the competence is used for),
and a place can plausibly be self-taught on the first while being structurally indispensable on the second.

### The both-are-true resolution on G2's DEFICIT

**Apparent tension**, flagged in the G2 table above: admissible canon states freighter arrivals "at seemingly
all hours," while real Antarctic resupply physically requires a constrained seasonal window. **Resolution**:
Sanay's port very plausibly is NOT a single-station resupply operation like the real SANAP case it draws on —
it is a regional HUB serving multiple downstream recipients (its own city, Halley, onward to Troll) within
whatever shipping season the Weddell Sea's ice conditions actually permit. "Active at seemingly all hours"
plausibly describes the rhythm WITHIN an open shipping season — rotating crews working around freighter
arrivals continuously once the window is open — not a claim that the window itself is open year-round. **This
is a genuine, stated divergence from the real-world G7 comparable** (a single-station seasonal resupply run),
not a contradiction requiring correction: Sanay's hub role is precisely what makes its within-season rhythm
busier and more continuous than any single station's own resupply cycle would be. **The exact length and
timing of Sanay's own shipping season remains a REQUESTED item** — admissible canon states the rhythm but not
the season's calendar bounds.

## C. Reading the shape

**Per `02` §4.0, stated with the input set that produced it:** on the G2/G4/G5 input set defined in the header
above (excluding G1 and all withheld culture-pass material), **this profile reads COST-DOMINANT.** All four
quadrants are populated, but STANDING COST is where the richest, most load-bearing finding sits — not merely
the largest by entry count, but the quadrant where the both-are-true synthesis above produced this profile's
single strongest claim (the port does everything, alone, for everyone, on both the competence and service
axes). The matching question per `02` §4: **"Is this place doing anything other than continuing to exist?"**

**Answer, stated plainly:** mostly, no — by design, and not as a failure. The port's entire admissible function
is continuous maintenance of itself (against climate, against its own knowledge gap) and continuous service to
others (receiving, trucking onward). This is the expected shape for the type per `02` §4's own table
("hostile-environment outposts... anything on life support"), and it is additionally reinforced by this run's
own Configuration note: a Band-1-equivalent, unsettled Installation with no external institutional support
structurally has very little slack to spend on anything beyond its own continuation.

**The convergent GRUDGING TOLERANCE shape, worth naming separately from the cost-dominant read:** all three
generators independently produced a Grudging Tolerance entry with the identical underlying structure — nothing
is refused at the point of contact, and the cost is deferred to land later or elsewhere (a bad crossing, an
outdated manual's gap, a trucking backlog). **This is a second-order finding the cost-dominant shape alone
would not have surfaced**: the port's culture of never refusing at the point of contact is itself a load-
bearing disposition, worth carrying forward into Phase 6 (Meaning) and Phase 7 (Order) as a candidate for this
location's own Failure State of the Core Value (`Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md` technique #2) — its
apparent core promise may be precisely "we do not turn anything away," and the failure state is what happens
when that promise's deferred costs finally land all at once.

## D. Deficit addresses (`02` §4.1) — read and counted

Three deficits, three DIFFERENT addresses — not one deficit with multiple remedy sources, so the "count above
one raises suspicion" rule does not apply here; this is three separate single-address deficits.

| Deficit | Address | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| G2 — no ice-class fleet / seasonal uncertainty | **In the parent** | No single city could unilaterally fund or build dedicated ice-class shipping — this is Federation- or subnet-scale infrastructure by its nature, since the port serves not just Sanay but the whole Halley subnet's Upper Earth trade. The remedy exists only at a scale above Sanay itself, which "means reaching it requires admitting the location cannot manage itself" (`02` §4.1) — a genuine, structural under-reporting risk: does anyone at Sanay actually name this as a gap, or is it simply absorbed as "how shipping here works"? |
| G4 — no living institutional knowledge | **In its own past** | The founding generation's living-institution knowledge is genuinely, permanently gone — not departed to a documented destination, not withheld by an authority, simply extinct. The remedy is historical, not geographical, and per `02` §4.1's evidence-check note, the people who could have supplied it (the pre-exile station operators) are not merely absent but were never part of the exile community at all — this is a DEEPER case than the standard "witnesses departed" pattern, since there were never any witnesses available to this population in the first place, only documents. |
| G5 — no own airfield | **In a neighbor's present** | Troll physically, permanently, and currently holds exactly the capability Sanay lacks — not a departed authority, not an abstract parent-level gap, but a living, co-located, same-subnet neighbor. This is the sharpest address type available (`02` §4.1's newest addition, added 2026-08-31) — the comparison is permanent and close rather than historical or abstract, and it is directly analogous to the worked case that motivated adding this address type (Sinheung's founding claim sitting permanently beside Zhongshan's organic one). |

## E. Research the deficits (Step 1F, after the profile — not before)

Per LAW 0 / `02` §5.1 step F: the profile says what the place cannot do; research supplies what the missing
thing looks like. G7 research (Rotterdam, SA Agulhas II/SANAP, Durban, Guangzhou, Silicon Valley — full detail
in `SanayMaritimeShippingPort_Research_Log.md`) was run BEFORE this phase was drafted, which is a deliberate
deviation from the letter of Step ordering (research normally follows the profile) but not from its spirit —
the research was scoped to the FOUR PROVIDED G7 picks and general Antarctic-port physical realism, not to any
specific deficit, so it did not contaminate the profile's own derivation; the profile above was built from
admissible canon first, and the research table's fusion candidates were checked against the finished profile
retroactively, which is the substance of what Step 1F asks for even though the literal sequencing differed.
**Recorded honestly as a minor procedural deviation, not concealed.**

**What the research actually changed vs. ornamented, per Gate 7 (to be finalized at Step 7, previewed here):**

- **Rotterdam/Durban/Guangzhou's convergent physical-differentiation fact** (all three real ports zone
  physically by cargo type — bulk, container, liquid, specialized) **CHANGED a finding**: admissible Sanay
  canon describes one undifferentiated "Shipyard Complex," and this research surfaces a genuine open question
  worth carrying into Phase 3/10 — does the Complex have internal zoning by cargo type (raw materials vs. dried
  food, per the admissible Economy & Industry claim that both flow through it), or is the admissible material
  simply silent on internal structure? **Flagged REQUESTED**, not invented.
- **SA Agulhas II's real single-vessel, seasonal-voyage, helicopter-transfer resupply model CHANGED the G2
  deficit resolution** directly (§B above) — without this comparable, the "at seemingly all hours" vs.
  physical-seasonality tension would have had no concrete real-world anchor for the both-are-true reconciliation.
- **Durban's landlocked-hinterland-served-by-one-port shape CHANGED the G5 strength finding's framing** — it is
  the closest real structural analogue to Halley's own total dependency on Sanay/Belgrano for any seaborne
  Upper Earth trade, and sharpened what was otherwise a flatter "receiving port" claim into a genuine structural
  dependency claim.
- **Silicon Valley's garage-origin material ORNAMENTED, not changed**, honestly recorded as the weakest of the
  four picks for this specific sub-location (see the research log) — its one portable mechanism (a purely
  practical, cheapest-available origin later treated as more meaningful than it was) is a genuine `Cultural_
  Synthesis_Techniques.md` technique #6 (Necessity Before Meaning) candidate, but this session found no
  admissible Sanay-port fact concrete enough to fuse it against yet. **Held for Phase 3/6/10 rather than forced
  here** — a pick is not owed a finding, per LAW 0.
