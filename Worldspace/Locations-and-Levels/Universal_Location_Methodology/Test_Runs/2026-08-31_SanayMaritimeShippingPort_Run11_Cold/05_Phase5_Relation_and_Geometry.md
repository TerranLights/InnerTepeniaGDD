# Phase 5 — Relation & Geometry

**Asks:** What is this place to the places around it, to the thing that contains it, and to anyone crossing its
edge?

**Weighting note:** this location is neither purely isolated nor purely clustered. It has no sibling set of its
own (Frame Declaration), but it sits inside a real, densely-adjacent set of facilities within its own parent
city (the excluded Arcanet nexus, business district, residential district) and has a real subnet-level peer
(Belgrano). 5c (the parent) is treated as primary per the isolated-location weighting default, but 5a is
genuinely available here, unusually so for this methodology's own isolated-location majority case.

---

## 5a — Peers

**External peer: Belgrano**, the Halley subnet's other receiving port for South African freighter shipments.
Admissible canon states both receive shipments "depending on whichever passage is open at the time" — this
reads as **functional load-sharing, not rivalry.** The quiet friction worth naming (per `03`'s own
loud-vs-quiet-friction instruction): neither port is described as having a stated division of labor beyond
weather-dependent passage availability — meaning in practice, whichever port's approach is open in a given
window absorbs the traffic the other cannot, an unglamorous, structurally cooperative relationship with no
recorded tension in admissible canon. **Recorded as a genuine finding, not a null**: the quiet, unremarked
cooperation between two functionally similar facilities is itself characterizing, in a project where most
sibling relationships carry at least some named friction.

**Internal peers (adjacent facilities within Sanay, excluded from this pass's own scope but real neighbors):**
the Arcanet relay nexus, the clifftop trucking depots/business district, and the residential districts. What
flows between the port and each: cargo and personnel flow to the depots/business district (Phase 3's vertical
seam); nothing flows to the Arcanet nexus directly (it is Halley-built and Halley-operated, per admissible
canon, functionally unconnected to freight); workers flow to and from the residential districts on the
rotation clock (Phase 4). **The Arcanet nexus's total functional non-connection to the port, despite physical
proximity, is worth stating explicitly** — two major pieces of the same city's infrastructure, built by
different hands for different reasons, coexisting without operational overlap.

## 5b — The three-way differentiation set (own-eras substitute, per `01` §5.3a — no sibling set exists)

**Category chosen: the port's own institutional self-reliance (Phase 1 G4), read across its own three eras.**

| Era | State of the deficit |
|---|---|
| **Founding** | Totally isolated — inherited physical plant, documentary record only, no living institution, a small operation scaled to whatever the original SANAE station's own footprint supported. |
| **Growth (Second Interwar Period, pre-war)** | The deficit does not shrink as the facility grows into "endless shipyards" — **it scales up with the operation.** More infrastructure and more workers mean more situations the internally-taught knowledge base has to cover, with the same absence of any external mentor institution. Self-reliance does not become easier with scale; it becomes a larger, more distributed burden carried by proportionally more people, none of whom have outside backup either. |
| **Now (this pass's own declared frame — late Second Interwar, pre-war)** | The mature state Phase 1 already found: the port "does everything, alone, for everyone" — self-taught competence at full institutional scale, serving not just itself but the whole subnet's Upper Earth trade dependency. |

**The finding this three-way set produces, not visible from any single era alone**: growth at this location
does not resolve its founding deficit — it **compounds** it. A place assumed to "grow out of" an early
resource-poverty condition instead grows more dependent on that same condition holding, because nothing about
scaling up manufactures the external institution that was never there to begin with. This is a genuinely
different shape from the standard "founding scarcity resolved by later development" story, and is this
pass's own strongest Relation & Geometry finding.

## 5c — The parent

**What Sanay needs from the port, and what it supplies, converge almost completely — an unusual absence of
the ordinary G3 divergence.** Sanay's own admissible self-description IS the port's function: "a major
shipping and transportation port city... a genuinely major logistics hub by function" is not one facet of
Sanay's identity among several — per the admissible Economy & Industry section, it is stated as Sanay's
primary, first-and-foremost identity. **Most locations carry some daylight between what they believe
themselves to be and what their parent structurally needs from them; this pass finds essentially none for
Sanay/the port.**

**What Sanay believes about its own port, and whether it is accurate:** admissible canon offers no stated
civic self-narrative distinct from the plain functional description — no evidence of either pride, resentment,
or any other stance toward the arrangement, beyond the bare fact of its centrality. **This produces a genuine
open finding rather than a resolved one**: a city whose entire economic identity is fused with one Installation
this completely has not, per admissible canon, been shown to have developed any independent civic sense of
itself beyond that function. Whether that fusion is experienced as simple, uncomplicated identity or as a
narrowness nobody has had reason to name is **REQUESTED** — genuinely open, not decided here, and flagged as a
candidate for whoever runs a full Sanay-city pass rather than resolved by this narrower one.

## 5d — The edge and the crossing

**Two distinct arrival types, not one:** freighter crews (transient, business-bounded, meeting the Trade
Yard's formal protocols first per Phase 3 C) and new port workers (posted/self-selected, per Phase 2, meeting
the rotation-clock structure first).

**What converts a new worker into an established one — a mechanism, not a ceremony**, per `Cultural_Synthesis_
Techniques.md` technique #10 (Membership by Unremarked Persistence), whose "shift/rotation based" category
fits this location precisely: **having survived a full rotation cycle, including whatever bad stretch it
contained** — a genuinely disruptive freighter delay, a period of especially harsh weather within the shipping
season (Phase 1 G2) — converts a newcomer into someone simply understood to belong here, with no announcement
and, per the technique's own governing logic, probably no moment anyone could point to as the change. This
fits the location's own established character (rotation-clock structure, self-taught peer knowledge, Phase
1's grudging-tolerance pattern of costs landing downstream rather than at a clear decision point) tightly
enough to be a strong, characteristically-inevitable finding rather than an imported template.

**Freighter crews are never converted at all** — the Trade Yard's bounded protocols (Phase 3 C) exist
precisely because this arrival type is not meant to become a member; it is meant to conduct business and
leave, which is itself worth stating as the deliberate opposite case to the worker-conversion mechanism above.

## 5e — Dependency

**Two named dependencies, asymmetric in visibility — the finding worth carrying forward.** Phase 1's G2
deficit (no ice-class capability) is addressed "in the parent" — a Federation/subnet-scale gap that may or may
not be perceived as a gap at all by anyone at Sanay itself (Phase 1's own under-reporting flag). Phase 1's G5
deficit (no own airfield) is addressed "in a neighbor's present" — Troll, a known, physically co-located,
functioning peer whose capability gap-filling role is presumably common knowledge across the subnet, given how
directly Sanay's own admissible canon states the Hwy 7 connection to Troll for onward aviation distribution.
**One dependency is visible and named; the other is structurally invisible by the address-type's own logic**
(`02` §4.1's diffuse/in-the-parent distinction: a parent-scale remedy "produces under-reporting, a shadow with
no author"). This asymmetry — knowing you depend on Troll, not necessarily knowing (or ever having had reason
to ask) whether you depend on a subnet-scale infrastructure investment nobody has built — is this phase's own
clearest shadow-adjacent finding, worth carrying into Phase 6/7's own shadow discipline.
