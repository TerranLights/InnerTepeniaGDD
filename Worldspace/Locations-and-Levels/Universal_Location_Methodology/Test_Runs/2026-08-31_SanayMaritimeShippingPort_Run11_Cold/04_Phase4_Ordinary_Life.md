# Phase 4 — Ordinary Life

**Asks:** What is an ordinary person's day here, apart from what this place is famous for?

**Headline function, stated in one sentence, then written away from:** the port receives and forwards freight.
The following is deliberately about what a worker's day is like *between, despite, and unrelated to* that.

**⚠ Phase 4 supplies the contradiction-check instrument (Gate 3); it does not run it here** (`03` §0.4). Checked
backward against Phases 1-3 only below; the phase is COMPLETE, not blocked, pending Phases 5-10.

---

## A. Routines

**No routine here is anchored to daylight.** Per Phase 1/2's rotating, freighter-tied crew structure, a shift
can begin at any hour of the notional day — the port's own "seemingly all hours" activity (Phase 1 G2, both-
are-true resolved as within-season continuous rhythm) means an ordinary worker's actual routine is organized
around **shift-and-tide, not sunrise-and-sunset.** For anyone whose role touches both dockside handling and
clifftop administration (Phase 3 F's vertical-tier finding), the day includes a real, physical vertical
commute between tiers — not a metaphor, an actual elevation change made routinely, whose length and difficulty
depends on whatever connector exists (a REQUESTED Phase 10 catalog item, per Phase 3 F).

## B. Mundane concerns

**Coordinating a life around an unpredictable rotation clock is the port's own ordinary-life signature
concern.** Phase 2 established the working population is posted/assigned into a rotation genuinely tied to
freighter arrivals rather than a fixed calendar — meaning, unlike an ordinary fixed-shift job, a worker cannot
reliably plan around a known schedule days in advance. The mundane cost of this lands specifically on
coordinating with the residential-district life this pass's scope excludes but does not deny exists: missed
plans, rescheduled visits, arrangements made around a shift that then moves because a freighter's arrival
window shifted. **This is the direct, ordinary-person expression of Phase 1's G5 Standing Cost** (nothing that
arrives stays, and neither does anyone's schedule) — the abstract capability-frame finding cashed out as
somebody's actual Tuesday.

## C. Personal struggles

**What happens when a worker hits a situation nobody taught them, and there is no living authority to ask.**
Phase 1's G4 finding (no inherited institutional knowledge, only documentation, reconstructed peer-to-peer
across generations with no external mentor institution) has a direct, personal-scale expression: newer workers
learn the trade entirely from other Sanay port workers, never from any outside institution, because none
exists (Halley's residents built the Arcanet nexus; nobody built or maintains the port's own operating
knowledge for Sanay). **The ordinary struggle this produces**: encountering a genuinely novel situation the
internally-taught knowledge base has no ready answer for, and having nobody more expert to call — not a rare
crisis-scale event, but a routine, low-grade uncertainty that is simply part of learning and doing this
specific job, everywhere else in Tepenia answerable by an institution this location does not have access to.

## D. Escapism / downtime

**Downtime cannot follow a conventional evening-leisure pattern**, because shifts end at no fixed hour. Per the
admissible Notable Locations entry, off-shift workers spend their time in the residential/commercial/leisure
districts (outside this pass's own scope, but the CAUSE of the pattern — the schedule — is in-scope). **The
finding worth stating here, even though the venue itself is out of bounds**: whatever social/leisure
institutions exist for port workers must, by the schedule's own nature, operate on an odds-and-all-hours basis
rather than a conventional evening pattern — a culture where "going out" happens whenever a shift ends, which
could be at any hour, rather than at a shared communal time. This is a REQUESTED item for whoever eventually
runs a full Sanay-city pass (the actual venues are out of this pass's scope), but the underlying cause and its
shape is confidently derivable from what this pass has already established.

## E. Do not write one universal worker

Per Phase 2's arrival-mode mix (posted-majority, self-selected-in, with the born-here/generational thread
REQUESTED rather than confirmed): a **posted** worker's day differs from a **self-selected** one primarily in
attitude toward the rotation-clock's unpredictability — someone who chose port work over the smaller research
economy (Phase 2 B) plausibly accepts the schedule's cost as a known trade-off; someone assigned into it by
institutional need may experience the same schedule as a genuinely imposed hardship. **This divergence in
lived experience of the identical external condition is itself a finding**, not collapsed into one universal
account.

## F. What the rhythm fails to provide

**For anyone whose life does not fit a shift-and-tide schedule** — a caretaking obligation with its own fixed
demands, a family structured around ordinary daylight hours — this location's own defining rhythm is
structurally hostile, independent of and prior to anything the (excluded, out-of-frame) post-war "damage
without erasure" condition would add. This is a pre-war, baseline-condition finding, consistent with this
pass's own declared temporal frame (`00_Frame_and_PreFlight.md` §1).
