# Step 8 — The Review Panel

Per `00f_Review_Panel.md`, carried unchanged, casting only. Six dispositions available: accepted · noted ·
rejected · refereed · unmet · declined.

## Panel A — the six Flat Archetypes

### 1. The Child — **cannot be cast**

Per `00f`'s own precedent (a position that cannot be cast at all is a finding, and a strong one — the
Frostlands' Neighbor case): **nobody's home is at the port** (Phase 2 D), and no domestic or family sphere
exists here by this pass's own established scope. There is no standpoint from which a Child could speak about
this location — not because childhood is forbidden here, but because the location itself has no residential
dimension at all. **Recorded as a result, not substituted with a different casting.** This independently
confirms Phase 2 D's own finding from the outside, exactly as the Frostlands precedent describes.

### 2. The Lover — cast as **a romantic pair, one or both on rotation here**

- **Notices**: whether intimacy is structurally possible here. **It is not — not because the port forbids it,
  but because there is nowhere private and nobody stays.** The Lover's standing question — *could I build a
  life with someone here, and where would we actually go?* — returns a sharp, specific answer: **not here**.
  "Here" is where the rotation clock is negotiated around (Phase 4 B's mundane-concerns finding); the actual
  life happens in the excluded residential districts.
- **Objection, in voice, uncensored**: *"You ask me if I could live and love here. I couldn't — not because
  anyone stops me, but because there's nothing here to build with. Every hour I spend here is an hour I'm not
  spending on the life I'm actually building somewhere else, and the schedule doesn't care which hour that
  is."*
- **Disposition**: **unmet.** Per `00f` §7 Rule 3: would satisfying this (giving the port its own domestic
  sphere) make it more like an ordinary Settlement? Yes — and this pass's own EXCEPTIONAL Configuration
  (port-only scope) is precisely what makes this port NOT that. The location characteristically would not and
  should not satisfy this want. **Recorded as characterization**: the port is a place two people negotiate
  around, never a place they live.

### 3. The Parent — cast as **a worker on rotation with a dependent elsewhere**

- **Notices**: who has the children during a crisis. Phase 4 B already establishes the mundane cost of
  coordinating with off-site life around an unpredictable rotation clock — the Parent sharpens this into a
  specific, sharper fear: *what happens when a freighter arrives late and a shift runs long past the point
  someone was supposed to be relieved for childcare?*
- **Objection, in voice**: *"Nobody tells me in advance when my shift actually ends. I plan around 'probably,'
  and probably has failed me before. Who has my kid when probably is wrong?"*
- **Disposition**: **noted.** Real, but this pass's own scope cannot answer it — the actual childcare
  arrangement is a residential-district fact, out of bounds here. **Deferred explicitly to a future full-Sanay
  pass**, not silently dropped nor forced into invention.

### 4. The Ruler — cast as **the informal safety/dock authority (Phase 7b)**

- **Notices**: whether the institutions actually function; resource flow; unadministrable rules. **Direct
  hit**: Phase 7b's own governance gap (no stated mechanism for both Sanay and Belgrano being simultaneously
  blocked) is exactly this archetype's own standing question — *who administers that, who funds it, and what
  happens when they stop?*
- **Objection, in voice**: *"I can tell you what happens when one port is down — the other one carries it.
  Ask me what happens when we're BOTH down at once, and I don't have an answer, because nobody's ever written
  one down. I hope we never find out which of us is supposed to decide."*
- **Disposition**: **accepted.** This is Phase 7b's own finding, independently reached by the Ruler position —
  strengthens Finding via corroboration from a different instrument, per `00f` §8's own "strengthens an
  existing finding" outcome.

### 5. The Elder — cast as **a worker aged out of continuous rotation-capable labor**

- **Notices**: what the place does with people it can no longer use; dignity in decline. **A genuine gap this
  pass had not previously surfaced**: Phase 7c addressed what happens to a worker who *cannot learn* the job
  (a candidate, reassignment), but never addressed a worker who learned it fully, for years, and simply can no
  longer sustain the shift-and-tide physical demand.
- **Objection, in voice**: *"I gave this place thirty years of knowing exactly what to do when the manual
  didn't say. Now my body can't take another storm-window scramble, and nobody has a name for what I am to
  this place anymore. Am I still a veteran if I can't do the thing that made me one?"*
- **Disposition**: **REJECTED, but productively — a new REQUESTED item, not a finding.** Admissible canon
  gives this pass nothing to build an answer from, and inventing one now would be exactly the "accepting the
  first coherent answer" failure LAW 0 warns against. **Recorded explicitly as a REQUESTED gap**: what
  happens to a long-serving worker who ages out of rotation-capable labor is genuinely open, and — given how
  central the veteran/new axis (Phase 5d/9, M-86) is to this pass's own strongest findings — this REQUESTED
  item is more load-bearing than a typical REQUESTED flag, since it sits directly downstream of this pass's
  own headline finding.

### 6. The Mentor — cast as **a veteran worker, teaching peer-to-peer with no institutional backing**

- **Notices**: whether knowledge actually passes; who is left unskilled. **Already this pass's own central
  finding (M-86)** — the Mentor's own standing question (*how does someone learn this, and what becomes of
  the ones who can't?*) is directly answered by Phase 1 G4/Phase 7c, with the "what becomes of the ones who
  can't" half correctly left as a stated candidate rather than a forced finding.
- **Disposition**: **accepted**, folding directly into the existing M-86/Phase 7c material rather than
  producing a new standalone finding — consistent with `00f` §8's own precedent that a panel run on an already-
  sound pass may strengthen existing findings rather than add new ones.

## Mandatory additions

### The Passer-Through — cast as **a visiting freighter crew member, here for one voyage**

- **What they see**: the Trade Yard's formal, bounded-protocol business register (Phase 3 C) — professional,
  procedural, competent. **They do NOT see** the cost-deferral pattern (Phase 1's "never refuse, pay for it
  later") — that cost lands on port WORKERS (the trucking backlog, the knowledge gaps, the schedule strain),
  never on a transient freighter crew whose entire interaction is bounded by design.
- **Verdict**: **PASS.** The shadow is discoverable, not surface — exactly the `00d` target. A single-voyage
  visitor sees a competent, functioning port and nothing else; the cost pattern only becomes visible to
  someone who stays long enough to be a worker, not a client. This is the correct shape for a `00d` shadow.

### The Neighbor From Across the Line — cast as **Belgrano**

- **Biased by design**: from Belgrano's own presumed standpoint (no admissible material on Belgrano's own
  culture exists — this position is cast provisionally, per this pass's own Gate 4/Step 6 limitation), the
  relationship reads as quiet, functional load-sharing with no stated friction — but a biased Neighbor should
  have SOME grievance. **The plausible one, derivable from Phase 7b's own governance gap**: Belgrano may
  reasonably resent that neither port has ever formally agreed on what happens when both are blocked — an
  unaddressed shared risk, not a resolved cooperation.
- **Disposition**: **noted.** This casting is honestly flagged as provisional (no Belgrano-side canon exists
  to ground it more firmly), consistent with Gate 4/Step 6's own stated limitation. A future Belgrano pass
  should check this specific claim directly.

## The Lover faculty (Moore & Gillette, Panel D) — mandatory every time

**Is this place alive, and could anyone love it?**

**Honest answer, not softened**: largely no, and this pass says so directly rather than forcing a false yes.
Phase 8 recorded genuine nulls on Music and most of Arts, reasoned from the port's own established shape
(nobody's home is here, most cultural/aesthetic life happens off-site by design). What IS here to love is
narrower and more specific: the competence itself (M-86's manual/workaround mechanism, the veteran/new
respect economy) — a worker might genuinely take pride in, and feel affection for, **being good at this**,
and for the peers who taught them, without the PLACE itself offering beauty, pleasure, or domestic warmth in
the way the Lover faculty asks about. **This is the most likely single omission this pass's own structure
would produce if not checked directly** (per `00f`'s own warning) — checked, and the honest answer is that
this location's own EXCEPTIONAL Configuration (a bare workplace, not a home) makes full "loveability" in the
Lover faculty's sense genuinely and correctly thin, not a gap to fix.

**Disposition**: **declined**, per `00f`'s six-disposition split (added 2026-08-30) — the want is real
(every location should ideally have something to love), satisfying it would require inventing domestic/
aesthetic content this location's own established facts do not support, **and nobody at the port has
occasion to perceive this as a lack**, because the port was never conceived as somewhere to build a life —
that's the residential district's job. This is `declined`, not `unmet`: the difference is that `unmet`
requires the location to knowingly protect something, and nothing here is being knowingly protected — the
absence is simply a scope fact nobody at the port has reason to notice or defend.

## One shadow position, pulled in deliberately given this pass's own richest material

### The Magician's Denying "Innocent" One (Panel E, immature precursor)

**What does this place refuse to know that it already knows?** Chosen specifically because M-86 (the
manual/workaround dual-authority mechanism) is this pass's own strongest, most load-bearing finding, and this
shadow position is the sharpest available test of it.

**Verdict: guilty, precisely and specifically** — not on concealment (nobody hides the manual's gaps) but on
structural non-reconciliation: the port's own established fact that "an old outdated manual's gaps are never
officially addressed" (Phase 1 G4) is not secrecy — everyone who works here plausibly knows both the written
manual AND the living workaround knowledge, and knows they diverge. **What is refused is not the knowledge
itself but the ACT of reconciling it** — nobody sits down and rewrites the manual, not because doing so is
forbidden, but because (per Phase 1 G4's own Standing Cost finding) there has never been an institution with
standing to do it. This is a genuinely different verdict shape than a simple "not guilty" or "guilty" —
**closer to the Undergrid's own "not guilty, with the conditions for guilt stated" pattern** (`00f`'s own
prior precedent): the port is not guilty of concealment, but the conditions that would produce genuine guilt
(an institution capable of reconciling manual and practice, choosing not to) do not exist here, and their
absence is itself the finding.

**Disposition**: **accepted**, strengthening M-86 with an independent confirmation from a different
instrument (the shadow-panel check) rather than producing a wholly separate finding.

## Four-corner check

Did the objections come from genuinely different directions? **Yes.** The Lover's objection (no domestic
sphere) and the Parent's objection (no reliable schedule for off-site dependents) are related but genuinely
distinct — one is about presence, the other about predictability. The Ruler's objection (the Belgrano
coordination gap) and the Elder's objection (no answer for aged-out workers) are both genuine gaps, but one
is institutional/structural and the other is individual/human-scale — different corners, not one objection
wearing two hats. **No collapse to a single lens detected.**

**Convergence from opposed corners, worth flagging per `00f`'s own instruction**: the Elder (individual,
past-tense, declining) and the Ruler (institutional, present-tense, structural) — who share almost nothing in
standpoint — both independently surface the SAME underlying pattern: **the port has a favorable-path
mechanism (veteran status; port-to-port load-sharing) fully written, with no stated route back or contingency
for the unfavorable case.** This is the identical shape Gate 9 already caught mechanically (the veteran
threshold's own asymmetry) — **confirmed here from two genuinely opposed standpoints**, which per `00f` §5 is
the strongest signal this panel can produce that the gap is real.
