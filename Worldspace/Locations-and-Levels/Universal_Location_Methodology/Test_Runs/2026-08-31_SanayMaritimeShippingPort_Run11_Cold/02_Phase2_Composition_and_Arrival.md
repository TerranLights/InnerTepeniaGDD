# Phase 2 — Composition & Arrival

**Asks:** Who is actually here, in what proportion, from where — and by what route did they come to be here?

**Band variance applied:** per `00_Frame_and_PreFlight.md` §6's self-correction, this phase runs at
Band-1-equivalent procedure (`01` §2.3's substitute rule — name the roles and their variance; the list IS the
composition) rather than a Band 3+ statistical treatment, since the port's own working population is small,
named-role-scale, and drawn wholesale from Sanay's general population rather than possessing an independent
census of its own.

---

## A. Composition, from canon, read for its own stated limits

Sanay's city-wide census (Germany Primary 25.00%; Brazil/UK Significant; 18 Notable-tier nations) is
**inherited from the parent as a Determined property** (`01` §5.1) — the port has no independent demographic
figures, and this pass will not invent any. `Official_Population_Census.md`'s own methodology footnote
(de-stacked share-weighting, "identical between Census I and II under this proportional model... not
Tepenia-wide") is respected: this is a relative-ranking figure, not a literal headcount, and this pass does not
read it as more precise than it claims to be.

**Whether port labor draws proportionally from this city-wide mix, or skews toward any particular tier, is
REQUESTED** — nothing in admissible canon states a demographic profile specific to dock work. Absent evidence
otherwise, this pass assumes proportional draw (the Determined default) and states that assumption explicitly
rather than silently inventing a skew.

## B. Arrival-mode taxonomy, applied to the port's own working population

Not one mode — the mix is the finding, per `03` Phase 2's own core instruction.

| Arrival mode | Applies? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Assigned / posted** | **Primary mode** | The rotating-crew structure itself (freighter-arrival-tied scheduling, per the admissible Economy & Industry section) implies an institution assigns crews to shifts rather than workers self-organizing arrival times around ship schedules nobody controls. Produces a population that "did not choose each other," per `03`'s own definition, and a genuine rotation clock everyone can name. |
| **Chose it** | **Secondary, plausible** | Sanay's Economy & Industry section frames the port as the city's dominant function ("first and foremost, a major shipping and transportation port city"), meaning a large share of the city's own working-age population plausibly self-selected into port labor over the smaller-share alternatives (research, per the admissible Founding section's note that scientific work is now "a smaller slice of the city's actual economy, well behind the port/logistics operation"). What this self-selecting population REPELS — i.e., who Sanay's economy pushes toward the sciences instead — is a candidate worth carrying into Phase 4/7, not resolved here. |
| **Born here** | **Plausible, unconfirmed** | Given the port is the city's dominant, generations-old economic function, a hereditary/generational dock-labor tradition is characteristically plausible but not established in admissible canon. **REQUESTED**, not invented — flag rather than assume a specific inheritance mechanism. |
| **Assigned / posted (institutional, cross-city)** — Halley labor at the Arcanet nexus | **Confirmed, but explicitly NOT part of this pass's population** | `Specs/Sanay.md`'s admissible Founding/Significance sections state the Arcanet nexus was built by Halley's own residents physically traveling to Sanay to do the construction labor, while Sanay's residents ran the ports. This is real, admissible arrival-mode content — but it belongs to the excluded Arcanet nexus, not the port-only scope this pass declared. Noted here only to make the boundary explicit: the port's OWN working population is Sanay-native by every admissible indication; Halley's labor presence at Sanay is a separate, adjacent population this pass does not claim. |
| Fled to it / sentenced to it / stopped while passing | **No admissible support** | Nothing in canon suggests port labor is punitive, refuge-driven, or accidental. Recorded as genuine nulls, not silently skipped. |

**Differentiation axis, named per `03`'s own instruction:** *"posted-majority port labor, self-selected out of
a smaller research-economy alternative"* — a mode-mix distinct from a purely posted/institutional population
(no self-selection) or a purely voluntary one (no rotation-clock structure at all).

## C. Native layer vs. transplanted layer

**The port itself has no transplanted population under this pass's scope** — its working population is drawn
from Sanay's own city-wide demographic mix (itself already a settled, multi-generational exile-descended
population per the city's own Founding facts), not from a distinct incoming wave specific to the dock. The
"transplant" layer that exists in Sanay's broader admissible canon (Halley's residents at the Arcanet nexus) is
explicitly excluded from this pass's population by the scope boundary in §B above.

**This produces a real, if modest, finding**: unlike many ULM subjects, the port's population question does
not have a genuine native/transplanted split to run — it inherits Sanay's own answer to that question
wholesale (Determined, per `01` §5.1), because the port itself has never had its own separate arrival wave.
**Recorded as a null on this specific sub-question, with the reason stated**, rather than forced.

## D. Who is not here

**The most characterizing absence, stated plainly:** nobody's home is here. Every person present at the port,
regardless of arrival mode, arrival tier, or shift, leaves at the end of their rotation for the residential
districts this pass's scope explicitly excludes. **This is not an oversight of the pass — it is the direct
consequence of the port-only, Band-1-equivalent Configuration this run declared**, and it should be read as
the defining shape of this location's own Phase 2 answer, not merely a boundary condition to note in passing:
a location whose entire population is, by its own nature, always in the process of arriving or leaving, never
settling. This is a genuinely different composition shape than either Mountain Pass Airport's own isolated,
resident-staff Installation or a Settlement's ordinary population, and is a direct, load-bearing consequence of
this run's own EXCEPTIONAL Configuration (scope, per `00_Frame_and_PreFlight.md` §1).

**Also absent, worth naming:** no stated non-human/robot-specific arrival distinction for port labor
specifically (Phase 9 will need to check whether Sanay's own general human/robot composition applies
proportionally here, or whether dock labor itself carries any robot-specific skew — REQUESTED, not assumed).
