## G2 — Physical & environmental constraint

**What it gives freely:** the Terra Nova Bay polynya — a year-round ice-free maritime access point, unique
on the Ross Sea coast; a sheltered bay position, "more protection than fully exposed coastal sites"; a
longer navigable season than the Atlantic approaches; katabatic air that "warms adiabatically as it
descends," arriving relatively less cold than static ambient air of the same origin; a shorter polar night
(~64 days) than Concordia's 82.

**What it withholds absolutely:** any reliable link between ambient temperature and outdoor safety — "the
practical distinction between 'blizzard' and 'high-wind clear conditions' collapses," so a place that reads
as calm and merely cold can become lethal with no precipitation at all; unbroken visibility (spindrift);
liquid water, warmth, or windbreak outdoors at any time of year.

**What it demands continuously:** rime-ice-resistant construction and specialized building orientation on
anything facing the bay ("structures require specialized orientation and design to survive long-term" —
i.e., ordinary construction does not survive here, this is not decoration); wind forecasting and warning
infrastructure; shelter-point routing for any planned exterior movement.

**What it permits but punishes:** going outside using ordinary judgment. Katabatic conditions are not
storms in the conventional, announced sense — they are "a near-permanent feature of the local climate
regime," and the specific hazard is that a person's ordinary heuristics (check the sky, check the
thermometer) fail here in a way they do not fail at, say, Concordia, where cold is simply cold. **Third-order
consequence (LAW 0):** this produces a place where trusting your own senses about the weather is itself a
punishable behavior — not because anyone forbids it, but because the environment specifically defeats the
intuition a newcomer would otherwise rely on. That is a textbook `00d` shadow candidate: unintended
(nobody designed the atmosphere to deceive), unnoticed by anyone acting in good faith (a resident who checks
the sky and steps out is not being careless by any normal standard), and discoverable rather than announced
(a visitor would have to be told, or learn once).

**Four-quadrant profile:**
| Quadrant | Content |
|---|---|
| STRENGTH | Year-round maritime throughput via the polynya; a genuinely sheltered position relative to the rest of the Ross Sea coast; adiabatic warming softens katabatic air below what raw ambient temperature alone predicts |
| DEFICIT | Cannot use ambient temperature as a safety proxy — the ordinary human heuristic for "is it safe to go out" is actively wrong here |
| STANDING COST | Continuous wind-forecast/warning infrastructure; continuous rime-ice structural maintenance; continuous shelter-routing discipline for anyone moving outdoors — none of this is seasonal, it never stops |
| GRUDGING TOLERANCE | Trusting one's own senses about outdoor conditions is permitted (nobody can forbid looking outside and deciding it's fine) and punished unpredictably — the collapsed blizzard/clear distinction means the punishment doesn't correlate with how careful someone was being |

## G4 — Founding condition

**Who / when:** primarily Unified Korean exiles — robots from Korean society and their human partners/
supporters — alongside the broader Tepenian exile population reaching the Ross Sea coast, shortly after the
Falkland Treaty (June 21, 2564).

**Under what constraint, with what:** the exile settlement was built **on existing real Jang Bogo Station
infrastructure**, explicitly not starting from nothing. This is the distinguishing fact of this generator —
most Tepenian founding populations presumably built on bare ice or a minimal real-world footprint; Janbogo's
founders inherited an already-standing research-station structure.

**Without what — RESEARCHED, Step 3 (full log: `Cities/Research_Logs/Janbogo_Research_Log.md`, Session 1).**
The real Jang Bogo Station: 16 buildings, ~4,000–4,661 m², three wings, housing **23 people in winter and 62
in summer** — a small rotating scientific crew, engineered to withstand −40°C and winds to 65 m/s (~234
km/h). **This is now a grounded, numeric finding rather than an assumption**: Janbogo's founders inherited a
physical footprint sized for dozens, not for a settling exile population that plausibly reached the low
thousands or more at founding and grew toward its eventual hundreds of thousands. The mismatch between
"what the inherited structure could physically hold" and "what the founding population actually was" is the
deficit's real content — **Janbogo's own founding-population figure is itself unstated in any admissible
source and remains REQUESTED** (logged as an open thread in the research log), so the mismatch is
directionally certain (any settling population plausibly exceeds a station's rotating staff) but not yet
exactly quantified.

**⚠ Cross-check that survived, worth recording:** the real station's engineered wind tolerance (65 m/s) sits
comfortably above the in-fiction "150+ km/h extreme katabatic events" figure in `Specs/Janbogo.md` (65 m/s ≈
234 km/h) — the fiction's extremes are not exaggerated relative to their own real anchor. A plausibility
check that passed, not a finding in its own right.

**G7 note.** Jang Bogo Station's namesake — a 9th-century Silla naval commander who built a garrison
specifically to guarantee safe passage for Korean maritime traders through pirate-infested waters, becoming
known as "King of the Yellow Sea" — supplies a genuine, if close, resemblance to Janbogo's own established
civic function (guaranteeing safe passage, maritime and overland, through a hostile Antarctic coast).
**Flagged honestly per `03` §3.4 as the "close resemblance with a new paint job" outcome, not deep
divergence** — but the resemblance is load-bearing, not decorative: it independently explains why the name
survived its own phonetic drift intact, without relying on the M-64-excluded Founding-paragraph sentence
that made the identical claim as an unearned assertion. **This is re-derivation, not citation** — the
research log documents the namesake fresh, from the real historical figure, not from the excluded sentence.

**Four-quadrant profile:**
| Quadrant | Content |
|---|---|
| STRENGTH | Not founded from zero — a physical head start (existing shelter, existing site layout, existing footprint already proven to survive the local climate) that a founding population starting on open ice would not have had |
| DEFICIT | The founders inherited a building shape designed for a research station's population and mission, not a settling city's — the civic template had to be adapted to inherited architecture rather than designed freely from the start |
| STANDING COST | **Grounded, Step 3.** Continuous outward expansion/retrofit against an original footprint (16 buildings, ~4,000 m², three wings) built for dozens, not for a city — every increment of growth is growth away from what the founders actually inherited, never growth *within* it. This is a standing cost with no natural end point: unlike G2's wind-maintenance (a fixed, repeating burden), this one only ever grows, since the gap between "what the original structure holds" and "what the city now needs" widens with every generation rather than staying constant. |
| GRUDGING TOLERANCE | Repurposing or building over the original station footprint informally, without a plan reconciling old and new construction, is permitted (nobody is positioned to forbid it) and punished by structural incoherence — a plausible seed for Phase 3's "seam" question (`01` §4 rule 3: is there a visible boundary between founding-era build and later-pressure build?). **Provisional, to be tested against Phase 3's own actual texture work, not asserted as settled here.** |

## G5 — Network position

Concordia to the outside world in general. Coastal shipping via the year-round polynya. Pre-war inter-city
trade: "goods moving between the Ross Sea cities and the inland stations passed through or alongside
Janbogo." Subnet administration: Janbogo is the Janbogo Subnet's own hub city. The subnet's own Arcanet
relay nexus, however, sits physically inside Concordia's Gemini district rather than in Janbogo itself.

admissible fact (a Where question, genuinely pre-war-compatible); *why* Concordia is regionally dominant,
and whether that dominance is itself a war consequence, is explicitly NOT asserted either way by this pass

candidate account among at least two, neither adopted here.

**Four-quadrant profile:**

Whatever "hub" means for Janbogo, it is not "holds the network's own central point." |

  no politics, no grievance ever forms." Candidate for the harder diffuse case (`02` §4.1's own escalation):
  *has anyone here ever had reason to formulate this as a problem?* Given the Katabatic Warning Towers

  response is institutional infrastructure" — to be confirmed once G4/G7 research fills in whether this
  institution is independently derivable from admissible sources, or must be re-requested.
- **G5's deficit** (derivative highway status / hub without its own nexus): address is **in the parent** —
  the remedy (main-line status, or holding the subnet's own relay hardware) would require action at a scale
  above Janbogo's own — the Federation's highway planning, or the subnet's founding administrative decision.
  Per `02` §4.1, this "produces under-reporting... a shadow with no author."
- **G4's deficit** (inherited building shape sized for dozens, not a city): address is **in its own past**
  (`02` §4.1) — the remedy, "a founding structure actually sized for what the city became," is historically
  unavailable; it cannot be retrofitted after the fact, only built around. Per `02` §4.1's own evidence-check
  escalation: **are the people who could name this deficit still here?** Yes — this is not an exodus case;
  the entire present population lives with the consequence daily (a city built outward from an undersized
  core), so the deficit is nameable and current, not lost to departed witnesses. This is the ordinary "in
  its own past" case, not its harder exodus-shaped variant.
- **G8's deficit** (no demographic weight behind the founding template): address not yet determinable —
  genuinely requires either further research (what, if anything, DID carry the founding-era civic template
  forward, if not population share?) or Phase 2/8 material not yet written. Left open rather than forced.

**Address count so far: one diffuse (G2), one in-the-parent (G5), one in-its-own-past (G4), one
undetermined (G8).** Per `02` §1.E, a count above one in the PEER-REQUIRED categories should raise
