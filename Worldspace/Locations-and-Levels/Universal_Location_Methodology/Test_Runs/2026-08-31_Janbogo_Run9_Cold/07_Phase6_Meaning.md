## Canon check, run per `03` §0.3's mandatory Phase 6 target: `National_Holidays.md`

**A real, sharp finding, from a canon check rather than invention.** `National_Holidays.md` establishes a
**Tepenian Saints** category — pre-war (pre-2083) Antarctic explorers venerated as civic "Saints," honorific
first-name (St. Robert/Scott, St. Ernest/Shackleton, St. Roald/Amundsen, St. Douglas/Mawson, St.
Richard/Byrd) — a roster explicitly tied to cities named for those explorers, **including two of Janbogo's
own subnet-mates, Scott and Fort McMurdo** (Hut Point remembrance, Tepenian Independence Day).

**Janbogo structurally cannot participate in this category, and the reason is worth stating precisely.**
The Saints are pre-war Antarctic exploration-era figures. **Jang Bogo — Janbogo's own namesake — is a
9th-century Korean naval commander, with no connection to Antarctic exploration at all.** This is not a gap
in Janbogo's own civic development; it is a structural mismatch between the Saints framework's own defining
criterion (pre-war *Antarctic* figures) and what Janbogo's real-world basis actually is (a modern research
station named for a medieval maritime figure with no Antarctic connection whatsoever). **Two of Janbogo's own
subnet neighbors (Scott, Fort McMurdo) have a Saint; Janbogo structurally cannot, by the same rule that gives
them theirs** — a genuine, non-generic absence, differentiated from a simple omission.

**What this produces, per `03` Phase 6's "does canon already answer this" discipline**: Janbogo's own
founding-namesake veneration, if it exists, cannot be a Tepenian Saints observance — it would have to be a
genuinely different category, since the object of veneration (a 9th-century foreign naval commander, not an
Antarctic explorer) simply does not fit the Saints' own defining criterion. **Flagged as a REQUESTED
canon-registration question** (per `00_RUNBOOK.md` §E item 4) rather than invented outright: does Janbogo
have any civic observance of Jang Bogo at all, and if so, what category does it belong to, since the
existing four-category `National_Holidays.md` framework has no obvious slot for "a city's own inherited
namesake, venerated on their own historical merit, unconnected to Antarctica"? **This is exactly the kind of
genuinely new category `Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md` §0 calls a legitimate discovery** — not adopted
here as settled, but named and handed forward per that section's own naming/cross-referencing requirement.

## C. Death and the dead

**The obligatory question, answered from physical constraint rather than invented from nothing.** Terra Nova
Bay's coastal katabatic terrain (Phase 1, G2) is frozen and rocky — conventional ground burial is a
physically hard, likely infeasible default at scale, the same real-world constraint every actual Antarctic
station and settlement faces. **This produces a genuine candidate finding, grounded in G2 rather than in any
excluded institutional material**: Janbogo's dead are plausibly not buried conventionally, which per Phase
6 C's own distinction between the *record* and the *rite* suggests a formal **record** (a registry, a
memorial listing) may carry more civic weight here than a physical grave site does. **This is a hypothesis
grounded in physical constraint, not yet confirmed by any specific admissible institution** — REQUESTED,
flagged for the same reason `01` §1.2's Enclosed-modifier note flags this question project-wide as
genuinely open (Concordia's own mortuary problem is the standing example that this is a real, unresolved
class of question, not unique to Janbogo).

**Why the absence itself might be the finding, per Phase 6 C's own fourth option** (added at Sinheung Run 5):
of the four reasons a place might outsource its dead — lack of room, lack of anyone yet to bury, would
rather not look, or a specific named civic failure — **Janbogo's candidate reason is none of these; it is
straightforwardly physical** (frozen/rocky ground), a fifth case this run's own admissible material supports
that the existing four-reason list does not name. **Flagged as a possible fifth reason to add to the
technique**, not asserted as settled — a genuine methodology-level candidate, logged in the observations
file rather than silently adopted into `03` Phase 6 §C without developer review.
