# Phase 4 — Ordinary Life

**Asks:** What is an ordinary person's day here, apart from what this place is famous for?

**Headline function, stated in one sentence, then written away from**: *Janbogo is a coastal supply port
and Federation highway/Arcanet subnet hub.* Of 957,570 residents, only a fraction plausibly work the port,
Hwy 183, or subnet administration directly — the rest of this phase deliberately does not describe dock or
highway work as the general day.

---

## B. The four elements

**Routines.** The sharp exterior/interior split established at Phase 3 has a direct, ordinary-day
consequence: **each crossing of the threshold between inside and outside is a real cost** (gearing up,
timing against the wind forecast, using an engineered wind-break entry), not a trivial act the way it would
be in a temperate city. **Third-order consequence (LAW 0):** an ordinary resident's day plausibly organizes
around *minimizing* the number of separate outdoor crossings — batching errands, timing movement to shared
windows rather than individual convenience, clustering activity rather than spreading it across the day the
way a calmer climate would allow. This is not a stated custom; it falls directly out of G2's own established
cost structure (Phase 1).

**Mundane concerns.** Checking the wind forecast before committing to any outdoor plan, the way another
city's residents might check the time. Individual-scale rime-ice maintenance on one's own windows and
exterior surfaces — the civic-scale standing cost (Phase 1, G2/G5) has a literal household-scale echo.
Given Phase 2's composition finding (a founding-minority population overtaken by a larger grown-in
majority), ordinary daily workplace and social contact between people of different national origin is
simply normal urban texture here — not solemn, not remarked on, the unremarkable byproduct of two and a half
centuries of the "chose it" arrival mode outnumbering the founding population.

**Personal struggles.** Phase 3's seam finding (the original ~4,000 m² founding-era core versus everything
built around it over 250 years) plausibly produces an ordinary, unglamorous housing-stock disparity —
residents in older, retrofitted construction near or within the founding core dealing with awkward,
patched-together space shaped by additions to a structure never meant to hold this many people, versus
residents in later-built areas with space actually designed for a city's scale. **A mundane, structural
struggle, not a dramatic one** — exactly the register `03` Phase 4 asks for.

**Escapism/downtime.** Phase 3's compounded worst-season finding (polar night and peak katabatic intensity
overlapping almost exactly, ~May–July) has a direct downtime consequence: **whatever this population does
for recreation is disproportionately concentrated indoors and disproportionately concentrated into that
same six-to-eight-week stretch**, when outdoor options do not merely diminish but vanish entirely for weeks
at a time. This is the escapism answer this phase requires — genuinely derived from the climate profile,
not from the excluded teahouse/communal-warmth material (M-64).

## C. Not one universal resident

**At minimum two distinct ordinary days, per Phase 2's own composition finding:**
- **The founding-descended minority** (~10% national-origin, though individual identification with
  "founding heritage" doesn't map cleanly onto national-origin percentage after 250 years of mixing) — no
  admissible material distinguishes their ordinary day from the general pattern above; **left as a genuine
  null rather than invented**, since the material that would ground this (institutional/cultural specifics)
  sits in the excluded `Character & Culture` section.
- **The grown-in majority** (China/USA-plurality descended, arrived via "chose it"/"stopped while passing")
  — the general pattern above describes this population most directly, since it is derived from function
  and climate rather than founding-specific material.
- **The outdoor-exposed minority** — anyone whose work requires sustained outdoor presence (port/dock labor,
  Hwy 183 maintenance) **cannot** follow the "minimize threshold crossings" routine that organizes the rest
  of the city's day. Their day is instead organized entirely around wind-exposure windows — working when
  conditions allow, regardless of clock time, which is the sharpest single contrast this phase produces.

---

## D. Physical scale for commute/logistics texture

**Concrete, non-thematic**: batched movement (per B) means an ordinary resident's daily travel is plausibly
fewer, longer indoor-connected stretches rather than many short outdoor hops — the opposite commute shape
from an open, temperate city grid. No specific distances are grounded in admissible material; this remains
a directional, structural claim rather than a numeric one.

---

## E. What the ordinary rhythm fails to provide

**For the outdoor-exposed minority (dock/highway labor)**, the entire "minimize crossings, batch movement"
rhythm the rest of the city runs on is simply unavailable — their day cannot be organized the way the
general population's is, because their job requires the opposite of what everyone else optimizes for.
**This is the sharpest finding of the phase**, and per `03` Phase 4's own framing, is exactly the kind of
"what does the ordinary rhythm fail to provide for people whose lives don't fit it" result the phase exists
to surface.

## Differentiation axis

**Named in three or four words, per `03` §0.2 item 3**: **"organized around wind-exposure windows, not
clock time."** Distinct from a shift-based, tide-based, or light-cycle-based day (the alternatives `03`
Phase 4 itself lists) — Janbogo's day is organized around a genuinely unpredictable hazard window rather
than any of those more regular alternatives. **Not yet checked against any sibling's own axis** — flagged
for Gate 6 at Step 7 (Cape Adare Run 7 comparison).
