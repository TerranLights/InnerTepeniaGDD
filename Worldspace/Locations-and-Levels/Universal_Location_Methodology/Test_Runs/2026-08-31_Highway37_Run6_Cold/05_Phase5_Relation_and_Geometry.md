# Phase 5 — Relation & Geometry

**Named axis: "underrated importance."** Every sub-question in this phase converges on the same shape (most
sharply stated in §5c): Highway 37 looks minor from outside — two sparse cities, no prestige, no population to
speak of — and is not. **This is the axis this phase's findings share**, stated here before the sub-question
work per `03` §0.2 item 3.

**Primary phase for a Corridor**, per `03` §0.1 — most of this location's real content lives here. **Unlike the
majority-case isolated location the weighting table in `03` Phase 5 anticipates, Highway 37 has real, named
peers**: five other highways meet it at named junctions. This pass runs the fuller peer-available version of
the phase rather than defaulting to the isolated-location substitutes, while still using the own-eras
substitute for 5b (stated explicitly, per the binding rule that an unstated substitute reads as an un-run
check).

## 5a — Peers: the five junction highways

**The core move, run properly: a relation as a mechanism, never a rivalry.** Highway 37 meets five other named
highways at three physical points (per `Highways.md`'s Major Junctions Index):

**At the Sayowa Junction** (with Hwy 4 and Hwy 7-ext): a genuine three-way crossing, physically separate from
Sayowa the city itself. **What flows:** cargo bound between Mountain Pass Airport's fabrication output and the
rest of the network can transfer here — onward via Hwy 4 toward Mawson/Soyuz, or via Hwy 7-ext toward the
Atlantic coast cities. **What doesn't:** nothing in canon suggests Hwy 37's own interior traffic and the
Sayowa Junction's own considerable civic/industrial traffic (Sayowa is established as a major fabrication and
trucking/dispatch hub) actually mix in volume — Hwy 37 is one spoke among three at a junction whose real
gravity belongs to Sayowa's own economy, not to the interior plateau route passing through.

**At the interior Hwy 22 dual-junction** (between Dome Fuji and Kunlun): Hwy 37's only connection point that is
not near any city at all — a crossroads in the empty plateau interior. **The quiet friction, not the loud one**
(per Phase 5a's own instruction to prefer it): Hwy 22 is explicitly the **Transcontinental Highway**, the
continent's headline spine, passing directly through Amundsen Station and carrying the prestige of the pole
itself. Hwy 37 carries none of that prestige — it is unglamorous utility infrastructure, a route named for a
mountain cut rather than for the continent. **And yet Hwy 37, not Hwy 22, is the only physical connection to
Kunlun and Vostok** — the two most extreme, most isolated cities in Tepenia. **The asymmetry is real and
uncontested**: the prestige road does not reach the places that most need reaching; the unglamorous one does.
Neither highway would frame this as a rivalry — it is simply how the network happens to be shaped — but it is
exactly the kind of quiet, structural imbalance Phase 5a asks a pass to notice rather than the more obvious,
louder story a headline "Transcontinental" name would suggest.

**At Concordia's outer ring** (with Hwy 110 and Hwy 183): Concordia is a genuine terminus for all three. **What
differentiates Hwy 37 among Concordia's three connecting highways is real and worth naming as its own axis:**
Hwy 110 (the Coastal Cut Highway) and Hwy 183 (the Janbogo Highway) both hug the coastline, linking Concordia to
the Ross Sea and East Antarctic coastal cities. **Hwy 37 alone reaches the deep interior.** Of Concordia's three
highway connections, exactly one touches the plateau at all — everything Concordia knows about the interior
beyond its own dome, physically, arrives via this single road.

## 5b — The three-way differentiation set: own-eras substitute, stated explicitly

**No second qualifying "hardest friction" exists to build a genuine three-way peer contrast**, per §5a above —
Hwy 22's asymmetry is real but singular; there is no second highway relationship sharp enough to complete a
three-way set. **Per `01` §5.3a and `03` Phase 5's own substitute note, this sub-question is run against the
corridor's own eras instead**: its establishment period versus its now-routine, mature operation (the same
two-state contrast already used for the Frame Declaration's sibling-set substitute and for Phase 1's G6 tense
axis).

**Category chosen: how a traveler experiences the corridor's own junctions.** At establishment, a junction with
another highway was presumably a bare physical crossing, marked only as much as immediate necessity required.
**Now, mature**, per Phase 3 §D's Retroactive Mechanism finding, junctions carry deliberate direction-independent
marking infrastructure — a genuine, dateable maturation of the same physical point, driven by the accumulated
practical need for reliable wayfinding across repeated crossings rather than by any single decision. **This
is a modest but real own-eras finding**, and is recorded honestly as thinner than the peer-based material in
§5a, per the substitute's own standing caveat that it is a fallback, not automatically as productive as genuine
peers.

## 5c — The parent

**Per the provisional assumption registered in Phase 0 (no single centralized maintenance authority), Hwy 37's
"parent" is diffuse by design** — no polity administers it as a single entity. **What the wider Federation needs
from it, though never stated by any single named institution, is inferable from what depends on it:** reliable
chamber output (part of the nationwide Cradle infrastructure, a genuine national interest) and physical
connectivity to two cities whose scientific output (Kunlun's astronomy program, Vostok's Lake Vostok research)
is independently significant.

**Where belief and reality disagree — the finding this sub-question is built to find:** a highway connecting
only two sparse, unglamorous interior cities, in a country of 35, is easy to underrate from outside as a minor
spur. **It is not one.** It is the sole physical channel to the country's highest-altitude city and its coldest,
and it carries a genuinely irreplaceable national manufacturing output. **The mismatch between Hwy 37's likely
perceived importance (low — it serves nobody populous) and its actual importance (disproportionate — it serves
the irreplaceable) is itself the finding**, and it is a mismatch nobody along the corridor is positioned to
correct, since nobody administers the road as a whole to notice the gap.

## 5d — The edge and the crossing

**Arrival types, enumerated rather than assumed to be one:** convoy crews entering from the Sayowa Junction
(coastal end); convoy crews and rotating personnel entering from Concordia (capital end); and personnel
transferring in specifically to Mountain Pass Airport's own posting, arriving via either direction depending on
whether they originate from Kunlun or Vostok's own side of the route.

**What converts a visitor into a member, for the one place on this corridor where membership is even a
meaningful question (Mountain Pass Airport itself):** per `Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md` Technique 10
(Membership by Unremarked Persistence), applied to a shift/rotation-based local economy — **the mechanism is
completing one full rotation, including its worst stretch, not a ceremony or an announcement.** Nobody at
Mountain Pass Airport is formally welcomed as a "real" member of the crew; **they simply stop being treated as
new once they have been through a full posting cycle once**, the same pattern the technique predicts for any
shift-based economy. **No canon names this explicitly — it is derived here, consistently with the arrival-mode
and rotation-clock findings already established in Phase 2 and Phase 4.**

**The asymmetry check, run directly (`04` Gate 9): what happens to someone the mechanism decides against — a
crew member whose rotation is cut short (injury, an emergency at home, an early reassignment) before it
completes?** The favorable path (complete the rotation, become a member) is the one that gets written by
default; the mechanism's other direction is not. **Answered here rather than left silent:** nothing in this
location's own established character (no malice, no punitive institutions, per Phase 7c's reassignment finding)
supports treating an interrupted rotation as a permanent mark against someone. **There is a route back:** a
return posting later, completed in full, earns the same standing a first attempt would have. **The cost is
delay, not exclusion** — the interrupted rotation simply doesn't count, and nobody is worse off for having
tried once and not finished, consistent with the same non-punitive discipline already established elsewhere in
this pass.

## 5e — Dependency, and who actually knows about it

**Phase 1's G6 profile already named the core dependency: the outpost cannot secure or control its own power
supply, running instead on Amundsen Tower's grid overflow.** This sub-question asks the relational question
Phase 1 did not: **who knows this, specifically?**

**Vostok's and Kunlun's own engineering staffs know it precisely** — they built and staff the arrangement, and
per Phase 4 §B, actively monitor it as part of the outpost's own daily maintenance routine. **Nothing in canon
suggests this dependency is known, tracked, or even considered anywhere else** — not by Concordia (which,
per §5c, likely underrates Hwy 37's importance generally), not by any other subnet, not by any named Federation
institution. **A dependency named and tracked by exactly the two parties who built it, and invisible to
everyone the outpost's own output actually serves, is a textbook `00d` shadow candidate**: unintended (nobody
designed the outpost to be this fragile on purpose — remoteness alone forced it, per Phase 1's founding
reasoning), unnoticed outside the two founding cities, and discoverable only by someone who goes looking at the
outpost's own operations rather than at its output. **Flagged forward to Phase 6 and the Review Panel rather
than resolved here** — per `00d`'s own governing rule, the shadow is filled in by the capability/relation frame
honestly, not derived separately and then checked.
