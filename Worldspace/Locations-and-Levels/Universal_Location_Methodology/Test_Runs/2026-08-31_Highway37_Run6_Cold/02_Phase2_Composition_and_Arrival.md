# Phase 2 — Composition & Arrival

**Per `03` §0.1's applicability table, a Corridor replaces Composition & Arrival with →users**: there is no
resident population to compose, so this phase asks *who uses this road, and by what route did they come to be
doing that* — the arrival-mode taxonomy applied to usage rather than residency.

---

## A. The population that exists at all: Mountain Pass Airport's joint-venture staff

**The only population Highway 37 has, anywhere along its length, is Band 1: the small staffed crew at Mountain
Pass Airport** (per Phase 0's Frame Declaration). Per `01` §2.3's Band-1 inversion, **there is no general
population to generalize to here — the correct answer is to name the individuals and their variance**, not
to average them into an abstraction. This pass cannot name real individuals (Phase 10's role-placeholder rule
applies, and no named figures exist in canon for this outpost), but it can and must name the **composition
split**, which is itself the sharpest available fact:

**Kunlun's contribution to the joint staff is necessarily all-robot** — Kunlun's own census is 0 humans /
123,449 robots (`Specs/Kunlun.md`), a deliberately curated astronomy-and-comms population with no human
residents at all. **Vostok's contribution is mixed** — its own research population includes both humans and
robots, per its own file's phrasing ("no single individual — human or robot — fully understands" the Vostok
research archive). **This produces an asymmetry the joint venture did not design and cannot avoid: one of its
two founding cities can only ever send one kind of person.** A visitor meeting the crew would find Kunlun's
delegation uniformly robotic and Vostok's delegation mixed — not by policy, simply because that is what each
city's own population *is*.

**Arrival mode for this staff: Assigned / posted**, per `03` Phase 2's taxonomy — an institution (the joint
venture itself) sent them, not personal choice in the ordinary sense. This produces exactly what the taxonomy
predicts: **a rotation clock.** Nobody at Mountain Pass Airport is there permanently in the way a city resident
is; everyone there knows, at some level, when their posting ends. **This is worth stating plainly because it
is the single most load-bearing composition fact for every later phase:** Mountain Pass Airport has no
"natives" in the Phase 2 sense — a place with *only* assigned staff and a fixed rotation has no population that
was "born here," and that absence is itself the finding (per Phase 2's own note that a place written entirely
from arrivals has no natives, and the natives' absence is worth stating rather than silently omitted).

---

## B. The corridor's actual traffic — freight, not residents

**Two directions of documented flow**, per Phase 1's G5 profile:

1. **Outbound**: fabrication-synthesis chambers, shipped from Mountain Pass Airport via the highway/airport
   network to cities across the region. This is the corridor's one confirmed, regular, high-value traffic.
2. **Inbound (REQUESTED, not directly documented)**: whatever sustains the outpost itself — food, fuel, spare
   parts, and the personnel-rotation traffic implied by §A above. Nothing in canon quantifies this, but its
   existence is a near-certain inference from the outpost's own continued operation (a staffed facility this
   remote cannot be self-sufficient in consumables, per the same logic already applied to its power supply in
   Phase 1).

**Arrival mode for whoever runs this traffic: Assigned / posted**, same as the outpost staff — hauliers,
pilots, and escort personnel moving along a fixed, scheduled route are doing a job, not making a personal
choice about this specific corridor. **No canon names a dedicated "Highway 37 haulier" role or trade**; this
pass records that absence rather than inventing a named trade prematurely (see Phase 8 for where this gap is
revisited against the general-population discipline).

---

## C. Who is not here, and why that is worth stating

**Per Phase 2's own instruction ("ask who is not here"):** Highway 37 has **no confirmed tourists, no
hitchhikers (the REQUESTED item from the pre-flight is still open), no settled families, and no permanent
maintenance crew living along its length** (per the provisional distributed-maintenance assumption, whoever
maintains it does so from the cities it touches, not from a base camp on the road itself). **This is not a
gap in this pass's research — it is the honest content of a Band 0 corridor**, and it should not be filled in
to make the phase feel more populated than the location actually is.

**A genuinely open question, flagged rather than invented:** does anyone travel this route for reasons *other*
than the joint venture's own logistics — a scientific team headed to Vostok's Lake Vostok program, an
astronomy specialist headed to Kunlun, a survey or maintenance crew checking the route itself? **Plausible, and
consistent with the founding-era construction implied by the road's own existence, but not documented anywhere
found.** REQUESTED: any canon naming a second, independent traffic type on this highway, distinct from the
joint venture's own shipments.

---

## D. Differentiation axis

**Named axis: "assigned-only, rotation-clocked, and asymmetric by origin."** Every highway in Tepenia presumably
carries *some* traffic; what differentiates Highway 37's specific composition, once any is written, is that its
one confirmed population is entirely posted (no residents, no chosen arrivals, no fled-to-it refugees) and
split unevenly by an accident of its own two founding cities' demographics rather than by any deliberate design.
**No sibling culture pass exists on any other highway to check this axis against** (per the Frame Declaration's
stated substitute use), so this is recorded as a first entry for a future differentiation table rather than
checked against an existing one.

**Null recorded, with reason:** the "chose it" and "fled to it" arrival modes (`03` Phase 2's taxonomy) produce
nothing for this location — there is no evidence anyone selected Mountain Pass Airport for its own qualities
(as opposed to being posted there), and no evidence of anyone arriving here because somewhere else failed.
**This is expected for a Band 1, fully-assigned population** and is recorded as a genuine null rather than a
gap.
