## A. G2 — Physical & Environmental Constraint

**Grounding research, logged in full in `Highway37_Research_Log.md`.** The route's real-world elevation profile,
verified by web search against Wikipedia/institutional sources, not recalled:

| Waypoint (in route order) | Real-world basis | Elevation (ASL) |
|---|---|---|
| The Sayowa Junction | near Syowa Station (Japan), East Ongul Island | **29 m** |
| Dome Fuji | Dome Fuji Station (Japan) | **3,810 m** *(established project canon, `02` §2 G2)* |
| *(Hwy 22 dual-junction)* | — | between the above two figures |
| Kunlun | near Dome Argus / Dome A, the East Antarctic Ice Sheet's highest point | **4,093 m** *(established project canon, `Specs/Kunlun.md`)* |
| Mountain Pass Airport | unspecified exact point between Kunlun and Vostok | **not stated — REQUESTED, but bounded between the two neighbors' figures, so somewhere in the ~3,500–4,000 m band regardless of the exact answer** |
| Vostok | Vostok Station (Russia) | **3,488 m** |
| Concordia | Concordia Station / Dome C (France–Italy) — **confirmed existing project canon**, `Specs/Concordia.md`: *"3,233 meters above sea level, deep inland, far from any coast"* | **3,233 m** |

**The finding, and it is load-bearing for everything else in this pass:** Highway 37 makes almost its entire
elevation change **once, near its own beginning**, and never gives it back. From the Sayowa Junction it climbs
roughly **3,780 vertical meters** to Dome Fuji, gains a further ~280 m to Kunlun (the highest point *any*
Tepenian city or road touches), then **descends only ~860 m over its remaining length to Concordia** — the
Federation's own capital, which is itself already deep on the high plateau. **There is no return to sea level,
or to anything resembling it, anywhere on this road after its first quarter.** A traveler who begins at the
coast experiences one enormous climb and then chronic high-altitude exposure for the rest of the journey,
*including on arrival* — Concordia's own file already establishes that its own residents require
acclimatization (`Specs/Concordia.md` §Altitude effects). **Highway 37 is therefore not a road that visits high
altitude; it is a road that, after its first quarter, never leaves it.**

**What this environment gives freely:** a viable line at all. The route is explicitly **"an East Antarctic
plateau traverse — not through the Transantarctic Mountains"** (`Highways.md`) — the plateau's relative
flatness, compared to the mountain ranges Hwys 22 and 175 have to route around or through, is what makes a
continuous road possible here in the first place. **What it withholds absolutely:** any low-altitude rest point
between Dome Fuji and Concordia — a span covering four of the route's six named waypoints and roughly 1,900+ km
of the corridor's stated length. **What it demands continuously:** acclimatization discipline from anyone
using more than the coastal segment, cold-weather survival equipment for the entire length, and (per the
REQUESTED chokepoint item) very likely seasonal restriction given East Antarctica's own documented polar-night
conditions. **What it permits but punishes:** traveling the full length without acclimatization stops — nothing
physically stops a traveler from attempting Sayowa Junction to Kunlun in one push, but doing so risks the
altitude-sickness symptoms Concordia's own file already names for *arrival alone*, compounded by never
returning below ~3,200 m again.

**Four-quadrant profile (G2):**

| Quadrant | Reading |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | The only viable overland line across an interior most of the continent's ranges make impassable — plateau topology, not engineering, is what makes this corridor possible at all. |
| **DEFICIT** | Cannot offer a low-altitude respite anywhere past its first quarter; the climb happens once, near the start, with no intermediate acclimatization stop named in canon. |
| **STANDING COST** | Continuous altitude/cold burden paid in full by every crossing, with no population resident anywhere along it to distribute, normalize, or infrastructure-harden the cost — see the shape note below. |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | Off-route deviation onto the open plateau is permitted (nothing polices it) and punished severely (whiteout, unmarked hazard, no rescue infrastructure); unacclimatized full-length travel is permitted and punished bodily. |

## B. G5 — Network Position

**From `Highways.md`'s own junction table and `Specs/Vostok.md`/`Specs/Kunlun.md`'s corrected connectivity
sections** (all admissible — route/connectivity facts, not culture-pass conclusions):

Highway 37 is the **sole physical link** connecting Kunlun and Vostok — both Mirny-subnet cities — to Concordia,
which belongs to no subnet at all. This link exists **despite, not because of, their shared subnet
membership**: Kunlun and Vostok's actual Arcanet subnet hub, Mirny, sits ~1,200–1,260 km away in a *completely
different direction*, unreached by this or any other highway. **Hwy 37 physically pulls its two settled
touchpoints toward a city that isn't their own subnet's center, while their real subnet capital stays
overland-unreachable by road.** *(Note: the informational Arcanet severance between subnets is itself a
post-war consequence of the Planetary Split Brain and is deliberately not used as a reinforcing fact here — in
this frame, Kunlun and Vostok's Arcanet link to Mirny is presumed intact. The finding stands on the physical
geometry alone: this road was never going to be the route home to their own subnet, war or no war.)* **Kunlun
and Vostok are physically oriented toward Concordia, not toward their own subnet.**

**Direction and volume, per `02` G5's instruction to ask both:** physical goods move in one direction only —
fabrication-synthesis chambers shipped **outward** from Mountain Pass Airport, via this highway and its airport,
to cities across the region. Nothing in canon states an inbound flow of comparable weight; the corridor's role
for its two settled endpoints is **export**, not resupply-by-road (their remoteness implies air/rare-overland
resupply for consumables, not documented here). **This pass does not use this same road's later, post-war
reachability role as an input** — that is a separate fact about a different frame, per the Frame Declaration's
correction.

**Four-quadrant profile (G5):**

| Quadrant | Reading |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | Sole physical channel to two cities the Arcanet cannot reach at all — where every other connection failed, this one didn't. |
| **DEFICIT** | Cannot connect its own two settled touchpoints to their actual subnet capital; every kilometer of Hwy 37 runs *away* from Mirny, never toward it. |
| **STANDING COST** | An asymmetric export relationship (chambers shipped out) with no correspondingly documented inbound flow — the corridor gives its endpoints presence elsewhere without evident return traffic. |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | Physical resupply via this route is not forbidden to Kunlun or Vostok, but the distance and altitude make it slow and costly enough that it functions as a last resort, tolerated rather than relied on. |

## C. G6 — Defining Event (the founding of the Mountain Pass joint venture)

> **Corrected 2026-08-31, at the developer's direct instruction — see `01_Frame_Typology_and_Inheritance.md`
> §4.1 and observations log M-45.** This section originally used the Long Night War and Amundsen Tower's
> destruction as Highway 37's defining event. **That is a post-war fact and does not belong in this pass**,
> which is written on the Second Interwar Period's own neutral baseline terms, before the war and before the
> Tower's destruction — this location's own merit, on its own terms, not the war's aftermath layered onto it.
> The section below is re-run against the one dateable event this frame actually contains.

The corridor's one settled point, Mountain Pass Airport, hosts **genuine, functioning cross-city cooperation**:
a joint venture between Vostok and Kunlun, staffed by scientists and engineers from both, manufacturing
fabrication-synthesis chambers that ship out along this same road. **This is notable precisely because Vostok
and Kunlun have no other established institutional link** — they share a subnet (Mirny) but sit roughly
1,200–1,260 km apart from its actual hub and from each other's cities directly; nothing else in canon connects
them administratively. The joint venture is, per existing canon, **"a concrete, physical expression of the
already-established 'two loneliest outposts' relationship"** — two cities whose defining shared trait is
isolation chose, deliberately, to answer it by building something together rather than separately.

The outpost runs on residual overflow from Amundsen Tower's continent-wide regulated grid, **not its own
dedicated power infrastructure** — because the outpost is too remote to support one economically. **This is a
structural fact of the arrangement, not a foreshadowed vulnerability**: in this frame, the Tower exists, the
overflow works, and the outpost is genuinely productive. **This pass does not know, and must not write as if it
knows, that this dependency will ever matter.**

**Four-quadrant profile (G6):**

| Quadrant | Reading |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | Two of Tepenia's most isolated cities, with no other administrative link between them, jointly built and staff real, producing infrastructure across ~1,200+ km of separation — a cooperative capacity that did not have to exist and was not compelled by anything above them. |
| **DEFICIT** | Neither founding city controls, or could unilaterally replace, the single external resource (Tower-grid overflow) the whole venture runs on. They founded a joint *operation*, not joint *self-sufficiency* — the thing that makes it work was never theirs to secure. |
| **STANDING COST** | Continuously staffing and provisioning a small, high-altitude, extremely remote outpost with no local resource base of its own — someone from each city is always, indefinitely, rotating through a marginal posting far from home. |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | Running the venture on borrowed power nobody administers locally is permitted (nothing stops it, nobody polices it) and quietly tolerated rather than ideal — the arrangement works, and works well enough that neither city has built the dedicated plant that would remove the dependency. |

**Tense axis, per `01` §5.3a's instruction to check it explicitly:** all four cells are present-tense and
load-bearing **within this frame** — there is no past/present split to report here, unlike the withdrawn
post-war reading. The own-eras substitute for this generator (per the Frame Declaration's corrected sibling-set
note) is instead the venture's **establishment period versus its now-routine, mature operation** — both states
sit inside this same neutral frame and require no reach across the war boundary.

## E. The spine finding — dependency without control

**The both-are-true test, run per `02` §5.3, on a case that turns out not to need it — three readings that
already agree are not a conflict to resolve, but the convergence is worth stating as its own finding rather than
letting it sit inside three separate deficit cells.**

**One property, expressed three ways: everything Highway 37 and its one settled point need comes from
somewhere they do not administer.** The road cannot supply its own altitude relief — that is simply geography,
and geography answers to nobody. The road's two settled touchpoints cannot supply their own subnet affiliation
— the remedy exists at a scale above them, in a hub 1,200+ km away in the wrong direction. And the joint venture
at Mountain Pass cannot supply its own power — the remedy exists in a nationwide grid neither founding city
built or controls. **Three different deficits, three different scales, one identical shape: this location's
entire structure runs on resources it depends on and does not own.**

**The deficit's address, per `02` §4.1, splits cleanly by which dependency is being asked about:**
- **The network deficit (cannot reach Mirny) addresses "in the parent"** — the remedy exists only at the scale
  of the Mirny subnet's own hub, which this corridor structurally cannot reach; admitting the deficit would
  mean admitting Kunlun and Vostok cannot manage their own subnet affiliation without help from a place 1,200+
  km away in the wrong direction.
- **The power deficit (cannot secure its own supply) also addresses "in the parent"** — the remedy is
  Amundsen Tower's own continent-wide grid, an authority and an infrastructure scale genuinely above anything
  the two founding cities could build for one small outpost on their own.
- **The altitude deficit (no relief past the first quarter) addresses "diffuse"** — there is no counterparty at
  all, nobody to petition, and per `02` §4.1's own harder case, **no critic could ever form**: a Band 0 corridor
  has no resident population to notice the deficit as a grievance in the first place. This is the sharpest of
  the three, precisely because it is the only one where a fix cannot even be imagined as belonging to somebody.

**A count of three addresses is worth checking rather than accepting** (per `02` §4.1's "a count above one
should raise suspicion"): confirmed genuinely distinct above — one geographic (diffuse), two institutional
(in the parent, at two different scales: a subnet hub and a national grid). Not one deficit restated three
times.

**Why this matters for everything downstream:** a location whose every dependency answers to an authority it
cannot reach — a subnet hub, a national grid, or nothing at all — is a location that can never, on its own
terms, become self-sufficient. **This sets up Phase 5** (the mandatory, primary phase for a Corridor) to be
substantially about that dependency's relational shape, and Phase 7 (Order) to ask directly who actually holds
the authority this location's whole operation quietly depends on.

## F. Reading the shape

**Per `02` §4, and per the Type-variance guidance in that same file: "A Corridor's strength is throughput and
its standing cost is almost the whole profile."** Checking the filled cells against that prediction, honestly:

- **STRENGTH:** one strong, well-grounded entry (uniqueness/only-ness), agreed by all three generators.
- **DEFICIT:** three related entries, converging on one spine finding (§E).
- **STANDING COST:** all three generators fire, and the content is substantial (continuous altitude burden,
  asymmetric export, continuous remote staffing) — and, notably, none of it is paid by any resident population,
  since none exists along the corridor itself.
- **GRUDGING TOLERANCE:** all three fire, in three distinct registers.

**Shape: COST-DOMINANT**, matching the type prediction rather than being assumed from it — the STANDING COST and
GRUDGING TOLERANCE quadrants together substantially outweigh STRENGTH, and this held before checking what the
type guidance predicted, not after.

> **Per `02` §4.0, the mandatory input-set disclosure:** this shape is reported from an admitted input set of
> **G2 (physical), G5 (network), and G6 (defining event) — all confirmed canon, nothing withheld, because
> nothing culture-conclusion-shaped exists for this location to withhold in the first place.** This is a
> genuinely different situation from every prior run: Zhongshan's and Sinheung's shape readings depended on
> *which* known institutions were admitted or quarantined. Highway 37 has no institutions to admit or quarantine
> — **the cost-dominant shape here is not an artifact of a chosen input set; it is the reading of the entire set
> of things that exist to be read.** This is itself worth recording as a methodology finding (M-44, shared log):
> **the input-set caveat in `02` §4.0 is conditional on there being a withholdable second input set at all**, and
> a genuinely thin location may be the first case where that condition doesn't hold.

**The unlocking question for a cost-dominant shape** (`02` §4): *is this place doing anything other than
continuing to exist?* Answered directly: **yes, genuinely** — it carries real, ongoing economic output (the
chambers, shipped outward) and sustains a real cooperative institution between two cities with no other bond.
But **the road's identity is not that traffic; it is the fact that using it at all costs something, every time,
that nothing along its own length ever pays down.** Every crossing starts the debt over — the venture's own
productivity does not offset the corridor's standing cost, it simply coexists with it.
