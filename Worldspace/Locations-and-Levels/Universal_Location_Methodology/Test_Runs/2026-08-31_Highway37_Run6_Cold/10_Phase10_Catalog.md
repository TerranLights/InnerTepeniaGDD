# Phase 10 — Catalog

**Mandatory for Corridor**, per `03` §0.1. Everything below is refracted through the pass's own established
theme — dependency without control, a road that climbs once and never returns — rather than a literal
restatement of it.

---

## A. Named places and landmarks

**Already established, formalized here rather than re-derived:** the Sayowa Junction (the three-way crossing
with Hwy 4 and Hwy 7-ext, distinct from Sayowa the city, per `project_sayowa_junction_correction`); the Sayowa
Spur (the connecting road linking Sayowa proper to the Junction); Dome Fuji; the Hwy 22 dual-junction (the
unmarked interior crossroads, Phase 5 §5a); Mountain Pass Airport itself; Kunlun; Vostok; Concordia's outer
ring terminus, where Hwy 37 meets Hwy 110 and Hwy 183.

**One new named place, produced by this pass:** **the reopening line** — an informal, unofficial marker (likely
nothing more than the first standing junction-marker tag past the worst of the seasonal-closure stretch) that
convoy crews treat as the practical "the road is passable again" point, distinct from any officially declared
reopening, since Phase 7b already established that no office actually makes that declaration. **Not a built
structure — a recognized point on the map that exists only because crews have informally agreed it exists.**

## B. Physical things

- **Junction-marker tags** — the direction-independent route markers (Phase 3 §D), doubling off-duty as
  "Waypoint" game tokens (Phase 4 §A / Phase 8).
- **Fabrication-synthesis chambers** — the outpost's headline cargo, in transit.
- **Canned rations** — the human population's general food baseline (Phase 8, corrected).
- **A carried, non-standard robot-coffee kit** — the one Band-1 fragility object (Phase 4 §B).
- **Portable music players and a small number of lightweight instruments** (acoustic guitar, accordion) —
  Phase 8's "whatever fits in the cab" finding, made concrete and catalogable.
- **The convoy vehicles themselves** — per Phase 3 §B's researched real-world comparable (a tractor-and-sledge
  convoy with onboard living quarters), adapted rather than copied: Tepenian engineering plausibly exceeds the
  real-world SPoT's equipment in reliability, but the vehicles are not otherwise specified in canon and are
  recorded here as a REQUESTED detail for a future pass wanting exact specifications, not invented to false
  precision now.

## C. People, as role-archetypes only (no invented proper names, per the binding rule)

- **A veteran convoy driver** who has run the full crossing more times than anyone else currently posted —
  the natural mentor figure implied by Phase 7c's apprenticeship finding.
- **A first-rotation technician**, still paired with a mentor, still short of the "member" status Phase 5 §5d
  establishes.
- **The Vostok-origin robot who still brews real coffee** — the Band-1 fragility figure, cast without a name.
- **Whichever mixed crew happens to be present for a given season's reopening** — deliberately unnamed and
  uncounted, since the reopening line (§A above) belongs to whoever is there, not to a fixed cast.

## D. Settings — atmospheric and situational textures, distinct from named landmarks

- **A whiteout crossing** — the corridor's own textureless first-impressions finding (Phase 3 §C), at its most
  acute.
- **The outpost's one warm common room** — the sole interior on the whole corridor where Phase 3 §D's
  sensory contrast (open exterior vs. enclosed institution) is actually inhabited day to day.
- **A convoy cab during a multi-day crossing** — the setting for §B's music/media finding and for the "Waypoint"
  minigame alike.
- **A junction stop for marker-tag maintenance or repair** — a small, mundane, recurring situation implied
  directly by the tags' own load-bearing safety function (Phase 3 §D), not yet named anywhere in canon.

## E. Significant events and notable figures — placeholders, per the binding naming rule

**Significant events:** the founding of the Mountain Pass joint venture (Phase 1 §C — already the pass's own
G6 defining event, formalized here as a catalog entry as well); the corridor's own construction (thin — see the
still-open REQUESTED item from the pre-flight); the recurring seasonal reopening (Phase 6 §E).

**Notable figures:** none named in canon and none invented here — the role-archetypes in §C above are the
correct form this category takes at this population scale, per Phase 10's own binding rule.

## F. Border-adjacency texture — checked, used lightly

**Per Phase 10's own caution not to force this:** the Sayowa Junction sits close enough to Sayowa's own
established industrial/trucking character (Phase 5 §5a) that some bleed-across is plausible — a convoy crew
passing through would register Sayowa's own heavier civic traffic as a contrast to the corridor's own emptiness,
without this pass claiming the Junction *is* Sayowa in miniature. **Not pursued further at Concordia's end** —
Concordia's own dome-and-corridor architecture (`Specs/Concordia.md`) is extensively established elsewhere and
this pass does not attempt to re-derive Concordia's own character from one highway terminus.

## G. Differentiation axis

**Named axis: "nothing here exists for its own sake."** Every entry in §A–D exists because it serves the
crossing itself — a landmark is a junction or a waypoint, a physical thing is cargo or crossing-equipment, a
role exists to move something or someone through, a setting is a moment within a crossing. **This is the axis
that would separate Highway 37's catalog from a settled location's**, where a meaningful fraction of what gets
catalogued typically exists independent of any single function. Nothing catalogued here does.
