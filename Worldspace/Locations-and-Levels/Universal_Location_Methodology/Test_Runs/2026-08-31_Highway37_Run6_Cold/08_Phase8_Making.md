**Rare for Corridor**, per `03` §0.1 — this phase depends heavily on Phases 1-7, which is exactly what makes it
usable here despite the type-rarity flag: this pass has real spine material to draw on rather than reaching for

## Play, sports, humor, leisure — "Waypoint," formalized

**Full derivation already run in Phase 4 §A**; recorded here as this phase's proper home for the mechanic
itself, per `03` Phase 8's component list. **Materials:** repurposed junction-marker tags (Phase 3 §D).
**Core mechanic:** ascending-value route-building with an overload/bust threshold, modeling Phase 1 §E's
never-descend altitude finding directly. **Special piece:** the bidirectional marker token, echoing the Hwy
22 crossing's own real infrastructure. **Win condition:** three completed routes, pulled from Concordia's own
real three-highway terminus count (Phase 5 §5a). **Deck flavor:** Kunlun-marked (precise, machine-uniform) vs.
Vostok-marked (visibly improvised), per the composition asymmetry (Phase 2). **Status, restated: `Proposed:`
seed material**, not a balanced or playtested design.

**Named axis: "the bust is the corridor's own signature, not a borrowed one."** Where a generic card game
might use an arbitrary target number, this game's central tension is derived directly from this specific
location's own strongest finding — a portability check any future differentiation table should apply if
another highway or corridor is ever run through this methodology and produces its own game.

## Language and speech markers

**No slang or jargon documented anywhere in existing canon for this corridor** — expected, given how thin the
existing material is. **One plausible derivation, offered cautiously and flagged as invented rather than
formalized from anything existing:** a convoy crew moving the same fixed route repeatedly would very likely
develop **short, functional names for its own waypoints** distinct from their official highway-file names — the
same pattern any working crew produces for a route they know intimately. **Not populated with specific invented
terms here**, since doing so without any canon anchor risks exactly the "vivid but ungrounded" failure `03`
Phase 8 warns against; **recorded as a REQUESTED-adjacent open item** for a future pass with more specific
research time to develop properly, rather than manufactured now to fill the slot.

## Cuisine — all populations present, per the discipline's own hardest-failing category

**Per `03` Phase 8's explicit warning that this category fails more than any other**, and its requirement to
cover the non-human population's version, not skip it: **rationing is the general condition for both
populations, but it takes a different physical form for each — worth stating precisely rather than collapsing
both into one shared synthesizer answer.** *(Corrected 2026-08-31, at the developer's direct instruction.)*

**For the human minority (Vostok-origin only, per Phase 9 §A): canned food, specifically** — not a synthesizer
producing fresh-analog meals, but genuine canned/preserved rations, the concrete, low-tech, easily-transported
answer a convoy-and-outpost logistics chain this remote and this cargo-constrained would actually produce. This
is the general baseline for every human present, not a minority exception. **For the robot population:**
coolant and siligel-related maintenance needs (Phase 9 §B) are the equivalent baseline category, run on their
own separate supply logic, not a robot-flavored version of the human ration.

**The one Band-1 fragility exception, still standing on top of this corrected general answer:** one Vostok-
origin robot crew member's real, carried-stock robot coffee, brewed rather than drawn from standard supply —
a narrow, explicitly-scoped minority practice sitting on top of the general canned/rationed baseline, exactly
the shape `00b` requires (general answer first, vivid minority practice clearly scoped on top of it, never as a
replacement for it).

## Dress and appearance

**Start from function, per the phase's own instruction, not aesthetics.** Cold-and-altitude survival gear is
the only plausible answer this location's own established conditions (Phase 1) support — no canon exists for
anything beyond the functional baseline, and none is invented here past that baseline. **Named axis: "gear as
the entire wardrobe"** — unlike a settled city where fashion diverges from utility over time, a Band 0-1
transit/outpost population this small and this recently established (within this pass's own frame) has not had
the two centuries a district gets to develop custom-becoming-fashion (per `Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md`
Technique 6's own logic) — **the practical necessity has not yet had time to accrete meaning**, and this pass
records that honestly rather than inventing a fashion culture ahead of its own timeline.

## Music, arts, craft

**Corrected 2026-08-31, at the developer's direct instruction — not a null after all.** The prior draft
concluded this category was too thin at this population scale to support anything; that reasoning skipped the
one generator that actually applies regardless of scale: **G2, run against the corridor's own vehicle-cargo
constraint.** Whatever music exists here has to fit inside a convoy vehicle or the outpost's own cargo
allowance — the same physical-transport logic Phase 1 already applies to everything else about this location.

**Two forms, both function-derived, kept distinct:**
1. **Pre-recorded media, played on a portable player** — the Tepenian equivalent of a personal music device,
   carried the same way any small personal effect would be. The general baseline: most of what's heard on a
   crossing is this, not live performance, simply because it requires nothing more than the device itself.
2. **Physical instruments, but only the kind that survive a convoy cargo hold** — **no pianos, no pipe organs,
   no drum kits**; the physical-constraint filter rules out anything large, fragile, or requiring a fixed
   installation. **Acoustic guitars and accordions are the characteristic answer**: lightweight, durable,
   requiring no power, and portable by one person — instruments that were never designed with this route in
   mind but happen to satisfy its one hard requirement anyway.

**Named axis: "whatever fits in the cab."** Every instrument or recording present here is present *because* it
survives the same cargo-weight-and-space logic that governs everything else this pass has established about
convoy travel — not because of any musical tradition specific to this crew. **This is a thin but genuine
answer, not an invented rich one**: it names what's plausible without asserting a developed musical culture
this population's own scale (per Phase 9) has not had time to build.
