# Phase 3 — Surface & Texture

**Mandatory for Corridor.** A Corridor's texture, per `03`'s Type variance note, is **its waypoints and its
surface underfoot** — there is no building stock or street life to describe, so this phase is carried almost
entirely by the road itself and its one enclosed installation.

---

## A. Formalize first — what already exists, unlabeled

`Highways.md` already establishes: an East Antarctic **plateau traverse**, explicitly **"not through the
Transantarctic Mountains"** (distinguishing it from routes like Hwy 22 that do cross mountain terrain); a
**bidirectional dual-junction** with Hwy 22 between Dome Fuji and Kunlun; a named waypoint (Mountain Pass
Airport) that is explicitly **not** a highway-to-highway junction. None of this was written as "texture," but
it is: a plateau traverse is a specific kind of surface (open, largely featureless, snow/ice), and a named
"bidirectional" junction is already gesturing at a texture question (§D below chases why that word is there
at all).

---

## B. Research — a real-world comparable, properly researched (not recalled)

**G7, per `02` §2**: the closest real-world case to "a marked overland route across the East Antarctic interior
plateau, connecting a coastal point to interior stations" is the actual **South Pole Traverse (SPoT)** — the
~1,601 km flagged route linking McMurdo Station to Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station. Researched directly
(logged in full in `Highway37_Research_Log.md`), not recalled from category-level familiarity with "polar
routes":

- **Not paved — flagged.** The real SPoT is "an approximately 995-mile-long flagged route over compacted snow
  and ice," built by "levelling snow and filling in crevasses," with flags marking the route rather than any
  fixed roadbed.
- **Convoy-based, not individual-vehicle.** Travel is by "a caravan of powerful tractors pulling a string of
  around 5 connected sledges with living quarters for the drivers and cargo," a journey of roughly 40 days
  one-way for the full route.
- **Actively re-maintained, not built once.** Pre-season surveys use ground-penetrating radar to detect
  crevasses in shear zones; **post-traverse re-grooming compacts the surface to maintain the flagged route**,
  and voids are filled and bridges reinforced as needed.

**The fusion question (Step C, per `Real-World_Basis_Extrapolation_Method.md`): does Highway 37's own
established character plausibly produce its own version of this?** **Yes, and the physical generator forces it
rather than merely permitting it:** an ice sheet is not a fixed substrate — it flows, however slowly — so
**no route across it can be a permanently fixed paved road in the way a highway elsewhere in Tepenia might be.**
`Highways.md`'s own use of the word "highway" for Hwy 37 is therefore a **functional** claim (a maintained,
reliable, named through-route with declared endpoints and junctions) rather than a **construction** claim
(pavement). **Divergence stated explicitly, per the source-not-specification rule:** Tepenia's civilization has
robot labor and more centralized administrative capacity than the real polar programs that maintain the SPoT,
so this pass does not assume Hwy 37 is *primitive* by the SPoT's standard — only that it shares the SPoT's one
non-negotiable physical constraint (a re-graded, marked, periodically-resurveyed route, not a poured road) while
plausibly exceeding it in reliability and maintenance cadence, given who is doing the maintaining.

**Necessity Before Meaning** (`Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md` Technique 6), run directly on this finding: the
rule "Highway 37 is called a highway, not a track" was **forced by nothing more than administrative convenience
and the same naming convention applied to the other ten named highways** — the *practical* necessity (a
maintained, periodically re-surveyed snow/ice route, exactly like the real SPoT) came first; the *meaning*
("highway," implying permanence and civic infrastructure on par with any other road) was assigned afterward, by
a naming convention that does not distinguish construction methods.

## C. The four sensory sub-fields, answered separately

**Sound.** The open plateau is, per real-world precedent at Dome C/Concordia and the Dome Argus/Kunlun region
(both real sites valued by actual astronomy programs for unusually still, stable air), **close to silent** away
from any vehicle — no surf, no vegetation, no wildlife this far inland. **The one constant sound is wind when
it is present, and its total absence is itself notable** on the calmest stretches. Against this baseline,
engine noise, tracked-vehicle sound, or any mechanical presence reads as **enormously loud and completely
isolated** — there is nothing else for it to blend into.

**Smell.** Extreme cold suppresses olfaction sharply; **the plateau itself has almost no smell a human traveler
can register**, and what little exists (machine exhaust, fuel, warmed cabin air, a vehicle's own interior) is
entirely artificial and entirely self-generated — nothing ambient competes with it. **A traveler's own vehicle
is, smell-wise, the only thing that exists.**

**Feel.** Thin air at altitude (see Phase 1's elevation table) makes exertion harder than the cold alone would
predict; dry air and static are a real, specific tactile signature distinct from ordinary cold-weather
discomfort; **the ground itself is not uniform** — the surface hardness and texture visibly and physically
changes between the near-coastal climb, the high interior plateau proper, and the descent toward Concordia,
which a traveler would feel through the vehicle before seeing any visual change.

**First impressions.** The climb from the Sayowa Junction is the corridor's one dramatic visual sequence —
terrain, vegetation-adjacent coastal texture (however sparse), and a visible horizon give way, over the ascent
to Dome Fuji, to **a genuinely flat, textureless plain with no reference points at all.** **The psychological
flattening is the finding, not merely the elevation change**: a first-time traveler's dominant impression is
not "this is high" but "there is nothing here to judge distance or scale by," which several real polar
accounts describe as disorienting in a way pure cold is not.

## D. The public/institutional split, and the retroactive mechanism it explains

**Per Phase 3's instruction to check for a split between a location's outdoor/public register and its
institutional interior:** Highway 37 has exactly one interior to contrast against its open exterior — **Mountain
Pass Airport itself.** The open road is silent, cold, textureless, and entirely self-generated in sound and
smell (§C above). The outpost's interior is the categorical opposite: **an enclosed, heated, working space, with
the ambient hum, smell, and clutter of an active fabrication operation** — chamber assembly, tooling, staff
quarters for a rotating crew. **The contrast is total, and it is the whole point of the outpost's existence**:
it is the one place on this entire corridor where the open plateau's sensory baseline is suspended.

**Retroactive Mechanism** (`Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md` Technique 5), run against the oddly specific,
already-established phrase **"bidirectional dual-junction"** for the Hwy 22/Hwy 37 crossing: why does this
junction, alone among several named in `Highways.md`, carry that specific qualifier? **The physical system that
would produce exactly this:** on a route this featureless (§C, First impressions), a junction is not a visually
obvious landmark the way a real-world highway interchange is — in whiteout or the long polar-night stretches
this frame's own climate implies (see §E below), a traveler approaching from *either* direction needs the
junction to be marked and confirmed as such regardless of which way they're coming from, or they risk missing
it entirely on a plateau with no other reference points. **"Bidirectional" is therefore not a redundant
descriptor — it is evidence that this corridor's junctions require deliberate, direction-independent marking
infrastructure**, which no other highway's junction table bothers to state explicitly, because Hwy 37 is the
only one crossing terrain this featureless for this long a stretch. **This produces a second-order consequence
for free**, per the technique's own promise: **the corridor must carry visible, redundant junction markers
sturdy enough to be found from either approach in poor visibility** — a concrete, catalogable piece of
infrastructure (Phase 10) that nothing in canon named until this derivation.

## E. Seasonal variance — resolving the pre-flight's REQUESTED chokepoint item

**Per `03` Phase 3 §E and the pre-flight's open REQUESTED item #3.** The real SPoT (§B) operates **only in the
Antarctic summer season**, not through the winter darkness — a direct, load-bearing precedent, not a coincidence
of convenience. Combined with this frame's own established East Antarctic interior conditions (extreme
latitude, per Concordia's own file — deep polar night alternating with unbroken daylight) and the corrected
polar-night discipline from Run 3 (M-20 — check the actual figure rather than assuming a stale one; the
Larsemann Hills' ~60-day figure is specific to that lower-latitude coastal cluster and is **not** assumed to
transfer here without checking, given Hwy 37's much higher, more southerly plateau route):

**Proposed finding: Highway 37 undergoes seasonal restriction during the deepest core of the interior plateau's
polar night**, when visibility drops to near-zero for an extended stretch and re-grooming/re-marking work (§B)
becomes both more hazardous and less effective. **This is stated as directionally confident but not
precision-dated** — the exact closure window is not derived here, since doing so would require the plateau's
own precise latitude-adjusted polar-night length, which is not computed in this pass and is flagged as a
genuine open item rather than asserted to a false precision. **What is resolved:** the road is not
canonically confirmed open year-round via any unstated infrastructure (nothing in canon claims this), and the
honest default — given the real-world precedent and the frame's own established climate — is that it is not.

## F. Differentiation axis

**Named axis: "texture by absence, not presence."** Every sensory finding in this phase (§C) is defined by what
is missing — no smell, no ambient sound, no visual reference points — rather than by a positive sensory
character the way a settled place's texture usually is described. **This is the axis that would separate
Highway 37 from a future differentiation table entry for any other highway or corridor**: the question is not
"what does this place smell/sound/feel like" in the ordinary sense, but "how much of the ordinary sensory
baseline has this specific location's physical conditions stripped away, and what does the one remaining
artificial source (a vehicle's own interior) come to mean against that absence."
