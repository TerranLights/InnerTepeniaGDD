# Phase 1 — Constraint & Capability (the spine)

**Named axis: "precedence without a majority."** Every finding below traces to the same underlying condition:
Cape Adare's civic identity is anchored in *being first*, not in any dominant population bloc — an unusually
clean case of founding-logic and demographic-logic pointing at the same absence from two independent
directions.

**Three generators selected**, chosen for independence: **G2** (physical & environmental constraint), **G4**
(founding condition), **G8** (demographic composition). **G5** (network position) is run as a fourth,
supplementary generator — genuinely thin for this location (a spur/connector, not a through-route), and its
thinness is itself informative rather than a reason to drop it.

**Admissible input only, per `00_Frame_and_PreFlight.md` §2/§6**: `Specs/Cape_Adare.md`'s factual sections
(Population & Composition, Geographic Basis's climate/physical content, Founding's factual content, Notable
Locations' bare listing) — **explicitly excluding** the Character & Culture section. Nothing below derives from
or references that section; where a finding might resemble it, this is flagged and tagged per the

## A. G2 — Physical & Environmental Constraint

**What it gives freely:** genuine coastal accessibility — Cape Adare is **"the most accessible point on the
Ross Sea coast from the open Southern Ocean,"** the first landfall for any ship approaching from the north.
Its climate, while genuinely cold (mean annual −12°C), is markedly less extreme than the deep interior cities
this methodology has examined so far (Vostok, Kunlun, Concordia, all well below −50°C mean) — a real,
measurable difference in kind, not degree.

**What it withholds:** shelter from weather. Its northern exposure means it is, per its own governing
description, **"often the first point to be hit by incoming storms"** — it receives Southern Ocean weather
systems the rest of the Ross Sea coast is spared or receives only after Cape Adare already has. Sea ice at this
latitude is also markedly less predictable than farther south: **"maritime access windows are longer but the
transition periods are less predictable."**

**What it demands continuously:** coexistence with an Adélie penguin rookery of **250,000+ breeding pairs**,
one of the largest in the world — not a background detail but, per the file's own words, **"the dominant
sensory fact of Cape Adare for any visitor,"** in smell and sound alike. **Pushed to third order, per LAW 0**:
a colony this size negotiating physical space with a human/robot settlement → therefore the city's own spatial
layout cannot follow an ordinary grid, since penguin territory and movement routes are not something a
250,000-pair colony accommodates to human planning → therefore residents develop practical, unwritten
space-sharing conventions that a visitor would not know on arrival and would have to learn fast. **This is a
genuine, independently-derived finding** (not drawn from the excluded Character section, which asserts
community character as a conclusion rather than deriving it from the rookery's own physical scale).

**What it permits but punishes:** approaching or resupplying by sea during the sea-ice transition periods,
which are explicitly less predictable here than at more southern Ross Sea cities — nothing forbids it, but
timing it wrong costs real delay or risk.

**Four-quadrant profile (G2):**

| Quadrant | Reading |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | Genuine first-landfall accessibility from the open ocean; a comparatively mild climate against every interior city this methodology has examined |
| **DEFICIT** | No shelter from incoming weather — first hit, every time, by whatever the Southern Ocean sends |
| **STANDING COST** | Continuous, unavoidable spatial negotiation with a quarter-million-plus breeding pairs of penguins, third-order-derived into an unwritten space-sharing convention |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | Sea approach during ice-transition periods is unrestricted and unpredictable — nobody forbids it, the ice itself makes it expensive to time wrong |

## B. G4 — Founding Condition

**Who, why, under what constraint, with what, and without what** — read directly from the admissible Founding
section: Cape Adare was settled **"post-Falkland Treaty, on the foundation of the existing heritage site
infrastructure"** — the founding motive was explicitly the site's own historical significance (Borchgrevink's
1899 hut, the first deliberate human overwintering on the continent), not resource extraction, not strategic
position, not a founding-nation's own colonization drive. **The founding population is explicitly established
as mixed, "no single dominant national community"** — and, per the bug-check memory's own confirmed reading,
this is deliberate and consistent across every layer of the city's canon: precedence organizes the city, not
nationality.

**The absence, which G4's own instruction says is the real yield:** Cape Adare was **never founded around a
strong national anchor** the way most Tepenian cities were (a Primary-tier nation's own colonization wave
setting the cultural tone). **What replaces that anchor?** Not derivable from the Founding section alone —
this is exactly the gap Phase 6 (Meaning) and Phase 7 (Order) will need to fill, and it is flagged forward
rather than answered here.

**Four-quadrant profile (G4):**

| Quadrant | Reading |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | A founding motive (historical precedence) durable in a way a resource-extraction or strategic founding is not — the site's significance does not deplete or become obsolete |
| **DEFICIT** | No founding-nation identity to organize civic authority or cultural tone-setting around, unlike nearly every other Tepenian city examined so far |
| **STANDING COST** | *(silent — see §D)* |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | *(silent — see §D)* |

## C. G8 — Demographic Composition

**Read directly from the Census I/II per-nation table** (admissible — raw figures, not interpretation): Cape
Adare's population spans **twelve nations across three tiers**, with the largest single share (USA, 22.26%)
notably lower than the dominant share in most other cities this methodology has examined, and a long,
genuinely populated tail down to Malaysia's 1.52%. **This is one of the flattest, least-concentrated national
distributions found in this project's census data so far** — a real, measurable fact, not an impression.

**What this produces, read straight from the numbers rather than assumed:** no single national bloc has the
numerical weight to set cultural default by sheer majority — the largest share (22.26%) is barely a fifth of
the population, compared to cities where a Primary-tier nation runs 30-40%+ of the total. **This independently
corroborates G4's own founding-logic finding** (no dominant national anchor) from a completely separate fact
(the census numbers, not the founding narrative) — genuine agreement between two independent generators, per
`02` §5.2's own strongest-grounding case.

**Four-quadrant profile (G8):**

| Quadrant | Reading |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | Genuine multi-national breadth — twelve nations meaningfully represented, none marginal even at the Notable tier |
| **DEFICIT** | No numerically dominant bloc to anchor cultural authority or default civic tone |
| **STANDING COST** | *(silent — see §D)* |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | *(silent — see §D)* |

## D. G5 — Network Position (supplementary, genuinely thin)

**Cape Adare is a spur/connector off Hwy 183, not a main-line stop** — the same access pattern as Fort McMurdo
and Scott. **Its own canon states its southward road connection to the Janbogo network is not yet
established** (Open Questions: *"the coastal road south from Cape Adare to the Terra Nova Bay cities is not
yet established; distance and route TBD"*) — a genuine REQUESTED item, not filled in here.

**One real strength, derived from G2's storm-exposure finding rather than restated from it:** Cape Adare is
**"often the first point to be hit by incoming storms"** on this stretch of coast — which means it is, whether
by design or accident, the first place on the Janbogo subnet's northern reach to know what weather is coming.
**This is a genuine informational-asymmetry finding, in G5's own signature register** (`02` G5: *"a place that
receives from three sources and sends to none"* — here, inverted: a place with weak physical connectivity
southward that nonetheless has real informational value to send, if anyone downstream is positioned to receive
it). **Flagged forward to Phase 5**, where this methodology's own primary relational phase belongs.

**Four-quadrant profile (G5):**

| Quadrant | Reading |
|---|---|
| **STRENGTH** | First-warning position for incoming weather along this stretch of the Ross Sea coast — an informational asset independent of its weak physical connectivity |
| **DEFICIT** | Not a through-route; its own southward road connection is not established in canon at all |
| **STANDING COST** | Everything physical must detour through the Hwy 183 connector rather than moving directly |
| **GRUDGING TOLERANCE** | *(silent)* |

## F. The deficit's address

**Per `02` §4.1**: where does the remedy for "no founding-nation anchor / no dominant bloc" actually live?
**Diffuse** — there is no single counterparty to petition, no absent authority to blame, and (per G4's own
founding logic) this was never a gap the city failed to fill; it was **the deliberate shape of how the city was
founded in the first place.** This is not a wound; it is a design condition. **Checked against `02` §4.1's own
harder case** (has anyone here ever had reason to formulate this as a problem?): **no — and there is no reason
to expect anyone would**, since nothing in this pass's admissible material suggests the absence of a dominant
bloc reads as a deficiency from inside the city. **This may not even belong in the DEFICIT quadrant at all** —
flagged honestly as a genuine ambiguity: is "no dominant anchor" a deficit, or is it simply a neutral structural
fact this city's own civic identity was built to not need? **Carried forward to Phase 6/7 as an open question,
not resolved prematurely here.**
