## A. Populations present, and the lens

**Robots and humans**, per the standard framework — Census II: 426,343 humans / 623,708 robots, a real,
admissible robot-majority split (Census I was close to even: 745,967 humans / 753,687 robots).

**Lens check, per the phase's own instruction to ask whether a sharper category exists**: **yes — arrival wave,
not national origin and not robot/human kind.** Phase 2's own founding-wave/modern-majority mismatch (New
Zealand's precedence vs. USA/China's numbers) cuts across both nationality and kind, and per Phase 6 §D it is
the axis the city's own real latent tension actually runs on. **Declared as the operative lens for this
phase.**

## C. Inter-population relations

**Restating the national baseline is not a finding.** The sharper question, per §A's declared lens: **does the
arrival-wave axis (not kind) determine who currently carries the city's founding memory?** Per Phase 2, yes —
and this cuts across both robots and humans within each national community, not along the robot/human line at
all. **The inter-kind question answers itself underneath this**: nothing in admissible material suggests
robots and humans relate to each other differently *because* of kind — what differs is whether a given
community arrived in the founding wave or afterward, a distinction that applies within both populations
equally.
