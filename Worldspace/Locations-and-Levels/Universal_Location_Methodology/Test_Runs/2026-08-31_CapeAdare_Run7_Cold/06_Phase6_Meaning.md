## A. Naming technique — a real hit, and a real registration gap

**"St. Carsten" is already established, admissible, civic-religious-register language** (`Specs/Cape_Adare.md`'s
own Founding section: *"the city inherited the association with St. Carsten"*). **Checked directly against
`National_Holidays.md`'s own Tepenian Saints framework** — a real, national-scale canon mechanism: pre-war
Antarctic explorers venerated as "Saints" for unknowingly preparing the home exiles would later need, honorific
using the first name (St. Robert/Scott, St. Ernest/Shackleton, St. Roald/Amundsen, St. Douglas/Mawson, St.
Richard/Byrd). **"Carsten" is Borchgrevink's own first name — the pattern matches exactly.** But
**`National_Holidays.md`'s own "known roster so far" does not yet list St. Carsten** — Cape Adare's own file has
already, informally, extended a national framework that hasn't caught up to include him.

**Per the four-question canon check (`00_RUNBOOK.md` §E): does canon already answer this?** Yes — the
mechanism exists and Borchgrevink fits it precisely (a pre-war explorer who was first to deliberately winter on
the continent, exactly the kind of act the framework venerates). **This pass's job is to explain why Cape Adare
was the place this happened to** — and the admissible Founding section already does: this is the *only* city
built directly around a Saints-eligible act happening on its own ground, rather than a Saint merely being
associated with a city that grew up elsewhere. **Registering back into canon (question 4)**: St. Carsten is a
genuine gap in `National_Holidays.md`'s own roster, not a new invention — **flagged as a direct, concrete
addition for that file**, not decided unilaterally here (a national-framework edit is beyond this pass's own
scope, but the gap is real and worth naming precisely).

## D. The Failure State of the Core Value

**Core promise, derived from Phase 1's spine finding: recognition organized by precedence, not by demographic
or national power.** **When does it fail?** When precedence itself becomes contested — when "who was really
first," or whose claim to the founding narrative is strongest, stops being a settled historical fact and
becomes something actively argued. **Phase 2's arrival-order mismatch (New Zealand's early precedence vs. USA/
China's modern numeric weight) is exactly the latent condition this failure state would activate** — this pass
does not assert the contest is currently happening, only that the structural precondition for it already
exists in the admissible census data. **What institution has grown up around it, if any**: not established in
admissible canon — REQUESTED, and flagged as a genuinely promising direction for a future pass or questline
development, since it connects a hard demographic fact to a real civic tension without requiring any invented
villain.

## E. Observance

**One found and formalized (§A): St. Carsten's own feast day.** Its exact date is genuinely open in canon
(`Specs/Cape_Adare.md`'s own Open Questions: *"the Southern Cross expedition overwintered 1899–1900; a
specific date TBD"*) — **not invented here**, left as the REQUESTED item it already is. **No second, unserious
observance is found or invented** — per `03` Phase 6's own instruction that at least one should be small and
unserious, this is a genuine gap this pass leaves open rather than papering over with a manufactured festival.
