# Phase 2 — Composition & Arrival

**Named axis: "arrival order and modern weight don't match."** Mandatory for Settlement. Band 5 — composition
is a distribution, patterned rather than averaged.

---

## A. A real, admissible mismatch in the census table itself

**Reading the tier annotations closely, not just the percentages:** the Significant tier is explicitly marked
**"(founding wave)"**; the Notable tier's New Zealand entry is marked **"earliest founding wave — geographically
closest nation to Cape Adare."** **Neither Primary-tier nation (USA 22.26%, China 19.85%) carries a founding-
wave marker at all.** Read together, this is a real, derivable finding: **the nations with the most modern
demographic weight were not the nations present at founding.** New Zealand — the closest nation geographically,
and explicitly the earliest arrival — sits at a mere 3.19%, Notable tier, by the modern census. The two nations
that now define the city's numerical majority arrived and grew afterward.

**Arrival-mode taxonomy, applied per tier:**
- **New Zealand and the "founding wave" Significant-tier nations (Japan, UK, South Korea, Canada, Indonesia,
  Australia): "Chose it,"** specifically drawn by the site's own historical significance (per Founding's
  admissible content — the site's precedence was the founding motive itself) rather than posted or fled.
  Proximity plausibly reinforced New Zealand's own early arrival, but the founding-wave marker on the whole
  Significant tier suggests shared motive across all of them, not proximity alone.
- **USA and China (Primary tier, no founding-wave marker): most plausibly "chose it" as well, but later** —
  ordinary post-Treaty immigration drawn to an established, historically significant site, growing to numerical
  dominance over time rather than being present at the founding moment. **Not asserted with false certainty**:
  this is the most consistent reading of the data available, not a canon-confirmed mechanism; flagged as such.
- **The Notable tier below New Zealand (Philippines 2.08%, Malaysia 1.52%): thin data, no arrival-mode marker
  at all** — recorded as a genuine gap rather than assigned an invented mode.

## B. What this produces

**A city whose demographic majority did not build its founding identity.** This is a sharp, differentiating
finding: Cape Adare's civic memory of "being first" (per the admissible Founding section) belongs, in a real
sense, more to New Zealand's own small, early-arriving community than to the USA or China, who now hold the
numbers. **Whether the modern majority actually defers to, absorbs, or is indifferent to that asymmetry is not
yet determined** — flagged forward to Phase 7 (Order), where the question of who actually holds cultural
authority belongs.

## C. Separating native from transplanted

**Per Technique 12**: what did Cape Adare develop on its own, versus what did incoming populations bring?
**Genuinely difficult to answer at this point in the pass** — the founding motive (precedence, not nationality)
is itself native to the site rather than imported by any single group, which is unusual: most Tepenian cities'
native layer descends from a dominant founding nation's own practices, and Cape Adare's does not have one to
descend from. **This is not a gap — it is the same "no anchor" finding from Phase 1, restated at the population
level rather than the founding-narrative level.**

## D. Who is not here

**No canon establishes any population explicitly excluded or absent from Cape Adare** — unlike some cities
with documented exclusions, this pass finds no such fact and does not invent one. **Recorded as a genuine null.**
