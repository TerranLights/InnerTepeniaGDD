## Panel A — the Flat Archetypes

**The Child** (cast as: a child from a founding-wave-descended family, growing up alongside a child from a
modern-majority family) — *asks: what am I not allowed to touch?* **Objection, addition:** the boardwalk-
corridor system (Phase 3 §C) is the first hard rule any Cape Adare child learns — not a social rule but a
physical, safety-critical one. → **`accepted`**, folds into Phase 7c's own transmission finding, sharpening it:
the corridor rule is likely the very first thing every child here learns, before any cultural or national
custom.

**The Lover** (cast as: a couple from different arrival-wave backgrounds) — *asks: could I build a life with
someone here, and where would we actually go?* **No objection** — nothing in this pass's material suggests any
barrier to this; Cape Adare's own "no dominant anchor" condition, if anything, makes cross-background pairing
structurally unremarkable rather than fraught. → **No comment**, a legitimate result per Rule 1.

**The Parent** (cast as: a worker whose income follows the arrival-dependent feast-or-famine cycle, Phase 4
§C) — *asks: who has my child during a crisis?* **Objection, addition:** an unpredictable work rhythm this
severe plausibly strains ordinary childcare arrangements in a way a steady economy would not. → **`noted`** —
real, but this pass's admissible material does not establish any specific childcare institution to check it
against; deferred rather than invented.

**The Ruler** (cast as: whoever currently coordinates maritime logistics) — *asks: where does it come from, and
who settles up?* **Objection, inconsistency:** per Phase 5 §5e, Cape Adare's own southward road connection to
its subnet is undefined in canon — the Ruler has no answer for how overland resupply actually reaches the city
at all. → **`accepted`**, folds into the existing Phase 5 §5e finding, sharpening it into a genuinely urgent
open question rather than a background gap.

**The Elder** (cast as: someone from the original founding-wave community, New Zealand-descended per Phase 2)
— *asks: was that always true?* **Objection, addition — a strong one:** the Elder is positioned to notice, more
sharply than anyone else, that the city's own founding memory belongs to a community that is now a small
demographic minority. → **`accepted`** — this is Phase 6 §D's failure-state finding, now given a concrete voice
rather than an abstract description; the strongest single addition this panel produced.

**The Mentor** (cast as: a corridor-maintenance veteran, teaching a newcomer) — *asks: how does someone learn
this, and what becomes of the ones who can't?* **Already covered directly**, Phase 7c. → **`accepted`**, folds
into the existing finding.

## Panel C — Shadows

**The Detached Manipulator** (cast against the arrival-wave tension, Phase 6 §D) — *asks: who withholds what
people need to know for their own well-being?* **Verdict: not guilty.** Nothing in this pass's admissible
material suggests the demographic mismatch is concealed from anyone — it is a matter of public census record,
openly available. **The tension, if it exists at all, is unaddressed rather than hidden** — a different, milder
shape than a genuine concealment shadow, and this pass does not force the stronger reading.

*Is this place alive, and could anyone love it?* **Answered honestly**: Cape Adare's own admissible material —
the rookery's overwhelming sensory presence, the genuine first-landfall drama, the ongoing, unresolved,

a generic cold outpost.** **A qualified yes**, more confidently answered than Highway 37's own version of this
question, consistent with Cape Adare being a genuine Settlement with real people living full lives, not a
transit corridor.
