## PHASE 2 — COMPOSITION & ARRIVAL

**Arrival-mode taxonomy, two distinct layers — the mix is the finding:**

1. **The founding cohort (Sinian Federation exiles): `assigned/posted`, with a `fled` undertone.** The Falkland
   Treaty exile was not individual choice — the Jeju-do court's diplomatic partition directed a national
   population to a specific site their government had already held infrastructure at. Unlike a punitive
   sentence, there is no return clock and no crime; unlike ordinary flight, the destination was not chosen by
   the people who arrived, it was settled by treaty on their behalf before they got there.
2. **Every later national-origin population (South Korea, Japan, Germany, Russia, Australia, and twelve
   Notable-tier nations): `chose it`.** Per the Specs file directly: this immigration arrived "the same way…
   as ordinary immigration into a city whose founding… was already settled before any of them arrived." These
   populations self-selected into an already-functioning city, not into a frontier.

**The differentiation axis this produces, named in three words:** ***recognized-claim-plus-chosen-arrival.***
Compare against Shirayuki's founding condition (Phase 1, G4 above): Shirayuki's site was **granted as empty**,
which means *its* founding cohort was also, in effect, choosing an unclaimed destination rather than being
posted to a pre-recognized one. **Zhongshan's founding cohort did not choose the site; its secondary population
did.** `[CLUSTER-DEPENDENT]` insofar as the comparison is against a named sibling, but the underlying finding
(a city whose *first* population didn't choose it and whose *later* population did) stands on its own without
the comparison.

**What this repels, per §2's "ask what it repels" rule:** a self-selecting founding myth. Zhongshan cannot
truthfully tell a story of pioneers who chose this ice because Prydz Bay called to them — the founders were
posted here by a court ruling. **Any origin story that reads as elective for the founding generation is wrong
for this city specifically** — a differentiator worth checking against whatever the eventual culture pass
writes for Founding Day observance (Phase 6).

**Native vs. transplanted layer (Step C):** the "native" layer, strictly, is the Sinian-Federation-derived
population and whatever CHINARE-heritage practices survived contact with the exile. Everyone else is
transplanted onto an already-Sinian-flavored base, not onto an empty slate — meaningfully different from a
city where all populations arrived onto genuinely neutral ground.

**Who is not here (Step E):** no population is recorded as having tried and failed to settle here and been
turned away — consistent with Phase 0's flagged null (no losing claimant). **A city with a recognized claim and
no rejected claimant on record is, structurally, a city that has never had to say no to anyone wanting in** —
worth testing against Phase 7's governance section for whether that has ever been tested.

## PHASE 3 — SURFACE & TEXTURE

**A. Formalize first:** the research log already supplies concrete sensory material — this section formalizes
it rather than inventing fresh.

**B. Four sub-fields, separately:**

- **Sound:** *(no admissible source yet gives this directly — recorded as a null, not invented. Flagged as a
  REQUESTED research target: what does an archipelago city with no drainage network actually sound like —
  standing water vs. moving water, wind across open ice-free rock vs. wind across ice.)*
- **Smell:** derivable from G2 directly — **lake chemistry differs basin to basin**, driven by sea spray, local
  geology, and periodic glacier-surge seawater intrusion at the oasis's eastern corner (near the Dålk glacier).
  **Third-order:** a resident moving between the city's eastern and western reaches crosses a genuine, mappable
  smell gradient with no single "city smell" to average against — the same "no city-wide fact" pattern already
  found in Phase 1's water deficit, now showing up independently in a sensory register. **Not tagged
  `[SELF-ORIGINATED]`** — this specific east/west smell-gradient framing was not in the exposed material
  (the exposure covered the *general* drainage fact and its water-testing consequence, not a sensory finding
  derived from the calving-surge detail); flagged for cross-check at Step 9 regardless, in the spirit of the
  honesty this run owes itself.
- **Feel:** archipelago geography (two large peninsulas, three small, ~130 islands) means genuine underfoot
  variation — orthogneiss-over-pegmatite-and-granite basement rock, per the research log, is a harder, more
  broken-up surface than a uniform ice-sheet city would offer.
- **First impressions:** an arrival by the coastal highway (Hwy 110, from Davis) meets a city that reads as
  scattered across islands before it reads as unified — **the archipelago IS the first impression**, not a
  detail discovered later.

**C. Public/institutional split:** not yet researched to a usable depth — flagged as a REQUESTED input for a
future pass rather than invented here.

**D. The seam:** no admissible material yet distinguishes a founding-era build from a later-pressure build for
Zhongshan specifically (Census I→II tracks population, not construction phases). **Recorded as a genuine null**,
distinct from "absent and unexplained" — the reason is stated: no admissible source addresses construction
chronology at this resolution.

**E. Seasonal variance:** Polar night ~May 22 → July 21 (~60 days); midnight sun ~November 21 → January 21
(~61 days) — both **directly from the Specs file, correct per the M-20 fix** (not the erroneous "six months"
figure that older material carried elsewhere in this project). At its worst point in the year (deep polar
night), the archipelago's island-to-island travel — already costly per Phase 1's grudging-tolerance finding —
becomes costlier still: crossing between islands in the dark, over broken basement rock, near basins that can
surge unpredictably, is a materially different act than the same crossing at midnight sun. **This is not yet
tagged `[SELF-ORIGINATED]`** and is offered as a genuinely independent seasonal reading of the archipelago
deficit named in Phase 1 — flagged for verification at Step 9.

**F. Retroactive Mechanism:** *what is established as simply true here that nothing explains?* Candidate: why
does a ~40 km² oasis with ~150 lakes not have a developed drainage network in the first place, when most
lake-dense terrain elsewhere on Earth develops one over time? **Candidate physical cause:** permafrost beneath
a thin active layer prevents the kind of subsurface percolation that would otherwise link basins over time —
**not verified against a hydrology source this session**; flagged as a REQUESTED research target (the
Copernicus lake-retention paper, already surfaced but unread per the research log's own open-thread list, is
the correct next source).

## PHASE 4 — ORDINARY LIFE

**A. Headline function, stated and written away from:** Zhongshan's headline function is *holding the
recognized Sinian claim on this stretch of coast, alongside maritime/scientific work continuing CHINARE
heritage.* **Writing away from it:** an ordinary resident's day is not spent defending a claim or doing
frontier science — those are institutional facts, not daily texture.

**B. Four elements:**

- **Routines:** per-basin water checking (Phase 1's standing cost) is not itself most residents' job, but its
  *consequence* — knowing which basin's water is currently good, a piece of local knowledge that changes
  without warning after a glacier surge — is closer to ordinary weather-awareness than to specialist labor.
  **Everyone tracks it the way people elsewhere track weather, not the way a hydrologist tracks it.**
- **Mundane concerns:** inter-island travel timing — crossing to a different peninsula or island costs real
  time, so an ordinary day is shaped by *which side of the oasis you need to be on and when*, not by a single
  city-wide commute pattern. **Different arrival-mode populations (Phase 2) very plausibly cluster by
  landform** — flagged as a REQUESTED input (is there any admissible material on which nation-tier settled
  which peninsula/island?) rather than invented.
- **Personal struggles:** for a member of a Notable-tier national-origin population (1.2–3.6% share each),
  daily life includes being a small minority among eighteen distinct national-origin groups with no single
  dominant "everyone else" to blend into beyond the ~36% Chinese plurality — a genuinely different social
  arithmetic from a two-population city.
- **Escapism/downtime:** not yet researched — flagged as a genuine null, not invented, per Step 4's discipline
  that escapism is the field most often cut for space and must not be skipped by omission.

**C. Not one universal resident:** the founding-cohort/later-immigration split from Phase 2 plausibly produces
at least two different relationships to the city's basic facts — a Sinian-heritage resident inheriting a claim
that was never in doubt, versus a later-arrived resident who chose a city already functioning, with an already
settled water-monitoring habit and an already-multinational social texture to learn rather than build.

**D. Physical scale for commute/logistics:** the ~40 km² oasis, ~130 islands, multiple peninsulas gives concrete,
derivable commute texture: this is not a city where "getting to the other side of town" is a road-length
question, it is an island-crossing question, and the archipelago's grudging-tolerance finding (Phase 1) is
where that cost actually lands on an ordinary Tuesday.

**E. What ordinary rhythm fails to provide:** for whichever population settled the less-favorable
landforms — flagged as a REQUESTED input, since the admissible material does not currently specify a
per-landform population distribution.

**Differentiation axis, named in three or four words:** ***organized around water-basin awareness***, distinct
from a shift-clock, a tide, or a light-cycle axis (though the light-cycle genuinely modulates it seasonally,
per Phase 3E).

## PHASE 5 — RELATION & GEOMETRY

**Weighting check first, per the runbook's isolated-vs-clustered table:** Zhongshan is the **rare clustered
case**, not the majority isolated one — 5a and 5b become mandatory rather than optional, exactly the situation
`[CLUSTER-DEPENDENT]` tagging exists for.

### 5a — Peers

Sinheung and Shirayuki are named peers (0.3–8 km distant, sharing the same physical oasis), but **their
completed culture material is withheld this pass.** What CAN be stated without opening quarantined material:

- **The physical relationship** (from admissible sources): all three sit in the Larsemann Hills oasis; the real
  Antarctic Treaty ASMA 6 management plan governs exactly this kind of multi-station coexistence (per the
  research log's flagged-but-unread open thread) — **a real governance document this pass has not yet read**,
  flagged again as the single highest-value unread source for this phase, consistent with what Run 3's research
  log already identified (this is attribute-level, not a conclusion, so it is legitimately re-flagged here
  rather than treated as exposed content).
- **The highway geometry** (fully admissible, from `Highways.md`, read fresh this pass): Zhongshan sits at the
  convergence of **three highways** — Hwy 110 (the **Coastal Cut**, running to Davis, Mirny, Casey, and
  ultimately **Concordia**, the capital), Hwy 22 (the **Transcontinental Highway**, running the full distance
  from Byrd through the South Pole/Amundsen Station to this tri-junction), and Hwy 4 (running via Mawson to
  Sinheung and onward implicitly to Shirayuki). **This is a genuinely new finding this pass produced from fresh
  canon reading, not present in the exposed Run 3 material** (which did not discuss highway geometry in what
  was manifested) — **not tagged `[SELF-ORIGINATED]`.**

**Third-order chase on the highway finding:** a city that is simultaneously the terminus of the
Transcontinental Highway (from the Pole) and the origin of the Coastal Cut (to the capital) is not merely
*a* junction city — **it is the specific point where interior traffic becomes coastal traffic and vice versa.**
Anyone traveling from Amundsen Station to Concordia overland passes through Zhongshan's tri-junction whether or
not they have any reason to stop. **Zhongshan is a mechanism, not a destination, for anyone making that
specific trip** — precisely the "asymmetry" G5 is described as yielding in `02` §6.2: a place that receives
transit traffic for a purpose that has nothing to do with Zhongshan itself.

**Distinguish loud friction from quiet friction (§5a):** no admissible source yet gives an actual grievance
between Zhongshan and its cluster-mates — flagged as a genuine null, deferred to Step 7 when Gate 6 opens
withheld material and any documented friction becomes checkable.

### 5b — The three-way differentiation set — SUBSTITUTE used, own-eras, DIFFERENT AXIS than Run 3

**Per the exposure handling in the header of this file's companion, Run 3's own-eras axis — "how does the city
learn something has stopped" — is exposed material and is deliberately NOT re-run here**, even though it would
likely still work; running it would produce a foregone match, not a genuine test.

**This pass's own-eras axis: *what does "the claim" mean, checked at three points in this frame's own
timeline?*** Only two of the three points are actually available within the pre-war, pre-unification frame this
pass writes (per Frame Declaration) — a genuine constraint worth stating rather than working around:

1. **At founding (Falkland Treaty era):** "the claim" means a diplomatic ruling recognizing an existing physical
   fact (CHINARE infrastructure, continuous habitation) — an abstract, institutional meaning, settled by a
   court the founding generation did not choose to appear before.
2. **Now, within this pass's own pre-war frame (Second Interwar Period, well after founding, still pre-
   unification):** "the claim" has had centuries to become simply **where you live** for a population that is
   now 64% non-Chinese-plurality by later immigration. The original diplomatic meaning is presumably still true
   as a fact but is no longer most residents' *lived* relationship to the phrase — flagged as a plausible
   reading, not yet verified against Phase 6/7 material this pass has not written.
3. **A third point (post-unification, post-war) is OUT OF FRAME for this pass** — per the Frame Declaration's
   own rule (`01` §4, "a location may need multiple passes at different frames… these are different instruments
   pointed at different objects"), this pass does not write what "the claim" means after Zhongshan becomes a
   sub-district of a unified city. **Recorded as a genuine limit, not filled in.**

**What this substitute actually produced:** only a two-point contrast, not the three the technique calls for.
**This is itself worth recording as a finding about the technique**, not just about the city: the own-eras
substitute assumes three meaningfully different states are available inside one frame, and **a pre-war,
pre-unification frame may only genuinely offer two** for an axis whose third state belongs to a different era
entirely. Logged to the shared observations file.

### 5c — The parent

Per the Frame Declaration's provisional assumptions: the Mirny subnet is unwritten. What Zhongshan appears to
need from it (Federation-wide currency, calendar, and — per Phase 2 — the diplomatic recognition of national
claims) is all supplied at a level above the subnet (Federation/Jeju-do court), **not by the subnet itself as
a distinct actor.** Tentative reading: **Zhongshan's relationship to its subnet is thinner than its relationship
to the Federation directly** — the subnet functions mainly as an Arcanet/administrative grouping (per the
`Arcanet Subnet: Mirny` header field) rather than as a felt political entity. Flagged as a provisional
assumption per `01` §5.2 rule 1, built on the physical/administrative fact (subnet-as-Arcanet-grouping) rather
than invented.

### 5d — Edge and crossing

**Arrival types, enumerated rather than assumed singular:** overland via Hwy 110 (from the coastal cities/
Concordia direction) or Hwy 22 (from the Pole/Byrd direction) or Hwy 4 (from Mawson/the cluster direction); by
sea via Prydz Bay (the maritime heritage route). **At least three genuinely distinct arrival experiences**,
each meeting a different first landform.

**What converts a visitor into a member — mechanism, not ceremony:** not yet derivable from admissible material
alone; the Membership-by-Unremarked-Persistence technique (`Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md` #10) points at
**continued participation in water-basin awareness** as a strong candidate (an outsider who has started
tracking which basin is currently good, unprompted, has started behaving like a resident) — **flagged as a
strong candidate finding, not yet confirmed**, since it depends on Phase 4 material (routines) this pass has
already written but not yet cross-checked against Phase 7 (governance/economy) for whether that tracking is
genuinely need-driven labor or optional local color.

### 5e — Dependency

What Zhongshan needs from elsewhere that it cannot make: **imported goods via the Hwy 110/22/4 network and
Prydz Bay shipping** — not yet itemized from admissible material (no Zhongshan-specific import list found).
Flagged as a REQUESTED input. **Who knows this dependency exists:** presumably anyone who has ever needed
something the local economy (Phase 1 G4/G7: maritime logistics, scientific research, and whatever the Phase 1
deficit research eventually surfaces) doesn't supply — not yet specific enough to be a finding.
