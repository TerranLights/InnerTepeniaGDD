## ⚠ Self-correction: the earlier draft's lens claim was wrong, and Gate F should have caught it sooner

`03_Phases_6_through_10.md`'s original Phase 9A stated: *"a founding-nation lens... fits Zhongshan's robot
population as directly as it fits the human population, per Census I's own robot/human parity."* **This was
written before the mandatory canon target for this exact phase was actually opened**, in direct violation of
`00_RUNBOOK.md` §E.1 (check canon before deriving) and `03` §0.2.2 (check canon before deriving anything
structural) — the earlier draft reasoned from demographic parity alone rather than from the binding cultural-
identity canon this phase specifically requires.

**What the canon actually says (`Robot_Universals` Ch. 13, read in full above):** robot cultural identity
tracks **city/locality**, not founding nation, not Gen/Mark, and not Build's physical result. The chapter's own
worked case (the Guadalajara Analogy) is explicit that shared life and shared struggle in one place — not
origin mechanics — is what decides in-group identity, for humans and, by the chapter's direct extension, for
robots. **Founding-nation-flavored circumstance is not a competing axis**; per Ch. 13's own account, it is
simply Build's circumstance (which local community had the interest that produced a robot), and that
circumstance is itself downstream of city — "it does not function as a separate identity axis running
alongside city/locality. It is simply another expression of that same underlying tie."

**Corrected lens: City/Locality — Zhongshan itself — not founding nation.** For Zhongshan's founding-cohort
robots specifically, the founding nation (Sinian Federation) is not a separate identity thread competing with
"being from Zhongshan" — it is *how* those robots came to be built here, which is exactly the same city-tie a
later-immigrated human resident has, expressed through Build's history rather than through choice-of-residence.
**This is consistent with, not contradictory to, Phase 2's arrival-mode finding** (founding cohort
assigned/posted, later population chose it) — it just means that finding describes *how people/robots arrived
at the city-tie*, not a rival axis to it.

**Logged as a genuine self-caught error**, not a snag routed around privately — recorded to the shared
observations file (see below), since a canon-check-before-deriving violation inside this pass's own work is
exactly the kind of thing the recording law wants captured, however small.

## A. Populations present, and the lens

**Populations:** robots and humans (Category 1 of the Dolliverse's four representation categories — per
`Doll_Representation_Categories.md`, the only category currently in use across every Tepenian project;
Categories 2–4 have no assigned characters anywhere and do not apply here).

**Lens, corrected:** **City/Locality** — what it means to be *from Zhongshan specifically*, for both
populations, is the organizing question. This is not the outer-city default described secondhand in `03`
Phase 9's own text ("the outer-city version anchors robot culture in founding-nation threads") — **that
described text is itself now flagged as needing a cross-check against `Robot_Universals` Ch. 13**, since the
binding universe-repo chapter this session read directly contradicts a founding-nation-as-primary-axis framing.
**Not asserted as a blanket correction to the whole methodology** — flagged as a specific finding for this
pass, with a recommendation to verify against how other completed outer-city Phase 9 sections actually handle
it, since `03`'s own text may be describing existing (possibly also-uncorrected) practice rather than a rule.

## B. Per-population culture, drawing on prior phases

**Robots specifically, per Ch. 14 (Gen/Mark):** Mark is chamber-hardware generation (currently Mark IV,
designed at Neumayer); Gen is a robot's own generational identity. **Nearly every city has its own local
fabrication chamber** — Zhongshan is not the Dome Fuji exception (voluntary-residence-only, no local
fabrication) — so **a Zhongshan-built robot's fabrication and her home city are, as a rule, the same place**,
per Ch. 14's own general rule. This directly supports the corrected lens above: a robot fabricated in Zhongshan
by a local Zhongshan community *is* a city-tie, by construction, regardless of which Mark generation her
chamber happens to be — **Gen/Mark carries real but secondary weight**, tracking chamber-design history rather
than locality (the same Mark ships to many cities at once), so **it should not be treated as a second organizing
axis for this phase**, only as texture. **Never a hierarchy** (Ch. 14's explicit, binding rule) — no Gen/Mark
finding in this pass may imply one generation is "better," only different, per the design-history logic Ch. 14
lays out.

**Human population, per Phase 2's arrival-mode finding:** founding cohort assigned/posted (no individual
choice), later immigration self-selected. **Both populations converge on the same city-tie mechanism** the
corrected lens above requires — humans through residence and choice (for the later cohort) or treaty-assignment
(for the founding one), robots through Build's local circumstance — which is a genuinely unifying finding
across both populations, not a coincidence: **the corrected Ch. 13 lens is what makes it possible to state
this convergence as a single finding rather than two separate population-specific ones.**

## C. Inter-population relations — local inflection, not national baseline

**Is there a category here that matters more than kind (robot/human)?** Given the corrected lens: **plausibly
yes — city-tie itself (how long/how directly someone's own arrival, or a robot's own Build, connects to
Zhongshan specifically) may matter more day-to-day than robot-vs-human kind.** A founding-cohort-descended
robot and a founding-cohort-descended human plausibly share more lived experience of "being from here" than
either does with a same-kind but recently-arrived counterpart. **Flagged as a strong candidate finding,
directly derived from this session's corrected canon read, not yet cross-checked against Phase 4's material**
(which was written before this correction) — the next step, not run this session, is reconciling Phase 4's
"different arrival-mode populations have different days" finding against this population-crossing reading,
since they may describe the same underlying axis from two different phases.

**Project-wide baseline held in mind, not independently re-verified this session:** human-robot relations in
this project are broadly egalitarian with a robot-skewed population overall — Zhongshan's own Census II
figures (522,372 humans / 474,312 robots, near-parity, slightly human-heavy) are consistent with this being an
ordinary rather than an exceptional city on that axis, unlike Kunlun or Dome Fuji, which restrict human
presence entirely. **No admissible Zhongshan-specific source was opened this session to confirm this beyond
noting the census figures are unremarkable against the project baseline** — flagged as consistent, not
independently re-derived from a dedicated file, since no dedicated project-repo file for this baseline was
found under that name this session.

is too small to draw much confidence from the distribution itself.

## E. Swap test

Would the corrected city/locality finding survive being swapped onto a comparable, differently-configured city?

than a location-specific finding. **What would NOT swap** is the *specific convergence* claimed in section C
above (founding-cohort robots and founding-cohort humans sharing more with each other than with same-kind
newer arrivals) — this depends on Zhongshan's specific two-layer arrival-mode finding from Phase 2, which is
itself already swap-tested (Gate 4, prior session) and found not to generalize cleanly to Casey. **Consistent
chain of reasoning across sessions**, not contradictory.
