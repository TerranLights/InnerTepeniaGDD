# Sinheung — Phase 4: Ordinary Life

## A. State the headline function, then write away from it

**Headline function:** chamber manufacturing / heavy industrial fabrication. **The failure mode to avoid:** "a
resident works at the Chamber Works or a fabrication line, described in more detail." Per `03` Phase 4's test
— *would this reasonably be someone's entire day, every day, forever?* — chamber manufacturing itself employs
only a fraction of 888,292 residents; most of Sinheung's population does not touch the Chamber Works at all.
**Writing away from it, per the phase's own core move.**

## B. Four distinct elements, covered separately

### Routines

Most residents' days are organized around **the general fabrication economy** (raw-material receiving,
finished-goods assembly, freight/logistics for the Mirny supply line — Phase 1's G3 profile, not the chamber-
specific slice of it) rather than the nationally-significant but numerically small chamber trade. **Differentiation
axis: organized around a freight schedule**, not a shift bell or a tide — Sinheung's stated economic geography
(raw material arriving via the Zhongshan tri-junction) means the day's actual rhythm-setter is when the Mirny
freight comes in, not an internal factory clock. This is a genuinely different organizing principle than a
single-institution "shift" city.

### Mundane concerns

**Physical-scale, non-thematic, per `03` Phase 4 §D:** given the density flag from Phase 1 (Sinheung may share
a ~34km² oasis with two full-scale sibling cities), the single most concrete, non-thematic mundane concern is
**where things go** — storage, right-of-way, whose delivery gets the narrow street first. A land-scarce
industrial city's Tuesday is dominated by logistics friction that a land-abundant Tepenian city never
experiences: two trucks cannot both use the same access road at once, and there may not be a second road.
**This is the third-order consequence of Phase 1's G2 land-scarcity deficit showing up in somebody's actual
day**, exactly per `03` Phase 1's promise that Phase 4 is where a capability deficit's cost surfaces.

### Personal struggles

**Chained directly from Phase 1's central finding** (Sinheung pays, continuously, on four fronts, to keep being
believed to have deserved to exist): an ordinary resident's personal struggle is plausibly not about survival
(the city is materially secure — inherited infrastructure, a load-bearing national trade) but about **status
anxiety at the level of the whole city, inherited personally.** A resident born after the founding generation
did not choose to inherit a claim that must be continuously proven — but per Phase 1's G4 standing cost, they
likely still carry some version of "prove it" pressure, whether or not they work anywhere near the Chamber
Works. **Concrete form, checked against the general-population discipline:** this should not be written as
literal daily anxiety (too solemn, too totalizing per `00d`'s shadow-proportion discipline) but as a low-grade,
mostly-unnoticed civic reflex — finishing tasks fully rather than adequately, a slight overcorrection toward
demonstrable output even in contexts (a hobby, a chore) where nobody is actually watching. **This is the
"unnoticed byproduct of a sincere pursuit" shape `00d` requires**, not a policy or a stated value.

### Escapism / downtime

**Explicitly required, most often cut for space — covered deliberately.** Given the land-scarcity constraint
(§B Mundane, above) and the freeze-thaw seasonal register (Phase 3), the sharpest escapism candidate is
**something that uses the thaw season specifically**, since roughly two-thirds of the year the ground is
actually walkable/usable in a way most Tepenian Antarctic cities' residents never get. **Candidate, flagged for
Phase 8/10 development rather than fully resolved here:** informal use of the oasis's meltwater lakes (Specs:
"the Larsemann Hills... meltwater lakes") for genuine outdoor leisure during the ~9-month non-polar-night
stretch — a real amenity most Tepenian Antarctic cities structurally cannot offer at all, since most of the
continent has no ice-free ground to walk on, let alone open water. **This is Sinheung's own inversion of the
usual Antarctic-settlement problem**: instead of "how do people find joy indoors, always," the question becomes
"what happens to a joy that is only available two-thirds of the year, and what do people do during the polar
night when it's gone?" — a genuine seasonal-identity question Phase 6 should pick up.

## C. Do not write one universal resident

Per Phase 2's composition: at minimum, distinguish (1) the numerically small chamber-trade workforce (the most
narratively visible but least representative population), (2) the broader fabrication/logistics workforce (the
actual majority pattern), and (3) non-industrial residents (services, administration, the "prove it" civic
reflex applying differently to someone with no direct link to the trade at all — arguably the sharpest test of
whether the finding in Phase 1 is really civic rather than merely occupational).

## D. Physical-scale commute/logistics texture

Given the extent ambiguity flagged in the Frame file (REQUESTED, unresolved), commute texture is written
provisionally on the "shared 34km²" assumption: **short physical distances, but heavily congested by the land-
scarcity/access-road friction named in §B** — a commute that is short in kilometers and long in wait-time,
which is a genuinely different mundane texture than either a sprawling city (long distances) or a dense but
spacious one (short distances, short waits). **If the true extent turns out closer to the one-third-share
reading (Phase 1's density flag), this entire commute texture would need to shift toward something closer to
vertical/enclosed-city congestion** — flagged as dependent on the still-open extent request.

## E. What the rhythm fails to provide

**For residents whose lives do not fit the freight-schedule rhythm** (§B Routines): anyone working night-shift-
adjacent or off the Mirny delivery cycle (Phase 2's population is Band 4 Urban — internally differentiated,
not uniform) experiences a city whose actual organizing clock is invisible to them, which is a plausible seed
for Phase 7's counterculture/private-life work: **people who are structurally out of sync with the freight
rhythm** (medical staff, night-shift chamber technicians, anyone whose work doesn't touch logistics at all) may
be Sinheung's least-served population by its own ambient rhythm, distinct from any national-origin-based
counterculture.
