# Sinheung — Phase 9: Populations

## A. Populations present, and the lens for each

**Robots and humans** (this project's established categories; the other three representation categories are
project-wide unused, not a Sinheung-specific gap).

**Lens decision, made explicitly per `03` Phase 9's requirement:** the outer-city default anchors robot culture
in **founding-nation** threads. **Departing from that default here, with reasoning stated:** Phase 8's own
finding — that Sinheung's robot population has an unusually *literal*, direct relationship to its own point of
origin, since this is one of only two Tepenian cities that physically manufactures the chamber apparatus robots
are made in — is a stronger, more differentiated anchor than founding-nation would produce. **Anchor chosen:
function** (specifically, proximity to the Chamber Works and the chamber-manufacturing trade), not origin.
**Stated per the phase's own requirement:** this is a deliberate departure from the outer-city norm, made
because Sinheung's own established facts (not a preference for novelty) supply a sharper anchor.

## B. Per-population culture

**Robots, anchored on function/proximity to chamber manufacture:** drawing on Phase 1's G1 profile (Electricity
— Animation, "the spark that turns inert matter into something conscious and active" — and its negative pole,
total dependency, collapse the instant the current is withdrawn) and Phase 8's chamber-cuisine candidate: a
robot population for whom the site of their own creation is not abstract or distant but **a specific, nameable
building in the city they live in** is a genuinely distinctive condition. **Candidate finding, chained rather
than invented:** robots manufactured at Sinheung's own Chamber Works, and robots manufactured elsewhere but now
living in Sinheung, plausibly have a different relationship to the building — the former have a direct
biographical tie to a specific local site; the latter live adjacent to a facility that is, for them, exactly as
abstract as it is for a Sinheung human. **This produces a within-robot-population distinction that has nothing
to do with national origin at all** — directly testing (and passing) `03` Phase 9C's own strongest-move
question: *is there a category here that matters more than kind?* **Yes — "made here" vs. "made elsewhere," a
distinction that cuts across the human/robot line only on one side (it doesn't apply to humans at all) but cuts
sharply within the robot population itself.**

**Humans, anchored on national-origin composition (Phase 2):** the "prove it" civic reflex (Phase 4/6) is
provisionally treated as a shared thin layer across the whole human population regardless of origin, since it
derives from Sinheung's founding condition (Phase 1, G4) rather than from any one nation's own culture. **Swap
test, run per §E below, before this is accepted.**

## C. Inter-population relations — the local inflection, not the national baseline

**Restating the project-wide human-robot baseline (egalitarian, skewed robot per composition) would not be a
finding** — per `03` Phase 9C's own instruction, checking instead for Sinheung's own local inflection.

**Candidate finding:** the "made here / made elsewhere" robot-internal distinction from §B, combined with
Phase 7's output-proven (not origin-based) status mechanism, suggests **Sinheung's actual locally-salient
social category is not human-vs-robot or nation-of-origin at all, but demonstrated-output vs. not**, cutting
across every other population line simultaneously. A "made here" robot with a strong output record and a
Korean-origin human with a weak one would, on this reading, sit on opposite sides of Sinheung's real local
status axis despite the origin-based categories suggesting otherwise. **This is the strongest possible form of
`03` Phase 9C's "a category that matters more than kind" move** — it subsumes both the national-origin axis
(Phase 2) and the human/robot axis under one civic logic (Phase 1's central cost-dominant finding, restated a
fourth time, now at the population-relations level). **Flagged as this pass's second-strongest candidate
finding, after Phase 1's own central finding**, and explicitly checked below for whether it rests on anything
provisional.

## D. Dual-tagging

| Finding | Inheritance tag | Depth tag |
|---|---|---|
| "Made here" vs. "made elsewhere" robot distinction | **Genuinely emergent** — not inherited from canon, not adapted from an existing project pattern; derived from Sinheung's own specific chamber-manufacturing fact | **Deep** — chains through three prior phases (1, 4, 7) rather than sitting on the surface |
| Output-proven status as the real cross-cutting local axis | **Genuinely emergent**, same reasoning | **Deep** — synthesizes Phases 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8 into one civic logic |
| The "prove it" reflex as shared across all national-origin groups equally | **Adapted** — extends Phase 1's founding-condition finding, not newly derived here | **Surface, pending §E's swap test** — this is the one finding in this phase most at risk of being asserted rather than earned |

**Per `03` Phase 9D's own warning, and a developer clarification worth recording exactly:** the diagnostic
guards against a pass that clusters *entirely* in "surface"/"inherited" — i.e., one that never actually
synthesized anything and is only restating canon under new headings. **It is not a target ratio, and multiple
"genuinely emergent, deep" findings in the same phase are not a risk to be hedged against — they are the actual
goal of the methodology**, per the developer's own framing during this run: *"the whole point of this
methodology is to build out, extrapolate, and fully develop locations... multiple 'emergent,' 'genuinely deep'
findings and results is something that's welcome, because those are attributes and characteristics that will
make a location feel real."* Two of this phase's three findings landing as emergent/deep is a good result, not
a ratio needing correction — the third finding is flagged as weaker because the swap test in §E genuinely
raised a risk for it specifically, not to manufacture balance against the other two.

## E. Swap test

**Would the "prove it" shared-reflex finding survive if Sinheung's name were swapped for a comparable city's?**
**Genuinely uncertain, and flagged rather than asserted.** The reflex was derived from Sinheung's specific
founding condition (a paper claim secured before the population existed) — Shirayuki shares that exact founding
mechanism (Phase 5's geometry: both diplomatically-engineered) and so a version of this finding might equally
apply there, which would mean the finding is about the **Jeju-do founding mechanism generally**, not Sinheung
specifically. **This is exactly the kind of swap-test failure Gate 4 is designed to catch, run here in advance
rather than only at Step 7.** Recorded honestly as a real risk: **this pass's central finding (Phase 1) and this
phase's second-strongest finding may partially describe the Jeju-do diplomatic-founding condition rather than
Sinheung uniquely** — Zhongshan, founded by organic station-claim confirmation rather than paper allocation,
would be the sharper swap-test partner to differentiate against at Step 7, since Zhongshan's founding mechanism
genuinely differs (confirmed prior claim vs. allocated-from-nothing claim) in a way Shirayuki's does not.
