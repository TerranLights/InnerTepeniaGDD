# Sinheung — Phase 2: Composition & Arrival

## A. Composition from canon, read with its own stated limits

Per Specs/Sinheung.md, figures use the project's standard share-weighting methodology (Primary=8 shares,
Significant=3, Notable=1), **de-stacked so same-tier nations don't show identical numbers — these are relative-
ranking figures, not a literal per-nation census**, respected per `05` §6's scoped-input test.

**Primary:** South Korea (34.62%). **Significant:** China (9.74%), Germany (8.64%), Japan (7.78%), Russia
(6.85%), Indonesia (4.41%), Australia (3.57%). **Notable:** Thailand, Estonia, Hungary, Lithuania, Malaysia,
Vietnam, Philippines, Bulgaria, Latvia, South Africa (1.23%–3.65% each).

**Census I → II:** 1,069,350 → 888,292 combined. **Retention: 83.1%.** Per the project's own standing rule
(`00_RUNBOOK.md` trap #2), this drop is **pre-war orbital emigration, not war loss** — the "damaged, partially
operational" status is a separate, later, post-war condition layered on top of an already-smaller Census II
population.

## B. Arrival-mode classification — the core move

**Sinheung's founding is a two-layer arrival, and the layering is the finding.**

**Layer 1 — Fled to it (the Antarctic diaspora generally).** Like nearly every Tepenian settlement, the
founding population arrived because the Upper Earth war made their prior lives impossible. This layer is
common to the whole project and is not, by itself, differentiating.

**Layer 2 — Inherited it (this specific site, specifically).** This is Sinheung's genuinely distinctive layer.
Per `03` Phase 2's taxonomy, "Inherited it" means the place **came with the population rather than being chosen
by them** — "acquired, annexed, absorbed... a population whose membership was decided elsewhere." That is
exactly Sinheung's founding mechanism: the Jeju-do court secured the claim **before a single Korean exile had
set foot on the ice**. The Korean founding population did not choose Sinheung the way a settler chooses a site,
and they did not simply happen to end up there the way most exile populations did — **a specific site was
assigned to them, at a negotiating table, by people who were not them.**

**The compound arrival mode — "fled to the continent, inherited the specific plot" — is the axis.** Most
Tepenian cities are single-layer ("fled to it," full stop, with whatever site happened to be occupiable). Only
Sinheung and Shirayuki (per Specs' own "Legacy" section: "one of only two Tepenian cities settled this way
rather than through organic station inheritance") carry this second layer. **This is differentiation handed to
the pass for free**, per `05` §2.4's special-case rule on a known "first/only/last."

## C. Native layer vs. transplanted layer

**There is no pre-existing indigenous population to separate against** (Antarctica had none) — the relevant
split per `Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md` §12 is instead **founding-generation-developed** versus **later-
diaspora-brought**, which maps onto Census I's founding cohort against everything added or layered on since.

**What the founding generation "developed on its own," within the bounds of admissible canon:** almost nothing
is stated outright — this is itself informative. Sinheung's admissible attribute record describes what it
**inherited** (Russian station infrastructure) and what it **produces** (chamber manufacture, per the Neumayer
schematic) far more than what it **originated**. Cross-referencing Phase 1's G4 finding: the founding population
inherited an advanced physical plant they did not build, and the standing cost of that founding condition was
continuously proving the claim deserved to exist. **A population whose founding energy went into proving a
claim, rather than into building physical infrastructure from nothing, would plausibly show fewer "native"
markers than a population that built its own settlement from the ground up** — this is a testable prediction
for Phase 8 (Making): expect fewer purely-Sinheung-originated material-culture items than at a settlement that
started from nothing.

## E. Who is not here — an absent population

**No Primary- or Significant-tier nation from India, the UK, or the Americas appears anywhere in Sinheung's
composition table**, at any tier. The UK's entire prior share was explicitly *removed and reassigned* during the
founding re-resolution (Round 1, Specs). **The India absence is project-wide canon** (per this project's
standing No-Subcontinentals rule, not a Sinheung-specific finding — noted for completeness, not claimed as
differentiation). **The UK's specific, deliberate removal (rather than simple absence) is more interesting**:
Sinheung's composition was actively re-engineered to *remove* a nation that had been present in an earlier
resolution round, specifically to make room for boosting Korea to Primary tier. **This means Sinheung's current
demographic profile is not simply "who arrived" but the visible trace of a correction process** — the UK's
absence is a scar from getting the founding-nation attribution right, not a natural demographic fact. Flagged
as a REQUESTED-input-adjacent observation: this is attribute-level (a documented editorial correction, not an
interpretation), so it is admissible, and it is a genuinely unusual thing for a location's composition table to
carry visibly.

## F. Pull vs. push, and retention (`02` G8, `05` §2.4's arrival pair)

**Sinheung reads as push-dominated, not pull-dominated**, and the reasoning chains directly from Phase 1:

- Nothing in admissible canon describes Sinheung as an attractive destination in the way Shirayuki is
  explicitly described (Specs' own arrival-pair worked example, `05` §2.4: Shirayuki is "the Federation's
  clearest attraction case... people make excuses to be able to move there"). Sinheung offers **no comparable
  pull mechanism** — its defining assets (inherited infrastructure, chamber manufacturing capacity, a
  diplomatically-secured claim) are not personally attractive to an individual chooser; they are structural
  facts about the place, not draws.
- **Retention (83.1%) is comparatively high.** ⚠ **Flagged rather than asserted as a firm z-score**: `05` §2.4
  cites a worked case where a pull city's retention (61.8%) sat against "a 71.9% national mean" — but that
  71.9% figure is itself drawn from a different worked example in the rule file, not independently re-verified
  here against the full 33-city set. **This run has not re-computed the actual current mean**, and per `04`
  Gate 11's own standing warning ("hand-check one row before trusting any computed figure"), treating 83.1% as
  simply "above average" without recomputing the mean would repeat exactly the kind of unverified-plausible-
  number error the methodology exists to catch. **REQUESTED**: the current, verified retention mean across all
  35 cities, to properly z-score Sinheung's 83.1% at Gate 7/11 rather than asserting it now.
- **What can be said without the z-score:** per `05` §2.4's durability note, a push-dominated population
  accumulates **high-switching-cost attachments** — a trade, a claim, an obligation, people who know your
  business — which are unpleasant and which hold. Sinheung's founding condition (an inherited claim that must
  be continuously proven) and its economic function (a nationally-load-bearing trade, chamber manufacture) are
  both exactly this shape: obligations, not amenities. **The retention figure is consistent with this reading
  even before a z-score confirms its magnitude**, and the prediction is falsifiable: if Sinheung's true z-score
  turns out low/unremarkable, this finding should be discarded rather than kept on vibes, per LAW 0's
  anti-decoration-research rule.
