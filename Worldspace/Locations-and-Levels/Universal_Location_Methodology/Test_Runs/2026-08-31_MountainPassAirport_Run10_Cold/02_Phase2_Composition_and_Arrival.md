# Phase 2 — Composition & Arrival

**Asks:** Who is actually here, in what proportion, from where — and by what route did they come to be
here?

---

## A. Composition, from canon, read for its own stated limits

**Two source populations, unequal in kind, not just in number:**
- **Kunlun** — 0 humans / 123,449 robots (Census I). Entirely robot. A deliberately curated astronomy-
  and-comms-heritage population (per `Specs/Kunlun.md`), the only Tepenian city whose composition reflects
  a specific professional/scientific lineage rather than founding-operator history.
- **Vostok** — 129,617 humans / 259,644 robots (Census I). Mixed, roughly 2:1 robot:human. Primary nations
  USA and Japan.

**Read for the source's own stated limits, per `05` §6.2:** Vostok's per-nation table states its figures
use "the project's standard share-weighting methodology," explicitly not a literal census count. This pass
does not build any finding on a specific percentage past what that limit allows — the human/robot totals
(from Census I directly, not the weighted per-nation breakdown) are used, since those are the figures the
composition file itself presents as real counts rather than weighted shares.

**No canon figure for the outpost's own actual staff composition.** This pass reasons from the two source
cities' own population *ratios* as the working hypothesis for staff draw, explicitly flagged provisional
(already logged as a REQUESTED item in `00_Frame_and_PreFlight.md`).

**Working composition, stated as a hypothesis, not a fact:** if staff are drawn roughly proportionally
from each city's own available technical workforce, the outpost's crew skews **robot-majority, plausibly
robot-supermajority** — Kunlun can only ever contribute robots, and even Vostok's own contribution is
roughly two-thirds robot on its home city's own ratio. **A crew of, say, 15–25 (open season) drawing
evenly from both cities would very plausibly include one human or none at all in the closure-season
skeleton crew, and perhaps two to four humans, all Vostok-origin, during the open season.**

## B. Arrival-mode taxonomy

**Overwhelmingly one mode, and this is itself the finding.**

| Mode | Present here? | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Assigned / posted** | **Dominant, near-total.** | Everyone here was sent by one institution or the other — Vostok's or Kunlun's own technical program. This produces the taxonomy's own predicted consequence directly: a population that did not choose each other, and a **rotation clock** everyone is aware of (the seasonal open/closure swing itself functions as this clock, more visibly than at almost any Settlement-type location this methodology has run). |
| **Chose it** | **Effectively absent.** | Nobody applies to Mountain Pass the way someone might choose Shirayuki for its art scene — per Phase 1's G5 reading, the place has no independent pull. A worker may have chosen the *career* (chamber engineering) without ever choosing *this specific posting* over another. |
| **Fled to it / sentenced to it** | **Absent**, no canon basis, not invented. | Nothing suggests punitive or refugee arrival here — this is skilled, voluntary-career-track assignment, not exile. |
| **Born here** | **Structurally near-impossible**, and worth stating plainly rather than leaving silent. | At Band 1 with a seasonal crew swing this small, a child raised entirely at Mountain Pass Airport is not a realistic case this pass invents — the "native" layer this location could have simply does not exist in this frame. Recorded as a genuine null, per `03` §0.2 item 4, not an oversight. |
| **Inherited it** | **Absent** — this is not an acquired or annexed place. |  |
| **Stopped while passing** | **Present, in a narrow and specific form.** | Per Phase 1's G5 reading, this is the corridor's only stop — a traveler with an unexecuted plan (heading further down Hwy 37, delayed by weather, awaiting the next viable window) genuinely does end up here longer than intended. This population is transient, not staff, and is treated separately below. |

**Differentiation axis, named:** ***assigned-majority, with a genuinely distinct transient-traveler
minority who did not choose to stay this long.*** This is a two-population mix, not a single mode — and
per Step 2's own instruction, the mix itself is the finding: an assigned-only population reads as barracks;
adding the involuntary-transient minority, however small, gives the place a second, structurally different
relationship to being here that the staff themselves do not share.

## C. Native layer vs. transplanted layer, separated explicitly

**There is no native layer**, per §A above — nobody was born here in any generation this frame can
support. **The entire population is transplanted**, in the strict sense of Technique 12 (Native Before
Transplanted): everyone's culture, at root, is Vostok's or Kunlun's own established culture, temporarily
relocated.

**This is a genuine, load-bearing null, not a gap.** Per Technique 12's own caution — "a place whose whole
culture is its immigrants' cultures has no culture" — Mountain Pass Airport's own distinct character
(insofar as it has one, developed across the remaining phases) has to come from what happens **when** two
different transplanted cultures meet inside one small, cost-dominant, function-first space, not from any
organic culture the place grew on its own. **This is stated explicitly here so later phases do not
silently invent a native tradition this location's own arrival profile does not support.**

---

## D. Who is not here

- **No children.** Per §B's "Born here" null and the seasonal/technical-posting nature of arrival, nobody
  brings dependents to a closure-season skeleton crew at this altitude.
- **No elderly, in the settled sense.** A posting this physically demanding (chronic hypobaric-hypoxia
  exposure, per Phase 1's research) plausibly has an implicit fitness/age ceiling, though canon does not
  state one explicitly — flagged as a reasonable inference, not invented as a rule.
- **No non-technical population at all.** Everyone present is here because of the manufacturing/airstrip
  function specifically — there is no service class, no support population beyond what the technical crew
  itself performs. This is the sharpest possible version of Phase 1's DEFICIT finding (no civic apparatus):
  it shows up here as literally nobody whose job is anything but the core function.

## E. Borrowed Form — checked, not forced

Per `Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md`'s Borrowed Form technique: before inventing any missing social
form, check what the incoming populations already carry. **Both source populations are themselves
technical/scientific-heritage cultures** (Kunlun's curated astronomy-and-comms lineage; Vostok's own
industrial/survival-hardened population) — neither carries an obviously portable *civic* or *domestic*
form the way, say, a refugee-heavy district's diaspora composition does. **This technique produces a null
here**, honestly recorded: the deficit this place has (no home-making apparatus) is not one either source
culture happens to carry a spare copy of. This sharpens rather than weakens Phase 1's Unrecognized
Instrument finding — the remedy, such as it is, had to be found *inside* the shipping-caretaking process
itself, because it could not be borrowed from either founding population's own home culture.

## Differentiation, Band, and closing notes

**Band variance applied correctly:** at Band 1, per `01` §2.3, there is no general population to
generalize to — "who lives here" is answered by naming the small crew's own variance (assigned-majority
technical staff, split by source city and by season) rather than by inventing an averaged Mountain Pass
resident. This phase deliberately does not produce a single composite "Mountain Pass worker" archetype.

**Feeds:** Phase 4 (whose ordinary day — the seasonal split matters directly), Phase 5 (who the traveler
population is), Phase 9 (the robot/human populations' own relation, given the Kunlun/Vostok asymmetry
named in Phase 1 §C).
