# Phase 5 — Relation & Geometry

**Asks:** What is this place to the places around it, to the thing that contains it, and to anyone
crossing its edge?

**Weighting note, per `03` Phase 5's own instruction:** most locations this methodology runs are isolated,
which weights this phase toward 5c/5e. **Mountain Pass Airport is an exception to that default** — it sits
*directly between* two real, active, close peers (Vostok and Kunlun are its literal endpoints), so 5a is
unusually rich for a non-Settlement location, alongside 5c/5e.

---

## 5a — Peers

**Vostok and Kunlun, both directly adjacent — not a "category" peer relationship but two specific, named
cities.**

- **What flows, and in which direction:** chambers ship outward from Mountain Pass toward both cities'
  onward distribution; staff and supplies rotate in from both; the comms/social lifeline (Phase 4) connects
  outpost workers to both home cities.
- **The loud friction — plausible, not confirmed, flagged as inferred:** resource-priority disputes when
  the outpost's limited weekly output can only fully serve one city's near-term need before the other's.
  No canon confirms this occurs; it is a structurally plausible consequence of joint ownership over a
  single scarce output, named as inferred material rather than invented as settled fact.
- **The quiet friction, and it is sharper than the loud one:** the demographic asymmetry from Phase 1/2 —
  Kunlun can only ever contribute robot staff; Vostok contributes both. **Nobody frames this as a
  grievance.** It simply means that, in any given crew, Kunlun's own "home team" presence skews entirely
  robot while Vostok's skews mixed — an asymmetry both cities know about and neither has ever needed to
  discuss, because it has never produced a conflict, only a quietly uneven texture.

## 5b — The three-way differentiation set — own-eras substitute

No sibling exists (per `01` §5.3a). Phase 3D found a null on the founding/crisis-era axis specifically —
but Phase 3E's own strong finding (open season vs. closure season) supplies a genuine three-state axis when
combined with the founding moment itself. **Category chosen: who has the final say when something goes
wrong.**

| State | Who decides |
|---|---|
| **Founding** (the Fort McMurdo-to-Mountain-Pass site decision) | Remote administrators in Vostok and Kunlun, jointly, before anyone was ever posted on-site — a decision made entirely *about* the place, by people who were never *in* it. |
| **Open season (now)** | Shared, joint, on-site engineering judgment — per `02` §6.3's own real-world grounding (the actual Concordia Station's "equal partnership, shared technical responsibilities," no documented single-chief hierarchy), decisions are made collaboratively among whoever is present from both founding cities. |
| **Closure season (now, the worst point)** | Whoever is present, alone or in a pair, makes the call — the Band-1 "the person IS the office" dynamic (`01` §2.2) is not a historical curiosity here, it is the literal, current operating condition for several months of every year. |

**The finding:** decision-authority at Mountain Pass Airport migrates from *external and administrative*
(founding) to *collaborative and joint* (open season) to *singular and unaccountable* (closure season) —
**and this is not a degradation, it is a designed-in seasonal oscillation nobody had to invent a policy
for.** The Band-1 fragility rule (`01` §2.3) that governs the closure-season state is a genuine structural
condition, not a footnote: for months at a time, a wrong call has no second opinion available at all.

## 5c — The parent

**Primary content, unusually so for this phase, since the "parent" here is two cities in active tension
with each other's own priorities rather than one settled authority.**

**What the parent(s) need versus what the outpost believes it is for:** already established in Phase 1
(G3) — the workforce's own self-conception ("we build chambers correctly") is narrower than what Vostok
and Kunlun actually need from the place (manufacturing output, corridor infrastructure for all of Hwy 37,
and — per canon's own explicit framing — mutual isolation relief for two cities that consider themselves
"the two loneliest outposts"). **This divergence is this pass's single strongest Phase 5 finding**: the
outpost does not know it is doing three jobs. It believes it is doing one.

**What each founding city individually believes about the outpost — flagged as inferred, not confirmed:**
a plausible, uncontradicted reading is that Vostok, with its larger and more industrially self-sufficient
population, may weight the outpost primarily as **logistics infrastructure** (a shipping asset on its own
highway), while Kunlun, smaller and entirely robot, may weight it more as the **tangible expression of the
loneliest-outposts relationship** itself — the actual point of contact, not merely a means to an end. Both
readings are consistent with each city's own established character (Vostok's industrial/survival-hardened
population; Kunlun's specifically curated, relationship-poor astronomy-and-comms population) but neither
is stated in canon, and this pass records the divergence as a REQUESTED-adjacent open question rather than
adopting either reading as settled.

## 5d — The edge and the crossing

**Who arrives, by what route, and what do they meet first:** everyone arrives via Hwy 37, from either
Kunlun's or Vostok's direction; per Phase 3B, what they meet first is the airlock/suiting-up transition
between the lethal exterior and the pressurized, dry, static-prone interior — a hard, immediate,
physically-enforced threshold with no gradient.

**What converts a visitor into a member?** Given the near-total absence of long-term settlement (Phase 2),
"member" does not carry its usual weight here. **The mechanism that exists is narrower and sharper: being
trapped by a missed changeover.** A transient traveler (Phase 2's "stopped while passing" mode) who misses
the safe pre-closure window is, structurally, folded into the closure-season skeleton crew's own routine
for the duration — not by ceremony or even by anyone's decision, simply because there is no meaningful
distinction between "guest" and "extra pair of hands" once the route is shut and every task needs doing.
**This is a mechanism, not a ceremony, exactly per §5's own governing rule** — and it is the single
sharpest version of "membership by unremarked persistence" (`Cultural_Synthesis_Techniques.md` technique
10) this pass has found: nobody decides you are now staff. The season simply makes it true.

### Arcanet status — caught at Gate C, folded in here

**Determined, not invented:** existing project canon establishes that extreme-altitude interior locations
(Dome Fuji, Kunlun, Vostok — all of which border or define this location's own position) carry
essentially no Arcanet coverage. Mountain Pass Airport, sitting directly on the same plateau stretch
between two of those three, inherits this condition rather than needing its own separate ruling — a
**Determined** property (`01` §5.1), not a local finding. This directly explains why Phase 4's comms
terminal functions as a dedicated point-to-point relay rather than an Arcanet endpoint, and why Gemini's
own Zodiac Lens reading ("The Relay Keeper") treats the link's continued operation as a fragile, actively
maintained thing rather than ambient infrastructure — consistent with, and now explicitly grounded by,
this Determined absence.

## 5e — Dependency

**What this place needs that it cannot make: everything except precision manufacturing and immediate
life support.** Food, most consumables, medical care beyond basic first aid, any repair capacity beyond
what a small technical crew can improvise. **This is a named dependency, not a shadow** — per `00d`'s own
distinction, a dependency everyone has openly acknowledged is politics/logistics, and this one is about as
openly acknowledged as a dependency can be: the entire founding condition (Phase 1, G4) already states the
place was never meant to be self-sufficient. There is no discoverable secret here, and this pass does not
manufacture one where the material genuinely does not support it.
