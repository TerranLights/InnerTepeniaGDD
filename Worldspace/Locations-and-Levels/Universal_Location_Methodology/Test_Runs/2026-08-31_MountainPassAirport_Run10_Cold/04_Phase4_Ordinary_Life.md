# Phase 4 — Ordinary Life

**Asks:** What is an ordinary person's day here, apart from what this place is famous for?

**Headline function, stated in one sentence, then written away from:** *Mountain Pass Airport builds
Cradle chambers and keeps an airstrip open.* The material below is deliberately not "residents build
chambers all day, described in more detail."

---

## A–B. Four elements, per population

Per Phase 2, this is not one population — staff (open-season and closure-season) and transient travelers
are genuinely different days.

### Open-season technical staff

- **Routines.** The day is organized around **acclimatization discipline**, not a shift clock in the usual
  sense — pacing exertion against the altitude, mandatory rest windows, hydration tracking (the air is too
  dry to trust thirst as a signal). The manufacturing work itself is a smaller share of the day than an
  outsider would guess; more of it is spent on the life-support and structural maintenance Phase 1 named
  as STANDING COST.
- **Mundane concerns.** Whether the redundant paper log was actually updated (per Phase 3's static-
  discharge finding) — a small, recurring, slightly resented chore nobody enjoys and everybody does anyway,
  because everyone has their own story about the one time a digital record vanished. Checking whether the
  day's weather window is closing early. Managing static shock from ordinary contact with metal surfaces —
  a genuinely constant minor physical irritant, not a dramatic hazard, just an unglamorous daily fact of
  the humidity level.
- **Personal struggles.** Per Phase 1's deficit-address finding: knowing exactly where home is (a highway-
  trip away, in one direction or the other) and not being there. This is sharper here than at almost any
  other location this methodology has run, precisely because the address is not diffuse or abstract — it
  is a specific, nameable, physically proximate place. For Vostok-origin humans specifically, the isolation
  compounds with the demographic finding from Phase 1/Phase 2: no "someone from home" community exists
  among the robot-majority crew the way it might for a Kunlun-origin robot worker.
- **Escapism / downtime.** Genuinely constrained, per Phase 1's GRUDGING TOLERANCE reading (off-hours with
  nowhere to go). The real-world research (Concordia Station, Dome C — see research log) grounds this
  directly: documented winterover psychological effects include altered time perception and interpersonal
  friction under confinement. **The specific, concrete escapism this crew has is communication with the
  two home cities** — the outpost's own relay/comms function, built for the manufacturing network, doubles
  informally as the crew's actual social lifeline. This is a real, physical, playable mechanism (a comms
  terminal, a scheduled call window), not an abstract "they feel lonely" statement.

### Closure-season skeleton crew

- **Routines.** Radically reduced in scope but not in vigilance — fewer people means each remaining task
  (life support, minimum airstrip upkeep, protecting chambers already mid-fabrication) is now one person's
  sole responsibility rather than a team's. Per Phase 1's `01` §2.3 Band-1 fragility rule: at this scale,
  **one departure — or one person becoming unable to work — can end a practice**, and the closure-season
  crew is small enough that this is a live, everyday risk rather than a theoretical one.
- **Mundane concerns.** Rationing attention, not supplies — the closure crew's actual daily concern is
  which of several always-necessary tasks gets done today and which waits, because there simply are not
  enough hands to do everything on the same schedule the open season affords.
- **Personal struggles.** The changeover-timing trap from Phase 1 is now lived experience rather than a
  structural risk: whoever is in the closure crew chose (or was assigned) to be here specifically through
  the total-darkness stretch, cut off until the route reopens — the real-world comparable's own documented
  9-month/4-month-dark isolation profile is the direct grounding for this.
- **Escapism / downtime.** Smaller crew, same comms lifeline, but now the *only* lifeline — no travelers
  passing through, no open-season colleagues to talk to in person, just the relay link home and each other.

### The transient traveler (stopped-while-passing, per Phase 2)

- **Routines.** None, by definition — a traveler's day here is whatever it takes to get moving again:
  checking the weather window, checking in with whoever is coordinating the next leg.
- **Mundane concerns.** How long the wait will actually be, and whether to trust an early departure window
  against the same changeover-timing risk the staff live with permanently.
- **Personal struggles.** An unexecuted plan, per the arrival-mode taxonomy's own predicted consequence —
  this is the one population at Mountain Pass whose entire relationship to the place is "I did not mean to
  be here this long."
- **Escapism / downtime.** Minimal and improvised — a traveler has none of the staff's routines to fall
  back on, and is, structurally, a guest inside someone else's cost-dominant workplace.

## C. Not one universal resident

Confirmed above — three genuinely distinct day-shapes (open-season staff, closure-season staff, transient
traveler), consistent with `03` Phase 4's own instruction not to collapse different arrival-mode
populations into one schedule.

## D. Physical scale for commute/logistics texture

At Band 1, "commute" collapses to **the distance between the manufacturing floor, the life-support core,
and the airstrip** — plausibly all under one roof or under connected, sealed structures, given the exterior
cost of moving between separate buildings at this exposure (per Phase 1's G2 GRUDGING TOLERANCE). This is
recorded as a physical inference from the constraint profile, not invented decoration.

## E. What the ordinary rhythm fails to provide

For anyone whose life does not fit the acclimatization-and-precision-work rhythm — someone who cannot
adapt well to the altitude, someone whose skills are needed but who struggles with the isolation — **there
is no accommodation built into this place at all**, per Phase 1's own DEFICIT finding (no civic apparatus,
no home-making capacity). This is not a policy failure; nobody decided to exclude anyone. The rhythm simply
was never designed with any slack in it, because slack was never a founding criterion (Phase 1, G4).
