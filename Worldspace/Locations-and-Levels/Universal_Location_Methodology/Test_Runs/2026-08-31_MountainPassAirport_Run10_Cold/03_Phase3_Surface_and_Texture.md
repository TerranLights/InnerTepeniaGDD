# Phase 3 — Surface & Texture

**Asks:** What is this place made of, and what is it like to be inside it — to the eye, ear, nose, and
skin?

---

## A. Formalize first

No existing scattered sensory material exists for this location in canon — it has never had any pass at
all before this one. This phase is written from Phase 1's constraint profile and Phase 1's own research,
not from prior fragments, and says so rather than falsely claiming a formalization step that had nothing
to formalize.

## B. The four sensory sub-fields, separately

**Sound.** Overwhelmingly mechanical and human-scaled, never crowd-scaled — this is a Band 1 location and
sound here is legible as *specific* sources, not ambience. The manufacturing floor's own equipment noise
(precision, not industrial-heavy — synthesis apparatus, not stamping presses); the airstrip's own
wind-scour, near-constant at this exposure; and, load-bearing per Phase 1's G2 STRENGTH reading, an
underlying **near-silence** between operational sounds that a visitor from anywhere else in Tepenia would
find unnerving — the atmospheric stillness that makes the site valuable is also acoustically total once
the machinery stops.

**Smell.** Cold has a smell-suppression effect (extreme dry cold carries almost nothing) — what does
register is sharp and specific: ozone-adjacent traces from precision electrical/synthesis equipment,
recycled-air staleness inside the pressurized/heated structure, and the faint machine-oil-and-metal smell
of a small manufacturing floor, all of it more noticeable for having so little competition from anything
else.

**Feel.** Two registers, sharply split — the pressurized, heated interior (dry, static-prone, per §C
below) versus the exterior (immediately dangerous, per Phase 1's G2 GRUDGING TOLERANCE). There is
essentially no intermediate zone; airlocks and suiting-up procedures are the actual texture of the
transition, not a gradient.

**First impressions.** A newcomer's first read is disproportion: an enormous amount of engineered
infrastructure (life support, structural reinforcement, precision equipment) wrapped around a very small
number of people, in the middle of nothing recognizable as a place. The disproportion **is** the first
impression, more than any single sensory detail.

## C. Public/institutional split — checked, thin result

At Band 1 with no civic apparatus (per Phase 1 and Phase 2), there is effectively no "street-level"
register distinct from "institutional" register — the entire built environment **is** the institution.
This is recorded as a genuine null for this sub-field rather than a forced answer: the split this
technique usually surfaces requires a population large enough to have both a public commons and separate
institutional interiors, and Mountain Pass Airport's Band 1 scale does not support the distinction.

## D. The seam — founding era vs. crisis era

Per the two-stage lens (`01` §4 rule 3): is there a visible boundary between the founding-era build and
anything added under later pressure? **Answer: not within this pass's own frame.** The Fort McMurdo
rejection and the move to Mountain Pass (Phase 1, G4) is the founding decision itself, not a later crisis
layered on top of an established base — there was no earlier Mountain Pass structure that a subsequent
pressure period modified. **This is a genuine null, correctly explained**: the seam this technique looks
for requires two distinguishable periods, and this location's neutral-frame history is short enough that
it has only had the one.

## E. Seasonal variance — the sharpest finding in this phase

**What does this place become at its worst point in the year, and is that the same place?** No — and the
gap is unusually large even by this methodology's own standard for hostile-environment locations.
**Open season:** a working, moderately populated technical facility, airstrip active, corridor traffic
passing through, the closest thing this location has to a public register. **Closure season:** a skeleton
crew of perhaps four to eight, the airstrip functionally idle, the corridor's own only stopping-place
reduced to almost nothing — per Phase 1's G2, the deep polar night closes the route entirely, meaning the
closure-season crew is not merely smaller, it is **cut off**, with no realistic prospect of the open-season
population's return until the season turns. **This is the location's single sharpest own-eras
differentiation axis** (per `01` §5.3a's own-earlier-states substitute, applied here across seasons rather
than across founding/crisis eras, since the latter produced a null in §D) — open-season Mountain Pass and
closure-season Mountain Pass differ more from each other than most locations in this methodology differ
from a genuine sibling.

## F. Retroactive Mechanism — the research-grounded finding

**What is established as simply true here that nothing yet explains, and what physical system would
produce exactly that?** Per Phase 1's research (real-world Concordia Station, Dome C — see the research
log): the same extreme atmospheric dryness that makes this site valuable for precision synthesis work also
**threatens the outpost's own digital records with static discharge**, and creates a genuine fire-hazard
profile wherever combustible material is present in the pressurized interior. **One physical property,
two opposite consequences on two different objects** — the dryness that serves the chambers being built
threatens the paperwork and data describing them.

**Second-order consequence, generated for free per the technique's own promise:** this plausibly explains
a specific, small, characterizing procedural habit — **manual, redundant, non-digital backup logging**
for anything safety- or tolerance-critical, alongside the digital systems, not because anyone distrusts
computers in the abstract but because this specific site has a concrete, repeatedly-relearned reason not
to trust a single digital copy of anything that matters. **This is exactly the kind of finding LAW 0 asks
for**: not "it is dry," which is a starting condition, but a specific behavioral consequence three steps
downstream of the same physical fact that also explains why the manufacturing itself is reliable here.

## Type and Band variance applied

**Installation type:** per `03`'s own Type-variance note for Structures/Network loci, no direct guidance
is given for Installation's own texture axis specifically — this pass treats it as closest to the
Settlement default (interior circulation and sensory register matter directly, since people live and work
inside), scaled down for Band 1.

**Band 1:** consistent with `01` §2.3's inversion — this phase describes twelve-or-fewer people's actual
sensory experience, not an averaged "Mountain Pass sensory profile" standing in for a population that does
not exist at this scale.

**Feeds:** Phase 4 (the seasonal split is the organizing fact of Phase 4's own ordinary-day answer),
Phase 5 (first impressions, above), Phase 10 (the static/backup-logging finding becomes catalog-shaped
material — a specific, nameable practice).
